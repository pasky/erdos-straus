# A five-square end extension that can be iterated for bounded common multiples

**Result (internal proof).** Starting with the countable external PID prime countermodel with IOpen and four squares, there is a countable model of **IOpen + Bézout + bounded common multiples + five squares** with the same positive prime `p≡1 mod 840` and `¬ES(p)`. Five squares means every nonnegative element is a sum of five squares. The construction does **not** give four squares in the final model. The PID used to execute each successor is not the discretely ordered ring in that successor. In particular no nonstandard external PID is claimed to satisfy bounded common multiples.

## 1. The recurring input, and where five matters

Keep an **upper** countable external PID `T` with fraction field `F`. Initially `D=T` is the old ordered ring. In subsequent steps `T` is an algebra over an ordered characteristic-zero field, with a distinguished prime `H`, residue field `k=T/HT`, and a subring `S⊂k`; the arithmetic ring is

`D={t∈T: t mod H∈S}`, with `Frac(D)=F`.

Assume `D` is discretely ordered, satisfies IOpen and five squares, and that **every** `a∈S` is a sum of five squares in `S`, with a **nonzero vector also at `a=0`**. Here `S` is not ordered; nonzero means nonzero over the field `k`, *not* a unit coordinate in `S`. No condition involving a sequence of composite-rank valuation completions of `D` is needed. We will verify hereditary Bézout separately.

At each prime `q` of `T` use the complete DVR `V_q` of the localization, fraction field `L_q`, and residue field `k_q`. Initially use the ordinary integrality condition at all `q`. Subsequently the local condition is `V_q` except at `H`, where it is `W_H={z∈V_H: z mod H∈S}`. These conditions on `F` cut out exactly `D`. For every such local condition `W`, the equation `Q_5(u)=b`, `Q_5=Σu_i²`, has a nonzero point over `L_q`, integral in `V_q` whenever `b∈V_q`, and with all coordinates in `W` whenever `b∈W`. Moreover the required points admit generic perturbation.

* Initial odd `q`: choose a positive generator `q`. Old Lag at `q` gives an isotropic vector for `Q_5` modulo `q` not all zero (otherwise `q²|q`). At each subsequent `q≠H`, use `±Hq>0` in `D` and five squares there. Its `q`-valuation is exactly one; the same argument works. At `H` use the nonzero isotropic vector from `S`. All these residue fields have characteristic different from 2 (indeed **zero** in subsequent successors). Over such a field a nonzero isotropic vector `u` extends to a hyperbolic pair: choose `u·v=1`, subtract `Q_5(v)u/2` from `v`. Then `u+bv/2` is a nonzero point for every residual `b`. Lift by Hensel in one coordinate with unit derivative, also at residual zero. At `H`, when the residual target belongs to `S` use its stipulated representation in `S⁵` instead, nonzero also for zero. The Hensel lift preserves these residues. At nonintegral `b`, scale by an even power of a uniformizer then scale back.
* Initially at 2, `V₂=ℤ₂` since old division by standard `2^j` gives exactly the ordinary remainders. Four squares cover `ℤ₂` by compactness and ordinary Lagrange. At **zero**, represent `−1` by four squares and append 1: this is a nonzero isotropic five-vector in `ℤ₂`. At any nonzero target a representation by four squares plus zero is already nonzero. The differential is nonzero **over `ℚ₂`**, which suffices for generic perturbation in an integral ball; no smooth reduction at 2 is asserted. In rank four, zero over `ℚ₂` has only the zero vector (divide out the common power of 2 and check mod 8). Its cone function field cannot embed there. This is a real failure of the same proof with four.

## 2. A new upper PID with one permanent kernel

Use `F[h]`, `h` greater than all of `F` in the extension order. Over `F` build a countable union `B` of finite-type rational-base algebras, with the following three tasks dovetailed. Here “rational-base” means **over `F`**, not necessarily a rational variety. Construct this entire algebraic tower and order before choosing marks. The base `F[h]` and all stages intersect the convex hull `O` of `F` in exactly `F`.

1. Relative floor approximations by free polynomial steps, using the ordered residue host `rc(Cauchy completion of rc(F))` and fresh residues of `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §§1–2. The point is approximation initially only within `O`, followed by its residue-to-unit lemma in §4 below.
2. For each pair divide by its **current stage** gcd. For nontrivial coprime cofactors `a,b` adjoin `aU+bV=1` as in `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §1. No rational point or single-field retraction is prescribed. A common height-one Gaussian divisor of the rational row would lie above a rational height-one common divisor (finite étale). Thus the row UFD proof by localizing at a pivot and Nagata works also over `F(i)`. Rational old primes remain prime by the two row charts, including a single surviving chart when one entry dies. On the generic line choose a free variable above the old real closure. The two old charts give intersection with the old field exactly the old algebra; the largest-scale device does not grow the relative residue field.
3. For every old stage element `s>0` outside `O`, adjoin `Q_5(u)=s`. In Gaussian coordinates `UV=s−z₁²−z₂²−z₃²`. The right side is prime over the Gaussian stage polynomial algebra: the leading form of rank three is not a product of two linear forms even over the algebraic closure; monicity embeds the quotient in the generic quotient. Hence `U` is prime and inverting it gives a Laurent UFD, proving Gaussian factoriality by Nagata. Units are the old Gaussian constants (the valuation at `U` eliminates nonzero Laurent powers). Finite étale Gaussian divisor descent, using Hilbert 90 for these constant units, gives rational factoriality, as in `LOGIC_CIRCLE_AUDIT.md` §1 but with constants `F(i),F`. Modulo **any** old rational prime the monic quotient embeds over its residual fraction field, and the leading rank-five test proves irreducible even at zero; old primes stay nonunit prime. The relative order is the scaled-coordinate order of `LOGIC_LAGRANGE_END_TEST.md` §2 with four independent fresh relative residues in a box inside the unit ball, and the fifth scaled coordinate the positive square root. Noncancellation after weighting coefficients by powers of `√s` follows from nonsquareness of `1−Σ_{1..4}t_i²` in the residue rational-function field. This keeps `B_j∩O=F` and the residue embeddings in the fixed host.

As in the external PID tower, finite stage factorization into **permanent** primes gives external atomicity, the rows give Bézout, and decreasing ordinary factor length along strict gcd descent gives external PID. In particular `h` survives and `k'=B/hB` is a **field**. It is the union of the stage domains `C_j=B_j/hB_j`. Over `F` these are geometrically integral: start at `F`, then take a line, a row with a nonzero pivot generically, or a rank-five quadric even at zero; the monic or row charts give the injection after base change. The embeddings needed next are of `k'`, not of `Frac(B)` with `h` mapping to zero.

## 3. Simultaneous residue-field marks and integrality

Choose `F`-embeddings `φ_q:k'→L_q`, extending the dense embeddings of `F`, with (a) each nonzero `z∈k'` a unit at all but finitely many `q`, (b) at every five-square step the coordinate images are in `V_q` when the target image is integral, and in `W_H` at the distinguished old mark when its image is in `W_H`. At the initial step there is no `H`.

Here are details of the simultaneous choice, especially for **non-finite** residue fields. For a fixed finite tower prefix and fixed finite list of nonzero regular functions/numerators/denominators in its stages, choose a dense open of the last `C_j` where all listed functions and chosen row pivots are nonzero and every step is smooth (delete quadric vertices even if a target is identically zero mod `h`). All functions on earlier stages pull back nontrivially by generic dominance. Spread the finite diagrams, charts and open out over `T[1/d]` for some nonzero `d∈T`, shrinking the base so this open has geometrically integral nonempty fibers and the tower on these charts stays smooth. For precision, all the data and finite-type spreading statements may first be made over the finitely generated subring of `F` of their coefficients, then inverted nonzero coefficients cleared in `T`; only finitely many primes of this external PID are lost. Enlarge the list to include coefficient denominators in the finite presentations. The rank-five forms on reduction are the same fixed form, with varying RHS.

At all large **finite** residue fields Lang–Weil gives points on the open, uniformly for the fixed degrees/number of equations (initial finite residue fields are the standard `𝔽_l`, by division). All **infinite** good residue fields admit dense rational points on the chart tower: over each such field the fixed `Q_5` has a hyperbolic plane by §1. After a fixed linear change solve a hyperbolic coordinate on the open where its partner is nonzero, giving a rational dense quadric chart for *every* target, even zero. Rows likewise have line charts and floor steps are lines. The iterated generic chart is rational; more explicitly the full reduced tower over this field is geometrically integral by the monic/row induction after spreading to keep its chosen pivot nonzero at each generic point. Thus the dense open restrictions do not discard all the rational points. This argument does not apply Lang–Weil to infinite residue fields or assume they are pseudofinite.

A residual point on this open gives prefix-compatible integral lifts, generic over the countable field `F`: choose free coordinates in the residue balls transcendently, and solve smooth pivot coordinates by Hensel with the actual previously lifted values. The local balls in a complete DVR of a countable field are uncountable (binary digit sequences suffice); avoid all algebraic dependences in the free parameters. This gives injectivity by the relative dimension and integral generic charts. Equivalently lift generically in the smooth open of the last stage. Assign each `q` a finite good prefix, with prefix lengths tending to infinity off finite sets: use an enumeration of primes and the successive cofinite success sets for longer prefixes/lists, and allow empty prefix at exceptions. Always give the old special prime `H` and the initial dyadic prime empty prefix. On the tail continue each embedding stage by stage. Any nonzero row pivot in the residual stage domain is a nonzero **field** pivot in `L_q`, not necessarily a unit there. Rows need no tail integrality condition. Floors are free; quadric tails use §1, perturbing in a ball preserving the residual coordinates when imposing `W_H`. This constructs (a),(b); no arbitrary infinite avoidance is imposed over one fixed residue field.

## 4. Arithmetic ring, floors and the renewed condition

Form `T'=⋂_q φ_q⁻¹(V_q)⊂k'`. Write `S'=T'` at the initial successor; otherwise impose also `red_H φ_H(z)∈S` to define `S'⊂T'`. The new arithmetic ring is `D'={b∈B: b mod h ∈ S'}`. In the next successor take **`B`, not `D'`**, as the upper PID; use distinguished prime `h`, residue field `k'`, and subring `S'`.

* `S'∩F=D`, so `D'∩O=D`. It contains `h/d` for every `0≠d∈D` because these have zero residue at `h`. Thus the extension is proper and end-extends the nonnegative cone. Clearing finitely many local poles by elements of `T` and an extra factor of `H` if present (both for integrality of the multiplier in `D` and zero residue of the product at `H`) gives `Frac(D')=Frac(B)` and `Frac(S')=k'`. In this statement take the clearing multiplier in `T` with sufficient positive valuation at `H` so it and its product have **strictly positive** valuation there.
* For any `b∈B` there is `c∈F` with `b−c∈D'`. In `T` take a common denominator `d` supported on the finitely many pole places of the marks of `b mod h`, also including `H` when present. Choose `dc∈T` by CRT matching the integral values `d φ_q(b mod h)` modulo `q^{v_q(d)}` at pole places, and modulo **`H^{v_H(d)+1}`** at `H`. Surjectivity to each completed quotient follows from `T` PID and density of its localization. Then the error at `H` has residue exactly zero, irrespective of whether `S` itself admits approximation. The relative floor tasks approximate each real-closure target within `O`; this CRT translation retains that bound. Now approximate its bounded error's relative residue inside the ordered host by an old `rc(F)` constant within `1/4` and use the old `D`-floor plus at most one integer adjustment (`LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §3). Hence `D'` is discrete with IOpen.
* Each relatively unbounded positive `s∈D'` was serviced, and by (b) all its five coordinates belong to `D'`; relatively bounded positives are old. Thus `D'` has five squares. More subtly **every signed `a∈S'`**, not just positive values of some ordered subring, has a five-square representation there: lift `a` to `b∈B` (hence `D'`) and replace it by `b+h(b²+1)>0`. It is positive since `h>1` and `b²+b+1>0`. Represent this in `D'` and reduce mod `h`. At `a=0` use a representation of `h` itself: not all coordinates lie in `hB`, since `h²∤h` in the PID. This verifies the nonzero residual-point assumption needed at the next special mark **without** proving any global quaternion splitting or keeping an infinite history of residue restrictions in the new local fields.

## 5. Why Bézout also survives

 `T'` itself is an external PID, though `D'` is not. For any nonzero `z∈k'`, (a) and the uniformizers from `T` scale all its valuations to zero by one element of `F`. Elements with all valuations zero are units in `T'`. Each `q` generates the kernel of reduction at its mark, with image `k_q` (already supplied by `T`), and is prime. This gives external factorization. A coprime pair `a,b∈T'` has an integral Bézout row: match by CRT in `T` an inverse of `a` modulo `q^{v_q(b)}` at the finitely many `q|b`; if `u` is the result, `(1−ua)/b` is integral everywhere. Therefore `T'` is atomic Bézout, hence PID. `S'` is either this PID or its pullback at `(H)` of the old subring `S`. Do not use a blanket assertion that such pullbacks preserve Bézout. In `B` a generator of `(h b₁,h b₂)` with `(b₁,b₂)B=(1)` can only be `h` times an upstairs unit, hence times an `F`-scalar; an arbitrary element of the residue field `k'` need not lift to such a unit. The extra content invariant below handles exactly this obstruction.

In the iteration `F^×` does generate the content of any finite tuple over `S'`, even at the extra place, as follows. In `T'` match its valuation minima by `c∈F^×` from `T`. For the residual pair `f,g` divided by `c`, at least one entry is a unit at `H`; their residues in `k=k_H` are in the **old residue field**, not the new `k'`. By induction maintain the auxiliary content property `(*)`: any nonzero fractional pair over `S` in `k` has its ideal generated by an element of `T^× mod H` (the image of upstairs units). Initially there is no `H`. Choose such a unit in `T^×` to correct `c` at `H` without changing any other valuation. Now the divided pair is in `S'`, is unimodular over `T'`, and its two `H`-residues generate 1 over `S`. Choose a `T'` row. Lift the desired `S`-row to `T'` (reduction surjects onto `k`), and correct the upstairs row by the syzygy `(g,−f)` times a lift of the difference parameter in `k`, so its reductions are the desired coefficients. The corrected row is in `S'`. This proves `(*)` for `S'` with upstairs `B^×`: `c` times the correcting `T`-unit lies in `F^×⊂B^×`. At the initial successor `S'=T'` and the first content scaling already proves `(*)`. Finally for a pair in `D'` divide out its `B`-gcd, scale it by an upstairs unit realizing the `S'` content of the two residual cofactors (not both zero as the cofactors are unimodular in `B`), and correct a `B` row by a lifted residual syzygy exactly as above. The scaled gcd itself belongs to `D'` since its residue is an `S'`-linear combination of the original pair's residues. This gives Bézout in `D'`; it does not give external atomicity there.

**Conclusion.** Iterate over the ordinary natural numbers. Endness and discrete order persist in the countable union; old floors in compatible real closures, Bézout rows and five-square tuples persist. Every positive bound lies in some `D`, its entire initial interval remains old, and the next `h` is a common multiple of that interval. A factorization or positive ES triple at the distinguished original prime would have old coordinates by endness: positive divisors are `≤p`, and sorted ES denominators are `≤2p⁴`. The old irreducible remains ring-theoretically prime by Bézout. This proves the statement above. It neither iterates four squares by this method nor proves a statement about standard ES.
