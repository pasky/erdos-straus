# Literature and novelty study for the signed refactor graph (2026-09-28)

Scope: novelty of the signed-graph results in `SIGNED_REFACTOR.md` /
`POINTWISE.md` §1, a survey of Erdős–Straus (ES) and related work since
2020, and a hunt for techniques that prove *every-prime* existence
statements. All PDFs read for this study are archived in
`sources/lit2026/` (provenance and SHA-256 in `sources/lit2026/README.md`);
older campaign sources stay in `sources/`. Page numbers are **PDF page
numbers of the archived arXiv version** unless a journal is named.
"Not found" means "not found in the sources listed here", not "new".

**Headline.** The **signed character theorem (2a) is already known**. It is
the case `n=p` of Bright–Loughran, *Brauer–Manin obstruction for
Erdős–Straus surfaces*, Bull. LMS 52 (2020) 746–761, arXiv:1908.02526v2,
Theorems 1.2 and 1.5 (p. 2). Their Lemma 3.4 (p. 11) evaluates the local
invariant. The identification also uses the campaign's signed valuation
lemma. The direction "positive ⇒ nonresidue" goes back to Yamamoto
(1965), as explained in their Appendix A (pp. 15–16). Finiteness of the
signed solution set is also theirs: Lemma 3.10 (p. 14). Existence of signed
solutions for every odd `n≥3` is Jaroma (2004). The label identity (2) and
the symmetric chart identity of §8 are, on positive points, reformulations of
the Elsholtz–Tao coordinates. The Type I/Type II rigidity is Jiang
Thm 3.2 in the positive case. I found **no** prior source
for the following:

* the refactor graph itself;
* the seed `(t,-2pt,-2pt)` and the three-edge hub bridge;
* the hub fibre counts `3τ(t²)` and `τ(t²)`;
* the Type II fibre bound "≤ 2 vertices";
* the large-`z` singleton bounds;
* the seed-component conjecture.

These results are elementary. Their value is structural, not a priority
claim. **No proof of ES has appeared.** Every recent claimed proof has an
identifiable gap (§2.3).

### 1(b) The signed character theorem (2a) — known (Bright–Loughran 2020)

**Statement in BL** (arXiv:1908.02526v2):

* **Thm 1.2 (p. 2).** For odd `n` and `u∈N³` a solution,
  `∏_{p|n}(−u₁/u₃,−u₂/u₃)_p = −1`.
* **Thm 1.5 (p. 2).** For an integer solution which is *not* all-positive,
  the product is `+1`.
* **Lemma 3.2 (p. 10).** For `v_p(n)≤1`, there exist `i≠j` with `u_i/u_j∈Z_p^*`.
* **Lemma 3.4 (p. 11).** If `u₂/u₃∈Z_p^*`, then
  `inv_p = ((−u₂/u₃)/p)^{v_p(u₁u₃)}`.
* **Cor 1.3 (p. 2).** For positive `u` and prime `n=p`,
  `((−u_i/u_j)/p)=−1` for the unit pair.
* **Proofs (p. 13).** The real invariant is `−1` exactly on `U(R)^+`
  (Lemma 3.1). Invariants at `p∤2n` are trivial (Lemma 3.5). At 2, for odd
  `n`, the invariant is trivial (Lemma 3.8). Hilbert reciprocity finishes the
  proof.

**Match with (2a).** For `n=p`, permute so that `u₂,u₃` is the
equal-valuation pair. The campaign's *signed valuation lemma* (§2) gives the
valuation patterns `(1,0,0)` or `(0,1,1)`, so `v_p(u₁u₃)=1` in Lemma 3.4. This
lemma is a necessary ingredient in addition to BL Lemma 3.2, which by itself
only supplies a unit ratio. The unit pair is exactly the "same-valuation pair"
`A,B` (with `p` removed), and `(−A/B | p) = (−AB | p)`. Hence, for every
**odd** prime `p`,

> vertex all-positive ⇔ `(−AB/p) = −1`.

For `p≡1 (4)` this is literally (2a). I re-checked the equivalence, including
`p≡3 (4)`, on all 8535 signed solutions for primes `3≤p<400`
(`scripts/literature_bl_character_check.py`). There were 0 violations; the
check also confirms that there is always exactly one equal-valuation pair.

**Historical layer.** BL Appendix A (pp. 15–16) shows that Yamamoto's
conditions are special cases of Cor 1.3 (Yamamoto, Mem. Fac. Sci. Kyushu
Univ. A 19 (1965) 37–47, Lemmas 2–4). These are, for example,
`(p/(4abd−1))=−1` for Type II and `(p/4abq)=−1` for Type I when `p≡1 (4)`.
Thus "positive ⇒ opposite characters" is Yamamoto 1965 / Mordell ch. 30
p. 290. The converse ("nonpositive ⇒ equal characters") is BL Thm 1.5. The
campaign's proof of (2a) is the elementary reciprocity device of Yamamoto,
EST Prop 1.6 (§4, p. 19) and Jiang Remark 8.5 (p. 17). It re-derives the BL
statement by hand.

**Comparison with arXiv:2609.09204 (Jiang).**

* **Status.** v1 was posted 5 Sep 2026. **v2 was withdrawn on 13 Sep 2026
  with the comment "some errors"**; only the v1 PDF is available and it is
  archived. Its character statements are:
  * Prop 7.3 (p. 14): `A(n)>0 ⇒ −1∈H_n` and `B(n)>0 ⇒ −4∈H_n`.
  * Prop 7.4 (p. 14): a real character mod `e_n=4n−p` that is trivial on
    the primes dividing `n` and has `χ(−1)=−1` kills *both* types.
  * Thm 8.3 (p. 17) and Cor 8.6 (p. 18): for numerator `m`, Type II is
    obstructed by `−1` and Type I by `−m`. The obstruction is type-blind when
    `m` is a square. When `m` is not a square, it is type-selective for a
    character with `χ(m₀)=−1`.

  I checked the short proofs of Prop 7.4 and Thm 8.3; they are correct.
* **Differences from (2a).**
  1. Jiang's characters live modulo the *moving layer modulus* `e_n`, not
     modulo `p`.
  2. They are conditional vanishing criteria for *positive* solutions. The
     hypothesis "χ trivial on the primes of n" holds on a density-zero set of
     layers (Jiang p. 21).
  3. They say nothing about signed solutions or about signs.

  (2a) is an unconditional *equivalence* for *all signed* vertices, with the
  character mod `p` on a pair of coordinates. The two share only the
  reciprocity device. Jiang's Cor 8.6 is the "counting" counterpart of the
  same phenomenon: at numerator 4, quadratic information is type-blind.
  BL Thm 1.6 says `Br U_n / Br Q ≅ Z/2`, generated by this one quaternion
  class. BL call it *transcendental*: it does not die over `Q̄`. So there is
  no *other* Brauer class, for instance no independent class of order 4,
  that could give a second Brauer–Manin invariant. This does **not** exclude
  non-Brauer invariants: fibre-level, component-level, or analytic ones.

**Other references checked for (2a).**

* **EST Prop 1.6** (p. 6, proof p. 19): vanishing at odd squares; the same
  device. BL Cor 1.4 recovers it.
* **Mordell ch. 30.** Not accessible; used only through EST and BL.
* **Schinzel** (Mathesis 65 (1956), Funct. Approx. 28 (2000)): no polynomial
  identity covers a quadratic-residue class. This is the polynomial shadow of
  the same character. Not accessed directly; statement via Jiang p. 2 and
  Chamberland p. 2.
* **Salez arXiv:1406.6307** (p. 4): the same fact for 3-Egyptian classes.
* **Bradford arXiv:1906.00561, 2403.16047.** No character criterion; only
  the remark that "quadratic residues play a role".
* **Chamberland**, Integers 26 (2026) #A42. No character criterion.

**Graph corollary.** "A Type II p-divisible bucket is entirely positive or
entirely nonpositive" is **not stated anywhere I found**. It is an immediate
corollary of BL together with label identity (2), whose status is given in
§1(c). It should be cited as such.

### 1(c) p-adic valuation lemma and the Type II fibre bound

**Valuation lemma** ("exactly one or two p-divisible coordinates, each with
valuation exactly 1").

* **Positive solutions.** EST p. 3 (one or two of `x,y,z` are divisible by
  `p`, by the displayed size argument) and **EST Prop 2.11** (p. 17, proof
  pp. 17–18): no denominator is divisible by `p²` when `m>3`. The latter is
  credited as a clarification of Mordell's classification.
* **Local part.** BL Lemma 3.2 (p. 10) gives the equal-valuation pair from
  the equation alone, for `v_p(n)≤1`. BL Remark 3.3 (p. 11) notes that it
  fails when `p²|n`.
* **Signed version.** Not found. Its proof is EST 2.11's archimedean
  estimate with signs tracked.
* **Verdict:** known in the positive case; the signed extension is routine.

**Labels.** In EST's torsor coordinates (arXiv:1107.1010v6 pp. 11–14) the
labels are literally EST parameters.

* **Type I.** `pm = x_EST = abdp`, and `4m−1 = pe` is EST (2.1)
  `4abd = pe+1`. Hence `λ=0`.
* **Type II.** `pm = acdp`, and `4m−1 = f` is EST (2.18). The label identity
  (2), `(4m−1)(4n−1)≡1 (mod p)`, is EST **(2.21)**
  `4c²dp+1 = f(4bcd−1)` reduced mod `p`.
* **"No p-divisible denominator is shared by the two types."** This is
  Jiang Thm 3.2 (p. 8) for positive solutions: `p|4V−1 ⇔` every solution
  containing `pV` is Type I. Jiang credits one step to Bradford, Integers 21
  (2021) #A24, Lemma 4. The signed version has the same proof.
* **"The seed is the only Type II vertex with label 1."** Not found; elementary.

**Type II fibre bound** (every Type II p-divisible denominator lies in at most
2 vertices, signed). Not found.

* **Closest statements.**
  * Jiang Thm 1.2 (p. 3, proof p. 12): a Type II solution is determined by
    `(a, g=cd)`, so there are `≤ Σ_{n≤K} τ(n)` solutions with `Y≤K`. The
    underlying uniqueness is "an interval of length g contains at most one
    element of a class mod mag−1"; it resembles the centered-representative
    argument of §5.
  * Jiang Thm 1.4 (p. 3): `A(n) ≤ 2(n/e+1)`, a bound at fixed p-free `x`,
    so the other side of the incidence.
  * EST Lemma 2.8 (p. 15): size relations.
* **Sanity check.** A brute-force check over positive Type II solutions,
  `p<2000`, finds maximal multiplicity 2, as predicted.
* **Verdict:** plausibly new, elementary. The large-`z` singleton bounds
  are in the same category.

### 1(d) Symmetric Type I identity `e|x² ⇔ e|m²` (SIGNED_REFACTOR §8)

**Known in disguise.** Take a positive Type I vertex in EST coordinates
(Prop 2.2, p. 12). Then `x = acd` (p-free), `m = abd` (`pm` is EST's `x`),
`A = 4x−p = f` (EST (2.6)) and `H = (4m−1)/p = e_EST` (EST (2.1)). The
chart quantity is therefore

    e = (AH−1)/4 = (e_EST f − 1)/4 = a²d          by EST (2.7): ef = 4a²d+1.

So `e | x² = a²c²d²` and `e | m² = a²b²d²` both hold identically. The
"symmetry" is the statement that EST's torsor coordinate `a²d` divides both
squares. On positive points it is therefore the chart form of
Proposition 2.2. EST do not state the signed chart equivalence itself, and
their Remark 2.4 (p. 13) exchanges the two p-free denominators `y,z`, not
`x` and `m`. Compare also Jiang Cor 4.7 (p. 10):
`δ|pa+c ⇔ δ|p²+4c²d`. The campaign's direct proof (`Hx=m+e`, `gcd(H,e)=1`)
is fine but should cite EST (2.1), (2.6), (2.7).

### 2.1 Substantive research papers

* **Bright–Loughran 2020** (above). Their main results:
  * no Brauer–Manin obstruction to ES (Thm 1.1/1.8);
  * a BM obstruction to strong approximation always exists (Thm 1.8, (1.5));
  * BM does not explain all failures of strong approximation (Thm 1.9,
    p. 4, via non-Zariski-density of `U_n(Z)`);
  * `Ũ_n ⊃ G_m²`, but the surface is not toric, so the Cao–Xu/Wei theorem
    "BM is the only obstruction" for toric varieties does not apply
    (Remark 3.12, p. 15).
* **Elsholtz–Planitzer**, Proc. RSE A 150 (2020) 1401–1427,
  arXiv:1805.02945. Counts solutions of `m/n`; for almost all `n`,
  `f(n) ≥ (log n)^{log 6+o(1)}` (per erdosproblems #242 and Jiang ref. [8]).
* **Bloom–Elsholtz**, "Egyptian fractions", survey written for *Nieuw Archief voor Wiskunde* (2022),
  arXiv:2210.04496. A survey. Its Theorem 1 is the covering criterion: ES
  holds iff for every prime `p` there are `a,c,d` with
  `p≡−a/c (mod 4acd−1)` or `p≡−(4c²d+1)/k (mod 4cd)` for some
  `k|4c²d+1`. The paraphrase follows erdosproblems #242.
* **Pomerance–Weingartner**, arXiv:2511.16817v2. Already audited in
  `reviews/external-pw-2511.16817-wave28.md`.
  * Schinzel exceptions exist with `n_m ≥ exp(m^{1/3+o(1)})` (Thm 1.1).
  * For `m ≥ 6.52·10⁹` there is an exceptional prime in `(m²,2m²)` (Thm 1.2).
  * An explicit-in-`m` Vaughan bound (Thm 1.3).

  The method is a first-moment count: exceptions exist where the expected
  number of solutions, `(log p)³/m`, is `<1`. It **cannot** say anything at
  `m=4`, where the mean is large.
* **Dahan**, arXiv:2608.24035v1. Already in the campaign (PROJECT.md
  Outcome 23).
  * Fixed search depth `J` leaves `≪_J N(log N)^{-1-𝔄(J)/2}` failures.
  * An **unconditional infinite obstruction at shift `c=7`**, obtained from
    FHRSS (below).
  * An `O(N log³N)` Type II / `Θ(N²)` Type I interval decision procedure.
  * Fourteen "hyperbolic-family" failures below `5·10⁹`.
* **Jiang**, arXiv:2609.09204v1, **withdrawn in v2** ("some errors"). Counting
  `f_I,f_II` via Bradford's layers:
  * Type II concentrates at `x/p→1/m` (Thm 1.2);
  * the character dichotomy above;
  * `f_I≡f_II≡1_{p≡3(4)} (mod 2)` for **ordered** counts (Thm 5.3(iii),
    p. 11);
  * conjecture `f_I>f_II` for `p≢1 (8)`.

  Because the paper is withdrawn, cite individual statements only after
  checking the proof (Prop 7.4 and Thm 8.3 were checked here).
* **Chamberland**, Integers 26 (2026) #A42 (published 3 Apr 2026). A Type II
  solution exists iff `p = qr − 4s₁s₂` with `q≡3 (4)` and
  `s₁,s₂ | (q+1)/4`. This is a reformulation, and the paper conjectures that
  every prime has this form.
* **Bello-Hernández–Benito–Fernández**, arXiv:2606.10922. A divisor
  parametrisation `fab(n,a,b)` of decompositions of `1/n` into three unit
  fractions whose denominators are all divisible by 4. Dividing by 4, these
  are exactly the decompositions of `4/n`
  (Thm 5), compared with Type I/II. A reformulation.
* **Mihnea–Bogdan**, arXiv:2509.00128. Verification to `10¹⁸` and data on
  `f(p)`; erdosproblems cites it as the current verification record.
* **Salez**, arXiv:1406.6307 (2014, pre-2020 baseline). Verification to
  `10¹⁷` and modular filters.
* **Bradford**:
  * Integers 21 (2021) #A24 = arXiv:1906.00561, `z = xyp/(gcd(y,p)·gcd(xy,x+y))`;
  * arXiv:2107.05400 (Pythagorean triples);
  * Integers 25 (2025) #A54 = arXiv:2403.16047, the bijection behind Jiang's
    layers and the conjecture that some `x∈[⌈p/4⌉,⌈p/2⌉]` works.
* **López**, arXiv:2206.10319, 2404.01508. Solution "shapes" and the
  Type A/B conjecture.
* **Schuh**, arXiv:2503.11672. Two conjectural representations of
  Pythagorean primes.
* **Ventas**, arXiv:2605.04551v2. The "ceiling continued fraction" framework
  and a **heuristic** Borel–Cantelli finiteness argument (explicitly
  heuristic).
* **Xu**, arXiv:2605.23601 and 2609.29250. Twenty-eight "tame" and
  thirty-four "wild" polynomial families. Numeric tests "cover all primes"
  `24m+1` in range, and 14 wild primes `≤2.4·10¹¹` are reported (Howerton).
  No claim of proof. By Schinzel, finitely many polynomial families cannot
  cover the square classes.
* **Gionfriddo–Guardo**, arXiv:2005.03273: `n≡13 (24)`, a classically
  solvable class.
* **Lazar**, arXiv:2003.01237: no solution with `x,y` coprime and
  `xy<√(z/2)`.
* **Arab**, arXiv:2108.02024: "well-defined equations"; also argues that
  known methods cannot prove ES.
* **Mballa**: arXiv:2502.20935, 2505.08716, 2602.20036, 2602.21112.
  Parametric reformulations. 2602.20036 proves an "explicit solutions for a
  density-one set" statement, which already follows from Obláth-type
  criteria. The zeta-function papers are speculative.

**Sierpiński 5/n and Schinzel.**

* PW (above) is the only substantial recent work.
* Ghermoul, arXiv:2508.07367, "Almost a complete proof…" for 5/a. The class

* Dyachenko, arXiv:2511.17716: parametric algorithms for 5/P. Its own

* Jiang v1 §8 (general `m`, withdrawn paper).
* Elsholtz–Planitzer 2020 (counts).

**Related unit-fraction work (not ES)**: Bloom, arXiv:2112.03726 and

### 2.4 AI-assisted reports

* **Akaka, "A cubic lower bound for strict Erdős–Straus solution counts at
  almost all primes"** (Zenodo 22754703, v0.7, 2026-09-14; archived as
  `akaka-zenodo-22754703-v0.7.pdf`). It is also listed as a *partial* proof
  claim on erdosproblems #242.
  * **Tools.** Declared AI-assisted: ChatGPT, Claude and Kimi.
  * **Claim.** `R_II(p) ≥ c(log p)³` for all but `O(N/(log N)⁴)` primes
    `p≤N`. The family is `M=3abu`, `s=3a²u`, `12abu−1 | p+12a²u`,
    `B = 2^{⌊⌊log₂N⌋/100⌋}` (so `B≈N^{1/100}`), `Q₀=12B⁸`, `Z=Q₀²`
    (§5 eq. (17), PDF p. 6). The proof uses a full-period unit variance
    `V≪L³` against
    mean `≍L³` and Bombieri–Vinogradov.
  * **Formalisation.** A Lean certificate is claimed, with BV imported from
    "FormalPantheon" and a platform-pinned toolchain.
  * **Assessment.** Unrefereed, but not implausible. It is a concentration
    statement for a sum over many *different* shifts `p+12a²u`, not for one
    divisor function. It agrees with the EST Poisson heuristic (EST
    Remark 1.2). It would strengthen EST Thm 1.8's density-one lower bound
    `(log p)^{0.549}`. It is **not** a proof of ES and does not beat Vaughan
    for zero solutions; the author says both explicitly.

## 3. Global ideas: techniques that prove statements for **every** prime

The seed-component conjecture implies ES for `p≡1 (4)` (SIGNED_REFACTOR §7).
Any technique must therefore produce positivity pointwise. For each
technique below I first state what it proves elsewhere, then its
applicability.

**(i) Parity / windmill involutions** (Heath-Brown, *Invariant* 1984;
Zagier, AMM 97 (1990) 144; Elsholtz, "A combinatorial approach to sums of
two squares and related problems", in *Additive Number Theory* (Nathanson
Festschrift), Springer 2010, 115–140, DOI 10.1007/978-0-387-68361-4_8).

* **How it works.** Take a finite set with an explicit, piecewise-linear
  involution that has a unique fixed point. A second involution then has a
  fixed point, and that fixed point is the desired representation.
* **Limits.** Elsholtz §4 (pp. 21–23) shows how fragile this is. The
  matrices close up into a finite partition only for special forms
  (`x²+4yz`, `x²+2yz`, `3x²+4yz`). For `3x²+2yz` infinitely many regions
  are needed and a gap remains ("we see how incidental the above described
  finite mappings are").
* **ES.** The natural finite set exists: the signed vertices (BL
  Lemma 3.10). On ordered **positive** solutions, the swap of the two
  same-type denominators has fixed points only when `p≡3 (4)` (Jiang
  Thm 5.3(i)–(ii)). On signed solutions it does have fixed points, the seed
  among them. For `p≡1 (4)` the ordered positive counts `f_I,f_II` are
  therefore even (Jiang Thm 5.3(iii)), so parity of the obvious counts is
  useless. SIGNED_REFACTOR
  §8(i) found no parity invariant for unordered counts either.
* **Seed conjecture.** A windmill proof would need a *component-preserving*
  involution on the finite signed graph. Its fixed points would have to be
  the seed plus an odd number of other vertices, all nonpositive except
  positive ones. No candidate is piecewise-linear: the moves are divisor
  moves, not linear maps. **Low prospect.**

**(ii) Topological / degree / divisibility arguments** (Bourgain–Gamburd–
Sarnak arXiv:1607.01530; W. Chen, *Ann. of Math.* 199 (2024) no. 1,
arXiv:2011.12940; D. Martin arXiv:2502.15960; Eddy–Fuchs–Litman–Martin–Tripeny
arXiv:2308.07579; C. Brown arXiv:2401.00630; de Courcy-Ireland–Litman–Mizuno
arXiv:2509.02187; Ma–Yang–Yang arXiv:2608.07880).

* **What it proves.** This is the one modern success at "a graph of
  solutions is connected for **every** large prime". BGS prove that the
  Markoff mod-`p` graph (Vieta moves) has a giant component and that every
  other component is small. Chen proves that *every component has size
  divisible by p*: a degree congruence on a Hurwitz-space map, i.e. moduli
  geometry. Together these give connectivity for `p > 3.45·10³⁹²` (Eddy et
  al.), and Brown checks `p<10⁶`.
* **Structural lesson.** Existence was forced by an *arithmetic invariant of
  component sizes* plus a *giant-component* estimate. Neither is visible
  for ES:
  * sterile component sizes are `1…16` with no divisibility pattern
    (SIGNED_REFACTOR §8(ii));
  * the ES graph lives over `Z` (finite, divisor-controlled), not over `F_p`;
  * the ES automorphism group is finite (notes §77), so there is no Vieta
    group.
* **A geometric coincidence (our observation).** The projective ES surface
  `S_n` is Q-isomorphic to Cayley's 4-nodal cubic (BL §2.1, p. 5). Any cubic
  surface with 4 rational non-coplanar nodes is Q-projectively equivalent to
  `Σ a_i ∏_{j≠i} x_j = 0`, and hence, after rescaling, to Cayley's.
  Examples include the degenerate Markoff-type surface
  `x²+y²+z²−xyz=4`, with nodes `(±2,±2,±2)` (even sign count). That
  surface is excluded from Ma–Yang–Yang's theorem and appears as the
  exceptional parameter in de Courcy-Ireland–Litman–Mizuno. The two log-K3
  structures (boundary divisors) differ, so Vieta dynamics does not transfer.
* **Real topology.** BL Lemma 3.1 shows `U(R)^+` is a whole connected
  component. Graph edges jump between real branches of the fibre hyperbola,
  so no homotopy or degree invariant of real paths constrains them.
* **Prospect:** only if one finds a *size congruence* for components (test:
  component sizes mod small primes, or mod `p`, over the census). None is
  visible.

**(iii) Class-number / genus-theory arguments.**

* **Model success.** Borwein–Choi, *Exp. Math.* 9 (2000) 153–158: `xy+yz+zx=n`
  is solvable in positive integers for all `n` except the 18 values of OEIS
  A025052, and at most one more, which is excluded under GRH (OEIS
  comment). The paper was not accessed; the mechanism below is my
  understanding of it. `n+z²=(x+z)(y+z)` shows that
  `(x+z)X²+2zXY+(y+z)Y²` is a form of discriminant `−4n`. Failure means
  that every class is represented only in the degenerate (reduced/ambiguous)
  way, an idoneal-type condition. OEIS A025052 records Peters' theorem that
  the exceptional list equals the list of discriminants without
  indecomposable positive forms. Siegel then allows only finitely many
  exceptions, ineffectively.
* **ES differs.** No formula writes `f(p)` or any Type count as a class
  number; EST show it behaves like averaged divisor functions. The campaign
  found that ES failure is **not** a subgroup condition on the class group
  of `−4p` (POINTWISE §2: at 2521 the principal genus is sterile, a
  non-principal genus is productive; at 193 a class fails while its square
  succeeds).
* **The whole Brauer layer is (2a).** BL Thm 1.6 shows
  `Br U_n / Br Q ≅ Z/2`, generated by the quaternion class whose local
  invariant at `p` is the quadratic character (2a). BL call the class
  transcendental, not algebraic. BL Thm 1.8, (1.4), shows
  that it gives no Brauer–Manin obstruction to the *existence* of positive
  solutions. (It does obstruct strong approximation, by (1.5).) So the
  *Brauer–Manin* content is exhausted by (2a): *positive vertices are
  exactly those with a character mismatch*. It cannot *create* a mismatch.
  This does not rule out other genus-type or class-group constructions.
* **Principal-genus trap (explained).** Every hub denominator is built from
  divisors of `t`. For `ℓ|t`, `(ℓ/p)=1`, so the hubs sit in the trivial
  character class, which (2a) says is the nonpositive one. This explains why
  exits need fresh primes; it is a consequence of (2a), not an independent
  obstruction.
* **Prospect:** negative, unless ES counts acquire a class-number
  interpretation.

**(iv) Brauer–Manin / fibration methods for integral points** (BL; Harpaz,
"Integral points on conic log K3 surfaces", JEMS 21 (2019) 627–664,
arXiv:1511.04876; Colliot-Thélène–Wittenberg, AJM 134 (2012); Ghosh–Sarnak,
*Invent. Math.* 229 (2022) 689–749).

* **Setting.** `u₁ ↦ u₁` fibres `U_p` in affine conics. The *generic* fibre
  is the split hyperbola `(ru₂−s)(ru₃−s)=s²`. Degenerate fibres are
  `u₂u₃=0` over `u₁=0` and the line `u₂+u₃=0` over `u₁=p/4`. `U_p` is
  singular at the origin (BL §2), whereas Harpaz's Definition 3.0.1 requires
  a smooth, simply connected surface with an snc boundary. So `U_p` is
  "conic log K3" only after passing to a suitable smooth model. Whether that
  model meets Harpaz's hypotheses was not checked.
* **Harpaz's tool.** Swinnerton-Dyer's descent-fibration method, with
  Schinzel's hypothesis replaced by Green–Tao–Ziegler. It produces integral
  points for families like (1.0.2) under explicit square-class independence
  hypotheses (Thm 1.0.1, p. 2).
* **Why it fails here.**
  * We already have integral points (signed). What is needed is a point in
    one real component, which is a *strong approximation* question.
  * BL Thm 1.9 shows BM does not control strong approximation on this
    surface in general.
  * GTZ-type inputs need linear forms with coefficients bounded
    independently of the box. Here `p` is both a coefficient and the box
    size (`x ≤ p/2`).
* **Ghosh–Sarnak** (Markoff-type `x²+y²+z²−xyz=k`). They show integral
  Hasse failures for infinitely many `k`, and the Hasse principle for almost
  all `k`, by counting. This is the log-K3 norm: almost-all is provable,
  every-`k` is not.
* **Prospect:** gives the right *language* (strong approximation on a conic
  log K3). There is no pointwise tool.

**(v) Bhargava-style composition.**

* **What it needs.** Higher composition laws (Bhargava, *Ann. of Math.* 159
  (2004)) give group structures on orbits of integral forms under
  `SL₂(Z)^k`. Here orbits of 2×2×2 cubes compose binary quadratic forms.
* **ES.** Neither the ES surface nor its universal torsor (Heath-Brown
  Lemma 1; EST (2.1)–(2.9)) is known to be such an orbit space.
* **What exists in the campaign.** The fixed-`(c,k)` monoid law
  `u∘_L v = u+v+Luv` (DISCOVERIES C31, notes §20.1). It composes Type I
  witnesses within one slice, but does not move between primes.
* **What would be needed.** A composition taking ES data for `p` and some
  auxiliary object to data for `p` with changed character. By (2a) that
  character is the only Brauer invariant (BL Thm 1.6). The following is a
  heuristic, not a theorem: any composition respecting genus characters
  multiplicatively would need one composand that is itself a nonresidue
  datum, which is what we are trying to produce.
* **Prospect:** low; no concrete composition law is known.

**(vi) Vieta jumping, thin orbits, Apollonian analogies** (Haag–Kertzer–
Rickards–Stange, arXiv:2307.02749; Friedlander–Fuchs–Harris–Hsu–Rickards–
Sanden–Schindler–Stange, arXiv:2410.00177).

* **Analogy.** The refactor moves are the ES analogue of Vieta moves. They
  are divisor-set moves rather than involutions, because the equation is
  linear in each variable.
* **HKRS result.** The Apollonian local-global conjecture is **false**
  because of quadratic and quartic *reciprocity obstructions*, attached to
  the *thin orbit* and not to its Zariski closure. They describe these as
  "reminiscent of a Brauer–Manin obstruction".
* **Relevance to the seed conjecture.** The seed component plays the role
  of a thin orbit inside all signed integral points. BL rules out a
  BM/quadratic obstruction for the full point set, but a **component-level**
  quadratic or quartic reciprocity invariant would *refute* the seed
  conjecture on a residue class of `p`. The campaign checked only quadratic
  colours (§8(iii)).
* **Quartic test (done here; negative).** On nonpositive vertices `(AB/p)=+1`
  by (2a), so the quartic symbol `(AB/p)₄ ∈ {±1}` is defined.
  `scripts/literature_quartic_component_test.py` enumerates every component
  for all 383 primes `p≡1 (4)` below 6000 (the complete graph from
  `pointwise_incidence`) and asks whether this symbol is constant on the
  component's nonpositive vertices.
  * **Seed components:** constant in 0 of 383.
  * **Sterile components:** 14 have 5 nonpositive vertices, 4 have 6, 1 has
    7, and 5 have 8 or more. Every one of these 24 is mixed.
  * **Smaller components:** the fraction that happen to be constant is close
    to the iid null `2^{1−k}` (k=2: 0.49 vs 0.50; k=3: 0.33 vs 0.25; k=4:
    0.26 vs 0.13). The mild excess at k=3–4 is expected, because vertices
    sharing a Type II bucket share a pair.

  So this quartic candidate carries no HKRS-type component invariant. This
  fits BL Thm 1.6: `Br U_n/Br Q = Z/2` has no element of order 4. An HKRS-style
  obstruction would have to come from the thin (component) structure
  itself, and none is visible in this candidate. Other quartic candidates
  remain untested: `(λ/p)₄` of Type II labels, and `(x/p)₄` of single
  anchors.
* **Orbit "class numbers".** Ghosh–Sarnak (arXiv:1706.06712 abstract):
  the Markoff morphisms act on `V_k(Z)` with *finitely many orbits*, which
  they study numerically as "class numbers". These orbits are the exact
  analogue of the components of the refactor graph. "Does the seed
  component contain a positive vertex?" is the analogue of asking which
  orbit meets a given real region. No theorem of that type is known even
  for Markoff-type surfaces.
* **Prime components.** FFH+ study maximal tangency-connected *prime*
  components, a close cousin of "the component of the seed". Their results
  are empirical and structural, with no every-`p` existence.

**(vii) Small prime factors of shifted primes in residue classes / least
nonresidues** (Pollack, arXiv:1508.05035; Linnik–Vinogradov; Burgess).

* **What is provable pointwise.** For every `p` there is a small prime
  `q ≡ 3 (mod 4)` with `(q/p)=−1`.
* **What ES needs.** ES needs a prime *factor* of a specific shifted value
  (`(p+q)/4`, `(pc+a)`, `(3p+1)/4`, …) in a class. No unconditional
  pointwise result of that kind exists beyond **forced** congruences.
  Example: for `p≡1 (4)`, `p+2` always has a factor `≡3 (4)`. Forced
  classes are exactly the polynomial-identity/covering classes, which
  Schinzel/Yamamoto/EST Prop 1.6 show cannot cover the square classes.
* **Test case.** Try `q=4x−p` a small prime nonresidue and the anchor
  `x=(p+q)/4`. The quadratic condition `(−x/q)=+1` then holds automatically
  by reciprocity. The divisor condition `−x ∈ ⟨primes of x⟩ mod q` depends
  on the factorisation of the single integer `x`, and nothing controls it
  pointwise. Prime `x` is useless (only `q|p+4` works), as notes §8.3
  already records.
* **Prospect:** negative for ES. For the seed conjecture it gives only
  "which branches are open", not a guarantee.

**(viii) Iwaniec's half-dimensional sieve and FHRSS** (Fuchs–Hsu–Rickards–
Schindler–Stange, arXiv:2504.20289, Thm 1.1 p. 1).

* **What it proves.** `≫ N/(log N)^{3/2}` primes `p≤N` in a progression are
  *primitively* represented by `Bf(x,y)+A`. This is lower bounds for thin
  sets, the opposite of every-prime. Dahan uses it (Thm 4.14) to show that
  shift `c=7` fails for infinitely many primes.
* **Use for the seed conjecture (negative side).** The h=1 two-move branch
  of (9) fails iff `(3p+1)/4` has no prime factor `≡2 (3)`, i.e. all prime
  factors `≡1 (3)` (3 cannot divide it). This is a representation of
  Equivalently, `(3p+1)/4` (which is coprime to 3) is **primitively**
  represented by `x²+xy+y²`. Ordinary representation is not enough: at
  `p=5`, `(3p+1)/4=4` is a norm but contains `2≡2 (3)`, and the branch
  succeeds.
  * **Upper bound.** The failure set is a half-dimensional sieve set. It
    has `≪ N/(log N)^{3/2}` elements by a standard upper-bound sieve at the
    BV level, as in the proof of Dahan Thm 4.14.
  * **Lower bound.** The matching lower bound would need an FHRSS-type
    input: the representation is primitive, and one must also arrange
    `p = (4f−1)/3` prime. I have not checked FHRSS's hypotheses for this
    shape. So `≍ N/(log N)^{3/2}` is **conjectural**.
* **Data check.** The campaign's 3052/12980 ≈ 0.235 at `p<3·10⁵` is
  consistent with a slowly decaying `C(log N)^{-1/2}` relative density, not
  a positive proportion.
* **Limit.** Forcing *all* `τ(t²)` branches to fail is a sieve of growing
  dimension, beyond these methods.
* **Prospect:** useful to quantify "branch failure is thin", never to force
  success.

**(ix) Geometry of numbers / Thue–Minkowski.**

* **What it proves.** Pigeonhole gives small solutions of linear congruences
  for every `p`, as in Thue's proof of the two-squares theorem.
* **The natural ES attempt.** Make `k = 4xy − p(x+y)` small and need `k|pxy`.
  This is `(4x−p)(4y−p) = p²+4k`. For `k=1` it fails for every `p≡1 (4)`:
  every odd prime factor of `p²+4` is `≡1 (4)` (−4 is a square), but
  `4x−p ≡ 3 (4)` is required.
* **Larger `k`.** For `k≥2` there is no such general trap. The prime
  factors of `p²+4k` satisfy `(−k/ℓ)=1`, but that is **not** the character
  of (2a). Example: `p=5, (x,y,z)=(2,4,20)` has `k=2` and `p²+4k=33`, yet
  it is all-positive.
* **The real obstacle.** Pigeonhole makes `k` small, but it cannot force the
  divisibility `k|pxy` together with the required factor classes of
  `p²+4k`.
* **Prospect:** negative, but for the uncontrolled divisibility, not
  because of a character trap.

**(x) Spectral / Linnik–Duke methods.**

* **What they prove.** Every-large-`n` theorems for ternary quadratic forms
  (Duke–Schulze-Pillot) come from equidistribution on homogeneous spaces.
* **ES.** The surface is a log K3, not homogeneous, and the relevant counts
  are divisor-type. There is no known spectral model.
* **Prospect:** negative.

**Synthesis for the seed-component conjecture.**

* **What the literature fixes.** The only Brauer class (`Br U/Br Q = Z/2`,
  transcendental) gives the BL/Yamamoto character (2a). It gives no BM
  obstruction to existence (BL Thm 1.8), and it identifies positivity
  exactly.
* **What a proof would need.** Something beyond Brauer–Manin, like Chen's
  component-size congruence, or an equidistribution statement for the
  factorisations of the specific shifted anchors `t+a` reachable from the
  seed.
* **One literature mechanism that could *disprove* the seed conjecture
  on a residue class** is an HKRS-style component-level reciprocity
  invariant. The natural quartic candidate was tested here and fails to be
  invariant (§3(vi)).
* **Citation changes.** In any write-up, (2a) must be cited to Bright–Loughran
  (and Yamamoto), the labels and the §8 symmetry to Elsholtz–Tao
  coordinates, and the Type I/II rigidity to Jiang Thm 3.2 (positive case).
