**PROVED (one step).** Let `R₀` be a countable discretely ordered external PID with `IOpen + Lag` on its nonnegative cone (`Lag`: each nonnegative element is a sum of four squares). There is a countable discretely ordered domain `R′` whose cone properly end-extends the old cone and satisfies `IOpen + Lag`. There is one positive `h>R₀` such that `h/t∈R′` for **every** nonzero `t∈R₀`. Every old prime element stays prime, and ES failure at an old positive prime persists. In particular one may use the countable PID of `LOGIC_FOUR_SQUARE_AUDIT.md` §3, or the joint PID of `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md` (both cross-reviewed; z74 Opus xreview fix). No external PID or Bézout hypothesis on the *output* is needed or established; indeed it cannot be an external PID. This is not global BCM.

### Local targets, including singular specializations

Put `F=Frac(R₀)`. For each positive prime representative `q` use `V_q`, the completion of the DVR `(R₀)_(q)`, and its field `F_q`. The residue field is `R₀/q`, not assumed finite. External PID is important: the uniformizer has ordinary discrete valuation, each nonzero scalar has **externally finite** support, and `R₀=F∩⋂_q V_q` (via diagonal embeddings).

For `q≠2`, its residue characteristic is not 2: the floor of `x/2` gives remainder 0 or 1, so `(2)` is maximal. Write `q=Σx_i²` by old Lag. Not all `x_i` are in `(q)`, as then `q∈(q²)`. Reducing gives a nonzero isotropic vector. Lift it to a primitive isotropic `v` over `V_q` by Hensel in a coordinate with `2x_i` a unit, keeping the other three fixed; the equation being lifted is `Q(v)=0`, `Q=ΣX_i²`. Take `w` with `2v·w=1`; then `w′=w−Q(w)v` is isotropic with `2v·w′=1`. For **any** `a∈V_q`, `Q(w′+av)=a`. This proof does not require Hensel over `R₀`, finite residue fields, or local compactness.

At 2, division with remainder by each standard `2^j` (floor in F by IOpen) shows `R₀/(2^j)=ℤ/(2^j)` compatibly: the interval of possible remainders consists exactly of the standard numerals in that range. Localization at `(2)` does not change these quotients. Thus `V₂=ℤ₂`. The image of `Q:ℤ₂⁴→ℤ₂` is compact and contains all nonnegative ordinary integers by classical Lagrange, hence is all of `ℤ₂`. At every q a nonintegral `a∈F_q` is represented over `F_q` by first multiplying by `q^(2j)` to make it integral and then dividing the coordinates by `q^j`. At `a=0` even the zero vector suffices: our marks are **ring maps**, not embeddings of function fields.

### Ordered stages and the single residue host

Let `K=rc(F)`. In ordered extensions use the convex hull `O` of F (bounded in magnitude by some old scalar), with residue killing elements smaller in magnitude than every positive old scalar. The order construction is relative to **F**, not to ℚ. As in `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §1, fix `H=rc(L)` for the ordered-field Cauchy completion L of K. Here are the density and supply checks. Countability of K gives a countable coinitial sequence of positive radii. A binary tree of separated nested K-intervals with diameters tending to zero gives uncountably many elements of L in any given open K-interval. K is dense in L by construction. K is cofinal and positive-coinitial in H by the polynomial root bound over L and inversion; likewise F is cofinal and positive-coinitial in K. For `α∈H` take a monic `f∈L[X]` vanishing at α, of degree d. For any positive `ε∈K`, approximate its coefficients in K, bounding `|α|` first by an element of K. This yields monic `g∈K[X]` of degree d with `|g(α)|<ε^d`. In `H[i]` one of the d roots of g is within ε of α by the product formula for the complex absolute value of the factorization. Its real part belongs to K, since `K[i]` is algebraically closed. Hence K is order-dense in H. No embedding theorem for arbitrary countable ordered extensions of K is being assumed.

Maintain a countable stage F-algebra A ordered in `E=rc(Frac A)`, with `A∩O=F` and a compatible ordered embedding of the residue of E into H fixing K. Start at `F[h]`, `h>K`, ordered at infinity over K. In `K(h)` a relative unit has residue the ratio of its leading coefficients (degrees equal); its residue field is K. Passing to ordered real closure gives algebraic residue extension, hence still K. In general algebraicity of residues follows by reducing an algebraic relation for a bounded element after dividing by a coefficient of smallest valuation. The ordered residue of a real closed field is real closed (lift square roots and roots of monic odd polynomials, bounding the latter by their coefficients). Hence at each real closure the residue embeds in H over the preceding embedded residue by uniqueness of ordered real closure.

Two operations suffice. In both, give new bounded parameters a Gauss order on `E(t₁,…,t_m)` by choosing residues algebraically independent over the current embedded residue field in H. Normalize each polynomial by a coefficient of greatest absolute value; its reduction at this tuple is nonzero, and its sign gives the order after restoring the coefficient. This is the Gauss valuation with residue the rational function field in the chosen residues; its valuation ring is precisely the relative convex hull since F is cofinal and positive-coinitial in H. Each stage is countable and any small K-interval offers uncountably many choices: choose the finitely many residues successively, avoiding the algebraic closure of a countable field each time. This works throughout the countable tower, with embeddings extending the previous ones.

* **Floor task.** For `α∈E` already O-close to some `a∈A`, skip. Else adjoin `Y=α+t` with one fresh residue for t in `(1,2)`. A bounded polynomial `Σa_iY^i` of degree `d>0` would have all Taylor coefficients at α bounded (Gauss). Then `a_d∈F×` and `d a_d α+a_{d−1}∈O`, contradicting the lack of an old O-approximation. Thus the invariant persists and `α−Y∈O`.
* **Sphere task.** For a positive `s∈A` **outside O**, adjoin four coordinates with `Σu_i²=s`. Over E set `u_j=√s t_j` for `1≤j≤3`, using three fresh residues in `(1/10,1/5)`, and set `u₄=√s √(1−Σt_j²)` with the positive root. For the monic normal form of degree ≤1 in `u₄`, scale each coefficient `a_I` by `(√s)^|I|`, and divide all by one of largest absolute value. After reduction the evaluation is `f(ξ)+√(1−Σξ_j²)g(ξ)`, not both polynomials zero. The radicand is nonsquare in the residue rational function field: `1−ΣX_j²` is nonconstant squarefree in characteristic zero (coprime to its derivative in `X₁`). Transcendence therefore prevents cancellation. The evaluation is a relative unit times the normalizing coefficient. This proves injectivity on the presented algebra and preserves `A∩O=F`: any nonzero coefficient on a positive-degree monomial has magnitude at least some positive old scalar, so its scaled magnitude exceeds every old bound. The square root and subsequent ordered real closure use only the algebraic residue extension discussed above. We never add a generic sphere at an old bounded value.

Alongside these orders keep maps `e_q:A→F_q` fixing F. Start with `e_q(h)=0`; at a floor send the polynomial variable Y (not its auxiliary ordered-field parameter t) to 0. At a sphere solve `Q(u)=e_q(s)` in the local field as above, **integrally whenever `e_q(s)∈V_q`**. These choices respect the full presentation, the sole relation being the sphere equation. No simultaneous rational landing or injectivity is needed. Each fixed stage element is integral at almost all q by induction: scalars have finite denominator support, floor variables are integral, and the only possible bad primes of the new sphere coordinates were already bad for s. This means finite *negative* support only; the zero evaluations of h at all q are intentional.

### Pullback, floors and preservation

Dovetail all floor tasks in each stage real closure and all positive outside-O sphere tasks in each stage. Let A now denote the union. The compatible real closures exhaust `rc(Frac A)` by algebraicity and real closedness of their union. Define `R′={a∈A : e_q(a)∈V_q for all q}`. For each `a∈A` choose `d=∏q^j∈R₀` supported on its finitely many bad primes with `d e_q(a)` integral there (`j` large enough at each). The quotient `V_q/q^jV_q=R₀/(q^j)` by localization and completion. CRT gives `b∈R₀` matching `d e_q(a) mod q^j` there. Thus `r=a−b/d∈R′`, including at all other primes since d is a unit there. In particular `Frac R′=Frac A`, and replacing an O-approximation by r changes it only by an F-scalar.

For any `α∈rc(Frac R′)` we now have `α−r∈O`. Approximate its embedded residue in H within `1/4` by `k∈K`. Upstairs `|α−r−k|<1/2`. The old floor of k exists by Shepherdson for `R₀`; adding that floor to r and correcting by at most one unit floors α. Also `R′∩O=R₀` by `A∩O=F` and the intersection of the old DVRs. This proves discreteness, endness of the cone and IOpen by Shepherdson. Properness and common divisibility use the same h: `h/t∈A` has **zero** evaluation everywhere for every nonzero old t. Bounded nonnegative elements of `R′` are old (bounded here means relative to F), so use old Lag. Every other positive element got a sphere task, and its four coordinates are integral at **every** mark since the input is. Take absolute values in the ordered ring if cone witnesses are required.

In fact `R′/qR′=R₀/qR₀`: reduction of `e_q` is onto via the constants, and a kernel element divided by q stays integral at q and at every other mark. Thus old primes remain prime even in the prime-ideal sense. For any sorted positive ES solution at old p, `x≤3p/4`, `y≤2px/(4x−p)≤2px`, `z=pxy/(4xy−p(x+y))≤pxy`, since positive denominators in these bounds are at least 1 by discreteness. All three would be old by endness. Finally the ideals `(h/2^j)R′` form a strictly increasing external chain (2 is not a unit). The construction therefore does **not** supply the input PID hypothesis at a second step, nor establish a common multiple for a new interval cofinal over the old cone.
