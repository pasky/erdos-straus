**Results in one paragraph.** The brief asked for a proof that K meets U, or a finite covering of K. Neither was
obtained. What was obtained:
* **(R) Reduction to smooth shadows (PROVED).** K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅ iff, for every bound P, the family classes with P-smooth

* **(N) U is Haar-null (PROVED, one line).** So every certificate must live on a measure-zero slice of profiles.
* **(M) Event equations for profiles that are 1 off a finite prime set S (PROVED).** All seven families, including the

* **(Obstruction, scoped.)** On free slices the class-measure series diverges as P → ∞ (PROVED). On rigid slices the
  tail at 11 needs explicit Type II point counts with exponent < 1/2 on n up to N² (Assessment, quantified in §4).

* **(Rational slices; CERTIFIED.)** Every slice "π_p = q for all p ≥ 11" with q a non-residue {2,3,5,7}-unit
  (24 values tested) is covered by explicit family classes, for q = 2 by the single class 6481 mod 8360.

  value that survives. So the only rational slices that can carry points of K̄ ∩ U look like UCOVER's "1 elsewhere".
* **(Data; CERTIFIED to stated levels.)**
  * Free 13-smooth shadow: 1 316 612 of 1 556 755 200 residues of K mod 3.1·10¹² survive all 13-smooth classes
    (fraction 8.5·10⁻⁴; no finite covering).

## 1. Precise statements

* **Family classes.** These are the residue classes r mod m of the seven ET Prop 1.9 families (exact list in the
  docstring of `scripts/z67_k11_classes.py`). Each is primitive: gcd(r, m) = 1. Define U := {π ∈ Ẑ^× : π lies in no
  family class}. U is closed and contains (Ẑ^×)².
* **The set K̄.** K̄ := {π ∈ Ẑ^× : π ≡ 1 (mod 840), (π₁₁/11) = −1}; it is compact and open.
  * π ≡ 1 (mod 840) forces π_p to be a square in ℤ_p^× for p = 2, 3, 5, 7.
  * Haar measure μ is normalised on Ẑ^×.
* **Conjecture K11** (LOGIC2 §5.6, UCOVER §5): K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅. By LOGIC2 Thm D this is equivalent to two statements:
  * no finite union of family classes covers K's unit profiles;
  * NOI ⊬ "ES at all large primes of K".
* **Refutation form.** K̄ ∩ U = ∅ ⟺ finitely many family classes cover K̄ (by compactness). Such a covering is a
  finite, checkable certificate.
* **Literature** (research subagent; quotes checked against the pages):
  * Salez (arXiv:1406.6307, §3.1): the filter S₁₁ = {0, 7, 8, 10} covers the non-residues 7, 8, 10 mod 11, not 2, 6.
  * Tao (blog, 2011-07-07, "On the number of solutions to 4/p = …"): "I do not know how to eliminate the 2, 6 mod 11
    classes."
  * No published statement settles finite coverability of K either way.

## 2. Reductions (PROVED)

**Lemma R (smooth shadows).** For P ≥ 11 let B_P := {(π_p)_{p≤P} : π_p ∈ ℤ_p^×, π ≡ 1 (mod 840), (π₁₁/11) = −1} be the
P-box. A family class is *P-smooth* if its modulus is P-smooth. Let X_P ⊆ B_P be the set of points of B_P lying in no
P-smooth class.
* (a) K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅ ⟺ X_P ≠ ∅ for every P.
* (b) X_P = ∅ ⟺ finitely many P-smooth classes cover B_P.
* (c) Hence K̄ ∩ U = ∅ ⟺ some single P has X_P = ∅.

*Proof.*
* Whether π lies in a P-smooth class depends only on π_{≤P} := (π_p)_{p≤P}.
* So K̄ ∩ U = ∩_P Y_P with Y_P := {π ∈ K̄ : π_{≤P} ∈ X_P}. The Y_P are closed and decreasing, and Y_P ≠ ∅ ⟺ X_P ≠ ∅
  (the components at p > P are unconstrained).
* Compactness of K̄ gives (a); compactness of B_P gives (b); (c) combines them. ∎

**Lemma N (U is Haar-null).**
* For a prime f ≡ 3 (mod 4), the II-2 class with a = 1, d = (f + 1)/4 is −4a²d = −(f + 1) ≡ −1 (mod f).
* So every π with π_f ≡ −1 (mod f) for some such f ≥ 13 lies outside U.
* Under Haar measure the events {π_f ≡ −1 (mod f)} are independent, with probabilities 1/(f − 1) summing to ∞.
* By Borel–Cantelli II, almost every π satisfies one of them. Hence μ(U) = 0, and μ(K̄ ∩ U) = 0. ∎

*Consequence.* A certificate of K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅ cannot be "the uncovered part of K̄ has positive Haar measure". It must
work on a Haar-null slice (UCOVER uses the slice "1 elsewhere") or be a non-measure argument such as an explicit point
or a König/compactness construction. LOGIC2's internal review had left "U could be a nonempty null set" open; the
second half of that sentence is now settled.

**Lemma M (event equations for profiles 1 off a finite set S).**

*Setting and notation.*
* Let S be a finite set of odd primes, γ ∈ ∏_{p∈S} ℤ_p^×, and let π = e_{S,γ} have component γ_p at p ∈ S and 1 at
  every other prime.
* For a positive integer X write X = X_S X′, where X_S is the S-part and X′ is prime to S.
* A class r mod m catches π iff r ≡ 1 (mod m′) and γ ≡ r (mod m_S). The second condition is the *cylinder* of the
  class.

*Catch conditions, family by family* (ET Prop 1.9 notation; c := (f + 1)/(4ad) for II-2):

| family | catch condition (r ≡ 1 mod m′) | cylinder γ ≡ … |
|---|---|---|
| I-1 (−f mod 4ad, f ∣ 4a²d+1) | f ≡ −1 (mod 4a′d′); equivalently f = 4a′d′C − 1 ∣ n + 4d′C², with n := a_S²d_S | −f mod a_S d_S |
| I-4 / II-1 (−1/e, −e mod 4ab, e ∣ a+b) | e = 4a′b′D − 1 and c(4a′b′D − 1) = a_S a′ + b_S b′ (c := (a+b)/e) | −1/e, −e mod a_S b_S |
| II-2 (−4a²d mod f, f = 4acd − 1) | f′ ∣ a + c | −a/c mod f_S |
| I-2 (mod 4ac·f) | f ≡ −1 (mod 4(ac)′) and f′ ∣ a + c | −f mod (ac)_S; −c/a mod f_S |
| I-3 (mod 4cd·f) | f ≡ −1 (mod 4(cd)′) and f′ ∣ 4c²d + 1 | −f mod (cd)_S; a root of x² ≡ −4c²d mod f_S |
| II-3 (mod 4ad·e) | e ≡ −1 (mod 4(ad)′) and e′ ∣ 4a²d + 1 | −e mod (ad)_S; −4a²d mod e_S |

*Proof.* Each row is the condition r ≡ 1 mod m′, worked out family by family.
* For I-1, write f = 4a′d′C − 1. Modulo f we have 4a′d′C ≡ 1, so f | 4na′²d′ + 1 ⟺ f | n + 4d′C²
  (gcd(4d′C², f) = 1).
* For II-2: c(4a²d + 1) − af = a + c and gcd(c, f) = 1. Hence −4a²d ≡ 1 (mod f′) ⟺ f′ | a + c.
* For I-3 the family contains a class for every root, and the roots modulo f = f_S f′ are exactly the CRT pairs of
  local roots. So the root ≡ 1 (mod f′) is available iff 1 ≡ −4c²d (mod f′).
* The rest is CRT on the coprime pieces of the modulus. ∎

*Reading.*
* With |S| = 1, the I-1, I-4/II-1 and II-2 rows are UCOVER's (A), (E/D) and (F).
* The rows I-2, I-3 and II-3 with the S-primes split between the two coprime parts of the modulus are the "mixed
  support" events. UCOVER §5 did not enumerate them beyond modulus 10⁶; its example is the II-3 class −1215 mod
  21736.
* Each row has finitely many events per S-level (v_p(m))_{p∈S}. For I-2, for instance:
  * f′ ≤ a + c and 4(ac)′ | f_S f′ + 1 bound min(a, c) in terms of the level;
  * a quotient argument then bounds the other variable.
  This makes exact multi-prime enumeration possible in principle. It was not implemented here: §4 shows that
  exactness at finite levels does not decide the question.

## 3. Rational slices (CERTIFIED coverings; EVIDENCE on the pattern)

*Setting.* For a finite set T of primes and a rational T-unit q, the **slice** Σ(q, T) ⊂ K̄ is the set of profiles with
π_p = q for every p ∉ T and with π_T free in the T-box. The script is `scripts/z67_k11_tbox.py`.
* It enumerates all seven families literally up to modulus MMAX.
* It keeps the classes with r ≡ q (mod m′) whose T-cylinder meets the box.
* It intersects the box at a T-level.
* Survivors = 0 is a CERTIFIED finite covering: the script writes it out, and it can be checked by hand.

**Results.**
* **T = {2, 3, 5, 7}.** Here q must itself be a non-residue mod 11. All 24 tested q are covered at the level
  2⁶3³5²7²: q ∈ {2, 6, 7, 8, 10, 1/2, 1/6, 2/3, 3/2, −1, −3, −4, −5, −9, 1/7, 7/2, 5/2, 2/5, 12, 14, 18, 24, 3/7, 10/3}.
  * For q = 2 a single class does it: the I-3 class c = 2, d = 19, f = 55, root 46, i.e. **6481 mod 8360**.
  * Check by hand: 6481 ≡ −55 (mod 152); 6481² ≡ 26 ≡ −4·2²·19 (mod 55); 6481 ≡ 1 (mod 40) and 6481 ≡ 2
    (mod 209). So the class contains every profile with π ≡ 1 (mod 40) and π_11 ≡ π_19 ≡ 2.
  * File: `data/z67/k11/slice_q2_cover.txt`.
* **T = {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13}** (q any T-unit).
  * At level 2⁴3²5·7·11²13² = 1.03·10⁸ with MMAX = 10⁵: q = 4, 9, 2, 3, −1 are covered (0/51480 survivors). q = 1
    leaves 161/51480.
  * At level 2⁵3³5²7·11²13² = 3.09·10⁹ with MMAX = 3·10⁵: q = 1 leaves 276/1 544 400 (`slice_q1_T13.txt`; this is an
    upper bound, since moduli above MMAX are not used).
* **Reading (EVIDENCE).**
  * A rigid slice catches few classes when r ≡ q (mod m′) is a strong condition. q = 1 is the strongest (UCOVER's
    "1 elsewhere").
  * The non-square q, and also the squares 4 and 9, are caught by explicit classes as soon as two primes outside T
    carry the value q. The q = 2 covering is of exactly this kind.
  * So the only rational-slice candidates for points of K̄ ∩ U are UCOVER-type: 1 at all but finitely many primes.
* **Rigid two-value profiles.** For π(q₁, q₂) := (q₂ at 2, 3, 5, 7; q₁ at every p ≥ 11), the classes with modulus
  ≤ 2·10⁴ that catch it are listed in `rigidprobe_2e4.txt`:
  * q₁ = 2: 5 classes, the first II-2 with a = 41, d = 1, f = 1475;
  * q₁ = 1/2: 3 classes;
  * q₁ = 6: 18 classes;
  * q₁ = −1/2: 4024 classes. Negative q are caught massively; e.g. −M lies in the II-2 class a = Mc for every c.

**Two-prime rational candidates (EVIDENCE).** `scripts/z67_k11_ratprobe.py` (data `ratprobe_4e5_K4J3.txt`) tests
profiles (g at 11, h at 13, 1 elsewhere) with g, h rationals of height ≤ 12. They are checked against all seven
families with modulus ≤ 4·10⁵, together with UCOVER's exact two-prime A/E/F events to level (11⁴, 13³) (A-splits capped
at N₁²N₂ ≤ 10⁹).
* 26 of 69 × 182 pairs survive.
* All survivors have g ≡ 2 (mod 11) and h ≡ 2 (mod 13), matching UCOVER's (2, 2) branch.
* The smallest survivor is π° = (2, 2).

**CONJECTURE (explicit witness).** π° := (2 at 11, 2 at 13, 1 elsewhere) ∈ U. This would give K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅.
* By Lemma M, π° ∉ II-2 classes iff there is no (a, c, d) such that 4acd − 1 = N f′ with N = 11^k13^j > 1,
  f′ | a + c and N | a + 2c.
* Equivalently: (a + c)(a + 2c) = st(4acd − 1) with N = (a + 2c)/s, where s, t are positive integers. This is a
  family of Vieta-jumping quadratics with an S-unit side condition. Similar systems hold for the other six families.
  No finite certificate is apparent.

## 4. Why no tail certificate exists with present tools (scoped)

The brief's route: exact survivors to a finite level, minus an explicit bound on all deeper events, must stay
positive. Three regimes:
* **(i) Free slices** (all components at p ≤ P free; this is Lemma R's X_P).
  * For fixed P the series Σ_{C P-smooth} μ_{B_P}(C) converges, because it is dominated by P-smooth Dirichlet
    series.
  * As P grows it diverges. The II-2 classes −1 mod f alone contribute Σ_{13 ≤ f ≤ P, f ≡ 3 (4)} 1/(f−1) → ∞
    (PROVED, as in Lemma N). So no single union bound works for all P; an argument uniform in P must be adaptive
    (König).
  * Numerically even P = 13 is out of reach. At the computed level the survivors have relative mass 8.5·10⁻⁴ (§5).
    The total measure of the classes not yet used is far larger; the 2-adic layer alone, measured at 2-levels 9–12
    with 3⁴5²7²11²13², gives about 0.42, 0.26, 0.15, 0.09 per level. The I-1 classes dominate, and the decay ratio
    is only about 0.6 per level.
  * A crude divisor bound τ(4a²d+1) ≤ C(a²d)^{1/4} makes the tail ≍ Σ a^{−1/2} over smooth a. Assessment: exact
    enumeration would be needed to 2-levels around 25–30 and comparable depths at 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, with
    factorisations of numbers 4a²d+1 of 60+ digits. That is infeasible.
* **(ii) Rigid slices** (1 elsewhere, free at S ∋ 11, 13; UCOVER's two-prime problem).
  * The I-1 events of Lemma M sit on ΣII at n = a_S²d_S, up to N² for cylinder level N.
  * The trivial bound ≍ √n gives non-decaying mass in the a_S-direction (a cylinder of measure 1/N carries ≍ N
    events). So the tail needs P_II(n) ≤ C n^θ with θ < 1/2 explicitly. This is ET's 2/5, which they prove with
    divisor-bound losses.
  * Assessment (constants as in UCOVER §4): such bounds become useful only for N ≳ 10³⁰–10⁵⁰. Exact enumeration on
    ΣII at n = N² costs ≍ N. The gap cannot be bridged by computation.
  * This is the same missing ingredient as UCOVER O1; ELL0 attacks it for large ℓ.
* **(iii) Rational slices.** By §3 they are covered (CERTIFIED for the tested q, T) except for q = 1. So they offer no
  escape from (ii).

**Scoped statement (Assessment, with PROVED parts as marked).** Every route to K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅ through a measure
certificate has two options:
* use a Haar-null slice (Lemma N, PROVED), where the tested rational slices other than 1-elsewhere are covered (§3,
  CERTIFIED); or
* bound ΣII point counts with exponent < 1/2 explicitly at n = 11^a13^b (open, = UCOVER O1).

Free slices fail uniformly in P (PROVED divergence).

## 8. Open problems
* **O1.** Is π° ∈ U? This is a fixed-profile Diophantine question: seven families of quadratic equations with S-unit
  side conditions. It is the most concrete form of the K-question.
* **O2.** Is X_P ≠ ∅ uniform in P? This needs a König argument with a per-prime extension lemma: every point of
  X_{<q} that is "generic" in some sense extends at q.
* **O3.** Is U ∩ K̄ contained in the closure of the profiles that are 1 at all but finitely many primes? §3 is
  consistent with yes.
