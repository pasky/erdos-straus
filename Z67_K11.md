# Z67_K11 — does K = {n ≡ 1 (840), (n/11) = −1} meet the uncovered set U? Verdict: narrowed (no proof, no refutation; HOT LEAD: no)

Lane K11, wave z67 (brief `z52bbriefs/z67_lanes.md` §K11; common rules `z58_lanes.md`, `z53_depth.md`).
**ES is OPEN. Nothing here proves or disproves ES or any case of it.** Internal, unrefereed; internal hostile review: §9.
Scripts `scripts/z67_k11_*`, data `data/z67/k11/`. Builds on `Z66_UCOVER.md` (cited UCOVER), `Z65_LOGIC2.md`
(Thm D, §5.6; cited LOGIC2) and Elsholtz–Tao Prop 1.9 (`sources/elsholtz-tao-1107.1010.pdf` p. 8 and §10; cited ET).

**Results in one paragraph.** The brief asked for a proof that K meets U, or a finite covering of K. Neither was
obtained. What was obtained:
* **(R) Reduction to smooth shadows (PROVED).** K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅ iff, for every bound P, the family classes with P-smooth
  moduli do not cover the P-box of K̄. A finite covering of K is equivalent to one P failing this.
* **(N) U is Haar-null (PROVED, one line).** So a certificate cannot be "positive Haar measure of the uncovered
  part"; it must work on a Haar-null slice or be a non-measure argument.
* **(M) Event equations for profiles that are 1 off a finite prime set S (PROVED).** All seven families, including the
  "mixed" I-2, I-3, II-3 cases that UCOVER §5 left open. Each row has finitely many parameter solutions per S-level,
  with explicit search bounds (PROVED, Prop M′).
* **(Obstruction, scoped.)** On free slices the *raw* class-measure series diverges as P → ∞ (PROVED). This defeats
  the unconditioned union bound, not every argument. On rigid slices the counting route would be completed by explicit Type II
  point counts with exponent < 1/2 on n up to N². This is sufficient, not proved necessary. The estimates tried
  here do not give a tail certificate at feasible depth (Assessment, §4).
* **(Rational slices; CERTIFIED coverings, EVIDENCE on the pattern.)**
  * T = {2, 3, 5, 7}: every *nonempty* K-slice "π_p = q for all p ≥ 11" tested (21 values of q, each a non-residue
    {2,3,5,7}-unit) is covered by explicit family classes. For q = 2 a single class suffices: 6481 mod 8360.
  * T = {2,…,13}: the slices q = 4, 9, 2, 3, −1 are covered at the tested level; q = 1 is the only tested value
    that survives.
  * This is consistent with the CONJECTURE that the only rational slices that can carry points of K̄ ∩ U are
    UCOVER-type, "1 elsewhere". That is not proved.
* **(Data; CERTIFIED to stated levels.)**
  * Free 13-smooth shadow: 1 316 612 of 1 556 755 200 residues of K mod Q = 3.1·10¹² lie in no family class whose
    modulus divides Q (fraction 8.5·10⁻⁴). No covering was found at this level. That X_13 ≠ ∅ is NOT proved:
    13-smooth moduli with higher exponents are not used.
  * Explicit candidate: the profile π° = (2 at 11, 2 at 13, 1 elsewhere) survives everything computed (EVIDENCE).
    **CONJECTURE: π° ∈ U**, which would give K ∩ U ≠ ∅.

## 1. Precise statements

* **Family classes.** These are the residue classes r mod m of the seven ET Prop 1.9 families (exact list in the
  docstring of `scripts/z67_k11_classes.py`). Each is primitive: gcd(r, m) = 1. Define U := {π ∈ Ẑ^× : π lies in no
  family class}. U is closed and contains (Ẑ^×)².
* **The set K̄.** K̄ := {π ∈ Ẑ^× : π ≡ 1 (mod 840), (π₁₁/11) = −1}; it is compact and open.
  * π ≡ 1 (mod 840) forces π_p to be a square in ℤ_p^× for p = 2, 3, 5, 7.
  * Haar measure μ is normalised on Ẑ^×.
* **Conjecture K11** (LOGIC2 §5.6, UCOVER §5): K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅. By LOGIC2 Thm D this is equivalent to two statements:
  * no finite union of family classes covers K's unit profiles;
  * NOI ⊬ "ES at all large primes of K".
* **Refutation form.** K̄ ∩ U = ∅ ⟺ finitely many family classes cover K̄ (by compactness). Such a covering is a
  finite, checkable certificate.
* **Literature** (research subagent; quotes checked against the pages):
  * Salez (arXiv:1406.6307, §3.1): the filter S₁₁ = {0, 7, 8, 10} covers the non-residues 7, 8, 10 mod 11, not 2, 6.
  * Tao (blog, 2011-07-07, "On the number of solutions to 4/p = …"): "I do not know how to eliminate the 2, 6 mod 11
    classes."
  * No published statement settles finite coverability of K either way.

## 2. Reductions (PROVED)

**Lemma R (smooth shadows).** For P ≥ 11 let B_P := {(π_p)_{p≤P} : π_p ∈ ℤ_p^×, π ≡ 1 (mod 840), (π₁₁/11) = −1} be the
P-box. A family class is *P-smooth* if its modulus is P-smooth. Let X_P ⊆ B_P be the set of points of B_P lying in no
P-smooth class.
* (a) K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅ ⟺ X_P ≠ ∅ for every P.
* (b) X_P = ∅ ⟺ finitely many P-smooth classes cover B_P.
* (c) Hence K̄ ∩ U = ∅ ⟺ some single P has X_P = ∅.

*Proof.*
* Whether π lies in a P-smooth class depends only on π_{≤P} := (π_p)_{p≤P}.
* So K̄ ∩ U = ∩_P Y_P with Y_P := {π ∈ K̄ : π_{≤P} ∈ X_P}. The Y_P are closed and decreasing, and Y_P ≠ ∅ ⟺ X_P ≠ ∅
  (the components at p > P are unconstrained).
* Compactness of K̄ gives (a); compactness of B_P gives (b); (c) combines them. ∎

**Lemma N (U is Haar-null).**
* For a prime f ≡ 3 (mod 4), the II-2 class with a = 1, d = (f + 1)/4 is −4a²d = −(f + 1) ≡ −1 (mod f).
* So every π with π_f ≡ −1 (mod f) for some such f ≥ 13 lies outside U.
* Under Haar measure the events {π_f ≡ −1 (mod f)} are independent, with probabilities 1/(f − 1) summing to ∞.
* By Borel–Cantelli II, almost every π satisfies one of them. Hence μ(U) = 0, and μ(K̄ ∩ U) = 0. ∎

*Consequence.* A certificate of K̄ ∩ U ≠ ∅ cannot be "the uncovered part of K̄ has positive Haar measure". It must
work on a Haar-null slice (UCOVER uses the slice "1 elsewhere") or be a non-measure argument such as an explicit point
or a König/compactness construction. LOGIC2's internal review had left "U could be a nonempty null set" open; the
second half of that sentence is now settled.

**Lemma M (event equations for profiles 1 off a finite set S).**

*Setting and notation.*
* Let S be a finite set of odd primes, γ ∈ ∏_{p∈S} ℤ_p^×, and let π = e_{S,γ} have component γ_p at p ∈ S and 1 at
  every other prime.
* For a positive integer X write X = X_S X′, where X_S is the S-part and X′ is prime to S.
* A class r mod m catches π iff r ≡ 1 (mod m′) and γ ≡ r (mod m_S). The second condition is the *cylinder* of the
  class.

*Catch conditions, family by family* (ET Prop 1.9 notation; c := (f + 1)/(4ad) for II-2):

| family | catch condition (r ≡ 1 mod m′) | cylinder γ ≡ … |
|---|---|---|
| I-1 (−f mod 4ad, f ∣ 4a²d+1) | f ≡ −1 (mod 4a′d′); equivalently f = 4a′d′C − 1 ∣ n + 4d′C², with n := a_S²d_S | −f mod a_S d_S |
| I-4 / II-1 (−1/e, −e mod 4ab, e ∣ a+b) | e = 4a′b′D − 1 and c(4a′b′D − 1) = a_S a′ + b_S b′ (c := (a+b)/e) | −1/e, −e mod a_S b_S |
| II-2 (−4a²d mod f, f = 4acd − 1) | f′ ∣ a + c | −a/c mod f_S |
| I-2 (mod 4ac·f) | f ≡ −1 (mod 4(ac)′) and f′ ∣ a + c | −f mod (ac)_S; −c/a mod f_S |
| I-3 (mod 4cd·f) | f ≡ −1 (mod 4(cd)′) and f′ ∣ 4c²d + 1 | −f mod (cd)_S; a root of x² ≡ −4c²d mod f_S |
| II-3 (mod 4ad·e) | e ≡ −1 (mod 4(ad)′) and e′ ∣ 4a²d + 1 | −e mod (ad)_S; −4a²d mod e_S |

*Proof.* Each row is the condition r ≡ 1 mod m′, worked out family by family.
* For I-1, write f = 4a′d′C − 1. Modulo f we have 4a′d′C ≡ 1, so f | 4na′²d′ + 1 ⟺ f | n + 4d′C²
  (gcd(4d′C², f) = 1).
* For II-2: c(4a²d + 1) − af = a + c and gcd(c, f) = 1. Hence −4a²d ≡ 1 (mod f′) ⟺ f′ | a + c.
* For I-3 the family contains a class for every root, and the roots modulo f = f_S f′ are exactly the CRT pairs of
  local roots. So a class whose root is ≡ 1 (mod f′) exists iff 1 ≡ −4c²d (mod f′), and its root modulo f_S is any
  local root.
  * The row is existential. A *given* I-3 class catches π only if its own root is ≡ 1 (mod f′).
  * Example (internal review): S = {11}, (c, d, f) = (1, 11, 3). The class 41 mod 132 has root 2 mod 3 and does
    not catch e_{11,8}; the class 85 mod 132 (root 1 mod 3) does.
* The rest is CRT on the coprime pieces of the modulus. ∎

*Reading.*
* With |S| = 1, the I-1, I-4/II-1 and II-2 rows are UCOVER's (A), (E/D) and (F).
* The rows I-2, I-3 and II-3 with the S-primes split between the two coprime parts of the modulus are the "mixed
  support" events. UCOVER §5 did not enumerate them beyond modulus 10⁶; its example is the II-3 class −1215 mod
  21736.
**Proposition M′ (finite, effectively enumerable events per S-level; PROVED).**
*Statement.* Fix the S-level, i.e. the S-parts of the modulus pieces. Then each row of Lemma M has finitely many
parameter solutions, with explicit search bounds.

*Notation.* Within each item below, the letters introduced as S-parts (A, B, F, N, N₁, N₂, and C, D in the I-3
item) are fixed by the level. The variables x, y, z are S-free. All other letters (C, w, D in the I-1, I-4 and I-2
items; s, t, u) are auxiliary positive integers, not necessarily S-free. The split of a fixed S-part between two
parameters (e.g. (ac)_S = A·B) has finitely many choices. The searches may produce extraneous tuples; exact
family, S-part and coprimality filters remove them without loss.

*Proof.*
* **II-2.** Let w := (a + c)/f′ and N := f_S. Then 4acdw = N(a + c) + w. For a ≤ c this gives w(4ad − 1) ≤ 2N, so
  adw ≤ 2N/3, and c = (Na + w)/(4adw − N) is determined (UCOVER §2.2 (F); the algebra uses only that N is an
  integer).
* **I-4 / II-1.** Write N₁ := a_S, N₂ := b_S and assume a ≤ b. Then 3a′b′cD ≤ ce = a + b ≤ 2N₂b′, so
  a′cD ≤ 2N₂/3. Hence b′ = (N₁a′ + c)/(4a′cD − N₂) is determined. The case a > b is symmetric (UCOVER §2.2 (E/D)).
* **I-1.** We have 4Cwa′d′ = C + w + na′ with w := (C + na′)/f.
  * w is an integer: C(4na′²d′ + 1) − na′f = C + na′ and gcd(C, f) = 1.
  * The equation is symmetric in C and w. If C ≤ w it gives w(4d′C − 2) ≤ n, so d′Cw ≤ n/2; the case w < C gives
    the same bound with the roles swapped.
  * Swapped solutions are kept, since they can give different residues. Example (review): S = {11}, a = 1,
    d = 11, f = 15 has C = 4, w = 1 (class 29 mod 44).
  * Then a′ = (C + w)/(4d′Cw − n) is determined (UCOVER §2.2 (A)).
* **I-2** (argument of the internal review). Put a = Ax, c = By, f = Fz. Write Fz + 1 = 4xyD and a + c = wz. Then
  w(4xyD − 1) = F(Ax + By).
  * If Ax ≤ By: 3wxyD ≤ 2FBy, so 3wxD ≤ 2FB. Then y = (FAx + w)/(4wxD − FB) is determined. The other case is
    symmetric.
* **I-3, II-3** (same algebra; I-3 written, II-3 is I-3 with (c, d) → (a, d)). Put c = Cx, d = Dy, f = Fz and
  G := C²DF.
  * Write 4C²Dx²y + 1 = zs and Fz + 1 = 4xyt.
  * Eliminating z gives s = 4xyu − F for an integer u ≥ 1, and then x(4tyu − G) = u + tF. Hence 4tyu > G and
    (*) 4tyu ≤ G + u + tF.
  * If 4yu > F, then (*) bounds t by (G + u)/(4yu − F). Taking t ≥ 1 in (*) gives u(4y − 1) ≤ G + F, so u and y
    are bounded by G + F.
  * If 4yu ≤ F, then y and u are at most F. Here x > F/(4yu), and x − F/(4yu) = (u + FG/(4yu))/(4tyu − G) → 0
    as t → ∞. An integer x must exceed F/(4yu) by at least η := ⌊F/(4yu)⌋ + 1 − F/(4yu) > 0. This bounds
    4tyu − G by (u + FG/(4yu))/η, hence bounds t.
  * In all cases x is then determined. ∎

This makes an exact multi-prime enumeration possible. It was not implemented here: §4 explains why exactness at
finite levels does not by itself decide the question.

## 4. Why the tail certificates tried here fail (scoped; mostly Assessment)

The brief's route: exact survivors to a finite level, minus an explicit bound on all deeper events, must stay
positive. This section shows that the specific bounds available here do not achieve this. It does not prove that
no certificate exists. Three regimes:
* **(i) Free slices** (all components at p ≤ P free; this is Lemma R's X_P).
  * For fixed P the series Σ_{C P-smooth} μ_{B_P}(C) converges, because it is dominated by P-smooth Dirichlet
    series.
  * As P grows the *raw* series diverges. The II-2 classes −1 mod f alone contribute
    Σ_{13 ≤ f ≤ P, f ≡ 3 (4)} 1/(f−1) → ∞ (PROVED, as in Lemma N). This defeats the unconditioned
    sum-of-all-class-masses bound uniformly in P. It does not exclude other arguments: bounds that tend to 0 with P
    but stay positive would suffice by Lemma R, and so would conditioning on survivors or removing overlaps.
  * Numerically even P = 13 is out of reach of the raw bound. At the computed level the survivors have relative mass
    8.5·10⁻⁴ (§5).
    * The 2-adic layers of classes at 2-levels 9–12 (with 3⁴5²7²11²13²) have summed K-relative cylinder masses
      0.42, 0.26, 0.15, 0.09 when family-tagged duplicates are counted, and 0.35, 0.21, 0.13, 0.08 for distinct
      (m, r).
    * Both are upper bounds that overcount overlaps. They show that the raw bound fails; they are not lower bounds
      on the true loss. Level 9 is already inside the §5 level.
    * The I-1 classes dominate, with a ratio of about 0.6 per level.
  * A crude divisor bound τ(4a²d+1) ≤ C(a²d)^{1/4} bounds the I-1 tail by a constant times Σ a^{−1/2}d^{−3/4}
    over smooth a, d (an upper bound, not an asymptotic).
  * Assessment, about these estimates only: to make the raw bound work one would need exact enumeration to 2-levels
    around 25–30 and comparable depths at 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, with factorisations of numbers 4a²d+1 of 60+ digits.
* **(ii) Rigid slices** (1 elsewhere, free at S ∋ 11, 13; UCOVER's two-prime problem).
  * The I-1 events of Lemma M sit on ΣII at n = a_S²d_S, up to N² for cylinder level N.
  * Upper-bound diagnosis: with N = a_S d_S and n = a_S²d_S, the trivial count ≍ √n against cylinder measure 1/N
    gives √n/N = 1/√d_S. This does not decay as a_S grows with d_S fixed.
    * So a union-bound tail along this route would follow from P_II(n) ≤ C n^θ with θ < 1/2, explicitly. That is ET's 2/5,
      which they prove with divisor-bound losses.
    * This is a sufficient condition for the counting route; it is not proved necessary. Nor does it show that the
      true event count is ≍ N; heuristically it is polylogarithmic.
  * Assessment (constants as in UCOVER §4): such bounds become useful only for N ≳ 10³⁰–10⁵⁰, while exact
    enumeration on ΣII at n = N² costs ≍ N.
  * This is the same missing ingredient as UCOVER O1; ELL0 attacks it for large ℓ.
* **(iii) Rational slices.** By §3 the tested ones with q ≠ 1 are covered (CERTIFIED for those (q, T)), so they give
  no escape from (ii) in the cases tested.

**Scoped summary.**
* PROVED: U is Haar-null (Lemma N). The raw class-measure series on free slices diverges as P → ∞.
* CERTIFIED: the tested rational slices with q ≠ 1 are covered.
* Assessment: the known way to finish along the measure route is an explicit ΣII count with exponent < 1/2 at
  n = 11^a 13^b (UCOVER O1). Other routes (an explicit witness such as π°, or adaptive König arguments) are not
  excluded.

## 8. Open problems
* **O1.** Is π° ∈ U? This is a fixed-profile Diophantine question: seven families of quadratic equations with S-unit
  side conditions. It is the most concrete form of the K-question.
* **O2.** Is X_P ≠ ∅ uniform in P? One proposed route is a König argument with a per-prime extension lemma: every point of
  X_{<q} that is "generic" in some sense extends at q.
* **O3.** Does U ∩ K̄ contain a profile that is 1 at all but finitely many primes? π° would be one. If so, are
  such profiles dense in U ∩ K̄?

    dense in Ẑ^×.)
* **O4.** The rational-slice CONJECTURE of §3: Σ(q, T) is finitely covered for every finite T and every rational
  T-unit q ≠ 1.
