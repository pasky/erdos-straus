**Statement (internal proof; no assertion of independence from PA).** Given a primitive quadratic-residue class `r mod M` (a square in the unit group), there is a countable discretely ordered **principal ideal domain** `R` satisfying open induction with a positive infinite prime `p≡r mod M` for which there are no positive `x,y,z` with `4xyz=p(xy+yz+zx)`. “PID” here is external: *every* ideal is principal, not merely every finitely generated or coded ideal. This is not a standard-prime counterexample. The existence of nonstandard PID models of open induction is known (S. T. Smith, J. Algebra 159 (1993), Thm 10.7; the prior construction and its row adjunctions are discussed in `LOGIC_PID_AUDIT.md`); the required additional property here is failure of the specified ES sentence, not existence of a nonstandard PID on its own.

The construction dispenses with a fixed integral landing point. The integrality conditions are adelic, and any hypothetical triple is specialized at a **different ordinary odd square** chosen *after* all of its coordinates and coprimality certificates are known. This allows residues other than 1 and avoids the nonfactorial kernel of a ring retraction to ℤ.

## 1. A tower of line fibrations

Start with `A₀=ℚ[P]` ordered with `P` infinite positive. Build a countable union of smooth factorial finitely generated ℚ-algebras, all having only ℚ as their elements bounded by a standard integer. Use the polynomial approximation operation from `Z73_OPEN_BEZOUT.md` to approximate each real algebraic target of each stage within bounded distance. This adds a single free variable. For every pair at a finite stage which is not both zero, divide out its current gcd and for the remaining coprime nonzero `u,v` adjoin

```
A' = A[U,V]/(uU+vV−1).
```

A pair already generating the unit ideal needs no change. There is **no landing to preserve**, so no deformation is needed. As proved in `Z73_OPEN_BEZOUT.md`, this is an affine-line torsor over the complement of `u=v=0`, trivial on each of the charts `D(u),D(v)`. The center has codimension ≥2 by factoriality. The algebra is smooth and factorial, by inverting `u` and using Nagata's criterion. Every old prime `s` stays nonunit prime: the quotient mod `s` is the same row construction on the domain `A/s`, where at least one row entry is nonzero. Its nonempty polynomial charts are domains with dense common open if both are nonempty. The order extends by a free coordinate larger than the old field; `A'∩Frac(A)=A` by the intersection of the two localizations (with the trivial unit cases included). These facts keep finite elements constant. Polynomial stages likewise preserve old primes. Use fair diagonal scheduling, taking gcds at service time.

In `A=⋃A_j`, every nonzero nonunit therefore factors into finitely many permanent primes. Every pair generates a principal ideal, by construction; the units are `ℚ*`. Its field `F` is purely transcendental over ℚ, with a countable basis including `P`: choose one free parameter at every nontrivial stage (not necessarily both row coordinates). Every member of `F^rc` has bounded distance to `A`.

## 2. Adelic integrality, floors and external factoriality

Choose, at each ordinary prime `ℓ`, a tuple algebraically independent over **ℚ** in `ℤ_ℓ` indexed by the countable basis, with the `P` coordinate a square unit, in class `r` at all primes of `M`. Require that for each nonzero integer polynomial `f` in the basis, `f` evaluates to a unit modulo all sufficiently large `ℓ` (the cutoff is allowed to depend on `f`). To see simultaneous existence, enumerate those polynomials. For a fixed initial block involving `k` variables, including `P`, its zeros modulo `ℓ` occupy at most `(sum of degrees)ℓ^(k−1)` points once reductions are nonzero (the thresholds below are also taken larger than every prime dividing the content of a polynomial in the block, so all reductions are nonzero; z74 Opus xreview fix). For odd `ℓ` the available nonzero-square residues for `P` give `(ℓ−1)ℓ^(k−1)/2` points. Outside successively larger thresholds avoid successively longer blocks. At the finitely many bad primes prescribe square units in the required congruence classes instead; at 2 use a small enough cylinder of square units. In each chosen product of residue cylinders choose individual coordinates algebraically independent (algebraic closures of countable fields remain countable). This gives field embeddings `φ_ℓ:F→ℚ_ℓ`. Every fixed nonzero `g∈F` has finite valuation everywhere and valuation zero almost everywhere, by writing it as a quotient of polynomials.

Let `R={g∈A : φ_ℓ(g)∈ℤ_ℓ for every ℓ}`. Its finite members are exactly ℤ, hence it is discretely ordered. For each `g∈A` some rational `b` makes `g+b∈R` (match the finitely many local fractional parts by CRT). This shifts `g` by only a bounded constant. Also any `g∈A` has a nonzero standard integer multiple in `R` by clearing its finitely many negative valuations. Thus `Frac(R)=F`, and for any `α∈F^rc`, approximation in `A` gives approximation in `R` within a standard bound. A standard integral adjustment gives an actual floor. Hence `R` satisfies open induction.

Scale any prime of `A` by a nonzero rational so that its valuation is 0 at *all* `ℓ`, using finite valuation support. It is then prime in `R`: dividing by it does not change local integrality, and it is a nonunit even in `A`. Ordinary primes `ℓ` are prime in `R` since `R/ℓR=𝔽_ℓ` by evaluation (the other completions impose no new restriction on division by `ℓ`). Factoring any nonzero `g∈R` in `A` using the normalized primes leaves a rational scalar integral at every `ℓ`, hence in ℤ. Thus `R` is externally factorial, and the prime `P` already has the required normalization and class.

To check Bézout, divide out the `R`-gcd of a nonzero pair and denote the result by `a,b`. They are coprime in `A` and have `min(v_ℓ a,v_ℓ b)=0` at every `ℓ`. Pick `u,v∈A` with `ua+vb=1`. The coefficients are integral except at finitely many `ℓ`. Locally the unit among `a,b` gives an integral pair of coefficients; the difference from `(u,v)` is `(bτ,−aτ)` over `ℚ_ℓ`. Choose a **single** `τ∈ℚ` sufficiently close to each of these local corrections and integral elsewhere, by CRT with denominators. Then `u+bτ,v−aτ` lie in `R`. Atomic Bézout implies PID: starting at any nonzero member of an ideal, repeatedly taking the gcd with a member not divisible by the current gcd strictly decreases its finite factor length, and each new gcd still belongs to the ideal. This terminates with a generator.

## 3. The specialization lemma needed here

Pull each finite tower back by `P=t²`. Starting at `ℚ[t]`, it is still a tower of affine lines and row operations away from algebraic sets of codimension ≥2: the maps of the pulled-back stages to the original stages are finite flat (base change of the monic map `P=t²`), so inverse images of codimension-two centers still have codimension ≥2. Inductively smoothness and geometric integrality follow from the line charts over their geometrically integral bases. On the region `t>0` the base real line is connected. At each stage deleting the codimension-two center leaves it semialgebraically connected, and the real row bundle there is trivial via `(u,v)/(u²+v²)` and `(−v,u)`. This remains true over any real closed extension. In particular an identity between three positive reciprocals at the ordered generic point (take `t=+√P`) stays positive at every real point of the square pullback with `t>0`: the positivity sector is open and closed since every coordinate is ≥`t²/4` wherever all are positive.

Here is the arithmetic input precisely. For such a *fixed finite* rational tower, an integral model can be chosen away from finitely many primes. On it one has strong approximation off the real place even after removal of an additional closed subset `W` of codimension ≥2, with the extra requirement `t>H` for any fixed real `H`. In the application below we only require p-adic approximation at a finite set `S` and integrality outside it; we may enlarge `S` at the start since at each added prime we have a prescribed local point. One also may require avoidance of a specified proper closed set at the rational point (impose this at an extra finite place). **This does not permit prescribing a small real interval as well as integrality everywhere.**

*Fibration proof.* At level zero the line minus a codimension-two subset is just the line. Integers with suitable fixed denominators at `S` in prescribed congruence classes can be made arbitrarily positive; this is CRT, not density of ℤ in ℝ. For a line or row-bundle step `π:Y→X`, let `C` be the missing base center and consider the additional bad set `W` in `Y`. Its image closure `D` in `X` is proper, since `dim W≤dim X−1`. In a trivializing line chart the condition that the *entire geometric fibre* lie in `W` is the vanishing of all coefficients of the finitely many defining equations in the fibre variable. The resulting locus in `X\C` is closed there of codimension ≥2 by fibre dimension. Its closure together with `C` is thus a codimension-≥2 set `Z` in `X`. Spread these charts, coefficient tests, defining equations for `D`, and inclusions over `ℤ[1/S₀]`; enlarge `S₀` so they hold uniformly in every remaining characteristic (finite polynomial relations, clearing denominators, generic flatness if necessary). In particular outside `Z` at most a fixed number `d` of points in an affine fibre are excluded by its nonidentically-zero equations. Include primes ≤`d` in the exceptional set.

Use induction to get `x∈X(ℚ)` avoiding `Z` integrally outside the chosen places, `t>H`, and `x∉D` rationally. Approximate projected local points as closely as necessary first: they can be perturbed in their open sets to avoid the proper sets `D,Z` over the local fields, since the varieties are smooth and the projections locally trivial. Any old integral-open constraints at added places are feasible whenever their specified open set was nonempty; otherwise the global statement makes no approximation claim. Outside the chosen places only finitely many reductions of `x` belong to `D` (a nonzero value of a defining polynomial). At each such prime the line fibre has an allowed residual point, since the field has more than `d` elements. (Use the row chart in which `u(x)` resp. `v(x)` is an `ℓ`-unit, which exists as `x` avoids the center on reduction; then the fibre parameter is an affine bijection mod `ℓ` onto that chart's line coordinate, to which the degree bound `d` refers. z74 Opus xreview fix) Everywhere else `W` is automatically absent. Finally the line over the `S`-integral point `x` has an `S`-integral section: for a row, `u(x),v(x)` generate the unit ideal of `ℤ[1/S]` by avoidance of the center, so take Bézout coefficients. Its solutions form a line with direction `(−v(x),u(x))` which is primitive locally. Choose the line parameter by ordinary CRT/weak approximation, integral at primes outside `S`, meeting the finitely many lifts just specified and the prescribed opens at `S`. It does not change `t`. This proves the induction. When enlarging `S` during this argument, keep existing local requirements as open constraints, including old good integral reductions. All finite enlargements for chart/degree data of the inductive proof can equivalently be made in advance. No simultaneous primality conjecture is involved.

## 4. Contradiction at an ordinary square

Suppose positive `x,y,z∈R` solve ES at `P`. Since `P` is prime it divides at least one coordinate, by the cleared equation and `P∤4`. It cannot divide all three: cancelling would give a representation of 4 by three positive unit fractions in a discrete ring. Include in a common finite stage every coordinate, every quotient by `P` where it divides, and Bézout coefficients for `(P,w)=1` for every remaining coordinate `w`. All these have integral local evaluations by construction. The finite-stage rational functions mentioned are regular at that stage, including any necessary polynomial expressions for the identities.

On the base change `P=t²`, at *every* ordinary prime `ℓ` the evaluations `φ_ℓ` and a unit square root of `φ_ℓ(P)` give compatible local points. At all but finitely many primes the stage generators evaluate integrally; spread all marked regular functions integrally outside that finite set. At the remaining primes take open neighborhoods on which the marked elements remain integral, and `t` is integral (an odd unit at 2). By the specialization lemma there is a rational point attaining these conditions, with `t>1`. Thus `t` is a positive odd integer. At this point all marked elements are integers and the three denominators are positive by real connectedness. The transported quotients give `t²|w` at each divisible coordinate, and the transported integer Bézout identities give `gcd(t²,w)=1` at each other coordinate. This is precisely a strict Type I or Type II representation of `4/t²`, impossible for an odd square (Elsholtz–Tao, Prop. 1.6). Here is a direct check. Write `n=t²`. In Type I name the denominators `nw,X,Y`; `n|4w−1` since `(4w−1)XY=nw(X+Y)` and `gcd(XY,n)=1`, so put `d=(4w−1)/n`. In Type II name them `w,nX,nY` and put `d=4w−n`. In both cases `1/X+1/Y=d/w`, `d>0`, `d≡3 mod 4`, `gcd(w,d)=1`, and `gcd(w,n)=1` (in Type I this follows from `nd=4w−1`). If an odd prime `l|w`, then `−d` is a unit square modulo `l`: it is `1/n` or `n` in the two cases. Reciprocity gives the Jacobi symbol `(l/d)=(−d/l)=1`. When `w` is even, `d≡7 mod 8` since `n≡1 mod 8`, so `(2/d)=1` too. Every positive divisor of `w²` therefore has symbol +1. Yet `dX−w>0` is such a divisor by `(dX−w)(dY−w)=w²`, and it has symbol `(−w/d)=−1`. The choice of rational point need not preserve primality of `P`; the point is exactly that the *divisibility witnesses* survive even though `P` becomes a square. ∎

**Additional transfer (pure ring language).** The same model satisfies every universal ring sentence true in ℤ. For if it realized a counterexample tuple, choose the corresponding conjunction of polynomial equalities and inequations true on that tuple. At its finite stage the equalities are identities, and all the inequations hold on a nonempty Zariski open. This remains a nonempty open on the integral square pullback (the base change is faithfully flat). Include the coordinates' integrality at the bad primes and require the open condition by local approximation at an extra place. The specialization in §3 gives an ordinary integer tuple witnessing the same counterexample, a contradiction. This does not transfer arbitrary ordered inequalities: `t>H` and the positivity of reciprocal sectors are special, not a proof of arbitrary real approximation.

**Scope.** Positive solutions at an ordinary prime are not ruled out. The counterexample in this ring is nonstandard and the ring is not a model of bounded induction (its 2-primary elements are exactly the standard powers of 2, so Δ₀ least-number fails below P; `LOGIC_PID_AUDIT.md`, z74 Opus xreview fix). Finite standard factors have no power to prove ES here even when all ideals are principal. The methods use Bézout certificates and square pullbacks, not four-square representability or full exponentiation. No inference to IE₁, IΔ₀, PA or ZFC, and no claim that this model already satisfies the norm or collection refinements of the different landing construction.
