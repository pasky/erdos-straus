Haag–Kertzer–Rickards–Stange, arXiv:2307.02749v3, Theorems 1.3 and 2.4 / Definition 2.2:
Theorem 1.3: infinitely many primitive Apollonian packings have Ω(√N) missing curvatures up to N. Theorem 2.4 table includes type (6,1,−1): quadratic families n²,2n²,3n²,6n²; type (6,1,1,−1): quartic families n⁴,4n⁴,9n⁴,36n⁴. Definition 2.2 requires infinitely many members admissible modulo 24. Proof mechanism, §1.3, pp. 3–4: mod n the tangent-circle form degenerates to Ax²; the Kronecker symbol is constant over invertible values. Quadratic reciprocity propagates χ₂ between tangent circles, hence through the packing. Quartic invariant is built from a fractional ideal over Z[i] and quartic reciprocity (§1.3, §5).

Rickards–Stange, arXiv:2401.01860v3, Theorem 2.5:
For nonnegative coprime x,y, any of the conditions (i) y odd and (x/y)=−1; (ii) (x,y)=(1,0) mod 4 and (x/y)=−1; (iii) (x,y)=(3,0) mod 4 and (x/y)=−(−1/y), implies neither numerator nor denominator of the Ψ-orbit of (x,y) is a square. Proposition 2.2: Ψ consists of Γ₁(4)≥0 matrices with (a/b)=1. Theorem 2.8: the missing positive numerators in Ψ(2/3) are exactly all squares plus {3,6,7,10,12,15,18,19,27,31,34,55,63,99,115}.

Shkredov, arXiv:2603.14116v2, Corollary 1 and Theorem 8:
Corollary 1: an absolute M≥2 works for every prime or square-free denominator p. Theorem 8: for all sufficiently large absolute M and any q, at least q^(2w_M−1−o(1)) coprime numerators have all partial quotients ≤100M. Remark 9 says the general-q case follows after Zhang's general-modulus growth results and states that Corollary 1 is valid for any q.

Zhang, arXiv:2605.02518v2, Theorem 1.2:
“Zaremba’s conjecture holds.” Conjecture 1.1 defines this as every natural q having a reduced a/q whose partial quotients are bounded by an absolute M. The text says method yields a large absolute M (not M=5); Remark 1.4 explains q-dependent generating sets defeat the fixed-set Bourgain–Varju spectral gap available there.

Chen, arXiv:2011.12940, Theorem 1.2.5 (PDF p. 7):
“For every p ≥ 3, every Out⁺(Π)-orbit on Epi(Π, SL₂(F_p))_{−2}/GL₂(F_p) has cardinality divisible by p. In particular, every Γ-orbit on X*(p) has cardinality divisible by p.” Together with BGS Theorem 1.2.3(b), whose complement bound is ≤p^ε, this forces one orbit for sufficiently large p; Chen Theorem 1.2.6 states finite effective exceptional set.
