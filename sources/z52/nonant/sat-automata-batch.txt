Keller: Brakensiek, Heule, Mackey, Narváez, “The Resolution of Keller’s Conjecture,” arXiv:1910.03740v5 (17 Apr 2023), https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03740. Abstract: in dimension 7, counterexample iff one of three finite graphs has a 128-clique; encode as propositional formulas and use SAT plus symmetry breaking to show no clique. Therefore every unit-cube tiling of R^7 has a face-sharing pair; verified R^8 counterexample settles conjecture. Journal of Automated Reasoning 66 (2022), 277–300, DOI 10.1007/s10817-022-09623-5 (source reference).

EDP: Konev & Lisitsa, “A SAT Attack on the Erdos Discrepancy Conjecture,” arXiv:1402.2184v2 (17 Feb 2014), https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.2184. Abstract: SAT gives sequence length 1160 with discrepancy 2 and a proof no length-1161 sequence has discrepancy 2, establishing C=2 finite boundary; partial results for C=3.

Boolean Pythagorean triples: Heule, Kullmann, Marek, “Solving and Verifying the Boolean Pythagorean Triples Problem via Cube-and-Conquer,” arXiv:1605.00723, https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.00723 (accessed; abstract/source result searched, see source record). Hybrid cube-and-conquer SAT encodes 2-colorings and proves no coloring through 7824; 7825 is the first forced monochromatic Pythagorean triple. (Use formal paper result, not claim about infinite arithmetic.)

Packing: Subercaseaux & Heule, “The Packing Chromatic Number of the Infinite Square Grid is 15,” arXiv:2301.09757v2 (12 Jun 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09757. Abstract: proves exact number 15 using novel propositional encoding and symmetry breaking; proof includes unsatisfiability certificate. This is an infinite graph coloring theorem but finite-state/local-constraint compactness converts it to finite search.

Empty hexagon: Heule & Scheucher, “Happy Ending: An Empty Hexagon in Every Set of 30 Points,” Springer chapter, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-57246-3_5, https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-57246-3_5 (opened 2026-10-30). Theorem 1: every 30-point planar set in general position has empty convex hexagon. SAT encoding uses triple orientations/hole variables, compact O(n^4) encoding, symmetry breaking and 312,418 cubes; 17,300 CPU-hours; LRAT proof validated by verified cakeLPR checker. Formal verification paper by Subercaseaux, arXiv:2403.17370, opened same date, says Lean formalization verifies the result/encoding.

Ramsey certificates: Li, Bright et al. (arXiv record authors see source), “Verified Certificates via SAT and Computer Algebra Systems for the Ramsey R(3,8) and R(3,9) Problems,” arXiv:2502.06055v2 (7 Jul 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06055. Abstract: MathCheck integrates SAT and CAS and cube-and-conquer; produces first independently verifiable certificates for R(3,8)=28 and R(3,9)=36; SAT+CAS outperforms SAT-only. IJCAI 2025 DOI 10.24963/ijcai.2025/292.

Automata palindrome papers: Cilleruelo, Luca, Baxter, “Every positive integer is a sum of three palindromes,” arXiv:1602.06208 (2016), https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06208; result for bases g≥5 via constructive digit arguments. Rajasekaran, Shallit, Smith, “Sums of Palindromes: an Approach via Automata,” arXiv:1706.10206v3 (30 Aug 2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10206. Abstract says decision procedure based on automata settles bases 2,3,4: every natural number sum of at most four palindromes in base 2 (optimal), similar results in bases 3,4; thus closes all bases.

Walnut: Shallit/Mousavi, Walnut automatic theorem-prover documentation, https://github.com/hamousavi/Walnut (project page opened/looked up 2026-10-30; primary citation to verify further). Walnut decides first-order Presburger-style statements about automatic sequences; relevant only when all relations/predicates are automatic/Presburger-definable.
