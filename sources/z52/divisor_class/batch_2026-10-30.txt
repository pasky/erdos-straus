# Primary-source excerpts opened 2026-10-30 (batch)

## Ford, “The distribution of integers with a divisor in a given interval” (arXiv:math.NT/0401223)

Abstract: determines order of magnitude of H(x,y,z), integers n≤x with a divisor in (y,z], for all x,y,z; studies H_r, exactly r divisors. For r=1 establishes order for z≤x^(1/2−ε); for r≥2 in stated y,z range.

Definitions/exact special case: H(x,y,z)=#{n≤x: τ(n;y,z)>0}. The piecewise order-of-magnitude formula for H is Ford’s Theorem 1 (restated as Theorem 1.1 in Koukoulopoulos’s paper; do not confuse it with Ford’s Theorem T1, which is Tenenbaum’s earlier result). For x>100000 and 100≤y≤z−1≤x, if y≤√x then H(x,y,z)/x is comparable to η for z≤z0(y), to β/[max(1,−ξ)(log y)^G(β)] for z0(y)≤z≤2y, to u^δ(log(2/u))^(−3/2) for 2y≤z≤y², and to 1 for z≥y²; if y>√x, H(x,y,z) is comparable to H(x,x/z,x/y) when x/y≥x/z+1, otherwise ηx. This is an ambient-integer count, not a prescribed-N assertion.

## Koukoulopoulos, “Divisors of shifted primes,” arXiv:0905.0163v3

Theorem 1.7: Fix nonzero integer s and B≥2. For x sufficiently large and 3≤y+1≤z≤x, assuming there is a d in (y,z] coprime to s and z≥y+y/(log y)^B, one has
H(x,y,z;P_s) ≍_{s,B} H(x,y,z)/log x,
where P_s={p+s:p prime} and H counts integers n≤x possessing a divisor in (y,z]. This is a population average over shifted primes up to x, not a pointwise assertion for a specified p+s or specified divisor class.

Theorem 1.6 (short intervals): under corresponding hypotheses y≤√x, x(log x)^(−B)≤Δ≤x, and z≥y+y/(log y)^B, the number of shifted primes p+s in (x−Δ,x] having such a divisor is ≫_{s,B} (Δ/x)H(x,y,z)/log x.

## Pollack, “Bounds for the first several prime character nonresidues,” arXiv:1508.05035v2

Abstract/Theorem 1.1: For every ε>0, constants m0(ε), κ(ε)>0 exist such that for each integer m>m0 and each nontrivial Dirichlet character χ mod m, there are more than m^κ primes ℓ≤m^(1/(4√e)+ε) with χ(ℓ)∉{0,1}. Character-wise pointwise statement, but scale is a power of m, not logarithmic-square, and it says nothing about divisors of one linked integer.

## Harman–Shparlinski, “Products of Small Integers in Residue Classes and Additive Properties of Fermat Quotients,” arXiv:1408.4515v2

Theorem 1: For every ε>0 and sufficiently large cube-free q, for every a coprime to q, there exist n1,…,n14≤q^(1/(4√e)+ε) with n1⋯n14≡a (mod q). Theorem 2: for a multiplicative subgroup G⊂(Z/qZ)^* of order t≫q^(1/2), there are n1,…,n9≤q^(1/(4√e)+ε) and u∈G with n1⋯n9≡au (mod q). These are pointwise residue-product coverage results for freely chosen small factors; they do not constrain factors to divide a prescribed N, nor imply the particular-divisor existence B2 requires. The authors state in §1 that a related almost-all-a theorem allows seven factors, but that does not replace the all-class theorem.

## Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan, “Conditional bounds for the least quadratic non-residue and related problems,” arXiv:1309.3595

Corollary 1.1: Assuming GRH, for prime q≥5 the least quadratic nonresidue modulo q is below (log q)^2. Because the least integer quadratic nonresidue is necessarily prime, this supplies a prime q_NR with (q_NR/p)=−1 and q_NR<(log p)^2, directly within the campaign's conditional forcing scale 2(log p)^2. It is a GRH-conditional character statement, not an unconditional divisor-in-class theorem. The introduction also attributes to Bach the explicit GRH bound: least prime outside H is <2(log q)^2 (or <3(log q)^2 if required not to divide the modulus); for quadratic residues the displayed LLS corollary is the cleaner directly applicable prime-modulus statement.

## Kerr–Shparlinski–Yau, “A refinement of the Burgess bound for character sums” (arXiv:1711.10582v2)

Theorem 2.1: Let `q` be a prime, `r≥2` an integer, and `M,N` integers with `N≤q^(1/2+1/(4r))`. For any nonprincipal multiplicative character `χ mod q`, the sum over `M<n≤M+N` is `≪ N^(1−1/r)q^((r+1)/(4r²))(log q)^(1/(4r))`, with an absolute implied constant. Short-character-sum estimate; it does not itself state a new least-nonresidue or prescribed-divisor result.

## Fromm–Goldmakher, “Improving the Burgess bound via Pólya–Vinogradov” (arXiv:1706.03002v1)

Theorem A: Suppose that for every even primitive quadratic character `χ mod q`, `Sχ(t)=o(√q log q)` uniformly in `t`. Then for every `ε>0`, odd primitive quadratic characters `ξ mod p` satisfy `Sξ(t)=o(t)` for `t≫ε p^ε`. This is a conditional implication between character-sum estimates.

## Bober–Goldmakher, “Pólya–Vinogradov and the least quadratic nonresidue” (arXiv:1311.7556v2)

Theorem 1: For each `g≥1`, under the paper’s hypothesis that every primitive even character `χ mod q` of order `g` satisfies `M(χ)≤√q log(q)/f(q)` for an eventually nondecreasing `f`, the conclusion is stated for primitive odd characters `ξ mod k` of order `g` with the additional hypothesis `3∤k`: `n_ξ≪exp(C_g log(k)/f(k))`, with the paper specifying the constant. Conditional transfer result, not an unconditional new bound.

## Additional primary-source excerpts opened 2026-10-30

### Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan, arXiv:1309.3595v3, §§1.1–1.2

Theorem 1.2: assuming GRH, for subgroup index `h`, the least prime outside `H` is `<(α(h)+o(1))(log q)^2`, with `α(2)=0.8`, `α(3)=0.7`, and `α(h)=0.66` for `h>3`. Theorem 1.4: assuming GRH, `q≥20000`, and subgroup index `h>1`, the least prime in a specified coset `aH` is either `≤10^9` or `≤((h−1)log q+3(h+1)+(5/2)(log log q)^2)^2`. The introduction reports Bach’s earlier least-prime-outside-subgroup bounds `<2(log q)^2`, `<3(log q)^2` if excluding primes dividing `q`, and `(1+o(1))(log q)^2` asymptotically. It reports Bach–Sorenson progression bounds `(1+o(1))(φ(q)log q)^2` and `2(q log q)^2`, and Wedeniwski’s quadratic-subgroup explicit bound `(3/2)(log q)^2−(44/5)log q+13`. These three older-source statements are reports/citations in LLS, not direct verification of the cited papers.

### Klurman–Shparlinski–Teräväinen, arXiv:2609.25393v1 (2026-09-21), Theorem 1.1

“For every `Q≥3`, there exists a set `P` ... with `|P|≤(log Q)^(1+ε)` ... such that for all but `O_{ε,A}(Q(log Q)^(−A))` positive integers `q≤Q`” the subset products equal `(Z/qZ)^×`; every prime in the selected set is at most `(log Q)^(C*(A+1))`. The theorem is explicitly almost-all-moduli, not a pointwise result for every prime modulus.

### Cédric Pilatte, arXiv:2404.16450v3, Corollary 1.4 and §1.4

Corollary 1.4: for fixed `N>2`, `d=⌈√log N⌉`, and primes `b_i≤X=d^(1000d)` sampled independently and uniformly from the primes not dividing `N`, with high probability every element of `⟨b_1,…,b_d⟩` has a representation as a product `∏b_i^(e_i) mod N` with `|e_i|≤e^(O(d))`. Section 1.4 explicitly says this subgroup may remain inside the squares; it does not assert that these primes generate the full unit group. Theorem 3.18 is the short-basis result. Source opened at https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16450v3, including the full text.

### Daniel J. Bernstein, author PDF (2008-05-02), §6, Theorem 6.4

The hypothesis is `v≥n^(1/4)≥2^75` and `(v,n)=1`. the number of divisors of `n` in `(u+vZ)∩[1,n^(1/2)]` is at most `33ℓ^(3/2)+4.5ℓ+10ℓ^(1/2)+2 (where ℓ=log_2(2n))`. It says this can be computed by covering the range with `O((log n)^(1/2))` arithmetic progressions/lattices. In the history paragraph, Bernstein reports CHGN’s `v≥n^(1/4+ε)` result for sufficiently large `n` depending on `ε`, with total lattice rank `O(ε^(−3/2))`, and Lenstra’s `v≥n^(1/4)` result with total rank `O((log n)^2)`; these are secondary reports in Bernstein’s paper. Publication: *Algorithmic Number Theory: Lattices, Number Fields, Curves and Cryptography*, CUP (2008), 421–446.
