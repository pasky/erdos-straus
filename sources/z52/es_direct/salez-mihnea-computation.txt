Serge E. Salez, “The Erdős-Straus conjecture: New modular equations and checking up to N=10^17,” arXiv:1406.6307v1, 2014-06-24 (PDF p.1). Abstract: seven modular equations yield an optimized sieve; C++ program checks through 10^17 in ~16 hours (typical computer). §2.4, Corollary 1: for odd prime p, 4/p is 3-Egyptian iff one of seven modular equations holds. §3 defines filters S_m as residue classes certified by modular equations. §4.2: sieve up to N=10^17 with odd m<5000, filters ordered by efficiency; all non-squares in the remaining residue classes receive a modular certificate. §4.3 exact headline counts: “we check 16 512 783 482 880 integers including 51 732 427 squares.” Table p.11 (PDF page numbering) for CRT candidate progressions:

No table of largest/hardest primes or denominator/certificate-size statistics appears in the PDF. The computational certificate is a modulus m and membership of n mod m in a filter S_m; it is not a small-denominator representation-size study.

Spiridon Mihnea and Bogdan C. Dumitru, “Further verification and empirical evidence for the Erdős-Straus conjecture,” arXiv:2509.00128v1, 2025-08-29 (PDF p.1). §2.1 extends Salez filters with S_29, obtains |R_8|=2,101,514 residues modulo G_8=25,878,772,920, and checks batches through k=38,641,709 (omitting first 3,864,170 batches already covered by earlier bound); each batch uses 140,000 prime filters generated by Salez's algorithm, C++/GMP, parallel batching; runtime about two weeks on a medium setup. §2.2 says nonfiltered residual integers were saved and none were prime. §3 computes f(p) with Bradford's finite x-search range and divisor identities, for the first N=66,737 hard-class primes p≤3.5×10^7. Exact aggregate (PDF p.3): T=29,860,049,601,808 divisors checked; S=18,601,583 satisfying at least one identity and producing a solution; S1=12,763,383 Type I; S2=5,838,200 Type II. Figure 1 is a semi-log scatter plot of f(P_i) by type. The paper does not identify a named “hardest prime,” tabulate extrema of f(p), or report certificate lengths/minimal denominators; its hardest-prime set is defined by six Mordell classes mod 840. Thus no published data in this paper supports a denominator-size or per-prime certificate-size inference.
