Bhargava–Hanke, Theorem 1 (290-Theorem): “If a positive-definite quadratic form with integer coefficients represents the twenty-nine integers” 1,2,3,5,6,7,10,13,14,15,17,19,21,22,23,26,29,30,31,34,35,37,42,58,93,110,145,203,290, “then it represents all positive integers.” Theorem 2 says each is critical (there is a positive definite form missing only that one). Intro states they prove Conway’s conjectured 290 criterion.

Analytic input: §4.2 explains Tartakowsky (dimension ≥5) and Kloosterman (quaternary) give sufficiently-large locally represented integers, but general effective bounds are too large. They use modular forms: theta series split Eisenstein + cusp; effective lower Eisenstein/local-density bounds and upper cusp-coefficient bounds certify representability beyond a bound. §4.3 performs finite eligible-number checks; proof also uses arithmetic escalation and computations. References list Duke–Schulze-Pillot, Invent. Math. 99 (1990), 49–57. For Fifteen theorem: theorem states positive-definite classical integral-matrix form is universal if represents 1 through 15; source Bhargava 2000.
