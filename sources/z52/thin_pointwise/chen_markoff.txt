In [BGS16b, BGS16a], Bourgain, Gamburd, and Sarnak studied the action of Γ on X∗ (p) (for p prime) and
conjectured that an analogue of Markoff’s result should also hold for its Fp -points:
Conjecture 1.2.2 (Bourgain, Gamburd, Sarnak [BGS16b]). For all primes p, Γ acts transitively on X∗ (p).
In other words, they conjecture that X(Fp ) is the union of at most two Γ-orbits: the singleton orbit {(0, 0, 0)},
and X∗ (p) (this is empty if and only if p = 3). Since X(Z) contains the solutions {(0, 0, 0), (3, 3, 3)} and
X(F3 ) = {(0, 0, 0)}, a positive solution to this conjecture would imply that for any prime p, the reduction mod p
map X(Z) → X(Fp ) is surjective, in which case, following [BGS16b], we say that X satisfies strong approximation
at all primes p.
To the author’s knowledge this conjecture was first posed by Baragar in his 1991 PhD thesis [Bar91, §V.3]. Since
the conjugation action of GL2 (Fp ) on SL2 (Fp ) generates the full automorphism group Aut(SL2 (Fp )), it follows
that this is also a special case of McCullough and Wanderley’s Conjecture 1.1.4 with (q, t) = (p, −2). However,
the first major progress on Conjecture 1.2.2 did not come until the work of Bourgain, Gamburd, and Sarnak in
2016 [BGS16a], where they were able to prove
