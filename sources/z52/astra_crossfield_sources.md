# Cross-field source notes (accessed 2026-10-30)

**Fact summary (source-checked):** Cilleruelo–Luca–Baxter prove every positive integer is a sum of three base-`g` palindromes for each base `g ≥ 5`; Croot proves a monochromatic reciprocal-sum identity inside a finite interval; Huang guarantees a high-degree vertex in every induced subgraph on more than half the hypercube vertices; Schur and van der Waerden guarantee finite monochromatic configurations; Hasse–Minkowski and Meyer concern rational isotropy; the Fifteen and 290 theorems are universal-form finite tests with different integrality conventions; Duke–Schulze-Pillot is an asymptotic ternary-form representation/equidistribution source, but its original full theorem text was not extractable in this access. “Existence” here is not a count of witnesses unless the source explicitly gives one.

## Source-accessed statements

1. **Every positive integer as three palindromes.** Javier Cilleruelo, Florian Luca, Lewis Baxter, “Every positive integer is a sum of three palindromes,” arXiv:1602.06208, v2 (revised 2017-06-17; initially submitted 2016-02-19). Abstract theorem: for every integer base `g ≥ 5`, every positive integer is a sum of three base-`g` palindromes. The source guarantees a decomposition, not a lower bound on the number of decompositions. [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06208), accessed 2026-10-30. Excerpt: “For integer g≥5, we prove that any positive integer can be written as a sum of three palindromes in base g.”

2. **Croot / Erdős–Graham unit fractions.** Ernest S. Croot III, “On a coloring conjecture about unit fractions,” *Annals of Mathematics* (2) 157 (2003), no. 2, 545–556; arXiv:math/0311421 v1 (submitted 2003-11-24; comments identify published version). Abstract theorem: there is `b>0` such that every `r`-coloring of the integers in `[2,b^r]` has a monochromatic set `S` with `Σ_{n∈S}1/n=1`. This is a finite interval/colored-set existence statement; it does not assert a fixed number of terms or count how many such sets exist. [Annals source](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2003/157-2/p04), [arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311421), accessed 2026-10-30. Excerpt (Annals abstract): “There exists a constant b>0 such that if we r-color the integers in [2,b^r], then there exists a monochromatic set S such that Σ 1/n=1.”

3. **Huang sensitivity theorem / hypercube witness.** Hao Huang, “Induced subgraphs of hypercubes and a proof of the Sensitivity Conjecture,” arXiv:1907.00847, v2 (revised 2019-08-29; submitted 2019-07-01). Abstract: every induced subgraph of the `n`-cube with at least `2^{n−1}+1` vertices has maximum degree at least `√n`; the bound is best possible. The guaranteed object is a vertex of degree at least `√n` in a subset of exponentially many vertices—not a theorem counting how many such vertices there are. [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00847), accessed 2026-10-30. Excerpt: “every (2^{n−1}+1)-vertex induced subgraph … has maximum degree at least √n. This result is best possible.”

4. **Schur’s theorem.** “Schur’s Theorem,” *Open Textbook: Ramsey Theory*, §4.2, Theorem 4.2.8 (page does not state a version/date or author in the extracted text). Theorem: in every finite coloring of the positive integers there are same-colored positive integers `x,y,z` with `x+y=z`. The page also gives the finite form (Theorem 4.2.11): for every number `r` of colors, some finite initial interval `[1,M]` forces such a triple. The guarantee is at least one triple; no witness-count lower bound is given. [source](https://opentext.uleth.ca/Ramsey/sec_SchurThm.html), accessed 2026-10-30. Excerpt: “If the set of positive integers N is finitely coloured then there exist x, y, z having the same colour such that x+y=z.”

5. **van der Waerden’s theorem.** “Proof of van der Waerden’s Theorem,” *Open Textbook: Ramsey Theory*, §3.3, Theorem 3.3.3 (page extraction did not expose an author or version/date). For every finite number `k` of colors and every length `l`, there is an `N(l,k)` such that every `k`-coloring of `[1,N]` contains a monochromatic `l`-term arithmetic progression; hence every coloring of the positive integers has one. The theorem asserts existence of a progression, not a count of progressions. [source](https://opentext.uleth.ca/Ramsey/sec_proof_vdW.html), accessed 2026-10-30. Excerpt: “Any k-colouring of positive integers contains a monochromatic l-term arithmetic progression. Moreover, there is a natural number N…”

6. **Hasse–Minkowski principle.** Pete L. Clark, “Quadratic Forms over Global Fields,” exposition PDF, version/date not stated in accessed document. Theorem 1: for a quadratic form `q` over a global field `K`, `q` is isotropic over `K` iff it is isotropic over every completion `K_v`. This is rational-field isotropy/local-global equivalence; it is not, as stated, an integral representation theorem. [source PDF](https://plclark.github.io/PeteLClark/Expositions/qforms-global.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30. Excerpt: “(i) q is isotropic. (ii) q is locally isotropic: for all places v of K, q_v is isotropic.”

7. **Meyer’s theorem.** Same source as item 6, Corollary 5 (there labeled “Meyer”; underlying source citation/version not separately given): for `n≥5`, every indefinite rational `n`-ary quadratic form is isotropic over `Q`. This is a rational zero statement, not integral universality. [source PDF](https://plclark.github.io/PeteLClark/Expositions/qforms-global.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30. Excerpt: “For n ≥ 5, an indefinite n-ary quadratic form q_Q is isotropic.”

8. **Conway–Schneeberger Fifteen Theorem.** Manjul Bhargava, “On the Conway-Schneeberger Fifteen Theorem,” in *Quadratic Forms and Their Applications* (Dublin, 1999), Contemporary Mathematics 272 (2000), pp. 27–37; opened paper scan hosted by Bilkent; no arXiv id/version. Theorem 1: a positive-definite quadratic form with integer matrix that represents every positive integer through 15 represents every positive integer. (Conway’s extended foreword distinguishes integer-matrix/classically integral forms from the broader integer-valued setting.) This is a finite test with a universal conclusion; it does not count representations. [paper scan](http://www.fen.bilkent.edu.tr/~franz/mat/15.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30. Excerpt: “If a positive-definite quadratic form having integer matrix represents every positive integer up to 15 then it represents every positive integer.”

9. **Bhargava–Hanke 290-Theorem.** Manjul Bhargava and Jonathan Hanke, “Universal quadratic forms and the 290-Theorem,” preprint PDF (accessed copy has no arXiv id/version/date metadata; bibliographic status not established from this copy). Theorem 1: if a positive-definite quadratic form with integer coefficients represents the 29 listed critical integers `1,2,3,5,6,7,10,13,14,15,17,19,21,22,23,26,29,30,31,34,35,37,42,58,93,110,145,203,290`, then it represents every positive integer. This is for integer-coefficient (integer-valued) forms and is a 29-value criterion, unlike the integer-matrix 15-test. [source PDF](https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/files/290-Theorem-preprint.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30. Excerpt: “If a positive-definite quadratic form with integer coefficients represents the twenty-nine integers … then it represents all positive integers.”

10. **Duke–Schulze-Pillot ternary representation/equidistribution.** William Duke and Rainer Schulze-Pillot, “Representation of integers by positive ternary quadratic forms and equidistribution of lattice points on ellipsoids,” *Inventiones Mathematicae* 99 (1990), no. 1, 49–57 (catalogue says pp. 49–58); DOI 10.1007/BF01234411; no arXiv/version. Journal and digitized-original records were accessed (Springer metadata; Göttingen digitization, 11-page PDF). The paper’s exact theorem text was **NOT ACCESSED**: the extracted digitized PDF contains only wrapper/blank OCR pages. Do not cite a more precise theorem paraphrase from this check. The article title and its identity/date are verified; the precise original theorem statement remains to be checked from legible article pages. [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01234411), [EuDML/Göttingen record](https://eudml.org/doc/143750), [digitized article PDF](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN356556735_0099/LOG_0011.pdf), accessed 2026-10-30.
