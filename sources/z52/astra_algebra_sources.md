## Chevalley–Warning and Ax–Katz

**Chevalley–Warning.** Original Warning paper NOT ACCESSED. Standard form, as recorded in the accessed David J. Grynkiewicz exposition, Theorem 1.1 (arXiv:2208.12895v1; see below): for polynomials F_i over F_q in n variables, if sum of degrees is < n, the number of common zeros in F_q^n is divisible by the characteristic p. (The exposition states the classical assertion at its opening.) This yields existence of a nonzero zero if the origin is a zero and the zero count is divisible by p; it does not bound the count above or provide a particular zero. **Exact original theorem/page NOT ACCESSED**; primary Ax article was accessed, but its PDF text extraction was blank.

**Ax.** James Ax, “Zeroes of Polynomials over Finite Fields,” *American Journal of Mathematics* 86 (1964), 255–261. DOI: 10.2307/2373177. Journal source: https://doi.org/10.2307/2373177; scanned PDF https://www.math.mcgill.ca/goren/SeminarOnCohomology/Ax.pdf (8 pp.; accessed 2026-10-30; PDF title/contents did not render in visitor extraction: exact theorem/page NOT ACCESSED). Ax's strengthening is p-power divisibility of the common-zero count, with exponent governed by n and the degrees; the exact formula is not quoted here because the original PDF's text was inaccessible. Do not conflate divisibility with few solutions.

**Katz.** Nicholas M. Katz, “On a theorem of Ax,” *American Journal of Mathematics* 93 (1971), 485–499, DOI 10.2307/2373370. Bibliographic/theorem lead found in the accessed 2023 exposition; Katz gives the sharper Ax–Katz divisibility exponent. Exact original journal theorem/page NOT ACCESSED. The secondary exposition confirms the purpose as estimating p-divisibility of the number of solutions, not bounding the number of solutions above. Parent re-accessed its Theorem 1.2: if n > (m−1) max_i deg(f_i) + Σ_i deg(f_i), then p^m divides the common-zero count. This stated sufficient bound is what the memo uses; original Katz theorem NOT ACCESSED.

Source accessed: David J. Grynkiewicz, “A Generalization of the Chevalley-Warning and Ax-Katz Theorems with a View Towards Combinatorial Number Theory,” arXiv:2208.12895v1, posted 2022-08-26 (arXiv HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2208.12895v1; accessed 2026-10-30). HTML opening states Chevalley–Warning's common-zero count is 0 mod p when sum degrees < number of variables; its treatment derives weighted Ax–Katz variants. No exact journal publication/version established here.

## EGZ, Olson, Davenport

**EGZ.** Paul Erdős, Abraham Ginzburg, and Abraham Ziv, “Theorem in the additive number theory,” *Bulletin of the Research Council of Israel* 10F (1961), 41–43. The primary 1961 source was NOT ACCESSED. An accessed source is Noga Alon, “Combinatorial Nullstellensatz,” *Combinatorics, Probability and Computing* 8 (1999), 7–29, DOI 10.1017/S0963548398003413; author PDF https://www.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/null2.pdf (accessed 2026-10-30). It states Proposition 8.2: for prime p, every sequence of 2p−1 elements of Z/pZ contains a p-element subsequence with sum zero (p. 16 of PDF pagination, print p. 16). Its bibliography identifies EGZ [26] as above. This is a guaranteed witness of exactly p terms when input length is 2p−1; the theorem does not say the number of such subsequences is small.

**Davenport constant.** For finite abelian G, D(G) is the least length such that every sequence of that length has a nonempty zero-sum subsequence. The accessed Grynkiewicz arXiv:2208.12895v1, §3, Theorem 1.5/proof, states D(G)=D*(G)=1+Σ_i(q_i−1) for finite abelian p-groups G=⊕ C_{q_i}, q_i powers of p. Quantifiers: every sequence of that many elements; witness is nonempty and has at most D(G) terms, but no uniform sublinear bound is asserted. Outside p-groups, equality D=D* is not general. Full theorem hypotheses are essential. Exact original Olson source for general groups NOT ACCESSED. Olson's 1969 result is commonly cited for D(G)=D*(G) for p-groups; primary citation exact title/pages NOT ACCESSED. The 2022 exposition's Theorem 1.5 is a verifiable modern source for the stated p-group result.

## Combinatorial Nullstellensatz

Noga Alon, “Combinatorial Nullstellensatz,” *Combinatorics, Probability and Computing* 8 (1999), no. 1–2, 7–29, DOI 10.1017/S0963548398003413; author PDF https://www.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/null2.pdf; accessed 2026-10-30. In the paper's §2, Theorem 1.2 (the nonvanishing form) says: if f∈F[x_1,…,x_n] has total degree Σ t_i and the coefficient of ∏x_i^{t_i} is nonzero, then for any sets S_i⊂F with |S_i|>t_i, there is a point in ∏S_i where f is nonzero. (The companion ideal-membership form is Theorem 1.1; it is not a coefficient formula.) Use the theorem numbers: the earlier research note's pagination was not reliable. This gives existence of one grid point, i.e. a witness with n coordinates; it does not imply that the set of successful points is small, nor that a short/efficiently verifiable certificate exists. Alon explicitly notes in §10, pp. 20–21, that the algebraic proofs discussed are nonconstructive and supply no efficient algorithm in general.

## Dirichlet and Minkowski

**Minkowski convex-body theorem:** standard form: for a full-rank lattice Λ⊂R^n and a centrally symmetric convex body K, vol(K)>2^n det(Λ) guarantees a nonzero lattice point in K. **Primary source theorem/page NOT ACCESSED.** Search found Lenny Fukshansky, “Geometric Number Theory” lecture notes, https://www1.cmc.edu/pages/faculty/lenny/papers/GNT_lecture_notes.pdf, and modern chapter lead “The Geometry of Numbers,” Theorem 3.10, but neither source was opened; do not use as verified citations.

**Dirichlet approximation:** standard simultaneous form: for α∈R^d and integer Q≥1, there are 1≤q≤Q^d and p∈Z^d with max_i|qα_i−p_i|≤1/Q (convention-dependent strict/non-strict endpoint). Often derived by pigeonhole; Minkowski gives related formulations. A primary published theorem/page and exact source were NOT ACCESSED. These existence results bound the witness parameters by the input scale (e.g. q≤Q^d), not by a constant or logarithmic quantity independent of dimension/scale; they do not furnish a small count of witnesses.

## Zagier two-squares involution

Don Zagier, “A One-Sentence Proof That Every Prime p≡1 (mod 4) Is a Sum of Two Squares,” *The American Mathematical Monthly* 97 (1990), no. 2, 144. DOI 10.2307/2323918. Publisher/JSTOR scan https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/doi/10.2307/2323918/fulltext.pdf (2 pp.; accessed 2026-10-30). The entire proof states an involution on finite S={(x,y,z)∈Z_{>0}^3:x²+4yz=p} with exactly one fixed point, hence |S| odd; a second involution (x,y,z)↦(x,z,y) has a fixed point, yielding the representation. For p=4k+1 its fixed point is (1,1,k). The source explicitly states: “the proof is not constructive: it does not give a method to actually find the representation of p as a sum of two squares.” Quantifier: each prime p≡1 mod 4 admits integers a,b with p=a²+b². The existence proof gives no small witness-count claim and no algorithm/certificate-size bound. The article is one page (p. 144); journal record is sufficient primary source.
