# Analytic-number-theory examples — source-checked notes

Access date for all links below: **2026-10-30**. Search/browse was used only to verify statements and bibliographic metadata; this is not a judgment about relevance to Erdős–Straus.

## Labelled summary
- **PROVED (classical):** Linnik gives a uniform polynomial bound in the modulus for a least prime in every reduced arithmetic progression.
- **PROVED (classical):** Burgess’s character-sum method gives least quadratic nonresidue `n(p) ≪ε p^(1/(4√e)+ε)` for primes.
- **PROVED (Heath-Brown, 1986):** for infinitely many primes, at least one of 2, 3, 5 is a primitive root; this is not a claim for each prime or a specified one of the three.
- **PROVED (Chen, 2021, as reported in source):** every connected component of the Markoff mod-p graph has size divisible by p; together with BGS this gives connectedness for all but finitely many p, not every p.
- **EVIDENCE (preprint claim, not independently proof-audited):** full Zaremba is claimed in 2026 preprint arXiv:2605.02518v2 (large absolute bound, not 5); earlier Bourgain–Kontorovich/Huang establish density-one results with bounds 50/5. Separately, Shkredov’s 2026 preprint establishes a prime-denominator result for sufficiently large primes.
- **PROVED (Lagarias–Odlyzko):** effective Chebotarev gives a least prime ideal with prescribed Frobenius class; GRH gives scale `(log Δ)^2` for the least prime ideal norm, while unconditional bounds involve the zero-free region and possible exceptional zero. Do not conflate prime ideals with rational primes splitting completely absent the Galois/rational setup.
- **PROVED (Chen / Vinogradov):** their famous assertions are pointwise existence for each sufficiently large even/odd integer, respectively; they are not merely lower bounds for a huge number of represented integers. Chen’s alternative has a prime plus a number with at most two prime factors.

## Least prime in an arithmetic progression (Linnik)
**Statement.** For every pair of coprime integers `a,q` (q positive), there is a prime `ℓ ≡ a (mod q)` with `ℓ ≤ C q^L`, for absolute constants C,L. The quantifier is uniform over reduced residue classes; the witness scale is polynomial in q (not a bound on the first prime near a chosen x).

**Source checked:** Stelios Sachpazis, “A pretentious proof of Linnik’s estimate for primes in arithmetic progressions,” arXiv:2209.14538, v6, revised 2024-09-16; journal DOI 10.1112/mtk.12211. The arXiv abstract (https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14538) states the paper proves a strongly uniform estimate for sums of Λ on arithmetic progressions analogous to Linnik’s estimate; the paper explicitly identifies Linnik’s theorem as the size bound for the smallest prime of an AP. **Primary original:** Yuri V. Linnik, “On the least prime in an arithmetic progression. I. The basic theorem,” Mat. Sb. 15(57) (1944), 139–178; bibliographic record confirmed from the reference list of Ahn–Kwon, DOI 10.5802/aif.3274; original not accessed. Accessed the arXiv abstract/record (v6); the full HTML was not separately opened.

## Least quadratic nonresidue (Burgess)
**Statement.** If p is an odd prime and `n(p)` the least positive quadratic nonresidue modulo p, Burgess’s bound implies `n(p) ≪ε p^(1/(4√e)+ε)` for every ε>0 (equivalently the familiar exponent threshold `1/(4√e)`). This is an upper bound on one witness, not a logarithmic bound unconditionally.

**Source checked:** Kevin McGown and Enrique Treviño, “The least quadratic non-residue,” survey PDF dated July 18, 2019 (parent re-read title page; §2 Theorem 2.6 and the least-QNR deduction following it, through p.6), https://campus.lakeforest.edu/trevino/SurveyLeastNonResidue.pdf. The accessed text says explicitly that `n_p ≤ p^(1/(4√e)+ε)` for p sufficiently large depending on ε, and identifies this as the Burgess estimate (see survey discussion and references). Burgess foundational references listed there include D. A. Burgess, “On character sums and primitive roots,” Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 12 (1962), 179–192; and “On character sums and L-series. II,” Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 13 (1963), 524–536. The survey was accessed in full; original Burgess papers were not accessed. Parent re-access confirms the deduction explicitly states n_p≤p^{1/(4√e)+ε} for sufficiently large p depending on ε. Avoid treating N. A. Carella, arXiv:2106.00544, as an established replacement: its 2025 v9 abstract asserts an unconditional logarithmic bound, a claim radically beyond the established Burgess estimate and not corroborated here.

## Heath-Brown: one of 2, 3, 5 primitive roots
**Statement and quantifiers.** Heath-Brown’s theorem says that **at least one member of the fixed set `{2,3,5}` is a primitive root modulo infinitely many primes**. Since the set is finite, this is equivalently that there exists a fixed g in `{2,3,5}` for which infinitely many primes p have g as a primitive root. It does **not** say that for each prime p one of 2,3,5 is a primitive root; nor does it assert that each of 2,3,5 individually works infinitely often.

**Source checked:** D. R. Heath-Brown, “Artin’s Conjecture for Primitive Roots,” The Quarterly Journal of Mathematics 37(1) (1986), 27–38, DOI 10.1093/qmath/37.1.27. Bibliographic metadata and the theorem statement are visible via the journal record/search-extracted source (https://academic.oup.com/qmath/article/37/1/27/1573662; publisher page itself blocked by bot verification). The paper/PDF was **NOT ACCESSED**; theorem statement cross-checked against the cited result as reported in sources returned for this exact title, but this limitation matters. The 1986 paper’s stated result also includes the related assertion that at most two prime values of a can fail to be primitive roots for infinitely many primes, under its hypotheses; do not import this stronger wording without reading the paper.

## Chen’s Markoff graph theorem
**Statement.** Chen (2021) proved a congruence for the degree of a map on the space of covers of elliptic curves; as a corollary, **each connected component** of the Markoff graph modulo p has cardinality divisible by p. Combined with Bourgain–Gamburd–Sarnak, this proves Baragar’s connectedness conjecture for **all but finitely many primes**. It does not prove connectedness for every prime.

**Source checked:** Daniel E. Martin, “A new proof of Chen’s theorem for Markoff graphs,” arXiv:2502.15960v1, submitted 2025-02-21, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15960 (3 pages). The accessed arXiv abstract states the component-size divisibility and “all but finitely many primes” connectedness conclusion explicitly. It is a 2025 new proof/note, not the original theorem. Original Chen paper: Wenhan Chen, *Nonabelian level structures, Nielsen equivalence, and Markoff triples*, Annals of Mathematics 199 (2024), arXiv:2011.12940 (identified in the existing campaign bibliography; original **NOT ACCESSED** in this lane). Note that later titles such as “Connectivity of Markoff mod-p graphs and maximal divisors” discuss related/special connectivity results; do not summarize Chen’s divisibility corollary as unconditional connectivity for every p.

## Zaremba: current full and density-one claims
The Zaremba property for q means there exists a reduced `a/q` whose finite continued-fraction partial quotients are all bounded by a single absolute constant M.

**Current full-conjecture claim (preprint).** Xin Zhang, “Expansion in SL₂(Z/qZ) and Zaremba’s conjecture,” arXiv:2605.02518v2, revised 2026-05-08, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02518. The accessed HTML (https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02518v2) explicitly states Theorem 1.2: “Zaremba’s conjecture holds,” i.e. for every natural q there is a coprime numerator with all partial quotients bounded by an absolute M. The author says the method yields a large absolute bound, **not** the conjectured M=5. The arXiv page records v2 and date; this source is a preprint, not a journal publication. Its HTML has an internal displayed date 2026-08-24, later than the arXiv revision date; record the arXiv’s revision metadata as version/date and flag that discrepancy rather than implying a new version.

**Density-one result.** Jean Bourgain and Alex Kontorovich, “On Zaremba’s conjecture,” Annals of Mathematics 180 (2014), 137–196; arXiv:1103.0422. The accessed arXiv full text states that a set of density one of positive integers satisfies the property for a sufficiently large absolute A (and the paper reports A=2189); the published result is commonly cited as A=50. Shinn-Yih Huang, “An improvement to Zaremba’s conjecture,” Geometric and Functional Analysis 25 (2015), 860–914, improves the density-one bound to A=5. (Metadata and the latter fact also appear in Zhang’s accessed 2026 paper’s introduction.) Thus the older almost-all theorem is strictly weaker than the 2026 full claim, if the latter proof is valid; do not describe the density-one result as full Zaremba.

**Prime-denominator result (separate scope).** Ilya D. Shkredov, “On some results of Korobov and Larcher and Zaremba’s conjecture,” arXiv:2603.14116v2, revised 2026-06-24, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14116. The accessed abstract says it proves Zaremba for any sufficiently large **prime** denominator; more precisely it gives an absolute bound for all sufficiently large M and Ω(q^(1−O(1/M))) numerators with bounded partial quotients for all sufficiently large q, plus its prime-denominator claim. This is a preprint and does not itself establish the all-natural-number result.

## Least prime splitting completely / Lagarias–Odlyzko
**Statement.** Effective Chebotarev bounds the least prime ideal of a number field K with Frobenius in a specified conjugacy class C of a finite Galois extension L/K. In particular, for C the identity, prime ideals of K splitting completely in L exist with an effective least-norm bound. Under GRH, the Lagarias–Odlyzko effective Chebotarev estimate gives scale `(log Δ_L)^2` (with degree factors in the general formulation); their corollary writes a corresponding rational-prime estimate in a splitting setup as `p ≤ c² (log Δ_L)²`. Unconditionally their explicit formula plus zero-free region yields a bound only at much larger scale and accounts for a possible exceptional real zero; do not quote the GRH scale unconditionally. If K=Q and L/Q is Galois, rational primes splitting completely correspond to identity Frobenius. For non-Galois fields, “least rational prime splitting completely” requires additional care and is not simply any least prime ideal norm.

**Source checked:** J. C. Lagarias and A. M. Odlyzko, “Effective versions of the Chebotarev density theorem,” in *Algebraic Number Fields: L-functions and Galois Properties* (Proc. Durham, 1975), Academic Press (1977), 409–464; scanned full text accessed at https://aareyanmanzoor.github.io/assets/articles/lagarias-odlyzko.pdf. Its Theorem 9.1 gives the GRH error estimate for ψ_C; immediately afterward the paper states the least-norm consequence `N p ≤ y² ≤ c₁₄² log² Δ_L` under GRH. It also states Theorem 9.2 with unconditional range `x ≥ exp(4 n_L (log Δ_L)^2)` and an error involving a possible exceptional zero. Bibliographic records confirmed by Ahn & Kwon, “An explicit upper bound for the least prime ideal in the Chebotarev density theorem,” Ann. Inst. Fourier 69 (2019), DOI 10.5802/aif.3274, whose reference list gives Lagarias–Montgomery–Odlyzko, “A bound for the least prime ideal in the Chebotarev density theorem,” Invent. Math. 54 (1979), 271–296, DOI 10.1007/BF01390234. Distinguish the 1977 L–O effective theorem from the 1979 L–M–O least-prime-ideal paper.

## Chen almost primes; Vinogradov three primes
**Chen.** For every sufficiently large even integer N, there is a prime p such that N−p is a product of at most two primes (conventionally a P₂ number; factors counted with multiplicity). This is a pointwise “each sufficiently large N has at least one representation” theorem. It is not merely a statement that a large total number of even integers have such a representation, nor does the statement by itself assert a huge number of representations per N.

**Vinogradov.** Every sufficiently large odd integer is a sum of three primes. Again, the quantifier is pointwise in each sufficiently large odd integer; it does not say each such integer has many representations. This theorem is distinct from the much stronger-looking but different aggregate counts of prime configurations.
