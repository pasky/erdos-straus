Conjecture 1.1 (Zaremba, 1972 [Zar72], p. 76). Every natural number is
the denominator of a reduced fraction whose partial quotients are absolutely
bounded. That is, there exists some M > 0 such that for any q ∈ N,
one can find a natural number a < q with gcd(a, q) = 1, such that writing
a/q = [a1 , a2 , · · · , as ], we have ai ≤ M for every 1 ≤ i ≤ s.

This bound was improved to O( logloglogq q ) for a specific subset of natural numbers by Murphy-Moshchevitin-Shkredov [MMS26], and in particular, this
set contains all primes. In a recent brilliant paper by Shkredov [Shk26], this
bound was further improved to an absolute constant M. Both [MMS26] and
[Shk26] are considering some cantor type sets with some additive structures
in Z/qZ that are originally due to Moshchevitin [Mos07], and a critical ingredient in these two works is Helfgott’s triple product theorem on SL2 (Z/pZ)
[Hel08] as well as its generalization to square free moduli by [BGS10].
Nevertheless, the set of integers covered by [MMS26] and [Shk26] still
only makes a positive proportion of all natural numbers. As hinted in the
concluding remarks of [MMS26], to deal with an arbitrary modulus q, one
naturally needs a growth theory in SL2 (Z/qZ) for a general q. In §2, we
provide such a theory (Theorem 2.1, Theorem 2.2, Theorem 2.4), albeit we
have to admit it is not light. In §3 we apply the results in §2 to estimate
the number of solutions to a particular equation in Z/qZ to obtain Theorem
3.6. Implementing Theorem 3.6 in the framework provided by the proof of
Theorem 8 in [Shk26] then leads to Theorem 1.2. For readers’ convenience,
in §4 we give an outline of Shkredov’s argument how to use Theorem 3.6 to
deduce Theorem 1.2. We invite the readers to Shkredov’s work [Shk26] for
details.
Remark 1.3. The bound M in Conjecture 1.1 is effectively computable.
The methods in [Shk26] and in this paper would produce a large number.
Lowering this bound to 5 or any reasonable magnitude requires a new idea.
