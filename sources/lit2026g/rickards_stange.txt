                                                    Abstract. We study orbits of semigroups of SL(2, Z), and demonstrate reciprocity obstructions: we show
                                                    that certain such orbits avoid squares, but not as a consequence of obstructions inherited from an algebraic

Definition 2.3. We say that an orbit Γ ( xy ) of a subsemigroup Γ ⊆ SL(2, Z) exhibits a congruence obstruction
modulo n if the set of residues of numerators (or, respectively, denominators) modulo n is not the set of all
residues modulo n. If this happens for at least one orbit, we say Γ exhibits congruence obstructions.
  The semigroup Ψ has congruence obstructions modulo 4: for example, all numerators of Ψ ( 10 ) are 1
(mod 4), and all denominators are 0 (mod 4). On the other hand, numerators in the orbit Ψ ( 23 ) have no
congruence obstructions.
Definition 2.4. We say that an orbit Γ ( xy ) of a subsemigroup Γ ⊆ SL(2, Z) exhibits reciprocity obstructions
if the set X of numerators (or, respectively, denominators) of Γ ( xy ) satisfy
     (1) for all n ≥ 1, the residues modulo n of X include squares; but
     (2) X contains no integer squares.
If this happens for at least one orbit, we say Γ exhibits reciprocity obstructions.
   In other words, a reciprocity obstruction is an obstruction to square values not induced by congruence
obstructions. In this paper we are restricting our attention to the numerator and denominator, but in another
context, one might wish to extend the definitions of congruence and reciprocity obstructions to other linear
functionals. We also restrict our attention to squares for the present, but the future may bestow upon us
other types of obstructions deserving of the name reciprocity (as it did in the Apollonian case, [HKRS23]).
   Our main result about Ψ is that it demonstrates reciprocity obstructions.

            4. Sufficient conditions for integers to be represented by orbits of Ψ
   In this section, we give a series of lemmas providing sufficient conditions for integers to appear in orbits of
Ψ. The results depend on the Kronecker symbol properties of Ψ from the previous section. The general form of
the conditions is as follows. Consider a non-negative integer n ≡ y (mod 4) (respectively x (mod gcd(y, 4))).
Then n will appear as a denominator (respectively, numerator) in Ψ ( xy ) if the following holds: any sequence
