# Salez (2014)

**Citation:** Serge E. Salez, “The Erdős-Straus conjecture: New modular equations and checking up to N = 10^17,” arXiv:1406.6307 (2014), https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.6307 (v1 PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.6307).

**Verified quotes** (paper pp. 1, 5–6, 12–13):

> “After having proved the existence of a ‘complete’ set of seven modular equations (including three new ones), this paper offers an optimized sieve based on these equations.” (Abstract)

> “Proposition 3 Let p be a prime polynomial of degree 1. The fraction 4/p is 3-Egyptian if and only if one of the next 7 modular equations holds.” (p. 5)

> “Rosati [3] (1954) gives only one condition for (1) and one for (2). Although they are not written in a modular form, his equations (3) and (6) are equivalent to (14a) and (15a).” (p. 8)

> “Yamamoto [6] (1965) gives two conditions for (1) and two for (2). Written in a modular form, his equations (3) to (6) are equivalent (not in the same order) to (14a), (14b), (15a), (15b).” (p. 8)

> “With N = 10^17 ... Therefore we check the conjecture up to N.” (p. 12; the context says the checked range has no uncertified nonsquares.)

**Interpretation (inference, not a quote):** Salez's “modular equations” are the seven congruence criteria (14a–c), (15a–d) equivalent to Rosati's two Diophantine forms and Yamamoto's conditions, developed by polynomial degree cases. They concern solvability of the full 3-unit-fraction problem / modular certificates, not the special constraint b=1 in the Elsholtz–Tao Type II coordinates. The 10^17 computation is a check of the full conjecture, not a reported exception list for the b=1 line. The PDF includes no reference to 193, 2521, or 66529 in this context.
