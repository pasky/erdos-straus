                                                     Abstract. We prove lower bounds of the form ≫ N/(log N )3/2 for the number of primes
                                                     up to N primitively represented by a shifted positive definite integral binary quadratic
                                                     form, and under the additional condition that primes are from an arithmetic progression.
                                                     This extends the sieve methods of Iwaniec, who showed such lower bounds without the
                                                     primitivity and congruence conditions. Imposing primitivity adds some subtleties to the
                                                     local criteria for representation of a shifted prime: for example, some shifted quadratic
                                                     forms of discriminant 5 (mod 8) do not primitively represent infinitely many primes.
                                                     We also provide a careful list of the local conditions under which a genus of an integral
                                                     binary quadratic form represents an integer, verified by computer, and correcting some
                                                     minor errors in previous statements. The motivation for this work is as a tool for the
                                                     study of prime components in Apollonian circle packings [FFH+ 24].

1.1. Methods. In this paper, we extend the methods of Iwaniec to prove Theorem 1.1. The
proofs follow the arguments of [Iwa72a] quite closely, so that we have chosen to preserve the
notation of that paper in order to facilitate reading them side-by-side.
   In [Sar07], Sarnak remarks that the more general result in [Iwa74] (and implicitly those
in [Iwa72a]) can be modified to include the condition of primitive representation. We found
this modification is not entirely straightforward. Indeed, the statement is false for certain
choices of A, B, and D.
   For example, if D ≡ 5 mod 8 and 2 ∤ AB, then the shifted quadratic form B(ax2 +
bxy + cy 2 ) + A does not primitively represent infinitely many primes. Indeed, if it were
to primitively represent infinitely many primes, then ax2 + bxy + cy 2 would primitively
represent infinitely often even numbers. However, this is not possible by Theorem 2.2. As
a concrete example, x2 − xy + y 2 + 1 does not primitively represent infinitely many primes.
   We also make use of methods from [BF11], which enable us to pass from squarefree
shifted primes represented by the genus of a quadratic form to those represented by one
quadratic form. Note that in [Iwa72a], Iwaniec also has a method for passing from the
genus of a quadratic form to the one quadratic form of interest, but in doing so he sacrifices
primitivity. Our strategy to focus on (mostly) squarefree shifted primes represented by
the genus first requires a careful analysis of local conditions on the various parameters
introduced in [Iwa72a]. Our application of [BF11] is the first example in the literature of
such an application, to our knowledge.

paper, which is a result in combinatorial group theory that has nothing to do with square

we must have L ≡ ad mod 3 and hence (BdL + A, 3) = (Bad2 + A, 3) = (Ba + A, 3) = 3
which is not allowed. Hence in this case we must have ε3 > 0. If we have ε3 > 0, i.e., 3 | d,
then we automatically have (BdL + A, 3) = (A, 3) = 1. I.e. the question reduces to when
an admissible tuple d, L at the place 3 exists. If we are allowed to choose ε3 freely, then
Table 1 shows that some admissible tuple always exists (for example by setting ε3 = ϑ3 in
rows (2), (3) or (4)). However, if we additionally want to achieve µ2 (d) = 1, then the only
possibility for ε3 is ε3 = 1. Then Table 1 shows that the only case in which there is an
admissible choice for L is in the case ϑ3 = ε3 = 1.
   For the more general case, we again assume for a moment that 3 ∤ AB, 3 | Ba + A and
ϑ3 > 1. If ϑ3 = 2, then we choose ε3 = 2, which is admissible by rows (2) and (3). If ϑ3 ≥ 3,
then we choose ε3 = 2 which is admissible by row (1).                                      □
