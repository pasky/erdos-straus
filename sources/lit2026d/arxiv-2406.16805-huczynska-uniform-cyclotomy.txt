                                                                                       Abstract
                                                      Cyclotomy, the study of cyclotomic classes and cyclotomic numbers, is an area
                                                  of number theory first studied by Gauss. It has natural applications in discrete
                                                  mathematics and information theory. Despite this long history, there are signifi-
                                                  cant limitations to what is known explicitly about cyclotomic numbers, which limits
                                                  the use of cyclotomy in applications. The main explicit tool available is that of uni-
                                                  form cyclotomy, introduced by Baumert, Mills and Ward in 1982. In this paper, we
                                                  present an extension of uniform cyclotomy which gives a direct method for evaluat-
                                                  ing all cyclotomic numbers over GF(q n ) of order dividing (q n − 1)/(q − 1), for any
                                                  prime power q and n ≥ 2, which does not use character theory nor direct calculation
                                                  in the field. This allows the straightforward evaluation of many cyclotomic num-
                                                  bers for which other methods are unknown or impractical, extending the currently
                                                  limited portfolio of tools to work with cyclotomic numbers. Our methods exploit
                                                  connections between cyclotomy, Singer difference sets and finite geometry.


                                           1      Introduction
                                           Cyclotomy, the study of cyclotomic classes and cyclotomic numbers, is an area of num-
                                           ber theory first studied by Gauss [12]. It is of considerable theoretical interest and has
                                           important applications, both to other areas of pure mathematics such as combinatorics
                                           and graph theory, and beyond pure mathematics to communications, information secu-
                                           rity and experiment design. Many constructions for difference sets, difference families,
                                           strongly regular graphs, binary sequences, codes and other combinatorial structures are
                                           based on cyclotomy [5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 19, 23, 27, 29, 31, 33]. Work on evaluating cyclotomic
                                           numbers has been carried out for many decades [1, 12, 16, 17, 25, 30, 32, 34].
                                               Despite this long history however, there are very significant limitations to what is
                                           known explicitly about cyclotomic numbers. Expressions for cyclotomic numbers are
                                           most commonly given via character sums (typically, Jacobi sums). However, results are
                                           currently not known for orders beyond order 24 [3] and not all known results are given

                                                                                            1
explicitly [11]. Moreover the expressions obtained from character sums are awkward to
work with or evaluate explicitly, require the use of tables, and many contain sign ambigu-
ity. (We note that in [21], methods for fast computation of Gauss sums were presented;
these may be used to obtain cyclotomic numbers via Jacobi sums.) It was as recently as
1987 [15] that an expression without sign ambiguity was obtained for the cyclotomic num-
bers of order 4. Hence, although cyclotomic classes have very useful structural properties
which make them desirable to use in various applications, practictioners are generally
limited to using specific small values of e, via direct calculation in the finite fields.
     The cyclotomic classes of order e of GF(q n ) (e | q n −1), written Cie (0 ≤ i ≤ e−1), are
the multiplicative cosets of the subgroup of index e in GF(q n )∗ . The cyclotomic number
(i, j)e counts the number of solutions to the equation zi + 1 = zj where zi ∈ Cie , zj ∈ Cje .
     To date, the main theoretical approach that offers a direct approach to the evaluation
of cyclotomic numbers is uniform cyclotomy. Uniform cyclotomy (sometimes called the
semi-primitive case) has been used to construct a variety of structures including strongly
regular graphs [5, 11, 19], cyclic codes [33], constacyclic codes [10], linear codes with small
hull [7, 27], external difference families [29], partial geometric designs [8] and many more.
The following definition was introduced by Baumert, Mills and Ward in [2].

Definition 1.1. The cyclotomic numbers (i, j)e over GF(q) are uniform if (0, i)e =
(i, 0)e = (i, i)e = (0, 1)e for i 6= 0, and (i, j)e = (1, 2)e for 0 6= i 6= j 6= 0.

    The cyclotomic numbers of order 2 are known explicitly [12, 25]. They are uniform
precisely when f is even, in which case (0, 0)2 = (f −2)/2 and (0, 1)2 = (1, 0)2 = (1, 1)2 =
f /2. The following result is proved in [2]:

numbers of order e over GF(q) are uniform if and only if −1 is a power of p modulo e.

    In this paper, we present results which extend uniform cyclotomy. We give a combina-
torial characterisation and direct evaluation method for all cyclotomic numbers of order
e(≥ 2) where e | (q n − 1)/(q − 1), for any n ≥ 2 and any prime power q. By Theorem
1.4, taking q = ps and n = 2 yields the uniform cyclotomy result of [2] with e | ps + 1,
while for the other uniform cyclotomy cases where e | pt + 1 and t is a proper divisor of
s, we take q = pd and n = 2s/d for suitable d | t. While the expressions in the n > 2
cases are not quite as simple as in the n = 2 case, we provide an explicit theoretical
description allowing straightforward direct evaluation working in Ze (e ∈ N), which does
not involve character sums nor computation in the finite field. This allows evaluation of
many cyclotomic numbers for which no direct methods are known.
    A key computational contribution of the paper is to extend the currently limited
portfolio of tools available to work with cyclotomic numbers, and to provide greater

                                               3
breadth without increasing computational requirements. Depending on the parameters
used, and methods of storage/lookup, the computation complexity of our approach is
comparable to the naive method of direct evaluation in the finite field, though likely
to be better in some small cases. However, in practice, practitioners do not typically
perform the general computation outside of a very narrow window. Hence one important
contribution of our approach is to give a practical and accessible method for working
directly with a wider range of cyclotomic numbers than is currently considered. Our
approach facilitates human/computer interaction and can be applied at different levels of
computer involvement (from using the computer to produce the initial Singer difference
set, to using it to perform the whole calculation). The algorithm is straightforward to
implement and does not require calculations in extension fields or any other techniques
that would require a computer algebra package.

2.2    The vector space viewpoint
Let q be a power of some prime p and n ∈ N with n ≥ 2. We may consider GF(q n ) as
a dimension-n vector space over GF(q) with basis {1, α, α2, . . . , αn−1}, where α is a primi-
tive element of GF(q n ). So GF(q n ) = {a0 + a1 α + · · · + an−1 αn−1 : ai ∈ GF(q), 0 ≤ i ≤ n − 1}
and in particular each element αk of GF(q)∗ (0 ≤ k ≤ q − 1) has a unique expression
as a nonzero polynomial in α over GF(q), of degree at most n − 1. The polynomial
a0 + a1 α + · · · an−1 αn−1 may be expressed as the n-tuple (a0 , . . . , an−1 ). We will work in
the projective space PG(n − 1, q), i.e. we will take the non-zero n-tuples and identify two
n-tuples if they differ by a scalar factor. This is equivalent to considering the factor ring
GF(q n )∗ /GF(q)∗ .
    Viewing GF(q n ) as a vector space allows us to work with its vector subspaces. Of
particular interest are the subspaces of codimension 1 (hence dimension n − 1). When
we take the quotient of one of these spaces by the scalars, we obtain a hyperplane of
PG(n − 1, q). A classic combinatorial application of these hyperplanes is in constructing
Singer difference sets (see [4, 24, 25]). A hyperplane may be defined by a suitable linear
equation in the coordinates. For example, we could take a0 = 0, or an−1 = 0, or Trn/1 (a0 +
a1 α + · · · + an−1 αn−1 ) = 0 [9]. Multiplying elements of GF(q n ) by α is a GF(q)-linear
operation that maps hyperplanes to hyperplanes, and in fact permutes all the hyperplanes
of PG(n − 1, q) in a single cycle.

forms an (n, k, λ)-Difference Set (DS) if the multiset {x − y : x, y ∈ D, x 6= y} comprises
λ copies of each non-identity element of G.

     In Section 2, a straightforward algorithm was given for producing the Singer difference

6      Conclusion
The sparsity of cyclotomic number results for specific orders e (indeed, the lack of results
for e > 24), and the cumbersome nature of obtaining explicit results from the character
theory approach for all but small e, has been a limiting factor on the practical use of
cyclotomy. Until now, uniform cyclotomy has been the only explicit theoretical tool
available, but is somewhat limited in its scope. In this paper, we have presented a
generalisation of uniform cyclotomy which is expressed in purely combinatorial terms,
and applies to all cyclotomic numbers of order e | (q n − 1)/(q − 1) over GF(q n ), for
any prime power q and n ≥ 2. We have presented a direct method of obtaining such
cyclotomic numbers, which requires neither use of character theory nor direct calculation
with field elements. We hope that this will go some way towards demystifying cyclotomy
for practitioners, and will facilitate an easier and more effective use of cyclotomy in
various applications, as well as leading to further research in this direction.
