                                                  Abstract
            Let G be a connected graph with n vertices and adjacency matrix A(G). The
        critical polynomial dG (x1 , . . . , xn ) is a degree-n multivariate polynomial defined as
        the determinant of the matrix MG (x1 , . . . , xn ), where

                              MG (x1 , . . . , xn ) = Diag(x1 , . . . , xn ) − A(G).

        For any positive integer r, define the set

                        VdG (r) = {dG (x1 , . . . , xn ) | xi ∈ Z⩾r , 1 ⩽ i ⩽ n} ∩ Z⩾0 ,

        where Z⩾r denotes the set of all integers not less than r. The subset VG (r) ⊆
        VdG (r) consists the elements u such that there exist a1 , . . . , an ∈ Z⩾r for which
        u = dG (a1 , . . . , an ), the matrix MG (a1 , . . . , an ) is positive definite if u 6= 0 and
        positive semi-definite with rank n − 1 if u = 0. Furthermore, the associated group
        ΦMG (a1 ,...,an ) must be cyclic. Motivated by Lorenzini’s exploration of whether the
        complement of VdG (2) in Z⩾0 might be finite for typical graphs [J. Number Theory
        257 (2024) 215–248], we establish that for any simple connected graph G, the subset
        VG (2) is dense in Z⩾0 . This provides additional evidence in support of Lorenzini’s
        hypothesis that the larger subset VdG (2) might actually be cofinite in Z⩾0 .

The matrix MG was introduced in [4]. The principal ideal of Z[x1 , . . . , xn ] generated by
dG (x1 , . . . , xn ) is referred to as the n-th critical ideal of the graph G. The polynomial
dG (x1 , . . . , xn ) is called the critical polynomial of G in [9]. Furthermore, as shown in [5],
two simple graphs G and G0 on n vertices are isomorphic if and only if there exists an
ordering of the vertices of G and G0 such that their critical polynomials dG (x1 , . . . , xn ) and
dG0 (x1 , . . . , xn ) are identical. Let Z⩾r denote the set of integers greater than or equal to
the integer r. For a1 , . . . , an ∈ Z⩾1 , let MG (a1 , . . . , an ) denote the integer matrix obtained
from MG by evaluating MG at xi = ai for 1 ⩽ i ⩽ n. Such matrices play an important
role in geometry. If the matrices of the form MG (a1 , . . . , an ) are positive definite, they are
referred to as M -matrices. The associated group of MG (a1 , . . . , an ) is defined as follows:

(a) If det(M ) 6= 0, the associated group ΦMG (a1 ,...,an ) := Zn / Im(M ) has order |det(M )|.

(b) If det(M ) = 0 and rank(M ) = ρ < n, then the associated group ΦM (a1 ,...,an ) :=
    Zn / Im(M ) is isomorphic to the torsion subgroup of Zn / Im(M ).

    For the matrix M , there exisit two matrices P and Q in GLn (Z) such that P M Q is a
diagonal matrix N = Diag(d1 , . . . , dρ , 0, . . . , 0) where di | di+1 for i = 1, . . . , ρ − 1. This
matrix is called the Smith normal form of M . The group ΦM is isomorphic to the torsion
part of Zn / Im N . Thus ΦM is cyclic if and only if ρ = 1 or dρ−1 = 1. An arithmetical
structure on G is a pair (M, R), where M = MG (a1 , . . . , an ) for some a1 , . . . , an ∈ Z⩾1 ,
and R> = (r1 , . . . , rn ) is a positive integer vector with gcd(r1 , . . . , rn ) = 1 satisfying
M R = 0. Lorenzini [9] showed that M is a positive semi-definite matrix of rank n − 1,
and the associated group is called the critical group of G. Recently, several notable
papers (e.g., [6, 3, 1]) have studied arithmetical structures and critical groups on various
families of graphs, including paths, cycles, bidents, paths with a double edge, and stars.
In particular, Lorenzini in [9] proved that for every connected graph G there exists an
arithmetical structure (M, R) on G such that its critical group is trivial.
    For any polynomial f ∈ Z[x1 , . . . , xn ] and an integer r ⩾ 1, consider the following set
of non-negative values

                          Vf (r) := {f (a1 , . . . , an ) | a1 , . . . , an ∈ Z⩾r } ∩ Z⩾0 .

Since dG (x1 , . . . , xn ) ∈ Z[x1 , . . . , xn ], for a non-negative integer r, the set VdG (r) is studied.
To focus on a more specific case, the subset VG (r) ⊂ VdG (r) is defined. The number
u ∈ Z⩾0 belongs to VG (r) if and only if there exist a1 , . . . , an ∈ Z⩾r such that:

  (i) u = dG (a1 , . . . , an ).

 (ii) If dG (a1 , . . . , an ) 6= 0, then the matrix MG (a1 , . . . , an ) is positive definite; if dG = 0,
      then the matrix MG is positive semi-definite of rank n − 1.

(iii) The associated group ΦMG (a1 ,...,an ) is cyclic.

    Motivated by geometric problems, Lorenzini [9] investigated the properties of the sets
VdG (r) and VG (r) for r ∈ {1, 2}.

Theorem 1 ([9]). Let G be a connected graph containing an induced subgraph H such
that 1 ∈ VH (2). Then VG (2) contains Z>0 . In particularly, if G is a connected simple
graph, then Z>0 ⊂ VG (2), except possibly if G is a tree, a cycle Cn , a complete bipartite

cone C(P3 ).

classes of graphs, this property is far from being proved. So, VdG (2) is considered for
these graphs. For a subset S ⊆ Z⩾0 and a positive integer l, let S(l) := {0, 1, 2, . . . , l} ∩ S

If d(S) = 1, then S is dense in Z⩾0 .
Theorem 2 ([9]). Let G be one of the following graphs:
  (i) G is a Dynkin diagram, an extended Dynkin diagram, a star Sn , or an extended star
      Sn+ .

 (ii) G is a cycle Cn or an extended cycle Cn+ .

(iii) G is the cone C(P3 ), the complete graph Kn , the complete bipartite graph K2,n or
      K3,n .
Then the set VdG (2) is dense in Z⩾0 .
   In fact, Theorem 1 shows that for any connected simple graph G, the set VG (2) is
dense in Z⩾0 , with the exception of certain graphs. These exceptional cases include trees,
cycles Cn , complete bipartite graphs Kp,q , complete graphs Kn with n ⩽ 13, extended
cycles Cn+ with n ⩽ 7, and the cone C(P3 ). Motivated by these results, we turn our
attention to these exceptional classes of graphs, leading to the following conclusions.
Theorem 3. If T is a tree on n vertices, then VT (2) is dense in Z⩾0 .
Theorem 4. If G ∈ {Cn , Kk,l , Kn , Cn+ , C(P3 )}, then VG (2) is dense in Z⩾0 .
    Combining Theorems 1, 3 and 4, we get the following result immediately.
Theorem 5. If G is connected simple graph, then VG (2) is dense in Z⩾0 .
Remark 6. In [9], Lorenzini posed the problem of whether the complement of VdG (2) in
Z⩾0 is finite for a broad class of finite graphs. Our main result (Theorem 5) addresses this
problem by establishing that for any simple connected graph G, the set VG (2) – and hence
VdG (2) – is dense in Z⩾0 . This density result provides a partial resolution to Lorenzini’s
question and simultaneously generalizes Theorem 2 within the framework of arithmetic
progressions.

5    Concluding Remarks
In his earlier work, Lorenzini showed that VG (2) contains all positive integers whenever a
connected graph G contains an induced subgraph H satisfying 1 ∈ VH (2). However, this
criterion does not apply to several special classes of connected simple graphs. Lorenzini
subsequently studied some of these remaining cases and proved that, for graphs in those
classes, the set VdG (2) is dense in Z⩾0 .
    In this paper, we resolve all of the remaining cases. Our main result (Theorem 5)
establishes that VG (2) is dense in Z⩾0 for every connected simple graph G; in other
words, VG (2) contains almost all non-negative integers. Since VG (2) ⊆ VdG (2), it follows
that VdG (2) is also dense in Z⩾0 . Thus, our results strengthen and generalize Lorenzini’s
earlier work to all connected simple graphs.
    Our approach highlights a close interaction between graph-theoretic constructions and
analytic number theory. The main ingredient is Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arith-
metic progressions. Using suitable families of primes, we construct subsets of VG (2) whose
union is dense in Z⩾0 . For trees, the proof begins with carefully chosen initial trees and
extends them inductively in a controlled manner. For the remaining graph classes, we ap-
ply these density arguments together with suitable graph reductions to deduce the desired
conclusion.
    Although our results show that VdG (2) is dense in Z⩾0 for every connected simple graph
G, it remains an open problem whether the complement of VdG (2) in Z⩾0 is finite. Our
results indicate that this complement is extremely sparse, and provide further evidence
that it may indeed be finite.
