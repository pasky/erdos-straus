# Square specialization: approximation for finite polynomial/row towers

Result: finite polynomial/row towers over the t-line, minus any closed set of codimension ≥2, satisfy finite-adelic approximation with t>H and rational avoidance of a proper closed set; this gives the odd-square Type I/II specialization used in `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md`. **Lemma (away from infinity, with a sign).** Start with the affine line over ℚ with coordinate t. At each of finitely many steps take either a product with the affine line or a row `aU+bV=1` in two new variables, where the common-zero locus C of a,b on the current base X has algebraic codimension ≥2 (empty is allowed). Denote the last affine variety by Y. For any closed W⊂Y of codimension ≥2, `Y−W` satisfies the following: every **nonempty basic finite-adelic open** contains a rational point with t>0, even with t greater than any fixed real lower bound and avoiding any prescribed proper algebraic closed subset J⊂Y over ℚ.

Concretely, prescribe open conditions in `(Y−W)(ℚ_l)` at finitely many primes; elsewhere require integral points of a fixed spread-out model of the open variety (in particular, avoiding W **on reduction**). Assume the resulting local conditions have a point at every prime. Integral congruences in a chosen finite list of regular functions can be imposed whenever locally soluble. This is *not* unconstrained integral avoidance at every prime: in the affine plane let W be the four rational points `{0,1}²`. Every integral point reduces into W at 2. Nor is the lemma simultaneous approximation of an arbitrary bounded real open set.

**Proof.** All bases in the tower are smooth geometrically integral since a row is a line bundle in the affine-bundle sense over X−C, trivial on each of `a≠0`, `b≠0`. For the initial line there is no nonempty W of codimension ≥2. Clear the finitely many local denominators, then use CRT; the resulting arithmetic progression divided by a fixed denominator is unbounded above and avoids the finitely many forbidden rational points.

For the induction step write π:Y→X, n=dim X. In a product set C=∅. Over X−C define Z₁ to be the locus on which the **geometric fiber** is entirely in W. On either row chart take finitely many defining equations for W, viewed as polynomials in the line coordinate. All their coefficients vanish precisely on Z₁; thus this locus is closed there. Since dim W≤n−1 and the fibers over Z₁ have dimension one, dim Z₁≤n−2. Put Z=C∪closure_X(Z₁), of codimension ≥2. Do **not** ask the induction to avoid the whole projection of W on reduction: its closure can be a divisor. Instead avoid `closure_X(πW)` only on the **rational generic point of the section** x: it is proper since dim W<n. Also avoid over ℚ the locus on which J contains the whole fiber; its closure is proper by the same chart/dimension argument, using dim J≤n. This latter precaution is needed when J itself dominates X.

Here are the spreading and lifting details. Enlarge a finite exceptional set S to include the prescribed primes, bad model primes and all small primes mentioned below. Over ℤ[1/S] use the tower equations, the closed models of W and Z, and the two polynomial charts. One can arrange that a geometric fiber over the complement of Z in **any** remaining characteristic is not contained in W: on each chart the common zero set of the finitely many coefficients is contained in the model of Z. Indeed the inclusion holds over ℚ; express powers of generators of the ideal of Z in the coefficient ideal on each chart by radical membership and invert the finitely many denominators. Spread the inclusion of C in Z likewise. Enlarging S identifies the original and these models of the open outside S. There is one finite bound d on the line-coordinate degrees in the chart equations for W. Include all l≤d in S. Above a residue point outside Z at l∉S some polynomial on a chart is nonzero of degree ≤d, so fewer than l line values are forbidden (if W is empty use the equation 1).

At **every** l∈S, including the primes just added, use a point of the originally required local open. For a newly added prime this means an integral point of the original open model, not permission to introduce arbitrary denominators. Shrink to a neighborhood preserving the original requirements. Its image contains a base neighborhood and every point there lifts into the required open: use a row chart at the given ℚ_l-point and an open product box. Its base point is outside Z on the generic ℚ_l-fiber: it lies outside C and has a lift outside W, and Z₁ is closed off C. Apply the induction assertion to X−Z with those base neighborhoods, the indicated proper closed exclusions over ℚ, and the lower bound on t. The required base adelic open is nonempty: away from S an original local integral point outside W projects outside C, and outside Z₁ on reduction for the chart coefficient model. More precisely, after enlarging S one can take equality of the fiber-full coefficient locus and the model of Z off C, not only inclusion: their radicals agree on the two generic charts, so spread both radical inclusions. Thus the projected residue point avoids Z. This explains why replacing Z by an arbitrary larger codimension-two set without checking local solubility would not justify induction.

For the resulting x, all old coordinates are integral outside S and a(x),b(x) generate the unit ideal of ℤ[1/S], since no prime outside S contains both. Choose one row solution over ℤ[1/S]. All lifts in the rational fiber are this solution plus λ(b(x),−a(x)), λ∈ℚ. Outside S this parametrizes the integral fiber and is a bijection on its residue line (unimodular row). For a product use the added coordinate as λ. Now W_x over ℚ is empty, because x avoids the closure of πW over ℚ. Its defining ideal becomes the unit ideal over ℚ[λ]; clearing denominators in an identity for 1 shows only **finitely** many l∉S can have nonempty excluded reduction. At each of these primes choose an allowed λ mod l by the degree bound. (Use the chart in which a(x) resp. b(x) is an l-unit, which exists because x avoids C on reduction; there λ ↦ the chart's line coordinate is an affine bijection mod l, and d bounds the degree in that coordinate. z74 Opus xreview fix) At S impose the open λ-conditions guaranteed by the local boxes. A single λ∈ℤ[1/S] does both by clearing local denominators and CRT; there are infinitely many such λ, so avoid the finite rational subset J_x as well. This proves the lemma.

**Double cover and ES test.** If the original tower starts with ℚ[p], base change by p=t² is finite free of rank two on each affine stage. The inverse image of any old codimension-≥2 common-zero locus still has codimension ≥2: finite pullback preserves its dimension bound and the ambient dimension. After base change the tower is again geometrically integral, successively by the product/row description starting at ℚ[t]. No factoriality of the square pullback is required. In the original tower the fiber p=0 is integral and nonempty: initially it is a point, and a new row restricts to a line bundle over a nonempty open in the previous integral fiber (its generic point cannot lie in C, of codimension ≥2 in the whole base). The same argument shows p is prime throughout. In the square tower the scheme t=0 is exactly this old fiber, **not** the nonreduced fiber t²=0.

Here is the precise specialization consequence, including its model-side hypotheses. Suppose X,Y,Z are regular functions from the original finite stage giving a polynomial ES identity at p. Suppose they are positive at its ordered generic point with p>0. For each coordinate d divisible by p include the regular quotient d/p on the list of functions required to be integral; write g for the product of the remaining coordinates, each nonzero modulo p. Assume a Type I or II split at p (one or two divisible coordinates). On the square tower delete `W=V(t,g)`; it has codimension ≥2 because t=0 is integral and g nonzero there. For all good primes integrality in the model now makes t and all listed functions integral, and ensures that a prime dividing the value of t does not divide the value of g. At the finitely many exceptional primes one must explicitly impose integrality, `t∉lℤ_l or g∉lℤ_l` on those integral values, and t odd at 2. These are open local conditions; their solubility is an **input**. A compatible adelic evaluation of all the listed functions with integral values and p evaluated at a unit square at every prime supplies the data: lift locally to a unit square root t_l, retaining the other coordinates. Almost all these lifts are integral points in the chosen finite-stage model (this requires finite-adele values for its finitely many generators). Additional congruence requirements must remain compatible. The lemma then gives a positive odd **integer** t>1 and gcd(t,g)=1 with all required values integral. In applications that infer the split from a model prime, check separately that p divides at least one coordinate in the stage, that d/p for divisible coordinates has integral local values, and that not all three are divisible. For example unit p-evaluations preserve local integrality on division; if all three quotients belong to the discrete ordered ring, their positivity would give `4≤3`.

Positivity here is special to the ES identity, not a real weak-approximation assertion. The real locus of the pulled-back tower over t>0 is semialgebraically connected: induct from the interval, deleting closed real subsets of codimension ≥2 in smooth manifolds (algebraic dimension gives the same real dimension bound) and using the product line or the global real row section `(a,b)/(a²+b²)` and line direction `(b,−a)`. For the deletion fact, in each small chart ball any two points outside a set of dimension ≤n−2 can be joined through an intermediate point outside the two cones of bad directions, of dimension ≤n−1. The resulting dense locally connected complements in a connected manifold form a single semialgebraic component. The component assertion persists under real closed extension. The positive square root of the ordered p lifts its ordered point to this locus. The triple-positive sector is clopen there by `min(X,Y,Z)≥t²/4` on the positive sector. Hence it includes the rational specialization. This gives a **Type I or II positive integer** solution at t², contrary to Elsholtz–Tao, Prop. 1.6 (`sources/elsholtz-tao-1107.1010.pdf`). An arbitrary positive ES solution at an odd square would *not* contradict that result: `4/9=1/3+1/12+1/36`. No result here asserts approximation for a norm-split or normal-cone stage, or the existence of a model meeting additional algebraic/adelic invariants.
