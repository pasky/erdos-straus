## Base and finite fields

Replace `ℚ[P]` by `ℚ[X,Y]`, e.g. order `X` positive infinite and `Y` larger than the whole old ordered field. The only standard-bounded polynomials are rational. `P` is a nonunit prime over ℚ (`X²+Y²` is irreducible over ℚ); `P−1` is even geometrically prime, the norm-one conic being geometrically integral. The old row/polynomial proofs preserve both primes. Build the rational tower and its progression tasks first, as before. Pivots can again be chosen nonzero modulo `P−1`. In a prefix its function field is rational in `X,Y,t₃,…,t_h`; in particular one must **not** assume pure transcendence over `ℚ(P)`. The divisor `(P)` itself is the instructive geometrically split case, not an exception to the custom-prime sign lemma.

For each prefix and scheduled scalar representative `s` apply §1 of `LOGIC_UNIVERSAL_NR_AUDIT.md` in these **absolute** rational parameters. Indeed the ambient stage is geometrically integral and normal, and the geometric divisor of `s` is nonempty and reduced in characteristic zero, so `s` is not a geometric function-field square. Remove poles, all chart pivot zeros, every prefix Dirichlet output zero, and `P=0`, all proper loci. The number of tuples giving `χ_q(s)=−1` is `q^h/2+O_prefix(q^{h−1/2})`. Project these tuples by the original polynomial `X²+Y²`, *not* by either individual coordinate. For `b≠0` the number of pairs solving `X²+Y²=b` over `𝔽_q` is `q−χ_q(−1)`: for nonsplit `q` it is the kernel size `q+1` of the surjective norm `𝔽_{q²}^×→𝔽_q^×`, and in the split case factor into two linear forms to get `q−1`. Consequently the fiber in the full parameter space has at most `(q+1)q^{h−2}` points. The projected set `B_q⊂𝔽_q^×` has size at least `q/3` for all sufficiently large odd `q`, in particular for arbitrarily large `q≡3 mod 4`. This uses an **upper**, not a lower, bound on fiber size; no estimate for the sign on any individual conic is needed, nor any rational point of `s=0` mod `q`.

Thus normalized Haar on the permitted `P_q`-cylinders has the same class-probability bound as before; store a witnessing tuple for each permitted residue. ## Anchoring and lifting to the *exact* norm

On `P=1` a dense rational chart starts with `(X,Y)=((1−t²)/(1+t²),2t/(1+t²))`. Do not fix `(1,0)` for every prefix: a future pivot or output may vanish there. The restricted row charts are nonempty over dense opens since coprimality prevents both entries lying in `(P−1)`; inductively the fiber is geometrically integral rational. The same primitivity check keeps each Dirichlet output nonzero in the fiber, since `Y_new` at that task is free. Thus for each fixed prefix there is a rational point at norm 1 avoiding **all** these zeros and denominators. Its reductions work away from finitely many primes. At deep odd primes choose increasing prefix lengths by the old cutoffs; at the exceptional ones the prefix may be empty. Independently, custom places already have growing good prefixes above `π_q mod q`.

At an odd prime `l` with an assigned prefix, the pair `(x₀,y₀)` from its residues has nonzero norm mod `l`; at least one norm partial derivative `2x₀,2y₀` is a unit. Suppose it is `2x₀`. Choose `y≡y₀ mod l` transcendental over `ℚ(π_l)`; then Hensel at `x₀` gives `x≡x₀ mod l` solving `x²=π_l−y²` in `ℤ_l`. Swap roles when necessary. At an empty-prefix odd place choose *any* pair over the nonzero norm residue (for an anchor `(1,0)` will do). This also works at an optional initial one-prime nonresidue fiber. **Do not** choose two unrelated generics and hope the norm equality holds. The field `ℚ(x,y)` contains the algebraically independent pair `π_l,y` in the indicated orientation, so `x,y` are algebraically independent over ℚ. The remaining prefix parameters can now be lifted transcendently over the field of both lifts in the stored residues, then the chronological tail rules apply, including the conditional unit rule for every later Dirichlet variable.

**The place 2.** Give it an empty tower prefix. Here a smooth residual derivative is *not* available. `π₂≡1 mod 8`; take `y∈4ℤ₂` transcendental over `ℚ(π₂)`. Then `π₂−y²≡1 mod 8` is a square in `ℤ₂^×`. Either root `x` gives integral independent `x,y` by the same transcendence-degree argument. Thereafter the usual tail rules work at 2. Nothing asks for all tower rows to be integral at this single exceptional place; `X,Y` and every Dirichlet variable **are** integral there. This construction neither needs nor asserts arbitrary-unit norm surjectivity at 2.

We now have embeddings at all places with `φ_l(P)=π_l` *exactly*. Both schedules give almost-everywhere integrality of every fixed tower element. The content descent, floors, exact progression witnesses, and classification and scheduling of all nonstandard `R`-irreducibles in §§3–4 of the preceding audit apply verbatim. In particular `P` is prime in `R` because its evaluations are units, and its two named norm coordinates belong to `R` because they are integral at **every** mark. The task `s=P` enforces a nonresidue of its norm value, which is compatible with being a sum of two squares mod `q≡3 mod 4` (the nonzero norm group is the whole residue unit group).

## Real and polynomial descent for ES

The former section `P=T` is not available over this base: in particular the norm cannot equal `T` at negative real `T`. The following proof replaces it entirely.

1. Suppose a positive ES triple occurs in a finite stage. At its last nontrivial step restrict to the line above the preceding **ordered generic base point**, in a real closed extension containing the ordered stage. A product coordinate is free, and on a nonempty row chart the three regular functions restrict to polynomials in the free parameter with old field coefficients. Here `P>0` is fixed. The triple-positive sector of the cleared identity is relatively clopen on the whole line: each positive coordinate is `≥P/4`, and the sector is open. It is nonempty at the ordered realization, hence is the whole line by semialgebraic connectedness. Any nonconstant coordinate now tends to `+∞` at `+∞`. Passing to the limit in the reciprocal sum leaves just the *constant* reciprocals summing to `4/P`; a further positive nonconstant reciprocal at a finite point is impossible. Thus all three are in the old fraction field. Its intersection with the new algebra is the old algebra (for rows use `A[1/u]∩A[1/v]=A` by coprimality). Descend one stage at a time. The argument needs no ordered specialization preserving arbitrary inequalities.

2. Write `x,y,z∈ℚ[X,Y]` for the descended denominators. Their positivity at the ordered point extends to the real punctured plane, by the same clopen test on the semialgebraically connected region `P>0` and real closed field transfer. So all three satisfy `x,y,z≥P/4` there. Each has total degree at least two. At least one has degree **exactly** two: otherwise along a generic real ray, avoiding the finitely many vanishing leading forms, all `P/x,P/y,P/z` tend to zero, whereas their sum is 4. Name a quadratic one `x`. It is irreducible over ℚ since a rational linear factor would give real zeros away from the origin.

3. In `ℚ[X,Y]` set `L=4x−P`, `F=Ly−Px`, `G=Lz−Px`. All three are strictly positive on the punctured plane (`F=Pxy/z`, similarly `G=Pxz/y`). The cleared identity gives `FG=P²x²`. Factoriality and irreducibility of `P,x` imply `F=kP^i x^j` for nonnegative `i,j` and `k∈ℚ_{>0}` (also valid when `x` is associated to `P`). Now `L | F+Px`; reducing `x≡P/4 mod L` gives

       L | k 4^{−j} P^{i+j} + P²/4 .

   This is a **nonzero** polynomial in `P` by the positive sign of `k`. Thus `L∈ℚ[P]` by the divisor fact below, including when `L` is constant. It follows that `x=(L+P)/4∈ℚ[P]`, and `F,G` are likewise in `ℚ[P]` as divisors of `P²x²`. From `Ly=F+Px` and `Lz=G+Px` the same divisor fact gives `y,z∈ℚ[P]`.

   **Divisor fact.** Every polynomial divisor over `ℚ[X,Y]` of a nonzero univariate polynomial in `P` belongs to `ℚ[P]`. Over `ℚbar`, put `u=X+iY, v=X−iY`. For every `c≠0`, `P−c=uv−c` is irreducible, since its quotient is a Laurent polynomial ring. Its conjugates are therefore entire fibers, with equal multiplicities on each Galois orbit for a rational divisor. At `c=0` the factors `u,v` are interchanged by conjugation, so their exponents in a rational divisor agree. Grouping monic fiber factors gives a rational polynomial in `P`, up to a rational scalar (compare the top `X` coefficient). In particular one must *not* assume all geometric fibers are irreducible at zero.

4. These three rational polynomials `g_i(P)` are integral on the marks because the original elements were in `R`. If `D` clears their coefficients, each `g_i(n)` is integral for integers `n≡π mod D` by congruence at the divisors of `D`, and the progression is primitive. They are positive for real `n>0` since `X²+Y²` attains each such value. The ES identity in the plane is an identity in the indeterminate `P`. This is a polynomially solvable progression containing `π`, contradicting its avoidance of the ET families (Prop. 1.9 and ordinary Dirichlet). Hence `¬ES(P)`.

**Actual limit on TS.** The ordinary Dirichlet task `a=1,m=4,H=2` introduces `Q=1+4Z` with `Z∈R`, `Q>2` prime in `R`. Over `ℚ(i)` the divisor `Q=0` is initially just the old geometrically integral stage (`Z=−1/4`). In every later row at least one entry remains nonzero on this divisor by rational primality and faithful scalar extension; the resulting quotient still has nonempty integral line charts with dense common open if both are nonempty. Polynomial extensions also preserve it. Hence `Q` stays nonunit prime in the union over `ℚ(i)`. If `Q=a²+b²` held in `R`, then there `Q` would divide one of `a±ib`, hence both by conjugation, implying `Q²|Q`. Thus the strengthened example distinguishes TS at the ES prime from universal TS; it does not resolve the theory with universal TS added.
