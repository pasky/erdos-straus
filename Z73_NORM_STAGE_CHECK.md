**PROVED (boundedness).** Let `A` be the ordered stage domain, `K=Frac(A)`, with bounded elements of `A` exactly ℚ. Write `F=X²+Y²` and take a nonunit prime `q` in `A`. In the stated UFD setting `FW−q` is primitive linear in `W` over `A[X,Y]`, hence `B=A[X,Y,W]/(FW−q)=A[X,Y,q/F]` inside `K(X,Y)`.

First, `B ∩ ℚ(X,Y) = ℚ[X,Y]`. Write an element of the intersection with **one common denominator** `P/F^k`, where

```
P = ∑_{j=0}^k a_j(X,Y) q^j F^(k−j),    a_j ∈ A[X,Y].
```

Since `P` is both a polynomial over `A` and a rational function over ℚ, `P∈ℚ[X,Y]` (field extension, or clear a rational polynomial denominator and apply the landing coefficientwise). Reduce the displayed identity **in `(A/q)[X,Y]`**, not in `B/(q)`. There `P=ā₀ F^k`. Since `F^k` is monic in `X`, the division remainder of `P` over `ℚ[Y]` maps to the same remainder over `(A/q)[Y]`. The latter vanishes, and ℚ injects into `A/q`; hence `F^k | P` over ℚ. This handles arbitrarily high `W`-degree in one reduction.

Extend the natural valuation of `K` first by a bounded unit `z` with residue transcendental **over the old residue field**, then by positive `T` of value `(0,−1)` in `Γ⊕lex ℤ`. Take `X=T, Y=Tz`. For a nonzero numerator `P=∑c_ij X^i Y^j` its value has first coordinate `min v(c_ij)`: in a fixed total degree the Gauss rule for `∑c_ij z^j` prevents cancellation, and different total degrees have distinct secondary values. `F^k=T^(2k)(1+z²)^k` has first coordinate zero. If `P/F^k` is bounded, all coefficients of `P` therefore have nonnegative old value and are in ℚ. Apply the intersection result. Every nonconstant polynomial over ℚ has secondary value minus its degree, so is infinite. This proves `bounded B=ℚ`. Nonsplitting, factoriality over ℚ(i), positivity and `e(q)=1` are not needed for the boundedness calculation itself.

Transcendental over ℚ **alone** is insufficient. For example take `A=ℚ[h,k]` with `h` infinite and `k=ch`, `c` a fixed real transcendental; only ℚ is bounded in `A` by looking at highest homogeneous degree. Take `X=T` infinitely slow, and `Y/X=c+ε` with a new positive transcendental `ε` infinitesimal compared with `1/(hT)`. Then the angular residue is transcendental over ℚ, but `0<hY−kX<1`.

**Separate obstruction: smoothness of `B` is false in this generality.** In `A=ℚ[s,t,u]` take `q=(s²+1)²+t²+u²` and evaluate at the origin. This `q` is strictly positive on the real locus and `e(q)=1`. It is prime even over ℚ(i): after a linear change it is `lm+(s²+1)²`, a primitive linear polynomial in `m`. Give the variables successively dominant positive infinite values; `A` has no bounded nonconstant polynomial. In the geometric hypersurface `FW−q=0`, the point `(s,t,u;X,Y,W)=(i,0,0;0,0,0)` annihilates every first derivative. Thus this construction does **not** preserve the global smooth-stage hypothesis of `Z73_OPEN_BEZOUT.md`. This does not refute that note's existing steps.

**PROVED (global smooth repair when `q` stays prime in `A[i]`).** Adjoin a second row `C=B[U,V]/(FU+XWV−1)`. Its two charts are polynomial lines over `B[1/F]` and `B[1/(XW)]`. Both bases are smooth over the old smooth `A`: the corresponding partial derivatives of `FW−q` are `F` and `2XW`. Thus `C` is globally smooth, despite singularities of `B`. Factoriality holds on both sides of adjoining `i`: `B[1/F]=A[X,Y,1/F]` is factorial, and `B/(F)=(A/q)[X,Y,W]/(X²+Y²)` is a domain because `i` is absent from `Frac(A/q)` (as `(A/q)[i]` is a domain; use monicity). Nagata gives `B` factorial. Over `A[i]` put `L=X+iY, M=X−iY`. Each of `L,M` is prime in `B[i]` by reduction to `(A[i]/q)[M,W]` or its analogue. Inverting `LM` gives a factorial localization, so `B[i]` is factorial. Neither prime divides `XW` (mod `L` this is `MW/2`, and symmetrically); nor does `F` divide `XW` over `B`. Thus the row entries are coprime over both rings. The same localization/prime-factor argument as in the original note gives factoriality of `C,C[i]`. Over `B`, `C∩Frac(B)=B` by the two charts, so choosing `V` dominant over `Frac(B)` keeps the bounded elements exactly ℚ. At real points `F=0` implies `XW=0`, so the row projects precisely onto `F>0`. The real locus above the old `p>0` component is its product with the punctured plane and a line (the row has a global semialgebraic section). This contains both the generic point and `X=1,Y=0,W=e(q),U=1,V=0`. Hence this row repairs global smoothness with the desired landing and connectivity, without inverting `F` in the ring. The factorization `q=FW` persists. This is a stage calculation, not a joint-model theorem.

**Alternative local approach.** Keep the generic point and landing in the same semialgebraic component of `X_smooth(p>0)` rather than demanding global smoothness. On the new chart `F≠0`, the smooth old component times the punctured plane is connected and includes the generic point and the landing `X=1,Y=0,W=e(q)`. For deformation to a smooth landing, the tangent fibre is still a smooth affine space; flatness and geometric smoothness of that fibre suffice for smoothness along it. Over `T>0` use the old smooth component times the positive line, and use the tangent arc inside that component. Likewise the row can restrict its connectivity argument to the old smooth component. These observations remove the specific geometric objection locally; checking all other stage invariants, scheduling conditions, and the intended sentence remains separate. The selected `q` itself becomes reducible in `B` and must not be counted as a surviving prime.

**Bézout review.** At a smooth rational landing interior to `p>0`, every rational tangent is accessible from its component by a smooth semialgebraic arc. A component of a smooth semialgebraic open manifold is open. Deleting a semialgebraic subset of real codimension ≥2 does not disconnect such a component (compatible triangulation/path perturbation, over any real closed field by transfer). No boundary-direction counterexample applies to the deformation in the actual note. No conclusion about PA or the joint two-squares theory is drawn here.
