# A uniform coefficient-height barrier for integral Pell inputs

(§0 block: z74 Opus xreview fix)
```
Result: at fixed arity r and degree D, menus with coefficients a/M₀, |a|≤H, succeed at ≪ H^(3S)√B(log 2H)^6
        primes in (B/2,B], p≡1 mod 840·M₀·2^(D+1); the least successful height is ≥p^(1/(6S))/(log p)^c on density one.
Label: PROVED (rel. Paredes–Sasyk arXiv:2202.10420 Thm 1.1 with its height dependence; PNT in progressions).
Scope: coefficient height at fixed degree/arity, integral witnesses; no all-miss progression for growing H; not a
       lower bound on algorithmic complexity; not a statement about ES.
Review: cross-model (Claude Opus), data/z74/xreview/REVIEW_PELLNOGO_OPUS.md — ACCEPT-WITH-FIXES (§0 only).
Triage: BARRIER-SCOPED
STATUS delta: none
```

**PROVED.** Fix `r,D≥0, M₀≥1`. Put `S=binom(1+2r+D,D)`, `J=M₀·2^(D+1)`, `M=840J`. Use all triples of total-degree ≤D polynomials in `P,x₁,y₁,…,xᵣ,yᵣ`, with coefficients `a/M₀`, `a∈ℤ`, `|a|≤H`. Let `E(B,H)` count primes `B/2<p≤B`, `p≡1 mod M`, for which at least one such triple, with **any** integral negative-Pell witnesses at p, yields three positive integral ES denominators. Then for `B≥2, H≥1`,

```
E(B,H) ≪_{r,D,M₀} H^(3S) √B (log(2H))^6.
```

In particular on this **fixed** progression the least allowable H is at least `p^(1/(6S))/(log p)^c` on a set of relative prime density one, for every fixed `c>7/(3S)` (take the least height to be infinity if necessary). No uniform all-miss progression for growing H is claimed.

**1. Uniform compression.** Use the bounded-value proof in `PELL_ES_MULTI_MENU.md`. We may assume `H≤B` since otherwise the asserted estimate is trivial. Thus `H≤2p`. For a monomial, substitution of the `Zⱼ^{±1}` expressions gives coefficients with denominators dividing `M₀·2^D·s^j` in the sense that a power `j≤D` suffices, `s²=P`. Multiplying every coefficient by `M₀·2^D·P^l`, `l=ceil(D/2)`, clears all denominators into `ℤ[s]`. Numerator degrees are bounded in terms of r,D, and coefficients are `O_{r,D}(H)` by linearity and the fixed binomial expansions. Thus both embeddings are bounded by a fixed power of p, as are this common integer denominator, the support size and the output bound `2p⁴`. The `A`, gap cutoff, exponent bound and number of retained patterns in that proof are bounded in terms of `r,D,M₀` alone, **independently of H**, including when coefficients or tied sums vanish. One may absorb fixed `M₀` by an absolute lower threshold for p if bounds on exponents only in terms of r,D are desired.

For each pattern replace powers by `(U+sV)^k` or `(−U+sV)^(−k)` for `k<0` in the latter case, and take the even part in s as in the gap proof (`U²−PV²=−1`). The only numerical division in averaging is by 2. Reduction to normal form of x-degree at most one (here x=U, y=V) uses `U²=PV²−1`, not division. Hence every derived coordinate has a representative

```
(A(P,V)+U B₁(P,V))/(J P^l),
```

where the two numerator polynomials have integer coefficients `O_{r,D,M₀}(H)` and bounded degrees depending only on the fixed parameters. In particular the heights have **not** been raised to a power at this stage. The sign choices, exponent assignments and the Cartesian product of patterns for the three coordinates cost a bounded factor per original triple.

**2. Identities and the fixed modulus.** Discard identically zero coordinates. For an identically valid cleared ES equation apply the degree-valuation argument of `PELL_ES_MENU_NOGO.md`: either there is no positive success or all three coordinates are in `ℚ(P)`. By uniqueness of the monic normal form the latter have numerators `a(P)∈ℤ[P]` in the displayed representation. At a successful prime, `p^l|a(p)`. If `p>max |coeff(a)|`, the coefficients of `P^0,…,P^(l−1)` vanish: reduce modulo p, divide by p and repeat (missing coefficients count as zero). Thus these are polynomials with fixed denominator J. Since `p≡1 mod J`, integrality transfers to 1 and positivity transfers by the clopen argument on `P>0`, contradicting `4>3`. The threshold is `p>C₀H` with fixed `C₀`; in the complementary range `H≥B/(2C₀)` the entire claimed bound follows trivially by counting ≤B integers and adjusting the implied constant. No lower bound on `1−δ` or constant depending on δ is needed.

**3. Height-controlled elimination for nonidentities.** Clear the denominators in the ES defect and reduce to the same monic normal form. The resulting `W(P,U,V)∈ℤ[P,U,V]` is nonzero on the generic conic, has bounded degree and height `O((2H)^3)` (in this proof all unnamed degree bounds and constants depend only on the fixed parameters). Every success lies in `W=0`. Over `K=ℚ(P)`, even geometrically, the conic is irreducible: its projective ternary quadratic has rank 3. Its intersection with W has at most `b=O(1)` distinct geometric affine points by Bézout, possibly none. Use these **distinct points irrespective of multiplicity**. A pair of distinct points clashes under `Z=U+kV` for at most one k, so some integer `0≤k≤binom(b,2)` separates the entire finite set (take k=0 for ≤1 point). Use a uniform upper bound for b when enumerating k. This bound is geometric, not a bound on coordinates of a primitive element.

Form the Sylvester resultant in V after substituting `U=Z−kV` into the conic and W. The conic has constant-in-Z leading coefficient `k²−P` in V, nonzero over K. The resultant `F(P,Z)` is not identically zero (else a common curve component); its roots over an algebraic closure of K correspond exactly to images of the generic affine points. No common root at infinity in V is possible. This also covers W of V-degree zero using the constant-polynomial resultant. In particular **no factor of Z-degree one over K** is possible for a separating k: a K-rational image would have a single preimage fixed by Galois in characteristic zero, giving `U,V∈K` on the conic. Such a point is impossible by specializing at any negative rational P off its poles. Conversely one can select k effectively just by trying the bounded list and rejecting an eliminant with a linear factor in Z. A constant eliminant is allowed. Every successful specialization gives `F(p,Z)=0` at its z value, by the determinant identity, even at a degree drop.

The bounded Sylvester size and bounded k give bounded bidegree and height `(2H)^O(1)` up to fixed constants. Factor over ℤ, taking primitive irreducibles and content: only `O(1)` primes p can be roots of its *nonconstant P-only factors*, since their total degree is bounded (a nonzero numerical content is harmless for evaluation in characteristic zero). Each positive-Z-degree factor G has degree ≥2 in Z, bounded bidegree, and height `O((2H)^O(1))`. For the last assertion substitute `(P,Z)=(T,T^m)` with fixed `m>deg_P F`. All factor coefficients remain separately readable since their P-degrees are ≤`deg_P F`; the substituted factors divide the bounded-degree univariate image of F. The univariate factor bound `||g||∞≤2^deg(F_image) ||F_image||₂` for integral divisors follows from multiplicativity of Mahler measure and its standard coefficient and length bounds. This avoids a height assumption on any curve normalization or field generator.

Outside `O(1)` zeros of leading coefficients, a rational root implies reducibility of the specialized factor. A Z-only irreducible factor of degree ≥2 has no rational root. For every other G, apply [Paredes–Sasyk, *Effective Hilbert's irreducibility theorem for global fields*, Thm 1.1, p. 2, arXiv:2202.10420v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10420) with `K=ℚ`: for fixed degree bounds the number of integers `|n|≤B` giving a reducible specialization is `≪_degrees √B (1+log H_ℚ(G))^6`. The height of the primitive integer coefficient vector is controlled by the preceding estimate. Thus `O(√B (log(2H))^6)` parameters are counted per compressed triple, including its vertical exceptions; there is no dependence on witness height.

**4. Count.** There are `(2⌊H⌋+1)^(3S) ≪_{r,D} H^(3S)` original triples, each giving a uniformly bounded number of compressed triples. Union-bound §3 and use §2 to get the result. With `H=B^(1/(6S))/(log B)^c` the bound is `O(B (log B)^(6−3Sc))=o(B/log B)` for the stated c. On a dyadic block the analogous function of p is eventually ≤ its value at B; summing blocks (or using a logarithmically smaller cutoff for the early blocks) and the PNT in the fixed progression gives relative density one. This is a lower bound for polynomial **coefficient height at fixed arity and degree**, not a lower bound on arbitrary algorithm complexity or a claim about selectors that may divide by expressions involving witnesses.
