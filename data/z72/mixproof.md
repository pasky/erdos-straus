**PROVED (with finite integer certificate below).** On an infinite progression of Mordell-hard primes, at `d=2` **all** the candidate-1 words miss although the good set is nonempty. At `d=1` the opposite phenomenon is also possible: one can force *every* word of a fixed menu good simultaneously by further congruences. Neither gives forcing at an arbitrary input prime, nor rules out a union over `d`. No density or class-group estimates used.

Fix `p ≡ 1 mod 8`, prime. A word ideal has norm `N=∏q^|k|`, root `r² ≡ −dp mod N`; reversing the sign of a letter conjugates its local root (lift to `q²` for exponent 2). Distinct prime powers combine by CRT, giving the form `[N,2r,(r²+dp)/N]`. For `d=1,2` there are two genera (fundamental discriminant with two prime discriminants); among unramified split primes `(q/p)=(p/q)=(−d/q)`. Take the first three odd split primes of each sign, support ≤3, exponents `±1,±2`, negative total character. These are exactly the review's target-genus words for these `d` (`Z70_GEN.md`, GW). Good means the class of `[A,4ad,de]` for positive `4abd=p+e, ce=a+b, A=4acd−1`; these equations give denominators `abd, acdp, bcdp` for `4/p`.

**PROVED — CRT glues roots, not divisors (`d=1`).** For a target word `N ≡ 3 mod 4`, set `H=(p+N)/4`. To realize `e=N` with this root up to conjugacy is exactly to find `a|H` with `2a≡±r mod N`: take `b=H/a`, then `b≡−a mod N`, so `c=(a+b)/N`. Conversely these equations force the test. The natural norm-`e` representative is conjugate to the `A` class by `(2a+√−p)`, and swapping `a,b` inverts the class. Complementation `a↦H/a` exchanges the two signs without fixed hits (`N` odd and `r` a unit), giving even total multiplicity, **not** a compulsory pair. At `p=409, N=5·7, H=111` the divisors solving `4a²≡−p` are `{3,37}` mod 5 and `{1,111}` mod 7 (sets of actual divisors); the intersection is empty, even allowing independent signs. The genus parity is right. This misses the `e` identity, not necessarily the class.

**PROVED — simultaneous constructive forcing on subprogressions (`d=1`).** Freeze an admissible reduced progression mod `L` by including 8, every tested prime up to the last menu prime, and every seed prime squared (residues units); this fixes the first six primes, norms and roots. For each signed target word `(N,r)` choose a *distinct* prime `ℓ∤L` with `2ℓ≡r mod N` (Dirichlet, unit class). Impose `p≡−N mod ℓ`. These extra prime moduli are disjoint, none divides its `N`, so CRT gives a reduced subprogression containing infinitely many primes. **At each such input** the displayed test works with `a=ℓ`; no enumeration of solutions is needed. This can force all words, including whatever the ranking selects, but requires extra hypotheses on the input. **CERTIFIED mixed hit without this construction:** at `p=15322362791157241 ≡ 1 mod 840`, a top-four word has `N=17²·19·23²`, root `622`; it gives `(a,b,c,e)=(311,12317016130314,2335,5274953375)` by the `A=N` identity. Conversely at `p=9601` the four smallest reduced `|B|/A` classes are `[62,±6,155],[59,±8,163]`, whereas the entire good set is `[23,±12,419],[31,±6,310]`. Sorted lexicographic ties; neither result depends on tie-breaking at the four-class cutoff. The enumeration need only check `min(a,b)²≤(p+(p+4)/3)/4`, since `ce=a+b≤ab+1`.

**PROVED — a whole nonempty fibre missed (`d=2`).** Put `M=840 ∏_{3≤q≤29, q prime}q²`. Take any prime `p ≡ 9601 mod M`, `p ≡ −8 mod 31`, `p > 2·5783557²`. These are compatible unit conditions (`p ≡ 361 mod 840`); Dirichlet supplies infinitely many. The fixed primes are `{3,11,13,17,23,29}`. The menu has 710 signed target words, 103 norms, `N≤5783557`. If one is good then **its norm is a natural norm**: by `Z70_CLASS.md` SR a good class has a primitive representative of norm `m≤√(2(2p+(p+8)/7))`. Thus `Nm<2p`; two primitive ideals in the same class with this product bound coincide (their conjugate product is principal of norm `<2p`, hence generated by a rational integer in `ℤ[√−2p]`; compare contents, FR′). The natural norms are `A=8ac−1, B=8bc−1, 2e`. The norm is odd so `2e` is impossible. By symmetry, necessarily `N≡7 mod 8`, `a|(N+1)/8`, `N|p+8a²` for some positive `a`. Since `N|M`, the **entire finite disjunction** reduces to this certificate:

```
for N in the 103 norms with N % 8 == 7:
    for a in divisors((N+1)//8):
        assert (9601+8*a*a) % N != 0
```

More generally this gives an **exact eventual membership test** for any fixed split-word menu at `d=2`: in the same free region, a signed `(N,r)` is good iff `N≡7 mod 8` and some `a|(N+1)/8` has `4a≡±r mod N`. Necessity is the ideal identity just proved; conversely `r²≡−2p` gives `N|p+8a²`, and `A=N, c=(N+1)/(8a)` recovers the solution (either sign since the good set is inverse-closed). Thus freezing the prime-power residues fixes **membership itself**, not just genus, for every sufficiently large input in the cell.
