for which p ≡ a (mod k) we denote by p(k, a). The existence of p(k, a) is guaranteed
by Dirichlet’s classical theorem on primes in arithmetic progression, which states that
there are infinitely many primes p ≡ a (mod k). A fundamental breakthrough was
achieved by Linnik [19, 20], who established that

                                       p(k, a) ≪ k L                                  (3)

for some absolute constant L. His proof is a masterpiece involving a log-free zero-
density theorem for Dirichlet L-functions and a result on the Deuring–Heilbronn
phenomenon, a “repulsion” effect due to the possible existence of the so-called
“exceptional” zero that generates a larger zero-free region for the Dirichlet L-functions.
    Subsequent research has focused on refining the value of the exponent in (3) (now
known as “Linnik’s constant”). In 1992, Heath-Brown [12] proved that L ≤ 5.5. In
2018, Xylouris [29, 30], using improvement ideas indicated in Heath-Brown’s paper,
lowered the bound to L ≤ 5, which has not been improved since. In the special case
when the modulus k is prime, Meng [22] achieved L = 4.5.
