It follows from [Zsigmondy's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zsigmondy's_theorem "Zsigmondy's theorem") that p(1,_d_) ≤ 2 _d_ − 1, for all _d_ ≥ 3. It is known that p(1,_p_) ≤ L _p_, for all [primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number "Prime number")_p_ ≥ 5, as L _p_ is [congruent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_arithmetic "Modular arithmetic") to 1 modulo _p_ for all prime numbers _p_, where L _p_ denotes the _p_-th [Lucas number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_number "Lucas number"). Just like [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_numbers "Mersenne numbers"), Lucas numbers with prime indices have [divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor "Divisor") of the form 2 _kp_+1.

It is known that _L_ ≤ 2 for [almost all](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_all "Almost all") integers _d_.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnik's_theorem#cite_note-3)
