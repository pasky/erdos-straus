# Hostile review of Z54_EXACTSPIN

Line numbers refer to the 401-line memo reviewed. I read the entire memo, the requested source sections, all six scripts, and the literature notes. ES remains OPEN. I found no unconditional assertion that ES has been solved; the conditional implication in §1 is correctly conditional. However, several statements labelled PROVED, and several numerical summaries, need correction before the proposed FRONTIER insertion is acceptable.

1. **[MAJOR] Lemma 4.1, lines 94–100: equality of Kummer fields is false.** Congruent radicands give the same residue symbol at the specified prime, not the same extension. For example, in the cubic case on the actual shell, −p/(2w) has valuation 1 at a prime above p, whereas −w″ is a p-adic unit. Since p ≠ 3 and p divides neither w nor w″, the former cubic extension ramifies at p and the latter does not. Thus the displayed equality of fields cannot hold. The character parametrisation, Ramanujan sum, and equality of the two local symbols are correct. **Fix:** replace the field equality by “respectively in these two generally different Kummer extensions, with the same power-residue symbol at 𝔩.”

2. **[MAJOR] §1 lines 25–28; §8 lines 344–345, 368–369: S₂ and S_o are not sums of only pure 2-power and pure odd-order characters.** The displayed decomposition is correct, but its verbal character classification is not. Expanding it gives S₂ = Σ_{4|k} Δ_k and S_o = Σ_{k≥3, 4∤k} Δ_k. In S₂ the odd character component is arbitrary; in S_o the 2-component can be any quadratic character. Already for G = (ℤ/13)×, S₂ includes the order-12 characters, and S_o includes order 6. **Fix:** call these the “order divisible by 4” and complementary non-real layers, or explicitly describe their mixed character support. Do not suggest that controlling only quartic/octic characters controls S₂.

3. **[MAJOR] Lemma 4.5 Reading, lines 170–172; Corollary 5.1(i), lines 220–221; §8 lines 338, 350–351, 376–377, 389–393: the Type-I impossibility claim exceeds the lemma.** Lemma 4.5 correctly proves small fixed-modulus fibres in the original coordinates. It does not define and exclude every Type-I decomposition, completion, factorisation, or transformed sum in those variables. Nor does complete-sum orthogonality require a box longer than the modulus in every possible analytic implementation. The source, GENUSX Corollary 4.2 and Lemma 4.3, explicitly reserves such transformations and calls method verdicts Assessments. **Fix:** retain as PROVED only the fibre cardinality/injectivity statements and the failure of a specified direct complete-fibre averaging implementation. Change “no Type I sum exists in any variable” and the corresponding “three proved reasons” to a scoped Assessment. Do not promote this to a universal no-go in FRONTIER.

4. **[MAJOR] Proposition 5.2, lines 228–236: the zero Fourier mode does not vanish for the stated set of moduli.** The sums run over all c coprime to 6, including cubes. Lemma 4.3 explicitly says the symbol is identically 1 on unit norm fibres for a cube. For example c = 343 is coprime to 6 and even satisfies c ≡ 3 (4). Its zero mode is |𝔑(c)|, not zero. **Fix:** either exclude perfect cubes consistently in N_f, D_f, and (W), or keep the zero mode explicitly. For (c,p)=1 its total is O(r² C^{4/3+o(1)}), since there are O(C^{1/3}) cubes and |𝔑(c)| = C^{1+o(1)}. The corrected bound is O(C^{2−δ}r^{−A} + r² C^{4/3+o(1)}); the extra relative error is C^{−2/3+o(1)}. This repairs the exponent calculation, not the printed exact-vanishing assertion.

5. **[MAJOR] Proposition 5.2 and its advertised consequence, lines 227–244, 352–354, 379–380: distinguish a Fourier majorant from a theorem excluding the exact-layer route.** For a uniformly scaled fixed smooth bump, A ≥ 0, and B > A+2, the calculation is correct: |f̂(m)| ≪ r²(1+r|m|)^{−B}, the lattice sum is ≍ r^{−2−A}, and the resulting relative upper bound has exponent (1−θ)(2+A)−δ. This *upper bound* tends to zero when δ > (1−θ)(2+A); it is not a necessary condition for actual cancellation. The proposition expressly does not handle Φ_c's prime, primary, window, or ν weights, yet the verdict calls it a closure of the route to (Sp-3). **Fix:** state a limitation of this precise smooth unweighted completion/triangle-inequality estimate, with the cube correction from finding 4. Require f_r(z)=f₀(z/r) (or uniform derivative bounds and ∫f ≍ r²), and A ≥ 0; arbitrary smooth functions merely supported in a small disc do not imply the claimed uniform Fourier constants. Keep the unsmoothed prime-window transfer OPEN.

6. **[MINOR] Proposition 5.2, lines 229–230: the stated geometric justification is inaccurate, although the exponent restriction is valid on this shell.** From d ≥ 4u−1 one obtains u ≤ (d+1)/4, not u < d/4. Moreover, a pointwise bound on u need not bound a global U₁ for arbitrary support. On the present family the stronger source statement w″ > 4U₁ is available, so a nonempty block C < c ≤ 2C has U₁ < C/2 and √U₁/C ≪ C^{−1/2}. On fixed Theorem-X windows, the strict exponent gap 1−γ > α₁ gives θ < 1/2. Thus θ ≤ 1/2 is genuinely justified here, not an obstruction fabricated by the Fourier calculation. **Fix:** cite Lemma 4.5(b)/GENUSX Lemma 4.1, explain the block restriction, and use the correct inequality for general (★) hits. The square-root benchmarks δ=1/2 for a sum of per-modulus magnitudes and δ=1 for a hypothetically signed global sum are arithmetically correct but are different types of input.

7. **[MAJOR] Proposition 4.6, lines 175–189: the identity's bookkeeping is mostly right, but its fibre-size and mean-zero descriptions are overstated.** For (c,3p)=1 the exact size is |𝔑(c)| = c ∏_{q|c}(1−χ₋₃(q)/q), also for nonsquarefree c. There are no uniform absolute upper and lower constants making this ≍ c over all c: products of split primes make the ratio small and products of inert primes make it large. Cubes are not mean-zero fibres, and the weight in the identity is Φ_cν, not just Φ_c. Also “odd c ∤ 3” is the wrong divisibility notation. The two primary conjugate primes, factor ν=2/(ℓ−1), condition c≡3 (4), and uniqueness of the small representative all check out: each shell point contributes 4 Re(symbol)/(ℓ−1), and distinct small representatives differ by a nonzero lattice vector of length at least c. **Fix:** state c>1, (c,3p)=1, use the exact product or c^{1+o(1)}, separate cube fibres, and describe a *weighted* covariance only on noncube fibres. The cube-count upper bound should use ((p+U₁)/(2Y))^{1/3}, not (p/(2Y))^{1/3}. The actual shell has c<p and (c,p)=1, but unrestricted auxiliary sums need this domain stated.

8. **[MAJOR] Proposition 4.6 final paragraph, lines 190–193: the full class event is a function on the cosets once the small representative is specified.** A coset meeting the small disc has a unique representative π. Consequently α ↦ Φ_c(α)·1[Nπ | c+1] is a perfectly well-defined function on 𝔑(c), extended by zero off the support. Varying π by cβ moves outside the chosen representative rule; it does not show that the resulting coset function is ill-defined. What is unavailable is a fixed-order residue-character description of that function. **Fix:** replace “not a function on 𝔑(c)” by this narrower distinction. Do not use the false statement as a barrier to other root-equidistribution approaches.

10. **[MAJOR] §5 hypothesis table and Corollary 5.1(iii), lines 213–225: the theorem matching omits essential mismatches and unjustified uniformity claims.** FIMR's spin is a quadratic symbol attached to an ideal and its Galois conjugate in a totally real cyclic field of degree ≥3. Finally λ(u) ≥ U₀^{1−o(1)} “on most u” does not follow from Lemma 3.3; it needs a separate quantitative result and a specified measure. **Fix:** add rows for the actual spin definition, field/unit/Galois hypotheses, and p-conductor/field uniformity. Delete the unsupported Carmichael normal-order claim or cite/prove it. Present the (log x)^{1/3} calculation only as a formal exponent heuristic ignoring ε and field-dependent constants, not the range of an applicable theorem.
