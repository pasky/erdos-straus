# Referee report: Z54_HCKILL.md

Verdict: major revision. Lemmas 1.1–1.2, Theorem Q, the formal curve calculations, and the reported census survive checking. Theorem E appears repairable, not refuted, but its uniform upper-sieve application needs correction. The algebraic classification repeatedly drops local hypotheses, and the literature survey misses a particularly relevant published conditional theorem. ## Issues

1. **MAJOR — §2.2, definition of confinement and Prop F1 (lines 125–138).** The definition uses one character for both window integers, whereas F1 and F4 allow different characters. Also, the proof appears to exclude every prime dividing `2a D (square cofactors)`, including harmless split primes and the necessarily present prime 2 for odd lines. This cannot literally work even for Q: its window integers are always divisible by 5, and 5 divides `a`. **Fix:** define characters separately for each endpoint/level integer. On an explicitly specified nonempty progression giving integral `p ≡ 1 (8)`, require every prime divisor to have character value +1. Outside the finite exceptional set this follows from the norm identity; inside it check the actual valuation/character condition, allowing split primes. In particular allow 2 when `D ≡ 1 (8)`, and forbid ramified/inert prime divisors. Retain this local compatibility in every subsequent equivalence. The Chebotarev/CRT necessity argument itself is correct for confinement on all values of a progression, not merely its prime-valued members.

2. **MAJOR — Props F2–F4 and their summaries (§§0, 7).** The square-class equations are formal necessary conditions, not by themselves an iff for actual confinement families. For example, `U=45, W=15` gives `(a,δ)=(120,105)`, with `δ+16a=45²` and `δ+a=15²`, but `y²+105 ≡ 0 (480)` is impossible already modulo 4. F3 itself subsequently discovers analogous integrality obstructions. **Fix:** state F2 as the exact parametrization of the *formal line-4 square-class conditions*; actual confinement additionally requires F1's local conditions. Make the same qualification in F3/F4 and replace the headline “lines {4,7} iff points on E₇” by “formal square-class solutions are parametrized by E₇; integrality, `p ≡ 1 (8)`, and exceptional-prime conditions remain.” A K(7)-under-Bunyakovsky conclusion additionally requires a resulting polynomial with no fixed prime divisor; integrality alone does not establish that.

3. **MAJOR — Prop F6's consequence (lines 184–187), also §0.** The no-go proof for nondegenerate two-variable quadratics and distinct offsets is valid in its stated *identically on a lattice coset* setting. Its advertised positive example is not: `p=3²+6²−4=41`, but `p+4=45 ∉ S`. Ordinary binary norms permit inert primes to even exponent. Indeed, even the zero-offset norm is not confined in the strict all-values lattice-coset sense: CRT can make both variables divisible by an inert prime. Nor does the lemma cover the degree-four Friedlander–Iwaniec polynomial, degenerate quadratic parts, or arbitrary algebraic constructions. **Fix:** delete “which is Iwaniec's theorem” and the unconditional assertion that this example lies in S; a primitive-representation restriction and a theorem actually supplying it would be needed. Replace “K(4) by algebra needs a one-variable family, hence Bunyakovsky” by “this particular identical-confinement construction cannot use a nondegenerate binary quadratic with two distinct offsets.” Use “window integer,” not “line,” in F6's heading.

4. **MAJOR — Theorem E, Step 4 (lines 220–230).** The claimed `Y ≥ x^0.4` does not follow from the displayed rectangular ranges: they only give `Y ≳ x^(1/2−2η)`, which is `x^0.3` at `η=0.1`. The statement also silently drops the actual restriction `k=q₂ ≥ q₁`; a sieve deleting small primes does not upper-bound *all* prime values if the prime itself is below the cutoff. These are repairable, but the stated uniform application is incomplete. **Fix:** count only `q₁ ≤ k ≤ Y` and omit empty pairs `Y<q₁`. Then every relevant pair has `Y ≥ q₁ ≥ z`, and both actual prime values exceed a small power cutoff. For a clean uniform argument, replace `z₀=x^(1/10)` by `w=x^(1/100)`, use the integer sequence in this interval with local remainders `O(ω(d))`, and a standard upper sieve with level `D'=Y^(1/2)`. Its weighted remainder is `O(Y^(1/2)(log Y)^C)=o(Y/(log x)^(5/2))`, uniformly here. Show the local-product bound with the stated `h(m)`, then enlarge the summation ranges only after obtaining the bound. For Bˡ first write `k=k₀+4j`; the same odd-prime calculation applies. This repairs the η-independent constant needed at Step 5 without any distribution theorem for switched primes.

5. **MAJOR — §4, §0's “closest” statement, and §7's literature claim.** The survey misses Friedlander–Iwaniec, *Hyperbolic prime number theorem*, Acta Math. 202 (2009), 1–19. This is explicitly discussed in the introduction of the very Nath–Xie paper marked “verified.” FI Theorems 2 and 4 prove, under a prime-distribution hypothesis with some level strictly below but sufficiently close to 1, `Σ_{n≤x} Λ(n) r(n−2) r(n+2) ≍ x`. Thus a published EH-type theorem for primes with *two* sum-of-two-squares shifts does exist. Sections 6–7 even use the stricter S-indicator for one shift and the same large-prime-pair switching mechanism. This is substantially closer to E than the cited one-shift/almost-prime result. It is not automatically the exact unweighted P(4,15) theorem. **Fix:** cite FI, distinguish its shifts, representation weights, and weaker ordinary-norm condition from the exact present target, and reassess novelty. Replace all absolute “not in the literature” statements by “no unconditional theorem for this exact S-valued pair was located.” Source personally checked: https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/6709-11511_2009_Article_33.pdf ; see also https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16723v1, introduction and reference [3].

6. **MAJOR — §§0, 3.2–3.3, 7: conclusions exceed the proved scope.** Faltings gives finitely many *ratios* `(a:δ)` at X≥8, not no such families, and certainly not saturation at X≤7. Likewise failure of the displayed dimension-2 lower sieve at `s=2` does not prove impossibility for all confinement sieves, and the BV discussion does not prove that every possible closing argument must be an asymptotic switched count. “Rungs X≥7 still need Dickson-type input” claims necessity that is not proved. **Fix:** say “only finitely many formal family shapes survive at X≥8; their existence and maximal reach remain undetermined,” “the displayed direct confinement-sieve implementation gives no K(7) lower bound even at level 1,” and “Dickson remains the established conditional route recorded here for arbitrary fixed X.” Scope the Polymath comparison to its smooth-modulus theorem; it is not EH(1/2+7/300) for unrestricted moduli. Do not call it “best known” without an up-to-date survey. Apply these changes to the proposed FRONTIER text before copying it.

7. **MAJOR — label discipline throughout, especially §§0, 2.2, 3.2, 5, 7.** Several substantive “Reading” paragraphs, screen claims, and summary deductions have no explicit status. “Assessment with PROVED arithmetic” and the final “EVIDENCE + Theorem E under EH” combine different kinds of assertions. Most seriously, the unqualified actual-family iffs are labelled PROVED although only their formal algebra is established. **Fix:** attach one status to each atomic result or clearly scoped bullet: PROVED for identities and formal reductions; CONDITIONAL (on EH(θ₀), Bunyakovsky for the specified polynomial, or BH as applicable) for infinitude/counting implications; CERTIFIED for exact computations; EVIDENCE for empirical comparisons; CONJECTURE for the proposed asymptotic model; Assessment for method limitations and coverage-limited literature conclusions. Split mixed claims rather than giving a mathematical conclusion a hybrid label. Faltings is an established theorem, so its application can simply be PROVED, not a conjectural condition.
