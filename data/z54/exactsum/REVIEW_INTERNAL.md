1. **MAJOR — Lemma 4.1, hypotheses and proof step 4 (lines 163–193): missing upper bound on U.** The proof uses `Z ≤ 2p`, although its hypotheses allow arbitrary U and Z is p+U or p+2U. The stated uniform error, independent of U, is false with those hypotheses. For example, take f=q=1, K large, and a prime p comparable to K². By CRT, choose U divisible by every composite k in (K,2K], and independently prescribe U modulo each prime ℓ in that interval to make `floor((p+2U)/ℓ)−floor((p+U)/ℓ)−U/ℓ ≥ 1/2−1/ℓ`. Composite k contribute zero discrepancy, so the error is ≫K/log K. This exceeds E for A³B, for which E ≍ K^(27/31). All three displayed K-hypotheses hold. **Fix:** add, for example, `2U ≤ p`, which holds in the intended application; alternatively replace p by p+2U in the bound and in the H-condition. Propagate this hypothesis into Corollary 4.2 and summaries.

3. **MAJOR — Prop. 4.3(d), §0 Reach, and §7: an upper-bound calculation is promoted to a necessary barrier.** Corollary 4.2 provides a sufficient power-saving regime; it does not prove that a block is resolved “only if fq ≤ U/E.” Moreover, the proof deliberately discards its helpful f,q factors. Keeping them and balancing `(K/q)/H` gives, when the resulting H≥1,
   `E_(f,q) = E f^(−κ/(1+κ)) q^(−λ/(1+κ))`,
   rather than E. The corresponding sufficient comparison is `fq E_(f,q) ≪ U`, with the endpoint error retained. In the formal (0,1/2) limit and f=1 this permits `q ≪ U²/K`, rather than `q ≪ U/√K` (up to the endpoint logarithm). Thus the universal “exact deficit”/“any pair” interpretation is not proved. There is also a notation problem: in Corollary 4.2, q is the character modulus and f is a divisibility restriction on u; fq is a cost parameter, not the primitive character conductor. For D_d, the natural choices are f=q=d, not a character of conductor d². **Fix:** call `fq ≪ U/E` the range certified by the displayed coarse estimate, distinguish modulus, conductor and cost, and withdraw the claimed necessary conductor ceiling. The inequalities `E ≥ K^((κ+λ)/(1+κ)) ≥ √K` themselves are correct.
