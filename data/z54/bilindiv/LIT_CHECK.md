3. **Divisor function in progressions — VERIFIED-PRIMARY for cited Fouvry–Iwaniec result; base error bound stated in their paper.** E. Fouvry and H. Iwaniec, “The divisor function over arithmetic progressions,” *Acta Arith.* 61 (1992), 65–75, [primary PDF](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa61/aa6134.pdf). They state the classical bound `Δ(x;q,a) ≪ (q^{1/2}+x^{1/3})x^ε`, yielding the asymptotic (1) uniformly for `q < x^{2/3−ε}` (for each fixed ε>0; `(a,q)=1`). Their Theorem 1 is **not** an individual-modulus extension: for squarefree `r`, `(a,r)=1`, `r≤x^{3/8}`, it proves `∑_{rs²<x^{1−6ε}, (s,ar)=1} |Δ_f(x;rs,a)| ≪ r^{−1}x^{1−ε}`. Thus the beyond-`x^{2/3}` statements concern a mean over the special moduli `q=rs` with squarefree part `r` and the displayed restrictions; a generic-modulus asymptotic there is not asserted. (The paper also reports Fouvry's earlier average result for `x^{2/3+ε}<q<x^{1−ε}`.)

4. **Ford — VERIFIED-PRIMARY.** Kevin Ford, “The distribution of integers with a divisor in a given interval,” *Ann. of Math.* (2) 168 (2008), 367–433, [primary PDF](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v168-n2-p01.pdf), Theorem 1(v), Corollary 2. With `H(x,y,z)=#{n≤x: ∃d|n, y<d≤z}`, Theorem 1(v) gives, for `x>100000`, `100≤y≤z−1`, `y≤√x`, `H(x,y,z)/x ≍` the applicable piecewise expression (in particular for `z=2y`, the second regime): `max(1,−ξ)(log y)^{G(β)}/β`, where `β=log(z/y)/log y`, `ξ=log(x/z)/log y`; the full piecewise theorem also has regimes `log(z/y)` (near `y`), `uδ(log u²)^{−3/2}` for `2y≤z≤y²`, and `1` for `z≥y²`. Corollary 2 states for fixed `c>1`, `c^{-1}≤y≤x/c`, `Y=min(y,x/y)+3`, `H(x,y,cy) ≍_c x/((log Y)^δ(log log Y)^{3/2})` (Ford's δ is a positive absolute constant). For short intervals, Theorem 6 is a different **upper bound**: for fixed nonzero integer λ, `1≤y≤x`, `y+1≤z≤x`, `H(x,y,z;P_λ) ≪_λ H(x,y,z)/log x` if `z≥y+(log y)^{2/3}`, and `≪_λ (x/log x)∑_{y<d≤z}1/φ(d)` otherwise. This is restricted to integers with no prime factor in the specified set `P_λ`; it is not a general short-interval asymptotic for H.

The exact primary theorem text was not retrieved in this run.

6. **Divisors in residue classes — VERIFIED-PRIMARY for Lenstra; Coppersmith et al. only abstract/secondary.** H. W. Lenstra, Jr., “Divisors in residue classes,” *Math. Comp.* 42 (1984), 331–340, [primary PDF](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1983g/art.pdf), Theorem: for integers `r,s,n` with `0≤r<s<n`, `s>n^{1/3}`, and `gcd(r,s)=1`, there are at most **11 positive divisors** of `n` congruent to `r (mod s)` (and a polynomial-time algorithm finds them). D. Coppersmith, N. Howgrave-Graham, and S. V. Nagaraj, “Divisors in residue classes, constructively,” *Math. Comp.* 77 (2008), 531–545. Its [ADS abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008MaCom..77..531C/abstract) says Lenstra's bound is `O((α−1/4)^{−2})` for `s≥n^α`, `α>1/4`; the requested more specific constructive bound is **UNVERIFIED** from the accessible abstract and is not substituted for that cited bound.

7. **Short-interval divisor asymptotic — UNVERIFIED exact best threshold.** For `D(x,y)=∑_{x<n≤x+y}d(n)`, an asymptotic with the expected main term is known for intervals longer than the `x^{1/3}` scale (often stated uniformly for `y≥x^{1/3+ε}`), whereas `y=x^{1/4}` is below that established range and does not follow from such an asymptotic. I did not retrieve a primary theorem establishing the sharpest currently known exponent/range or verbatim error term, so the precise “best known” threshold is left UNVERIFIED; Shiu supplies an upper bound, not this asymptotic.
