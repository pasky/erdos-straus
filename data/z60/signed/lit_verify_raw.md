# Literature verification report

## 1. Squarefree coefficients of half-integral weight forms

**(a) Iwaniec. PARTIALLY VERIFIED.** H. Iwaniec, “Fourier coefficients of modular forms of half-integral weight,” *Inventiones Mathematicae* **87** (1987), 385–401 (some indexes give vol. 88/pages 385–402; the standard citation is 87, 385–401). A primary-source contemporary account is W. Duke, “Hyperbolic distribution problems and half-integral weight Maass forms,” *Invent. Math.* **92** (1988), 73–90, p. 74, which states: “Iwaniec proves that for k > 5/2 and n square-free” the bound (in Duke’s coefficient normalization) is `a(n) ≪_{k,ε} n^{k/2−2/7+ε}`, with a divisor/logarithm factor available instead of `n^ε`. Thus the claimed exponent `k/2−1/2−1/28` is **not** the exponent printed in Duke’s statement. Duke labels this bound (1.2), not an Iwaniec theorem number. The hypothesis is weight `k>5/2` (Duke’s notation), squarefree `n`; no level/character restriction beyond the stated holomorphic cusp form on `Γ₀(N)`, `N≡0 mod 4`, with the displayed Petersson normalization. The original Iwaniec theorem itself was not retrieved here, so exact original theorem number and normalization remain unchecked.

**(b) Duke. VERIFIED (negative answer to proposed claim).** Duke, same reference, pp. 73–76. His introduction explicitly says the `k>5/2` holomorphic bound in (1.2) is Iwaniec’s and then notes his own Theorem 5 includes holomorphic weight `3/2` as a case (“not covered directly in [15]”). Theorem 5 is a bound for spectral Maass forms; it is not a theorem giving the proposed squarefree holomorphic weight-`3/2` estimate. In particular, the paper does not state the proposed `n^{1/2−1/28+ε}` bound for weight-`3/2` holomorphic cusp forms orthogonal to unary theta series. Theorem 5’s full statement concerns Fourier coefficients of spectral Maass forms with specified level/weight and is distinct. So citing Duke 1988 for that claimed holomorphic estimate is **not verified**.

**(c) Duke–Schulze-Pillot. NOT FOUND.** W. Duke and R. Schulze-Pillot, “Representation of integers by positive ternary quadratic forms and equidistribution of lattice points on ellipsoids,” *Invent. Math.* **99** (1990), 49–57. The primary Springer page verified the bibliographic data, but the article text/lemma citation could not be accessed in this pass; I cannot confirm which lemma states/cites the coefficient bound. Do not attribute a particular lemma number without checking the article scan.

## 2. Prime sums for holomorphic newforms

**PARTIALLY VERIFIED; precise requested theorem not located in primary text.** The expected assertions are the standard consequence of the prime number theorem for Rankin–Selberg `L`-functions plus nonvanishing at `s=1`: for distinct normalized newforms, the prime correlation has zero main term, while the diagonal has its Rankin–Selberg pole. The cited candidate “Perron’s formula and the prime number theorem for automorphic L-functions,” J. Liu and Y. Ye, *Pure Appl. Math. Q.* **3** (2007), was located bibliographically, but the accessible search results did not expose a theorem directly asserting the three exact prime sums for classical holomorphic forms. Nor could I verify the proposed Iwaniec–Kowalski theorem numbers. **No precise theorem number/quote is certified here.** Note the normalization distinction: `|λ_f(p)|≤2` follows for level-one forms from Deligne; at general level it is valid for unramified primes for holomorphic forms, with finitely many ramified primes omitted. Real coefficients make the displayed products unambiguous. A primary-source theorem covering the exact formulation should be obtained before citing this item as verified.

## 3. Siegel–Weil for lattice cosets, ternary positive definite case

**PARTIALLY VERIFIED / range unresolved.** A. Weil, “Sur la formule de Siegel dans la théorie des groupes classiques,” *Acta Mathematica* **113** (1965), 1–87, is the relevant primary source for the adelic Schwartz–Bruhat formulation. However, I could not verify from the paper the proposed convergence condition `m−r>n+1` or establish that it directly supplies the genus-average identity for an arbitrary lattice coset and its prescribed congruence class. A coset condition can be encoded at finite places by a Schwartz function, but this observation alone does not identify its theta coefficient with the desired genus-weighted representation count.

For positive-definite ternary `m=3`, `r=0`, and Siegel genus `n=1`, the often-quoted **absolute-convergence range** `m−r>n+1` fails (`3>2` actually holds, whereas formulations with the stricter `m−r>2n+2` do not). These are different conventions/versions, so the requested precise validity range cannot responsibly be settled from the sources retrieved. In particular, the classical ternary case can involve a regularized/analytic-continuation or holomorphic Eisenstein-series formulation; a precise coset theorem and its convergence hypotheses remain unchecked. **Do not cite the proposed inequality as verified without checking Weil’s exact convention and the relevant local Schwartz function.**

## 4. Signs of half-integral weight coefficients

**(a) Bruinier–Kohnen. VERIFIED.** J. H. Bruinier and W. Kohnen, “Sign changes of coefficients of half integral weight modular forms,” in *Modular Forms on Schiermonnikoog*, Cambridge Univ. Press, 2008, 57–65. Theorem 2.1 states: for `t∈D` with `a(t)≠0` (and their character condition, with `χ_{t,N}` taking values in `(0,1)`), “the sequence `(a(tn²))_{n∈N}` has infinitely many sign changes.” This is a fixed square class, not primes or squarefree indices. Their Theorem 2.2 gives infinitely many changes along `a(tp^{2m})` for eigenforms, real character, and all but at most `r` primes (where `2r` is the highest power of 2 dividing the level). Theorem 2.4 treats squarefree `t` with a varying square multiplier. No prime-index result follows.

**(b) Hulse–Kiral–Kuan–Lim. PARTIALLY VERIFIED.** T. A. Hulse, E. M. Kiral, C. I. Kuan, and L.-M. Lim, “The sign of Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight cusp forms,” *International Journal of Number Theory* **8** (2012), 749–762; arXiv:1106.3606. The paper was located through its primary arXiv record/publisher page, but the full theorem statement was not retrieved in this pass. Secondary search snippets describe sign changes of `λ_f(t)` for squarefree `t` for suitable forms; exact theorem number, hypotheses, and quote remain unchecked. No verified claim about primes.

**(c) Lester–Radziwiłł. VERIFIED.** S. Lester and M. Radziwiłł, “Signs of Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight modular forms,” *Mathematische Annalen* **379** (2021), 1553–1604; arXiv:1903.05811. Theorem 1: assuming GRH, for integer `k≥2` and Hecke cusp form `g∈S⁺_{k+1/2}(Γ₀(4))`, the nonzero coefficients at `N♭={8n:n>0 odd squarefree}` have `≫X` sign changes through `X`; it also gives `≫X` positive and `≫X` negative coefficients there. Theorem 2, unconditionally, gives `≫_ε X^{1−ε}` sign changes on the same set. Fourier coefficients are normalized real; theorem 1 assumes GRH for the quadratic twists of the level-one Shimura lift. Appendix extends conclusions to Hecke cusp forms on `Γ₀(4)` and certain level `4N`, `N` odd squarefree, corresponding to newforms. This is fundamental discriminants in a specified sign/class (positive discriminants of form `8n`), not primes.

**(d) Primes / residue classes. NOT FOUND.** None of the verified statements above proves sign changes of `a(p)` as `p` runs over primes, nor primes in a fixed arithmetic progression. Lester–Radziwiłł is on fundamental discriminants, and Bruinier–Kohnen’s fixed-square-class sequence has indices `tn²`, not prime indices. I found no primary source establishing the requested prime progression result in this search.

## 5. Simultaneous quadratic-twist nonvanishing

**PARTIALLY VERIFIED; no qualifying theorem confirmed.** Search surfaced Kumar–Mallesham–Sharma–Singh, “Moments of derivatives of modular L-functions,” arXiv:2303.16864 (later journal publication), and a 2024 paper citing their unconditional simultaneous nonvanishing result. The located descriptions concern moments/derivatives or an orthogonal basis family, not a verified theorem asserting infinitely many (or positive proportion of) *fundamental discriminants* `D` for which both `L(1/2,f⊗χ_D)` and `L(1/2,g⊗χ_D)` are nonzero. No exact theorem or prime-discriminant range was confirmed. **No verified positive-proportion or prime-`D` assertion.**

## 6. Waldspurger Corollaire 2

**NOT VERIFIED.** Waldspurger, “Sur les coefficients de Fourier des formes modulaires de poids demi-entier,” *Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées* **60** (1981), 375–484, Corollaire 2 p. 379, was not available as a readable primary scan in this pass. I therefore cannot confirm whether its hypotheses give every odd `k≥3`, the stated `χ₀(n)=χ(n)(−1/n)^{(k−1)/2}`, or that condition (H2) is unnecessary. The claimed unrestricted weight range must remain **unchecked**, not treated as confirmed.
