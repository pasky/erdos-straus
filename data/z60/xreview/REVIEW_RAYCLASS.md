## Proof audit

1. Put `A=t₀n`, `N=1+Ar²`. If odd `q|N`, then `q∤2Ar` and `−A=(r⁻¹)² mod q`; q is split and unramified. The order discriminant is `−4A`, so q avoids its conductor even when A is not squarefree. If both primes above q divided β, then β would lie in `qO`, impossible from its constant coordinate 1. Local maximality gives multiplicity `v_q(N)` on the unique prime. For any odd `d|N`, the map `O→Z/d`, `ω↦−r⁻¹`, has kernel `(d,β)` and norm d. This constructs the inverse dictionary, including prime powers and composite d.
2. Extension/contraction preserves these ideals and norms between the specified order and `O_K`. The RC order `Z[√−Cn]`, the ray order `Z[√−t₀n]` with `C=t₀s²`, and `Z[2√−Cn]` differ only at primes dividing `2s` (and the underlying conductor), all avoided by odd divisors of `1+4Cn`. No inert/ramified odd factor or unit multiplicity was lost. For an arbitrary order, this fails to be a statement about integral β: at n=5, β=`1+2√−5` is not in `Z[3√−5]`, whose conductor also meets `Nβ=21`.
3. `(4Cu−1)(4CB−1)=4Cn+1` gives (★) and its inverse with positive u,B. The a=1 line is `b(4Td−1)=Tn+1`. Full Type II gives `(4acd−1)(4bcd−1)=4c²dn+1`; at `m=c²d`, choosing the largest c with `c²|m` gives the weakest modulus `4m/c`. Taking `m=t₀s²`, `c=s`, `d=t₀` yields Lemma 1(iv); t₀=1 is precisely S₁. The n=5,T=4 counterexample in (iii) is valid.
4. For `n=sm²`, each odd q dividing N has `χ_disc(K')(q)=1`, since `−t₀s(mr)²=1 mod q`. Products retain +1; when the character conductor divides M, `d≡−1 mod M` requires −1. For squares, `disc Q(√−t₀)|4t₀|M` in each allowed line. For primes with `p∤t₀r`, p ramifies in K′ but does not divide M. The (★) bound `C≤(p+1)/3<p` ensures this. For Cor. 2.1, cancelling `gcd(s,t₀)²` shows every discriminant prime is in C iff every prime of s is in C; the 2-part is absorbed by 4C.
5. Write `F=L_O∩K(ζ_M)`. Complex conjugation inverts `Cl(O)`, so abelian quotients of `Gal(L_O/Q)` kill its squares; `Gal(F/K)` has exponent ≤2. The fibre product over this quotient makes the possible residues of a class exactly one coset of `S=Gal(K(ζ_M)/F)`. Chebotarev supplies infinitely many degree-one primes in each allowed pair. Hence the indicator criterion is exactly as stated. For prime p not dividing 4C the intersection `K_C∩Q(ζ_4C)` is Q by ramification at p. The full unit group has exponent ≤2 iff `4C|24`. None of this identifies the norm residues on the restricted divisor set of one β.
6. In Prop. 3.2, χ_n and χ_−4 are independent nonprincipal characters (their parity differs). Dirichlet supplies `q≡3 mod4` with `(n/q)=−1`; choose the odd root x of `nx²=−1 mod q`. Then C is integral, `q|4Cn+1`, `gcd(x,4C)=1`, and `−q=x² mod4C`. Thus all real characters take the desired values on q. No positivity conclusion for R follows.
7. In D₄, commutators depend on the alternating form on `(a mod2,b)` for `r^a s^b`. Swapping the coordinates in the other factor preserves that form over F₂. The commutator subgroup is therefore exactly `{(1,1),(r²,r²)}`. G has 16 elements; `(r,s)²=(r²,1)` survives, giving a cyclic-quartic quotient. Independent reduced-form enumeration/PARI composition gives cyclic groups of orders 4 and 8 at discriminants −68 and −1156; `[2,2,145]` has order 2. The different split sets certify distinct D₄ fields with the common genus field; the cyclotomic identification beyond the sample is correctly left unproved.
8. Prop. 4.2 is multiplicativity of the Artin map on ideals prime to its conductor; principal ideal images depend on β modulo that conductor in an imaginary quadratic field. Removing a factor over 2 changes the ideal: the remaining product has norm `oddpart(Nβ)`, but one must not retain a claim about `Art((β))` there. “No other relation was found” is an honest assessment, not a theorem of exclusion.
9. Cox's formula gives `h(O)=h_K f∏(1−χ_K(ℓ)/ℓ)/[O_K×:O×] ≥ φ(f)/3`; `f²|d_K|=16Cm²` and `|d_K|≤4C` imply `f≥2m`. The standard totient lower bound, with small m absorbed into the constant, finishes Prop. 5.1. Comparing this with a `(Cn)^{1/2−ε}` bound requires fixing C; the displayed unconditional m-bound is not a uniform lower bound in √C.
10. For Prop. 5.2(a), invert 4p modulo f. For (b), `s↦2t₀ps` is a bijection onto roots of `x²=−t₀p mod f`. At a good odd ℓ every positive prime-power root count is `1+χ(ℓ)`, so the Euler factor is `(1+χ(ℓ)ℓ^−σ)/(1−ℓ^−σ)`. Bad factors are removed and the series converges absolutely for `Re σ>1`. Unique squarefree decomposition of each C proves the box identity. Counting roots imposes neither the tied norm class nor nonvanishing of S₁.

Its Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2 independently support the generalized-dihedral/cyclotomic argument. Correct §8's author/reference pairing; do not call the existing pairing source-verified.
- **Siegel:** read [Elkies, “A nearly zero-free region for L(s,χ), and Siegel's theorem”](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~elkies/M229.18/free_q.pdf), pp. 5–7: `L(1,χ)≫_ε q^−ε`, with **ineffective** constants, and the imaginary-quadratic class-number consequence. No explicit cutoff such as 10¹⁷ follows. For `D=f²D_K`, `L(1,χ_D)=L(1,χ_DK)∏_{ℓ|f}(1−χ_DK(ℓ)/ℓ)`; the finite product is at least `φ(f)/f≫_δ f^−δ`, extending the Siegel bound to orders. For the orders here w=2, so `h(D)=√|D| L(1,χ_D)/π`: both −16Cp and −4t₀p formulas are correct. Exceptional-zero language concerns the inducing primitive character, not a second independent exception attached to an imprimitive modulus.
- **Burde:** read [Lemmermeyer, arXiv:1310.6591](https://arxiv.org/html/1310.6591), equation (7) and its preceding hypotheses. The quoted symbol formula and parity/Legendre hypotheses agree; it is used only as context, not as proof of the sample's universal identification.
- **Mean versus pointwise:** Z59_MSPLUS Theorem 1 averages over **all integers in a fixed progression**. It does not prove an L-free prime mean conditional on a small L-value, nor that an exceptional p leaves (★) unaffected. The L-weighted fixed-ray expectation and “would not care to first order” are heuristics. Prop. 5.2 proves candidate densities only. Keep these qualifications in the bottom line and any FRONTIER text, not solely in the proof section.
