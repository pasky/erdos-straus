# Literature report: shifted sums of two squares and sieve distribution

## Q1. Two shifts of a prime both sums of two squares

**Friedlander–Iwaniec (2009), “Hyperbolic prime number theorem,” *Acta Mathematica* 202, 1–19.** [PDF](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/6709-11511_2009_Article_33.pdf). In the paper's opening, the stated prime-distance count is conditional: “we are able to show that this order of magnitude is correct, that is … (1.4) … for suitable positive constants … and all large x, subject to a standard conjecture concerning the distribution of rational primes in arithmetic progressions.” Theorem 4 (p. 6) says: “Under the same assumption as Theorem 2, we have S(x) = ∑_{n≤x} r(n−2)r(n+2)Λ(n) ≍ x.” Thus the requested ≫x lower bound is **conditional there**, not unconditional. The paper's unconditional Theorem 5 is a different correlation, T(x)=∑_{n≤x}r(n−2)r(n+2)Λ(n+2), and evaluates a single shifted-prime condition (equivalently its displayed (1.17), a sum over primes of r(p−4)); it does not give primes p with both p−2 and p+2 sums of squares.

Searches included the requested phrase variants and citation-focused searches (“hyperbolic prime number theorem,” “p−2 and p+2,” “primes p such that p+a and p+b,” and shifted-prime/two-square variants). **No source found** proving the double-shift lower bound unconditionally, with only level 1/2+δ, or proving infinitely many primes with both p−2 and p+2 sums of squares. This is a bounded literature search, not proof of nonexistence. In particular, an improved distribution theorem for primes by itself would not remove the conditionality without a compatible proof for this correlation.

## Q2. A shifted sum of squares plus an almost-prime/rough partner

**Joni Teräväinen (2018), “The Goldbach problem for primes that are sums of two squares plus one,” *Mathematika* 64, 20–70; arXiv:1611.08585.** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08585), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.08585). Theorem 1.1 states: “Almost all even positive integers n ≢ 5, 8 (mod 9) can be represented as n=p+q with p,q∈P,” where the paper defines P as primes represented by x²+y²+1. Theorem 1.2 states: “All large enough odd positive integers n can be represented as n=p+q+r with p,q,r∈P.” These are additive results about the special prime set x²+y²+1, not a theorem on a prime p with two specified shifts both in S or with one shift having few 3-mod-4 factors. The paper explicitly says its arguments generalize to primes x²+y²+a for a≠0, but that concerns the same additive setting.

The specified-name/topic searches (Wu Jie, Cai Yingchun, Matomäki, Teräväinen, Merikoski, Lichtman, Dartyge, Granville, Fouvry; shifted primes/two-square and almost-sum-of-two-squares wording) produced no verified theorem implying the requested simultaneous shifted-prime assertion. **No such result located**; this does not claim a comprehensive proof of absence. The cited relevant results above and prior-survey results concern one shift, integer correlations, or additive representations, rather than two shifts of a prime.

## Q3. Beyond 1/2 and semilinear sieve weights

**James Maynard (2021), “Primes in arithmetic progressions to large moduli I: Fixed residue classes,” *Memoirs AMS* 306 (2021), arXiv:2006.06572.** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06572), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.06572). The abstract says the result concerns a **fixed residue class** and moduli of size x^(1/2+δ) with a conveniently sized factor. Theorem 1.1 gives a weighted mean bound for π(x;q₁q₂,a), with a fixed a and constraints (1.3)–(1.5); Corollary 1.2 states an error bound summed over a subset of moduli q≤x^(1/2+δ), and Corollary 1.3 gives the expected asymptotic for all but at most 18δQφ(a)/a moduli in [Q,2Q], for δ<1/55. Corollary 1.4 reaches x^(11/21−ε) for factored moduli. These results are not a uniform unweighted Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem for all moduli beyond 1/2.

**Jared Lichtman (2023), “Primes in arithmetic progressions to large moduli, and the twin prime conjecture,” arXiv:2309.08522.** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08522). Corollary 1.5 states (verbatim in notation): for nonzero a∈Z, A,ε>0, and λ_q triply well-factorable of level x^θ with θ<66/107,
“∑_{q≤x^θ,(a,q)=1} λ_q(π(x;q,a)−π(x)/φ(q)) ≪_{a,A,ε} x/(log x)^A.” This is a **fixed a** (implied constant depends on a) weighted estimate, not arbitrary linear-sieve weights. His “A modification of the linear sieve, and the count of twin primes” (arXiv:2109.02851) Theorem 1.1 constructs special λ*_d∈{−1,0,1} with equidistribution to level D=x^(10/17−ε) for each fixed a and a nearly optimal linear-sieve upper bound (F*(s)≤1.000081F(s), 1≤s≤3). The paper says the weights are specifically modified to acquire stronger factorization; it does not say ordinary semi-linear weights inherit that level.

**Joni Teräväinen (2018), citation above.** Theorem 1.5 is a Bombieri–Vinogradov-type *exponential-sum* estimate for semilinear sieve weights λ^{±,SEM}_d. It defines upper/lower levels ρ+=1/5−ε and ρ−=1/3−ε (with z+≤N^(1/2), z−≤N^(1/3−ε)), and bounds the weighted progression-restricted sum of Λ(n)e(αn) by N/(log N)^C in its stated rational-approximation range. It is not a theorem that those weights have an ordinary AP distribution level >1/2; its role in the paper is the exponential-sum/Bohr-set application for P.

**Kaisa Matomäki (2009), “A Bombieri–Vinogradov type exponential sum result with applications,” *Journal of Number Theory* 129(9), 2214–2225.** [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X09000535). The source abstract states: “We prove a Bombieri–Vinogradov type result for linear exponential sums over primes. Then we apply it to show that, for any irrational α and some …” The exact abstract supports an exponential-sum result, not a beyond-1/2 distribution result for semi-linear sieve weights. Detailed exponent/weight interpretation for a general semilinear sieve is **UNVERIFIED** from the accessible abstract.

## Q4. Quadratic-polynomial almost-primes

**Henryk Iwaniec (1978), “Almost-primes represented by quadratic polynomials,” *Inventiones Mathematicae* 47, 171–188.** [EuDML record](https://eudml.org/doc/142575). Its key P₂ result for x²+1 is explicitly summarized and generalized by Lemke Oliver below. I could not verify the complete original Iwaniec theorem block/source text directly; for the exact general theorem statement use the independently accessible generalization rather than attributing an unverified quote to Iwaniec.

**Robert J. Lemke Oliver (2012), “Almost-primes represented by quadratic polynomials,” *Acta Arithmetica* 151(3), 241–261.** [PDF](https://lemkeoliver.github.io/papers/04-Quadratic.pdf), [EuDML](https://eudml.org/doc/279604). Theorem 1 states verbatim: “If G(x)=c₂x²+c₁x+c₀∈Z[x] is irreducible, with c₂>0 and Γ_G≠0, then there are infinitely many positive integers n such that G(n) is in P₂.” The paper defines P_r as squarefree positive integers with at most r distinct prime factors, and Γ_G=(1/2)∏_p(1−ρ_G(p)/p)(1−1/p)^(−1), with ρ_G(p) the number of roots modulo p. It calls this a generalization of Iwaniec’s result.

For G(k)=720k²+1, c₂>0 and G is irreducible (negative nonsquare discriminant −2880). It has no local obstruction: its constant term 1 means it is not identically zero modulo any prime, and ρ_G(p)≤2<p for p>2 (while ρ_G(2)=0). Hence Γ_G≠0 and Lemke Oliver’s theorem applies: **720k²+1 is squarefree with at most two prime factors infinitely often**. It does not say those factors are 1 mod 4, nor that G(k) is prime.

## Q5. Prime values on SL₂ and saturation

**Amos Nevo and Peter Sarnak (2010), “Prime and almost prime integral points on principal homogeneous spaces,” *Acta Mathematica* 205, 133–173; arXiv:0902.0692.** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.0692), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.0692). Theorem 1.2 states, for the integral determinant-matrix variety and the stated weak-primitivity/geometric irreducibility hypotheses, that if r>18·t(f)·n_e³·deg(f), then the points with f having at most r prime factors have the asserted positive-order lower bound. Corollary 1.3 gives saturation number at most 18·t(f)·n_e³·deg(f)+1.

For f=∑_{i,j=1}²x_{ij}² on SL₂(Z), this gives only an **almost-prime** conclusion: t=1 and deg(f)=2 when the function is irreducible on the variety, and n_e=2, hence the theorem gives r>288 and the stated saturation bound ≤289. This is not a prime-values theorem. The same paper’s Theorem 1.4 proves simultaneous prime entries for determinant-m matrices only for n≥3 (under its local condition). Its Remark 1.5(2) says the n=2 analogue “should hold” but that the fixed-m problem is “apparently not known.” I found no theorem giving prime values of the sum-of-squares polynomial on SL₂(Z), and its almost-prime result cannot imply them.

## Verdict

No unconditional theorem located implies P(4,15), nor the weaker general pattern “p+a and bp+c are both sums of two squares for infinitely many primes p.” The closest direct prime-correlation result is Friedlander–Iwaniec Theorem 4, which is conditional on their distribution assumption; their unconditional Theorem 5 controls a different, single-shift prime sum. Results for quadratic-polynomial P₂ values, sparse prime sets, semilinear sieve weights, or affine-sieve almost-primes do not supply the missing simultaneous two-shift prime assertion.
