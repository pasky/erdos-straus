## Negative result / answer to (d)–(e)

No pointwise **lower-bound/existence** theorem was located that says every integer in a broad class (for example, every nonsquare n≡1 mod 4) has a divisor in a specified residue class modulo a modulus growing with n. The verified Lenstra and CHN results are upper bounds on the *number* of such divisors. Hales’s 2024 “revisited” paper retains that upper-bound framework; it does not reverse it. The familiar divisor-distribution and Erdős–Hall/Tenenbaum/Ford results encountered in the repo concern averages, densities, or intervals and do not provide the campaign’s one-fixed-integer guarantee. No such result found that uses primality of p and exempts finitely many small primes in a way that supplies d|p+u, d≡−1 (mod 4u).

Thus, within this checked literature: **no verified pointwise lower bound exists for divisors of one arbitrary/tied integer in a residue class modulo a modulus growing with that integer.** This is a scoped search conclusion, not a proof that no theorem could exist. The prime-AP and least-nonresidue mechanisms give pointwise prime/character witnesses, but the missing step remains precisely the divisor-in-class condition.
