## Independent derivation and model audit

1. Work with the ordinary cohomological Brauer group of the smooth affine **ℚ-scheme** S: kuBC−u−B=n, n≠0. Integral points refer to its displayed ℤ-model, not to an arbitrary isomorphic model. This is not a logarithmic Brauer-group computation.
2. With x=u, y=kC, f=xy−1, one has fB=n+x. Away from H={f=0}, projection is an isomorphism; above H only P=(−n,−1/n) remains, with fibre E≅𝔸¹_ℚ. The chart (f,B), x=fB−n, y=(f+1)/x identifies S with Bl_P(𝔸²) minus H̃.
3. On X=Bl_P(ℙ²), D=L∞+H̃ has class 3L−E=−K_X, with two transverse intersection points. Boundary classes L and 2L−E form a basis of Pic(X̄); the localization sequence gives Pic(S̄)=0 and only constant geometric units. Conversely **Br(X)/Br(ℚ)=0**: the compactification does not have the claimed nonzero quotient.
4. Purity gives Br(U)/Br(ℚ)→H¹(H,ℚ/ℤ) for U=𝔸²\H. The target is H¹(ℚ,ℚ/ℤ)⊕ℤ/2: geometric characters of 𝔾_m are ℚ/ℤ(−1), whose Galois invariants over ℚ have order 2. Constant characters are realized by (χ,f); the nonconstant summand by α=(−f,−x), unramified on x=0 since −f=1 there. Thus no extra algebraic or transcendental classes have been omitted.
5. At E, ord(f)=1 and x=−n, so residues are χ and [n]. The kernel consists precisely of 0 and α+([n],f), modulo constants. This differs from β=(−f,−nx) by the constant (n,−1). Its geometric residue on H is nonconstant, hence it is genuinely transcendental. Br₁(S)=Br(ℚ), not ℤ/2; the quotient of the **full** group is ℤ/2.
6. Removing L₀={u=0} gives V≅𝔸²\{dB=n}. Deligne gives abelian π₁(V), and the degree relation for the conic/line gives H₁(V)=ℤ, generated by the conic meridian. Reattaching L₀ kills that generator because u=dB−n vanishes to order one. Thus π₁(S(ℂ))=1. This is geometric, not the assertion π₁(S/ℚ)=1: finite arithmetic torsors become trivial after the appropriate constant twist and have a section, so they add no étale–BM restriction.

### Every local place, and positivity

Write e_v=exp(2πi inv_v β)∈{+1,−1}. The additive invariant is 0 or 1/2; orthogonality means ∏e_v=1.
- Real place, n>0 and u,B,C>0: d=(n+u)/B>0, so (−d,−nu)_∞=−1. This uses both n>0 and the selected region.
- Odd ℓ dividing d: u is a unit and −nu=u²−udB is a nonzero square modulo ℓ; e_ℓ=+1.
- Odd ℓ not dividing d: e_ℓ=(−d/ℓ)^{v_ℓ(nu)}. If ℓ divides u, −d≡1; if ℓ divides neither n nor u the exponent is zero. If ℓ|n and ℓ∤u, B is a unit and −d≡−u/B, giving exactly (−uB/ℓ)^{v_ℓ(n)}. In particular all ℓ∤2n are trivial.
- At 2, 1−kuC is odd. It is 1 mod 8 for k=8, or for k=4 with uC even, hence a square. Otherwise it is 5 mod 8 and e₂=(−1)^{v₂(−nu)}=(−1)^{v₂(n)}. There is no omitted unit-unit factor because the first unit is 1 mod 4.
- At u=0 or d=0 the displayed symbol is undefined, not the class: near u=0 its first entry approaches 1; near E its second approaches n². Local squarehood/local constancy gives evaluation +1 on these divisors. This validates the continuity convention.
- A uniform finite-place +1 point is (u,B,C)=(1,−n−1,0). A positive real point is (1,(n+1)/(k−1),1). At an odd ℓ with odd v_ℓ(n), set u=1 and choose unit d≡c mod ℓ: C=(d+1)/k and B=(n+1)/d are integral and e_ℓ=(−c/ℓ) takes both signs. At 2 for k=4, C odd/even supplies both signs when v₂(n) is odd.
- Thus a single adjustable finite place cancels the real −1. Without one, every finite sign is +1. This proves exactly squares for k=4, and squarefree part 1 or 2 for k=8. For example n=2 has a k=4 point (1,1,1) but is BM-obstructed for k=8.
- **Without Ω, the “empty at squares” claim is false:** (1,−n−1,0) is a global integral point for every n. These are failures of existence **with positivity**, not failures of unrestricted integral solubility.

### Other proved claims

The four generic-fibre residues rederive as [−n/(kC)], [−nu], [−nB], [−d]; each has an odd-order zero/pole in its base function field, hence is nonsquare. Restricting to the generic fibre therefore detects β, so the vertical subgroup inside Br(S) is precisely Br(ℚ).
At an odd ℓ|n, the ℓ points with u=0 contribute +1, while the (ℓ−1)² remaining points split evenly. Smooth reduction gives negative proportion (ℓ−1)²/[2(ℓ²−ℓ+1)]=1/2−δ_ℓ. Independence gives 1/2−2δ_aδ_b for distinct odd a,b. This expression is not established at 2 (for k=4 and odd v₂(n), the negative 2-adic share is 1/4).
The compactness inequalities in §5 check for n≥1. Deleting uBC=0 instead gives nonconstant units, hence infinitely many classes (χ,u); it changes the adelic problem. The memo correctly leaves this alternative unresolved.
