# Literature verification

Abstract quotations below are verbatim excerpts (ellipses mark omissions) from the linked arXiv/journal abstracts. “Integral Hasse principle” means local integral solubility at every place implies a global integral point.

1. **PARTLY.** Amit Ghosh and Peter Sarnak, “Integral points on Markoff type cubic surfaces,” *Inventiones Mathematicae* **229** (2022), 689–749; arXiv:1706.06712. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06712). Quote: “We show that for almost all k the Hasse Principle holds ... and that there are infinitely many k’s for which it fails.” This verifies the stated infinite failures and almost-all claim. The abstract does **not** give the proposed lower bound `≫ K^{1/2}/(log K)^{1/4}`; its “numerical study points to some basic conjectures” is not a proved quantitative count. It says almost all k, but does not qualify this as conditional in the abstract.

2. **PARTLY.** Daniel Loughran and Vladimir Mitankin, “Integral Hasse principle and strong approximation for Markoff surfaces,” *International Mathematics Research Notices* **2021**(18), 14086–14122; arXiv:1807.10223. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.10223). Quote: “We study the failure of the integral Hasse principle and strong approximation for Markoff surfaces ... using the Brauer-Manin obstruction.” Supports the study using BM, but does not say in the abstract that failures exist beyond BM or state a quantitative count. The precise claimed “not explained” assertion/count should not be attributed to this abstract without a theorem-level check; note paper’s topic is in part BM analysis, but avoid equating failure of strong approximation with failure of BM explanation.

3. **PARTLY.** Jean-Louis Colliot-Thélène, Dasheng Wei and Fei Xu, “Brauer-Manin obstruction for Markoff surfaces,” *Annali della Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa, Classe di Scienze*, Ser. 5, **21** (2020); arXiv:1808.01584. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01584). Quote: “we systematically study the Brauer group and the Brauer-Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle”; and “for m ≠ 0, 4, the Brauer group does not control strong approximation.” Thus BM is studied as an explanation for integral Hasse failure, and BM does not control strong approximation in the specified range. Strong-approximation failure is not itself an integral Hasse failure, so the latter abstract sentence does not establish BM-unexplained Hasse failures. The abstract does not specifically say “some Ghosh–Sarnak failures are explained.”

4. **VERIFIED.** Jean-Louis Colliot-Thélène and Olivier Wittenberg, “Groupe de Brauer et points entiers de deux familles de surfaces cubiques affines,” *American Journal of Mathematics* **134**(5) (2012), 1303–1327; arXiv:0911.3539. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3539). Quote: “If a is not congruent to 4 or 5 modulo 9 then there is no Brauer-Manin obstruction to the existence of integers x, y, z such that x³+y³+z³=a. In addition, there is no Brauer-Manin obstruction ... x³+y³+2z³=a.” The congruences 4 or 5 mod 9 are exactly ±4 mod 9. (This says no BM obstruction, not that an integer solution necessarily exists.)

5. **VERIFIED (with scope clarification).** Yonatan Harpaz, “Integral points on conic log K3 surfaces,” *Journal of the European Mathematical Society* **21**(3) (2019), 627–664; arXiv:1511.04876. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04876). Quote: “Adapting a powerful method of Swinnerton-Dyer, we give explicit sufficient conditions for the existence of integral points on certain schemes which are fibered into affine conics. This includes, in particular, cases where the scheme is geometrically a smooth log K3 surface.” Fibration is supported; “descent” is not stated in the abstract.

6. **PARTLY.** Jean-Louis Colliot-Thélène and David Harari, “Approximation forte en famille,” *Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik* **710** (2016), 173–198; arXiv:1209.0717. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.0717). Quote: under hypotheses on split fibres, a generic fibre which is a homogeneous space of a simply connected almost-simple semisimple group, connected reductive geometric stabilizers, a locally sectioned place, isotropy, and `Br(X)=Br(k)`, “strong approximation holds for X away from the place v.” Confirms a family/fibration strong-approximation result with BM condition; not the particular equation `x²−ay²=P(t)` as a general example, nor an unrestricted theorem for tori.

7. **PARTLY.** Ulrich Derenthal and Dasheng Wei, “Strong approximation and descent,” *Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik* **731** (2017), 235–258; arXiv:1311.3914. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3914). Quote: “We introduce descent methods to the study of strong approximation ... [and] apply them to two classes of varieties defined by P(t)=N_{K/k}(z).” Supports descent and strong approximation, but not the broad phrasing about partial compactifications of tori/toric varieties: the abstract specifies norm-form classes.

8. **PARTLY.** Jean-Louis Colliot-Thélène and Fei Xu, “Brauer-Manin obstruction for integral points of homogeneous spaces and representation by integral quadratic forms,” *Compositio Mathematica* **145**(2) (2009), 309–363; arXiv:0712.1957. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.1957). Quote: “For several types of homogeneous spaces of linear algebraic groups, this obstruction is shown to be the only obstruction to the existence of integral points.” It also says many failures of integral quadratic-form representation can be accounted for by BM. The abstract does not itself formulate the full “semisimple simply connected + noncompactness at a place” hypotheses or the claimed >=4-variable/ternary spinor-exception applications; those need theorem-level citation, not this broad summary.

9. **VERIFIED.** Yonatan Harpaz and Olivier Wittenberg, “On the fibration method for zero-cycles and rational points,” *Annals of Mathematics* (2) **183**(1) (2016), 229–295; arXiv:1409.0993. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0993). Quote: “We prove that these conjectures are compatible with fibrations, for fibrations into rationally connected varieties over a curve”; for rational points “conditionally on a conjecture on locally split values of polynomials,” with an unconditional case established by Matthiesen for linear polynomials over Q. This is a rational-connectedness/curve fibration result, not simply any fibres satisfying HP/BM.

10. **PARTLY.** William Duke, “Hyperbolic distribution problems and half-integral weight Maass forms,” *Inventiones Mathematicae* **92** (1988), 73–90; and William Duke and Rainer Schulze-Pillot, “Representation of integers by positive ternary quadratic forms and equidistribution of lattice points on ellipsoids,” *Inventiones Mathematicae* **99** (1990), 49–57. The key Duke–Schulze-Pillot theorem is commonly stated: every sufficiently large integer locally represented by a positive definite integral ternary quadratic form and satisfying the appropriate spinor-genus condition is represented; the threshold is ineffective. A concise abstract quote establishing this exact formulation was not located in the abstract material consulted, so treat the claim as theorem-level rather than an abstract-supported result. “Locally represented” alone needs the spinor-genus qualification; without it the assertion is false because of spinor exceptions.

11. **VERIFIED (one recent example; not a general density-one result).** Quang-Duc Dao, “Rational and integral points on Markoff-type K3 surfaces,” arXiv:2504.10992 (2025 preprint; no journal citation verified). [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10992). The arXiv abstract (as indexed) states, “for almost all m, the integral Hasse principle holds,” while infinitely many parameters fail it. This is a close match, but it is a specific Markoff-type K3 family and a preprint; it does not justify a general density-one statement for affine/log-K3 families. One verified example is listed rather than padding with papers whose abstracts do not support the requested density-one claim.

12. **VERIFIED.** Mark Gross, Paul Hacking and Sean Keel, “Mirror symmetry for log Calabi-Yau surfaces I,” *Publications Mathématiques de l’IHÉS* **122** (2015), 65–168; arXiv:1106.4977. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.4977). Quote: “We give a canonical synthetic construction of the mirror family to a pair (Y,D) of a smooth projective surface with an anti-canonical cycle of rational curves.” This verifies existence and the Looijenga-pair/maximal-boundary setup.
