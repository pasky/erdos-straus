2. **MAJOR — Both statistics programs omit μ = ∞ from the numerators of P(μ > D), but include it in the denominator.**
   **Locations:** `scripts/z58_ladder_mu.c`'s `if(!mu){ big++; continue; }`; analogous `continue` in `z58_ladder_mdec.c`; §1 probability table and stability claims.
   A zero of R has μ = ∞ and belongs to every finite-D tail. The printed fractions are neither unconditional tail probabilities nor probabilities conditional on R > 0. For primeM in (10⁶,10⁷], the data contain 18,143 primes and four zeros. The three printed finite-tail counts are 3,670, 225, and 2. Correct unconditional fractions are **0.20250234, 0.01262195, 0.000330706**, not 0.20228, 0.012401, 0.0001102. The last error is a factor of three. Similarly, the small decider's >10⁴ fractions must include nine prime zeros and eighteen composite zeros. Calling `max_mu` simply “max μ” is also false in a bin containing zeros: its actual maximum is ∞.
   **Fix:** count a zero in each applicable tail, or explicitly report conditional finite-μ tails with denominator `count - zeros`. Label maxima “maximum finite μ.” Regenerate affected statistics and revise “stable from 10⁶”; the high-range zero-free fit is not affected by this particular bug. Preserve separate zero counts.

3. **MAJOR — F2 misidentifies the C-even restriction and falsely applies exact R₈ failures to the relaxed cap ladder.**
   **Location:** falsifier table F2.
   First, `d ≡ 7 (8)` is necessary but not sufficient for C even: the exact d-form condition is **r | (d+1)/8**, not merely r | (d+1)/4 with d ≡ 7 (8). A counterexample inside 𝔐 is the prime **7561 = 4·6·329·1 − 6 − 329**. Its d = 23 ≡ 7 (8), r = 6, and C = 1 is odd. The pair-form replacement by modulus 8uB is correct when its positivity condition is retained.
   Second, relaxing the exponent produces a relaxed numerator-8 statement, not R₈ positivity. The four quoted R₈ zeros actually have small numerator-8 k = 2 witnesses. With m = (d+1)/8 and r = −n mod d, the tuples (n,d,r,m) are:
   `(10301993881,1495,289,187)`, `(10529331121,239,60,30)`, `(10532106649,79,25,10)`, `(10868474401,2207,828,276)`.
   In each case r divides m². Even 409 has `(d,r,m)=(63,32,8)` although R₈(409)=0. Thus those zeros do **not** refute the relaxed rungs.
   **Fix:** give the exact C-even divisibility condition. Separate the k = 1 R₈ screen from k ≥ 2 relaxations; mark the latter screen unestablished rather than false. Retain the legitimate conclusion that a full-family proof at threshold 10¹⁰ cannot simply remain valid for exact R₈ positivity. At the Salez threshold these finite zeros do not themselves refute any eventual statement.

4. **MAJOR — The composite character/modulus explanation drops essential unit hypotheses.**
   **Locations:** F1's “a hit modulus needs a prime ℓ with χ_n(ℓ) = −1”; §2's claims that a factor of n “excludes every modulus divisible by it” and that this explains the composite excess.
   The d-form always has gcd(n,d)=1, but the pair-form for composites need not have gcd(n,4uB)=1 or gcd(u,B)=1. For an explicit example **inside 𝔐**, take `(u,B,C)=(17,918,1)`. Then n = 61,489 ≡ 169 (840), q = 62,424 = 2³·3³·17², and n+u+B=q. Nevertheless `(2/n)=1`, `(3/n)=1`, and `(17/n)=0`: this pair hit contains **no** prime with χ_n(ℓ)=−1. A factor of n therefore does not delete every pair modulus divisible by it. The unconditional identity χ_{−4uB}(−u−B)=−1 is likewise false without coprimality (it is zero here).
   **Fix:** restrict the non-residue and modulus-deletion explanations to d-forms, or to coprime pair forms. Establish F1 for arbitrary composite n using the d-form Jacobi obstruction, which remains valid. Recast the claim that small factors *cause* the entire observed tail excess as a hypothesis unless a decomposition including non-unit pair hits is measured. Do not add a gcd filter to the exact composite decider: it correctly permits these solutions.

5. **MAJOR — F3's finite counterexamples do not force an eventual proof to use 𝔐-membership or fail outside 𝔐.**
   **Locations:** F3; §2 “Reading”; §6 proposed FRONTIER text and bottom line.
   The counts 285/270/265/15/20 are correct, but the conclusion is not. Fifteen of the 285 F3 composites are **inside** 𝔐. More importantly, a finite list below 8·10⁶ (or the larger finite lists here) refutes an unqualified, threshold-free assertion, not an assertion valid above a sufficiently large threshold. An eventual theorem for *all* non-square n ≡ 1 (4) could accommodate these exceptions without using 𝔐 at all. The memo correctly allows a threshold to remove the 18 interior exceptions, then incorrectly denies the same escape to the exterior exceptions.
   **Fix:** replace “must fail outside it or at multiples of 3,5,7” and “F3 is a statement about classes outside 𝔐” by the scoped observation: known finite composite exceptions do not exclude a primality-blind proof on 𝔐 with an adequate threshold. They also do not exclude a still broader eventual theorem. Preserve 𝔐 as a useful sufficient domain, not a necessary mechanism inferred from this census.

6. **MAJOR — Fixed-D congruence densities are conflated with uniform growing-D control and extreme-value predictions.**
   **Location:** §1 “Reading,” and its consequences in §§0,5,6.
   For fixed D and sufficiently large p, μ(p)>D is indeed a finite congruence event. An asymptotic density F(D) among primes in 𝔐 follows from the **prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions**, not just Dirichlet's infinitude theorem. This is a limit with D fixed. It supplies no uniform error for D growing as p^0.4, or even for the very large modulus lcm(840, all relevant m≤D). The finite table does not establish that such densities already describe the moving extreme tail. The fit uses three points and three coefficients, so it has no residual degrees of freedom; extrapolating from D≤10⁴ to D≈10⁷, and then to astronomical p, is particularly fragile. The equation F(D*)=1/N is a heuristic expected-exceedance criterion, not a bound on a maximum or on an every-prime covering.
   **Fix:** explicitly separate the fixed-D density theorem, observed finite-range proportions, and the unvalidated growing-D/extreme-value model. Say “the model predicts,” not “the hardest primes need,” at 10¹⁷. Treat `D* ≈ exp(2.66√log p)` as a leading-order logarithmic heuristic, not an asymptotic equivalent with controlled multiplicative error. Do not export model-derived exponents as established size requirements.

9. **MINOR — The quoted 10¹³³ crossover is only a leading-order estimate, not the crossover of the fitted model.**

10. **MINOR — The C programs need explicit supported ranges and input/allocation checks; capk silently fails on large accepted integers.**

## Proof and implementation audit: what survives

- **Lemma 1.1(a): correct for all positive integer n, prime or composite.** In the d-form, r≥1 and r|(d+1)/4 give positive integral u,C; divisibility and n+r>0 give B≥1. A solution has u≤(d+1)/4<d, so the least-residue reconstruction is unique. The pair converse explicitly includes `n+u+B≥4uB`; mdec implements it. No coprimality assumption is needed for these converses.
- **Lemma 1.1(b): correct.** `uB≥u+B−1` and C≥1 imply `n≥3(u+B)−4`, hence `n+u+B≤4(n+1)/3`. The product of the unsubtracted quantities is `4(n+u+B)²`; subtracting 1 from two only decreases the minimum. `(64/9)^{1/3}=1.9229994270765445<1.9231`. At X=10⁸ the displayed sufficient cap is about 414,319.338, below 420,000. Scanning both m≡3 (4) d-forms and m≡0 (4) pair forms through the bound is therefore an exact positivity decision for composites as well as primes. Role-swapping is covered by the same d-form scan.
- **Lemma 1.1(c) and Proposition 1.2: correct after making the automatic prime hypotheses explicit in finding 8.** The Jacobi product −1 supplies an odd non-residue prime divisor of d, even if d is composite. Reciprocity uses p≡1 (4). The pair proof supplies a prime factor of uB, possibly 2. Thus each of the three moduli is at least n_p. The beyond-Burgess implication is genuine; no converse or proof of SC(D) follows. The literature comparison is explicitly an Assessment, not an exhaustive priority claim verified by this review.
- **Lemma 3.1: correct, including k=∞.** `rad(r)|m` ensures gcd(r,d)=1. For each odd ℓ|r, reciprocity against the odd composite denominator d is valid and gives (ℓ/d)=1; if 2|r, m is even and d≡7 (8), so (2/d)=1. Hence (−r/d)=−1, inconsistent with a coprime square. In fact the argument excludes even squares too. The d cutoff is sufficient for R^(1)=R; for k>1 it is an imposed part of the relaxation's definition, not a bound derived anew from r|m^k.
- **mdec enumeration: correct in the advertised range.** At B=10¹¹, `capmax=41,431,935`, SPF indices are covered, and all q=m/4 are at most about 10.36 million. Their maximum divisor count is 448 (at q=8,648,640), safely below 4096; the prime/exponent arrays are ample. The divisor-building loops enumerate every divisor exactly once, including q=1. The extra +1/+2 cap margins are safe, and the numerical coefficient has ample slack over the exact constant. Arithmetic here fits uint64; modular multiplication uses uint128. The twelve prime Miller–Rabin bases through 37 are deterministic on uint64; the known seven-base prefix already suffices well beyond 10¹¹. This is not a probable-prime census.
- **mu sieve: correct solution/minimum enumeration in the reported range.** Starting at d−u and q−u−B enforces B≥1 and C≥1, respectively. No negative-start underflow occurs for positive parameters. The two minima jointly cover the three symmetric moduli. Its defects are the tail accounting and unsupported-input handling described above.
- **capk divisibility: correct in the reported range.** Each gcd stripping pass removes `min(vℓ(r),vℓ(m))`; k passes test r|m^k without computing m^k. The unbounded loop terminates and tests radical support. Its inclusive d cutoff and all reproduced table counts agree with the definition.
- **Ladder implications:** L2^𝔐(P)⇒MS(P) and “proved S with S⇒L2 proves L2” are valid. No invalid L6⇒L5 or L5⇒L4 implication is asserted. The multiplicative-transfer observation is valid via `(u,B,C)↦(ku,kB,C/k)` when k|C; writing that full map would make the claim clearer. It does not produce primes from composite factors.
