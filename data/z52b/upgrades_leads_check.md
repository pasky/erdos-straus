1. **EXISTS-AS-DESCRIBED** — Enrique Treviño, “The Burgess inequality and the least kth power non-residue,” *Int. J. Number Theory* 11 (2015), 1653–1678, [arXiv:1412.3062](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.3062). Gives explicit Burgess character-sum estimates with constants depending on the parameters/range; its quadratic-character all-prime explicit constant is 3.38 (revised from 3.64), not one universal constant for all cases. [source](https://www.ams.org/journals/proc/2020-148-05/S0002-9939-2020-14766-1/)
2. **EXISTS-DIFFERENTLY** — Yonatan Harpaz, “Integral points on conic log K3 surfaces,” *J. Eur. Math. Soc.* 21 (2019), 1501–1535, [arXiv:1511.04876](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04876). Gives explicit sufficient conditions for integral points on certain schemes fibred into affine conics (including some log K3 surfaces); it is conditional on stated arithmetic/geometric hypotheses, not an unconditional theorem and not simply a Schinzel-conjecture result. [journal](https://ems.press/journals/jems/articles/15880)
3. **EXISTS-AS-DESCRIBED** — Lilian Matthiesen, “Linear correlations of multiplicative functions,” *Proc. Lond. Math. Soc.* (3) 121 (2020), no. 2, 372–425, [arXiv:1606.04482](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04482). Proves Green–Tao-type asymptotics for fixed systems of pairwise non-proportional linear forms and fixed multiplicative functions; it does not allow coefficients to grow with the summation range. [journal](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1112/plms.12309)
4. **EXISTS-AS-DESCRIBED** — Sary Drappeau, “Théorèmes de type Fouvry–Iwaniec pour les entiers friables,” *Compositio Math.* 151 (2015), no. 5, 828–862, [arXiv:1307.7554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.7554). Establishes Bombieri–Friedlander–Iwaniec/Fouvry–Iwaniec-type distribution results for smooth (friable) integers in arithmetic progressions, at beyond-square-root levels under its smoothness and averaging conditions. [journal](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X14007933)
5. **EXISTS-DIFFERENTLY** — John B. Friedlander and Henryk Iwaniec, “Exceptional characters and prime numbers in arithmetic progressions,” *Int. Math. Res. Not.* 2003, no. 37, 2033–2050. It is conditional on an exceptionally strong Siegel-zero hypothesis and derives expected prime-counting asymptotics in progressions in that setting; it is not an unconditional level-of-distribution theorem. [journal](https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article-abstract/2003/37/2033/863200)
6. **EXISTS-DIFFERENTLY** — H. W. Lenstra, Jr., “Divisors in residue classes,” *Math. Comp.* 42 (1984), no. 165, 331–340: if 0≤r<s<n, gcd(r,s)=1 and s>n^(1/3), at most 11 positive divisors d|n satisfy d≡r (mod s). David Coppersmith, Nick Howgrave-Graham, and S. V. Nagaraj, “Divisors in residue classes, constructively,” *Math. Comp.* 77 (2008), 531–545: constructive enumeration under s≥n^α for any fixed α>1/4; this is not the same unconditional “at most 11” bound at threshold n^(1/4). [Lenstra](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1984b/art.pdf) [CHN](https://www.jstor.org/stable/40234519)
7. **EXISTS-AS-DESCRIBED** — Christian Elsholtz, “A combinatorial approach to sums of two squares and related problems,” in *Additive Number Theory: Festschrift in Honor of the Sixtieth Birthday of M. B. Nathanson*, Springer, 2010, 115–140. It develops elementary/combinatorial approaches to classical representation results, especially primes represented by binary quadratic forms and the two-squares theorem. [chapter](https://ideas.repec.org/h/spr/sprchp/978-0-387-68361-4_8.html)
8. **EXISTS-DIFFERENTLY** — Louis J. Mordell, “On the number of solutions in positive integers of the equation yz+zx+xy=n,” *Amer. J. Math.* 45 (1923), 1–4. The familiar class-number formula is for prime n: the number of positive solutions is 3G(n)−3 in Mordell’s notation; related weighted counts for nonnegative solutions differ, so there is no such simple formula for arbitrary n. [paper](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2387223)
9. **EXISTS-AS-DESCRIBED** — Andreas Strömbergsson, “On the uniform equidistribution of long closed horocycles,” *Duke Math. J.* 123 (2004), no. 3, 507–547. Gives effective uniform equidistribution; in particular, every subsegment longer than ℓ^(1/2+ε) of a closed horocycle of length ℓ equidistributes as ℓ→∞ (square-root threshold, with power-saving error in the effective estimates). [journal](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/duke-mathematical-journal/volume-123/issue-3/On-the-uniform-equidistribution-of-long-closed-horocycles/10.1215/S0012-7094-04-12334-6.full)
10. **EXISTS-AS-DESCRIBED** — Kathrin Bringmann and Jan Manschot, “From sheaves on P² to a generalization of the Rademacher expansion,” *Amer. J. Math.* 135 (2013), no. 4, 1039–1065, [arXiv:1006.0915](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.0915). Derives exact Rademacher-type expansions for Fourier coefficients of the generating functions of Euler characteristics of moduli spaces of rank-two sheaves on P², involving mixed mock modular forms. [journal](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/517110)
11. **EXISTS-DIFFERENTLY** — The named foundational results include Hiroshi Mikawa, “On the sum of a prime and a square,” *Tsukuba J. Math.* 17 (1993), no. 2, 299–310, and Alberto Perelli & János Pintz, “The exceptional set for the sum of a prime and a square,” *Acta Math. Hungar.* 53 (1989), 347–365. The best unconditional bound located is Hongze Li, “The exceptional set for the sum of a prime and a square,” *Acta Math. Hungar.* 99 (2003), 123–142: E(X) ≪ X^0.982; these are exceptional-set results, not a proof for every large nonsquare. [Mikawa](https://www.jstor.org/stable/43685851) [Li](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1024513613734)
12. **EXISTS-AS-DESCRIBED** — Jimmy Tseng, “Shrinking target equidistribution of horocycles in cusps,” *Math. Z.* 302 (2022), 1201–1230, [arXiv:2106.00836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00836); Dmitry Kelmer, “Shrinking targets problems for flows on homogeneous spaces,” *Israel J. Math.* 229 (2019), 227–250, [arXiv:1708.08953](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08953). Tseng proves shrinking-cusp-target equidistribution for long closed horocycles (including an effective theorem); Kelmer treats shrinking targets/logarithm laws for unipotent flows and cusp excursions. [Tseng](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00209-022-03118-0) [Kelmer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08953)
