# Z2431 — composite-modulus sum-product checked against the exact slice

**PROVED algebra; Assessment of applicability. ES is OPEN.**

The cited paper exists: Jean Bourgain, *Exponential sum estimates over subgroups of Z_q^*, q arbitrary*, J. Anal. Math. 97 (2005), 317–355, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02807410 (publisher metadata visited and saved). The accessible full text of Bourgain's 2007 follow-up, https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.crma.2007.01.019.pdf, states its theorem precisely in §2, Remark (2): for every ε>0, a subgroup H≤(Z/q)^× of size >q^ε has max_{ξ∈(Z/q)^×}|Σ_{h∈H}e_q(ξh)|<q^(−δ)|H|, δ=δ(ε)>0, with arbitrary q. This is primitive additive frequencies only. That text's Theorem 1 gives |A+A|+|AA|>q^δ₃|A| if |A|<q^(1−δ₁) and |π_{q₁}A|>q₁^δ₂ for every q₁|q above q^ε. Its Theorem 2/Corollary 3 require nonconcentration of every large divisor projection; small multiplicative doubling alone is insufficient. It cites Bourgain–Chang, GAFA 16 (2006), 327–366, for composite moduli with few factors.

Put q=4C, N=qp+1. The repo's (★) slice is
 p=4Cab−a−b ⇔ (qa−1)(qb−1)=N.
Let P_N=∏_{ℓ^v||N}{1,ℓ,…,ℓ^v} in (Z/q)^×, with exponents independently bounded by v. Then existence of positive a,b is exactly
 0 ∈ {1}+P_N in Z/q,
i.e. −1∈P_N (a divisor d≡−1(q) has complementary divisor in the same class). This is the requested additive translate intersecting a product set. If interior words u,v≥2 are required, additionally exclude factors q−1; the impossibility of applying sum-product already occurs before that restriction. The weakened modular product relation alone, (−1)(−1)=1, loses the exact integer equation de=N.

**Naming correction.** The literal ET Type I condition is 4abd=pe+1, a+b=ce. For a fixed (d,e), N_e=(pe+1)/(4d), it asks for a paired factorisation ab=N_e with a+b≡0(e), i.e. 0 in a divisor-pair sum modulo e. In Z/(4d), only pe≡−1 remains. The q=4C,N=qp+1 divisor slice in this item's hint is (★), a Type II subfamily in ET's convention; calling it Type I does not make those different equations equivalent. Both versions have the same bounded-exponent-box versus subgroup mismatch.

At C≈p^θ with fixed θ>0, N≈p^(1+θ), so |P_N|≤τ(N)=p^o(1), whereas q^ε=p^(θε). Thus the subgroup size hypothesis fails for the actual set for every fixed ε>0; Theorem 1's projection-size hypothesis fails already at q₁=q. Enlarging P_N to the subgroup generated by its prime factors may satisfy a size hypothesis, but allows arbitrary exponents and no longer produces a divisor of N. Even additive Fourier cancellation for that larger subgroup does not force −1 into the original exponent box or furnish a two-summand target identity. For C=p^o(1), the cardinality contradiction disappears, but subgroup and projection/nonconcentration hypotheses must still be proved, and expansion is not the required target-hit theorem.

This verifies and sharpens BRAINSTORM/gon_incidence_kloosterman.md §3(c3), whose decisive line is: “D is not a subgroup but a box in H_e”; its finite-field statement cannot be silently upgraded to arbitrary q, but the actual composite-modulus theorems do not cure the mismatch either. No explicit θ>0 is reached by this application. The original 2005 full proof was not downloaded; the theorem used is quoted verbatim by its author in the fully read 2007 paper.
