## AA: source-level audit and re-derivation

1. **BDH [S1].** Equation (1) states Σ_{q≤Q}Σ_{c*}|ψ(t;q,c)−t/φ(q)|² ≪_M tQ log t for t(log t)^−M ≤ Q ≤ t. For the memo's smaller Q, enlarge it to Q′=t(log t)^−(A+1) and use positivity. This gives exactly t²(log t)^−A whenever Q≤Q′. The same paper proves the number-field generalization, with the classical case K=ℚ. This independently verifies the needed statement; it does not rely on a remembered Davenport chapter number.
2. **Conversion to π.** For each q let Δ_c=ψ(t;q,c)−θ(t;q,c)≥0. Then Σ_c Δ_c²≤(Σ_c Δ_c)²≪t log²t, hence Σ_{q≤Q,c}Δ_c²≪Qt log²t. A pointwise bound alone would not justify this step. In the crude θ estimate, the main-term squares summed over c are t²/φ(q), so their q-sum is O(t² log(2Q)), not O(t²). The displayed bound O((t²+Qt)log²(2tQ)) nevertheless follows. Partial summation and the split t₀=Q(log x)^(A+1) then give the claimed arbitrarily strong logarithmic saving; x^(−η) absorbs every fixed logarithmic loss.
3. **CRT averaging.** For squarefree d, assigning each root to d=d₁d₂ overcounts harmlessly at common roots. The congruence restrictions a≡0,b≡1 mod 4 give multiplicity at most (H/(4d₁)+1)(H/(4d₂)+1)≤H²/(16d)+H. There is no hidden average over a fixed residue. With 3^ω(d)τ(d)=6^ω(d), Cauchy–Schwarz costs only a fixed log power: its square is 36^ω(d). Thus the two prefactors are O(H² log^C x) and O(HD log^C x); D=H/8 makes both acceptable. The O(ω(d)) errors also vanish after summation. Markov gives (3.3).
4. **Lower sieve [S2].** Nath–Xie (3.4), Lemma 3.6, Remark 3.7 and §5 give the product-condition beta sieve, β(1)=2, and f(s)=2e^γ log(s−1)/s for 2≤s≤4. Its combinatorial weights have absolute value ≤1, so the memo's 3^ω-weighted remainder is more than sufficient. Here g(3)≤1/2 and g(ℓ)≤2/(ℓ−1) for inert ℓ≥7. The upper product axiom has an absolute constant independent of a,b; no two-sided density asymptotic is required. Consequently s−2≥2η and XV(z)f(s)≥c₁η x/log²x uniformly.
5. **Mertens input [S2, p.19].** The source states Σ_{ℓ<t,ℓ≡3(4)}1/ℓ=(1/2)log log t+C+O(1/log t). Taking differences and expanding logarithms gives the dimension-1 product above and the dimension-5/2 product below. Combining this with ordinary Mertens gives the split-prime version. The same source uses the split-prime weighted estimate on p.20. Hence the q₁-sum is O(η), and the Euler-product bound for Σ_{m∈S,m≤5x^(2η)}h(m)/m is O(√(η log x)), with absolute constants once x≥x₀(η).
6. **Survivors and upper sieve [S3].** Both shifts are odd and 1 mod 4. Since 5x<z³, each survivor has zero or exactly two inert factors, counted with multiplicity. In the switched k-variable, the generic root numbers are 1 at 2, 2 at 3, 2 at split primes, and 3 at inert primes ≥7. Admissibility prevents three roots at 3. At primes dividing m or ab(4a−b), deleting roots only improves the product axiom. Ford's (Ω) and Theorem 2.4 give O(YV(w))+Σ_{d≤w}|r_d| with constants depending only on the dimension/product constants; |r_d|≤3^ω(d) makes the error O(w log²w). Since Y≥x^0.4 and w=x^0.01 this is negligible. For the mirror case, first put k=k₀+4j: at 2 only the primality condition on p removes one j-class; odd-prime root counts are unchanged. Discarding p≤w makes survival legitimate even for large a,b.
7. **Uniform local factor.** All collisions are supported on m·ab(4a−b), with each ratio bounded by 1+6/ℓ as claimed. This yields C₂K_{a,b}η^(3/2)x/log²x for each bad set. Since 4a−b≠0 on 𝒫_H, K is defined. The three-line count modulo each prime is ≤3ℓ. Expanding the product below L=100 log H gives the convergent sum Σ18^ω(d)/d² and the harmless error 19^π(L)=H^o(1); primes above L contribute at most 2 eventually. Thus ΣK_{a,b}≪H² without any unproved distribution assertion.
8. **Quantifiers and positivity.** Choose K=4C₃/ε, then η≤(c₁/(8C₂K))² and η≤1/10. The two exceptional sets each have size ≤εH²/4. Outside them, the main coefficient exceeds c₁η/4 before removing 2w. This proves AA with c=c₁η/8. Its constants and threshold can depend on ε; it never selects a fixed pair. The admissible density 7/144 follows from seven of nine residue pairs modulo 3 and the modulo-4 restrictions.

## Required fixes, in priority order

1. **Keep AA; repair only the two explanatory estimates in Step 2′** as recorded above, and give explicit source locators [S1–S3]. No new distribution conjecture is needed.
2. **Demote the hybrid implications to a proposed route.** For example, g=e=7,d=1 occurs in 𝒟 for large x: c≡−4≡3 (mod 7), but 4c+1≡0 requires c≡5 (mod 7). Thus c(g,d,e) does not exist. Compatible tuples require an explicit treatment; merely omitting incompatible tuples still does not justify dropping the genuine d sharing factors with g. Divisor pairing also introduces π evaluated at g²d²-dependent endpoints, not just x. Supply a hypothesis covering the actual expansion and its endpoints, evaluate the local densities, and prove/assume S before claiming a conditional theorem. The explicitly unproved Poisson/Weil sketch does not supply S.
3. **Replace hardness/impossibility language by analogy and a scoped failure of the methods used.** FI [S4] and Sedunova [S5] support the quoted det-1 status, not a reduction to this det-15 problem or a proof that prime distribution is its only obstruction.
