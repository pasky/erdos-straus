        An improved exceptional-set bound for the Erdős–Straus equation
                          via saturated divisor fans
                       Sushaan Kandukoori                      Shrikrishna Ramesh                     Pranava Kumar
                                                                July 29, 2026

                                                                    Abstract
         Let 𝐸 (𝑁) be the number of integers 2 ≤ 𝑛 ≤ 𝑁 for which 4/𝑛 = 1/𝑥 + 1/𝑦 + 1/𝑧 has no solution in positive integers. Vaughan
      proved 𝐸 (𝑁) ≪ 𝑁 exp{−𝑐(log 𝑁) 2/3 }. We prove

                                                     𝐸 (𝑁) ≪ 𝑁 exp −𝑐(log 𝑁) 3/4 .
                                                                    

      The improvement comes from the Type II divisor fan: for each 𝑎 ≥ 1, every divisor of 𝑎 2 supplies a congruence class modulo 4𝑎 − 1
      of representable 𝑛, and the classical representing congruences are the boundary cases of this family. A conductor-separated subfan
      keeps a mass of order (log 𝑋) 3 , and a finite-interval sieve turns this mass into the stated saving at the scale log 𝑋 ≍ (log 𝑁) 1/4 .
          We also record consequences for prime denominators, for propagation along large prime factors, and for the Erdős–Straus–Schinzel
      problem with fixed numerator 𝑚 ≥ 2, together with a geometric interpretation on the associated log 𝐾3 surfaces. Variants with
      growing numerator or at Elliott–Halberstam strength are stated under explicit additional hypotheses.

1    Introduction and main theorem
The Erdős–Straus conjecture asserts that for every integer 𝑛 ≥ 2 the equation
                                                                4 1 1 1
                                                                 = + +                                                                          (1)
                                                                𝑛 𝑥 𝑦 𝑧
has a solution in positive integers. Let 𝐸 (𝑁) count the exceptions 2 ≤ 𝑛 ≤ 𝑁. Vaughan proved the classical estimate

                                                       𝐸 (𝑁) ≪ 𝑁 exp{−𝑐(log 𝑁) 2/3 }

for some absolute 𝑐 > 0 [16]. Our main result improves the exponent. The same construction also recovers a logarithmic factor that a
one-rough-coordinate sieve loses, through the small-prime saturation device described in Remark 12.1.
Theorem 1.1 (main theorem). There is an absolute constant 𝑐 > 0 such that, for all 𝑁 ≥ 3,

                                             𝐸 (𝑁) ≪ 𝑁 exp −𝑐(log 𝑁) 3/4 .
                                                             

  The proof covers residue classes by congruence certificates. Each selected certificate gives a residue class modulo some 𝑞, and
every integer in that class has an explicit representation. The difficulty is to keep cubic total weight while making the residual
intersections sparse enough for Brun–Suen: small-prime factors are absorbed into the base class, while conductor separation keeps a
cubic mass of residual events. We estimate the number of integers missed by all selected classes.
  The unconditional consequences include the geometric form in Corollary 2.5. In Section 13 we also record an exceptional-
prime bound with the same exponential saving, a large-prime-factor propagation theorem, and a fixed-numerator version for the
Erdős–Straus–Schinzel equation.
  The growing-numerator statements are separated from these consequences. They assume the uniform numerator-saturation
package introduced in Hypothesis 13.5, and the Elliott–Halberstam variant also assumes the reciprocal-prime distribution hypothesis
in Hypothesis 13.8. Under those stated assumptions, the growing-numerator theorem improves the Vaughan-type Pomerance–
Weingartner exponent in a logarithmic range of numerators [13].
  The structural starting point is the complete Type II divisor fan

                                                         4𝑎 − 1 | 𝑛 + 4ℎ,         ℎ | 𝑎2 .

The three traditional boundary rays ℎ = 1, 𝑎, 𝑎 2 recover the classical shifts appearing in the work of Mordell, Yamamoto, and Webb
[12, 19, 18], but the interior divisors carry cubic logarithmic mass. We also prove the fixed-modulus no-obstruction statement for

  Vaughan’s exceptional-set sieve is the comparison point for the saving, while representation-count results situate the construction
among related work on the Erdős–Straus equation. Elsholtz and Tao proved average-order bounds for the number of representations
over primes [5], and Elsholtz–Planitzer and Bloom–Elsholtz give further results on representation counts and Egyptian fractions
[6, 2]. Bright and Loughran studied the associated surfaces through the Brauer–Manin obstruction [3]. Pomerance and Weingartner
recently treated exceptions to the Erdős–Straus–Schinzel generalization and generalized Vaughan-type almost-all results [13].
  The proof here keeps Vaughan’s exceptional-set-covering viewpoint, but changes the certificate family. The dense divisor fan is
thinned to a conductor-separated subfan, while the small-prime part of the medium coordinate is saturated. This keeps the logarithmic
mass that a one-rough-coordinate sieve would lose.
  The same result has a geometric interpretation. Let

                                              𝑈𝑛 :    4𝑋𝑌 𝑍 = 𝑛(𝑋𝑌 + 𝑋 𝑍 + 𝑌 𝑍) ⊂ A3

be the affine Erdős–Straus surface in the positive chamber, and let 𝑆 𝑛 be its projective cubic compactification

                                              4𝑋𝑌 𝑍 = 𝑛(𝑋𝑌𝑊 + 𝑋 𝑍𝑊 + 𝑌 𝑍𝑊) ⊂ P3 .

Bright and Loughran identify these as affine cubic log 𝐾3 surfaces, all geometrically isomorphic after scaling, and study their Cayley
nodal cubic compactification and desingularisation [3]. In this language, Theorem 1.1 says that outside 𝑂 (𝑁 exp{−𝑐(log 𝑁) 3/4 })
parameters 𝑛 ≤ 𝑁, the integral log surface 𝑈𝑛 has an integral point in the positive real chamber. Theorem 2.4 below shows that the
divisor-fan certificates lie on explicit rational plane sections of the Cayley cubic.
  Section 6 collects the standard analytic estimates used below.
Formalization. A companion Lean 4 development [11] formalizes the deduction of Theorem 1.1 from nine named theorems, either
cited here or proved in the manuscript. It verifies the certificate identities, parameter inequalities, mass and tensorisation estimates,
dependency bounds, finite transfer, optimization, and smooth lifting. The weighted reciprocal Bombieri–Vinogradov estimate and
the long squarefree progression estimates are stated in the exact forms used here rather than derived from more primitive versions

14     Future directions
The cubic mass is intrinsic to the three-coordinate split 𝑒 = 𝑟 𝑠2 , 𝑑 = 𝑑 − 𝑑+ , 𝑄 = 𝑑𝑝. The main open problem suggested
by Theorem 13.11 is to construct a quartic saturated packet unconditionally, which would push the exponent to 𝐸 (𝑁) ≪
𝑁 exp{−𝑐 4 (log 𝑁) 4/5 }; the obstruction is a fourth independent coordinate that retains logarithmic mass without inflating the
dependency graph. A different route would replace the second-moment Brun–Suen input, which limits the per-class saving to 1 − 𝑜(1),
by a higher-cluster correlation estimate controlling triple and higher overlaps directly. Separately, the growing-numerator results of
Section 13 are conditional on the uniform-saturation and reciprocal Elliott–Halberstam hypotheses; making either unconditional,
even for a restricted range of 𝑚, would remove the conditionality from Theorems 13.6 and 13.9.
