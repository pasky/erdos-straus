# z81-cap-zero literature hunt (2025-01-01..2026-11-22)

Scope: looked for shifted-smooth primes, small-prime generation of unit groups, and restricted-denominator Egyptian fractions. The central gap remains pointwise production of primes r with r−1 dividing the lane’s structured L; none of these papers gives that input. Proofs not audited except for statements explicitly qualified by the sources.

## Relevant hits

- **arXiv:2609.24347, Zhiyuan Yang, “The lower bound of shifted primes with large prime factors”** (`src/2609.24347.txt`, Theorem 1.1, lines 75–83). Exact: “For any 0 < c ≤ 1/2, we have … lim inf_{x→∞} T_c(x)/π(x) ≥ max(1−ρ(16/(5c)), 1−c+g(c)), where g(c)>0 for 11/32<c≤1/2, and g(c)=0 for 0<c≤11/32. In particular, g(1/2)>0.0436.” Here T_c counts p≤x with P⁺(p−1)≥p^c (definition in lines 54–64). **Averaging:** density over primes up to x; not fixed p or smooth shifts. **Verdict: INSPIRATION / NULL** for cap-zero: informative obstruction/frequency bound on large factors, but no positive supply of sufficiently smooth p−1, let alone p−1|L. **Trap:** lower bound for large-factor cases cannot be complemented into a structured smooth-prime theorem.

- **arXiv:2610.15674, Li–Zhuang, “Coprime pairs with equal sum of divisors, assuming smooth shifted primes”** (`src/2610.15674.txt`, lines 41–64). Exact quoted Theorem 1.1 (attributed to an OpenAI preprint, not this paper): “For every fixed 0<δ<1/4, as x→∞, #{p prime: 2x<p≤5x, P⁺(p−1)≤x^δ}≥x^{1−o(1)}.” Their Theorem 1.4 (lines 58–64) says, conditional on correctness of that argument, the analogous p+1 count holds on 2x−2<p≤5x. **Averaging:** many primes in a dyadic-scale interval, not pointwise at a prescribed modulus/class. **Verdict: INSPIRATION (conditional reported input)**: strongest directly relevant smooth-shifted-prime claim found, but only says largest prime factor is small relative to p; does not give r−1|L or the exact common-exponent structure. **Trap:** source explicitly calls the input unrefereed/unverified; do not promote as established. Theorem 1.3 is only a conditional implication for divisor-sum pairs, not an ES result.

- **arXiv:2507.03727, Czenky–McGovern–Plavnik–Rowell–Watkins, “Egyptian Fractions for Few Primes”** (`src/2507.03727.txt`, Corollary 2.9, lines 198–202). Exact: “If there exists a solution in rank R, then p−1 divides R−1.” The setting is representations of 1 as a sum of unit fractions whose denominators are powers of one fixed prime p (defined at lines 184–198); Theorem 2.11, lines 204–210, gives existence when R is an “admissible rank.” **Averaging:** none; fixed p, exact denominator restriction, arbitrary rank R. **Verdict: INSPIRATION** for the common-exponent divisibility barrier only: it exhibits precisely a p−1 divisibility constraint in a restricted Egyptian-fraction setting. **Trap:** not three terms and not m/n=1/x+1/y+1/z with bounded denominator set; divisibility of rank−1 does not supply a prime divisor of a prescribed L.

- **arXiv:2609.25393, Klurman–Shparlinski–Teräväinen, “On the generation of multiplicative groups by small primes”** (`src/2609.25393.txt`, Theorem 1.1, lines 89–110). Exact: “There is an absolute constant C*≥1 … Let ε∈(0,1) and A>0. For every Q≥3, there exists a set P⊆[1,(log Q)^{C*(A+1)}] of primes, with |P|≤(log Q)^{1+ε}, such that for all but O_{ε,A}(Q(log Q)^{−A}) positive integers q≤Q we have {∏_{p∈P}p^{a_p} mod q:a_p∈{0,1}}=(Z/qZ)×.” **Averaging:** almost all moduli q≤Q, with one common small-prime set; not a fixed q. **Verdict: ALREADY-COVERED** (explicitly known in lane brief/repo). Useful subset-product mechanism, but wrong direction: generation modulo q does not force a selected prime r with r−1|L. **Trap:** exceptional moduli and highly averaged quantifier.

- **arXiv:2610.04678, Scott D. Hughes, “Few small primes suffice to cover unit groups”** (`src/2610.04678.txt`, Theorem 1, lines 40–50; Theorem 2, lines 59–68). Exact Theorem 1: for every A>0 and sufficiently large Q there is a set of distinct primes with (log Q)^{A+3}<p≤(log Q)^{C_A}, cardinality ≤(log Q)/log 2+O_A(log Q/log log Q), covering every 2≤q≤Q outside O_A(Q(log Q)^{−A}) moduli. Theorem 2 removes additional uncovered moduli at cost ≈2(log Q)/log 2, with an explicitly described exceptional set. **Averaging:** almost all q≤Q, not fixed q; same globally selected prime set. **Verdict: ALREADY-COVERED** (brief identifies it; prior repo notes also cover it). Sharper count, but no pointwise divisor/shifted-prime link. **Trap:** covering units by subset products is not generation by divisors of a prescribed shifted integer.
