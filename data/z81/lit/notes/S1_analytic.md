# z81 analytic literature notes

Proofs not audited; statements below are transcribed/condensed from the indicated source `.txt` files. Line numbers refer to those files.

## Achenjang–Woo, arXiv:2611.16062 — prime values of linear forms and a quadratic form
- **Theorem A (source lines 135–162):** For `L_i ∈ Z[x,y]` whose homogeneous parts are pairwise linearly independent; `T_1,T_2 ∈ Z[t]` linear with leading coefficients `t_1,t_2`; imaginary quadratic `K=Q(α)` with `α` integral; and `Q(x,y)=N_{K/Q}(T_1(x)+T_2(y)α)`, they give
  `Σ_{x,y∈Z, Q(x,y)≤X} (∏_{i≤k}Λ(L_i(x,y))) 1_P(Q(x,y)) = S'_{L_1,…,L_k,Q} ρ_Q X/log X + O_{L_i,T_1,T_2,α}(X(log log X)^2/(log X)^2)`,
  where `ρ_Q=2π[O_K:Z[α]]^{-1}|disc(K)|^{-1/2}|t_1t_2|^{-1}` and `S'` is the stated truncated singular series (primes through `exp((log X)^{1/10k})`). If the full singular series is positive, infinitely many simultaneous prime tuples follow. The asymptotic is for the von Mangoldt-weighted count; the error is as displayed, not a pointwise-in-parameter estimate.
- **Scope clarifications:** Yes, `L_i` can be arbitrary affine linear forms subject to pairwise independent homogeneous parts (and local admissibility for the positive result); they need not be just coordinates. But the quadratic factor is specifically this norm form, with `T_1` depending on `x` and `T_2` on `y`. `Z[α]` need not be the maximal order. It is not a theorem for every definite binary quadratic form/discriminant. The authors say generalization to all nondegenerate binary quadratic forms, including indefinite ones, is anticipated to have no major difficulties (lines 163–165)—that is a remark, not their proved theorem. `K` is imaginary quadratic in the theorem, not arbitrary real quadratic.
- **Prime represented by a quadratic form plus prime shifts?** The theorem does count a norm-form prime simultaneously with several linear-form primes in the *same* `(x,y)`. It does not directly count `p=N(x+yα)` together with primality of prescribed shifts `p+c_i`: those shifts are quadratic polynomials in `(x,y)`, not eligible linear forms. Nor does it give simultaneous failure/nonrepresentation estimates for several shifted forms. Its sieve/semiprime, Type I and Type II machinery is internal to proving positive prime counts (contents lines 45–48); no reusable lower bound for primes avoiding several representation conditions is stated. Thus at most a technique lead for the z81 gap lane, not the required joint nonrepresentation bound.

## Gou–Wang–Xu, arXiv:2611.18825 — Landau’s problems through ratios
- **Exact conditional inputs:** Hyp. 1.1 `RCD(ω_D)` is a 2/2 ratios formula for primitive Dirichlet `L`-functions, all conductors `q>1`, shifts `Re(a)=Re(b)=1/2`, `Re(s)=Re(t)=1/2+η`, imaginary parts at most `100 log(2q)`, with error `O_{η,ε}(q^{1−ω_D+ε}(1+T)^B)` (source lines 78–108). Hyp. 1.2 `RCZ(ω_Z)` is the 2/2 CFZ ratios conjecture for zeta shifts, integrated over `0≤y≤Q`; `1/log Q≤Re(a),Re(b)≤1/16`, imaginary shifts up to `Q^{1/2}`, `|u|,|v|≤min(1/100,Re(a)/2,Re(b)/2)`, error `Oε(Q^{1−ω_Z+ε})` (lines 108–125). Hyp. 1.3 `RCH(ω_H)` is a 1/1 ratio for angular Hecke `L(s,ξ_k)` on `Q(i)`, averaged unweighted over `1≤k≤Q`, with `2/log Q≤Re(c)≤1/16`, `|Im c|≤Q^{3/4}`, `|a−c|≤1/100`, error `Oε(Q^{−ω_H+ε})` (lines 125–130). Each theorem assumes its own ratio input and the specified GRH/RH.
- **Theorems 1.1–1.4 (lines 131–173):** Goldbach: for every even `N`, `Σ_{m+n=N}Λ(m)Λ(n)=N S(N)+Oε(N^{1−ω_D+ε})` under GRH for Dirichlet L-functions + RCD. Prime pairs: for fixed `h≥1`, `Σ_{n≤X}Λ(n)Λ(n+h)=S(h)X+O_{h,ε}(X^{1−ω_Z+ε})` under RH + RCZ. Legendre: `ψ((n+1)^2)−ψ(n^2)=2n+1+Oε(n^{1−ω_Z/3+ε})`; also `p_{j+1}−p_j ≪ε p_j^{(1−ω_Z)/(2−ω_Z)+ε}` (at `ω_Z=1/2`, error `n^{5/6+ε}`, gap exponent `1/3+ε`). Quadratic prime values: `Σ_{n≤X}Λ(n²+1)=S_E X+Oε(X^{1−ω_H+ε})` under GRH for angular Hecke family + RCH. These are pointwise in the displayed `N`, fixed `h`, `n`, or `X`, but conditional on strong family-average ratio hypotheses; function-field theorems 1.5–1.6 are separate unconditional results over fixed large finite fields (lines 174–189).
- **BCR Conjecture 1 replacement plausibility:** Not an established implication. The paper’s ratios hypotheses yield power-saving explicit-formula information for particular prime correlations/weighted counts, so a ratios conjecture tailored to the relevant family might plausibly supply the missing normal-order input. But this paper does not treat the divisor-type statistic averaged over primes or prove that its ratios assumptions imply Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłł Conjecture 1. Different family, weights, and uniformity are essential; cannot claim replacement without deriving the needed moment/correlation formula.

## Grimmelt–Merikoski–Pandey–Pascadi, arXiv:2610.41356 — primes `n²+h`
- **Theorems 1.1–1.3 (source lines 62–119):** For `ν>0`, `H=X^{1/2−ν}`, all but `≪_C H/(log X)^C` shifts `h≤H` satisfy `Σ_{n²+h≤X}Λ(n²+h)=(1+O(ν))S_h X^{1/2}`; for `H=X^{1/2−o(1)}`, this is `(1+o(1))S_hX^{1/2}` (Thm. 1.1). For `X^{2/5}≤H≤X/2`, all but the same exceptional-set size satisfy the lower bound `ΣΛ(n²+h)≥0.098 S_h(X−h)^{1/2}` (Thm. 1.2). The Möbius cancellation theorem is an aggregate sum over `h≤H`, uniformly for `H=X^{1/2−ν}`, not fixed `h` (Thm. 1.3).
- **Pointwise?** No result for a prescribed fixed `h`; “all but few shifts” is the quantifier in the prime theorems. The aggregate Möbius result is explicitly averaged over shifts. The paper describes a Harman sieve with Type I, Type I₂, Type II and factorable Type I estimates (lines 135–186), including a Type II mechanism overcoming parity, but its key cancellation uses the `h`-average. Useful as sieve/Type I/II inspiration for averaged quadratic-prime questions, not a fixed-prime or simultaneous shifted nonrepresentation lower bound. This is an average over `h`, precisely the sort of averaging that does not bridge the campaign’s fixed-prime B2 barrier.

## Bettin–Drappeau–Lee, arXiv:2610.42008 — central values of additive twists
- This is **not** a paper on new Kloosterman-fraction sum bounds. Theorem 1.2 (source lines 130–150) proves a central limit theorem: for a discrete cofinite `Γ⊂SL(2,R)` with singular cusp at infinity and nonzero integer-weight automorphic form `φ`, under (a) cuspidal or `Re(s_φ)=1/2`, and (b) the stated holomorphic-form exclusion, the normalized values `L(x)/(σ_φ(log Q)^{1/2}(log log Q)^{β_φ/2})` over parabolic fixed points modulo `Z` with denominator `≤Q` converge to standard complex Gaussian; `β=0,1,3` in the three cases specified. Theorem 1.8 gives the corresponding example for the two-squares theta coefficients (lines 216–222).
- The main theorem’s averaging variable is the cusp/parabolic point `x`, ordered by denominator, not primes or a fixed modulus. The proof invokes an existing result “[BDL, Theorem 1.3]” (lines 1916, 1945; bibliography lines 3297ff), not a new bound in this paper. No reusable new bilinear/trilinear Kloosterman-fraction estimate for the z81 specific-class lane is asserted; the transfer-operator/limit-law machinery is not such an input.

## Kerr–Punch–Trudgian, arXiv:2611.08816 — Burgess without log
- **Theorem 1.1 (source lines 62–94):** For nonprincipal `χ mod prime p`, integer `r≥2`, `α>1`, `p>r^{2r/(2r−1)}`, and `N≤p^{1/2+1/(4r)}`, if explicit condition (1.2) holds, then `|Σ_{n≤N}χ(n)|≤C(r)N^{1−1/r}p^{(r+1)/(4r²)}`. Both `C(r)` and the threshold condition are explicit in the theorem. Corollary 1.2: for `N≤p^{5/8}`, bound `37N^{1/2}p^{3/16}` (explicit; illustration says condition holds for `p≥3×10^12`, `r=2, α=1.002`, `C(2)<37`).
- This removes the logarithmic factor in Burgess’s bound for prime moduli, with explicit constants; authors caution the result is unlikely useful in applications in its current range (lines 90–107). Character-sum ingredient potentially useful in residue-class products, but no direct Erdős–Straus fixed-prime count or multi-window lower bound.

## Banks–Shparlinski, arXiv:2610.19597 — EOS for prime-power moduli
- **Theorems 1.1–1.4 (source lines 81–129):** For fixed prime `p`, `q=p^γ` with `γ` large, if `q_•≤q` and `log q_•≥C₁(log q)^{2/3}(log log q)^{4/3}`, every reduced class `a mod q` has a weighted asymptotic for products `ℓ₁ℓ₂ℓ₃≡a`, `ℓ₁,ℓ₂≤q`, `ℓ₃≤q_•`: sum of `logℓ₁logℓ₂logℓ₃` equals `q²q_•/φ(q)` times `1+O(exp(−c₁ log q_•/((log q)^{2/3}(log log q)^{1/3})))` (Thm. 1.1). Thus `ℓ₃=q^{o(1)}` is possible. For the binary problem, the number of classes not representable by two primes `≤q` is `≤φ(q)exp(−C₂(log q)^{1/3}(log log q)^{−1/3})` (Thm. 1.2). For arbitrary `q`, conditional on `QRH[q,σ₀]` (all nonprincipal `L(s,χ mod q)` zero-free for `Re(s)>σ₀`), every class is a product of two primes `≤q` and a third `≪(log q)^{2/(1−σ₀)}` (Thm. 1.3); binary missing classes `≪φ(q)q^{−2(1−σ₀)}(log q)^3` (Thm. 1.4).
- **Reusable residue-class lemma?** Theorem 1.1 is directly a uniform weighted existence/asymptotic result for representing *each* prescribed reduced residue class by a product of three primes for prime-power moduli; the proof is character orthogonality plus prime sums. Could inspire a product-of-primes lemma when the relevant modulus is a fixed prime power and the target class is reduced. It does not establish that products hit a prescribed class for an arbitrary moving modulus unconditionally, nor does it produce the `−1` divisor class modulo a solution-dependent modulus needed at fixed `p` in B2. Binary theorem is only almost-all classes; the “all but” estimate cannot imply every class.
