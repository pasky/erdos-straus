## Geometry / logic papers

The theorem statements below are transcribed compactly from the opened local `.txt` files; line numbers refer to those files. **Proofs not audited.** None supplies a pointwise fixed-prime Erdős–Straus result.

### Jiang, “An explicit uniform connectivity bound for generalized Markoff graphs” (arXiv:2611.24452)
- **Main theorem:** Theorem 1.1: “For every prime `p > 10^320` and every `D ∈ F_p \ {4}`, the pure Vieta group `Γ` acts transitively on `S_D(p)`.” (`2611.24452.txt:38–39`; abstract scope at 10–16.)
- Theorem 1.2 additionally gives a weighted maximal-divisor estimate over **all odd integers** `P`, real `m≥1`, and `η≥0`, then a bound for `K_p(m)` (`:85–96`). This is an explicit finite-field connectivity threshold, not a statement about ES denominators.
- **Averaging/pointwise:** The transitivity claim is uniform and pointwise for every prime above threshold and every allowed parameter `D`; the divisor estimate is uniform in `P,m,η`. No averaging in primes is used in the theorem.
- **Lane/relevance — b2-duality:** Useful proof template only: Vieta orbit transitivity on Markoff-type cubic surfaces plus maximal-divisor control, and an explicit descent from a larger Nielsen action to pure Vieta moves. A possible lemma to investigate would be whether the ES cubic `4xyz=p(xy+yz+zx)` has an analogous finite-field orbit structure compatible with positive-integral lifts. The theorem itself does not establish that bridge or any moving-modulus `−1` divisor at fixed `p` (B2).
- **Trap:** Different surface and orbit set; mod-p transitivity is not positive-integral ES solvability. Huge explicit threshold still does not transfer to ES. Source lines 10–16, 29–39, 68–83.

### Berman–Kingsbury-Neuschotz, “Congruence patterns in spectral gaps of mod p Markoff graphs” (arXiv:2611.23168)
- **Main theorem:** Theorem 1.1: if `p≡1 (mod 4)`, `λ₂(p)>φ₁−o(1)` as `p→∞`, where `φ₁` is the unique real root of `x³−x²−4x−4`; in particular the lim inf exceeds `2√2`. For `p≡1,2,4 (mod 7)`, `λ₂(p)>φ₂−o(1)`, `φ₂` the unique real root of `x³+x²−7x−11` (`:180–200`). Numerics are `φ₁≈2.875129794`, `φ₂≈2.835975919` (`:189–196`).
- **Averaging/pointwise:** Asymptotic lower bounds along congruence families of primes, not an estimate for each fixed prime; proof uses integral orbits over number fields and reductions (`:9–30`, `:176–200`).
- **Lane/relevance — b2-duality:** Technique/analogy for studying Vieta-generated orbits and arithmetic-dependent Markoff dynamics, possibly informing orbit decomposition for the ES cubic. No directly usable ES lemma found: spectral eigenvalue lower bounds do not imply a solution or class-`−1` divisor.
- **Trap:** Markoff graph is not the ES solution surface; lower bounds on second eigenvalue are not expansion/upper bounds and do not yield pointwise representation. Source lines 9–30, 34–49, 176–204.

### Zhao, “Counting Rational Points on Cayley’s Cubic Surface” (arXiv:2611.14226)
- **Main theorem:** Theorem 1.1: for the split Cayley cubic `S`, its open subset `U` obtained by removing the nine lines, and maximum-coordinate height `H`, `N_U(B)=c_Peyre B(log B)^6+o(B(log B)^6)` as `B→∞` (`:103–115`; setup at `:34–47`). The abstract says the proof combines universal-torsor counting, two-dimensional lattice counts controlled by averages of reciprocal shortest-vector lengths/character moments, and Poisson summation (`:13–28`).
- **Averaging/pointwise:** Global average/count over rational points of height ≤B, with auxiliary torsor variables and lattice averages; emphatically not pointwise at a fixed `n` or prime. The paper explicitly notes the ES reciprocal embedding but that integrality, positivity and size constraints differ (`:79–91`).
- **Lane/relevance — geom-comb:** Potentially reusable toolkit for torsor/lattice enumeration: uniform two-dimensional lattice counts, reciprocal shortest-vector estimates, coprimality-preserving Poisson summation, and tail control. A concrete use would require extracting a uniform lattice lemma adapted to an ES numerator/denominator slice with positivity and the campaign’s moving-modulus condition.
- **Trap:** Rational-point height asymptotics on a projective surface do not imply positive integral ES points at a prescribed denominator; global averaging cannot overcome the B2 fixed-prime barrier. Source lines 13–28, 79–115.

### Bhargava–Burungale, “A Diophantine definition of Z in Q” (arXiv:2611.16130 v2)
- **Main theorem:** Theorem A: “`Z` is a Diophantine subset of `Q`. More precisely, the set `T_C0` is Diophantine over `Q` for any fixed integer `C0>1`”; for sufficiently large `C0`, `q∈Z` iff the stated witnesses `(u,a,b),s,t,z∈Q` satisfy `a≤q≤b` and `(g−u²)(1−g²s⁴)(1−g²t⁴)=gz²`, with `g=G(16q+8)` (`:83–86`; formal polynomial corollary `:87–96`).
- **Averaging/pointwise:** A uniform definability statement for all rational `q`, using existential rational witnesses; it is not a prime-average theorem. Its construction uses elliptic-curve arithmetic and a denominator/height bound (`:16–23`, `:77–86`).
- **Lane/relevance — farey-proof:** Potential logic toolkit for expressing integer/Farey data in weak arithmetic, and suggests testing whether ES’s divisibility/denominator predicates can be encoded in a Diophantine model. It is not a direct proof in `IOpen`/`IE₁⁻`, nor a Farey short-vector estimate.
- **Trap:** Definability of integers over `Q` is far stronger/different ambient machinery than a bounded elementary-arithmetic proof of ES; it supplies no representation for a particular `n`. Source lines 8–23, 77–96.

### Sun, “Z is Diophantine over Q with 26 unknowns” (arXiv:2611.14605 v2)
- **Main theorem:** Theorem 1.1: “`Z` is diophantine over `Q` with 26 unknowns,” i.e. a polynomial `F∈Z[X,Y₁,…,Y₂₆]` satisfies `x∈Z ⇔ ∃y∈Q²⁶, F(x,y)=0`; Theorem 1.2 reduces this to 16 unknowns under `abc` (`:77–90`).
- **Averaging/pointwise:** Uniform existential characterization for every `x∈Q`, not an average over denominators/primes. The introduction describes this as a variable-count reduction using prior elliptic/height and compilation ingredients (`:94–109`).
- **Lane/relevance — farey-proof:** A source of compact arithmetic encoding ideas; potentially motivates a formal comparison of Diophantine definability with weak arithmetic predicates used by the lane. No direct Farey, prime short-vector or `IOpen` lemma is stated.
- **Trap:** Conditional 16-variable improvement depends on `abc`; even the unconditional result is about definability/decidability over `Q`, not ES or an elementary proof of ES. Source lines 77–109.
