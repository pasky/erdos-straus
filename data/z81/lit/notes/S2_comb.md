## Ford–Gabdullin–Pratt, 2611.15534
- **Theorem 1.1** (lines 39–51): For fixed δ>0, 100≤y≤x/100, Y=min(y,x/(2y)) sufficiently large, and 1≤r≤(log Y)^(log 4−1−δ), writing r=(log Y)^θ, `H_{≥r}(x,y,2y) ≍δ x log(2^r)/[(log Y)^(1−E(θ))(log log Y)^(3/2)]`; constants depend only on δ. Here H counts **integers n≤x** having at least r divisors in (y,2y]. This is an order-of-magnitude census averaged over n, not a theorem about each fixed integer. (Theorem’s displayed formula lines 39–45; definition lines 15–23.)
- No lemma here gives a fixed N a divisor in (y,2y] in a prescribed residue class. Even the main result counts n with many unrestricted divisors; no residue-class condition, and exceptional N are allowed. Could inform expected abundance/scale for divisor-window searches, but does not address B2’s fixed-prime, moving-modulus, prescribed-class demand. **Trap:** do not convert a positive/large count among n≤x into pointwise existence for a selected N. (Lines 15–23, 39–51.)

## Jain, 2611.21136
- **Theorem 1** (lines 40–54): For every β>√(103/(256e)), there are N₀(β), K(β)>0 such that every integer N>N₀ has N=a+b, a,b>0, with P⁺(a)≤N^β and P⁺(b)≤(log N)^K. This is pointwise for every sufficiently large integer N, hence applies to a fixed sufficiently large prime p to give p=a+b of those smoothness types. (Abstract/statement lines 7–20, 40–54.)
- **Theorem 2 (uniform residue-class convolution estimate)** (lines 207–245): For ε,A>0 and integer k≥1, some δ,C>0 exist such that for x≥2, nonzero a₁,a₂ with |a₁|≤x^(1+δ), |a₂|≤x^δ, (a₁,a₂)=1, and uniformly (log x)^C≤y≤x^(1/C),
  `Σ_{q≤x^(153/256−ε), (q,a₁a₂)=1} τ_k(q) sup_{0≤z≤x} |Ψ(z,y;a₁a₂,q)−Ψ_q(z;y)/φ(q)| ≪_{ε,A,k} Ψ(x;y)/(log x)^A`.
  It averages the discrepancy over **moduli q** (with divisor weights), uniformly in residue and cutoff z; it is not an estimate for each q individually. (Statement lines 207–245.)
- For the smooth-x lane, Theorem 1 genuinely supplies fixed-prime p=a+b, with a N^β-smooth and b polylog-smooth. It does **not** impose a=4x−g, divisibility by 4, or a small prescribed g; it cannot by itself yield p+g=4x with x very smooth. The averaged-in-q theorem is not a fixed-modulus guarantee. **Trap:** pointwise in N for an unconstrained sum is not pointwise in a specified residue-class/divisor construction.

## Gao–Geroldinger–Wang–Zeng, 2611.24775
- **Theorem 1.1** (lines 51–58): If n=2k with k≥3 odd, then for every positive integer r, `D(C_n^r) ≥ r(n−1)+1+⌊r/(8n−7)⌋`; in particular the van Emde Boas–Kruyswijk formula fails for every r≥8n−7. (For each such n, this is a theorem for all r; strict counterexample begins at threshold.)
- Smallest parameter pair supplied by this theorem is (n,r)=(6,41). The first even n of form 2k, k≥3 odd, is 6; at n=6 the floor first becomes positive at r=41. This identifies the smallest counterexample **from the theorem’s bound**, not a claim that no other independent counterexample has smaller parameters. (Lines 51–58.)
- The formula `D(C_n^r)=r(n−1)+1` remains known for p-groups (Olson) and rank ≤2 (van Emde Boas–Kruyswijk; lines 31–32). Thus groups with n=2k, odd k≥3, rank r≥8n−7 are explicitly in the disproof range. `(Z/2)^r×(Z/4)^s` is a 2-group, so Olson’s formula applies. In contrast, `(Z/4uZ)^*` is not generally a 2-group (odd factors of u contribute odd-order components); its actual invariant factors/rank must be checked. It is outside the displayed homocyclic disproof unless it is itself of the relevant form `C_(2k)^r`; if its rank is ≤2, the known rank-2 result applies. Do not use the conjectured formula for arbitrary n/r. **Trap:** the result is for homocyclic `C_n^r`, not all finite abelian groups.

## Zhi, 2611.24034
- Exact headline bounds (abstract lines 7–22; main-results summary lines 182–217): for A⊂F, |A|=N, L=1+log(2N), N≤p^(1/2), `|AA+A| ≫ N^(129/85)L^(−72/85)` (129/85≈1.5176); `|AA+λAA| ≫ N^(214/135)L^(−7/9)`; `|AA−AA| ≫ε N^(6681/4175−ε)`; and mixed `AA+λA/A` has stated characteristic-sensitive bound, in particular `≫N^(214/135)L^(−7/9)` for p≥N²/L. Also `|A(A+λ)|, |(A+λ)/A| ≫N^(49/38)L^(−67/38)` for N≤p^(1/2) (lines 119–150). These are image-set growth theorems; the sharpest headline exponents are as above, with field/characteristic hypotheses retained.
- Relevance to divisor sets of one integer is essentially nil: these concern expansion of arbitrary finite field sets under algebraic maps, proved by energy/incidence arguments, not divisors of a fixed integer, prescribed congruence classes, or a pointwise divisor-window assertion. No direct B2 bridge. **Trap:** finite-field set expansion does not imply expansion/coverage of the divisor set of one N. Proof not audited.

## Matijasevick, 2611.17205
- **Theorem 1.2 (Inverse theorem)** (lines 77–82): for s≥1, q=snd(s), ε>0, some T₀(s,ε), if T≥T₀, E≥1, 2q^(−2)E≤sT and A⊆[1,E] has |A|≥E/q+εT, then sT is a sum of at most `2q^(−2)(⌈2s/ε⌉+1)` distinct elements of A; in particular sT∈Σ(A). (Theorem 1.1 regular-range formula is separately at lines 71–76.)
- This is a density-to-subset-sum inverse theorem with a specified target and bounded number of summands, not a statement about divisor residues or ES decompositions. Possible very remote geom-comb analogy (density forcing an additive representation); no concrete lane application identified. **Trap:** its threshold/density and interval setting do not yield the sparse, multiplicative, fixed-prime B2 condition. Proof not audited.

## Farnamfar, 2611.19594
- **Theorems 1–2** (lines 86–115): entropy inequalities for X,Y in an abelian group: in groups without 2-torsion, `H(X−Y)≤aH(X)+bH(Y)+cH(X+Y)`, with rational coefficients summing to `209357544569848128/114631769327023765 ≈1.8263483657`; for every abelian group a universal inequality with coefficient sum `10304335425987192362/5641833174538280329 ≈1.8264161855`. Consequently these are Katz–Tao sum-difference exponent upper bounds. Certificates are exact computational entropy proofs (abstract/lines 12–22; theorem 1 lines 86–103; theorem 2 lines 104 onward).
- No immediate ES application: abstract entropy inequalities for arbitrary random variables do not give a pointwise divisor in a designated residue class. At most a general additive-combinatorial tool if an ES construction can first be encoded as a suitable finite-set sum-difference problem; none is supplied. **Trap:** an improved universal exponent is not a new pointwise arithmetic representation theorem. Proof not audited.

## Dai–Li–Li–Yi, 2611.15110
- **Theorems 1.1–1.3** (lines 44–55, 86–95): for an odd-modulus distinct covering, 45|Q; at least one modulus is ≤9; every distinct covering has least modulus <14000, and if all moduli are squarefree it is ≤7. (Main statements lines 44–55, 86–95.)
- Method: distortion reweights the probability measure on residues left uncovered stage by stage; a prescribed class modulo a prime-power divisor then has controlled conditional mass (lines 179–251). Their new joint comparison uses independent random exponents to compare expectations of convex functions of sums of residue-class indicators, retaining dependence jointly across coordinates; directional convexity enables a sharper multivariate comparison than scalar bounds (abstract lines 12–21; Theorem 2.6 around 429 onward).
- Transfer idea, not theorem: in a residue-class coverage problem, track the measure of a surviving set under successive class exclusions and use joint indicator/convexity bounds rather than multiplying crude one-coordinate densities. This could help certify positive-measure sterility/non-coverage if the covering has a finite product-coordinate model and conditional distributions under control. It does not directly handle ES: ES needs a divisor in one moving prescribed class, not a global covering-system contradiction, and no transfer is established. **Trap:** their finite modular covering structure and explicit recursions are essential. Proof not audited.

## Luca–Pink–Sándor, 2611.20783
- **Theorem 1.1** (lines 27–36): for f(n), the number of ordered nondecreasing positive-integer solutions of `n(x₁+⋯+x_n)=x₁⋯x_n`, `liminf_{n→∞} log f(n)/log n=0`. They also prove along primes `liminf log f(p)/log p=0`, `limsup≤1`, and `limsup≥50/259` (lines 58–64). They conjecture full limsup is 1 (lines 37–53).
- The equation resembles the ES surface `xyz=x+y+n`, but the results are about the solution-count exponent of a different variable-length equation. Divisor manipulations/counting may be a loose analogy; no transfer shown to ES’s three-denominator equation, fixed-prime existence, or residue-class B2. **Trap:** liminf sparsity of solution counts is not an ES obstruction or construction. Proof not audited.

## Sándor–Zakarczemny, 2611.20799
- **Theorems 3–5** (lines 126–129, 156–171): for ELE(h,n), `x_n≤n^h(n^h+n−1)` (Theorem 3); `x₁⋯x_n≤n^h(n^h+1)(n^h+n−1)` (Theorem 4); for h=1, `x_{n−1}≤n+max_{d|2n²−2n, d≤√(2n²−2n)} d`, while for h≥2, `x_{n−1}≤2n^h` (Theorem 5). The abstract also states a lower bound on the growth of g(h,n) and solution-count upper/lower bounds; see lines 9–20 for exact statements. Theorem statements at lines 126–171.
- The bounds via divisors of `2n²−2n` illustrate how an equation can force a large coordinate to divide a controlled expression; a possible methodological analogy for eliminating variables on an ES surface, but no identified derivation for `xyz=x+y+n`. Counting solutions / liminf bounds likewise do not transfer without a new reduction. **Trap:** h,n here are the coefficient and number of variables, not ES numerator/denominator parameters; do not equate the equations. Proof not audited.

## Agrawal, 2611.06798
- **Theorem 1.3 (Main result)** (lines 98–109): for nonzero integer h and positive coprime a,b with (b,h)=1 and (b,h−1) Golomb-prime-free, every y≥|h| admits a finite set B of Golomb primes all >y with `a/b=Σ_{g∈B}1/(g−h)`. This is a distinct unit-fraction representation using shifted Golomb-prime denominators. **Theorem 1.4** gives their AP asymptotic for b≤(log x)^A under the stated compatibility conditions (lines 112–130); **Theorem 1.5** bounds non-smooth shifted Golomb primes (lines 137–157).
- As requested: this is an “every rational has a distinct unit fraction representation” result (under the specified shifted-Golomb-prime denominator family), not a 3-term ES representation. Its sieve/AP and smoothness tools do not give the three reciprocal terms or B2 prescribed divisor. **Trap:** distinctness and denominator set are the point; this is not an ES proof or direct lane result. Proof not audited.
