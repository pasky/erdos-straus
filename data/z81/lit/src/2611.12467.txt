1.1. Outline of the proof.      Write L = log x. We first establish an asymptotic formula for the
weighted sum Θb,W (x, Φ) = n Λ(n)b(n − 1)W(n − 1)Φ(n/x), where Φ is smooth and supported in
                             P

[1/2, 1]. We form W from the numbers of prime divisors in K disjoint sets Pν of primes congruent
to 1 modulo 4. These sets lie in the separated ranges defined in (2.4).
   Vaughan’s identity, in Section 2, reduces the weighted sum to Type I and Type II sums. Section 3
treats the Type I sums through the distribution of b(n)W(n) in arithmetic progressions. We
deduce the required Bombieri–Vinogradov estimate from Fouvry and Tenenbaum’s theorem [7,
Theorem 1.8] by applying Cauchy’s formula to a family of multiplicative functions with complex
parameters. A Dirichlet convolution with b then reduces the evaluation of the main term to
Landau’s asymptotic formula and an Euler product.
   The Type II sums have the form

where αm = µ(m)miv on its support. The support and coefficient conditions are stated in
Proposition 4.2. Expanding W(mn − 1) extracts a divisor a = p1 · · · pK , with pν ∈ Pν . Since
every pν is congruent to 1 modulo 4, the relation mn − 1 = ah implies b(mn − 1) = b(h) without
a coprimality condition. After dividing a into dyadic intervals, Cauchy’s inequality over h removes
b(h) and reduces the problem to the mean square QY in (4.3). For each prescribed R > 0,
Theorem 4.1 proves QY ≪ XY L−R , where X = M N , once K is sufficiently large.
   Expanding this mean square pairs the equations mn − 1 = ah and rs − 1 = ch, and hence
introduces the determinant relation
                                         cmn − ars = c − a.
We compare QY with its major arc approximation. Sections 5 and 6 estimate the difference by
harmonic switching and high moments of an operator on labelled primitive points. We follow
closely the method of [30, 22], adapting it to the auxiliary weights W considered here. We establish
the high moment estimate and the minor arc comparison in Proposition 5.1.
   Section 7 estimates the major arcs. We write c = pc′ with p ∈ P1 and, for fixed c′ , obtain a
prime Dirichlet polynomial of length xo(1) . We collect the unit intervals containing a point where
this polynomial is large; [18, Lemma 6.5] bounds their number. The contribution outside their
union is negligible. On these intervals, we combine Möbius cancellation from Lemma 7.2 with the
mean square estimate in Lemma 7.3. The Möbius estimate uses the bound for 1/L(s, χ) in [27,
Theorem 24.20], together with the exclusion of Landau–Siegel zeros [1, 11]. This completes the
proof of Theorem 4.1.
   Finally, Section 8 removes the auxiliary weights using Selberg’s sieve. Smooth approximation
then completes the proof of Theorem 1.1.
