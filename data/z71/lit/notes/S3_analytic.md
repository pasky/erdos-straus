## Source facts

### Elsholtz–Stadlmann, 2610.20554 — prime-square large sieve (priority 1)
**Theorem 3** (lines 121–142), verbatim in substance: If `Mk ≤ N^{1/2}`, `P` is a set of primes in `[M/2,M]`, each has a nonempty selected set `Ω(p)⊂{1,…,p}`, and `A⊂[1,N]` satisfies
`|{a mod p² : a∈A, a≡c (mod p)}|≤Θ` for every `p∈P,c∈Ω(p)`, then
`|A| ≤ ξ(k,ω,P,Θ) 2N ( Σ_{p1>…>pk∈P} ∏_{i=1}^k ω(pi)/(pi−ω(pi)) )^{-1}`,
assuming the explicitly defined `c*`, `d*`, `ξ` are positive. See lines 96–142 for their exact definitions/statement. **Corollary 1** (lines 153–169): if `ω(p)∈[c2p,c3p]`, `|P|≥c1 M/log M`, and same fibre cap, then `|A|≪_{k,Θ,c_i} N(log M)^k/M^k`. Uniform over A and such prime sets; restrictions are over a dyadic interval of prime moduli, `Mk≤√N`. This is not an all-modulus statement. They explicitly compare a configuration with about half the classes mod p selected but only one survivor mod p² per selected class: Johnsen–Selberg gives `≪N^{3/4}log N`, theirs gives `≪N^{1/2}log²N` (lines 203–229). In general it matches Montgomery large-sieve strength up to constants in this regime (lines 236–245), not an unconditional strict improvement over classical LS. Theorem is explicitly limited to prime-square moduli; no extension to composite moduli claimed (lines 282–290).
**Method:** large-sieve inequality, lower-bound its quadratic form using selected residue classes and bounded p²-fibres, then Gallagher larger sieve for additive decomposition. **OpenAI:** no dependency; paper says its work predates OpenAI’s Ostmann result (lines 291–293).

### Walsh, 2610.40439 — shifted divisor correlations (priority 1)
**Theorem 1.1** (lines 65–95): for every fixed `k,l≥1`, fixed nonzero integer `h`, and smooth compactly supported `Φ`,
`Σ_n Φ(n/x)d_k(n)d_l(n+h) = [S_{k,l}(h)/((k−1)!(l−1)!)] x(log x)^{k+l−2} ∫Φ + O_{k,l,h,Φ}(x(log x)^{k+l−2−η})`, with `η=η(k,l)>0`; singular series Euler product (1.2). Thus **all fixed k,l**, not uniform as they grow; fixed-shift asymptotic, not uniform in h, and constants depend on h. The sharp-interval consequence (1.3) is asymptotic for each fixed h. It does not assert uniform `|h|≤x^{1−ε}`; this is described only as a plausible extension in lines 273–274. **Method:** weighted-dilation graph estimates; density-model weights and graph comparison; prime sum evaluated by zero-free input. Lines 88–90 say the zero-free region is helpful but “not strictly necessary”; references [38],[39] are OpenAI manuscripts, lines 2772–2775 ([39] explicitly quasi-RH `Re s>7/8`). The main new graph input is attributed to [38], so **yes, substantive dependence on OpenAI weighted-dilation graph work**; quasi-RH is optional / used for a convenient zero-free region, not essential.

### Hu–Lü–Wang–Wu, 2611.12756 — higher-order Titchmarsh
**Theorem 1.1** (lines 83–97): for each fixed `k≥2` and fixed `h≠0`, `Σ_{x<n≤2x} Λ(n)τ_k(n+h)=C_{k,h}x(log x)^{k−1}+o_{k,h}(x(log x)^{k−1})`, with the displayed positive Euler product `C_{k,h}`. Pointwise in every fixed shift/order, **not uniform in either**; all k are covered one at a time. **Method:** sieve decomposition and a Type-II estimate generalized from earlier work; select primes, exploit CRT, bilinear estimates and Bombieri–Vinogradov/zero-density inputs (outline lines 140–160, 628–700). References include OpenAI “Poisson–Dirichlet law…” and “Weighted dilation graphs…” (lines 1813–1817); the paper’s Type-II argument extends the latter framework. No evidence that quasi-RH itself is needed; unlike Walsh, the displayed proof has no cited quasi-RH input. Do not call it independent of all OpenAI work: weighted-dilation-graph lineage is cited.

### Baier, 2610.15706 — small entry in binary quadratic forms (priority 1)
**Theorem 1** (lines 125–138): for every `n≥1`, parity `e∈{0,1}`, `1≤M≤n`, and `ε>0`, weighted integral solutions of `x1²+x2²−x3²=n²` with `x3≡e (mod 2)` have explicit main term (involving `Ψ1,Ψ0,ℓodd(n),βe(n)`) plus `O_{Φ,ε}(n^{1+θ+ε}M^{−1/2})`; the sum is weighted by `Φ(x3/M)`. Combined parity count `R(n²)` is displayed in same theorem. **Corollary 2** (lines 140–148): asymptotic `Re(n²)=(4/π)(log n)∫_R Φ(x/M)dx+O(M loglog n)` for `n^{71/96+ε}≤M≤n`; with Ramanujan (`θ=0`), range improves to `n^{2/3+ε}≤M≤n`. This is pointwise for every square parameter n, not a mean over n. **Theorem 3** (lines 166–182): non-square discriminant `D=f²D0`, `1≤M≤D`, gives `N(D)=(6/π)L(1,χD0)hD0(f)∫Φ(x/M)dx + O_{Φ,ε}(D^ε f^{1+θ}D0^{2/3}M^{−1/2})`; asymptotic when `M≥f^{4/9}D0^{71/96}D^ε`; fundamental D range `M≥D^{4/9+ε}` and bounded D0 range `M≥D^{71/192+ε}`. **Method:** exact spectral trace formula; square case is a single geodesic, non-square case closed geodesics/Hecke relation, spectral error from Ramanujan-type exponent θ (lines 210–231, 270–326). No OpenAI dependency.

### Yang, 2611.01522 — smooth numbers in simultaneous progressions
**Theorem 1.1** (lines 152–183): for fixed ε, some δ,C>0, `|a2|≤x^{1+δ}`, `y∈[(log x)^C,x^{1/C}]`, `Q≤x^{1/10}`, and `R=x^{5/9−ε}Q^{−14/9}`, gives the stated averaged two-progression discrepancy `≪Ψ(x,y)/(log x)^A` (all A>0). The moduli/runs are averaged with a maximum over reduced classes; this is not pointwise distribution for each pair of moduli. Remark 1.2 gives a more technical extension for `|a2|≤x^δ`, level `Q≤x^{25/449}`, `R=x^{5/8−ε}Q^{−649/200}`. Theorem 1.3 (lines 186–194) gives one-progression level `x^{153/256−ε}`; Theorem 1.4 (205–212) gives a positive-proportion smooth-number consequence for adjacent large prime factors. **Method:** well-factorable sieve weights, bilinear forms/Kloosterman estimates, smooth-number decomposition (contents and §1–3). No OpenAI dependency seen.

### Xi, 2610.11083 — horizontal Kloosterman moments / density sieve
**Theorem 1.1** (lines 80–100): `Σ_{n≤X}|Kl(1,n)| ≍ X(log X)^{8/(3π)−1}` for all large X. Theorem 1.2 (lines 103–117) extends weighted moments to fixed ν and squarefree restriction with stated uniform parameters. These are averages over n, not pointwise. **Method:** lower bound on structured factorizations and vertical Sato–Tate; upper bound by new “density version” of upper-bound sieve plus divisor averages in progressions and vertical Sato–Tate (lines 120–174; Theorem 2.1 lines 203ff). No OpenAI dependency.

### Jiang–Lau, 2610.10372 — mixed arithmetic/Kloosterman moments
**Theorem 3.1** (lines 207–231) gives uniform upper bound for short sums of products `F(|Qj(n)|)∏|Kl(a;|Qj(n)|)|^{νj}` over `x<n≤x+y`, with `x≥c0||Q||^δ`, `x^α<y≤x`, for fixed admissible polynomials/parameters and multiplicative growth class; Euler factors and weighted congruence density appear explicitly. Corollaries 3.3–3.6 (253–368) specialize. **Theorem 3.8** (375ff) gives matching lower bound for fixed z,ν on `Σ_{n≤x} z^{ω(n)}|Kl(a;n)|^ν`, uniform in bounded-power range of a as stated there. It is an average/short-interval theorem, not pointwise. **Method:** extend Nair–Tenenbaum polynomial-value estimates to Kloosterman-weighted moments; refine Fouvry–Michel for lower bound. No OpenAI dependency.

### Chatterjee, 2610.31832 — divisor sums with spacing constraints
**Theorem 1.1** (lines 104–129): with `L=loglog x`, `y=(log x)^{I(λ)}`, (i) for fixed λ>2, `S(x,y) ≍λ x(log x)^{λ log2}/[y(loglog x)^{log2/(2logλ)}]`; (ii) for fixed κ>0, uniformly `1<λ≤2+2κ/√L`, `S(x,y)≍κ xlog x`; (iii) for fixed `1<λ<2`, `S(x,y)∼xlog x`. This is an extremal selection over y-separated subsets, not a pointwise divisor assertion. No uniform result in the gap `λ=2+η(x), η√L→∞` (lines 128–143). **Method:** Sathe–Selberg, smooth squarefree generating-function estimate, correlation upper-bound sieve, Caro–Wei and permutation concentration (abstract/contents, lines 12–24, 160–179). No OpenAI dependency.

### Hughes, 2610.04678 — few primes cover unit groups (priority 1)
Definition and **Theorems 1–2** (lines 4–18, 40–71): “P covers q” means *every unit modulo q is a product of a subset of distinct primes in P* (each used at most once). For each A>0 and large Q, Theorem 1 supplies `|P_Q|≤(1/log2)logQ+O_A(logQ/loglogQ)`, primes `(logQ)^{A+3}<p≤(logQ)^{C_A}`, covering every `2≤q≤Q` except `O_A(Q(logQ)^{-A})` moduli. Theorem 2 uses twice the leading constant and covers every eligible q outside explicitly defined exceptional set `E_an` of size `O_A(Q(logQ)^{-A-9})`, additionally coprime to product of chosen primes. **Almost all moduli**, not every q. Proposition 3 proves the leading constant optimal (lines 71–75). **Method:** random subset-product growth in finite abelian group; character estimates, zero-density; repair exceptional low-conductor characters once (lines 86–100, 195–225). Explicit AI disclosure line 28; OpenAI dependence not claimed, classical zero-density and Linnik inputs.

### Johnston–Naik, 2610.08569 — least prime primitive root / larger sieve
**Theorem 1.1** (lines 64–71): assuming GRH, for `B>2+2e^{-1/3}`, all but `O((log N)^{B−1})` integers g in dyadic `[N,2N]`, `g≠−1` and nonsquare, have least prime `p_g≤(log|g|)^B`; Corollary 1.2 fixes B=3.44 (lines 73–80). Almost-all g, conditional GRH, not each modulus. Theorem 1.3 (128–139): unconditionally any diverging Φ(N) bounds `p_g` for all but o(N) g in intervals; with Φ≤(1−ε)logN exceptional count `N exp(−(c+o(1))Φ/logΦ)`. **Method:** variant Gallagher larger sieve permits occupancy in positive proportion of nonexcluded residue classes (Proposition 2.2, lines 206ff), integrated in Fan–Pollack argument. No OpenAI dependency. The paper states AI assistance in acknowledgments (check exact disclosure if needed).

### Richter–Teräväinen, 2610.03417 — sum-product patterns in shifted primes
**Theorem 1.3** (lines 59–64): for fixed nondegenerate linear forms `Lj(x,y)=ajx+bjy` (`ai bj−aj bi≠0`) the shifted primes contain infinitely many patterns `{x,xy,L1(x,y),…,Lk(x,y)}` with arbitrarily large distinct x,y. Theorem 1.6 (84–120) is the quantitative asymptotic input with selected small primes and averaging ranges; Theorem 1.3 is infinitude, not every shift/prime. **Method:** transference/Gowers norms for Siegel-corrected Λ, Bombieri–Vinogradov with correction, remove correction and evaluate singular series (contents and lines 194–220, 313ff). OpenAI manuscripts cited for local Gowers/dilation framework (references 18–19, lines 1813–1817); classify as dependency on that framework, not demonstrated quasi-RH dependency.

## Assessment (unreviewed)

### (i) Pointwise `R(p)≥1` / tied-modulus divisor event
- **Hughes 2610.04678:** superficially the closest finite-group coverage result: a small prime set’s *subset products* hit every unit modulo most q. But this says nothing about the divisors of the one integer `N=(p+e)/4`: the chosen primes need not divide N, subset products are squarefree products from an external set, and it is almost-all q rather than the particular moving modulus e for each p. It supplies no divisor in class −1 mod e and no mechanism to force it for every p. F1/F2: irrelevant; no integer n or ES numerator occurs.
- **Baier 2610.15706:** closest geometric shape: pointwise for every n, an asymptotic count of integral solutions/forms with `x3` of size M, equivalent to a count along SL₂/geodesic data. But it counts a weighted family averaged over a growing `x3` window and supplies no required congruence tying `e` to p or a divisor of `(p+e)/4` in class −1 mod e. Not a direct bound for one fiber/event. F1: despite pointwise square-discriminant asymptotic, does not contradict `R(m²)=0`; different count/surface. F2: no numerator-general ES construction.
- **Walsh / Hu et al.:** shifted divisor sums are for fixed shift h and average over n or primes; they could give asymptotics for fixed correlations or potentially fixed-modulus components of a prime sum, but `R(p)` has a moving tied modulus and divisor incidence with h/modulus correlated to p. The papers do not state an asymptotic or improved error for `Σ_{p≤X}R(p)` or `Σ_pR(p)^2`; second moment would require multiple coupled shifts/moduli and uniformity unavailable. No consequence for the exceptional set is justified. F1/F2: a mean shifted-correlation input does not ensure single-prime lower tail; no numerator robustness.
- Yang’s progression averages, Xi/Jiang–Lau Kloosterman averages, Chatterjee’s spacing extremum, primitive-root papers, and sum-product shifted primes likewise do not force a tied divisor for every prime. At F1, all averaging/finite-group arguments can persist on squares absent an essential quadratic-character input; at F2 none proves a numerator-uniform statement.

### (ii) Exceptional-set exponent / residue-dense large sieve
- The point to test for Elsholtz–Stadlmann is **many forbidden classes per modulus** (`ω(p)≈c p`, selected classes Ω(p) for which each surviving p²-fiber has occupancy ≤Θ), with about `M/logM` prime moduli in `[M/2,M]`, and `M^k≤√N`. Their bound is `|A|≪N(logM)^k/M^k` in the simplified regime; for the illustrative half-density / one-lift-per-selected-class setup it improves Johnsen–Selberg from `N^{3/4}logN` to `N^{1/2}log²N`. This is genuinely apt for **prime-square** residue-dense restrictions, not evidence for composite moduli; the theorem itself is prime-square-only and dyadic, and authors note the dyadic limitation. It matches Montgomery large sieve up to constants for these restrictions, rather than uniformly beating it. Whether it beats the campaign’s precise A* architecture in a many-level exceptional-set application remains open; no transfer to θ>3/4 established. F1: residue density alone is compatible with odd-square obstruction, so any transfer must exploit prime quadratic character; F2: cannot infer a numerator-8 result.
- Hughes’s subset-product covering is an almost-all-modulus theorem with `O(Q/log^A Q)` exceptions; it is not a residue-dense sieve estimate for sets of integers with restricted lifts. Johnston–Naik’s larger sieve is an almost-all base-g theorem, conditional on GRH for its strongest bound. Neither directly alters the CRT architecture/exponent.
