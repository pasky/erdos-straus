## 2611.03036 — de Saint Germain–Zhang, *Positive integral points on Mordell–Schinzel surfaces* (priority 1)
**READ:** §§1, 3–6, 7.1, Appendix A (main results, mutation maps, generalized cluster algebra, finiteness/orbits); especially source lines 102–159, 580–750, 760–1000, 1016–1120, 1764–1838.

**Theorem A (source lines 114–120):** “Fix `G ∈ Z≥0[x,y]` with bidegree `(m,n)` and `c ≠ 0`. (i) If `mn < 4`, then `YG(Z>0)` is finite. (ii) If `mn = 4`, then `YG(Z>0)` is infinite. Furthermore, if `abc^(m+n−4) = 1` [extraction notation: `ab c^{m+n−4}=1`], then `YG(Z>0)` is not Zariski dense and consists of only finitely many `Aut+_Z(YG)`-orbits. (iii) If `mn > 4`, then `YG(Z>0)` is Zariski dense.” (The source typesets the coefficient condition as `abcm+n−4 = 1`, i.e. `a b c^{m+n−4}=1`.) Theorem C (lines 146–147): if `mn=4` and this coefficient condition holds, `EG(N)=CG log N+OG(1)` for a constant `CG>0`.

**The campaign surface.** `xyz=x+y+n` is `G=x+y+n`, bidegree `(1,1)`, constant term `c=n`; hence `mn=1<4`: finite type, and the paper proves only that the number of positive integral points is finite (for each fixed n), not an exact count or orbit count for this parameter family. The finite bound is effective and depends on coefficients (Remark A.1, lines 120–123; Proposition 5.1/Lemma 5.3, lines 1017–1101). It is *not* uniform in n or coefficients; Proposition 5.5 (1102–1119) even shows point counts are unbounded across positive-coefficient bidegree-(1,1) surfaces. The paper does give an elementary positive point for every `n≥1`: `(x,y,z)=(1,1,n+2)`, since the equation becomes `z=n+2`; this existence is immediate from the equation, not a stated construction theorem. It does not guarantee the extra campaign constraint `4|z`; this exhibited point has `4|z` iff `n≡2 (mod 4)`. No general positive point satisfying `4|z` is produced.

The rank-two exchange relations are `x_k x_{k+2}=E_{k+1}(x_{k+1})` (Theorem 4.3 setup, lines 785–818), with initial exchange polynomials `E_0(t)=A(t), E_1(t)=B(t)`, where `A(x)=c+Σa_i x^i`, `B(y)=c+Σb_j y^j`. For `G=x+y+n`, `A(t)=B(t)=n+t`, so the adjacent mutations are exactly `x·x' = y+n` and `y·y'=x+n` (equivalently `x'=yz−1`, `y'=xz−1` on the surface; see (4.8), lines 930–955). They are mutations in the generalized cluster pattern, not in general integral automorphisms of this fixed surface: the integral positivity-preserving automorphism/orbit results are under the special coefficient hypotheses (3.6)/(3.7), which fail for general `n` here (lines 681–750). The finite/affine/indefinite thresholds are by `mn<4`, `=4`, `>4`; this case is finite type, not one of the A2/B2/G2 periodic exchange patterns (those correspond to rank-two exchange matrix products 1,2,3; this paper’s generalized exchange *polynomials* and its finite-type periodicity need not be conflated with the ordinary coefficient-free rank-two cluster cases).

**Theorem 4.5 (lines 990–1012):** “The evaluation map `f ↦ (f(x0), f(x1), f(Ψ(z)))`, `Frieze(Um,n,pG) → YG(Z>0)`, is a bijection.” The generalized upper cluster algebra and strong positive Laurent phenomenon are described at lines 785–818 and Appendix A: all cluster variables lie in the positive upper cluster algebra; this gives Laurent positivity for the adapted friezes. This is not an ES witness-production theorem: for a given n it does not manufacture a point with `4|z`, nor solve the constrained divisibility problem.

**Method (≤8 lines).** Model positive points as positive integral evaluations (“adapted friezes”) of a rank-two generalized upper cluster algebra. Use positivity/Laurent expansions and mutation/exchange recurrences. Finite type gives periodic exchange variables and coefficient-dependent inequalities bounding all entries. Affine type uses mutation groups, reduction to finitely many orbit minima, then exponential orbit growth/logarithmic height counts. Indefinite type constructs infinitely many unitary friezes and proves Zariski density.

## 2611.07454 — Tian–Wei, *Brauer groups of generalised Markoff surfaces*
**READ:** §§1, 3–7 (main theorems, Brauer calculations and integral Hasse examples), source lines 12–143, 192–290, 317–475, 542–1147, 1360–1485.

**Theorem 1.1 (lines 52–65):** “Let `k` be a field of characteristic zero with `a,b,c∈k×` and let `m∈k\{0,4abc}`. One has `Br(Xm)=Br1(Xm)` and [the displayed case distinction] `Br(Xm)/Br(k) ≃ 0`, `(Z/2)^2`, or `Z/2` according to the stated square-class conditions.” **Theorem 1.2 (lines 68–95)** computes `Br1(Um)/Br(k)` from the four square classes `[aΔ],[bΔ],[cΔ],[mΔ]`, including possible `Z/4`; **Theorem 1.3 (lines 96–108)** computes the transcendental quotient over number fields, and **Theorem 1.4 (lines 114–134)** describes its extension by the algebraic part over a real field. These are exact classification statements; see cited lines for the displayed formulas.

Integral-Hasse result from the introduction (lines 136–139): they construct two explicit families over Q having real and all p-adic points but no integral points, with quaternion classes giving the BM obstruction. **Proposition 7.1.1** (lines 1371ff) and **Proposition 7.2.1** (1417ff) are the two constructions; Example 7.2.2 at 1472ff specializes one. This is an integral BM obstruction, not a new obstruction beyond BM and not a local-to-global sufficiency theorem.

**Method (≤8 lines).** Compute the boundary triangle/Picard lattice and its Galois action for `ax²+by²+cz²−xyz=m`. Use residues/purity for the projective and affine Brauer groups; calculate the algebraic and transcendental pieces and their extension. Evaluate explicit quaternion classes at local integral points, then use reciprocity to show the Brauer set is empty in the constructed locally soluble examples.

## 2610.40815 — Alessandrì–He, *Integral Brauer–Manin obstruction for Fermat near misses*
**READ:** §§1–6, source lines 59–109, 203–231, 365–422, 599–658, 664–745.

**Theorem 1.1 (lines 63–67):** “Let `m>1` be odd and squarefree. Then there is an integral Brauer–Manin obstruction to the Hasse principle for `x⁴+y⁴−w²=m²` if and only if `ω5(m)=0` and `ω3(m)` is odd.” Theorem 1.2 (lines 83–86): for odd squarefree `m>1`, `Br Um²/Br Q = Br Xm²/Br Q ≃ (Z/2Z)²`. An odd prime m has obstruction iff `m≡3 (mod 8)` (Remark 5.2, lines 646–647). This is a new family-specific BM obstruction, not a reciprocity/descent obstruction beyond BM. The paper explicitly notes a parameter with no BM obstruction for which exhaustive search found no integral points (Remark 5.3, lines 648–662); it does not assert BM sufficiency.

**Method (≤8 lines).** Identify the affine surface’s degree-2 del Pezzo compactification and conic-bundle structure. Compute its algebraic and transcendental Brauer classes, including two explicit quaternion generators. Determine their local invariants at 2 and primes dividing m, and combine by reciprocity to characterize when the integral BM set is empty. Count parameters satisfying that criterion by a Tauberian argument.

## 2610.13528 — Wei, *Campana strong approximation on generalized Markoff surfaces*
**READ:** §§1–4 (main theorem and proof strategy), source lines 27–109, 300–438, 563–580.

**Theorem 1.1 (lines 69–80):** “Let `a,b,c∈k×`, let `m∈k\{0,4abc}`, and let `Xm` be the surface in (1.1). For every hyperplane section H of Xm and every integer n≥2, Campana strong approximation with the Brauer–Manin obstruction holds for `Xm,(1−1/n)H`; that is, `(Xm\H)(k) = (Xm,ΔH)(Ak)^Br(Xm)`. The closure is taken in the Campana topology.” (The intended equality is the closure equality; OCR drops the closure marker.) Corollary 1.2 (lines 103–109) gives unequal weights on the three boundary components. This is Campana approximation for orbifold points, not ordinary integral-point existence. Indeed ordinary strong approximation with BM off infinity fails for the affine Markoff surface (lines 75–78).

**Method (≤8 lines).** Use conic-bundle fibrations from k-lines and a fibration criterion when H differs from the boundary triangle. For H equal to the triangle, exploit Vieta involutions to move local points reducing to the boundary near its vertices, then apply approximation. The argument depends essentially on retaining the three boundary vertices and finite Campana weights.

## 2610.21762 — Wang, *Linear positive torsor presentation and arithmetic purity of strong approximation*
**READ:** §§1, 3 (main statements and proof), source lines 14–162, 226–284, 345–400.

**Theorem 1.5 (lines 123–127):** “Assume that X admits a positive linear torsor presentation. Then X satisfies (APSABM) off ∅. Moreover, if the structure group G is of multiplicative type, then X satisfies (APSABM1) off ∅.” The theorem concerns arithmetic purity of strong approximation with BM (and its stated variant), not existence of integral points on arbitrary affine cubics. **Theorem 1.7 (lines 148–156):** under the same presentation, two K-points are P¹-chain equivalent iff their torsor classes in H¹(K,G) agree; δq is surjective and induces `X(K)/~P¹,ch ≅ H¹(K,G)`. (OCR typography is garbled; equations are as stated.)

**Method (≤8 lines).** Lift adelic points through a positive linear torsor presentation, twist the torsor, and apply strong approximation on the complement of codimension ≥2 in affine space. Positivity of a central cocharacter controls the affine coordinates. Relate rational curves and torsor classes via the same torsor presentation. Applies to eligible varieties; no ES-specific torsor construction or integral existence theorem is supplied.

## 2610.36497 — Wu, *Failure of the congruence Hasse principle for commutators in orientable surface groups*
**READ:** §§1–4 and Appendix A (criterion, construction, theorem), source lines 34–84, 97–162, 225–280, 377–467, 510–546.

**Theorem 2.5 (lines 156–162):** “Let Γ be a group and `ρ:Γ→SL2(OK,S)` a homomorphism, and let E be a set of maximal ideals of OK,S. Let `w∈[Γ,Γ]` be an element that is not a commutator in Γ. Assume (i) `ρp(Γ)=SL2(Fq)` with `|Fq|≥4` for every `p∉E`, (ii) the image of w in `ρp(Γ)` is a commutator for every `p∈E`. Then the image of w is a commutator in `ρp(Γ)` for every maximal ideal p. In particular, w witnesses the failure of the congruence Hasse principle for commutators with respect to ρ.” **Theorem 4.1 (lines 380–386):** for every `k≥2`, `[a1,b1]^k` is not a commutator in the genus-2 surface group, but is a commutator modulo every congruence quotient for the constructed representation. Corollary 4.3 extends failure to every genus `g≥2` (lines 457ff).

**Method (≤8 lines).** A group-theoretic commutator-length obstruction proves the word is not a commutator. Embed a Sanov free subgroup into `SL2` over a ring of S-integers and use reductions to finite fields. Away from a small exceptional set, every element of `SL2(Fq)` is a commutator; check the exceptional reductions separately. The resulting global-vs-all-congruence failure is a genuine reciprocity/congruence-Hasse phenomenon, but for commutators in groups—not an integral-point obstruction for a cubic surface.

## 2610.16407 — Chen, *Stable reduction and large Nielsen orbits on generalized Markoff surfaces* (priority 1)
**READ:** §§1, 2–4, 6–8 (main statements, stable-reduction argument, corollaries), source lines 82–108, 112–123, 176–235, 260–365; also §7.2.

**Theorem 1.1 (source lines 112–123):** “(Component-degree bound). For a prime p, let X be a connected component of `M(SL2(Fp))abs_Q`. Let `jX` denote the map given by taking j-invariants, `jX:X→A¹j`. Then `dX:=deg jX≥p`.” **Corollary 1.4 (lines 200–205):** “There exists an integer N such that for all primes `p>N` and all `τ∈Fp`, the group `Out(Π)` acts transitively on `F(p)τ` if `τ≠2`, and `F(p)τ` is empty if `τ=2`.” The integer N is asserted to exist, without an effective bound in the theorem/corollary; the “all but finitely many” assertion is not effective as presented. Corollaries 1.5–1.6 (217–239) give connectedness/degrees/genus asymptotics.

The orbit-size mechanism is not a point-count. A Nielsen/mapping-class orbit corresponds to a connected component of the moduli cover of absolute `SL2(Fp)`-structures over the j-line, and its cardinality equals that component’s degree (lines 184–199). The component-degree theorem forces that degree ≥p, hence **every** orbit of surjective representations has size ≥p. Proof mechanism: degenerate at a cusp to a three-point `Gp=PSL2(Fp)` cover; stable reduction says the relevant cover has bad reduction above p (Theorem 1.7, lines 293–305). Assuming a small j-map degree `<p` would make monodromy tame and yield good reduction, contradiction; the ramification/degree constraints force the component degree ≥p (strategy lines 336–355). For Markoff τ=−2, earlier special argument had ramification index `2p` at the origin (lines 260–267); Chen’s generalization handles arbitrary τ via stable reduction and noncongruence modular curves.

**Method (≤8 lines).** Translate orbit size into degree of a component of the moduli curve over the j-line. Analyze a cusp degeneration and associated three-point covers using stable reduction. A bad-reduction theorem for `Gp`-covers conflicts with the good reduction forced by a hypothetical j-map degree `<p`; thus each component has degree at least p. Combine this uniform lower bound with the previously established “one giant orbit plus O(p^ε) leftovers” theorem of Kingsbury–Neuschotz to eliminate all other orbits for sufficiently large p.

## 2610.06166 — Chiu, *Tower of coverings and polylog-size coverings of rational points*
**READ:** §§1, 3–5 (definitions, main theorems and abelian-variety case), source lines 51–168, 285–305, 419–550, 551–610.

**Definition 1.1 (lines 51–58):** “The variety X has a polylogarithmic-size covering of rational points if for any B>e, the E-points of X with exponential Weil height at most B can be covered by `polyX(log B)` irreducible subvarieties over E of dimension `<dim X` and degree `polyX(log B)` with respect to an ample line bundle on X.” **Theorem 1.5 (lines 109–116):** if X has a tower of finite étale covers with `Γ_{p^m}`-structures and exceptional set, height comparison on twists, and the number of twists `|H¹(Gal(ET/E),Gm)|` is polynomially bounded in `|Γ_{p^m}|`, then X has such a polylog-size covering. **Theorem 1.8 (lines 129–135):** every abelian variety over a number field admits a tower satisfying those hypotheses. It does not say there are polylogarithmically many rational points: it covers them by lower-dimensional subvarieties of polylogarithmic number and degree. The text explicitly notes that deriving Néron’s point-count from this would need new input (lines 138–143).

**Method (≤8 lines).** Lift rational points to twists of finite étale tower covers. Bound the number of twists, apply arithmetic Broberg-type hypersurface containment on each twist, and compare heights through the cover. In the abelian case use multiplication towers, Koizumi’s theorem, twisted trace, geometry of numbers and canonical metrics to verify the hypotheses. No direct application to the campaign surface is given.

## Assessment (unreviewed)

### (i) Cluster-algebra overlap / witness production
The requested `git grep -n -i -E 'cluster alg|Lyness|frieze|Vieta|Mordell.Schinzel' -- '*.md' | head -60` is represented by the scan: existing campaign material is extensive on classical pentagon/Farey/frieze/Ptolemy structures (`FRIEZE_ES.md`, `INVOLUTION_ES.md`, `BRAINSTORM/farey_chain_frieze.md`) and Vieta/Markoff variants, but it does **not** already lay out the generalized upper-cluster-algebra package for `xyz=x+y+n`: nonnegative Laurent phenomenon, F-polynomials, or the generalized rank-two exchange classification. Existing frieze relations are related combinatorics, not an equivalent explicit account of the cluster algebra for this surface. The requested headers/summaries were skimmed: FRIEZE_ES §0 says no ES solution and emphasizes pentagon Ptolemy/frieze structure; INVOLUTION_ES §0 says its involution route gives obstructions, no construction, with T1/square failure. Crucially, 2611.03036 offers no method producing a point with `4|z` for a given n. It does establish finiteness and classifies/organizes positive points; existence of an unconstrained positive point here is already trivial `(1,1,n+2)`. Under F1, any unconstrained-positive-point assertion survives trivially even at odd squares and says nothing about the Type I/II ES witness; requiring `4|z` must be treated separately. Under F2, no uniform numerator-M construction is given; the theorem is only about this surface/family.

### (ii) Chen and B2 relevance
The requested grep finds Chen/BGS/Markoff discussion already in `MARKOFF_ES.md` and `FRONTIER.md`; notably `B2_ACROSS_FIELDS.md` already records a Chen theorem as the wrong axis (mod-p connectivity of one fixed Markoff surface vs pointwise existence on a varying family). Chen’s non-counting engine is genuinely a forced event at each sufficiently large p: each mod-p Nielsen orbit is a moduli-curve component, whose degree is an orbit size; stable reduction at p plus ramification constraints forbid degree `<p`. This does not transfer directly to `xyz=x+y+p, 4|z`: no corresponding moduli cover/component has been identified whose degree both represents the relevant constrained positive integral solutions/orbit and is forced to be divisible by p or ≥p. The closest conceivable analogue would need a geometric cover/ramification invariant attached to the constrained `4|z` locus, with p in its ramification data; no such object is supplied by Chen or apparent in the current formulation. F1/F2: Chen’s theorem is about mod-p surjective representation orbits, not integral ES points, so it neither contradicts square-case emptiness of the ES slice nor yields a numerator-8 claim.

### (iii) Brauer–Manin vs new obstruction / sufficiency
Tian–Wei and Alessandrì–He give explicit *BM* obstructions (quaternion classes and reciprocity calculations), not an obstruction type beyond algebraic/transcendental BM. Wu does produce a new-looking congruence-Hasse failure via a separate group-theoretic commutator reciprocity obstruction, but it concerns surface groups and finite group quotients, not integral Hasse for affine cubic/log-K3 surfaces. None proves “no BM obstruction ⇒ integral point exists” for the listed surface families; Alessandrì–He even records a case with no BM obstruction but no points found, leaving failure unexplained. Wei’s Campana strong approximation and Wang’s purity theorem have different hypotheses/notions and do not imply such integral existence.
