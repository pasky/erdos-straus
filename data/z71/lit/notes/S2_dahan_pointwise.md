* **Prop. 8.1–8.2 (lines 5076–5240; roadmap 475–490):** false-positive-free four-variable equivalence `n+4u²v=w(4uav−1) ⇔ 4uv(aw−u)=n+w`; partial-fraction phase identity and comparison of unfactored bilinear weights with unit-weight sifting. This is an algebraic/weight identity, not a pointwise positive encounter theorem.
* **Thm. 9.1 (lines 5314 onward; §9.1):** three-term stationary-phase separation on `V_n`, with square-root-scale benchmark. Pointwise in fixed `n`; no positivity conclusion.
* **Thm. 9.2 (lines 5383 onward):** archimedean box volume `Vol(B) ≍ n`, bounded slice integral `I_B(n)≪1`, and cofactor congruence `m′|(aw−u) iff w≡−n (mod m′)`. Pointwise geometric identities/bounds.
* **Thm. 9.3 (lines 5451 onward):** exact Farey-diagonal L² dispersion after averaging `n∼N`, for power scale `S=4UAV≤N^{1/2−ε}`; off-diagonal Farey fractions are `O_B(N^{-B})`, exact Ramanujan-sum diagonal `≍N(UA)²`. This is explicitly averaged, not pointwise in n.
* **Thm. 9.4 (lines 5590–5878):** for fixed prime `n≡1 (24)`, `c≡3 (4)`, `c<n`, `K_c=(n+c)/4`, `gcd(c,K_c)=1`, positivity of the divisor-difference convolution at `−1` is a sufficient Type-II certificate; with multiplicity-weighted independent Bernoulli factors its support is exactly `D(K_c)`, so positivity is equivalent to `−1∈D(K_c)`. Also odd/even character decomposition and short-interval/Matomäki–Radziwiłł couplings. Fixed-n criterion plus estimates; couplings do not claim every n has a hit.
* **Thm. 9.5 (lines 5879–6034):** unconditional cumulative `(log P)^4` main-term growth, alongside shift-wise absolute-value cost and exact cancellation of small-LCM divisor pairs. Main term summed over shifts/candidates, not a pointwise lower bound for an encounter at each prime; part (c) expressly warns large-LCM pairs can alter the signed sum.
* **Thm. 9.6 (lines 6043–6184):** exact cyclic quotient parity identity, Jacobi theta heuristic/cancellation, phase-locking reciprocity and antipodal obstruction certificate (details below). Pointwise deterministic obstruction criteria, not a universal positive result.
* **Thm. 9.7(a) (lines 6185–6200):** for `L(Y)=4 lcm(1,…,Y)` and least prime `n_Y≡1 mod L(Y)`, `n_Y≪L(Y)^5`, every Type-II solution has `uv>Y`, every witness `s>Y`; hence along infinitely many primes `min(uv,s)≥(1/5−o(1))log n_Y` (GRH constant 1/2). Witness-depth lower bound, not absence of ES solutions. Part (b) gives pairwise disjointness of prime factors `q>H` among shifts in `PNR(n)∩[1,4H]`.
* **Thm. 9.9–9.10, Props. 9.12–9.13 (lines 6259–7206):** universal factor reciprocity `(q/p)=(q/n)`; ambient discrete cross-polytope bound `|Δ_k^−(d_p)|/d_p^k≤1/k!`; deterministic Kneser/reciprocity and spectral saturation criteria; cofactor and archimedean support obstructions. Pointwise structural criteria/bounds, not an unconditional all-prime solution theorem.
* **Thm. 9.14 (lines 7207–7405):** synthesis: unconditional ingredients plus conditional regimes; conditional finiteness relies on T (or stated alternative saturation control), with no numerical threshold. Remark 9.15 / §9.6 repeats open status.

### Positivity duality and obstructions

**Thm. 9.4, lines 5590–5660.** For square-free Bernoulli measure `μ_c`, `μ̃_c(g)>0 iff g∈D(rad K_c)`; thus `μ̃_c(−1)>0` suffices for Type II but need not be necessary for nonsquare-free `K_c`. If each prime factor is repeated `ν_q(K_c)` independent Bernoulli factors, support is all `D(K_c)` and positivity at `−1` is equivalent to `−1 mod c∈D(K_c)`. Twisted target `−n⁻¹` gives the stated first-denominator Type-I condition. This is exactly the same basic ratio-set target as campaign’s Type-II e-slice, subject to matching its notation.

**Thm. 9.6(a), source lines 6043–6120.** On cyclic quotient `G_p/ker χ≅Z/(2d)`, for odd `χ` of order `2d`, `E(χ)−O(χ)=2d·P_{g∼μ̃_p}(χ(g)=−1)≥0` (equation 142). This is exact Fourier inversion, not a positivity lower bound for the encounter count.

**Thm. 9.6(b), lines 6120–6148.** If an odd character has `Σ_{q|K_p}ν_q(K_p) arg χ(q)<π` (or unweighted sum over `rad(K_p)` `<π`), then `−1∉D(K_p)` (respectively `D(rad K_p)`), and the corresponding cyclic excess is zero. It certifies failure of that shift, not success.

**Thm. 9.7(a), lines 6185–6200.** Infinite sequence and logarithmic lower bound quoted above, using Linnik–Xylouris least-prime bound; only Type-II witness sizes `uv,s` are covered.

### Hypothesis T and conditional theorem

**Exact T, Thm. 9.7(b), lines 6200–6234.** For distinct shifts in `PNR(n)∩[1,4H]`, common prime divisors of `K_p,K_p′` divide `(p−p′)/4`; consequently prime factors `q>H` are disjoint across shifts. Then T is: “there exists a fixed constant A>5 such that for every sufficiently large prime n≡1 (mod 24), there exists at least one shift p∈PNR(n)∩[1,(log n)^A]” for which
`Σ_{χ∈Ĝ_p^-\{ψ₀}} μ̂_p(χ)^2 < 1 + Σ_{ψ∈Ĝ_p^+\{χ₀}} μ̂_p(ψ)^2` (146).
The text says (128) makes this equivalent to `φ(p) μ̃_p(−1)>0`, hence `−1∈supp(μ̃_p)⊆D(K_p)`. Thus **T implies ES for every sufficiently large prime `n≡1 mod 24`**, and finiteness of the Type-II exceptional set (hence ES exceptional set). Threshold is some unspecified `N_T`; Remark 9.8 lines 6235–6243 explicitly says no numerical bound, and factorial volume does not bound it. This is not a probabilistic equidistribution assertion: it directly postulates existence of a successful tied-modulus shift, with a particular spectral rewriting. In substance it is the B2 atom itself with a logarithmic shift window plus an exact positive spectral certificate, not an independently motivated standard conjecture shown to imply the atom. Pairwise disjoint large factors are not independence (Remark 9.8).

**Open signed targets, App. A.7 lines 8253–8297.** (C17), sufficient for `R(n;n^θ)=M+E>0` for every prime `n>N₀`, is exactly `E(n;n^θ)≥−(κ₀θ−δ)log n`, for some `θ∈(0,1)`, `δ∈(0,κ₀θ)` (or text’s alternative `E≥−o((log n)^4)` in view of (134)). (C76), for summed sifted square-free shift family `G_j`, is `E_j^sf≥−o(log H/(log log n)^{3/2})`. Neither follows from unconditional main-term lower bounds (134),(187); small-LCM cancellation and high-order odd characters remain uncontrolled. C17/C76 are stronger error-control routes to positivity, distinct from T; the paper does not assert either is proved.

### F1/F2 and correctness flags

* F1 (odd squares): §9’s setup assumes prime `n≡1 mod 24` for the positive criterion and its reciprocal/nonresidue framework; it does not offer a mechanism valid at odd squares. Universal quadratic reciprocity and T’s `PNR(n)` are visibly quadratic-character dependent. That avoids the blunt F1 contradiction, but the excerpt does not establish precisely which step supplies the necessary T1 sign. F2: no numerator-8 analogue is stated; identities and constants are built around `4/n`, and no argument is presented that uniformly transfers to numerator 8. Thus no direct F2 falsification of a claimed generalized theorem, but also no robustness demonstration.
* **Substantive logical red flag:** Theorem 9.4’s simple Bernoulli convolution support is `D(rad K)`, while the campaign’s criterion is the full divisor ratio set; paper itself admits the square-free certificate is only sufficient unless multiplicities are included. Avoid treating the two positivity statements as identical without the multiplicity-weighted construction.
* **Terminology red flag:** T’s name “Transversal” and pairwise disjointness of large prime factors may suggest independence, but Remark 9.8 expressly disclaims that inference; its inequality is an assumed successful positivity event.
* **Scope red flag:** T yields only eventual primes `1 mod 24`, with unknown threshold, and says nothing by itself about all integers. The Linnik–Xylouris lower bound is not an ES failure and only concerns Type-II witness size.
* **Evidence / reliability:** v1 dated Nov. 2026 and extraordinarily broad collection of novel claims; no external verification/proof audit was performed, as instructed. The paper itself labels central inputs open. Do not treat its conditional statements or analytic estimates as campaign-verified facts.
