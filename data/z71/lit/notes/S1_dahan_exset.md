## Source claims and exact scopes

* **Global set.** Introduction defines `E(N)` as integers `n≤N` for which `4/n` has no three-unit-fraction representation (lines 99–104). For prime `n≡1 mod 24`, Type II is equivalent to some coprime `(u,a)` having `s|(an+u)`, `s≡−1 (mod 4ua)` (lines 111–121). `P₂(J)={(u,a)∈[1,J]²:gcd(u,a)=1}` and `E₂(N;J)=E_{P₂(J)}(N)` comprises primes `n≤N`, `n≡1 mod24`, with no such Type-II witness for any pair in the box (lines 121–131). Text explicitly says `E(N)∩[2,N]` is contained in `E₂(N;J)` up to primes ≤24, for every `J≥1` (line 131); this containment is asserted, not a same-set identity.
* **Theorem 7.11 (verbatim conclusion/hypotheses).** “Choosing `Q=exp(t^{1/3})` and `t=log Z=η(log N log log N)^{3/10}` with `η>0` a sufficiently small absolute constant, there exists an absolute constant `c>0` such that, for all `N≥3`: `|E(N)|≪N exp(−c (log N)^{7/10}/(log log N)^{3/10})`.” Lines 4973–4980 (equation (106)).
* **Theorem 7.12 (verbatim conclusion/hypotheses).** “Choosing `Q=exp(c₂₃t^{1/2})` and `t=log Z=η(log N log log N)^{2/7}` with `η>0` a sufficiently small absolute constant, there exists an absolute constant `c′>0` such that, for all `N≥3`: `|E(N)|≪N exp(−c′ (log N)^{5/7}/(log log N)^{2/7})`.” Lines 5021–5030, (107). Also claims for each fixed `λ<5/7`, `|E(N)|≪λ N exp(−(log N)^λ)` (lines 5030–5032). This is for all exceptional integers, not just primes.
* **Theorem 6.10(a):** for `λ∈(1/3,1/2)`, `J=(log N)^λ`, the certified hierarchy `E₂cert(N;J)` satisfies `≪N exp(−c(λ)(log N)^{2λ}log log N)`; likewise its small-sheet subset has the stronger displayed constant `5(1−2λ)²/(32π²)` (lines 3459–3527, especially (70)). It is not a bound on all `E₂`.
* **Corollary 6.12(b), exact stated result:** for fixed `κ>0`, adaptive `J(N)` in (73), and the small-sheet component defined there, `E₂small(N;J(N)) ≪κ N exp(−(1/40960+o(1)) log N/[(log log N)(log log log N)^κ]) = N exp(−(log N)^{1−o(1)})` (lines 3624–3686, (74)). The `o(1)` and implied constant depend on the construction/κ. A separate CRT degree ceiling is stated in (c), lines 3687–3704: for generating-polynomial degree `h` supported on `p>J(N)^5`, modulus `z^h≤Q=N^{1/2}` forces `hmax≤log Q/log(J(N)^5)≍log N/log log N` (OCR fraction); the scale is described elsewhere as `log N/log log N`, not “logloglogNN”.

## Structural terminology / exact exceptional remainder

* `Σ_I(an+u)` is the set of residues modulo `4ua` of divisors formed from prime factors of `an+u` in interval `I`; §5 defines the stage split and sets (Theorem 5.1, lines 1821–1879). `K_I,u,a(n)=Stab_{G_u,a}(Σ_I(an+u))` there; `G_u,a=(Z/4uaZ)^×` (notation lines 567–593). In §3, `K(M):=Stab_{G_u,a}(Σ(M))`, with `Σ(M)={d mod 4ua:d|M}` for coprime divisors (lines 1071–1080; Theorem 3.2 at 1166ff).
* **Container:** a structured encoding of divisor-residue subsets by Kneser stabilizer kernels/subgroups, then popular symmetry sets, quotient data, or character/Fourier data, reducing subset enumeration; the source’s overview calls these “Kneser stabilizer kernels, Markov 1/2-popular symmetry sets, quotient containers, and Pontryagin–Plancherel character containers” (lines 209–216; §§3,6). It does not supply one universal formal definition of “container.”
* **Subcritical-collision branch:** pairs/exceptional integers whose aggregate off-kernel collision count is below `R₀`; Theorem 5.1 defines `E₂Ker` by `Σ_(u,a) ω_{G\H}(an+u;I)≤R₀`, with `R₀≈(1−2λ)P loglogN/8` (lines 1821–1857). It is closed with exponent `2λ` there. The complementary **Scarcity branch** `E₂Scar` has high off-stabilizer prime overload and must avoid the inverse first-stage sheet on stage II with zero collisions (lines 1857–1930; Theorem 5.2 lines 1983ff). Thus Scarcity is not “few witnesses” in an undefined generic sense: it is this explicitly high-collision complement.
* **Small sheet:** Corollary 6.12 defines `E₂small` as primes in `E₂(N;J)\[1,z]` for which at least `P/4` pairs have `|Σ_I1(an+u)|≤φ(4ua)^{ε₁(N)}` (lines 3624–3635); nearby overview says `P/2` at one point (lines 3452–3457). It is a subset selected by a structural condition, not all exceptions. **Diffuse divisor sheet** `E₂diffuse:=E₂\[1,z]\E₂cert`: scarcity primes where for >`3P/4` pairs sheets have intermediate size `φ(4ua)^ε₁<|Σ|≤φ(4ua)` without low-index Kneser stabilizer and spectrum misses subcritical buckets except high-order angularly diffuse characters (lines 3429–3457). Author’s explicit obstruction: “Bypassing `E₂diffuse` … without enumerating divisor sheets is precisely the role of the semi-composite CRT sieve of Section 7” (lines 3452–3457), and “Extending … to the full global exceptional set … requires eliminating the residual unlocalized diffuse component … without enumerating divisor sheets” (lines 3813–3829). Therefore the 1−o(1) claim leaves this complement out; it does not claim a bound of that strength for `E₂\E₂small`.
* The paper’s own stated 5/7 bottleneck: CRT/Bonferroni balance, not a theorem that 5/7 is an absolute barrier. §7 says the single-prime modulus budget locks saving at `t²` / exponent 2/3; cofactors amplify mean by `log Q/log t` without enlarging BV modulus; at maximal BV scale, balancing yields 5/7 (lines 3831–3881; 5019–5064). The `(loglogN)^{2/7}` loss comes from the `t^{5/2}/log t` sifting mean and polynomial Bonferroni degree/cost `exp(O(t^{7/2}/log t))` under CRT budget; see Theorem 7.12 proof lines 5033–5064.

## Two method architectures (≤25 lines each)

1. **Sections 2–6: container/Kneser route.** Composite divisor witnesses `s|an+u`, `s≡−1 mod4ua`; organize prime-factor residues as sheets `Σ`. First obtain quadratic/Jacobi/cofactor rigidity (§2), then replace arbitrary subgroup generation by `K(M)=Stab(Σ(M))`, 2-Sylow character kernels (§3), avoiding `2^{Θ(J⁴)}` enumeration. Couple with imperfect-avoidance Montgomery large sieve (Thm 4.1) and especially Krawtchouk–Montgomery generating-polynomial large sieve (Thm 4.3): resums alternating binomial signs into positive product before absolute values. CRT independence/conditioning is used for residue sieve and smooth conditioning (Thm 6.4), not as a direct independent witness model. Low collision (“subcritical”) gets sieve closure; high-collision Scarcity gets two-stage Kneser growth, Markov popular-symmetry, quotient and Plancherel character containers, subcritical escalation. It bounds full `E₂` only in limited depth regimes, `E₂cert` at polynomial depths, `E₂small` at adaptive depth with 1−o(1). It does not solve diffuse sheets; no full-set 1−o(1). Limits include the generating-polynomial CRT degree/capacity and certified structural coverage; the source says `hmax≈logN/loglogN`.
2. **Section 7: semi-composite CRT/BV route.** Prescribe witnesses `s=pq≡−1 mod12ua`, `p∈(Z^{9/10},Z]` prime and rough cofactor `q≤Q`; `u,a` in `[U,V]`, `U=Z^{1/10}`, `V=Z^{1/8}`, `y=(log Z)^6` (lines 3829–3851; 3888ff). CRT condition on `b=n mod Msmall=lcm(q)` makes indicators over distinct large `p` conditionally independent; disjointness for fixed `p` is Proposition 7.3. Decouple progression modulus `12ua≤12Z^{1/4}` from `q`, then Siegel-zero-free Bombieri–Vinogradov estimates prime `p` (Prop. 7.7, Thm 7.10). Polynomial even Bonferroni majorant transfers no-hit to integers (Lemma 7.4/Prop 7.5); not a Kneser-sheet method. `q` multiplies conditional mean by `≈logQ/logt`; choose `Q=exp(t^{1/3})` gives 7/10, saturate BV `Q=exp(c t^{1/2})` gives 5/7. Loss `(loglogN)^{2/7}` arises in optimizing `t=η(logN loglogN)^{2/7}` against the `t^{7/2}/logt` CRT/Bonferroni cost; the 5/7 is the balance, with constants chosen small. This bypasses/handles diffuse sheets uniformly by explicit witnesses.

## Prior work, barriers, and red flags

* Companion [1] is cited as “Sieve dimension and search depth for the Erdős–Straus conjecture, `n≡1 (mod24)`, companion paper (v2), 2026” (lines 8313–8315); **this article gives no arXiv id for it**. Intro calls it a companion (lines 95–110). The repo’s literature catalog has Dahan’s prior arXiv:2608.24035, likely the cited Paper I; the source does not explicitly equate the two, so that identification is bibliographic inference rather than a statement in this paper. `rg` on the article found no Kandukoori, Ramesh, Kumar references and no claim/citation of a 3/4 exponent. It does mention 3/4 as density threshold / local density and unrelated constants, but does not acknowledge K-R-K or campaign’s 3/4 result.
* Explicit barrier statements: abstract/intro says prime-witness large sieves capped at 2/3 by τ₃ divisor barrier (lines 15–20, 145–150); fixed-depth composite enumeration costs `2^{Θ(J⁴)}` in OCR / text (lines 22–24, 152–166); Theorem 6.12(c) gives Shannon–Bombieri CRT degree capacity (3687–3704). The 1−o(1) claim is explicitly “matching” that generating-polynomial CRT capacity (lines 32–40, 3687ff), not a general barrier theorem for all methods.
* **Assessment (unreviewed)—campaign comparison.** Numerically 5/7 is weaker than 3/4. Both concern full `E(N)` of insoluble integers, and Dahan states an all-`n≤N` bound (not merely primes); uniform constants differ in notation, so the sets/uniformity broadly match, but this paper never mentions the prior K-R-K claim or campaign 3/4. Its subset 1−o(1) does not contradict “3/4 sharp for CRT architectures”: it is a structurally selected subset (`E₂small`), and uses non-CRT Kneser/Plancherel structure, including growing-order/container hierarchy. The campaign cap’s hypotheses concern CRT majorants/forced-class congruence sieve over the whole avoider set; subset restriction alone already violates whole-avoider coverage, and Kneser input is additional non-CRT input. It does not establish full-set exponent >3/4.
* **Correctness red flags (not proof audit):** (i) internal definition inconsistency: `E₂small` threshold is at least `P/4` in Cor. 6.12 (lines 3624–3635), but overview/theorem discussion says at least `P/2` (lines 3452–3457); (ii) constant `1/40960` in Cor. 6.12 appears abruptly after elaborate optimization and is exceptionally specific (lines 3678–3686), deserving independent derivation check; (iii) multiple OCR-corrupted formulas/notation (`J⁴`, `logloglogNN`, fraction exponents) make hypotheses hard to parse, although source PDF could resolve; (iv) narrative asserts technical transitions (“force 3/2-geometric growth,” “all hypotheses … satisfied”) and summarizes results across many long sections—high burden to validate, no external refereeing indicated; (v) broad abstract claim “full `E₂` … unconditionally” up to `exp(c√loglogN)` and λ≤1/78 should be reconciled carefully with later `E₂cert`/diffuse decomposition and exact range (lines 54–61, 213–221); (vi) stated `E₂(N;J)` containment of `E(N)` relies on Type-II equivalence in paper, while global E includes composites; the “up to primes ≤24” phrasing should be checked against the reduction. These are audit targets, not findings of falsehood.
