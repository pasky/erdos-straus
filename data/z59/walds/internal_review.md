## Findings

1. **FATAL — Finite counterexamples are used to refute eventual nonvanishing, then to close E1.**
   **Location:** lines 8–20; §6.1, lines 319–321; proposed FRONTIER, lines 365–373; bottom line, lines 379–386.
   The statement “nonzero for all sufficiently large primes” is not refuted by any finite list of zeros, including the 13 rigorously cross-checked twists. A threshold beyond the largest listed prime accommodates every example. Thus “neither valid nor effective” is not a theorem: global R-support validity can indeed fail at one example, but effectiveness is an eventual property. Nor do a finite-level screen and a conjecture about one eigensystem exclude arbitrary higher-level signed/multiple-eigensystem proxies, which are still in the stated ternary-theta class. The bottom line expressly lists such unexcluded proxies immediately after declaring that class closed.
   **Fix:** retain the proved filter, scoped finite invalidity, and conjectural infinite vanishing as three separate conclusions. Replace “nor effective” with “its everywhere-nonvanishing version fails at these primes; eventual effectiveness remains unproved and is conjecturally false.” Delete “cannot carry (MS)” and “E1 is closed” as global conclusions. Do not export the current FRONTIER paragraph.

2. **MAJOR — The hypothesis that makes Lemma W1′ work disappears in its applications.**
   **Location:** lines 138–147; lines 253–257, 297, 307, 337–340, 366–367; title and opening item 1.
   The quadratic-form factorisation is correct **under its stated hypothesis** that some squarefree t₀ in the specified local class has nonzero L-value. But the following “So” asserts the dimension bound for every exceptional eigensystem without establishing that hypothesis. If all those twists have L=0, Corollaire 2 gives only 0=0 and no proportionality of coefficient functionals. In particular, the sign-negative/(H2)-absent case is explicitly left unresolved in Remark W3. A nonzero Fourier coefficient alone does not supply a nonzero L-value from Corollaire 2. Even with an L-nonzero anchor, the restriction may be the zero space, rather than an actual line.
   **Fix:** carry the L-nonzero-anchor hypothesis throughout; alternatively use Théorème 1 under (H2), which proves rank ≤1 including the all-zero case, or supply an independently verified extension/nonvanishing theorem. The comparison's restriction to sign-positive rank-≥2 rational curves is not the entire W1 exceptional set without this additional argument. In W4(b)'s sum and W4(c), require (H2) for **every contributing** eigensystem, not just the individually fixed φ.

3. **MAJOR — “Saturation” is not a certificate, and finite ranks do not identify the infinite exceptional span.**
   **Location:** §5 heading and §5.2, especially lines 246, 253, 259–280; §7, lines 341–343; F1/F4; `scripts/z59_walds_compare.py:56`.
   `rank(cond) < #rows` says only that some tested equations are redundant. Later square equations can still increase rank. It supplies neither a stopping criterion nor proof that a finite-kernel vector satisfies all odd-square equations. Moreover, W1/W1′ concern actual T1 forms and squarefree coefficients, whereas the comparator uses truncated kernels and all C1 indices. Its RHS is an explicitly incomplete elliptic-curve search, not a complete decomposition, and excludes irrational forms. Equality of these two finite numbers is evidence, not the advertised exact eigensystem identification. “If no irrational φ” at line 297 is insufficient: missing rational exceptional forms and the anchor/identification gaps must also be addressed.
   **Fix:** label each finite matrix rank CERTIFIED, but call rank stabilisation and the exceptional-span identification EVIDENCE. A genuine global certificate needs a justified finite test (e.g. Hecke decomposition plus applicable coefficient/Sturm arguments), complete exceptional enumeration or a different argument avoiding enumeration, and the required anchor hypotheses. Important distinction: for a **full-space** finite kernel, rank zero on the tested indices really does prove that every globally valid proxy vanishes on those indices; that upper-bound conclusion needs no saturation assumption.

## Proof steps that pass

- **W1 Step 1:** at n=m² with ℓ∤m, the middle term is ψ(ℓ)(−1/ℓ)a(m²); at n=ℓ^(2j)m², j≥1, it vanishes. The recurrence has last coefficient −ψ(ℓ)²ℓ. Thus the numerator 1−εX and denominator 1−λX+ψ(ℓ)²ℓX², and the induction over distinct prime factors, are correct for weight 3/2.
- **Step 2:** good m² lie in C1. A unary support parameter t′|N can contribute only with t′=1 when gcd(m,N)=1; Thm F removes that component. This also handles nonsquarefree old unary support parameters: their prime factors would have to divide m.
- **Step 3:** the induction is valid because the assertion is quantified over every enlarged finite excluded-prime set P′. Subtracting the shifted relation removes φ₁ while retaining φ₂. Distinct active systems differ at infinitely many good primes: otherwise their “almost all primes” defining spaces coincide, contradicting the direct sum with a nonzero component. No extra Rankin–Selberg argument is needed.
- **Step 4:** nonzero S implies (H1) by Waldspurger Prop. 2. The local square-ratio hypotheses include 2 and hold on C1. Cor. 2 has exactly the quoted character ψ̄χ_{−4}χ_t, powers sqrt(t), and no (H2) hypothesis. Its use proves (ii).
- **Step 5 and W2:** unary support cannot meet squarefree t>1 that is a unit at all bad primes. At n=1, W2 also follows from W1(i), the killed unary anchor and E(1)=0.
- **W1′ as stated:** the polynomial-ring factorisation is sound; the sign is independent of f. The missing-hypothesis problem is in the subsequent generalisation, not that factorisation.
- **W4(a),(d):** Cor. 2 proves the asserted one-way nonvanishing and invalidity implications without an L-nonzero-anchor assumption. **W4(b):** local unit-square invariance and c_p=1 for good squarefree indices give the displayed formula under (H2); the issue is preserving its hypotheses in sums/applications.
- **W3:** the root-number calculation is correct. Since squarefree t∈C1 has t≡1 mod 8, χ_t has conductor t, coprime to the level of G, and χ_t(2)=(t/2)=1 (Kronecker symbol). At every odd bad q, χ_t(q)=1. IK Prop. 14.20 therefore gives the same sign. The stated trivial-central-character conductor-exponent 2/3 supercuspidal example is consistent. The all-zero implication from Théorème 1 is correctly conditional on (H2).
- **W0:** Thm F applies to a single lattice; a zero positive-weight Siegel average forces each nonnegative representation number, including r_L, to vanish. The nonnegative-combination and termwise-recipe consequences follow. **W5:** its elementary eventual equivalence is correct, with the interpretation caveat in finding 7.
