## (2) Tame specialization / lifting

- Andrew Obus, “The (local) lifting problem for curves,” arXiv:1703.01191 (2017; revised 2017), §1, Theorem 1.5, pp. 2–3 of the PDF. Source read: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.01191 (also https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01191). **V**.
- Theorem 1.5 (Obus, explicitly identifying it as Grothendieck’s “tame Riemann existence converse”): for a complete DVR R with algebraically closed residue field k of characteristic p and fraction field K, a smooth projective relative curve X_R, and disjoint marked sections x_i,R, a tamely ramified finite cover f:Y→X, étale off the marked special-fiber points, has a unique finite flat branched-cover lift f_R:Y_R→X_R, étale off those sections, with the same ramification indices and special fiber f; if f is G-Galois, its lift is G-Galois. Obus’s proof cites SGA 1, Exp. XIII, Cor. 2.12 (or Wewers 1999).
- Scope note: this is the lifting theorem. Obus’s Remark 1.9 gives the induced isomorphism on maximal prime-to-p fundamental-group quotients; this is not the assertion that the full tame π1 in characteristic p equals the characteristic-zero π1. The commonly stated prime-to-p completion comparison is supported by the remark, subject to its setup.

## (3) Raynaud / Obus three-point good reduction

- Andrew Obus, “Good reduction of three-point Galois covers,” arXiv:1208.3909v3 (2 Aug 2016), **Theorem 5.2**, p. 1; source read: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.3909 (abstract https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.3909). **V**.
- Exact theorem (near-verbatim): Let G have cyclic p-Sylow subgroup; K0=Frac(W(k)) for algebraically closed k of characteristic p; K/K0 finite of degree e(K), less than the number of conjugacy classes of order-p elements in G. Any three-point G-cover f over K has potentially good reduction, realized over a tame extension L/K of degree dividing the exponent of Z(G); in particular, if Z(G) is trivial, f has good reduction.
- Obus explicitly says this is equivalent, though phrased slightly differently, to Raynaud [Ray99, Théorème 0], in the case the p-Sylow has order exactly p; and notes the ramification indices are prime to p by Raynaud Lemma 4.2.13. **V2** for Raynaud’s restatement only; the original Raynaud theorem itself was not read. Bibliography: Michel Raynaud, “Spécialisation des revêtements en caractéristique p > 0,” *Annales scientifiques de l’École Normale Supérieure*, 4e série, 32(1) (1999), 87–126 (Obus bibliography; source https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.3909).

## (4) Abhyankar conjecture

- Andrew Obus, “The (local) lifting problem for curves,” arXiv:1703.01191, §1, pp. 1–2, source https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.01191. **V** for this survey’s formulation; original Raynaud/Harbater papers not read.
- Affine line formulation in Obus: every finite group G generated by its p-Sylow subgroups (equivalently, the normal subgroup generated by p-elements is G; often called quasi-p) occurs as the Galois group of a finite étale G-Galois cover of A¹_k, for algebraically closed k of characteristic p. Obus attributes this to Raynaud [Ray94].
- Punctured projective line formulation: for U=P¹_k minus r+1 points, the realizable finite groups are those whose quotient by the normal subgroup generated by the p-Sylow subgroups is generated by at most r elements (equivalently, G/N is an r-generated group); this is the standard Harbater generalization. **NOT FOUND exact theorem number / exact statement in primary Harbater source** during this pass. The user’s shorthand “quasi-p groups” applies to A¹, not to arbitrary P¹ minus r+1 points: the quotient condition matters. Survey bibliographic references Raynaud 1994 and Harbater 1994 were not independently completed here.
