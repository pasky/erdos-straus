2. **MAJOR — R8, lines 260–264: the Type II algebra is correct, but Type I confuses ideal and element factorization.**
   Substitution of ce = a+b verifies `(4acd−1)e = p+4a²d`, and `[f,4a,e]` has discriminant −4p when d = 1. However p = 13, `(a,b,c,d) = (1,2,1,5)` is a Type I solution: `40 = 13(1+2)+1` and `(20−13)(40−13) = 7·27 = 189`.    **Fix:** formulate the statement using ideal divisors, stating the necessary order/conductor qualifications; do not imply principality. For d = 1 say **odd** Gaussian norms are 1 modulo 4, not all norms.
