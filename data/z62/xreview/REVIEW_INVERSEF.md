## Derivation details

1. From a triple, Y-face fixes C=(n+u+B)/(4uB); C-face fixes d=4uC−1 and d'=(4Cn+1)/d=4BC−1. Conversely d,d'≡−1 (4C) give positive u,B and n=(dd'−1)/(4C). The d-face fixes r=u, C=(d+1)/(4r), B=(n+r)/d. Both cofactors ≥4C−1 give C≤(n+2)/4. In the final avoidance formula use 0∤(d+1)/4 when d|n.
2. Write C=2^a C₀, C₀ odd. Reciprocity expresses (−C/q) through q mod 4C₀ and, when a>0, q mod 8. Thus it is a real character on units mod 4C. Lemma 2.1 gives χ_C(−1)=−1, so its kernel has index two. For q|4Cp+1, (−C/q)(p/q)=1 and (p/q)=(q/p). The full product is 1 mod 4C, giving even QNR multiplicity. Every actual divisor corresponds to a bounded-multiplicity subproduct, not an arbitrary element of the subgroup generated by the factors.
3. With h(q)=q for QR factors and h(q)=−q for QNR factors, a subproduct equals −1 exactly when it has odd QNR multiplicity and product of h-components 1. For q₁q₂, the only possible nontrivial divisors are q₁,q₂, and either is −1 iff both are. At n=m², every factor q has (−C/q)=(m²/q)=1, so the same arrangement fails vacuously. Independently tested 1160 prime/C slices, character multiplication for C≤40, and square slices.
4. Type II: y'=gY,z'=gZ with gcd(Y,Z)=1 gives xκ'=pgYZ and gcd(κ',YZ)=1. Since p∤x, κ'=pm, m|g; writing g=mg' gives p+s=4g'YZ and ms=Y+Z. Type I: p∤xy implies 4w−1=pt; (tx−w)(ty−w)=w². Reduce δ/w=b/a; coprime a,b both divide w, hence ab|w, and δ≡−w (t) gives t|a+b. Conversely the displayed reciprocal sums are exactly 4/p.
5. Lemma 3.3(a): with m now meaning (Y+Z)/s, d=4g'Ym−1 gives 4g'm²n≡−1 (d). A square n forces (−g'/d)=+1, whereas d≡−1 (4g') gives −1. For (b), every odd q|w has (q/t)=(−t/q)=1; if 2|w then t≡7 (8). Thus all divisors of w have symbol +1 mod t, contradicting a≡−b (t).
6. Prop 4.1: positivity prevents leading-term cancellation. Type II has deg s≤1 (otherwise g',Z,m constant force Y linear), leaving A/B/C. Type I with constant t gives A'/B'. For growing t, order deg a≥deg b: deg b+deg h≤1. Both constant would force unbounded t|1+4b²h. Case (0,1) gives constant λ=4bhe−p and F1. Case (1,0) gives u=4aeh−p dividing p²+4e²h; irreducibility over Q makes u a scalar multiple of that quadratic, yielding F3 after swapping a,b. This is the same irreducibility mechanism as ET §10.
7. “Every identity family is one of [these classes]” is literally too strong: fixing Y=1,Z=2,s=3 on p≡5 (16) is a valid family, but its maximal A-class is p≡5 (8). Classification of polynomial *denominators*, rather than the already-polynomial master parameters, additionally needs ET's finite residue-subdivision/gcd argument. Do not silently export the narrower definition as a new proof of that broader theorem.
