###### Abstract.

In this article, we obtain an explicit version of Heath-Brown’s large sieve inequality for quadratic characters and discuss its applications to L-functions and quadratic fields.

###### 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification

Primary 11L40, 11N35; Secondary 11L26, 11N36

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1. Motivations and applications

In many instances of analytic number theory (e.g. §7, §12, and §17 in [[5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib5)]), one is confronted with the task of estimating mean squares of character sums

A(\mathcal{S},N,a)=\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal{S}}\left|\sum_{n\leq N}a_{n}\chi(n)\right|^{2},

where \mathcal{S} is a designated finite collection of Dirichlet characters, N\geq 1, and a=\{a_{n}\} is any sequence of complex numbers. By the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality, one has A(\mathcal{S},N,a)\leq|\mathcal{S}|N\|a\|^{2}, so

\Delta(\mathcal{S},N)=\sup_{\|a\|\neq 0}{A(\mathcal{S},N,a)\over\|a\|^{2}}<\infty.

A preliminary method to improve \Delta(\mathcal{S},N) makes use of the orthogonality relations of Dirichlet characters. Expanding A(\mathcal{S},N,a) gives

A(\mathcal{S},N,a)=\sum_{n\leq N}|a_{n}|^{2}\sum_{\chi}|\chi(n)|^{2}+\sum_{\chi}\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}}a_{n_{1}}\overline{a_{n_{2}}}\chi(n_{1})\overline{\chi}(n_{2}).

The first “diagonal” term is \leq|\mathcal{S}|\|a\|^{2}, and the second “off-diagonal” term is usually treated with the orthogonality of characters in \mathcal{S}. When \mathcal{S} denotes all characters modulo q, this method gives (see [[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib8), Theorem 6.2])

\Delta(\mathcal{S},N)\leq q+N,

and when \mathcal{S} denotes all primitive characters with conductor \leq Q, there is

(1)\Delta(\mathcal{S},N)\leq Q^{2}+N-1,

which is known as the large sieve inequality [[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib1), §27].

In a fundamental paper, Heath-Brown [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Corollary 2] investigated the case where \mathcal{S} is S(Q), the set of all primitive quadratic characters with conductor \leq Q and proved that for all \varepsilon>0, we have

(2)A(S(Q),N,a)\ll_{\varepsilon}(QN)^{\varepsilon}(Q+N)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\leq N\\
n_{1}n_{2}=\square\end{subarray}}|a_{n_{1}}a_{n_{2}}|,

which is referred to as the quadratic large sieve inequality. The estimate ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) is optimal up to the magnitude of the \ll_{\varepsilon}-constant.

Compared to the large sieve, the inequality ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) achieved a power saving in Q-aspect by relaxing the \ell^{2}-norm \|a\|. When \{a_{n}\} is supported on squarefree integers, \sum_{n_{1}n_{2}=\square}|a_{n_{1}}a_{n_{2}}| coincides with \|a\|^{2}. For general \{a_{n}\}, the quantity can be conveniently estimated. For instance, according to [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), p. 267], there is

(3)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\leq N\\
n_{1}n_{2}=\square\end{subarray}}|a_{n_{1}}a_{n_{2}}|\ll(N\log N)\max_{n\leq N}|a_{n}|^{2},

transforming the inequality ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) into a more applicable form.

We cannot replace \sum_{n_{1}n_{2}=\square}|a_{n_{1}}a_{n_{2}}| in ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) with \|a\|^{2} when \{a_{n}\} is an arbitrary sequence. If a_{n}=\mathbbm{1}_{\square}(n), then

\displaystyle A(S(Q),N,\mathbbm{1}_{\square})\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in S(Q)}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{2}\leq N\\
(n,q_{\chi})=1\end{subarray}}1\Biggr|^{2}\geq\sum_{2<p\leq Q}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{2}\leq N\\
n\neq p\end{subarray}}1\Biggr|^{2}\gg{QN\over\log Q},

but \|\mathbbm{1}_{\square}\|^{2}\leq\sqrt{N}, so A(S(Q),N,a)\ll_{\varepsilon}(QN)^{\varepsilon}(Q+N)\|a\|^{2} is generally false.

The quadratic large sieve has produced Lindelöf-on-average-type results for various moments of L-functions with quadratic twists. For instance, if L(s,\chi) denotes the Dirichlet L-function, Heath-Brown [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Theorem 2] showed that

(4)\sum_{\chi\in S(Q)}|L(1/2+it,\chi)|^{4}\ll_{\varepsilon}(Q(|t|+1))^{1+\varepsilon}.

If f is an SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})-cusp form of weight \kappa that is also an eigenfunction to all Hecke operators with Fourier expansion

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\kappa-1\over 2}e(nz)

and L(s,f\otimes\chi) be its L-function twisted by \chi:

L(s,f\otimes\chi)=\sum_{n\geq 1}{\lambda_{f}(n)\chi(n)\over n^{s}},

then an argument similar to the proof of ([4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E4 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) leads to

(5)\sum_{\chi\in S(Q)}|L(1/2,f\otimes\chi)|^{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}Q^{1+\varepsilon}.

In 2010, Soundararajan and Young [[13](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib13)] indicated that under GRH, when \kappa\equiv 0\pmod{4} and S^{*}(Q) denotes the collection of \chi(\cdot)=\left(2\over\cdot\right)\left(\cdot\over m\right) with 1\leq m\leq Q squarefree odd, there is an asymptotic formula:

(6)\sum_{\chi\in S^{*}(Q)}L(1/2,f\otimes\chi)^{2}\sim C_{f}Q\log Q.

The quadratic large sieve inequality ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) has recently gained renewed interest due to the breakthrough of Xiannan Li [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib7)]. Specifically, by improving ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) for special choices of a=\{a_{n}\}, Li dropped the requirement of GRH for the asymptotic formula ([6](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E6 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), which consequently turns many previously conditional results unconditional.

Apart from moments of L-functions with quadratic twists, the quadratic large sieve has been applied to study the frequency of sign changes of Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight modular forms along fundamental discriminants. Specifically, let k\geq 2 be an integer and g(z) be a weight k+\frac{1}{2} Hecke eigenform for the group \Gamma_{0}(4) with Fourier expansion

g(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}c(n)n^{k-\frac{1}{2}\over 2}e(nz).

Then Lester and Radziwiłł [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib6)] deduced from ([5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E5 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) that when N_{g} is the number of sign changes of the sequence \{c(n)\}_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(c)}, one has

(7)N_{g}\gg X^{1-\varepsilon}.

Moreover, if one assumes GRH, this can be improved to N_{g}\gg X.

The inequality ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) also has applications to quadratic fields. For instance, let f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x] be a fixed separable polynomial of degree \geq 2 and \mathcal{F}(N) be the set of reduced fractions a/b such that 1\leq a,b\leq N. For squarefree number m\geq 1, define R_{f}(m,N) to be the number of r\in\mathcal{F}(N) such that \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{f(r)})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m}). Then Shparlinski [[12](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib12)] applied Heath-Brown’s square sieve [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib3)] and the quadratic large sieve to deduce that

(8)\sum_{m\leq M}\mu^{2}(m)R_{f}(m,N)\ll_{\varepsilon}N^{4/3+\varepsilon}M^{5/6},

which improves the trivial bound O(N^{2}) in the range M\leq N^{4/5}.

For a prime q\equiv 3\pmod{4}, denote by P_{q}(X) the number of rational prime p\leq X that splits in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q}). Then Dunn et al. [[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib2)] used the quadratic large sieve ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) to show that for all \varepsilon>0, the asymptotic

(9)P_{q}(X)\sim\frac{1}{2}\cdot{X\over\log X}

is true for almost all q\sim Q provided that X\geq Q^{\varepsilon} and Q\to+\infty.

### 1.2. Statement of results

By the unique factorization of n into kr^{2} with k squarefree and the well-known decomposition of quadratic characters [[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib1), §5], Heath-Brown deduced the estimate ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) by proving the following [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Theorem 1]:

(10)\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\leq M}\left|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n\leq N}a_{n}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)\right|^{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}(MN)^{\varepsilon}(M+N)\|a\|^{2},

where \sum^{*} means a sum over squarefree odd integers.

In this paper, we establish an explicit form of this inequality:

###### Theorem 1.

Let M,N\geq 1 and \{a_{n}\}_{n\sim N} be a sequence of complex numbers. Define

\Sigma(M,N,a)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\sim M}\left|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n\sim N}a_{n}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)\right|^{2},\quad\mathcal{B}(M,N)=\sup_{\|a\|\neq 0}{\Sigma(M,N,a)\over\|a\|^{2}}.

Then there exists C>0 such that for every \varepsilon>0, we have

(11)\mathcal{B}(M,N)\leq\exp_{4}(C\varepsilon^{-1})\cdot(MN)^{\varepsilon}(M+N).

This improvement exhibits the explicit \varepsilon-dependence in the implied constants, and setting \varepsilon=C_{1}/\log_{4}X for some large C_{1}>0 allows us to replace each X^{\varepsilon}-term in the estimates ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), ([4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E4 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), ([7](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E7 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), ([8](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E8 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), and ([9](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E9 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) with \exp(C_{2}\log X/\log_{4}X) for some C_{2}>C_{1} when X\geq\exp_{4}(1). To demonstrate this refinement, we prove the following:

###### Corollary 1(Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality).

There exists C>0 such that for all Q,N\geq 1 satisfying QN\geq\exp_{4}(1),

\sum_{\chi\in S(Q)}\left|\sum_{n\leq N}a_{n}\chi(n)\right|^{2}\leq\exp_{4}(C(QN)/\log_{4}(QN))(Q+N)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\leq N\\
n_{1}n_{2}=\square\end{subarray}}|a_{n_{1}}a_{n_{2}}|.

###### Corollary 2(Fourth moment of Dirichlet L-functions).

There exists C>0 such that when s=\frac{1}{2}+it, T=|t|+1, and Q\geq\exp_{4}(1),

(12)\sum_{\chi\in S(Q)}|L(s,\chi)|^{4}\leq\exp\{C(QT)/\log_{4}(QT)\}(QT).

###### Corollary 3(Rational primes splitting in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q})).

There exists C>0 such that the asymptotic ([9](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E9 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) is valid for all but \ll Q\exp(-C\log Q/\log_{4}Q) amount of primes q\in(Q,2Q] that are \equiv 3\pmod{4}, provided that Q\to+\infty and X\geq\exp(3C\log Q/\log_{4}Q).

Although our explicit quadratic large sieve ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) can also produce an improvement on ([5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E5 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) analogous to [Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary2 "Corollary 2 (Fourth moment of Dirichlet 𝐿-functions). ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), it is not comparable to the asymptotic obtained by Soundararajan and Young [[13](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib13)] and Xiannan Li [[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib7)]. This is because they exploited information concerning the coefficients \{a_{n}\}. The former invoked the GRH and estimates on shifted moments of L-functions, and the latter utilized the multiplicativity of the coefficients and the approximate functional equations for L-functions. In contrast, our [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") and [Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1 (Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality). ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") make no assumptions on the coefficients \{a_{n}\}. This is particularly useful when these coefficients emerge from sieves. For instance, to obtain the estimate ([8](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E8 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), Shparlinski applied the quadratic large sieve with coefficients \{a_{n}\} given by Legendre symbols. In [Corollary 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary3 "Corollary 3 (Rational primes splitting in ℚ(√{-𝑞})). ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), we apply [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") with \{a_{n}\} supported on primes.

Our [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") could serve as a baseline for further improvements. As \exp_{4}(C\varepsilon^{-1}) still grows rapidly, it would be interesting to see if one can reduce the number of iterations. If the inequality ([11](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E11 "In Theorem 1. ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) is valid with \exp(C\varepsilon^{-1}) instead, then (MN)^{\varepsilon} can be replaced with a fixed positive power of \log(MN), which would be more convenient for various applications.

## 2. Outline for the proof of [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")

As our methods are built on Heath-Brown’s method of recursive estimates, we use the same notations and develop a set of lemmas similar to his. For convenience in bookkeeping, Lemma X in our paper refers to a modification of [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Lemma X], and Proposition X in this paper refers to a result not present in [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4)]. Unless stated otherwise, proofs of results mentioned in this section are detailed in [Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S4 "4. Proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality").

We study \Sigma(M,N,a) via the dual sum

\Sigma_{1}(M,N,a)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\sim M}\left|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n\sim N}a_{n}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)\right|^{2}.

Specifically, \Sigma and \Sigma_{1} are related by

###### Lemma 1.

We have \mathcal{B}(M,N)\leq 2\mathcal{B}(N,M). Moreover, for every sequence \{a_{n}\}_{n\sim N}, there exists \{a_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\sim N} such that |a_{n}^{\prime}|=|a_{n}| and \Sigma(M,N,a)\leq 2\Sigma_{1}(M,N,a^{\prime}).

The proof is omitted as the statement is identical to [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Lemma 1]. From now on, we study \mathcal{B}(M,N) using \Sigma_{1}.

### 2.1. Preparation for Poisson summation

Let

(13)W(x)=\exp\left(\frac{8}{3}-{2\over 1-(x-\frac{3}{2})^{2}}\right)\chi_{[\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2}]}(x).

Then W(x) is a non-negative smooth function with compact support and \geq 1 on [1,2]. Although this is different from that used by Heath-Brown [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), p. 241], it still fulfills the purpose and is convenient to derive explicit estimates.

For each integer m, let s(m) denote the unique positive squarefree divisor of m that makes |m/s(m)| a square. Then for all K\leq M/2, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}\displaystyle\leq\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}W\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)\left|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n\sim N}a_{n}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)\right|^{2}
(14)\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\text{ odd}\\
s(m)>K\end{subarray}}W\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)\left|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n\sim N}a_{n}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)\right|^{2}:=\Sigma_{1}^{(K)}(M,N,a).

We expand \Sigma_{1}^{(K)} as follows:

\Sigma_{1}^{(K)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\text{ odd}\\
s(m)>K\end{subarray}}W\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n_{1},n_{2}\sim N}a_{n_{1}}\overline{a_{n_{2}}}\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right).

Let \Delta=(n_{1},n_{2}). Then \chi(m)=\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right) is a quadratic Dirichlet character with conductor q=n_{1}n_{2}/\Delta^{2}. To utilize this relation, we partition \Sigma_{1}^{(K)} according to the magnitude of \Delta. Define

\Sigma_{2}(M,N,K,\Delta,a)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\sim N\\
(n_{1},n_{2})=\Delta\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}\overline{a_{n_{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\text{ odd}\\
s(m)>K\end{subarray}}W\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right),

so we have

\Sigma_{1}^{(K)}(M,N,a)=\sum_{\Delta\leq 2N}\Sigma_{2}(M,N,K,\Delta,a).

By making trivial estimates, we can restrict our focus to \Sigma_{2} with small \Delta’s:

###### Lemma 2.

Define

\mathcal{B}(M,N,K)=\sup_{\|a\|\neq 0}{\Sigma_{1}^{(K)}(M,N,a)\over\|a\|^{2}},\quad\mathcal{C}(M,N,K,\Delta)=\sup_{\|a\|\neq 0}{\Sigma_{2}(M,N,K,\Delta,a)\over\|a\|^{2}}.

Then there exists absolute C>0 such that for all \varepsilon>0, K\leq M/2, and \Delta_{0}\leq N, there is some N_{1}\leq N/\Delta_{0} such that

\mathcal{B}(M,N,K)\leq\exp_{2}(C\varepsilon^{-1})\cdot N^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{B}(M,N_{1},K)+\sum_{\Delta\leq\Delta_{0}}\mathcal{C}(M,N,K,\Delta).

To handle \mathcal{C}(M,N,K,\Delta) with \Delta\leq\Delta_{0}, we express \Sigma_{2} as a difference:

\Sigma_{2}(M,N,K,\Delta,a)=\Sigma_{3}(M,N,K,\Delta,a)-\Sigma_{4}(M,N,K,\Delta,a),

in which

\Sigma_{3}(M,N,K,\Delta,a)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\sim N\\
(n_{1},n_{2})=\Delta\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}\overline{a_{n_{2}}}\sum_{m\text{ odd}}W\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right)

and

\Sigma_{4}(M,N,K,\Delta,a)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\sim N\\
(n_{1},n_{2})=\Delta\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}\overline{a_{n_{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\text{ odd}\\
s(m)\leq K\end{subarray}}W\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right).

### 2.2. Asymptotic developments

Applying Poisson summation formula to \Sigma_{3} and \Sigma_{4} produces asymptotic formulas:

###### Lemma 3.

Let \varepsilon>0 be arbitrary and M,N,K,\Delta satisfy

N>\Delta,\quad N^{2}M^{-1}(MN)^{\varepsilon}\leq K\leq M(MN)^{-\varepsilon}.

Then \Sigma_{3}=M_{3}+E_{3}\|a\|^{2}, where

M_{3}=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{b\leq K}\sqrt{\frac{M}{b}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\sim N\\
(n_{1},n_{2})=\Delta\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}\overline{a_{n_{2}}}{\varphi(q)\over 2q}\kappa(\Delta,q)\left(\frac{b}{q}\right)\int_{0}^{+\infty}W(x^{2})\mathrm{d}x

and

{E_{3}\over\exp_{2}(C\varepsilon^{-1})}\leq 1+(MN)^{\varepsilon}\Delta\frac{M}{N}m_{0}\sqrt{D_{1}D_{2}}\mathcal{B}\left(B,{N\over D_{1}\Delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}\left(B,{N\over D_{2}\Delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

in which

q={n_{1}n_{2}\over\Delta^{2}},\quad\kappa(\Delta,q)=\prod_{p|\Delta}\left(1-\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)p^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right),\quad m_{0}=\min\left\{1,{N/\sqrt{MB}\over D_{1}D_{2}}\right\},

and B,D_{1},D_{2} are some numbers satisfying

(15)D_{1},D_{2}\gg{1\over\log(2MN)},\quad 1\ll B\ll K.

###### Lemma 4.

Let \varepsilon>0, N>\Delta, and K\leq M^{1-\varepsilon}. Then \Sigma_{4}=M_{4}+E_{4}\|a\|^{2}, where

M_{4}=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\leq K\\
(v,2\Delta)=1\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\frac{M}{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\sim N\\
(n_{1},n_{2})=\Delta\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}\overline{a_{n_{2}}}{\varphi(q\Delta)\over 2q\Delta}\left(\frac{v}{q}\right)\int_{0}^{+\infty}W(x^{2})\mathrm{d}x

and

{E_{4}\over\exp_{2}(C\varepsilon^{-1})}\leq 1+(MN)^{\varepsilon}\left(M\over BD_{1}D_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}\left(B,{N\over D_{1}\Delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}\left(B,{N\over D_{2}\Delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

in which B,D_{1},D_{2} are some numbers satisfying ([15](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E15 "In Lemma 3. ‣ 2.2. Asymptotic developments ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) and

D_{1}D_{2}\gg\varepsilon^{2}(MN)^{-\varepsilon}\Delta^{-1}M^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

To produce upper bounds for \Sigma_{2} (hence for \mathcal{C}(M,N,K,\Delta)), we estimate the difference of the main terms:

###### Lemma 5.

When 1\ll\Delta\ll N and 0<K\leq M/2, there is

M_{3}-M_{4}\leq\exp_{2}(C\varepsilon^{-1})(MN)^{\varepsilon}M^{\frac{1}{2}}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}(K\Delta^{2}L_{1},NL_{2}),

in which L_{1}=C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1}(MN)^{\varepsilon} and L_{2}=C_{2}\varepsilon^{-1}(MN)^{\varepsilon} for some absolute C_{1},C_{2}>0.

### 2.3. Recursive estimates

Combining [Lemma 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma3 "Lemma 3. ‣ 2.2. Asymptotic developments ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), [Lemma 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma4 "Lemma 4. ‣ 2.2. Asymptotic developments ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), and [Lemma 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma5 "Lemma 5. ‣ 2.2. Asymptotic developments ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), we find that a given estimate of \mathcal{B}(M,N) can be used to produce bounds for \mathcal{C}(M,N,K,\Delta). By [Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2. ‣ 2.1. Preparation for Poisson summation ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), this translates into a bound for \mathcal{B}(M,N,K), which is also an upper bound for \mathcal{B}(M,N) according to the inequality ([14](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E14 "In 2.1. Preparation for Poisson summation ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), so we can estimate \mathcal{B}(M,N) recursively.

If there is some \xi>0 and some decreasing F(\cdot)\geq 1 such that

(16)\mathcal{B}(M,N)\leq F(\varepsilon)(MN)^{\varepsilon}(M+N^{\xi})

for all \varepsilon>0, then our lemmas above produce a new inequality similar to ([16](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E16 "In 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) except with a smaller exponent \xi on N:

###### Lemma 6.

Under the assumption ([16](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E16 "In 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), there exist absolute C_{1},C_{2}>0 such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(M,N,K,\Delta)\displaystyle\leq\exp_{2}(C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1})F(\varepsilon)(MN)^{C_{2}\varepsilon}
\displaystyle\times\Delta^{4}(M+N+M^{\frac{1}{2}}K^{\xi-\frac{1}{2}}+M^{\frac{1}{2}}NK^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

###### Lemma 7.

Under the assumption ([16](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E16 "In 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), there exist absolute C_{1},C_{2}>0 such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(M,N)\leq\exp_{2}(C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1})F(\varepsilon)(MN)^{C_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}(M+M^{1-\xi}N^{2\xi-1}).

###### Lemma 8.

If the assumption ([16](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E16 "In 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) holds for some \xi>1, then there exists some absolute P>0 such that

\mathcal{B}(M,N)\leq\exp_{2}(P\varepsilon^{-2})F(\varepsilon^{2}P^{-1})(MN)^{\varepsilon}(M+N^{2-\frac{1}{\xi}}).

By expanding and applying the Pólya-Vinogradov inequality, one has the trivial bound [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), p. 237]:

(17)\mathcal{B}(M,N)\ll M+N^{2}\log N,

which means we can launch the recursion with \xi=2 and F(\varepsilon)=Q\varepsilon^{-1} for some large Q>0. Therefore, it follows from [Lemma 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma8 "Lemma 8. ‣ 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") that when

(18)\xi_{r}=\begin{cases}2&r=0,\\
2-{1\over\xi_{r-1}}&r>0\end{cases}

and

(19)F_{r}(\varepsilon)=\begin{cases}Q\varepsilon^{-1}&r=0,\\
\exp_{2}(P\varepsilon^{-2})F_{r-1}(\varepsilon^{2}P^{-1})&r>0,\end{cases}

the following inequality holds for all r\in\mathbb{N}:

\mathcal{B}(M,N)\leq F_{r}(\varepsilon)(MN)^{\varepsilon}(M+N^{\xi_{r}}).

By induction, one easily finds \xi_{r}=1+(r+1)^{-1}, so we have

(20)\mathcal{B}(M,N)\leq F_{\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}\rfloor}(\varepsilon)(MN)^{2\varepsilon}(M+N).

It remains to estimate F_{\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}\rfloor}(\varepsilon):

###### Proposition 1.

There exists C_{2}>C_{1}>0 such that for all \varepsilon>0 there is

\exp_{4}(C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1})\leq F_{\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}\rfloor}(\varepsilon)\leq\exp_{4}(C_{2}\varepsilon^{-1}).

Plugging this into ([20](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E20 "In 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) and renaming \varepsilon complete the proof of [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality").

## 3. Auxiliary lemmas

In this section, we state and prove results necessary to the treatments of \Sigma_{3}, \Sigma_{4}, and the recursive estimates. Comes first is a monotonicity principle for \mathcal{B}(M,N):

###### Lemma 9.

There exist C,C_{1},C_{2}>0 such that if M_{1},M_{2},N_{1},N_{2} satisfy

M_{2}\geq M_{1}C\log(2M_{1}N_{1}),\quad N_{2}\geq N_{1}C\log(2M_{1}N_{1}),

then

\mathcal{B}(M_{1},N_{1})\leq C_{1}\mathcal{B}(M_{2},N_{1}),\quad\mathcal{B}(M_{1},N_{1})\leq C_{2}\mathcal{B}(M_{1},N_{2}).

###### Proof.

See [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Lemma 9]. ∎

Now, we develop a technical lemma used to estimate bilinear forms emerging in the course of estimating E_{3}, E_{4}, and M_{3}-M_{4}:

###### Lemma 10.

For complex sequences \{a_{n}\}_{n\sim N},\{b_{n}\}_{n\sim N}, define

(21)S(M,N,D,a,b)=\sum_{d\sim D}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\sim M}\Biggl|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1},n_{2})=1\\
d|n_{1}n_{2}\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}b_{n_{2}}\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right)\Biggr|

Then we have

S\leq\exp_{2}(C\eta^{-1})(MN)^{\eta}(D_{1}D_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}(M,N/D_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}(M,N/D_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\|a\|\cdot\|b\|,

in which D_{1} and D_{2} satisfy

{1\over\log(2MN)}\ll D_{1},D_{2}\ll D,\quad D_{1}D_{2}\asymp{D\over\log^{2}(2MN)}.

###### Proof.

As the result becomes trivial for large D, we assume D\ll N. Moreover, assume without loss of generality that \{a_{n}\} and \{b_{n}\} are supported on squarefree odd integers.

Since (n_{1},n_{2})=1, d factors uniquely into d_{1}d_{2} such that d_{1}|n_{1} and d_{2}|n_{2}, so

\displaystyle S\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\\
d_{1}d_{2}\sim D\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\sim M}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1},n_{2})=1\\
d_{1}|n_{1},d_{2}|n_{2}\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}b_{n_{2}}\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right)\Biggr|
\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\\
d_{1}d_{2}\sim D\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\sim M}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\\
d_{1}|n_{1},d_{2}|n_{2}\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}b_{n_{2}}\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right)\sum_{\delta|(n_{1},n_{2})}\mu(\delta)\Biggr|
\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\delta\leq 2N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\\
d_{1}d_{2}\sim D\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\sim M}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\delta,d_{1}]|n_{1}\\
[\delta,d_{2}]|n_{2}\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}b_{n_{2}}\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right)\Biggr|

Notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\\
d_{1}d_{2}\sim D\end{subarray}}\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\leq{\log D\over\log 2}}\sum_{d_{1}\sim 2^{\mu}}\sum_{d_{2}\sim D/d_{1}}\leq\sum_{\mu}\sum_{d_{1}\sim 2^{\mu}}\sum_{{D\over 2^{\mu+1}}<d_{2}\leq{D\over 2^{\mu-1}}}
(22)\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mu}\sum_{d_{1}\sim 2^{\mu}}\sum_{\nu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{D\over 2^{\mu+1}}<d_{2}\leq{D\over 2^{\mu-1}}\\
d_{2}\sim 2^{\nu}\end{subarray}},

so we have

\displaystyle S\leq\#\{(\mu,\nu)\}\times\sum_{\delta\leq 2N}\max_{\mu,\nu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}\sim 2^{\mu}\\
d_{2}\sim 2^{\nu}\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\sim M}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\delta,d_{1}]|n_{1}\\
[\delta,d_{2}]|n_{2}\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}b_{n_{2}}\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right)\Biggr|.

By the relation ([22](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E22 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), we see that each \mu corresponds to a uniformly bounded range of \nu, so \#\{(\mu,\nu)\}=O(\log N)=O(\eta^{-1}N^{\eta/4}), which means there exists some P_{1},P_{2} such that

1\ll P_{1},P_{2}\ll D,\quad P_{1}P_{2}\asymp D,

and

S\ll(\log N)\sum_{\delta\leq 2N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}\sim P_{1}\\
d_{2}\sim P_{2}\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\sim M}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\delta,d_{1}]|n_{1}\\
[\delta,d_{2}]|n_{2}\end{subarray}}a_{n_{1}}b_{n_{2}}\left(m\over n_{1}n_{2}\right)\Biggr|.

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality on the right-hand side gives

(23)S\ll(\log N)\Sigma_{a}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Sigma_{b}^{\frac{1}{2}},

in which

\Sigma_{a}=\sum_{\delta\leq 2N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1}\sim P_{1}\\
d_{2}\sim P_{2}\end{subarray}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\sim M}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
[\delta,d_{1}]|n\end{subarray}}a_{n}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)\Biggr|^{2}

and \Sigma_{b} is defined similarly. By the duality principle for bilinear forms (see [[5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib5), p. 170]) and [Lemma 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma1 "Lemma 1. ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), there is

\displaystyle\sum_{m\sim M}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
[\delta,d_{1}]|n\end{subarray}}a_{n}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)\Biggr|^{2}\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{B}(N/d_{1},M)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
[\delta,d_{1}]|n\end{subarray}}|a_{n}|^{2}
\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{B}(M,N/d_{1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
[\delta,d_{1}]|n\end{subarray}}|a_{n}|^{2},

so by [Lemma 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma9 "Lemma 9. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), there is some C_{0}>0 such that when \theta=C_{0}\log(2MN), there is \mathcal{B}(M,N/d_{1})\ll\mathcal{B}(M,\theta N/P_{1}). Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{a}\displaystyle\ll P_{2}\mathcal{B}(M,\theta N/P_{1})\sum_{\delta\leq 2N}\sum_{d_{1}\sim P_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
[\delta,d_{1}]|n\end{subarray}}|a_{n}|^{2}
\displaystyle=P_{2}\mathcal{B}(M,\theta N/P_{1})\sum_{n\sim N}|a_{n}|^{2}\sum_{\sigma|n}\mathop{\sum_{\delta\leq 2N}\sum_{d_{1}\sim P_{1}}}_{[\delta,d_{1}]=\sigma}1.
\displaystyle\leq P_{2}\mathcal{B}(M,\theta N/P_{1})\sum_{n\sim N}|a_{n}|^{2}\sum_{\sigma|n}3^{\omega(\sigma)}
\displaystyle=P_{2}\mathcal{B}(M,\theta N/P_{1})\sum_{n\sim N}|a_{n}|^{2}4^{\omega(n)}.

Finally, notice that when n\sim N is squarefree, 4^{\omega(n)}=[d(n)]^{2}\leq\exp_{2}(C_{1}\eta^{-1})N^{\eta/2}, so

\Sigma_{a}\ll\exp_{2}(C_{1}\eta^{-1})N^{\eta/2}P_{2}\mathcal{B}(M,\theta N/P_{1})\|a\|^{2}.

By the same line of reasoning, we obtain a similar bound for \Sigma_{b}. Plugging these bounds into ([23](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E23 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) and setting D_{1}=P_{1}/\theta,D_{2}=P_{2}/\theta complete the proof. ∎

The next few lemmas are dedicated to Poisson summations:

###### Lemma 11.

Let W be a smooth function whose support is a compact subset of (0,+\infty). Then for any positive squarefree odd q, there is

\sum_{m\geq 1}W\left(\frac{m}{M}\right)\left(\frac{m}{q}\right)={M\tau(q)\over q}\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{W}\left(h\over q/M\right)\left(\frac{h}{q}\right),

where \tau(q) denotes the quadratic Gauss sum with modulus q.

###### Proof.

See [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Lemma 11]. ∎

The following lemma is used to separate variables during the treatment of E_{3}:

###### Lemma 12.

Let \varrho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R} be a Schwartz function. Define \varrho_{+}(s),\varrho_{-}(s) by

\varrho_{\pm}(s)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\varrho(\pm x)x^{s-1}\mathrm{d}x.

Then \varrho_{+}(s),\varrho_{-}(s) are holomorphic in \Re(s)>0 and satisfy

\varrho_{+}(s),\varrho_{-}(s)\ll_{A,\sigma}|t|^{-A}\quad(s=\sigma+it).

In addition, for any \sigma>0, we have

\varrho(\pm x)={1\over 2\pi i}\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}x^{-s}\varrho_{\pm}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

and when \sigma\to 0^{+}, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\varrho_{\pm}(\sigma+it)|\mathrm{d}t\ll\sigma^{-1}.

###### Proof.

Every result apart from the last one is identical to those in [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Lemma 12]. For the last result, notice that integration by parts gives \rho_{\pm}(s)\ll(\sigma+|t|^{2})^{-1}. ∎

The following variant of the Poisson summation formula helps identify the main terms and error terms in \Sigma_{3} and \Sigma_{4}:

###### Lemma 13.

Let \psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R} be a Schwartz function and G,H:\mathbb{[}0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{[}0,+\infty) be increasing functions such that

|\psi(x)|\leq G(A)|x|^{-A},\quad|\widehat{\psi}(x)|\leq H(A)|x|^{-A}.

If A\geq 0, 0<X_{1}\leq X\leq X_{2}, L\geq(X_{2}/X)^{2} and J=\min(X/X_{1},X/X_{2}), then for any integer k\geq 2, there is

(24)\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
(n,k)=1\end{subarray}}\psi\left(\frac{n}{X}\right)\displaystyle={\varphi(k)\over k}X\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x-\psi(0)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d\leq X_{2}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)
\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
d>X_{2}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\frac{X}{d}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x
\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq|l|\leq L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|k\\
X_{1}<d\leq X_{2}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\frac{X}{d}\widehat{\psi}\left(lX\over d\right)
\displaystyle+\max\{G(A+1),H(A+2)\}\cdot O(XJ^{-A}).

###### Proof.

By tracking the implied constants in the proof of [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Lemma 13], one finds that the error term is of the form

\ll G(A_{1})X(X_{2}/X)^{1-A_{1}}+H(A_{2})X(X/X_{1})^{-A_{2}}+H(A_{3})X(X_{2}/X)^{2-A_{3}},

so setting A_{1}=A+1, A_{2}=A, and A_{3}=A+2 gives the desired result. ∎

The next few results concern explicit estimates for smooth weight functions. Comes first is a chain rule for higher-order derivatives:

###### Proposition 2(Faà di Bruno).

Let f(x) be and g(x) be n-times differentiable. Then

{\mathrm{d}^{n}\over\mathrm{d}x^{n}}[f(g(x))]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n}\geq 0\\
r_{1}+2r_{2}+\cdots+nr_{n}=n\end{subarray}}{n!\over r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{n}!}f^{(n)}(g(x))\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left[g^{(j)}(x)\over j!\right]^{r_{j}}.

In particular, when g(x) is a quadratic polynomial, there is

{\mathrm{d}^{n}\over\mathrm{d}x^{n}}[f(g(x))]=\sum_{0\leq r\leq n/2}{n!\over 2^{r}r!(n-2r)!}f^{(n-r)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n-2r}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{r}.

###### Proof.

For the general case, see [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib10), Theorem 2]. The following is a proof for the case where g(x) is a quadratic polynomial:

When n=0, there is nothing to prove. Suppose the result holds at n. Since

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}\over\mathrm{d}x}\displaystyle\left\{f^{(n-r)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n-2r}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{r}\right\}
\displaystyle=f^{(n+1-r)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n+1-2r}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{r}
\displaystyle+(n-2r)f^{(n-r)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n-1-2r}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{r+1},

writing n=2\mu+\nu for \nu\in\{0,1\} gives

(25)\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}^{n+1}\over\mathrm{d}x^{n+1}}[f(g(x))]\displaystyle=f^{(n+1)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n+1}
(26)\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{\mu}{n!\over 2^{r}r!(n-2r)!}f^{(n+1-r)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n+1-2r}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{r}
(27)\displaystyle+\sum_{r=0}^{\mu-1}{n!\over 2^{r}r!(n-1-2r)!}f^{(n-r)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n-1-2r}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{r+1}
(28)\displaystyle+{n!(n-2\mu)\over 2^{\mu}\mu!(n-2\mu)!}f^{(n-\mu)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n-1-2\mu}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{\mu+1}.

By re-indexing the term ([27](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E27 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), we compute that the sum of ([26](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E26 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) and ([27](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E27 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) is

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\mu}\left[{n!\over 2^{r}r!(n-2r)!}+{n!\over 2^{r-1}(r-1)!(n+1-2r)!}\right]
\displaystyle\times f^{(n+1-r)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n+1-2r}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{r}
\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{\mu}{(n+1)!\over 2^{r}r!(n+1-2r)!}f^{(n+1-r)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n+1-2r}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{r}.

Extending this sum to r=0 includes the right-hand side of ([25](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E25 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")). When \nu=0, ([28](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E28 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) vanishes. When \nu=1, ([28](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E28 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) becomes

\displaystyle={n!\over 2^{\mu}\mu!}f^{(n-\mu)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n-1-2\mu}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{\mu+1}
\displaystyle={n!(\mu+1)\over 2^{\mu}(\mu+1)!}f^{(n+1-(\mu+1))}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n+1-2(\mu+1)}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{\mu+1}.

Notice that \mu+1=(n+1)/2=\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor, so in all cases, the combination of ([25](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E25 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"))-([28](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E28 "In Proof. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) is

=\sum_{0\leq r\leq(n+1)/2}{(n+1)!\over 2^{r}r!(n+1-2r)!}f^{(n+1-r)}(g(x))[g^{\prime}(x)]^{n+1-2r}[g^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{r},

completing the proof. ∎

###### Proposition 3.

Let W(x) be as in ([13](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E13 "In 2.1. Preparation for Poisson summation ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")). Then there exist absolute constants C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0 satisfying

|W^{(j)}(x)|\leq C_{1}^{j}j^{3j},\quad|\widehat{W}^{(j)}(k)|\leq C_{2}^{j}C_{3}^{A}A^{3A}|k|^{-A}

for all A,j\geq 1.

###### Proof.

Let f(y)=e^{y} and g(x)={-1\over 1-x}. Define h(x)=f(g(x)) and

\omega(x)=e^{-{2\over 1-x^{2}}}\chi_{[-1,1]}(x)=h(x)\cdot h(-x)\chi_{[-1,1]}(x),

so there is W(x)=e^{\frac{8}{3}}\omega\left(x-\frac{3}{2}\right). Since for 0\leq x<1,

g^{(j)}(x)=(-1)(-2)\cdots(-j)\cdot{1\over(x-1)^{j+1}}=-j![-g(x)]^{j+1}.

By [Proposition 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmproposition2 "Proposition 2 (Faà di Bruno). ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), we deduce that for 0\leq x<1,

\displaystyle|h^{(n)}(x)|\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n}\geq 0\\
r_{1}+2r_{2}+\dots+nr_{n}=n\end{subarray}}{n!\over r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{n}!}e^{g(x)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}[-g(x)]^{(j+1)r_{j}}
\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n}\geq 0\\
r_{1}+2r_{2}+\dots+nr_{n}=n\end{subarray}}{n!\over r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{n}!}e^{g(x)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}[-g(x)]^{2jr_{j}}
\displaystyle=[-g(x)]^{2n}e^{g(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n}\geq 0\\
r_{1}+2r_{2}+\dots+nr_{n}=n\end{subarray}}{n!\over r_{1}!r_{2}!\cdots r_{n}!}\leq[-g(x)]^{2n}e^{g(x)}n!e^{n}.

Finally, by the elementary inequality u^{R}e^{-u}\leq R^{R}e^{-R} for all R,u>0, we have

|h^{(n)}(x)|\leq(2n)^{2n}n!e^{-n}\leq 4^{n}n^{3n}.

By the Leibniz rule for derivatives, this means

\displaystyle|\omega^{(j)}(x)|\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{j}\binom{j}{n}[4^{n}n^{3n}]\cdot[4^{j-n}(j-n)^{3(j-n)}]
\displaystyle\leq 4^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{j}\binom{j}{n}[j^{3n}]\cdot[j^{3(j-n)}]\leq 8^{j}j^{3j}.

Thus, we can take C_{1}=8e^{\frac{8}{3}} in the estimate for W^{(j)}(x). The estimate for \widehat{W}^{(j)}(k) is a consequence of integration by parts. ∎

The following propositions are used to establish explicit asymptotic estimates for the approximate functional equation for L(s,\chi)^{2}:

###### Proposition 4.

Let s=\sigma+it with \sigma bounded and |t|\geq 1 and u=x+iy with x\to+\infty. Then

{\Gamma(s+u)\over\Gamma(s)}\ll C^{x}x^{2x}|t|^{x}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}|y|}.

uniformly for all y\in\mathbb{R} and |t|\geq 1.

###### Proof.

By Stirling’s approximation, when |\eta|\ll|\xi|, there is

|\Gamma(\eta+i\xi)|=|\xi|^{\eta-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|\xi|}e^{O(|\eta|)}.

Combining with the fact that |\Gamma(\eta+i\xi)|\leq\Gamma(\eta)\ll\eta^{\eta} for \eta>0, we have

\displaystyle{\Gamma(s+u)\over\Gamma(s)}\displaystyle\ll C_{1}^{x}x^{x}{(|t+y|+1)^{\sigma+x-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|t+y|}\over|t|^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|t|}}
\displaystyle=C_{1}^{x}x^{x}|t|^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}(|t+y|+1)^{x+\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}(|t|-|t+y|)}.

If |y|\leq 4|t|, it follows from |t|-|t+y|\leq|y| that

(|t+y|+1)^{x+\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}(|t|-|t+y|)}\ll C_{2}^{x}|t|^{x+\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}|y|}.

If |y|>4|t|, then it follows from |t|-|t+y|\leq 2|t|-|y|<-|y|/2 that

\displaystyle(|t+y|+1){}^{x+\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}(|t|-|t+y|)}\ll C_{2}^{x}|t|^{x+\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}|y|}
\displaystyle\ll C_{3}^{x}(\pi|y|/4)^{x+\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\pi|y|/4}.
\displaystyle\leq C_{3}^{x}\left(x+\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x+\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\ll C_{4}^{x}x^{x}.

Combining the bounds from both cases gives the desired result. ∎

###### Proposition 5.

If 0\leq\sigma\leq 1, |t|\geq 1, and a\in\{0,1\}. Define s=\sigma+it and

(29)V_{s}(y)={1\over 2\pi i}\int_{3-i\infty}^{3+i\infty}(\pi y)^{-u}e^{u^{2}}\left\{\Gamma\left(s+u+a\over 2\right)\over\Gamma\left(s+a\over 2\right)\right\}^{2}{\mathrm{d}u\over u}.

Then for all A\geq 1, there is

(30)V_{s}(y)\ll C^{A}A^{A}e^{A^{2}}(y/|t|)^{-A}

as y\to+\infty and

(31)V_{s}(y)=1+O\{(y/|t|)^{1/3}\}

as y\to 0^{+}. The implied constants are absolute.

###### Proof.

To prove the bound ([30](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E30 "In Proposition 5. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), we move the line of integration to \Re(u)=A, so writing u=A+iv, [Proposition 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmproposition4 "Proposition 4. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") gives

\left\{\Gamma\left(s+u+a\over 2\right)\over\Gamma\left(s+a\over 2\right)\right\}^{2}\ll C_{1}^{A}A^{A}|t|^{A}e^{\frac{\pi}{2}|y|}.

Notice that |e^{u^{2}}|=e^{A^{2}-v^{2}}, so ([29](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E29 "In Proposition 5. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) becomes

V_{s}(y)\ll C_{1}^{A}A^{A}(\pi y)^{-A}|t|^{A}e^{A^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}|v|-v^{2}}\over|v|+1}\mathrm{d}v\ll C_{2}^{A}A^{A}e^{A^{2}}(y/|t|)^{-A}.

For the asymptotic ([31](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E31 "In Proposition 5. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), observe that the integrand has a simple pole at u=0 with residue 1, so moving the line of integration to \Re(u)=-\frac{1}{3} and proceed similarly give us the desired result. ∎

###### Proof of [Lemma 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma7 "Lemma 7. ‣ 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality").

Replacing [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Lemma 2] and [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), Lemma 6] with our explicit [Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma2 "Lemma 2. ‣ 2.1. That is, whenever 1\leq\Delta_{0}<N\leq N_{0},

(34)N_{0}(MN_{0})^{\varepsilon}\leq M,

and K=N_{0}^{2}M^{-1}(MN_{0})^{\varepsilon}, there is

\mathcal{B}(M,N,K)\leq G\{\mathcal{B}(M,N_{1},K)+H\},

in which N_{1}\leq N\Delta_{0}^{-1},

\mathcal{B}(M,N_{r-1},K)+{GH\over G-1}\leq G\left\{\mathcal{B}(M,N_{r},K)+{GH\over G-1}\right\},

\mathcal{B}(M,N_{0},K)\leq G^{r}\{\mathcal{B}(M,N_{r},K)+GH\}\leq G^{2r}\{\mathcal{B}(M,N_{r},K)+H\}.

Let \Delta_{0}=N_{0}^{1/R}, so there is some 1\leq r_{0}\leq R such that N_{r_{0}}\leq\Delta_{0}<N_{r_{0}-1}, allowing us to conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(M,N_{0},K)\displaystyle\leq\exp_{2}(C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1}+\log 2r_{0})(MN_{0})^{2C_{2}r_{0}\varepsilon}\{\mathcal{B}(M,N_{r_{0}},K)

Let R=\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}, C_{3}>2C_{1}, and C_{4}>2C_{2}, so we have

\mathcal{B}(M,N_{0},K)\leq\exp_{2}(C_{3}\varepsilon^{-1})(MN_{0})^{C_{4}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}F(\varepsilon)(M+M^{1-\xi}N_{0}^{2\xi-1}),

Since \Sigma_{1}\leq\Sigma_{1}^{(K)}, this is an upper bound for \mathcal{B}(M,N_{0}) as well, completing the proof in the case under the assumption ([34](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S4.E34 "In Proof of . ‣ 4.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 4. Proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")). For the case where this assumption is false, it follows directly from the estimate ([16](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E16 "In 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) and \xi\leq 2 that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(M,N_{0})\displaystyle\leq F(\varepsilon)(MN_{0})^{\varepsilon}(M+N_{0}^{\xi})
\displaystyle=F(\varepsilon)(MN_{0})^{\varepsilon}(M+M^{1-\xi}N_{0}^{2\xi-1}(M/N_{0})^{\xi-1})
\displaystyle\leq F(\varepsilon)(MN_{0})^{2\varepsilon}(M+M^{1-\xi}N_{0}^{2\xi-1}),

which is consistent with the conclusion as 2\varepsilon\leq C_{4}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}. ∎

###### Proof of [Lemma 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma8 "Lemma 8. ‣ 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality").

Let \alpha>0 be some parameter to be determined later. If M\leq N^{\alpha}, then it follows from [Lemma 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma9 "Lemma 9. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") and [Lemma 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma7 "Lemma 7. ‣ 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") that there is some C_{0}>0 satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(M,N)\displaystyle\ll\mathcal{B}(N^{\alpha}C_{0}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}(MN)^{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}},N)
\displaystyle\ll\exp_{2}(C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1})F(\varepsilon)[\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\alpha+1}(MN)^{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}]^{C_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}(N^{\alpha}+N^{\alpha(1-\xi)+2\xi-1})
\displaystyle\leq\exp_{2}(C_{3}\varepsilon^{-1})F(\varepsilon)(MN)^{C_{4}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}(N^{\alpha}+N^{\alpha(1-\xi)+2\xi-1}).

Let \alpha=2-\xi^{-1} so that both exponents in the last line are equal. Suppose M\geq N^{\alpha}. Then it follows from [Lemma 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmlemma7 "Lemma 7. ‣ 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(M,N)\displaystyle\leq\exp_{2}(C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1})F(\varepsilon)(MN)^{C_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}(M+N^{\alpha(1-\xi)+2\xi-1})
\displaystyle=\exp_{2}(C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1})F(\varepsilon)(MN)^{C_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}(M+N^{2-\xi^{-1}}).

Therefore, in any case, there is

\mathcal{B}(M,N)\leq\exp_{2}(C_{3}\varepsilon^{-1})F(\varepsilon)(MN)^{C_{4}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}(M+N^{2-\xi^{-1}}),

provided that C_{3}>C_{1}. Let Q=\max(C_{4}^{2},1), so there is

\mathcal{B}(M,N)\leq\exp_{2}(C_{3}Q^{2}\varepsilon^{-2})F(\varepsilon^{2}/Q)(MN)^{\varepsilon}(M+N^{2-\xi^{-1}}).

Setting P=C_{3}Q^{2}>Q and using the monotonicity of F(\cdot) produce the conclusion. ∎

###### Proof of [Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmproposition1 "Proposition 1. ‣ 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality").

Define \varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon and \varepsilon_{r}=\varepsilon_{r-1}^{2}/P. Then \varepsilon_{r}=P^{1-2^{r}}\varepsilon^{2^{r}} and it follows from the definition ([19](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S2.E19 "In 2.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 2. Outline for the proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) of F_{r}(\varepsilon) that

(35)F_{r}(\varepsilon)=Q\exp(e^{1/\varepsilon_{1}}+e^{1/\varepsilon_{2}}+\dots+e^{1/\varepsilon_{r}})\varepsilon_{r}^{-1},

which means when r=\lfloor\varepsilon\rfloor, there is

\displaystyle F_{r}(\varepsilon)\displaystyle\leq Q\exp(re^{1/\varepsilon_{r}})\varepsilon_{r}^{-1}\leq Q\exp\left(\varepsilon^{-1}e^{P^{2^{\varepsilon^{-1}}-1}\varepsilon^{-2^{\varepsilon^{-1}}}}\right)P^{2^{\varepsilon^{-1}}-1}\varepsilon^{-2^{\varepsilon^{-1}}}
\displaystyle=Q\exp_{2}\left(P^{2^{\varepsilon^{-1}}-1}e^{2^{\varepsilon^{-1}}\log\varepsilon^{-1}}+\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right)P^{2^{\varepsilon^{-1}}-1}e^{2^{\varepsilon^{-1}}\log\varepsilon^{-1}}
\displaystyle\leq Q\exp_{2}(\exp_{2}(C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1})+\log\varepsilon^{-1})\exp_{2}(C_{2}\varepsilon^{-1})\leq\exp_{4}(C_{3}\varepsilon^{-1}).

On the other side, the expression ([35](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S4.E35 "In Proof of . ‣ 4.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 4. Proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) can be bounded below using

\displaystyle F_{r}(\varepsilon)\displaystyle\geq\exp_{2}(1/\varepsilon_{r})\geq\exp_{3}\{(2^{r}-1)(\log P+\log\varepsilon^{-1})\}
\displaystyle\geq\exp_{3}(2^{r}C_{4})\geq\exp_{3}(2^{\varepsilon^{-1}-1}C_{4})\geq\exp_{4}(C_{5}\varepsilon^{-1}),

completing the proof. ∎

Subsequently, we apply this result to moments of Dirichlet L-functions ([Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary2 "Corollary 2 (Fourth moment of Dirichlet 𝐿-functions). ‣ 1.2. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), and rational primes splitting in quadratic fields ([Corollary 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary3 "Corollary 3 (Rational primes splitting in ℚ(√{-𝑞})). ‣ 1.2. ### 5.1. Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality

To prove [Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1 (Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality). ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") from [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), we begin by proving an explicit form of the inequality ([10](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E10 "In 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) by breaking the range of m and n into dyadic intervals so that for M,N\geq 2

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\leq M}\left|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n\leq N}a_{n}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)\right|^{2}\displaystyle\ll(\log M)\max_{1\leq M^{\prime}\leq M}\sum_{m\sim M^{\prime}}\left|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n\leq N}a_{n}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)\right|^{2}
\displaystyle\ll(\log M)(\log N)^{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq M^{\prime}\leq M\\
1\leq N^{\prime}\leq N\end{subarray}}\sum_{m\sim M^{\prime}}\left|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n\sim N^{\prime}}a_{n}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)\right|^{2}
\displaystyle\leq(\log MN)^{3}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq M^{\prime}\leq M\\
1\leq N^{\prime}\leq N\end{subarray}}\mathcal{B}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})\sum_{n\sim N^{\prime}}|a_{n}|^{2}.

Notice that \log MN\ll\varepsilon^{-1}(MN)^{\varepsilon/2}, so plugging in [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") with \varepsilon replaced with \varepsilon/2 gives

(36)\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{m\leq M}\left|\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{n\leq N}a_{n}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)\right|^{2}\leq\exp_{4}(C\varepsilon^{-1})(MN)^{\varepsilon}(M+N)\|a\|^{2}.

For the inequality ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E2 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), we can reuse the arguments in [[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib4), p. 265–267]. To obtain explicit \varepsilon-dependence in \ll-constants, it suffices to notice that all X^{\varepsilon}-terms during the derivation are introduced only through the use of ([36](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S5.E36 "In 5.1. Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality ‣ 5. Applications ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")), the divisor bound d(n)\leq\exp_{2}(C\varepsilon^{-1})n^{\varepsilon}, and \log X\ll\varepsilon^{-1}X^{\varepsilon}. Thus, we have

(37)\sum_{\chi\in S(Q)}\left|\sum_{n\leq N}a_{n}\chi(n)\right|^{2}\leq\exp_{4}(C\varepsilon^{-1})(QN)^{\varepsilon}(Q+N)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\leq N\\
n_{1}n_{2}=\square\end{subarray}}|a_{n_{1}}a_{n_{2}}|,

which becomes [Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary1 "Corollary 1 (Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality). ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") after setting \varepsilon=C^{\prime}/\log_{4}(QN) for some large C^{\prime}>0.

### 5.2. Moments of Dirichlet L-functions

In this section, we prove [Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary2 "Corollary 2 (Fourth moment of Dirichlet 𝐿-functions). ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") by showing that when s=\frac{1}{2}+it, T=|t|+1, and S^{\prime}(Q)=S(2Q)\smallsetminus S(Q) represents all primitive quadratic characters \chi modulo q_{\chi}\sim Q, there is

(38)S(Q,s)=\sum_{\chi\in S^{\prime}(Q)}|L(s,\chi)|^{4}\ll\exp_{4}(C\varepsilon^{-1})(QT)^{1+\varepsilon},

which immediately implies ([4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S1.E4 "In 1.1. Motivations and applications ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) by dyadic summation.

Heath-Brown’s original argument began with the formula

L(s,\chi)^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}{d(n)\chi(n)\over n^{s}}e^{-n/U}-{1\over 2\pi i}\int_{\alpha-i\infty}^{\alpha+i\infty}U^{w-s}\Gamma(w-s)L(w,\chi)^{2}\mathrm{d}w

for some 0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2} and used a recursive estimate process to obtain the bound

(39)S(Q,\sigma+it)\ll_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(QT)^{\varepsilon}(Q+(QT)^{2-2\sigma})

valid for all fixed \sigma>\frac{1}{2}. Explicating Heath-Brown’s argument indeed produces ([38](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S5.E38 "In 5.2. Moments of Dirichlet 𝐿-functions ‣ 5. Applications ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")). However, as demonstrated in [Section 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S4.SS3 "4.3. Recursive estimates ‣ 4. Proof of ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), tracking \ll-constants in recursive estimates is a complicated procedure. In this section, we give a simpler proof of ([38](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S5.E38 "In 5.2. Moments of Dirichlet 𝐿-functions ‣ 5. Applications ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) by appealing to the approximate functional equation, which completely avoids the need for recursion.

For primitive quadratic \chi modulo q=q_{\chi}, it follows from [[5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib5), §5.2] that

L(s,\chi)^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}{d(n)\chi(n)\over n^{\frac{1}{2}+it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}+it}\left(n\over q\right)+\varepsilon(t)\sum_{n\geq 1}{d(n)\chi(n)\over n^{\frac{1}{2}-it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}-it}\left(n\over q\right),

where V_{s}(y) is defined in ([29](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S3.E29 "In Proposition 5. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) and \varepsilon(t) is some function satisfying |\varepsilon(t)|=1.

Applying Cauchy–Schwartz inequality, we obtain

S(Q,s)\leq 2S_{1}(Q,t)+2S_{1}(Q,-t),

where

S_{1}(Q,t)=\sum_{\chi\in S^{\prime}(Q)}\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}{d(n)\chi(n)\over n^{\frac{1}{2}+it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}+it}\left(n\over q_{\chi}\right)\right|^{2}.

By [Proposition 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmproposition5 "Proposition 5. ‣ 3. Auxiliary lemmas ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"), when X\gg QT and A\gg 1, we have

\sum_{n>X}{d(n)\chi(n)\over n^{\frac{1}{2}+it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}+it}\left(n\over q_{\chi}\right)\ll e^{2A^{2}}X(QTX^{-1})^{A}.

Setting 0<\delta\leq 1, X=(QT)^{1+\delta}, and A=2\delta^{-1} makes this term \ll\exp(8\delta^{-2}), so one has

\displaystyle S_{1}(Q,t)\displaystyle\ll Q\exp(16\delta^{-2})+\sum_{\chi\in S^{\prime}(Q)}\left|\sum_{n\leq X}{d(n)\chi(n)\over n^{\frac{1}{2}+it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}+it}\left(n\over q_{\chi}\right)\right|^{2}.
\displaystyle\ll Q\exp(16\delta^{-2})+(\log X)^{2}\max_{1\leq N\leq X}\sum_{\chi\in S^{\prime}(Q)}\left|\sum_{n\sim N}{d(n)\chi(n)\over n^{\frac{1}{2}+it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}+it}\left(n\over q_{\chi}\right)\right|^{2}.

By ([37](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#S5.E37 "In 5.1. Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality ‣ 5. Applications ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality")) and the fact that V_{s}(y)=O(1), we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in S^{\prime}(Q)}\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\sim N}{d(n)\chi(n)\over n^{\frac{1}{2}+it}}V_{\frac{1}{2}+it}\left(n\over q_{\chi}\right)\right|^{2}
\displaystyle\ll\exp_{4}(C_{1}\varepsilon^{-1})(QN)^{\varepsilon/8}(Q+N)N\max_{n\sim N}|d(n)^{2}n^{-1}|
\displaystyle\ll\exp_{4}(C_{2}\varepsilon^{-1})(QN)^{\varepsilon/4}(Q+N)\leq\exp_{4}(C_{2}\varepsilon^{-1})(QX)^{\varepsilon/4}(Q+X)
\displaystyle\ll\exp_{4}(C_{2}\varepsilon^{-1})(QT)^{1+{3\varepsilon\over 4}+\delta}.

Finally, set \delta=\varepsilon/8, so it follows from \log X\ll\varepsilon^{-1}(QT)^{\varepsilon/16} and \exp(16\delta^{-2})\ll\exp_{4}(C_{2}\varepsilon^{-1}) that

S_{1}(Q,t)\ll\exp_{4}(C_{3}\varepsilon^{-1})(QT)^{1+\varepsilon}.

Plugging this into S(Q,s) gives the desired result.

### 5.3. Rational primes splitting in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q})

In this section, we prove [Corollary 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary3 "Corollary 3 (Rational primes splitting in ℚ(√{-𝑞})). ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality"). Let q denote a prime \equiv 3\pmod{4}.

By a well known property of quadratic fields [[9](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#bib.bib9), Proposition 8.5], a rational prime p splits in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q}) if and only if

\left(-q\over p\right)=1,

so it follows from the reciprocity law that the number of such p\leq X is

P_{q}(X)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2<p\leq X\\
p\neq q\end{subarray}}\left[1+\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\right].

Now, let m_{Q} be the number of q\sim Q such that |P_{q}(X)-\frac{1}{2}\pi(X)|>\delta\pi(X). Then there is

m_{Q}\delta^{2}\pi(X)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{q\sim Q}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2<p\leq X\\
p\neq q\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\right|^{2},

so it follows from [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1. ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality") that

m_{Q}\ll\delta^{-2}\exp_{4}\left\{C\log(XQ)\over\log_{4}(XQ)\right\}\left(1+\frac{Q}{X}\right).

Finally, we set \log X\geq 3C\log Q/\log_{4}Q so that \log X\geq 2C\log(XQ)/\log_{4}(XQ), completing the proof of [Corollary 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09637v1#Thmcorollary3 "Corollary 3 (Rational primes splitting in ℚ(√{-𝑞})). ‣ 1.2. Statement of results ‣ 1. Introduction ‣ Explicit quadratic large sieve inequality").
