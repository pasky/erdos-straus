# Hostile review of Z53_NONCRTLOGIC.md

**Verdict:** The finite-tree argument in Theorem A and the number-field argument in Theorem T are sound with their full hypotheses. The memo is not ready to promote into FRONTIER: its claimed exact escape criterion is false, Proposition S omits essential hypotheses, and the Gaussian toy is not an admissible instance of the global trap-point theorem as advertised. ES remains open. No repository files were changed.

## Findings

1. **MAJOR — §3.1 Corollary T3, last sentence; §§0, 7–8: the “escape iff non-formal QNR” equivalence is false.** Non-formality is not equivalent to producing a non-residue, or even an integer containing a non-residue prime factor. For example, the integer oracle `O(p)=2^floor(sqrt(log p))` is unbounded but grows more slowly than every positive power of q on every polynomial family `p=P(q)`. No finite polynomial menu can represent it on an infinite unbounded input set. Nevertheless its only prime factor is 2, a residue at every prime under consideration (`p≡1 mod 24`). Conversely, merely producing a non-residue does not supply an ES certificate. **Fix:** distinguish escape from this formal model from successful witness production. State only that finite formal trees are obstructed, that QNR-valued outputs obstruct trap-point polynomial semantics subject to nonvacuity, and that successful witnesses must contain a QNR prime. Delete the biconditionals throughout, including the proposed FRONTIER text.

2. **MAJOR — §3.1 Corollary T3: the unconditional non-semantics conclusion lacks prime nonvacuity.** The pointwise statement “if P(q) is prime in the refined class, O(P(q))≠G(q)” follows unconditionally. It does not prove that no eventual prime-domain semantics exists: the refined nonlinear progression might contain only finitely many prime values. Infinitude is already unknown for `P=X²+1`. An arbitrary oracle specified only on primes can be extended constantly on composite inputs, so the original all-integer version of F9 does not repair this inference either. **Fix:** label the no-semantics consequence conditional on H for the refined family, or another explicit prime-infinitude hypothesis. Keep the pointwise incompatibility unconditional. Apply the same qualification to the taxonomy's “escape every trap point; PROVED” entry.

3. **MAJOR — §3.1 Corollary T3, converse; §3 Reading and summary formulations: QR values are confused with QR prime factors.** Lemma CT requires a non-residue *prime factor*; Lemma T4 gives the symbol of the entire polynomial value. A square can contain non-residue prime factors. Concrete example within T4's finite local conditions: `P=X²+1`, `q=24`, `p=577`, `h=X+1`. The relevant rational prime for β and `h^∨(β)=1+i` is just 2, and `8|q`; nevertheless `h(q)=25` contains the non-residue prime 5. Thus T4 alone does not prove the claimed statement about polynomial output denominators. Also, “every polynomial value is a residue” needs the order of quantifiers: **for each fixed finite family**, refine the congruence class depending on that family, and exclude values divisible by p. It cannot mean every polynomial simultaneously at one actual prime. **Fix:** obtain the denominator obstruction by appending FACTOR and imposing Theorem T's enlarged admissibility conditions; use H when asserting infinitely many failures. Rewrite the abbreviated transparency claims with these quantifiers.

4. **MAJOR — §6 Proposition S, definition and application of Theorem A: `C_P=1` is missing.** The stated r-generic condition controls only primes ≤r. Universal nondegeneracy does not imply `C_P=1`. For example, with r=5 one may choose the 7-adic coordinate in `2+343 Z_7`, giving `v_7(24q*_7+1)=2`, while satisfying the stated small-prime conditions and avoiding all polynomial roots. Then `C_P` has a factor 49; primality of `P(q)/C_P` does not make `P(q)` prime. Theorem A cannot be applied. The character assertion on line 311 is also false under this definition: for `h=2X+3`, `H_h=70`, the r=5 valuation is odd, but at every prime `p≡73 mod 840`, both 5 and 7 are non-residues and `h((p−1)/24)` is a residue. **Fix:** require P(q*) to be a unit everywhere; for the advertised one-bad-prime character rule require square units at every prime other than r. For normalized formal primes include the factor `(C_h/p)` explicitly.

5. **MAJOR — §6 final bullet and §7 “What survives”: Proposition S does not reduce all QNR oracles, especially the least primitive root, to the r-generic line.** Fixing `n_p=r` is determined by a finite prefix of quadratic symbols. Fixing the least primitive root is not: primitivity also requires power-residue/order information. For instance modulo 97, 5 and 28 are both non-residues but have orders 96 and 32. An entire unbounded quadratic-character sequence can determine p itself, but that observation gives no finite-prefix or r-generic reduction. **Fix:** restrict Proposition S's consequence to the least non-residue and separately justified prescribed-symbol searches. Remove the asserted reduction of `g_p` and arbitrary QNR-valued oracles.

6. **MAJOR — §4 F2; `z53_noncrtlogic_gausstoy.py` docstring: the advertised admissibility constants cannot occur at a global Gaussian trap point.** The test uses `C_X=120` and `C_{X²+4}=4`. At 13, the Gaussian trap condition requires both `1−5q` and `1+5q` to be nonzero squares. Direct enumeration gives precisely `q≡0,3,10 mod 13`. The first forces `13|C_X`; the latter two force `13|C_{X²+4}`. Therefore no global trap point has the constants used by this toy. **Fix:** describe it as a finite-local QR-transparency/window test, whose failure conclusion is still valid from the local conditions at 2,3,5, rather than an end-to-end admissible instance of Theorem T. Alternatively construct an actual global trap point and recompute its constants and admissible sample. The reported 322 failures themselves reproduce exactly.

7. **MAJOR — §4 “CERTIFIED computations,” especially F1; `z53_noncrtlogic_trapcheck.py:82`: primality is only probable.** `gmpy2.is_prime` explicitly promises “probably prime,” not a primality certificate. Several tested families exceed 64 bits. The first accepted quartic value is `58260289478187906869760001` (86 bits), for which `gmpy2.is_probab_prime` returns 1, not the proven-prime status 2. Jacobi computations and reproducibility do not certify that these are prime inputs to T4. **Fix:** retain primality certificates/use a proven-prime engine for the large values, or label the experiment as exact symbol computations on probable-prime inputs. This is a certification gap, not evidence that an accepted number is composite.

8. **MAJOR — §6 EVIDENCE 1, interpretation; §§0, 7–8: “only parity,” “nothing about the class,” and “the tied-modulus screen fails” overstate the experiment.** The pooled conditional frequencies reproduce, but they compare different populations of `(p,ℓ)` and do not establish causal mediation, independence, or an impossibility theorem. Conditioning on “has a QNR prime factor” does not condition away parity: the QR-root and QNR-root populations still have different parity. The mean root distributions and factorization structures also differ. **Fix:** state the measured aggregate association only. Keep the proposed explanation explicitly heuristic; do not promote it to an exact characterization of what seeding can buy.

9. **MAJOR — §5 “Answer to S3,” lines 298–300: CF norms are not QNR-blind at every prime.** The quoted JOINT_BUDGET Proposition 4.2 explicitly assumes an odd window; the memo silently includes the prime factor 2. At p=13 the first norm of √13 is 4 (`3²−13=−4`), and `(2/13)=−1`. Indeed `4/13=1/4+1/26+1/52` uses that norm as its p-free denominator. **Fix:** restrict the claim to p≡1 mod 8 (in particular the campaign's p≡1 mod 24), where 2 is also a residue, or explicitly exclude the 2-part. The odd-prime-factor reciprocity argument is correct in the stated p≡1 mod 4 setting.

10. **MINOR — §6 Proposition S, “only if ... its formal output is SUCCESS”; §2 Remarks.** With arbitrary oracle branches there is no single formal output, and unreachable branches may output FAIL even if every actual run succeeds. Correctness constrains actual oracle-consistent runs, not all formal leaves. An extended program can also have an infinite formal branch despite finitely many oracle calls. **Fix:** retain the defined/finite-tree condition, quantify over realized branches on admissible inputs, and state the H assumption when inferring failures infinitely often. Say that *all computation*, not merely the oracle-call count, must terminate on every formal branch.

11. **MINOR — §2 Theorem A(e), trap alternative; §3 trap definition.** A(e)'s trap alternative does not explicitly require universal nondegeneracy, although appending denominator factorizations uses it. Being a trap point alone is insufficient: q*=0 is a trap point and is degenerate for X. Also, the definition's “every admissible p is 1 mod 8 and mod 3” requires actually adding 2,3 and sufficient precision to Λ; these congruences do not follow from an arbitrary initial admissibility set. **Fix:** import Theorem T's universal-nondegeneracy hypothesis explicitly and attach the congruence claim to the refined admissibility data used in its proof.

12. **MINOR — §2 Corollary A2: supply domain and reduction qualifications.** A divisor-valued oracle is not itself finite-valued; its bounded *index in the formal divisor list* is. The reduction is valid on the admissible set after factoring F and enlarging the tree data. `DIVISORS` lists positive divisors, so signed values need a sign bit. Multiplicative orders require `m≥1` and `gcd(a,m)=1`, or a stated finite-valued exceptional convention. **Fix:** state these conditions and describe a bounded-index/default-FAIL reduction, rather than identifying the unbounded integer output literally with a finite-information oracle. The φ(m) formula is correct after thresholds make the constant prime factors and formal prime factors pairwise coprime.

13. **MINOR — §5 taxonomy, last row: the “Siegel growth” justification is wrong.** Siegel bounds the imaginary class number, or the real class-number–regulator product; it does not prove that real quadratic class numbers individually tend to infinity. Discrete logarithms can also be constant (for example, log of the base itself). A regulator is real, not an integer, unless an encoding/floor is specified. **Fix:** remove this blanket rationale and define each unresolved oracle precisely. Retaining “open” does not validate the stated growth claim.

14. **MINOR — §6 EVIDENCE 2; `z53_noncrtlogic_qnrseed.py:127–138`: probabilities and averages are mislabeled.** The engine discards every R(p)=0 prime before computing these statistics. Thus 0.9597 and 0.9776 are conditional on R(p)>0, not probabilities over all primes. The unconditional values are `9327/9728=0.958779` and `32956/33714=0.977517`. The s_p use rate additionally conditions on s_p existing: there are 665 and 499 “none” cases. The quoted 0.9452 applies only to the upper interval and uses denominator 33212, not 33714. “Share of solutions” is actually an unweighted mean of per-prime shares. The 0.2162 figure for ℓ=11 excludes `ℓ=n_p`; combining both non-residue categories gives 0.228651. Finally, “larger still when ℓ=n_p” is not true for every recorded ℓ: at ℓ=37 in the upper interval, 0.0490<0.0513; at ℓ=47 the sole LEAST observation is zero. **Fix:** label conditioning, weighting, categories, interval, and small sample sizes explicitly.

15. **MINOR — `z53_noncrtlogic_qnrseed.py:97–107`: even CF norms are not fully factored.** Trial division starts at 3 without stripping powers of 2, so an even residual cofactor can hide a qualifying odd prime. At p=2377 the norm 130 occurs and the code misses its factor 13 after removing 5. The minimum seed happens to remain 5. I independently checked all 9728 primes in `[300,10⁶)` and found missing norm factors but no changed minimum seed; this does not justify the defective factorization generally. The step cap also returns a partial answer without marking truncation. **Fix:** strip the 2-part first (or factor Q exactly), and fail explicitly on an unfinished period; rerun the seed statistics.

16. **MINOR — several numerical/sample descriptions disagree with the stored output.** In §4 F1 the unpredicted-symbol share is 0.511 for X²+1, not within the asserted 0.48–0.50 range. In F2 the controls are the first 5000 accepted inputs with **t prime**, not the first 5000 prime values in the whole `q=120t` class. In §6 EVIDENCE 1 the last deep-sample prime is 60,428,281, exceeding the stated bound `6.0·10⁷`. In EVIDENCE 3 “least residue prime” actually means **least residue prime greater than 3**; at these primes the literal least residue prime is always 2. **Fix:** use the exact sample definitions and corrected range/bound in the prose.

17. **MINOR — §2 Corollary A3, parenthetical advice-complexity claim.** Failure infinitely often for every fixed B gives an unbounded worst-case advice requirement, not a pointwise `ω(1)` lower bound as p→∞; easy primes can continue to require no advice. **Fix:** define a fixed decoding procedure and its worst-case complexity up to X, or say simply “no fixed constant advice bound suffices.” The conditional `O(log log p)` upper bound from a successful-window index is otherwise justified, with a ceiling on the bit count.

18. **MINOR — §3.1 Corollary T1 / §5 full one-radicand data claim: specify encoding and supply the general-k extension.** The cited Lemma 1.4 proves the detailed cycle/unit statements for k=1. The displayed Richaud–Degert identity correctly gives the periodic CF of √(kp), but by itself does not establish all the asserted `(1+√(kp))/2` cycle data and the fundamental unit in the intended maximal order for arbitrary nonsquarefree k. Also, ε itself is not an integer polynomial register: its coordinate pair is. **Fix:** specify coordinate encoding and the order/field, and add the short auxiliary derivation for general k, including the half-integral cycle and fundamental-unit choice. Keep the finite-formal-tree restriction next to “arbitrary program-computable functions”; it is not a statement about unrestricted algorithms.

19. **MINOR — §5 Corollary T4, line 289: the coordinate swap is only valid for k=1.** On `p=kq²+1`, the representation is `(1,q)`; `(q,1)` instead represents `q²+k`. **Fix:** attach “or (q,1)” solely to k=1. The uniqueness assertion itself is correct: for k>1, the two representations give `p | x₁y₂±x₂y₁`, while both expressions have absolute value at most `p/√k<p`; the plus case is impossible and the minus case forces equality. This also supplies a proof instead of the citation from memory.

20. **NIT — domain/references/reproduction wording.** T1's residue-symbol formula needs p odd; its current hypotheses also allow `P=X+1,q=1,p=2`. T2 cites a theorem number explicitly from memory; replace it with a checked reference or just the named Hilbert product formula. The RD display should mark its repeating block (for r=1 the minimal period is one). The trapcheck docstring describes the shifted control as `Mt+1`, whereas the code uses `Mt+24`. The §6 forestroot command omits the required output-path argument, although §9 includes it. **Fix:** synchronize these details and replace the pending §10 review status before calling the memo final.

## Claims verified as correct

- **Theorem A(a)–(d), and the core of (e):** induction along the actual branch works; finite unions/maxima handle all thresholds, precisions, factorization lists and order comparisons. Universal nondegeneracy makes the union H-family admissible. No Frobenius equidistribution or Schinzel–Chebotarev hypothesis is needed. This genuinely closes the fixed-finite-information E3 issue for the stated witness-procedure/finite-tree model, not for E1/E4.
- **A2's substantive reduction:** formal divisor lists have bounded length at each node. The order/index examples work with the domain conventions above. In particular, `φ(K∏r_h^e)=φ(K)∏r_h^(e−1)(r_h−1)` is legitimate beyond the distinctness/coprimality thresholds, and `r_h−1` is polynomial. The displayed six-value `ord_p(2)` menu on the prime-q line is consistent with `(2/p)=1` and exclusion of orders dividing 12.
- **Lemmas T1–T4:** the reversed polynomial is monic integral and irreducible; the norm identity and the β exponent/sign in T1 are right. A principal ideal of norm p supplies the required degree-one prime directly: there is no missing `p∤[O_K:Z[β]]` assumption. The odd-place Hilbert-symbol formula and global product formula give exactly T2. Negative real β imply positive π_q at real places. T4 is correct with its explicit local congruences and nonvanishing assumptions.
- **Lemma T5:** the ramified 2-adic argument is valid. For every finite extension of Q₂, `Y²+Y−2z` has a simple root modulo the maximal ideal at Y=0, proving `1+8O_v` consists of squares. The good-prime factorization excludes the index correctly; the finite-field norm square criterion, the conservative Weil lower bound, and the countable-root/degree diagonal construction all work.
- **Theorem T itself:** with universally nondegenerate trap points, denominator factorization, constant-prime factors, enlarged precision and thresholds are handled correctly. Theorem M(b) really does apply to nonlinear P; its proof uses no linearity. The resulting primes are 1 mod 24 after the stated enlargement.
- **Corollary T2:** the cyclotomic/binomial construction for finitely many rational powers and the leading-degree/leading-coefficient necessary condition are correct. The irrational-exponent, logarithm, and simultaneous √p/√(2p) growth obstructions follow. **T1 at k=1**, the RD periodic identity, and the Gaussian trap conditions at 2,3,5 check out. **Cornacchia uniqueness** checks out as above.
- **Forest identity in §6:** for a unit prime root ℓ, the stated divisor criterion for a bichromatic larger edge is correct, including the strict inequality. The sign identity and “QNR root implies p+ℓ has a QNR prime factor” are correct. Proposition S's initial finite congruence criterion for `n_p=r` is correct. Its quotation of (J)70/STERILE_LOWER is accurate, including `2^21.4375≈2,840,079.73`, the small-r coverage and the unresolved r=17 case.
