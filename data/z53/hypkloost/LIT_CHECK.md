# Literature verification: determinant bounds, Kloosterman fractions, and unit fractions

Primary-source checks were made by fetching the cited PDFs (Heath-Brown arXiv:math/0405392; Pila, Astérisque 228 PDF; DFI local PDF; Bettin–Chandee arXiv:1502.00769; Elsholtz–Tao arXiv:1107.1010; Browning–Elsholtz publisher-author PDF; Elsholtz–Planitzer arXiv:1805.02945). OCR/transcription caveats are noted.

## 1. Heath-Brown, “The density of rational points on curves and surfaces”

**Source correction.** The API record for `math/0405392` verifies title (API has “in curves and surfaces”), journal *Ann. of Math.* (2) 155 (2002), no. 2, 553–598, authors D. R. Heath-Brown and J.-L. Colliot-Thélène (appendix). The affine box/determinant theorems below are in this paper. It is not appropriate to describe the relevant affine result as requiring absolute irreducibility: the paper says irreducible over Q, and explicitly explains its convention.

Let `V=∏ B_i`, and let `T=max ∏ B_i^{f_i}` over monomials with nonzero coefficient in the form `F`. Theorem 14 (general auxiliary-hypersurface theorem) states: “Let ε > 0, and suppose that B1, . . . , Bn ≥ 1 and a form F, irreducible over Q, are given. Then there exists D depending only on n, d and ε, and an integer k satisfying
`k ≪_ε (V^d/T)^{1/d^((n−1)/(n−2))} V^ε (log ||F||)^(2n−3)`,
with the following properties. For each j ≤ k there is an integral form Fj(x), in n variables, having degree at most D, such that (1) F(x) ∤ Fj(x); (2) for every point x counted by N(B) there is an integer j ≤ k such that Fj(x)=0.” (The exponent formatting is reconstructed from the PDF’s typeset fraction; see the direct specialization below.)

For an affine surface `F(x1,x2,x3)=0`, homogenize to four variables and take `B4=1`. The paper explicitly specializes this in its proof of Theorem 13: `B1=B2=B3=B, B4=1`, `V=B^3,T=B^d`, so the count of auxiliary curves is `O_ε(B^(2/√d+ε))`, degrees `O_ε(1)`. Thus the unequal-side surface formulation has count `≪_ε (V^d/T)^(1/d^(3/2)) V^ε (log||F||)^5` auxiliary hypersurfaces in the homogenized setting; each is proper modulo F and has degree bounded in terms of `d,ε`. Intersecting with F gives curves (or lower-dimensional components). The coefficient/height dependence is explicit: `(log ||F||)^5` (and constants may depend on ε); the degree cap depends only on n,d,ε. Do not silently drop this height factor in the general surface theorem.

**Plane curve theorem (Theorem 3; not theorem 4/5).** Verbatim: “Let n=3 and ε>0. If F is irreducible over Q, then `N(F;B1,B2,B3) ≪_ε T^(−2/d) V^(−1/d+ε)`.” (The extracted typesetting shows the product `T^{-2/d} V^{1/d+ε}`; mathematically and from the stated equal-box specialization it is `T^{-2/d} V^{1/d+ε}`.) For equal sides this gives (1.9) `N(F;B) ≪_ε B^(2/d+ε)`. For nonsingular F, (1.10) is `N(F;B1,B2,B3) ≪_ε V^(2/(3d)+ε)`. It is uniform in F: the paper says these estimates are “completely independent of F”; Theorem 4, using coprime coefficients, absorbs coefficient height. **Transcription caution:** the extracted equation (1.8) is garbled in the first pass; check the printed PDF formula before quoting the unequal-side exponent. The intended expression from the typeset equation is `T^(−2/d) V^(1/d+ε)`.

**Counting points on the resulting curves.** Theorem 5 says: “Let C be an irreducible curve in P3, of degree d, not necessarily defined over the rationals. Then C has `O_ε(B^(2/d+ε))` points `x ∈ Z^4` in the cube `max |xi| ≤ B`.” Here the implied constant is independent of the curve; degree and ε govern the bound. This is the bound used for the curves on the surface. Its statement is equal-cube/projective, not an unequal-side theorem.

## 2. Bombieri–Pila / Pila / Walsh

**Pila primary source:** J. Pila, “Density of Integral and Rational Points on Varieties,” *Astérisque* 228 (1995), 183–187; fetched Numdam PDF. Theorem A (quoted, OCR-normalized): “Let V be an irreducible affine variety, of dimension n and degree d, embedded in N dimensional affine space. Let H>1. There is a constant c(n,d,N) such that the number of integral points of V of height <H is bounded by
`c(n,d,N) H^(n−1+1/d) exp(c2 √(d log H log log H))`.” The scanned PDF’s exponential constant is typographically/OCR ambiguous (`c2`); the exponent is the familiar `O(√(d log H loglog H))`. It explicitly says `c(n,d,N)` is effectively computable and uniform over varieties of these dimension/degree/ambient parameters. For a curve (`n=1`) this is `H^(1/d) exp(O_d(√(log H loglog H))`, and consequently `≪_{d,N,ε} H^(1/d+ε)`; this last form follows by absorbing the subpower factor, with constant depending on ε. The theorem is affine integral points, any ambient dimension.

Bombieri–Pila, “The Number of Integral Points on Arcs and Ovals,” *Duke Math. J.* 60 (1989), 1–25, is the plane-curve predecessor (Theorem 5 as cited by Pila). I did not fetch/verify that primary PDF in this pass, so I do not quote its exact theorem wording. Pila’s 1995 theorem does verify the requested `N^(1/d+ε)` consequence uniformly in coefficients, for an irreducible curve in R^n, with constants depending only on degree, ambient dimension, and ε. Caveat: `R^n` here means an algebraic curve defined over R, and integral points in affine coordinates.

Walsh, “Bounded rational points on curves,” was not primary-source verified in this pass. I therefore make no claim about an eps-removal theorem or exact theorem number. The requested `N^(1/d+ε)` already follows from Pila A; do not present that consequence as a Walsh result.

## 3. Kloosterman fractions

**Duke–Friedlander–Iwaniec (DFI),** “Bilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions,” *Invent. Math.* 128 (1997), 23–43; checked against the local PDF. For their bilinear form `B(M,N)` (the sum over the prescribed m,n ranges, `(m,n)=1`, with coefficients and exponential `e(a \bar m/n)`), Theorem 1 states (CORRECTED after cross-model review, which inspected the rendered printed p. 24 of the local PDF):
`B(M,N) ≪ ||α||₂ ||β||₂ [ (M+N)^(1/2) + {1+a/(MN)}^(1/2) min(M,N) ] (MN)^ε`,
with implied constant depending only on ε. **This is a SUM of two terms.** The earlier transcription in this file (a product with min(M,N)^(1/2)) was wrong. The paper itself remarks that Theorem 1 is non-trivial except when one range is much larger than the other or when they are nearly equal.

Theorem 2 states exactly (1.5):
`B(M,N) ≪ ||α||₂ ||β||₂ (a+MN)^(3/8) (M+N)^(11/48+ε)`,
“the implied constant depending only on ε.” Thus the dependence is `(a+MN)^(3/8)`, not `(a+MN)^(? )` or merely `(1+a/MN)^(1/2)`. Theorem 1 is (1.4), useful in a different range. **Primary-source caveat:** the extracted notation does not preserve the exact definitions of the ranges M,N; consult the paper’s introduction for those interval/sequence conventions before applying it to a specific sum.

**Bettin–Chandee,** “Trilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions,” arXiv:1502.00769; published *Adv. Math.* 328 (2018), 123 intuitively? The exact volume/pages were not independently checked here (arXiv PDF is primary for theorem). Theorem 1: for `ϑ ≠ 0`, with `A=[A/2,A]`, arbitrary coefficients `ν_a` supported on A, and the coprimality condition `(m,n)=1`,
`B(M,N,A) ≪ ||α||₂ ||β||₂ ||ν||₂ (1+|ϑ|A/(MN))^(1/2) [ (AMN)^(7/20+ε)(M+N)^(1/4) + (AMN)^(3/8+ε)(AN+AM)^(1/8) ]`. (CORRECTED after cross-model review: the second term is (AMN)^(3/8+ε)(AN+AM)^(1/8), not (AMN)^(1/4+ε)(AN+AM)^(1/8).)
This transcription is from the fetched PDF’s Theorem 1 (equation (1.2)); implied constant is absolute apart from ε (the paper states the bound with `≪`, and ε-losses). This is trilinear, includes the additional averaging over a, and is not a direct replacement for DFI’s bilinear theorem. In the important regime `|ϑ|A≪MN`, `M≈N`, the authors state a saving over the trivial bound of roughly `min(A^(3/20)N^(1/20),N^(1/8))`.

## 4. Erdős–Straus solution counts and later literature

**Browning–Elsholtz (2011),** “The number of representations of rationals as a sum of unit fractions,” *Illinois J. Math.* 55 (2011), 685–696 (primary author-hosted revised PDF fetched). Define ordered-count `f3(m,n)` for `m/n=1/a1+1/a2+1/a3`, `a1≤a2≤a3`. Their Theorem 2: “For any ε>0 we have `f3(m,n) ≪_ε (n/m)^(2/3) n^ε`.” In particular `f3(m,n)≪_ε n^(2/3+ε)`, uniform in m,n. Their proof (Section 4) is based on divisibility/reparameterization, dyadic decomposition, divisor estimates, and induction for higher k; it is **not** the Heath-Brown determinant method. The equation after clearing denominators is `mxyz=n(xy+xz+yz)`. Elsholtz–Tao later describe this as the prior general bound.

**Elsholtz–Tao,** arXiv:1107.1010, “Counting the number of solutions to the Erdős–Straus equation,” *J. Number Theory* 133 (2013),  counting article; fetched arXiv/local PDF. Proposition 1.7 (Upper bounds), verbatim statement: “For any `n∈N`, one has `f_I(n) ≪ n^(3/5+O(1/log log n))` and `f_II(n) ≪ n^(2/5+O(1/log log n))`. In particular … for any prime p one has `f(p) ≪ p^(3/5+O(1/log log p))`.” The count is for their Type I/II classification; the paper’s proof uses multiple parameterizations and the divisor bound (Appendix A.6), not a determinant-method argument. Exact `O(1/loglog n)` is in the exponent, with absolute implied constants as written (not a uniform `n^ε` statement). Their Type II parametrization includes `4acde=n+4a²d+e` (their variable naming), and the source discusses the corresponding divisibility constraints.

**Later improvement, Elsholtz–Planitzer,** “The number of solutions of the Erdős–Straus Equation and sums of k unit fractions,” arXiv:1805.02945 (2018), published in *Proc. Roy. Soc. Edinburgh A* (publication details were not verified from journal primary source). Theorem 1 gives, for any `m,n∈N`, `ε>0`, at most `O_ε(n^ε (n^3/m^2)^(1/5))` positive-integer solutions to `m/n=1/a1+1/a2+1/a3`; for m=4, this gives `O_ε(n^(3/5+ε))` for every denominator n. This improves the general Browning–Elsholtz exponent and extends the prime-denominator Elsholtz–Tao estimate to all n. Their proof explicitly extends the Browning–Elsholtz lifting/parameter method, not determinant method.

**Search for more recent pointwise bounds / determinant-method attacks.** Search results (2011–2026) located the above substantial general 3/5+ε result (arXiv:1805.02945), but no primary-source paper proving a further pointwise improvement for `f(n), f_I(n), f_II(n)` or a sub-`n^(1/3)` bound for the Type II `c=1` subfamily. I also did not find a determinant-method paper specifically treating equations of the forms `4abc−a−b=n` or `(4ac−1)(4bc−1)=4cn+1`. This is a search conclusion, **not proof that no such result exists**. The known source material does not establish a bound below `n^(1/3)` for that c=1 subfamily. Treat the status as unverified/open pending a targeted MathSciNet/zbMATH and primary-literature search. Relevant newer broader results should not be misrepresented as pointwise improvements of the restricted Type II family.

* The Heath–Brown source metadata verifies `math/0405392`; the question’s “probably” is correct. Its theorem labels: plane curve Theorem 3, uniform coefficient-height lemma Theorem 4, projective space curve Theorem 5, auxiliary forms Theorem 14.
* PDF text extraction corrupted several fractional superscripts. The Heath–Brown unequal-box formula and the Bettin–Chandee formula above have been normalized from the displayed PDF; if a publication-ready verbatim quotation is needed, visually inspect the original equations.
* Pila’s 1995 scanned PDF was fetched and its theorem A confirmed; the OCR obscures the constant within the exponential, so retain `exp(O_{d,N}(√(log H loglog H)))` rather than asserting a particular numerical constant.
