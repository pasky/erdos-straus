### Pomerance--Weingartner audit

- **Verbatim quote:** PW Theorem 1.3 says, “There is an absolute positive constant C” (`sources/pw.txt:69-72`); the paper nowhere calls this C, the implied constants, or the asymptotic threshold effectively computable (`rg` hits for “effective” occur only in unrelated numerical/maximal-order discussion).
- **Verbatim quote:** PW defines the exact class count f(p) and says that Vaughan supplies at least f(p) good residue classes (`sources/pw.txt:454-475`).  **Assessment:** this is an exact finite class construction, but the inaccessible Vaughan paper prevents a direct audit of that cited step.
- **Assessment:** the upper half of PW Lemma 4.1 uses partial summation (`:481-487`), the elementary divisor inequality for τ3 (`:496-512`), Brun--Titchmarsh (`:515-523`), and an elementary divisor sum.  These inputs are effective, although PW leaves C2 implicit.
- **Verbatim quote:** the lower half truncates to d,t at most x^(1/6) and Ω(dt) at most 3 log log x (`:544-598`), then says, “We now use the Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem noting that the number of triples m,d,t with given product q is at most (log x)^O(1)” (`:600-616`).
- **Assessment:** this ordinary Bombieri--Vinogradov invocation is Siegel-sensitive as printed.  The polylogarithmic multiplicity is harmless after an arbitrary-log-saving excluded-conductor theorem, but PW neither selects nor deletes an exceptional conductor.  Thus the lower constant C1 and PW's resulting C are not proved effective by the printed argument.
- **Verbatim quote:** after class supply, PW says “We now employ the large sieve” (`:625-644`) and derives the avoider bound 4N/S; it then uses an elementary Rankin truncation with v=1/log X and Euler products (`:650-762`).
- **Assessment:** no further prime-distribution input occurs on the N-side.  The Section 4 analytic ledger is therefore: effective Brun--Titchmarsh upper supply; potentially ineffective ordinary-BV lower supply; effective combinatorial larger sieve and Rankin/product algebra.
- **Assessment:** Lemmas 51.4--51.5 repair the m=4 supply by deleting multiples of the one conductor while retaining class mass.  This is a campaign-derived repair, not a PW assertion.  It does not by itself establish PW-uniform effectivity for every varying m, because an exceptional conductor may divide m and then every modulus mdt is deleted.

## T1(b): effective Bombieri--Vinogradov

### Exact citation that closes the caveat

- **Verbatim reference:** H. W. Lenstra Jr. and C. Pomerance, “Primality testing with Gaussian periods,” *Journal of the European Mathematical Society* 21 (2019), no. 4, 1229--1269, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/861, Lemma 11.2, pp. 1258--1260.  The published paper is archived as `sources/lenstra-pomerance-jems-2019.pdf`.
- **Verbatim theorem content:** Lemma 11.2 is headed “effective Bombieri--Vinogradov inequality.”  It gives absolute effectively computable c6,c7 and, for every x at least 3, an integer s(x) between `(log x)^(1/2)` and `exp((log x)^(1/2))` such that for every `Q` in `[x^(1/3) log x,x^(1/2)]`,
  the sum over `q<=Q, s(x) not dividing q` of `max_{2<=y<=x} max_(a,q)=1 |psi(y;q,a)-y/phi(q)|` is at most `c6 sqrt(x) Q log^5 x + c6 x exp(-c7 sqrt(log x))`.
- **Verbatim proof content:** when the Page character exists, s(x) is its primitive real modulus; when it does not, the proof chooses a dummy s(x).  The proof works uniformly in y before induced characters are summed and cites Davenport, *Multiplicative Number Theory*, 2nd ed., Springer, 1980, Chapter 28.
- **Assessment (derivation of 51.20):** choose `B >= A+6`.  For `Q >= x^(1/3) log x`, the first term is `O(x/log^A x)` and the exponential term is smaller effectively.  For smaller Q, enlarge the nonnegative sum to `Q0=x^(1/3) log x`, whose first term is `x^(5/6)log^6 x`, again smaller than `x/log^A x` effectively.  Restricting from `q<=Q` to `(Q1,Q2]` only decreases the sum.
- **Assessment (quantifier gap):** Lemma 51.4 is a corollary rather than a verbatim theorem: LP writes a concrete two-term error and always names s(x), whereas 51.4 repackages arbitrary log saving and says “no exceptional conductor” when the proof's s(x) is only a dummy.  There is no substantive max-residue, all-modulus, range, or effectivity gap.
- **Assessment (dyadic form):** apply LP at the upper endpoint 2x.  Its `max_{2<=y<=2x}` keeps the same s(2x) at both endpoints, so effective partial summation gives the dyadic π-form without the two-conductor problem flagged by review 51.
