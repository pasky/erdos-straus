# Local-global failures, thin groups, and reciprocity obstructions

## §0 Summary
- **Genuinely new since 2026-09-28:** Shkredov v2 and Zhang v2 state the full all-denominator Zaremba conclusion (large absolute digit bound), rather than only prime-denominator results; this sharpens the 2026 analogue entry in `BRAINSTORM/z31_zaremba_thin_orbits.md`, but not the ES transfer verdict.
- **Transferable structural features:** expansion proves pointwise Zaremba only with a power-size, dimension-tunable menu; Chen's Markoff result combines a giant orbit with a divisibility lower bound, not an existence count; thin-group reciprocity can exclude structured families but does not create ES certificates.
- **Campaign verdict changes:** none. ES remains open; no verified every-prime representation theorem with polylogarithmic or O(1) expected count found.
## §1 Verified entries

### 1. Thin Apollonian local-global failure / reciprocity
**Citation:** J. Haag, A. Kertzer, J. Rickards, K. E. Stange, “The local-global conjecture for Apollonian circle packings is false,” *Annals of Mathematics* 200 (2024), arXiv:2307.02749v3, PDF reopened and read 2026-10-30. **Statement:** The abstract states that the local-global conjecture is false for many packings: quadratic and quartic families are missed as a thin-group effect from quadratic/quartic reciprocity. Theorem 1.3: infinitely many primitive packings have Ω(√N) missing curvatures up to N. Theorem 2.4 tabulates missed families by packing type: e.g. type (6,1,−1) misses n², 2n², 3n², 6n²; type (6,1,1,−1) misses n⁴, 4n⁴, 9n⁴, 36n⁴. Definition 2.2 requires infinitely many family members admissible mod 24. Mechanism: tangent-circle curvatures are values of translated binary quadratic forms; modulo curvature n, the form degenerates to Ax², so (n/c) is constant over invertible tangent curvatures. Quadratic reciprocity propagates χ₂ between tangent circles and across the packing. For quartic obstruction the authors use an associated fractional ideal in ℤ[i] and quartic residue symbols; constancy propagates by quartic reciprocity. Theorem 1.2 (Bourgain–Kontorovich) gives only O(N^(1−η)) missing curvatures; Conjecture 1.5 that the residual sporadic set is finite remains open. **Status:** Published; local-global conjecture not proved in full. **Campaign relation:** `BRAINSTORM/apollonian_reciprocity.md`, `BRAINSTORM/thin_group_orbits.md` (prior mechanism and ES comparison; no changed verdict).

### 2. Reciprocity obstructions in semigroup orbits (including continued fractions)
**Citation:** J. Rickards, K. E. Stange, “Reciprocity obstructions in semigroup orbits in SL(2,Z),” arXiv:2401.01860v3 (2025-01-23), *Duke Mathematical Journal* 174(15) (2025), DOI:10.1215/00127094-2025-0017; PDF reopened and read 2026-10-30. **Statement:** Theorem 2.5: for nonnegative coprime (x,y), if (i) y is odd and (x/y)=−1, or (ii) (x,y)≡(1,0) mod 4 and (x/y)=−1, or (iii) (x,y)≡(3,0) mod 4 and (x/y)=−(−1/y), then neither numerator nor denominator in the Ψ-orbit of (x,y) is a square. Proposition 2.2 characterizes Ψ by matrices in Γ₁(4)≥0 with (a/b)=1; quadratic reciprocity proves preservation under the Möbius actions. Theorem 2.6 classifies congruence/reciprocity obstructions; Theorem 2.8 gives the exact missing numerator set for Ψ(2/3): squares plus {3,6,7,10,12,15,18,19,27,31,34,55,63,99,115}. Theorem 2.10 gives finitely generated examples with dimension δ arbitrarily close to 1. **Status:** Published. This is an exclusion result for specified orbits, not a full Zaremba result. **Campaign relation:** `BRAINSTORM/apollonian_reciprocity.md`, `BRAINSTORM/z31_zaremba_thin_orbits.md`, `BRAINSTORM/z36_reciprocity_obstruction_home.md` (already mapped; no new ES invariant).

### 3. Zaremba: almost-all theorem and 2026 pointwise claims
**Citation:** J. Bourgain, A. Kontorovich, “On Zaremba’s conjecture,” *Annals of Mathematics* 180 (2014), arXiv:1107.3776; PDF reopened 2026-10-30. **Statement:** Theorem 1.8: for δ_A > δ₀, denominators occur for almost every admissible integer, with a power-saving exceptional proportion and multiplicity ≫ N^(2δ_A−1001/1000). The paper says individual error control of size N^(2δ−1−ε) would imply the full conjecture, but establishes the requisite error bound only on average (equation (1.37)). **Status:** Published; almost-all, not all denominators. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/z31_zaremba_thin_orbits.md`, `BRAINSTORM/thin_group_orbits.md`.

**Citation:** I. D. Shkredov, “On some results of Korobov and Larcher and Zaremba's conjecture,” arXiv:2603.14116v2 (2026-06-24); PDF reopened and read 2026-10-30. **Statement:** Corollary 1 states: “There is an absolute constant M ≥ 2 such that for any prime (or square–free) number p there exists a, (a,p)=1” with p/a=[0;c₁,…,cₛ] and every cⱼ≤M. More strongly, Theorem 8 states for all sufficiently large absolute M and any positive integer q there are at least q^(2w_M−1−o(1)) coprime numerators a with all partial quotients ≤100M. Remark 9 records that after Zhang's general-modulus expansion, Lemma 14 holds for any q and bounded M, so Corollary 1 “is valid for any q” and “we now possess a proof of Zaremba's conjecture in full generality.” Theorem 8 itself presents the all-q bound. **Status:** Preprint; theorem claims read in PDF, proof not independently referee-verified; this is not merely a prime-only claim. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/z31_zaremba_thin_orbits.md` already records the claim and mechanism; no new transfer.

**Citation:** Xin Zhang, “Expansion in SL₂(Z/qZ) and Zaremba's conjecture,” arXiv:2605.02518v2 (2026-05-08); PDF reopened and read 2026-10-30. **Statement:** Theorem 1.2 says “Zaremba’s conjecture holds.” Its preceding Conjecture 1.1 defines this as every natural q being the denominator of a reduced a/q with all partial quotients bounded by one absolute M; the text says the method produces a large absolute bound, not conjectured M=5. Theorem 2.1/2.2/2.4 give general-modulus growth/flattening inputs; Remark 1.4 explains that fixed-generator spectral gap is insufficient because the generating set depends on q and has size about q^(2τ). **Status:** arXiv preprint v2 (2026-05-08; no journal/refutation found in records searched); PDF read, proof not independently referee-verified. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/z31_zaremba_thin_orbits.md` already records the claim and its conditional transfer screen.

### 4. Markoff strong approximation: pointwise orbit-size mechanism
**Citation:** W. Y. Chen, “A proof of the Markoff conjecture,” arXiv:2011.12940 (2020), *Annals of Mathematics* 199 (2024); PDF reopened and read 2026-10-30. **Statement:** Theorem 1.2.3 (Bourgain–Gamburd–Sarnak): for every ε>0, the exceptional primes up to x are O(x^ε), and the complement of a largest Γ-orbit in X*(p) has size ≤ p^ε. Theorem 1.2.5: every Γ-orbit on X*(p) has cardinality divisible by p. Theorem 1.2.6: the exceptional set is finite and explicitly bounded; outside it there is strong approximation. **Status:** Published. Pointwise conclusion follows because a nonempty orbit has at least p elements while the complement to the giant orbit is at most p^ε; this proves transitivity for all sufficiently large p, with explicit finite treatment. It is an orbit-connectivity statement on a fibre with ≍p² points, not existence of an integral point on every varying surface. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/z31_markoff_chen.md`, `MARKOFF_ES.md`, `BRAINSTORM/markoff_hasse_failures_vs_es.md` (mechanism and axis distinction already documented).

### 5. Markoff integral Hasse principle is not pointwise
**Citation:** A. Ghosh, P. Sarnak, “Integral points on Markoff type cubic surfaces,” arXiv:1706.06712; *Inventiones Mathematicae* 229 (2022); arXiv abstract reopened 2026-10-30; full PDF not reopened. **Statement:** Abstract says the Hasse principle holds for almost all parameters k, and fails for infinitely many k. **Status:** Published; the precise quantitative failure bound and theorem numbering were not verified from the primary source in this lane. **Relation:** `LITERATURE_AUTOMORPHIC`, `BRAINSTORM/z31_markoff_chen.md`, `BRAINSTORM/markoff_hasse_failures_vs_es.md`; supports the existing warning that varying-family integral existence is distinct from Chen's fixed-fibre connectivity.

### 7. 2024–26 Markoff connectivity completions and nearby results
- **N. Kingsbury-Neuschotz, “Strong Approximation for the Character Variety of the Four-Times Punctured Sphere,” arXiv:2603.04096v3 (2026-07-29), PDF/HTML reopened 2026-10-30.** Theorem 1.1: for any nondegenerate integer parameter quadruple (A,B,C,D), a density-one set of primes has a single giant Γ-orbit on the Markoff-type surface over F_p, possibly alongside bounded orbits descending from complex finite orbits. The paper extends this to exactly two or four large orbits for density-one primes for degenerate parameters (Theorem 10.1 plus §11). It expressly says the prime set for transitivity depends on the parameters; this is density-one, not every-p. **Relation:** strengthens `BRAINSTORM/z31_markoff_chen.md` distinction between giant-orbit/average results and all-p Chen divisibility.
- **J. Bourgain, A. Gamburd, P. Sarnak, “Strong approximation and Diophantine properties of Markoff triples,” *Journal of the American Mathematical Society* 39(1) (2026), 177–204, DOI:10.1090/jams/1061; Crossref abstract read 2026-10-30.** Abstract: establishes transitivity properties of Vieta actions modulo primes, obtaining forms of strong approximation for the Markoff surface; applies these to show almost all Markoff numbers are highly divisible. This is not the all-p connectivity conjecture and the arithmetic conclusion is almost-all. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/z31_markoff_chen.md` and `BRAINSTORM/markoff_hasse_failures_vs_es.md`.
- **D. E. Martin, “A new proof of Chen’s theorem for Markoff graphs,” arXiv:2502.15960v1 (2025-02-21); *Inventiones Mathematicae* 241 (2025), DOI:10.1007/s00222-025-01346-9; PDF accessed/read 2026-10-30.** Theorem 1.3 restates Chen's pointwise input: each connected component has vertex count divisible by p, so BGS's |G_p\C_p|<p^ε giant-component bound implies connectivity for all sufficiently large p. Martin supplies a new proof, not a new “all p” theorem. The paper reports Eddy et al.'s effective p>3.489·10³⁹² (rounding differs slightly from their abstract). **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/z31_markoff_chen.md`.
- **C. A. Brown, “An almost linear time algorithm testing whether the Markoff graph modulo p is connected,” arXiv:2401.00630v3 (2024-01-26); *Experimental Mathematics* (2025), accessed 2026-10-30.** Abstract: algorithm runs in o(p^(1+ε)) for every ε>0 and verifies connectivity for every prime below 10⁶. This is finite certification, not completion of Baragar's all-primes conjecture.
- **M. de Courcy-Ireland, M. Litman, Y. Mizuno, “Divisibility by p for Markoff-like Surfaces,” arXiv:2509.02187v3 (2026-06-05), accessed 2026-10-30.** Theorem 1.1: for their stated generic parameter conditions, every nonzero orbit has size divisible by p; certain exceptional parameter families also satisfy divisibility under an extra relation. The authors expect one generic orbit but identify special cases with multiple orbits. This extends the orbit-size mechanism, not a blanket connectivity result.
- **J. Ma, M. Yang, Z. Yang, “Sharp vertex connectivity of the Markoff graphs modulo p,” arXiv:2608.07880v1 (2026-08-08), accessed 2026-10-30.** Abstract: if G_p is connected then it is 2-connected; consequently 2-connected for sufficiently large p; not 3-connected for any p≥7. No new proof of connectivity for every prime.
- **S. Satake, Y. Yamasaki, “Topological properties of generalized Markoff mod p graphs,” arXiv:2512.21963v1 (2025-12-26), accessed 2026-10-30.** Abstract concerns nonplanarity, embeddings, and short cycles via K₃,₃ subdivisions; it does not settle connectivity.
- **B. Nica, “Counting in Vieta graphs over F_p,” arXiv:2608.03097v1 (2026-08-04), accessed 2026-10-30.** Abstract develops vertex/degree counts for multivariate quadratic Vieta graphs, including three/four variables; counts do not assert all-point integral lifting or universal orbit connectivity.
- **Eddy–Fuchs–Litman–Martin–Tripeny, “Connectivity of Markoff mod-p graphs and maximal divisors,” arXiv:2308.07579 (2023), *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society* (2026), DOI:10.1112/plms.70027; abstract reopened 2026-10-30.** The abstract proves connectivity for p>3.448·10³⁹² using Chen+BGS and maximal-divisor estimates. This is an effective finite threshold, not all p. The threshold is a computational consequence of bounds, not a different pointwise existence mechanism.

### 8. Zaremba/C.F. distinctions and exact-denominator controls
- **N. G. Moshchevitin, I. D. Shkredov, “On a modular form of Zaremba's conjecture,” arXiv:1911.07487v1 (2019), accessed 2026-10-30.** Abstract: for every prime p, some q=O(p³⁰) divisible by p has a coprime numerator with bounded partial quotients; also an O(p^C) multiple with all digits ≤2. This proves a multiple-denominator relaxation, not the exact denominator p, and its count/height scale is polynomial rather than polylog.
- **Zaremba full theorem clarification:** Shkredov v2 Theorem 8 and Zhang Theorem 1.2 both state all-q bounded partial quotient existence with some absolute A (Shkredov's displayed digit bound 100M for large M; Zhang says the resulting bound is large and effectively computable). Neither asserts A=5. Zhang's theorem is genuinely the full all-denominator conjecture, not merely primes. A same-date exact search for “2605.02518” response/correction returned no result; arXiv's v2 says only typos fixed. Shkredov v2 incorporates that development in Remark 9; this strengthens the earlier campaign's characterization of these as prime-only/unchecked claims.
- **Length ≤3 vs bounded partial quotients:** Zaremba bounds every digit but does not bound the number of digits by 3. Conversely, ES₅'s campaign dictionary is the specialized predicate λ(p)≤3 (a height-like sum of selected odd-position digits, with a quadratic-nonresidue condition), not “some ordinary CF of p has ≤3 terms” and not a bounded-digit condition. Targeted web search and checks of BK/Shkredov/Zhang sources found no theorem that every denominator has an ordinary CF of length ≤3, nor a result implying the constrained ES₅ predicate. The only verified connection is the already established ES₅ equivalence in `DEDEKIND_ES.md`/`QUESTION_R.md`; no new transfer theorem.

### 9. Pointwise thin-regime audit (the requested high-value check)
**Operational criterion:** theorem gives each admissible n/p a representation while a natural per-object count is only polylogarithmic or O(1), not merely a theorem whose input set is thin or whose global exceptional set has density zero.

- **Rickards–Stange Theorem 2.6/2.8:** effective cofinite representation of permitted nonsquares as numerators in a particular semigroup orbit, with an explicit finite sporadic list. This is the closest every-n result in the reciprocity/thin-semigroup material. But theorem proves membership, not an expected O(log^C n) multiplicity; the paper's dimension-1 semigroup and its counting bounds do not establish the campaign's requested polylog/O(1) per-target event regime. **Near-example, not certified match.**
- **Shkredov/Zhang Zaremba:** all q is asserted, but Theorem 8 gives q^(2w_M−1−o(1)) eligible numerators for fixed sufficiently large M (power-size, not polylog); not a thin-count example.
- **Markoff/Chen:** all sufficiently large p have one orbit, but this is connectivity among ≍p² finite-field solutions and uses a giant component; not a representation problem with polylog expected count. EFLMT makes the finite exceptional range effective, not empty.
- **Apollonian HKRS and follow-ups:** pointwise exclusion families, not universal positive representation; BK's density-one theorem is averaged.
- **Conclusion (Assessment):** no verified every-object representation theorem with independently established expected multiplicity polylogarithmic or O(1) surfaced in this literature lane. The universal examples fall into the wrong regime (power-size menus / large ambient fibres); the thin reciprocity theorems establish exclusions or do not quantify per-target multiplicity. This directly leaves B2/B6 untouched.

**Campaign relation for the follow-up entries:** they refine the analogues already inventoried in `BRAINSTORM/apollonian_reciprocity.md`, `BRAINSTORM/z31_zaremba_thin_orbits.md`, and `BRAINSTORM/z31_markoff_chen.md`; none supplies a new ES certificate mechanism.
