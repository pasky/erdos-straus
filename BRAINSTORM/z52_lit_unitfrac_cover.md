# z52 L2 — Unit fractions, covering systems, and multiplicative combinatorics

## §0 Summary
- **New since 2026-09-28:** Filaseta–Kalogirou–Klein (arXiv:2610.34541, 2026-10-25) prove a reciprocal-modulus lower bound for distinct covering systems with prime minimum modulus; this is still finite-system control.
- **Transferable features:** density/mass or many freely chosen terms; aggregate uncovered-density control; subset-product forcing given sufficient length/spread.
- **ES overlap:** Partial algebraic analogy only. No source-verified result yields pointwise coverage or forces the needed divisor ratio; B2/B3/B6 remain unchanged.
- **Campaign verdict changes:** None. The detailed distortion and zero-sum verdicts were already screened in `BRAINSTORM/covering_distortion_dual.md`, `BRAINSTORM/addcomb_unit_fraction_density.md`, and `BRAINSTORM/z31_zero_sum.md`.
- **Status note:** This dossier verifies abstract-level claims from arXiv API records; it does not assert proof-level verification of unaccessed theorem details (listed in §4).

## §1 Verified entries

Access date for all listed source records: 2026-10-30. The decisive abstract text is preserved in `sources/z52/unitfrac_cover/arxiv-abstracts.txt`; arXiv API records were directly accessed. These abstract-level checks verify the quoted theorem statements; details not in abstracts are not asserted here.

1. **Thomas F. Bloom**, “On a density conjecture about unit fractions,” arXiv:2112.03726v2 (updated 2023-10-12; published JEMS 2025 per repo). Abstract theorem: every positive-upper-density `A⊂N` contains a finite `S⊂A` with `Σ_{n∈S}1/n=1`. Status: published. Relation: `BRAINSTORM/addcomb_unit_fraction_density.md`, `BRAINSTORM/z31_unit_fractions.md`; density-of-input, not fixed three-term pointwise.
2. **Yang P. Liu, Mehtaab Sawhney**, “On further questions regarding unit fractions,” arXiv:2404.07113v1 (2024-04-10). Abstract theorem: if `A⊂[1,N]` has reciprocal sum at least `(log N)^{4/5+o(1)}`, it contains `B` with reciprocal sum 1; techniques refine Croot/Bloom. Status: preprint (v1 source record accessed; publication status not verified). Relation: same two repo memos above.
3. **David Conlon, Jacob Fox, Xiaoyu He, Dhruv Mubayi, Huy Tuan Pham, Andrew Suk, Jacques Verstraëte**, “A question of Erdős and Graham on Egyptian fractions,” arXiv:2404.16016v2 (updated 2025-12-17), *Discrete Analysis* 2025:28. Abstract theorem: for each fixed positive rational `x`, the number of distinct-denominator representations by denominators ≤`n` is `2^{(c_x+o(1))n}`, with explicit `c_x` increasing with `x`. Status: published. Relation: `BRAINSTORM/addcomb_unit_fraction_density.md`; not an every-prime three-term assertion.
4. **Thomas F. Bloom**, “Unit fractions with shifted prime denominators,” arXiv:2305.02689v1 (2023-05-04), DOI 10.1017/prm.2024.42, *Proc. R. Soc. Edinburgh A* 155 (2025). Abstract theorem: every positive rational is a finite sum of distinct `1/(p−1)`; analogous `p−h` and positive-relative-density prime-subset versions require a stated necessary congruence condition. Status: published. Relation: `BRAINSTORM/z31_unit_fractions.md`; denominator family supplies unboundedly many terms, not three divisors at one prime.
5. **Will Sawin**, “Sets of unit fractions without two members whose average is a unit fraction,” arXiv:2607.15419v1 (2026-07-16). Abstract theorem: for all sufficiently large `N`, there is `A⊂{1,…,N}` of size `>cN` avoiding pairs whose reciprocal average is a unit fraction; also improves a lower bound for unit-fraction 3-term-AP-free sets. Status: preprint. Relation: `BRAINSTORM/addcomb_unit_fraction_density.md`; this is a dense-set avoidance result, not a representation theorem for each target.
6. **Samuel Korsky**, “Approximating 1 by Sums of Unit Fractions,” arXiv:2607.04157v2 (updated 2026-10-08). Abstract theorem: any finite multiset of unit fractions with sum `>K` has a sub-sum within `exp(−c√(K log K))` below 1, for sufficiently large K. Status: preprint. Relation: `BRAINSTORM/addcomb_unit_fraction_density.md`; approximation from a large available total is not exact three-term representation.
7. **Vjekoslav Kovač, Quanyu Tang**, “Eventually greedy best Egyptian underapproximations of rational numbers via optimal control,” arXiv:2607.28387v2 (updated 2026-08-04). Abstract theorem: every positive rational has eventually greedy best Egyptian underapproximations, both allowing repeated denominators and requiring distinct denominators. Status: preprint. Relation: `BRAINSTORM/z31_unit_fractions.md`; greedy approximation concerns an infinite sequence, not a bounded-length exact identity.
8. **Paul Balister, Béla Bollobás, Robert Morris, Julian Sahasrabudhe, Marius Tiba**, “On the Erdős Covering Problem: the density of the uncovered set,” arXiv:1811.03547v1 (2018-11-08), *Invent. Math.* 228 (2022), 377–414. Abstract verifies a sufficient near-best-possible condition for positive uncovered density for large distinct moduli/reciprocal weights; proves **Schinzel’s conjecture** that every covering system has two moduli, one dividing the other; and reports progress on Erdős–Selfridge. The density theorem is average over integers; the Schinzel conclusion is pointwise structural for each covering. Status: published. Relation: `BRAINSTORM/covering_distortion_dual.md`; the latter analyzes the distortion method and ES congruence-class analogue.
9. **Michael Filaseta, Alexandros Kalogirou, Jonah Klein**, “Results for distinct covering systems with a prime minimum modulus `q≥11`,” arXiv:2610.34541v1 (2026-10-25). Abstract: reciprocal-modulus sum is at least `1+10^{-28}`; no covering system can use distinct multiples of `q` as moduli. Status: preprint. Relation: `BRAINSTORM/covering_distortion_dual.md`; a finite-system bound, not an infinite/growing-modulus pointwise theorem.

**Subset-product / zero-sum scope.** The campaign's relevant deterministic multiplicative-set claims are already screened in `BRAINSTORM/addcomb_unit_fraction_density.md` (Kneser consequence: prime factors linear in the relevant index) and `z31_zero_sum.md`; no new sharp theorem with hypotheses matching the divisor-ratio set was source-verified in this lane. No theorem is added from memory.
### §1a Additional source-checked unit-fraction entries (all opened 2026-10-30)

10. **Ernest S. Croot III**, “On a coloring conjecture about unit fractions,” arXiv:math/0311421v1 (submitted 2003-11-24), *Ann. Math.* 157 (2003), 545–556. Full HTML opened today. Main Theorem: if `C⊂C'(N,N^{1+δ};θ)`, `δ+θ<1/4`, and `Σ_C 1/n>6`, then some subset sums to 1; corollary: every r-colouring of `[2,b^r]` has a monochromatic subset summing to 1. **Pointwise in every colouring**, but uses a huge set of smooth, normal denominators. Engine from full text: Fourier orthogonality on the lcm and major/minor arcs; smoothness plus a divisibility concentration lemma controls minor arcs. This is the foundational source behind the later Bloom method.
11. **Greg Martin**, “Denser Egyptian Fractions,” arXiv:math/9811112v1 (1998-11-18), *Acta Arith.* 95 (2000), 231–260. arXiv source page opened today; its abstract says every positive rational has representations with term count of the same order as the largest denominator; for fixed rational, integers that cannot appear as largest denominator have density zero, while only finitely many integers fail as the second-largest, third-largest, etc. **Pointwise representation/exceptional-set density statements**, but term count is unbounded. HTML conversion unavailable, so proof engine not verified. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811112.
12. **Yong-Gao Chen, Christian Elsholtz, Li-Li Jiang**, “Egyptian Fractions with Restrictions,” arXiv:1108.6118v1 (2011-08-31), *Acta Arith.* 154 (2012), 109–123. Full arXiv record opened today. For the multiplicative semigroup generated by distinct primes, if the sum of reciprocals of its elements >1, then the number of k-term representations of 1 is nonzero for infinitely many k, with such k a finite union of arithmetic progressions (abstract result 2). This is **infinitely many lengths**, not an every-target three-term theorem. Exact proof engine not asserted from abstract.
13. **Wouter van Doorn, Quanyu Tang**, “The smallest denominator not contained in a unit fraction decomposition of 1 with fixed length,” arXiv:2512.22083v2 (updated 2026-05-24), *Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc.*, published online 2026. Abstract theorem: `v(k)≥exp(c k²)`, where `v(k)` is the least integer >1 absent from every distinct k-term decomposition of 1. **Uniform extremal statement for every k**, not a density result or an existence theorem for one prescribed denominator. Abstract only; no engine claimed.
14. **Shisheng Li**, “Unit fractions with semiprime denominators: an elementary proof of Erdős Problem #306,” arXiv:2609.32140v1 (2026-09-26). Full arXiv page opened today. Every positive rational `a/b` with squarefree b is a finite sum of distinct reciprocals of products of two distinct primes. Abstract states a complete-bipartite-subgraph construction, congruence modulo 1, finite Fourier sum, frequency split, divisor bound and no-wrap-around CRT; Lean 4 formalization is included except a cited Ramanujan inequality. **Pointwise for each eligible rational**, with unbounded number of terms and chosen prime denominators. A potentially useful recent engine, but the input freedom is unlike ES.
15. **Christian Elsholtz, Stefan Planitzer**, “The number of solutions of the Erdős–Straus Equation and sums of k unit fractions,” arXiv:1805.02945v1 (2018-05-08). Source page opened today. For fixed m, the number of representations `m/n=1/a₁+1/a₂+1/a₃` is `O_ε(n^{3/5+ε})`; also gives infinitely many primes in each reduced residue class with exponentially large-in-log p counts, per the abstract. **Counting bound / subsequence lower bound**, not pointwise existence for every n. Proof engine not claimed from abstract. Relation: `FRONTIER.md` §1 and `BRAINSTORM/z31_unit_fractions.md`.
16. **David Eppstein**, “Egyptian Fractions with Denominators from Sequences Closed Under Doubling,” arXiv:2109.12217v1 (2021-09-24), *J. Integer Sequences* 24 (2021), Article 21.8.8. Source page opened today. Every rational has a distinct-unit-fraction representation with practical denominators; same method applies to sets closed under doubling and containing a multiple of every positive integer. **Pointwise in target**, but an expansive denominator system and unbounded length are central. Abstract only; method detail not claimed.
17. **Thomas F. Bloom**, “Unit fractions with shifted prime denominators,” arXiv:2305.02689v1 (2023-05-04), DOI 10.1017/prm.2024.42, *Proc. R. Soc. Edinburgh A* 155 (2025). Abstract record accessed 2026-10-30. Every positive rational is a sum of distinct `1/(p−1)`; `p−h` and positive-relative-density prime-subset versions require a necessary congruence condition. **Pointwise in target**, but freely chosen prime denominators and unbounded terms. Abstract only for this lane; engine not claimed.
18. **Bob Hough**, “Solution of the minimum modulus problem for covering systems,” arXiv:1307.0874v3 (last revised 2016-05-03), *Ann. Math.* 181.1 (2015), 361–382. Abstract page opened today. The abstract states the least modulus of a distinct covering system is at most `10^18`. **Uniform statement for each covering system**, not a pointwise ES statement. The paper title/metadata and bound are source-verified; this abstract bound differs from some later secondary summaries, so I preserve the primary-source version rather than silently harmonize it.
19. **Paul Balister, Béla Bollobás, Robert Morris, Julian Sahasrabudhe, Marius Tiba**, “The Erdős–Selfridge problem with square-free moduli,” arXiv:1901.11465v1 (2019-01-31), *Algebra & Number Theory* 15 (2021), 609–626. Abstract page opened today. If a covering system has distinct squarefree moduli, it must have an even modulus. **Pointwise structural obstruction** under squarefree hypothesis; does not resolve Erdős–Selfridge for all odd distinct moduli.
20. **Maria Cummings, Michael Filaseta, Ognian Trifonov**, “An upper bound for the minimum modulus in a covering system with squarefree moduli,” arXiv:2211.08548v1 (2022-11-15). Abstract page opened today. Distinct squarefree covering systems have minimum modulus at most 118; additionally the kth smallest modulus in a distinct covering system, if required, is bounded by an absolute constant. **Uniform covering-structure bounds**, not pointwise ES.
21. **Jonah Klein, Dimitris Koukoulopoulos, Simon Lemieux**, “On the j-th smallest modulus of a covering system with distinct moduli,” arXiv:2212.01299v2 (2023-08-23), *Int. J. Number Theory* 20 (2024). Abstract page opened today. For minimal distinct-modulus covering systems, the jth smallest modulus is at most `exp(c j²/log(j+1))`. **Uniform index-dependent bound**; not a growing-moduli covering theorem for ES.
22. **Paul Balister, Béla Bollobás, Robert Morris, Julian Sahasrabudhe, Marius Tiba**, “The structure and number of Erdős covering systems,” arXiv:1904.04806v2 (2022-11-16), *J. Eur. Math. Soc.* 26 (2024), 75–109. Abstract page opened today. Gives asymptotic count for minimal systems of size n and structural description of near-optimal systems. **Enumeration/structure**, not ES. The source's abstract says Hough's min-modulus result as `10^16`, unlike Hough's own arXiv v3 abstract (`10^18`); note version/source discrepancy, not resolve here.
23. **Miguel Ángel López**, “A Complete Congruence System for the Erdos-Straus Conjecture,” arXiv:2404.01508v3 (2024-04-15). Abstract record opened today. It defines congruence classes giving solutions when met and **conjectures**, rather than proves in the abstract, that this system includes every prime (and that each prime has a Type A or Type B solution). This is an **unproved pointwise coverage conjecture**, not a covering theorem.

### §1b Newer arXiv sweep and lead audit (search run 2026-10-30)

The arXiv API query `all:"unit fractions"`, sorted by `submittedDate` descending, surfaced (among relevant items) Korsky 2607.04157, Sawin 2607.15419, Kovač–Tang 2607.28387, Li 2609.32140, and van Doorn–Tang 2512.22083. The separate proposed title **“Set-coloring Ramsey / Erdős–Graham” does not exist as one paper** in the checked records. Conlon et al.'s actual paper is entry 3, about exact Egyptian-fraction representation counts; Conlon's author page lists set-coloring Ramsey numbers as a separate, unrelated Ramsey/code paper. This title distinction is source-checked today. “all:unit fractions” is a discovery query, not a claim of complete coverage: arXiv indexing and phrase matching may omit relevant work.

**Engine assessment from full texts:** Bloom and Croot are genuinely Fourier/circle-method arguments on restricted smooth/normal denominators, with large collections and pruning/divisibility control. Liu–Sawhney refine this framework with random subset weights, Fourier major/minor arcs, smooth-number sieving and pruning; source does not describe the proof as a density-increment argument. Conlon et al. is distinct: entropy counts subsets up to a target mass, then absorption repairs the residue/denominator constraints using modular-inverse subset sums. The remaining source abstracts don’t justify classifying their proofs as Fourier or density-increment methods; where they name a technique, it is stated entrywise above.
