## §0. Taxonomy and novelty audit

**Assessment / scope.** This is a source-limited catalogue, not a claim to have exhausted all mathematics. “Expected count” needs a specified sampling model and height cutoff. Below, **thin** means a bounded or polylogarithmic *number of final witnesses*, not a short description, a sparse ambient set, or a theorem whose statement merely says “there exists”. Exact counts override heuristics. Most examples proposed in the brief are useful countercomparisons, not thin precedents.

### Genuine sparse precedents, and nearby non-counting mechanisms

| # / label | Pointwise statement and witness count | Mechanism; ES comparison; precise source locator |
|---|---|---|
| 1 **PROVED** Two squares | Every prime p≡1 (4) is a²+b². Exactly 8 ordered signed integer representations, equivalently one up to signs/order (the uniqueness is the elementary Gaussian-factorization count). | A genuinely O(1) final target. Zagier uses an auxiliary finite set with a unique fixed point and a second involution; Minkowski uses a determinant-p lattice and 0<a²+b²<2p. Neither counts the final target asymptotically. Zagier, Monthly 97 (1990), p.144, DOI 10.2307/2323918, unnumbered proof (P3); Dong, unnumbered two-square theorem (P7). `INVOLUTION_ES2.md`: only the specified finite affine menu is barred, not every growing auxiliary set. |
| 2 **PROVED** Dirichlet approximation | For every real α and integer Q≥1, ∃1≤q≤Q and a∈Z with |qα−a|<1/Q. Under uniform α mod1 the expected number of denominators with ||qα||<1/Q is 2 for Q≥2 (endpoints have measure zero). No uniform O(1) count for each α: rational α can give many. | Pigeonhole creates a difference; closeness is exactly the desired property. Paulson, *Formalising an easy proof: Dirichlet’s approximation theorem* (2025-01-22), unnumbered theorem, accessed authored exposition (S). ES needs an exact divisor, not just a small remainder; `DETERMINISTIC_EXISTENCE.md` row1, B4. |
| 3 **PROVED** Minkowski | Every full-rank Λ and centrally symmetric convex K with vol(K)>2^d detΛ has a nonzero lattice point. At fixed d and volume ratio O(1), a random-translate lattice count has O(1) mean, although the theorem is about the centered symmetric body and counts can be large in degenerate bodies. | Symmetry+convexity lets a collision be halved back into K. Dong, unnumbered general-lattice theorem, first note p.3 (P7); original Minkowski NOT ACCESSED. ES's divisor subset is not a convex body; `BALANCED_LATTICE.md`, B4/checklist12. |
| 4 **PROVED** Pell | For every positive nonsquare D, x²−Dy²=1 has positive integer solutions; all are powers of the fundamental unit. For fixed D there are O_D(log H) solutions through coordinate height H. | Periodicity/unit group supplies existence before counting. Important mismatch: first height is unrestricted, not a prescribed polynomial in D. Lenstra, *Solving the Pell equation*, MSRI 44 (2008), §1 pp.1–3 of accessed copy; unnumbered power formula (P4). `DETERMINISTIC_EXISTENCE.md` §4 already tests the wrong-divisor-class problem; B-fin. |
| 5 **PROVED** Sperner / Brouwer | Every Sperner-labelled finite triangulated simplex has an odd number of fully labelled simplices. Every continuous self-map of a compact simplex has a fixed point. One witness is possible; there is no canonical “expected count” without a labeling/map distribution. | Boundary parity survives subdivision; Brouwer then needs compactness and continuity. Ivanov, *Sperner’s lemma, Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem, and cohomology*, arXiv:0906.5193v2 (2019-08-24), named unnumbered statements (S). A real limit is not a positive integral point. Prior `BRAINSTORM/invol_topological.md`; finite-index arithmetic variant remains the only relevant analogy. |
| 6 **PROVED** Chevalley–Warning / Ax–Katz | For f_i over F_q of characteristic ℓ in r variables, r>Σdeg f_i implies ℓ divides the common-zero count. A known zero therefore forces another. The sufficient higher-divisibility bound in the accessed exposition is r>(j−1)max deg f_i+Σdeg f_i ⇒ ℓ^j divides the count. | Count congruence, not count approximation. A zero count itself is allowed unless an initial zero is known; there is no archimedean conclusion. Grynkiewicz, arXiv:2208.12895v1 (2022-08-26), Thms1.1–1.2 (P1); original papers NOT ACCESSED. §2.2 gives the missing ES boundary computation. |
| 7 **PROVED** Combinatorial Nullstellensatz | If deg f=Σt_i and [∏x_i^{t_i}]f≠0, then f is nonzero somewhere on every grid with |S_i|>t_i. A single nonzero value is possible; no general thin-count assertion. | An exact coefficient, not randomness, forces existence. Alon, *Combinatorial Nullstellensatz*, CPC8 (1999), DOI 10.1017/S0963548398003413, Thm1.2 (P2). Encoding a target divisor in the polynomial does not prove its coefficient nonzero. `BRAINSTORM/polynomial_method.md` errata correctly leave target-specific constructions open. |
| 8 **PROVED** Cauchy–Davenport / EGZ / Kemnitz | Nonempty A,B⊆F_ℓ have |A+B|≥min(ℓ,|A|+|B|−1). Every 2n−1 terms in C_n contain n terms summing to0; every 4ℓ−3 terms in C_ℓ² contain ℓ terms summing to0. | Polynomial/nonvanishing plus induction or double counting. Alon Thm3.2, Prop8.2 and adjacent induction paragraph; Grynkiewicz Thm1.6 (P1–2). In an independent uniform C_n model the expected EGZ witness count is binom(2n−1,n)/n, exponential—not polylog! Their witnesses have length n, not log n. Prior `BRAINSTORM/z31_zero_sum.md`; target −1 and budget remain missing. |
| 9 **PROVED** Olson / Davenport for p-groups | D(⊕C_{ℓ^{a_i}})=1+Σ(ℓ^{a_i}−1): every sequence of that length has a nonempty zero-sum subsequence. | Group algebra or CW, Grynkiewicz Thm1.5 (P1), original Olson NOT ACCESSED. For C_ℓ, D=ℓ, not logℓ. For C_2^r, D=r+1=log₂|G|+1: genuine logarithmic length comes from bounded exponent/high rank, not arbitrary cyclic multiplicative groups. `BRAINSTORM/z33_target_davenport.md` retains the correct cyclic counterexample; no transfer from C_2^r to (Z/q)^×. |
| 10 **PROVED** Huang | Every (2^{d−1}+1)-vertex induced subgraph of the d-cube has maximum degree at least √d. No theorem here counts high-degree vertices; one vertex is the certificate. | Signed adjacency matrix with rigid spectrum and interlacing. Huang, *Induced subgraphs of hypercubes and a proof of the Sensitivity Conjecture*, arXiv:1907.00847v2 (2019-08-29), abstract (X3). Huge domain density >1/2 is essential to this statement. `BRAINSTORM/z45_interlacing_stable.md` checks that ES forest signings are gauge-equivalent, not a useful spectral family. |

**Assessment.** The strongest answers to the meta-question are #1–5, not Burgess or three squares. They show that a small final count is not a universal obstruction to pointwise existence. What forces the count is a norm identity, a lattice difference with a size-to-equality step, an infinite unit orbit, or a boundary index. The corrected B3 is about counting architectures, not a ban on those methods.

### Rejected leads and access limits (not silent omissions)

- **PROVED, wrong quantifier:** Heath-Brown's “one of2,3,5” means ∃a∈{2,3,5} with a primitive root at infinitely many primes, not ∀p∃a. Journal DOI 10.1093/qmath/37.1.27; full original **NOT ACCESSED** (bot gate), statement checked through K. Conrad's opened 2022 MathOverflow answer (E). `BRAINSTORM/z31_small_count_pointwise.md` already flags the mismatch. No smallest-primitive-root refinement is invoked here.
- **PROVED, wrong direction:** Erdős–Selfridge, *The Product of Consecutive Integers is Never a Power*, Illinois J.Math.19 (1975), DOI 10.1215/ijm/1256050816, Thm1 (opened PDF, E). An obstruction theorem, not existence of a prescribed factor class. The PDF OCR's `>`/`≥` transcription in its setup is not used to narrow the standard theorem.
- **PROVED, average only:** Romanoff gives positive lower density for p+2^k; Davenport–Heilbronn gives aggregate cubic-field/3-torsion counts. Sources and versioned modern restatements in E. **CONJECTURE/heuristic:** Cohen–Lenstra distributions do not supply every-field existence. All fail the desired quantifier, B6, before any ES transfer.
- **NOT ACCESSED at theorem level:** Duke–Schulze-Pillot exact ternary hypotheses (journal metadata opened; PDF OCR failed); Ono–Soundararajan/Ramanujan exact exceptions and GRH clauses; Ratner/Margulis–Oppenheim precise hypotheses; Bertrand's original/source statement; exact least-primitive-root bounds; coding-theory existence bounds; Sierpiński/Riesel covering examples and attribution of Selfridge-type p+2^k failures. They remain leads, not source-verified catalogue entries. No universal thin-existence theorem is asserted from them.
- **Assessment / cardinality trap:** a coding/logic theorem producing a finite string or a short certificate is not automatically a small-witness-count theorem. A compactness theorem with an infinite model as witness is not a finite-height integer theorem. The catalogue does not invent a probability space to classify those as O(1).
