# z52 L6 — Pointwise divisor-in-class results at one integer

## §0 Summary
- **Genuinely new since 2026-09-28:** Verified an unconditional almost-all-moduli subset-product generation theorem (Klurman–Shparlinski–Teräväinen, 2026); it is not pointwise for a prescribed prime/modulus. No pointwise lower bound for divisors of one prescribed integer in a prescribed class found.
- **Transferable structural features:** GRH character separation forces a prime in every fixed subgroup coset at a logarithmic-square scale; Burgess/generation arguments combine small witnesses; lattice methods enumerate divisors once the modulus/interval hypotheses hold. The first concerns primes free of divisibility constraints; the latter two do not yield B2’s prescribed divisor.
- **Campaign verdict change:** None. ES remains open; no verified theorem supplies the B2 lower-bound/forcing step. GRH does supply the character-side prime nonresidue criterion (LLS), not an unconditional divisor-in-class result.

## §1 Verified entries

Access dates below are 2026-10-30. Primary-source excerpts are saved under `sources/z52/divisor_class/`.

1. **H. W. Lenstra, Jr., “Divisors in Residue Classes,” Math. Comp. 42 (165) (1984), 331–340; DOI not listed in consulted source.** Source: author-hosted journal scan (1984), Theorem p. 332. If `0<r<s<n`, `s>n^(1/3)`, and `(r,s)=1`, at most 11 positive divisors of `n` are congruent to `r mod s`, and all can be found in polynomial time. **Status:** published. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/s3_lll_lenstra.md`, `BRAINSTORM/bsN_divisor_box.md`, `BRAINSTORM/s6_divisor_pigeonhole.md`; grep also surfaced `EXSET_SHIFT_DIVISORS.md`. It is an upper bound/algorithm, not existence; `s≈N^(1/4)` is outside the theorem.
2. **Don Coppersmith, Nick Howgrave-Graham, S. V. Nagaraj, “Divisors in Residue Classes, Constructively,” Math. Comp. 77 (2008), 531–545; Cryptology ePrint 2004/339 (2004-12-07).** Primary ePrint abstract: reexamines Lenstra’s problem and improves its divisor-count bound while constructing the divisors; its parameter condition and bound are lost in accessible abstract transcription, so I do not quote them. **Status:** published; source abstract accessed. **Relation:** same repo files as entry 1; no existence guarantee.
3. **Kevin Ford, “Integers with a divisor in (y,2y],” arXiv:math/0607473v5 (2013-03-17); published in *Anatomy of Integers*, CRM Proc. Lect. Notes 46 (2008), 65–80.** Abstract / (1.1): `H(x,y,2y) ≍ x/((log y)^δ(log log y)^(3/2))`, `3≤y≤√x`, where `H` counts integers `n≤x` having a divisor in `(y,2y]` and `δ=1−(1+log log 2)/log 2`. **Status:** published. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/s6_divisor_pigeonhole.md`, `EXSET_SHIFT_DIVISORS.md`; ambient counting only, no pointwise structured-`N` statement.
4. **Marc Munsch, “On the minimal number of small elements generating prime fields,” arXiv:1703.06673v1 (2017-03-20).** Theorem 3.2 bounds the number of elements ≤`p^(1/(4√e)+ε)` needed to generate `F_p*` by `≪ ω_l(p−1)+(ω(p−1)−ω_l(p−1))/log l`; Corollary 3.7 gives `≪(log_3 p)^2` for almost all `p`. **Status:** preprint. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/bsN_divisor_box.md` (group/coset route), `BRAINSTORM/z35_modp_least_residue.md`; pointwise generating-set conclusion, not an arbitrary prescribed residue or a factor of `4Cp+1`.
5. **N. A. Carella, “Upper Bound of the Least Quadratic Nonresidues,” arXiv:2106.00544v9 (2025-10-09).** Theorem 1.1 claims unconditionally `n(p)≪(log p)(log log p)^(1+ε)`. **Status:** claimed proof with a gap. In §7, Lemma 7.2, (7.6) starts with `v<p/x`, where `uv<p`, then extends the sum to `v<p` and applies `1/|sin(πuv/p)|≪p/(uv)`, asserting `uv<p`. That assertion fails in the enlarged range; no bound for the added terms is supplied. The asserted error estimate and theorem do not follow from the displayed argument. **Relation:** `FRONTIER.md` §3/§4 and `BRAINSTORM/z35_modp_least_residue.md` contain the established pointwise QNR context; no verdict change.
6. **Jonathon Hales, “Divisors in Residue Classes Revisited,” arXiv:2410.05030v1 (2024-10-07).** Abstract: generalizes Lenstra’s algorithm to other Euclidean rings and `Z[x]`; integer algorithm referenced under `s^3>N`. **Status:** preprint. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/s3_lll_lenstra.md`, `BRAINSTORM/gon_lll_relation_lattice.md`; algorithmic generalization, not a lower bound or pointwise existence theorem.
7. **Kevin Ford, “The distribution of integers with a divisor in a given interval,” Ann. of Math. 168 (2008), 367–433; arXiv:math.NT/0401223.** Abstract states order-of-magnitude results for `H(x,y,z)`, integers `n≤x` with some divisor in `(y,z]`, for all parameters; also studies the count `H_r` with exactly `r` divisors. For `r=1` it obtains the order in the range `z≤x^(1/2−ε)`; for each `r≥2` it gives the order in a specified broad range. **Status:** published. **Relation:** this refines the earlier Ford entry’s scale/role, but remains an ambient population count, not a prescribed-`N` assertion. Primary excerpt: `sources/z52/divisor_class/batch_2026-10-30.txt`.
8. **Dimitris Koukoulopoulos, “Divisors of shifted primes,” arXiv:0905.0163v3 (2009; published 2010).** Theorem 1.7: for fixed nonzero integer `s` and `B≥2`, sufficiently large `x`, `3≤y+1≤z≤x`, some `d∈(y,z]` coprime to `s`, and `z≥y+y/(log y)^B`, `H(x,y,z;P_s) ≍_{s,B} H(x,y,z)/log x`, where `P_s={p+s:p prime}`. Theorem 1.6 gives a corresponding short-interval lower bound for `x−Δ<p+s≤x`, `x(log x)^(−B)≤Δ≤x`, under `y≤√x` and the same interval-width condition. **Status:** published. **Pointwise/average:** count over a population of shifted primes, not a specified shifted prime or prescribed divisor class. **Relation:** strong B6-style average; does not imply B2.
9. **Paul Pollack, “Bounds for the first several prime character nonresidues,” arXiv:1508.05035v2 (2015).** Theorem 1.1: for each `ε>0` there are `m_0(ε),κ(ε)>0` such that every nontrivial Dirichlet character `χ mod m`, `m>m_0`, has more than `m^κ` prime numbers `ℓ≤m^(1/(4√e)+ε)` with `χ(ℓ)∉{0,1}`. **Status:** preprint. **Pointwise/average:** pointwise in each modulus and character, but the scale is polynomial in `m`, far above logarithmic-square; it neither furnishes B2’s prescribed divisor nor the required GRH forcing bound.
10. **Glyn Harman and Igor E. Shparlinski, “Products of Small Integers in Residue Classes and Additive Properties of Fermat Quotients,” arXiv:1408.4515v2 (2014).** Theorem 1: for every `ε>0` and sufficiently large cube-free `q`, every reduced residue `a mod q` is a product of 14 integers `n_i≤q^(1/(4√e)+ε)`. Theorem 2 allows nine such factors up to a subgroup translate when the subgroup order is `≫q^(1/2)`. **Status:** published in *J. Number Theory* 155 (2015), 1–21. **Pointwise/average:** pointwise coverage of every residue class (with freely chosen small factors), not factors constrained to divide a specified integer. It does not meet B2’s prescribed-divisor condition.
11. **Youness Lamzouri, Xiannan Li, Kannan Soundararajan, “Conditional bounds for the least quadratic non-residue and related problems,” Math. Comp. 84 (2015), 2391–2412; arXiv:1309.3595.** Corollary 1.1: assuming GRH, for every prime `q≥5`, the least quadratic nonresidue modulo `q` is below `(log q)^2`; this least integer is prime, so it supplies a prime `q_NR` with `(q_NR/q)=−1`. **Status:** published. **Pointwise/average:** pointwise conditional result. This directly meets the character-side numerical scale in the forcing criterion `q_NR≤2(log p)^2`, indeed with strict `(log p)^2`, and therefore supports the GRH-forcing equivalence conditional on the campaign’s stated criterion; it does not supply an unconditional B2 divisor-in-class result. Theorem 1.2 further gives, under GRH, for subgroup index `h`, least prime outside `H` below `(α(h)+o(1))(log q)^2` with `α(2)=0.8`, `α(3)=0.7`, and `α(h)=0.66` for `h>3`. Theorem 1.4 treats an arbitrary coset `aH`: for `q≥20000`, its least prime is either `≤10^9` or at most `((h−1)log q+3(h+1)+(5/2)(log log q)^2)^2`. These are pointwise prime-in-coset statements, but the primes are freely chosen and need not divide a specified `N`. Introduction §1.1 reports Bach’s ERH bounds for the least prime outside a proper subgroup: `<2(log q)^2` without excluding primes dividing `q`, `<3(log q)^2` with that exclusion, and asymptotically `(1+o(1))(log q)^2`; LLS Theorem 1.1 refines these. This does not change the campaign’s conditional forcing assessment.
12. **Bryce Kerr, Igor E. Shparlinski, Kam Hung Yau, “A refinement of the Burgess bound for character sums,” Michigan Math. J. 69 (2020), 227–240; arXiv:1711.10582v2.** Theorem 2.1: for prime `q`, integer `r≥2`, and `M,N` with `N≤q^(1/2+1/(4r))`, every nontrivial multiplicative character `χ mod q` satisfies `Σ_{M<n≤M+N}χ(n) ≪ N^(1−1/r)q^((r+1)/(4r²))(log q)^(1/(4r))`, absolute implied constant. **Status:** published. **Pointwise/average:** pointwise bound on short sums, improving the logarithmic factor in Burgess; no new least-QNR scale or prescribed-divisor conclusion is claimed.
13. **Elijah Fromm, Leo Goldmakher, “Improving the Burgess bound via Pólya–Vinogradov,” Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 147 (2019), 461–466; arXiv:1706.03002v1.** Theorem A: if the Pólya–Vinogradov estimate improves to `Sχ(t)=o(√q log q)` for all even primitive quadratic `χ mod q`, then for odd primitive quadratic characters `ξ mod p`, `Sξ(t)=o(t)` for all `t≫ε p^ε`. This is a conditional implication between character-sum bounds, not an unconditional pointwise QNR improvement or divisor-in-class result.
14. **Jonathan W. Bober, Leo Goldmakher, “Pólya–Vinogradov and the least quadratic nonresidue,” Math. Ann. 366 (2016), 853–863; arXiv:1311.7556v2.** Theorem 1: assuming the stated improvement for all primitive even characters of order `g`, the paper concludes for primitive odd characters `ξ mod k` of order `g` with `3∤k` that `n_ξ≪exp(C_g log(k)/f(k))`, with the constant specified in the theorem. **Status:** published. This is a conditional transfer from improved long character sums to least nonresidues, not itself a new unconditional bound.
15. **Oleksiy Klurman, Igor E. Shparlinski, Joni Teräväinen, “On the generation of multiplicative groups by small primes,” arXiv:2609.25393v1 (2026-09-21).** Theorem 1.1: for fixed `ε∈(0,1)`, `A>0`, and every `Q≥3`, there exists a set `P` of at most `(log Q)^(1+ε)` primes, each at most `(log Q)^(C*(A+1))`, such that all but `O_{ε,A}(Q(log Q)^(−A))` integers `q≤Q` have every unit modulo `q` represented as a subset product of primes in `P`. **Status:** preprint. **Pointwise/average:** overwhelmingly almost-all-moduli; one shared set works across that population. It does not assert the result for each prescribed prime `q` or require the primes to divide a linked `N`. This is a newly verified generation result, not a B2 transfer.
16. **Cédric Pilatte, “Unconditional correctness of recent quantum algorithms for factoring and computing discrete logarithms,” Cryptology ePrint 2024/629 (2024-04-24); published Forum Math. Pi 14 (2026), e5.** Abstract: proves a version of Regev’s conjecture on short products of very small primes in `(Z/NZ)^×`, yielding unconditional correctness of a modified quantum algorithm and subsequent variants. The theorem concerns representation/generation in the full unit group with selected small primes; it is not an arbitrary prescribed coset or divisors of `N`. **Status:** published. **Primary-source limitation:** the ePrint abstract page opened; the linked full text was not opened in this pass, so no theorem number or stronger quantitative statement is claimed.
17. **Daniel J. Bernstein, “Reducing lattice bases to find small-height values of univariate polynomials,” in ANTS-VI, LNCS 3076 (2005), 42–59; author PDF dated 2008-05-02.** §6, Theorem 6.4: for `v≥n^(1/4)` and `(v,n)=1`, the number of divisors of `n` in `(u+vZ)∩[1,n^(1/2)]` is at most `33(log_2 n)^(3/2)+4.5log_2 n+10(log_2 n)^(1/2)+2`; the paper gives an algorithm using `O((log n)^(1/2))` arithmetic progressions/lattices. It describes the Coppersmith–Howgrave-Graham–Nagaraj method as handling `v≥n^(1/4+ε)` with lattice rank `O(ε^(−3/2))`, and Lenstra’s `v≥n^(1/4)` result with rank `O((log n)^2)`. **Status:** published. **Scope:** explicit upper-bound enumeration, no existence/lower bound. The exact Coppersmith history statement here is Bernstein’s report; the CHGN ePrint remained inaccessible, so no stronger primary-source claim is made.
18. **Eric Bach and Jonathan Sorenson, “Explicit bounds for primes in residue classes,” Math. Comp. 65 (1996), 1717–1735, DOI 10.1090/S0025-5718-96-00763-6.** LLS §1.2 reports their GRH bound for the least prime in `a mod q`: `P(a,q)≤(1+o(1))(φ(q)log q)^2`, and an explicit bound `≤2(q log q)^2`; LLS Corollary 1.2 sharpens this to `P(a,q)≤(φ(q)log q)^2` for `q>3`. **Status:** published. **Verification note:** the Bach–Sorenson paper’s metadata and its reported bounds were checked via LLS; the Bach–Sorenson primary full text was not accessed in this pass. These bounds concern the trivial subgroup (a fixed arithmetic progression), not divisors of one `N`.
19. **Eric Bach, “Explicit bounds for primality testing and related problems,” Math. Comp. 55 (1990), 355–380.** LLS §1.1 cites Bach’s earlier ERH bounds for the least prime outside a proper subgroup: `<2(log q)^2`, or `<3(log q)^2` when requiring it not to divide `q`; LLS proves its own sharper explicit Theorem 1.1. **Status:** published. **Verification note:** the older paper is cited and its bound summarized in the opened LLS source; Bach’s own paper was not accessed. No independent theorem-level claim beyond that report is made.
20. **Wiesław Wedeniwski, “On the least quadratic non-residue,” (cited in LLS as the source of an improved explicit bound; full bibliographic details not independently verified).** LLS introduction §1.1 states that, for the quadratic-residue subgroup, Wedeniwski gave the explicit GRH bound `(3/2)(log q)^2−(44/5)log q+13`. **Verification status:** citation and statement seen in LLS, but the cited paper itself was not accessed; treated as a lead, not an independently verified result.
Primary excerpts for entries 7–20: `sources/z52/divisor_class/batch_2026-10-30.txt`.
