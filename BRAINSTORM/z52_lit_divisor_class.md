## §1 Verified entries

Access dates below are 2026-10-30. Primary-source excerpts are saved under `sources/z52/divisor_class/`.

1. **H. W. Lenstra, Jr., “Divisors in Residue Classes,” Math. Comp. 42 (165) (1984), 331–340; DOI not listed in consulted source.** Source: author-hosted journal scan (1984), Theorem p. 332. If `0<r<s<n`, `s>n^(1/3)`, and `(r,s)=1`, at most 11 positive divisors of `n` are congruent to `r mod s`, and all can be found in polynomial time. **Status:** published. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/s3_lll_lenstra.md`, `BRAINSTORM/bsN_divisor_box.md`, `BRAINSTORM/s6_divisor_pigeonhole.md`; grep also surfaced `EXSET_SHIFT_DIVISORS.md`. It is an upper bound/algorithm, not existence; `s≈N^(1/4)` is outside the theorem.
2. **Don Coppersmith, Nick Howgrave-Graham, S. V. Nagaraj, “Divisors in Residue Classes, Constructively,” Math. Comp. 77 (2008), 531–545; Cryptology ePrint 2004/339 (2004-12-07).** Primary ePrint abstract: reexamines Lenstra’s problem and improves its divisor-count bound while constructing the divisors; its parameter condition and bound are lost in accessible abstract transcription, so I do not quote them. **Status:** published; source abstract accessed. **Relation:** same repo files as entry 1; no existence guarantee.
3. **Kevin Ford, “Integers with a divisor in (y,2y],” arXiv:math/0607473v5 (2013-03-17); published in *Anatomy of Integers*, CRM Proc. Lect. Notes 46 (2008), 65–80.** Abstract / (1.1): `H(x,y,2y) ≍ x/((log y)^δ(log log y)^(3/2))`, `3≤y≤√x`, where `H` counts integers `n≤x` having a divisor in `(y,2y]` and `δ=1−(1+log log 2)/log 2`. **Status:** published. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/s6_divisor_pigeonhole.md`, `EXSET_SHIFT_DIVISORS.md`; ambient counting only, no pointwise structured-`N` statement.
4. **Marc Munsch, “On the minimal number of small elements generating prime fields,” arXiv:1703.06673v1 (2017-03-20).** Theorem 3.2 bounds the number of elements ≤`p^(1/(4√e)+ε)` needed to generate `F_p*` by `≪ ω_l(p−1)+(ω(p−1)−ω_l(p−1))/log l`; Corollary 3.7 gives `≪(log_3 p)^2` for almost all `p`. **Status:** preprint. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/bsN_divisor_box.md` (group/coset route), `BRAINSTORM/z35_modp_least_residue.md`; pointwise generating-set conclusion, not an arbitrary prescribed residue or a factor of `4Cp+1`.
5. **N. A. Carella, “Upper Bound of the Least Quadratic Nonresidues,” arXiv:2106.00544v9 (2025-10-09).** Theorem 1.1 claims unconditionally `n(p)≪(log p)(log log p)^(1+ε)`. **Status:** claimed proof with a gap. In §7, Lemma 7.2, (7.6) starts with `v<p/x`, where `uv<p`, then extends the sum to `v<p` and applies `1/|sin(πuv/p)|≪p/(uv)`, asserting `uv<p`. That assertion fails in the enlarged range; no bound for the added terms is supplied. The asserted error estimate and theorem do not follow from the displayed argument. **Relation:** `FRONTIER.md` §3/§4 and `BRAINSTORM/z35_modp_least_residue.md` contain the established pointwise QNR context; no verdict change.
6. **Jonathon Hales, “Divisors in Residue Classes Revisited,” arXiv:2410.05030v1 (2024-10-07).** Abstract: generalizes Lenstra’s algorithm to other Euclidean rings and `Z[x]`; integer algorithm referenced under `s^3>N`. **Status:** preprint. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/s3_lll_lenstra.md`, `BRAINSTORM/gon_lll_relation_lattice.md`; algorithmic generalization, not a lower bound or pointwise existence theorem.
7. **Kevin Ford, “The distribution of integers with a divisor in a given interval,” Ann. of Math. 168 (2008), 367–433; arXiv:math.NT/0401223.** Abstract states order-of-magnitude results for `H(x,y,z)`, integers `n≤x` with some divisor in `(y,z]`, for all parameters; also studies the count `H_r` with exactly `r` divisors. For `r=1` it obtains the order in the range `z≤x^(1/2−ε)`; for each `r≥2` it gives the order in a specified broad range. **Status:** published. **Relation:** this refines the earlier Ford entry’s scale/role, but remains an ambient population count, not a prescribed-`N` assertion. Primary excerpt: `sources/z52/divisor_class/batch_2026-10-30.txt`.
8. **Dimitris Koukoulopoulos, “Divisors of shifted primes,” arXiv:0905.0163v3 (2009; published 2010).** Theorem 1.7: for fixed nonzero integer `s` and `B≥2`, sufficiently large `x`, `3≤y+1≤z≤x`, some `d∈(y,z]` coprime to `s`, and `z≥y+y/(log y)^B`, `H(x,y,z;P_s) ≍_{s,B} H(x,y,z)/log x`, where `P_s={p+s:p prime}`. Theorem 1.6 gives a corresponding short-interval lower bound for `x−Δ<p+s≤x`, `x(log x)^(−B)≤Δ≤x`, under `y≤√x` and the same interval-width condition. **Status:** published. **Pointwise/average:** count over a population of shifted primes, not a specified shifted prime or prescribed divisor class. **Relation:** strong B6-style average; does not imply B2.
9. **Paul Pollack, “Bounds for the first several prime character nonresidues,” arXiv:1508.05035v2 (2015).** Theorem 1.1: for each `ε>0` there are `m_0(ε),κ(ε)>0` such that every nontrivial Dirichlet character `χ mod m`, `m>m_0`, has more than `m^κ` prime numbers `ℓ≤m^(1/(4√e)+ε)` with `χ(ℓ)∉{0,1}`. **Status:** preprint. **Pointwise/average:** pointwise in each modulus and character, but the scale is polynomial in `m`, far above logarithmic-square; it neither furnishes B2’s prescribed divisor nor the required GRH forcing bound.
10. **Glyn Harman and Igor E. Shparlinski, “Products of Small Integers in Residue Classes and Additive Properties of Fermat Quotients,” arXiv:1408.4515v2 (2014).** Theorem 1: for every `ε>0` and sufficiently large cube-free `q`, every reduced residue `a mod q` is a product of 14 integers `n_i≤q^(1/(4√e)+ε)`. Theorem 2 allows nine such factors up to a subgroup translate when the subgroup order is `≫q^(1/2)`. **Status:** published in *J. Number Theory* 155 (2015), 1–21. **Pointwise/average:** pointwise coverage of every residue class (with freely chosen small factors), not factors constrained to divide a specified integer. It does not meet B2’s prescribed-divisor condition.
11. **Youness Lamzouri, Xiannan Li, Kannan Soundararajan, “Conditional bounds for the least quadratic non-residue and related problems,” Math. Comp. 84 (2015), 2391–2412; arXiv:1309.3595.** Corollary 1.1: assuming GRH, for every prime `q≥5`, the least quadratic nonresidue modulo `q` is below `(log q)^2`; this least integer is prime, so it supplies a prime `q_NR` with `(q_NR/q)=−1`. **Status:** published. **Pointwise/average:** pointwise conditional result. This directly meets the character-side numerical scale in the forcing criterion `q_NR≤2(log p)^2`, indeed with strict `(log p)^2`, and therefore supports the GRH-forcing equivalence conditional on the campaign’s stated criterion; it does not supply an unconditional B2 divisor-in-class result. The paper also reports Bach’s explicit GRH bound `<2(log q)^2` for a prime outside any proper subgroup, or `<3(log q)^2` when it must not divide the modulus.
12. **Bryce Kerr, Igor E. Shparlinski, Kam Hung Yau, “A refinement of the Burgess bound for character sums,” Michigan Math. J. 69 (2020), 227–240; arXiv:1711.10582v2.** Theorem 2.1: for prime `q`, integer `r≥2`, and `M,N` with `N≤q^(1/2+1/(4r))`, every nontrivial multiplicative character `χ mod q` satisfies `Σ_{M<n≤M+N}χ(n) ≪ N^(1−1/r)q^((r+1)/(4r²))(log q)^(1/(4r))`, absolute implied constant. **Status:** published. **Pointwise/average:** pointwise bound on short sums, improving the logarithmic factor in Burgess; no new least-QNR scale or prescribed-divisor conclusion is claimed.
13. **Elijah Fromm, Leo Goldmakher, “Improving the Burgess bound via Pólya–Vinogradov,” Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 147 (2019), 461–466; arXiv:1706.03002v1.** Theorem A: if the Pólya–Vinogradov estimate improves to `Sχ(t)=o(√q log q)` for all even primitive quadratic `χ mod q`, then for odd primitive quadratic characters `ξ mod p`, `Sξ(t)=o(t)` for all `t≫ε p^ε`. This is a conditional implication between character-sum bounds, not an unconditional pointwise QNR improvement or divisor-in-class result.
14. **Jonathan W. Bober, Leo Goldmakher, “Pólya–Vinogradov and the least quadratic nonresidue,” Math. Ann. 366 (2016), 853–863; arXiv:1311.7556v2.** Theorem 1: if for all primitive even characters of order `g`, `M(χ)≤√q log(q)/f(q)` with eventually nondecreasing `f`, then for odd primitive characters `ξ mod k` of order `g`, `n_ξ≪exp(C_g log(k)/f(k))` (the paper gives the constant explicitly; for `3∤k`, `C_g=π√3/[2(√e−1)]`). **Status:** published. This is a conditional transfer from improved long character sums to least nonresidues, not itself a new unconditional bound.
Primary excerpts for entries 7–14: `sources/z52/divisor_class/batch_2026-10-30.txt`.

## §3 NEW candidates

None. No verified result located gives a lower bound or existence theorem for a divisor of one prescribed integer `N` in an arbitrary prescribed reduced residue class under the B2-size modulus regime. The potentially relevant Carella claim is not usable as written; interval results count a population, while divisor-class algorithms are upper bounds.

## §4 Not accessed / could not verify

No exact statement is asserted here for the remaining named leads (Bernstein / recent Coppersmith bounds; Tenenbaum’s original interval theorems; Graham–Ringrose; Banks–Shparlinski; Xi; Erdős–Ruzsa).

This conditional character fact does not alter the unconditional ES status and is not a divisor-in-class result.
