# Non-ANT literature lane (L7, z52)

## §0 Summary
- **New since 2026-09-28:** verified arXiv:2601.07421v5, a Lean-formalized AI-assisted proof for Erdős #728 (a factorial-divisibility statement), based on carry-rich digit patterns plus a count/union bound. Its author notes a similar Pomerance argument; no novel technique or ES implication claimed.
- **Most transferable features:** (1) Kummer converts divisibility into base-p carry inequalities; (2) simultaneous digit constraints can be achieved by counting bad residue classes and a union bound on a growing interval. Both prove density/infinite-family results, not all-point claims.
- **Campaign verdict changes:** none. This route averages over candidate m in [M,2M] (B6), and its density-sized choice set is unlike ES's fixed-p, ≍ log²p lottery (B2). SAT results remain finite-domain certificates; no compactness route to infinite ES located.

## §1 Verified entries

**Keller’s conjecture in dimension 7 (SAT).** Joshua Brakensiek, Marijn Heule, John Mackey, David Narváez, “The Resolution of Keller’s Conjecture,” arXiv:1910.03740v5 (17 Apr 2023), DOI 10.1007/s10817-022-09623-5, accessed 30 Oct 2026. The abstract’s finite reduction: a counterexample in dimension 7 exists iff one of three finite graphs has a clique of size 2⁷; SAT plus symmetry breaking proves all three formulas unsatisfiable. Consequently every unit-cube tiling of ℝ⁷ has a face-sharing pair, while a counterexample exists in dimension 8. **Status:** published, J. Automated Reasoning 66 (2022), 277–300. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md`; finite graph reduction, unlike B1-forbidden finite residue menus. Excerpt: `sources/z52/nonant/sat-automata-batch.txt`.

**Boolean Pythagorean triples.** Marijn J. H. Heule, Oliver Kullmann, Victor W. Marek, “Solving and Verifying the Boolean Pythagorean Triples Problem via Cube-and-Conquer,” arXiv:1605.00723v1 (3 May 2016), DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-40970-2_15, accessed 30 Oct 2026. The source asks whether ℕ can be 2-coloured without a monochromatic Pythagorean triple and reports a proof of impossibility by cube-and-conquer (look-ahead plus CDCL); the DRAT proof is nearly 200 TB and a 68 GB compressed certificate reconstructs it. **Status:** published, SAT 2016, LNCS 9710, 228–245. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md`; the universal quantifier is compressed by an explicit finite Ramsey bound/formula, a feature not known for ES. Excerpt: `sources/z52/nonant/sat-automata-batch.txt`.

**Erdős discrepancy constant C=2.** Boris Konev, Alexei Lisitsa, “A SAT Attack on the Erdos Discrepancy Conjecture,” arXiv:1402.2184v2 (17 Feb 2014), accessed 30 Oct 2026. Abstract reports a sequence of length 1160 with discrepancy 2 and a proof no sequence of length 1161 has discrepancy 2; the C=2 finite boundary is thereby settled. **Status:** SAT 2014, LNCS 8561, 219–226, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-09284-3_17. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md`; bounded witness-length threshold, not a proof of the infinite conjecture for arbitrary C. Excerpt: `sources/z52/nonant/sat-automata-batch.txt`.

**Empty hexagon number h(6)=30, SAT plus Lean.** Marijn Heule and Manfred Scheucher, “Happy Ending: An Empty Hexagon in Every Set of 30 Points,” Springer chapter (2024), DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-57246-3_5, accessed 30 Oct 2026. Theorem 1: every 30-point planar set in general position contains an empty convex hexagon. The paper uses a compact O(n⁴) propositional encoding, symmetry breaking, 312,418 cubes and 17,300 CPU-hours; a verified cakeLPR checker validates LRAT proofs. Bernardo Subercaseaux et al., “Formal Verification of the Empty Hexagon Number,” arXiv:2403.17370v1 (26 Mar 2024), accessed same date, formalizes the reduction in Lean; CNF unsatisfiability is an axiom in Lean and the generated CNF is checked externally by cakeLPR, so the proof boundary includes the encoding/CNF correspondence. **Status:** ITP 2024, LIPIcs 309, 35:1–35:19, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2024.35. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md`, `BRAINSTORM/z45_program_synthesis.md`; an explicit finite combinatorial encoding and certificate completeness are essential. Excerpt: `sources/z52/nonant/sat-automata-batch.txt`.

**Packing chromatic number of the infinite square grid.** Bernardo Subercaseaux, Marijn J. H. Heule, “The Packing Chromatic Number of the Infinite Square Grid is 15,” arXiv:2301.09757v2 (12 Jun 2025), DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-30823-9_20, accessed 30 Oct 2026. The abstract proves the exact number is 15 using a novel propositional encoding and symmetry breaking; it says the unsatisfiability proof includes both techniques. **Status:** published conference version (DOI above; arXiv v2). **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md`; the local colouring constraints admit finite obstruction/certificate reductions, unlike ES’s unbounded denominator witnesses. Excerpt: `sources/z52/nonant/sat-automata-batch.txt`.

**Ramsey numbers R(3,8), R(3,9): independently verifiable SAT+CAS certificates.** Zhengyu Li, Conor Duggan, Curtis Bright, Vijay Ganesh, “Verified Certificates via SAT and Computer Algebra Systems for the Ramsey R(3,8) and R(3,9) Problems,” arXiv:2502.06055v2 (7 Jul 2025), DOI 10.24963/ijcai.2025/292, accessed 30 Oct 2026. The abstract reports MathCheck certificates for R(3,8)=28 and R(3,9)=36, with a SAT+CAS architecture and cube-and-conquer for the harder case; it describes them as the first independently verifiable certificates, not as the first discovery of the numerical values. **Status:** IJCAI 2025. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md`; finite exhaustive graph search plus independently checkable lower/upper certificates. Excerpt: `sources/z52/nonant/sat-automata-batch.txt`.

**Base-b palindrome sums: constructive proof plus automata variants.** Javier Cilleruelo, Florian Luca, Lewis Baxter, “Every positive integer is a sum of three palindromes,” arXiv:1602.06208 (2016), accessed 30 Oct 2026: every positive integer is a sum of three base-g palindromes for g≥5. Aayush Rajasekaran, Jeffrey Shallit, Tim Smith, “Sums of Palindromes: an Approach via Automata,” arXiv:1706.10206v3 (30 Aug 2017), accessed same date: the proof uses nested-word automata (a restricted pushdown model) in base 2, and ordinary automata with a modified representation in bases 3 and 4; it proves at most four palindromes in base 2 (optimal), completing the all-base problem. **Status:** arXiv sources; Cilleruelo–Luca–Baxter published in *Mathematics of Computation* 87 (2018), 997–1017 (journal metadata not independently opened). **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_divisibility_lgp.md`, `EXPONENT_COVERING.md`; these automata are tailored to the digit-sum predicates and representation choices in the palindrome proof, not a Walnut decision procedure for arbitrary divisibility. ES positivity involves unbounded multiplicative/divisibility witnesses and is not Presburger. Excerpt: `sources/z52/nonant/sat-automata-batch.txt`.

**AI/Lean-assisted Erdős-problem resolution (related divisibility problem, not ES).** Nat Sothanaphan, “Resolution of Erdős Problem #728: a writeup of Aristotle’s Lean proof,” arXiv:2601.07421v5 (26 Jan 2026), accessed 30 Oct 2026. Theorem 1 proves: for any 0 < C₁ < C₂ and 0 < ε < 1/2, infinitely many triples (a,b,n) satisfy εn ≤ a,b ≤ (1−ε)n, a!b! | n!(a+b−n)!, and C₁ log n < a+b−n < C₂ log n. The proof reduces to binomial divisibility, then uses Kummer carries and a scale-by-scale counting/union-bound argument to choose carry-rich but spike-free integers m; the Lean proof is described in §6. **Status:** arXiv preprint, formal proof reported; no journal publication verified here. Its own Appendix “beyond this proof” explicitly notes a similar Pomerance argument and reports close/similar results, so the AI provenance does not establish a novel mathematical method. **Relation:** existing `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md` and `BRAINSTORM/z45_program_synthesis.md`; this is a fresh specific case, but no ES verdict changes. Decisive excerpt: `sources/z52/nonant/erdos728.txt`.

**SAT-certified finite extremal theorem.** Marijn J.H. Heule, “Schur Number Five,” arXiv:1711.08076v1 (submitted 21 Nov 2017), accessed 30 Oct 2026. Abstract states the maximal n for a 5-coloring of {1,…,n} without monochromatic a+b=c is 160; it was encoded in propositional logic, solved by massively parallel SAT, and the two-petabyte proof certified by a formally verified checker. **Status:** published at AAAI 2018 (arXiv page says accepted); finite extremal computation. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md`, `BRAINSTORM/z32_adversarial_search.md`; the finite-domain boundary is exactly why this is not an infinite ES certificate. Excerpt: `sources/z52/nonant/schur5.txt`.

**Pointwise ergodic averages over arithmetic times (not “every orbit”).** Jean Bourgain, “Pointwise ergodic theorems for arithmetic sets,” *Publ. Math. IHÉS* 69 (1989), 5–41, accessed 30 Oct 2026 via EuDML/Numdam. The source is an ergodic theorem for arithmetic averaging sequences in measure-preserving systems; the convergence is a.e. for each fixed system/function under the paper’s sequence-specific hypotheses, not a uniform claim at every point and not a theorem about each prime. **Status:** published. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/z31_duke_equidistribution.md`; no pointwise fixed-p divisor event follows from a.e. convergence (B6). Source: `sources/z52/nonant/dynamics-model-lll.txt`.

**Duke-type theorem for cubic fields (individual field parameter, averaged orbit packets).** Manfred Einsiedler, Elon Lindenstrauss, Philippe Michel, Akshay Venkatesh, “Distribution of periodic torus orbits and Duke’s theorem for cubic fields,” arXiv:0708.1113v2 (3 Feb 2010), DOI 10.4007/annals.2011.173.2.5, *Ann. Math.* 173(2) (2011), 815–885; accessed 30 Oct 2026. The abstract proves uniform distribution of equivalence classes of periodic torus orbits and equidistribution of ideal classes of cubic totally real fields in a modular 5-fold, via subconvexity, measure classification, and local harmonic analysis. **Status:** published. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/z31_duke_equidistribution.md`; “individual discriminant” means one field at a time in a growing family, while each statement still averages the field’s many ideal classes/orbits; ES asks for a single divisor at one fixed p (B2/B6). Source: same excerpt.

**Effective unipotent equidistribution (2022–26).** Elon Lindenstrauss, Amir Mohammadi, Zhiren Wang, “Effective equidistribution for some one parameter unipotent flows,” arXiv:2211.11099v2 (23 Sep 2025), accessed 30 Oct 2026; Annals of Mathematics 204 (2026), article page opened. Abstract: polynomial-error effective equidistribution for unipotent subgroup orbits in arithmetic quotients of SL₂(ℂ) and SL₂(ℝ)×SL₂(ℝ), using Margulis functions, incidence geometry, and spectral gap. **Status:** published in Annals (volume/page metadata beyond the opened journal page not transcribed). **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/z31_duke_equidistribution.md`; pointwise orbit-segment distribution is still a statistic on a growing orbit, not a witness for every prescribed thin arithmetic target (B2/B3). Source: `sources/z52/nonant/dynamics-model-lll.txt`.

**O-minimal lattice counting (a genuine conditional existence route).** Fabrizio Barroero, Martin Widmer, “Counting lattice points and o-minimal structures,” arXiv:1210.5943v2 (27 Apr 2013), accessed 30 Oct 2026. The paper gives sharp lattice-point estimates for fibers of definable families and controls projected volumes. **Status:** published in *IMRN* (2014; exact issue/DOI not verified in this session). This *can* yield existence when a positive volume main term exceeds the boundary/error terms; the counting theorem itself applies to bounded definable fibers, including lower-dimensional ones. It is not a universal lower-bound theorem, and the volume-dominates-error argument additionally requires suitable lattice/error control. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_ominimal.md`; the displayed ES hyperbola-thickening dies at B3 because sufficient thickness averages over neighboring integers. Source: same excerpt.

**Ax–Kochen/Ershov transfer principles (recent scope check).** Margarete Ketelsen, Philip Dittmann, “Composition Ax-Kochen/Ershov principles and tame fields of mixed characteristic,” arXiv:2601.05790v2 (15 Jan 2026), accessed 30 Oct 2026. Abstract: composition AKE holds for tame equal-characteristic coarsenings, but counterexamples occur in mixed characteristic; in general, theory of the valued field need not be determined by underlying field, residue field, and value group. **Status:** preprint. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_ominimal.md`, `BRAINSTORM/logic_divisibility_lgp.md`; AKE transfers first-order valued-field truth, not unbounded integer positivity or a fixed prime’s missing divisor (B2). Source: same excerpt.

**AI-assisted 2025–26 Erdős-problem outcomes (official problem-page records; catalogue is not a proof audit).** In addition to #728, the teorth/erdosproblems AI-contributions wiki (accessed 30 Oct 2026; it states its data stop at 30 Jun 2026) and linked official problem pages report these examples. They are included as research-record entries, not as independently proof-checked Lean developments:

- **#729, factorial/binomial divisibility.** Kevin Barreto and Leeham, with ChatGPT and Aristotle (AI wiki and `erdosproblems.com/729`, opened 30 Oct 2026): affirmative result by modifying the #728 argument. Mechanism: factorial valuations / base-prime carry control, with small primes treated separately. **Status:** website reports solved; closely related to existing human literature (Pomerance), not a new ES tool. Link: https://www.erdosproblems.com/729.
- **#401, related factorial divisibility.** Boris Alexeev, Kevin Barreto, Leeham and Nat Sothanaphan (AI wiki; `erdosproblems.com/401`, opened 30 Oct 2026): the intended “infinitely many” version is proved by essentially the same construction as #729; the alternative “for all large” reading is disproved by a simple n=2m specialization. **Status:** website reports full Lean solution to a variant/quantifier-resolved statement; the original wording is explicitly ambiguous. Link: https://www.erdosproblems.com/401.
- **#125, digit-restricted intersections.** DeepMind prover agent (AI wiki; `erdosproblems.com/125`, opened 30 Oct 2026): the site reports an argument forcing lower density zero (hence a negative answer to the natural positive-lower-density question); a Lean formalization is linked. Mechanism reported on the page: compare upper and lower densities and produce infinitely many scales with a density deficit; not a finite SAT reduction. **Status:** official-site solution claim; full proof not audited here. Link: https://www.erdosproblems.com/125.
- **#205, shifted values with few prime factors.** Kevin Barreto and Leeham using ChatGPT/Aristotle (`erdosproblems.com/205`, opened 30 Oct 2026): negative answer, with infinitely many n for which every value n−2^k (0≤k and 2^k<n) has many prime factors; the site says the construction makes n divisible by a large power of 2. **Status:** official page reports solved (odd counterexamples remain open); technique is a scale/valuation obstruction, not a uniform local identity. Link: https://www.erdosproblems.com/205.
- **#457, least prime not dividing an integer.** GPT-5.2 Pro prompted by Barreto (`erdosproblems.com/457`, opened 30 Oct 2026): affirmative construction for infinitely many n, improving the constant to arbitrarily large values; the page identifies products of primes in intervals as the basic model example and records a stronger elaboration by Tao. **Status:** official page reports construction; method is primorial-style / prescribed-prime-factor construction. Link: https://www.erdosproblems.com/457.
- **#38, essential component of an additive basis.** GPT-5.5 Pro (`erdosproblems.com/38`, opened 30 Oct 2026): positive solution via a sparse random set with the required robust additive property. **Status:** official page reports a solution in comments; probabilistic existence exploits a random-set density model, not pointwise arithmetic at a prescribed prime. Link: https://www.erdosproblems.com/38.
- **#90, unit-distance problem.** OpenAI model / human-verified exposition: Will Sawin, “An explicit lower bound for the unit distance problem,” arXiv:2605.20579v1 (20 May 2026), and Alon et al., “Remarks on the disproof of the unit distance conjecture,” arXiv:2605.20695v1, both accessed 30 Oct 2026. The former proves arbitrarily large planar n-point sets with > n^1.014 unit-distance pairs, using number fields of large degree/small discriminant and Golod–Shafarevich; the latter gives a human-verified exposition of the AI-generated counterexample. **Status:** preprints; the problem site records an OpenAI-generated disproof. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md`; a concrete algebraic construction is a witness, not an open-ended finite certificate. Sources: cited arXiv pages (the source excerpt is recorded in `sources/z52/nonant/sat-automata-batch.txt`).

**Hough’s covering-system theorem: LLL gives a pointwise universal conclusion under finite-system structure.** Bob Hough, “Solution of the minimum modulus problem for covering systems,” arXiv:1307.0874v3 (opened 30 Oct 2026), later *Annals of Mathematics* 181 (2015). The paper’s proof overview says that for any distinct covering system with minimum modulus above an absolute threshold it proves a positive lower bound for the density of integers left uncovered; a staged sieve uses the Lovász Local Lemma and a relative LLL to control dependencies between congruence events. **Status:** published theorem. **Relation:** `BRAINSTORM/prob_lll_fixed_p.md`, `BRAINSTORM/logic_complexity.md`; this is a genuine “every finite system” result because CRT gives product structure and the LLL leaves positive density. ES lacks a fixed finite congruence system whose avoidance contradicts the target; this theorem does not cross B1/B2. Source: `sources/z52/nonant/hough-lll.txt`.

## §3 NEW candidates

**None.** The only recent lead, #728, is an interval-averaged carry argument and does not supply fixed-p forcing. Repurposing it would require a genuinely new lemma: for every sufficiently large hard p, exhibit one bounded-cost candidate whose simultaneous valuation/carry constraints hold, without averaging over p, over m, or over a family large enough to hide failures. The lemma would still have to pass `FRONTIER.md` §4 checklist 1 (odd squares), 4 (tied modulus), 5 (p fixed), and 8 (not a congruence menu); its cheapest test is exact enumeration on the listed odd-square, numerator-8 and hard-prime controls. No such input emerged, so no candidate is proposed.

## §4 Not accessed / could not verify / incorrect leads

- **NOT ACCESSED:** the SAT/Lean landmark papers and #728 were checked from their arXiv abstracts/HTML as recorded in §1; source text/PDF was not independently proof-audited. Publication status of arXiv:2601.07421v5 beyond the preprint was not verified.
- **NOT ACCESSED / PARTIAL:** primary source for Walnut was opened, but exact decision-theorem scope and whether it decides the family in `EXPONENT_COVERING.md` remain unverified here; no transfer claim is made. Pila–Wilkie, alternative spread/sunflower results, Ratner/Margulis–Oppenheim, Bourgain along primes, and Sarnak Möbius disjointness were not newly source-checked. Existing campaign files are cited as prior screening, not represented as fresh literature verification. The entries above provide fresh primary-source checks for Bourgain’s arithmetic ergodic theorem, Duke’s cubic-field theorem, effective unipotent equidistribution, o-minimal lattice counting, and AKE.
- **No evidence found** for a precedent reducing an unrestricted infinite number-theory assertion like ES to a finite SAT instance by ultraproduct/profinite compactness. Finite SAT certificates prove their finite encodings; no such compactness bridge was located.
- Thin exponential families are not established here as an automatic-sequence decision procedure. `EXPONENT_COVERING.md` gives a family-specific finite-covering/certification framework, not a decision algorithm for all numbers with bounded binary digit sum. Walnut's first-order automatic-sequence decision procedure cannot be applied merely because exponents have a digit restriction; a uniform automatic encoding of the relevant divisibility predicates and unbounded exponent parameters would need to be exhibited and verified.
