# z45_program_synthesis — bounded synthesis of growing witness searches

## 0. Summary

**CERTIFIED (original census); EVIDENCE (extrapolation).** Enumerative synthesis over 924 programs finds a simple policy: inspect the divisor lists of `p+4,p+8,p+12,p+16`, then scan the unique-parent edges of the word forest in increasing larger endpoint. It solves all 9,732 primes `p≡1 (24), p≤10⁶`, and the additional fixture 5,101,441, using at most 194 charged candidate operations and `1.118(log p)²`. Every saved fraction and the policy's cost were independently checked. Frozen, it also succeeds on all 73,155 such primes in `(10⁶,10⁷]`, at maximum cost 268. However, the **factor-free parent-scan baseline already solves the entire range through 10⁷**, with a better mean cost and better extrapolation tail. CF-derived policies lose badly. The search discovers a useful benchmark, not a mechanism: the learned prefix is López's known line, and the growing fallback is precisely a moving-divisor event. No ES progress is claimed.

**Verdict — PARTIAL: H-A remains open at the bounded-endpoint escape implication; promise 1/5.**

**Assessment, novelty audit.** Read FRONTIER §§2–7, the complete brainstorm index, LOWER_TAIL_ASTRA, LOWER_TAIL_FABLE with its repairs, greedy_algorithm_dynamics, z7_automatic_families, CONSTRUCTIVE_ES, the routes-board memo, and the relevant CAP_POINTWISE discussion. Repository searches for “FunSearch”, “genetic”, “program synthesis”, and “symbolic search” found no earlier dedicated implementation. The underlying mathematics is emphatically not new: forest parents are LOWER_TAIL_ASTRA Lemma 2.1; five-slot certificates and their sign are the existing five-slice dictionary; bounded-root obstructions and growing-window conjectures already exist. The increment is a reproducible, held-out program-search comparison and its negative selection result. No LLM calls or external theorem claims enter the experiment.

## 1. Precise dictionary and the DSL

### 1.1 Witnesses and a genuinely cheap primitive

**PROVED (existing dictionary, elementary).** A word is a positive triple satisfying

`p = tuv − u − v`, equivalently `(tu−1)(tv−1)=tp+1`.

For prime `p≡1 (4)`, a five-slice certificate is a word for which one of

`S(t,u,v) = {t,u,v,tu−1,tv−1}`

is divisible by 4. This is equivalent to `(uv/p)=−1` on a forest edge; it is equivalent to **ES₅**, not known equivalent to unrestricted ES. References: LOWER_TAIL_ASTRA Lemma 2.2, DEDEKIND_ES Theorem 4.5, CONSTRUCTIVE_ES Proposition 4.1.

**PROVED (explicit identities, also valid with 4 replaced by k).** Put `A=ta−1`, `B=tb−1`, where `(a,b)` is either `(u,v)` or `(v,u)`:

| Divisible slot | d | Certificate denominators for k/p |
|---|---|---|
| t | t/k | `(uvd, pvd, pud)` |
| a | a/k | `(Bd, Btdp, tdp)` |
| A | A/k | `(Bdp, bd, Bbd)` |

*Proof.* The first sum has numerator `p+u+v=tuv` over `puvd`. In the second, use `p+b=aB`; in the third use `AB=tp+1`. These give exactly `k/p`; denominators are positive because a word of positive determinant has `ta,tb>1`. These are sufficient k-certificates; no equivalence with all k-term-numerator solutions is asserted. In particular the five-slot test for 8 is an algebraically valid sufficient test, not an 8-specific sign theorem.

**PROVED (parent primitive, restating the forest dictionary).** For `2≤v≤p+2`, set

`q=ceil(p/v), r=qv−p=(−p mod v)`.

If `r>0` and `gcd(p,v)=1`, the pair `{r,v}` is an edge iff `r | q+1`. Indeed `p+r+v=(q+1)v`, so the edge quotient is `t=(q+1)/r`. This equivalently tests `r | p+v` because `gcd(r,v)=1`. For primes, the only nonunit vertex in the range is `v=p`, where `r=0` and the procedure rejects. Thus the parent test uses **no factoring**, only division/remainders and five divisibility tests. Scanning `v=2,…,H` examines every non-diagonal edge whose larger endpoint is at most H, in exactly H−1 probes. The omitted diagonal word at a prime is not a certificate.

**Assessment.** This is a useful computational compression, not an escape from B2. In root coordinates, the same event is `v | p+u`, `v≡−p (mod u)`, with a sign/slot restriction. In parent coordinates the modulus r is generated by the solution candidate, and `r | q+1` is still uncontrolled at fixed p.

### 1.2 Grammar and accounting

**Definition (implemented DSL).** A program is either an atom, or

`Prefix(atom_a, h); atom_b`, with `a≠b`, `h∈{1,2,4,8,16}`.

There are 14 atoms, hence `14+14·13·5=924` programs. A prefix length counts raw root proposals, including rejected/duplicate proposals. Each atom stops at its first certificate; concatenation restarts the fallback and charges repeated work. No prime-specific lookup table or exceptional-case branch is allowed.

| Atom | Root/candidate stream |
|---|---|
| asc / desc | roots `u=1,2,…`; divisors of p+u in increasing/decreasing order |
| nr / nrdesc | same roots, retaining quadratic nonresidues before factoring |
| four / three | roots `u=4i` / `u=4i−1` |
| cfD | successive complete denominators of the CF of √p |
| cfP / cfQ | successive convergent numerators/denominators, reduced modulo p |
| sqrt | `floor(√p), floor(√p)+1, floor(√p)−1,…` |
| quot | `floor(p/(i+1))` |
| parent / parentnr | larger endpoints `v=2,3,…`, directly test their unique parent; optional QNR filter on v |
| bfsnr | explore actual forest neighbours from successively proposed QNR seeds, advancing seeds when the queue empties |

CF atoms traverse at most the first period and at most the operation cutoff; **their failures are not claims about unrestricted reduction cycles or Pell methods**. Roots outside `[1,p+2]` and duplicates are rejected. `bfsnr` is a growing seed procedure, not the already-refuted single-least-nonresidue component claim. Full self-generated mute-set/cap transport is not implemented: that would require an additional cap-zero theorem, not a new scheduling primitive.

**Definition (budget).** Charge one operation per raw root proposal, and one per divisor inspected, including divisors rejected by the edge predicate. A parent probe costs one, since it produces at most one edge. Budget `B(p)=ceil(8(log p)²)`; logs are natural. Full divisor generation, sorting and integer factorization are **additional computational work**, not secretly claimed polylogarithmic. The grammar is a candidate-count benchmark, not a factoring-free complexity theorem. The parent baseline, separately, really has only elementary bit operations per probe.

**Assessment, subtlety.** A prefix can require a full factorization even when it inspects only two divisors. Reported cost must not be mistaken for elapsed time or number of generated divisors. CONSTRUCTIVE_ES Proposition 4.2's repaired warning about divisor enumeration applies. This makes the successful factor-free baseline more interesting than the learned factoring prefix.

## 2. What program synthesis can and cannot force at one p

**PROVED (soundness).** Every successful program produces a positive rational identity, by §1.1. This is pointwise and needs no distributional input. Candidate generation and verification are a legitimate H-A implementation when their budget grows with p.

**Assessment (no completeness mechanism).** Enumerative synthesis minimizes a finite empirical loss; it does not prove termination with a certificate on unseen inputs. The synthesis engine can discover a ranking invariant or identity, but none was found here. A factorization oracle makes deciding each root easy, not proving that some root wins. CF recurrence, least nonresidues and forest reachability do not supply that last implication.

**PROVED (interpretation of the winning prefix).** At root `u=4d`, for prime `p∤4d`, an edge is equivalent to a divisor `v | p+4d` with `v≡−p (mod 4d)`. Its complementary divisor `w=(p+4d)/v` satisfies `w≡−1 (mod 4d)`, and conversely. Therefore the first four winning roots are exactly the first four López b=1 slices, traversed in complementary-divisor order. Their successes are not a discovered identity family.

**CONDITIONAL (Dickson, existing theorem).** Any fixed initial set of forest roots, even with whole-component exploration, fails infinitely often: LOWER_TAIL_ASTRA Theorem 3.2. The learned finite prefix has no all-prime future by itself. **Assessment:** the growing fallback lies outside that fixed-root hypothesis, but has no new arithmetic control. On the finite census even the fallback never needed endpoints beyond 247 through 10⁷, so those data alone are also consistent with a finite congruence menu.

## 3. Search, verification and actual numbers

### 3.1 Protocol

**CERTIFIED (finite program selection and original census).** The grammar, split and loss were fixed before the run. Training: all `p≡1 (24), p≤200000`, plus all ten named hard fixtures, including 5,101,441: 2,213 inputs. Holdout: the other 7,520 primes through 10⁶. Lexicographic loss: number of budget failures, worst `cost/(log p)²`, mean cost on the named hard set, then overall mean cost. Thus the named hard set gets dedicated priority rather than being diluted among easy primes.

The training winner is `Prefix(four,4); parent`. The JSON encodes prefix *index* 2, meaning length 4, not length 2. No holdout or extrapolation result was used to change this policy. Search CPU time was 21.2 seconds. Each independently reproduced verification/extrapolation run took about 11–12 CPU seconds; the entire experiment used well under two CPU minutes. Every run had a 2,000,000 KiB virtual-memory cap and a 600/900-second timeout. No dense product arrays are used.

**CERTIFIED.** An independent implementation generates divisors by trial divisor-pairs rather than prime-power products, reconstructs the frozen schedule and costs, verifies the census prime set with `sympy.primerange`, and checks all 9,733 saved identities using exact `Fraction` arithmetic. Zero discrepancies.

### 3.4 Controls

**CERTIFIED (complete word scans).** Unique-parent enumeration, independently of the learned cutoff and of Euler's prime-only QNR predicate, finds zero certificates with a slot divisible by 4 at all 99 odd squares `9,…,199²`, and zero certificates with a slot divisible by 8 at `n=11,17,131,241`.

**PROVED relative to the existing sign/T1 theorem.** At unit endpoints of an odd square, the Jacobi endpoint colours are all +1; the negative fibre needed for numerator 4 is absent. The direct divisibility checks also cover nonunit endpoints in the finite controls. For numerator 8 the algebraic identities remain sound, but the numerator-4 equivalence between a negative character and a divisible slot does **not** transfer. Sound checking therefore correctly fails, rather than interpreting a negative character as an 8-certificate.

## 4. All 17 screening lines

Here PASS means “this experiment does not make the forbidden inference”, not “an all-prime proof passes”.

1. **PASS — T1.** The checker has no odd-square hits in the complete controls; the negative endpoint character is the failed step at unit vertices. No scaled/composite descent is claimed.
2. **PASS — 8/131, 8/241 (also 11,17).** Exact adapted slot checks fail. We do not reuse the 4-specific sign equivalence for 8.
3. **PASS — no numerator-uniform success law.** Reconstruction is numerator-uniform; the observed success assertion is restricted to 4. A numerator-uniform totality claim would fail. No universal transport/recombination law is supplied.
4. **FAIL — B2.** A growing parent must satisfy `r | q+1` and the slot/sign condition. Equivalently one divisor must occupy a moving class modulo u. No fixed-p existence theorem controls this.
5. **PASS — fixed p.** Every certificate is checked at its prescribed p. Empirical averages are performance summaries only, never used to certify an unseen p.
6. **PASS as a search benchmark / N.A. to main-term error bounds.** Candidate budget is polylogarithmic; no power main term, divisor-density heuristic or analytic error estimate is asserted. Factoring costs are separately disclosed.

8. **FAIL for the learned prefix; growing fallback not excluded.** Four fixed López slices are B1 data. The allowed parent endpoint grows with log p, so the fixed-menu theorem does not directly kill the full procedure; no factorization-control theorem replaces it. Finite-range success does not demonstrate genuine asymptotic escape.
9. **FAIL at forcing, PASS at checking.** The checker tests the exact class, not parity alone. QNR filtering improves neither existence theory nor empirical mean; it is not a class-forcing argument.
10. **PASS — no pooled large primes.** Each divisor stays with its own p+u. Shared factors are not used to force a cross-shift collision.
11. **N.A. — no signed reflection.** All candidates and reconstructed denominators are positive.

## 5. New-theory sketch: bounded-endpoint escape profiles

**Definition (proposed organizational framework, not claimed novel mathematics).** Define

`H₄(p) = min{ v≥2 : r=(−p mod v)>0, r | ceil(p/v)+1,`
`                     4 divides one of t,r,v,tr−1,tv−1 },`
`             t=(ceil(p/v)+1)/r`,

with infinity if the set is empty. This is the least larger endpoint of a bichromatic word-forest edge, not the smallest root of a mixed component. It is also the exact parent-scan stopping time plus one. A synthesis system should optimize a *proved escape profile* H₄, separating candidate generation, checking, and factorization work; finite empirical loss alone is inadequate.

**CONJECTURE (testable but unproved; ES₅-strength).** There is an absolute C such that `H₄(p)≤C(log p)²` for every prime `p≡1 (24)`. The factor-free census through 10⁷ supports C=2 there, but does not justify that constant globally. This is a new statistic/budget formulation of the old growing-root question, not a new standard hypothesis known to imply ES. Any proof still needs the exact moving-class hit.

**PROVED (connection to greedy windows, elementary).** If a certifying word has both endpoints ≤H, it supplies a denominator `x=(p+e)/4` with `e≤2H`: if `4|t`, take `e=u+v`; if `4|a`, take `e=b`; if `4|ta−1`, take `e=a`. The identities of §1.1 give these denominators directly. Thus the escape conjecture implies a polylogarithmic greedy-window bound. The converse is not established: a small-window solution need not have a five-slice word with both endpoints small. This prevents mislabelling the parent search as a wholly different existence mechanism from the earlier greedy memo.

**PROVED (toy obstruction, elementary).** Fix H≥3 and let `M=lcm(24,1,…,H)`. Every prime `p≡1 (mod M)` has `H₄(p)>H`.

*Proof.* For `2≤v≤H`, `r=v−1`. An edge requires `v−1 | p+v`; since `p≡1 (mod v−1)`, this requires `v−1|2`, so only v=2,3 remain. At v=2, `(t,r,v)=((p+3)/2,1,2)` has no slot divisible by 4 because `p≡1 (24)`. At v=3, the word is `((p+5)/6,2,3)`, again with no such slot. ∎

**PROVED relative to Dirichlet's theorem on primes in reduced arithmetic progressions.** The toy gives infinitely many primes missing every fixed endpoint cutoff H. It is not a disproof of the conjectured growing `C(log p)²` cutoff: the prescribed modulus itself grows approximately exponentially in H, so freezing H and bounding the least prime are different questions.

Calling this a new *theory* would overstate the result; it is a precise target for future synthesis and falsification.

## 6. Verdict and reproducibility

**Assessment — PARTIAL, promise 1/5.** The bounded experiment completes the requested machine search and finds practical small-budget procedures. Its positive part is a finite certificate census; its theoretical residue is exactly H-A/B2. The tested CF/square/quotient scheduling instantiations lose, but neither CF methods in general nor all adaptive witness procedures are ruled out. The main useful lesson is adversarial: the simple factor-free baseline matches coverage and beats the synthesized policy's mean, so there is no evidence that machine search uncovered new arithmetic structure.

**Concrete next steps (Assessment).** First try to falsify the bounded-endpoint conjecture with square-mimicking primes and cost-aware adversarial search; keep the policy frozen. Only then enlarge the DSL to reduction-cycle or cap-box operations, and require a candidate invariant that explains an out-of-sample advantage. Do not spend a larger run merely optimizing the four-slice prefix or relabel the observed finite maximum as an all-prime constant.
