### New arXiv item in the sweep

**Yichuan Cao and Xiaoping Xu, “Prime Numbers of the Form 24m+1 and Erdös-Straus Conjecture,” arXiv:2610.32302v1, submitted 2026-10-23 (13 pp.); accessed 2026-10-30.** The arXiv abstract and full HTML were accessed. The paper gives 56 polynomial families and says the numerical test suggests they imply ES; in §3 it explicitly states the covering claim “We have used AI to test the claim for m≤10¹¹ and it holds. But we failed to get a logic proof.” Thus this is a computationally tested conjectural covering, not an ES proof; the paper itself identifies the missing step. It additionally gives analogous numerical evidence for Howerton’s 14-wild-prime conjecture, §4, and a modulo-24 Goldbach conjecture, §5. Status: unrefereed v1 preprint; no ES proof supported. Source excerpt: `sources/z52/es_direct/2610.32302.txt`. Relation: earlier Xu family work appears in `LITERATURE_2026_UPDATE.md`; `git grep -il '2610.32302' -- '*.md'` found no older record.

**Shisheng Li, “Unit fractions with semiprime denominators: an elementary proof of Erdős Problem #306,” arXiv:2609.32140v1, submitted 2026-09-26; accessed 2026-10-30.** The abstract states that every positive rational a/b with b squarefree is a finite sum of distinct unit fractions, each denominator a product of two distinct primes. Structural proof: construct subgraphs of a complete bipartite graph on prime intervals; count reciprocal sums congruent to a/b mod 1 by a finite Fourier expansion; small integer frequencies give a positive main term, other frequencies are controlled by divisor counting and a no-wrap-around CRT; a small-total-mass bound forces equality. Abstract states the proof is formalized in Lean 4 except a cited Ramanujan inequality and describes substantial AI contribution to construction, experiments, and writing. It is not a proof of ES: neither the number of terms nor the denominator pattern is the three-term 4/p constraint. Status: v1 preprint. Important status discrepancy: the teorth database README still lists #306 open and the rendered #306 page does not discuss this preprint; see §4. Source excerpt: `sources/z52/es_direct/2609.32140.txt`. Relation: #306 appears in the current tagged-unit-fraction inventory. `reviews/lit-audit-C-exceptional-set.md` mentions arXiv:2609.32140 and calls it irrelevant to that audit's ES exceptional-set scope; this lane changes that narrow relevance verdict because the paper proves a broad Egyptian-fraction theorem with a possible (not established) Fourier/CRT transfer. It does not change ES status.

The exact #242 statement in DeepMind’s `FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/242.lean` is: “For every $n>2$ there exist distinct integers $1 ≤ x < y < z$ such that $4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z$.” The theorem binds `n : ℕ`, assumes `2 < n`, existentially quantifies `x y z : ℕ`, states `1 ≤ x ∧ x < y ∧ y < z`, equates rational coercions, and ends `by sorry`. **Computational verification sources.** Serge E. Salez, “The Erdős-Straus conjecture: New modular equations and checking up to N=10^17,” arXiv:1406.6307v1 (2014-06-24), pp. 1, 9–12: proves seven modular equations form a complete set for prime polynomials/derives seven integer reference equations, builds residue filters S_m, combines them by CRT and searches the residual prime candidates. Its table gives mean gap 6,056 after the first seven prime filters (G_7=892,371,480; 147,348 residues); it uses an optimized list of odd moduli <5,000 and reports checking 16,512,783,482,880 integers including 51,732,427 squares. Abstract reports C++ runtimes from minutes at 10^14 to ~16 hours at 10^17 on its contemporary typical computer. It gives no list of hardest individual primes, denominator sizes, or witness-size distribution.

Spiridon Mihnea and Bogdan C. Dumitru, “Further verification and empirical evidence for the Erdős-Straus conjecture,” arXiv:2509.00128v1 (2025-08-29), §§2–3: extend filters with S_29 to 2,101,514 residues mod 25,878,772,920; process batches with 140,000 prime filters, C++/GMP, in parallel, about two weeks on a medium setup, proving verification through 10^18. ## §3 New candidates

1. **Candidate (Assessment): reciprocal-preserving switches + a three-term constraint.** The non-obvious input in the GPT-5.5 Pro #283 proof is the combination of the Roth–Szekeres–Graham complete-sequence theorem, switches preserving a chosen reciprocal sum, and finite congruence correction. A concrete first lemma would ask whether, for every hard prime p, one can start from a complete polynomial-value reciprocal representation and perform switches to reach exactly three distinct denominators with reciprocal sum 4/p. This is highly speculative: the source theorem allows an unrestricted number of terms and targets arbitrary large polynomial sums, so neither feature controls the exact three-term cardinality. Must pass checklist lines for fixed-numerator adequacy and odd squares, plus B2’s one-prime divisor-residue lower bound. Cheapest test: derive the switch invariant and search small p for a proof-preserving reduction to 3 terms; check controls 17, 241, 131 and odd squares before scaling.
2. **Candidate (Assessment, weak): semiprime bipartite graph/Fourier setup.** Li’s #306 proof suggests first testing whether any complete bipartite denominator family with each edge encoding a semiprime can have reciprocal edge weights summing to 4/p using exactly three edges, uniformly in hard p. The crucial input would be an explicit positive main term that dominates all nonmajor Fourier frequencies using only Chebyshev bounds and CRT no-wrap. This must pass B2 and B1; the fixed-prime expected count is only polylogarithmic, so the #306 abundant-subgraph mechanism may fail at the outset. Cheapest test: formulate and calculate the three-edge Fourier count for each hard-prime residue class, beginning with 193/409 and odd-square controls. Neither proposal changes the campaign verdict; both are generators, not results.
