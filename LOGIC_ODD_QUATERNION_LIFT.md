Let `D` be a countable Bézout **domain**, call a modulus odd if `(2,m)=1`, and assume `−1` is a sum of two squares in each `R_m=D/mD` for odd nonzero `m`. Ignore unit moduli. In `H_R=(−1,−1)_R` reduced norm is `Q₄=Σx_i²`. If `a²+b²=−1` in `R`, send `i` to `I=[[0,−1],[1,0]]`, `j` to `J=[[a,b],[b,−a]]`. Then `I²=J²=−1`, `IJ=−JI`. Since 2 is invertible, the commuting and anticommuting subspaces for `I` are complementary free planes; the first has basis `1,I`, and right multiplication by the unit `J` identifies it with the second. This is an algebra isomorphism to `M₂(R)`, identifying norm with determinant. The choices need not be compatible.

**PROVED: arbitrary unit-norm solutions lift.** For any `m|M` as above, any `s∈R_M^×` and any `w∈H_{R_m}` with norm `s mod m`, there is a lift of norm `s` modulo `M`. Indeed `SL₂(D)→SL₂(R_m)` is onto. For the first row lift `a,b` to `D`, write `(a,b)=g(a',b')` with `(a',b')` unimodular; `(g,m)=1`. A determinant-one coordinate change sends the lifted row to `(g,0)`; replace this by `(g,m)`, now unimodular, and undo the change. A zero lifted row would have required the trivial modulus. Complete the new row to determinant 1 over `D`. Modulo `m` its second row and the desired one differ by a multiple of the first, as seen in the basis of that invertible matrix. Lift and apply the row correction. To lift `w`, use one splitting at **`M`** and its reduction, remove `diag(s,1)` from the target matrix, lift the resulting determinant-one matrix, then restore `diag(s,1)`. Countability permits a cofinal chain of moduli by taking products, so in the odd inverse limit every unit, in particular `−1`, has four-square norm. The argument does not assume elementary generation of `SL₂`.

**Consequences and obstruction.** A compatible four-vector `u` of norm `−1` represents *every* `s` by **five** squares, via `((s+1)/2, ((s−1)/2)u)`. It does not in general split the quaternion or provide a nonzero isotropic four-vector. For the latter logical gap take the field `K=ℚ₂(T)`: `−1` is a sum of four squares (already in `ℤ₂` by ordinary Lagrange and compactness), but `T` is not. The four-square form over the `T`-adic residue `ℚ₂` is anisotropic by mod 8, so any nonzero norm has even `T`-valuation. This is a counterexample to the **unit-norm shortcut**, not a counterexample with all the quotient-splitting hypotheses. For the actual five-square iteration, including at 2, see `LOGIC_FIVE_SQUARE_BCM.md`; it avoids this inverse-limit issue.

**Sufficient extra condition for all four-square values, not requiring compatible splittings.** Suppose `Pic(R_m)[2]=0` for a cofinal chain of odd moduli. Inductively lift not the full isomorphism, but a rank-one idempotent `e` obtained as the image of `E₁₁` under some splitting downstairs. Work in a chosen splitting upstairs and its reduction downstairs. The image and kernel of this idempotent acting on `R_m²` are isomorphic invertible modules `L,L'` (matrix units from the *previous* splitting identify them), and taking determinants shows `L⊗L≅R_m`. Hence both are free. In the chosen downstairs coordinates, `e=gE₁₁g⁻¹`; take generators of the two lines as columns and scale one by the inverse determinant to get `g∈SL₂(R_m)`. Lift `g` by the row lemma and conjugate the *upstairs* `E₁₁`. The result is again realized by matrix units and specializes to `e`. In the inverse limit the compatible quaternion `e` has trace 1, norm 0 and square `e`. Then `e+s(1−e)` has norm `s` for **arbitrary** `s`, as can be checked at every level on the diagonal. This circumvents lifting determinant units of a general `GL₂` matrix and needs no Smith form for arbitrary primitive matrices. It applies, for example, to an external PID, whose nonzero principal quotients are finite products of local rings.

Without the extra condition, simply asserting that any two splittings differ by conjugation by a **matrix over the base ring** is not justified: matrix units trivialize the two lines relative to *each other*, not relative to the ring. For general rings primitive rank-one idempotents need not have a free image. Nothing here proves the necessary Picard vanishing for principal quotients of arbitrary Bézout domains, or disproves four-square surjectivity under the original odd-modulus hypotheses. Also odd-modulus completion need not see the dyadic part of a nonstandard modulus: after adjoining a common multiple an element can be divisible by every standard power of 2. One must not decompose it by a largest standard 2-exponent without proof.
