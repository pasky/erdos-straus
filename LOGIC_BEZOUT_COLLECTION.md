**End-extension theorem.** Every countable discretely ordered Bézout domain whose nonnegative cone satisfies open induction has a proper countable ordered extension of the same kind, end-extending its nonnegative cone. Consequently adjoining full first-order collection to `IOpen + Bézout` is conservative for universal closures of bounded formulas in the arithmetic language.

**ES application.** There is a model of `IOpen + Bézout + Coll` with an infinite prime `p ≡ 1` modulo every positive standard numeral and with no positive solution of `4xyz=p(xy+yz+zx)`. In particular the theory does not prove ES for all primes beyond any standard cutoff, even restricted to `1 mod 840`. This neither decides standard ES nor gives a result for PA, IE₁ or IΔ₀. The end-extension proof and the audit of the initial ES model are separate below. Neither uses a norm-adjunction step or any unproved assertion about primes in ℕ.

Here `Bézout` means that every two-generated ideal in the signed ring is principal, with its generator a linear combination of the inputs. Statements on the cone may express signed coefficients by differences. `Coll` is **bounding, not coded choice**: for each first-order formula φ of arbitrary complexity, with all parameters z allowed,

    ∀a,z [(∀x<a ∃y φ(x,y,z)) → ∃b ∀x<a ∃y<b φ(x,y,z)].

Finite input/witness tuples work as well. We do not add bounded induction by definition. In particular, the theorem `IE₁ + Coll ⊢ PA` (Enayat–Łełyk–Visser, [Theorem B.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14758)) has a different induction hypothesis.

## 1. Relative order and residue choices

Fix the countable input ring R₀, put F=Frac(R₀), K=rc(F) with its given order, and fix compatible embeddings of ordered real closures. By Shepherdson's integer-part criterion, R₀ floors every element of K. In an ordered extension E of F use the convex valuation ring O={x: |x|≤c for some positive c in F}. Its residue kills precisely the x with |x| smaller than **every** positive element of F. Write k for the ordered residue field, v for the valuation (larger v means smaller magnitude). Its restriction to K is trivial: F is cofinal in K by the polynomial root bound, and coinitial on positive elements by inversion.

**A fixed ordered host.** Use the ordered-field **Cauchy** completion L of K for the uniformity |x−y|<ε, ε∈K positive; the Dedekind completion need not be a field. Here K is countable, so a decreasing sequence of positive radii coinitial at zero suffices. Equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences give the ordered field L: a non-null Cauchy sequence is eventually bounded away from zero, of constant sign, and invertible. K is dense there. L is uncountable: recursively nest two separated closed K-intervals inside each one, with diameters tending to zero along the coinitial radii. Each infinite binary branch is Cauchy and distinct branches have distinct limits (start, e.g., in (1,2)).

Set H=rc(L). K is order-dense in H. Indeed K is cofinal and positive-coinitial there (density in L and the root bound over L). For α∈H algebraic over L with monic polynomial f of degree d, approximate the coefficients by K to obtain monic g with |g(α)|<ε^d, for any ε>0 in K: first bound |α| by an element of K to control the coefficient errors. Factor g over K[i]. One root is within ε of α in complex absolute value, so its real part belongs to K and approximates α within ε. This proves density also in arbitrarily small intervals of H. In particular F remains cofinal in H. The point is **not** that every countable ordered field embeds over K in H; we construct a compatible embedding of each residue field encountered.

The following devices are used with a countable real closed old ambient field E, having an ordered embedding k→H extending the residue copy of K.

* **Fresh relative residue.** Choose ξ∈(1,2) in H transcendental over the image of k (countable). Give E(t) the Gauss valuation, v(t)=0. Explicitly, divide a polynomial by an old coefficient of minimal valuation, reduce its coefficients and evaluate at ξ. This is nonzero for a nonzero polynomial. Its sign times the sign of the chosen coefficient specifies the order; this is independent of the choice and respects products and positive sums by reduction. The valuation of the polynomial is precisely the minimum coefficient valuation. Ratios give residue field k(ξ) inside H. In particular a bounded polynomial in t has all coefficients bounded. Transcendence over ℚ alone would not suffice.
* **Intermediate scale.** Adjoin T>c for all c∈F, with T^j<d for every positive old d outside O and every positive standard j. Any finite constraints are realized already by a sufficiently large element of F; take their ordered-field type. The comparison is strict even up to any positive F-factor (apply the bound also to d/c). Among finitely many nonzero monomials c_i T^i with distinct integer i, one dominates all others by a factor exceeding F: compare the old coefficient ratios, separating relative units, relative infinitesimals and their reciprocals. In particular T is transcendental. A quotient of two Laurent polynomials of relative valuation zero must have dominant terms with the same exponent and coefficients of equal valuation. Its residue is their old residue ratio. No residue increase occurs.
* **Largest scale.** A variable S larger than every element of E is ordered at +∞. For E(S), degree and then leading coefficients give the dominant terms. Residues of relative units are old, since such a ratio must have equal degrees and its leading coefficient ratio is a relative unit. Any positive-degree polynomial in S is larger in absolute value than every old constant.

For clarity concerning real closure in these devices: on an ordered algebraic extension take the convex hull of F; it restricts to the original convex hull. In the Gauss order the stated valuation ring is indeed that hull: units have residues bounded by F in H, while positive elements of negative valuation dominate F. On algebraic extension the residue extension is algebraic (for a bounded algebraic element normalize an old polynomial relation by a coefficient of minimal valuation and reduce; the reduction is nonzero). The residue of a real closed field by a convex valuation is real closed: lift positive square roots, and lift a monic odd-degree polynomial; an ordered real root is bounded by the coefficient root bound. Thus on real closure the residue embeds over the previous residue in H by uniqueness of ordered real closure. In the two scale devices the old k was already real closed, so does not grow. If a stage field is only a subfield of this constructed ambient field, use its relative ordered real closure there and restrict the embedding. Start with F[h], h above F, evaluated at h=0; rc(F(h)) is also the real closure of K(h). Its residue is K by the largest-scale device over K.

## 2. The algebraic stages

At a finite stage A is a smooth geometrically integral affine F-algebra of finite type, a UFD, with a retraction e:A→F and an ordering of its fraction field extending F. Require A∩O=F. Initially F[h] has these properties. E=rc(Frac(A)) is the old ambient field. The retractions and orders always extend their old restrictions. We use two kinds of tasks.

**Approximate an algebraic element α∈E.** If α−a∈O for some a∈A, do nothing. Otherwise adjoin Y=α+t using fresh residue over E. This is a polynomial variable. If a polynomial ∑a_iY^i of degree d>0 were bounded, all its Taylor coefficients at α would be bounded by the Gauss test on t. The top two are a_d and d a_d α+a_{d−1}, whence a_d∈F× and α is O-close to −a_{d−1}/(d a_d)∈A, a contradiction. Thus A[Y]∩O=F. Set e(Y)=0. Now α−Y∈O. Polynomial extension preserves all the algebraic stage invariants.

**Principalize a pair.** Zero entries need nothing. Divide a nonzero pair by its gcd **in the UFD at the time of service**. Write a,b for the coprime cofactors; skip if they already generate 1. If not both vanish at e, go directly to the row step below. Otherwise write m=ker(e) and use an intermediate scale T to form

    B=A[T,m/T] ⊂ A[T,T⁻¹].

This is finite type. Its coefficient at T^(−j), j>0, is in m^j. A nonzero such coefficient lies outside O, so remains outside O after division by T^j. For positive exponents even a nonzero coefficient from F gives a term outside O; all other nonzero coefficients of A are larger in magnitude than F. The dominance test therefore gives B∩O=F. We have B[1/T]=A[T,T⁻¹] and B/TB=gr_m A: the piece represented by a/T^j is m^j/m^(j+1). These pieces agree with the corresponding local ones at m, and the associated graded of the smooth local ring at the F-point is polynomial over F. Thus T is prime; Nagata's localization criterion gives factoriality. B is flat over F[T] (torsion-free over a PID), of finite presentation, and geometrically smooth on all fibers (copies of Spec A off zero; affine tangent space at zero). Hence B is smooth over F. It stays integral after extension of constants by its inclusion in the geometrically integral Laurent ring.

Let j=min(ord_m a,ord_m b); orders of nonzero elements are finite by Krull intersection in the local ring. Membership in m^j can be read locally since A/m^j is local. Then u=a/T^j and v=b/T^j are coprime in B: away from T they remain coprime, and at least one transform has nonzero reduction modulo T. Pick an F-rational tangent vector where that nonzero initial form is nonzero (F is infinite). It gives a retraction of B extending e with u,v not both zero there. If the deformation was skipped, take B=A and u=a,v=b instead.

**Row step.** If necessary set D=B[U,V]/(uU+vV−1). Embed it by U=S, V=(1−uS)/v for a largest-scale S. Indeed over Frac(B) the kernel is the linear relation and Gauss's lemma contracts it to the stated primitive ideal over the UFD. A relatively bounded element of D must be constant as a polynomial in S. Such a constant lies in B[1/u]∩B[1/v]=B, by the two polynomial charts and coprimality; it thus belongs to F. The two charts exhibit an affine-line bundle over Spec B minus the common zero locus. It is smooth and geometrically integral. Each prime factor f of u remains prime: modulo f the presentation becomes (B/f)[1/v][U], nonzero by coprimality. Localizing at u gives a polynomial UFD over B[1/u], so Nagata gives factoriality of D. Solve the row at the F-landing (not both coefficients zero) to extend e. The original ideal is now generated by the old gcd times T^j if a deformation was used, and by the old gcd otherwise. This equality of ideals persists even if its generator is reducible later. No UFD assertion at the infinite union is needed.

## 3. End extension, floors and collection

Dovetail all pairs from each stage and all targets from the ordered real closure of each stage field. Countability allows each request to be processed after finitely many later requests (enumerate by diagonals of stage number and within-stage index). Compatible ordered real closures have real-closed union algebraic over the union fraction field, hence exhaust its ordered real closure. Let A* be the union algebra and let R'=e⁻¹(R₀) in A*. Then A* is Bézout by the pair tasks. Frac(R')=Frac(A*): every a decomposes as (a−e(a))+e(a) with the first summand in R' and the second in F=Frac(R₀).

**Floors.** For every real-closure target α we obtained a∈A* with α−a∈O. Set r=a−e(a)∈R'. Use the compatible residue embedding to approximate the residue of α−r by a k₀∈K within 1/4 in H. Upstairs |α−r−k₀|<1/2 (the strict residue inequality gives this, including when the difference has zero residue). Let b be the R₀-floor of k₀. Exactly one of r+b−1,r+b,r+b+1 floors α: compare with the adjacent unit intervals. This bounded-to-unit approximation is essential when an F-bound can itself be infinite. Also R'∩O=R₀, since A*∩O=F and e fixes F. Thus R' is discrete and its nonnegative cone end-extends that of R₀. It is proper because h is in R' and exceeds F. Shepherdson gives IOpen.

**Bézout descent.** For f,g∈R' not both zero, take h₁ generating (f,g) in A*, f=h₁f₁, g=h₁g₁ and Pf₁+Qg₁=1. The two evaluations α₁=e(f₁), β₁=e(g₁) are not both zero. The fractional R₀-ideal they generate is principal: clear a common denominator in R₀ and use Bézout there. Write its generator γ=iα₁+jβ₁≠0 with i,j∈R₀. Then γh₁, f₁/γ, g₁/γ∈R' by evaluation. For δ=γ−if₁−jg₁, e(δ)=0, and

    (i+δP)f+(j+δQ)g=γh₁

is an identity over R'. This gives the claimed gcd, including the case e(h₁)=0. R' is countable, completing the end-extension theorem.

**Iteration.** Starting at any countable model, repeat at each successor below ω₁. Unions at limits preserve discreteness, endness and ideal-generator witnesses. They preserve IOpen by compatible real closures and old floors. Every stage below ω₁ is countable. In the final union M, a bounded input interval is contained elementwise in a countable stage, as are finite products of such intervals. If the collection antecedent holds **in M**, select witnesses there externally. Their countably many stages have countable supremum, and a later successor has an element bounding them all. The formulas need not be absolute between stages; the parameters are arbitrary in M. No internal sequence code is produced. Bounded arithmetic formulas *are* absolute for these end extensions by induction on formulas. If a universal closure of a bounded formula fails in a model of the base theory, first take a countable elementary submodel containing the counterexample, then perform the iteration. The same parameters still witness failure. This proves the stated conservativity, not elementarity of the constructed extensions.

## 4. Independent audit of the initial ES model

Here is the extra argument needed to start at an actual ES counterexample, reconstructed from the stage proof in `LOGIC_BEZOUT.md` (logic-lift commit `1c6110c19`). The general end-extension theorem alone does **not** supply this starting point. Use §§1–3 with R₀=ℤ, F=ℚ, h positive infinite, p=1+h. Maintain two more finite-stage requirements: p remains a prime element, and the ordered generic point and e lie in the same semialgebraic connected component of the smooth real open set {p>0}. Components may be taken over the real algebraic numbers and interpreted in any real closed extension; semialgebraic connected components commute with that interpretation (finite semialgebraic triangulation). Ordinary topological connectedness in a nonarchimedean field would be the wrong condition. Initially the open set is the interval h>−1.

* **Prime test at each operation.** p stays prime in a polynomial extension. At the deformation, p−1∈m makes T invertible modulo p, since T((1−p)/T)=1−p. Hence B/pB=(A/pA)[T,T⁻¹] via localization, a nonzero domain. At the row, reduce modulo p: the charts are polynomial rings over localizations of B/pB. At least one survives since p cannot divide both u and v; if both survive, their intersection is nonempty dense in each. Their union is reduced and integral, so D/pD is a nonzero domain. Thus p is prime and not a unit at every stage and in the union. With e(p)=1, division by p in A* preserves membership in e⁻¹(ℤ), so p is prime there as well.
* **Component test.** At a floor adjunction the variety is a product with a line. Over T>0 the deformation above the old real component W is W×(0,∞). Its closure *inside {p>0}* contains the chosen tangent vector: at the smooth point e choose a real smooth curve c(s) through e with derivative that vector, staying in the open W near e. On the positive slice T=s the coordinates y/T for generators y of m tend to dy_e(c′(0)), exactly their values at the tangent landing. This uses local real smooth charts only at the real algebraic landing; transfer the component conclusion, not a transcendental curve, to the ordered real closure. At a row the common zero locus has algebraic codimension ≥2 by coprimality in the UFD, unchanged as a dimension bound by real scalar extension. Its real dimension is therefore at most dim W−2. Deleting this closed subset does not disconnect the open smooth manifold W: stratify it into finitely many smooth strata and perturb a path with endpoints fixed transversely to the strata; dimensions force an empty intersection. Equivalently use semialgebraic triangulation. In dimension <2 there is nothing to delete. The real row bundle on the complement is trivial with section (u,v)/(u²+v²) and direction (v,−u). Hence the chosen rational point upstairs and the ordered point have the required component. Neither a single fixed analytic arc nor elementarity of ring stages is assumed.
* **Transfer test for the actual equation.** A hypothetical positive triple x,y,z in e⁻¹(ℤ) occurs at a common finite stage. Its identity holds on the whole variety. On {p>0} the locus where all three are positive is open and closed: there each denominator is ≥p/4 by the sum of positive reciprocals, so at any boundary point with p>0 all three are still positive. Positivity at the ordered generic point therefore forces positivity at e. The resulting positive integers give `4=1/e(x)+1/e(y)+1/e(z)≤3`, impossible. Also (p−1)/m has integral evaluation for every positive numeral m, proving the asserted residues.

Finally, in **any** discretely ordered ring a positive divisor of p is ≤p. For an ES solution sorted x≤y≤z we have 4x≤3p, and `(4x−p)/(px)=1/y+1/z≤2/y` gives y≤2px by discreteness. Also `z(4xy−px−py)=pxy` implies z≤pxy≤2p⁴. Thus both a factorization refuting primality and a triple refuting ES-failure would already occur in the original cone under every end extension. Start §3's ω₁-iteration at this countable prime countermodel to obtain the ES application. No claim is made here about other unit-square classes, internally coded choice, or simultaneous norm axioms.
