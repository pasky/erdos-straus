# The row tower at a mark divisible by infinitely many primes

**Verdict on §§3–4 of `/tmp/z73-nonsquare-adelic-audit.md`.** The field cannot embed in the finite adeles with the specified image of `P`. It does not have to. The embeddings into the *individual* local fields, with their joint restriction to the stage algebra taking finite-adele values, suffice. The Bézout, Dirichlet and marked-section arguments survive this distinction. Below is a construction extending the evaluations one stage at a time, without changing earlier local choices. It also identifies exactly which field elements have finite-adele values in this construction.

**Input, not proved here:** a unit profile π in the uncovered set of ET Prop. 1.9, each π_q transcendental over ℚ, with π_q≡1 mod q almost everywhere. For the advertised congruence and NR conclusion also assume π≡1 mod 840 and π_l nonsquare mod one standard prime l≡3 mod 4. This audits the model half; it is not an independent verification of the counting or mass estimate producing π.

## What can take finite-adele values?

Write `H=P−1` and `𝔸_f=∏′(ℚ_q,ℤ_q)`. A tuple with nonzero coordinates is a unit of `𝔸_f` iff its valuations are zero almost everywhere. Thus H has no inverse in `𝔸_f` at the proposed marks. In particular even `ℚ(P)` does **not** embed there sending P to π. The maps we use are

```
φ_q : F=Frac(A) ↪ ℚ_q       (every q),
φ   : A ↪ 𝔸_f               (same coordinates on A),
R   = φ⁻¹(∏ ℤ_q) ⊂ A.
```

Here and below “integral almost everywhere on a finite stage” means **for each fixed element**. For any finite generating set there is one finite exceptional set for those generators, hence for their ℤ-algebra. There is no such uniform exceptional set for their ℚ-algebra: it contains `1/q` for every q.

Use the tower of `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §1 (no deformation to the normal cone). Start at `B=ℚ[P]`; the steps are a free polynomial variable or `E=B[U,V]/(uU+vV−1)` for nonzero coprime u,v in the current factorial B. Skip zero or already unimodular rows after division by the gcd. Schedule all pairs, real-closure approximation tasks, and primitive Dirichlet tasks as in `LOGIC_PID_DIRICHLET_AUDIT.md`. A Dirichlet task adjoins a *fresh polynomial variable* Y, ordered above the old field, with output `Q=a+mY`, `gcd_B(a,m)=1`, `m>0`. No output is inverted. These are constructions of the ordered rings, independent of how they will be evaluated in ℚ_q.

For clarity, modulo any old prime s, at least one row entry remains nonzero. Over the domain `B/sB` the row is covered by its two polynomial line charts; omit an empty chart, and if both are nonempty they have dense common open. The quotient is a nonzero domain, and the base injects in it (localize at a surviving entry). Thus old primes stay nonunit prime. The same chart argument works before reduction; localizing at u gives a factorial ring, and its inverted prime factors in B remain prime in E, giving factoriality by Nagata. Units of E are old units: each is constant in the polynomial ring over `Frac(B)` and `E∩Frac(B)=B` by intersecting the localizations at u and v. In particular H stays prime. Its fiber at each step is a line over the nonempty open where the entries do not both vanish, or a product line. Inductively it is geometrically integral and rational over ℚ. A fixed row pivot not divisible by H gives a dense chart on that fiber; it need not give a chart at *all* integral local points.

## Stage-by-stage local choices

Maintain the following invariant on each stage B, writing `v_q(g)` for valuation after evaluation (`v_q(0)=∞`):

1. There is an embedding `Frac(B) ↪ ℚ_q` extending the previous one at each q. For each `g∈B`, `v_q(g)≥0` almost everywhere.
2. For each `g∈B` with `g∉HB`, `v_q(g)=0` almost everywhere.

For the base this follows by reduction to `g(1)∈ℚ`: if it is nonzero then it is a unit away from finitely many primes, and `π_q≡1 mod q` almost everywhere. Transcendence of each π_q gives injectivity. The stronger second clause reserves enough choices for *future* rows. Simply choosing the previous free coordinates arbitrarily integral is not sufficient.

**One-variable avoidance.** Enumerate the polynomials `L∈B[T]` having some coefficient outside `HB`. For each initial segment of length n, all relevant coefficients are integral at almost every q, and each listed polynomial has at least one unit coefficient there, by the invariant. For all sufficiently large q the total degree of this list plus one is <q. Choose increasing cutoffs tending to infinity so these statements hold above the nth cutoff for the nth list. At each good q handle the longest list its cutoff permits; at small q the list may be empty. There is an allowed residue for T outside the roots of all polynomials on the list **even if one additional residue is forbidden**. Lift it to an element of ℤ_q transcendental over the old embedded field: that field is countable, and the residue cylinder uncountable. Consequently every fixed listed polynomial has unit value almost everywhere. This does not require avoiding infinitely many roots at one finite residue field.

* **Polynomial step.** Choose the new variable as T. Every polynomial in it over B is then integral almost everywhere. Reduction modulo H is coefficientwise, so the second clause follows from avoidance. For a Dirichlet step choose `Y∈ℤ_q` **everywhere**. If a,m are integral there with at least one a unit, require `a+mY` a unit. For m a unit this forbids precisely `−a/m` modulo q; otherwise a is a unit and nothing is forbidden. At small primes impose this rule even with an empty avoidance list. At q=2 there is still a choice. Without the local hypothesis do not require the output to be a unit. This extends the embedding and the two invariants.
* **Row step.** Interchange slots so `u∉HB` and use `T=V` on the chart `U=(1−vT)/u`. Away from a **finite** set of primes u is a unit and v integral. Choose T there by the avoidance rule: both U,V are integral. Every `g∈E` is represented on the chart by `G(T)/u^k`, `G∈B[T]`. If `g∉HE` then `G mod H` is nonzero: `E/HE` is a domain and the surviving u can be inverted injectively there. Hence the second invariant holds for E; the first follows from integrality of old coefficients and of U,V almost everywhere.
* **Exceptional row primes, with no change of old evaluations.** At a prime where the chart argument did not apply, let `c` be an entry of *least* valuation and b the other entry. If `v_q(c)≤0`, write the new row as `cW+bT=1`, choose `T=t∈ℤ_q` transcendental over the embedded old field, and set `W=1/c−(b/c)t`. Both are integral since `1/c,b/c∈ℤ_q`. This gives an integral lift whenever one exists, even for nonintegral inputs. If `v_q(c)>0`, no two integral coefficients can give 1; choose a transcendental integral free coordinate and solve in ℚ_q, allowing a denominator. Changing chart at this prime still extends the field embedding: either free coordinate generates the same rational function field over the base. This finite exception for **this** row causes no problem for the next stage. There is no requirement that a particular place become good for all later rows.

Iterating gives compatible embeddings on the union. More precisely for every fixed nonzero `x∈Frac(B)` write `x=H^k g/h` with `g,h∈B` not divisible by H (factoriality). Then

```
v_q(x) = k v_q(π_q−1)    at almost every q.
```

The order k along H agrees in every later stage by injection on the H-fibers. Thus the inverse image of `𝔸_f` in F under the product map is exactly the valuation ring `ord_H≥0` (equivalently `A_(H)`). In particular it contains A and excludes `H⁻¹`. This stronger identity is for the construction just given; the descent only needs the first invariant.

The finite-prefix argument in the target note is another way to get that first invariant. On the rational H-fiber the chosen pivots are generically nonzero. A primitive Dirichlet task has `a+mY` not identically zero there since a,m are not both multiples of H and Y is free. Later projections are dominant on that fiber. Therefore a *finite* prefix has a rational chart point avoiding its denominators and its finitely many output zeros; clear its finitely many bad rational primes. Prescribing its residues with P replaced by π_q lifts the chart at the remaining primes. Different prefix points need not agree because each q handles only one prefix. It would be wrong to fix one rational point on the fiber and require it to handle all future tasks.

## Exact descent, including exceptional primes

Set `A=⋃B`. For nonzero a,b in R choose h with `(a,b)A=hA`; this uses the **serviced union**, not merely a finite-stage UFD gcd. Write `a=hu, b=hv, su+tv=1` with u,v,s,t in A. For almost every q all four values are integral; the identity forces `min(v_q(u),v_q(v))=0` there. At other q this minimum `e_q` is a finite integer of either sign. Set `γ=∏q^{e_q}∈ℚ^×`. Then

```
d=γh ∈ A,       v_q(d)=min(v_q(a),v_q(b))≥0,     f=a/d, g=b/d ∈ R.
```

Here `f=u/γ, g=v/γ`, so their membership in A is automatic. They are integral and at least one is a unit **at every q**. Take `S=γs, T=γt` in A, so `Sf+Tg=1`. The coefficients need correction at only finitely many q. If f is a local unit, set `τ_q=T/f`, taking `(S,T)+(g,−f)τ_q` to `(1/f,0)`. Otherwise g is a unit and `τ_q=−S/g` takes the row to `(0,1/g)`. By CRT choose one `τ∈ℚ` equal to τ_q modulo ℤ_q at the bad primes and integral elsewhere (clear the finitely many prime-power denominators, impose the resulting ordinary congruences). Since f,g are integral everywhere, `S+gτ,T−fτ` belong to R and still witness 1. Multiplying by d proves `(a,b)R=dR`; zero cases are immediate. **It is the residual minima that have finite support, not the valuations of h.**

For example, assigning `Y≡1 mod q` along with `P≡1 mod q` almost everywhere makes the coprime pair `(P−1,Y−1)` impossible to service by restricted-adelic coefficients: reduce its putative identity modulo q. Dropping the second congruence for Y at the outset fixes it; a rational rescaling after these bad choices does not. Conversely for inputs `(2H,3H)` one can take `h=H, γ=1`; the ideal in R is `HR` since `−2+3=1`, despite H having infinitely many positive valuations.

**Dirichlet in R.** For `a,m,B₀∈R`, `m>0`, `(a,m)R=R`, no nonunit rational-stage prime can divide both inputs in their common stage: permanence would contradict their eventual identity for 1. Thus their task is serviced. `Q=a+mY` is primitive linear and prime in the polynomial UFD, hence remains nonunit prime in A; `Q>B₀,2` in the stage order. The identity over R implies local integral inputs with at least one unit everywhere. The mandatory rule therefore gives `v_q(Q)=0` at *every* q. Both `Y∈R` and `Q∈R`; division by Q in A preserves local integrality, so Q is prime in R and `Q−a=mY` holds there. Rational rescaling of Q would not suffice. This also covers `a=0` (then m is a positive unit in R). It is the unbounded internal progression axiom, without a bound on the witness.

**Floor and prime checks.** For each x∈A a standard integer clears its finitely many local denominators; hence `A=R⊗_ℤℚ` and `Frac(R)=F`. For the floor one needs a *translation*: CRT as above gives `r∈ℚ` with `x−r∈R`. Only ℚ is bounded in A by a standard integer (ordered tower invariant), and only ℤ of these lie in R. R is discretely ordered. Each element of the real closure of F lies in a finite-stage real closure and gets a bounded-distance approximant in A by the approximation tasks. Subtracting r preserves bounded distance; then a standard integer adjustment gives a floor. This is the integer-part criterion for open induction. P, already nonunit prime in A, descends just as Q since its marks are units. For standard q, evaluation modulo q maps R onto `𝔽_q` with kernel qR, since division by q preserves integrality elsewhere. If distinct `q_i` divide H at their marks, the ideals generated by `H/(q₁⋯q_n)` form a strictly increasing chain in R: all the fractions lie in R and each successive quotient of generators in the dividing direction is the nonunit `q_{n+1}`. Thus R is **not** an external PID.

## ES and NR at this P

**Subsequent simplification:** `Z73_ES_ROW_DESCENT.md` §§1–2 descends the actual triple by a polynomial leading-coefficient comparison, without any integral specialization or condition (E). Its §3 uses local *ring maps* to treat arbitrary prescribed unit profiles. The original marked-section verification below is a sufficient but unnecessary alternative.

For a hypothetical positive ES triple in R choose a common stage. The triple consists of regular functions there satisfying the identity. For the marked-section lemma of `LOGIC_MARKED_SECTIONS.md`, take W empty at the top. Each φ_q supplies a point in the fixed fiber over π_q; outside finitely many q all stage generators and expressions for the three functions spread integrally, and at the other q retain just the open requirements that the three values be integral. This is locally feasible, even where some row coefficients have poles. (E) holds, since no nonzero rational polynomial vanishes at any π_q. In particular when the section lemma changes a line parameter from λ₀ to `λ₀+f(P)c` it needs `f(π_q)≠0` at the finitely many correction primes, **not** unit values elsewhere. At primes where f merely vanishes on reduction, an integral c preserves the already good reduction of λ₀. There is no appeal to an embedding of F in `𝔸_f` in that lemma.

The resulting section sends P identically to T, and the triple to `G_i∈ℚ[T]` with all `G_i(π_q)∈ℤ_q`. The stage real locus over `P>0` is semialgebraically connected: products preserve this; at each row one removes a codimension ≥2 algebraic center from a connected open smooth manifold (real dimension at most ambient dimension minus two), then takes a real line bundle trivialized by `(u,v)/(u²+v²)` and `(v,−u)`. The deletion preserves path connectivity by semialgebraic triangulation or path perturbation. This holds over a real closure containing the ordered stage point as well. On the positive sector each ES coordinate is ≥P/4, so the sector is open and closed there. It contains the ordered point, hence includes the section at every real `T>0`. If D clears the polynomial coefficients then for `n≡π mod D`, `D G_i(n)≡D G_i(π_q) mod q^{v_q(D)}` at every q dividing D. The `G_i(n)` are therefore integers and, for positive n, positive. This gives a polynomially solvable primitive class containing π. ET Prop. 1.9 covers its sufficiently large primes by a finite union of family classes; if π missed that union, the primitive subclass obtained using its residues at the common modulus would contain arbitrarily large ordinary primes by Dirichlet. Contradiction.

For NR choose ordinary integers x,c with `x²≡−π_l mod l`, `cπ_l≡1 mod l`. The root exists since π_l and −1 are both nonsquares in `𝔽_l^×`; the inverse exists since π_l is a unit. Then `y=(x²+P)/l` and `u=(1−cP)/l` belong to R: at l by the congruences, elsewhere because l is a local unit. This explicitly gives `x²+P=ly` and `ul+cP=1`. Likewise `(P−1)/840∈R` by the assumed mark. This is NR **at this prime**, not a universal NR result.

## Finite stress test

`uv run --no-project python scripts/z73_adelic_rows.py` tests row reductions and the rational CRT correction, not occurrence of an uncovered profile. Take `Q=1+Y`, then `(P−1)U+Y(Y−1)V=1`, then `(P−1)W+(V−1)X=1`. Both rows are coprime in their respective stages: on the first H-chart `V=1/[Y(Y−1)]` is not identically 1. At 2 even the first row has **no** integral lift if `P≡1`; at 3 it would need `Y=2`, which the Dirichlet unit condition forbids. At 5 the first row permits `Y=3`, but the second then fails (`V=1`); `Y=2` permits both. For every prime 2 through 199 the script enumerates the available residues for both prefixes and finds only 2,3 infeasible. The proof of almost-everywhere feasibility is the generic-fiber argument, not this finite run. A separate test starts with `2(8/15)+3(−1/45)=1`, uses the two distinct local corrections at 3 and 5 and one CRT scalar, and ends with `2·2+3·(−1)=1`.
