**Claim.** There is a discretely ordered Bézout domain `R` which is an integer part of the real closure of its fraction field, with an infinite prime `p≡1 (mod k)` for every standard positive integer `k`, at which `4xyz=p(xy+xz+yz)` has no positive solution. Thus the nonnegative cone satisfies open induction (Shepherdson) and Bézout, but not ES. This is a nonstandard model, **not** a counterexample in ℕ or an independence result for PA/ZFC. The previously open combination is specified in `Z66_IE1.md` §6. We work in ordinary classical mathematics; neither a recursive model nor an elementary extension is needed.

## Stage invariants

A stage consists of a smooth finitely generated factorial ℚ-domain `A`, an ordering of its fraction field `F` under which the elements of `A` bounded in absolute value by some standard integer are exactly ℚ, and a rational point `e:A→ℚ`. There is a fixed nonunit prime element `p`, with `e(p)=1`. In `X=Spec A` require the ordered generic point and `e` to belong to the same **semialgebraically connected component** of `X(p>0)`, in the real closure of `F`. Semialgebraic, not ordinary topological, connectedness is essential. These components are defined over the real algebraic numbers and commute with extensions of real closed fields (semialgebraic triangulation). The generic point just uses the ordered embedding of `A` into its real closed field. Start with `A=ℚ[h]`, `h` positive infinite, `p=h+1`, `e(h)=0`. Each operation extends both the order and `e`.

## Approximation step

For `α` in the real closure of `F`, nothing is required if `α` is at finite distance from `A`. Otherwise adjoin a polynomial variable `Y`, ordering it as `α+t` where `t` is bounded and its residue in the natural ordered residue field is transcendental over that of `F^rc`. Such an extension exists: on `F^rc(t)` take the Gauss extension of the natural valuation with value-zero indeterminate, ordering its residue as a transcendental real number over the countable old residue field (or by the ordering at a transcendental Archimedean cut). For clarity, embed the old Archimedean residue field in ℝ and order `t` by the corresponding cut among the bounded constants; then any polynomial in `t` has valuation the least coefficient valuation since its initial residue cannot vanish. This yields `Y` transcendental over `F` and a countable new ordered fraction field. If `G(Y)=a_dY^d+…+a_0` with `d≥1` were bounded, the Taylor expansion at `α` evaluated at `t` would have **every coefficient bounded**. Hence `a_d∈ℚ*` and `d a_d α+a_(d−1)` bounded, contrary to the assumption. Choose `e(Y)=0`. The variety is a product with a line, so landing, smoothness, factoriality and primality of `p` are preserved.

## Making a pair Bézout

For nonzero `a,b∈A` first divide out `d=gcd_A(a,b)` and write the resulting coprime pair as `a₀,b₀`. We want a principal ideal for the original pair generated by an explicit linear combination in an overring. It will then remain principal under all further extensions. The smooth center below is the **entire** landing point, not the possibly singular intersection `a₀=b₀=0`.

**Separate the pair at the landing.** If either value at `e` is nonzero, put `B=A, u=a₀,v=b₀` and skip to the row operation. Otherwise let `m=ker e`, `B=A[T,m/T] ⊂ A[T,T⁻¹]`. This is the deformation to the normal cone of a smooth rational point. Here are the facts used:

1. `B[1/T]=A[T,T⁻¹]`; collecting Laurent coefficients gives `B/(T)=gr_m A ≅ Sym_ℚ(m/m²)` (regularity at a rational smooth point; `A/m^j` agrees with its localization at `m`). Thus `T` is prime, and `B` is factorial by Nagata's criterion. It is smooth over ℚ. Away from `T=0` this is immediate. Along `T=0`, the map to the affine `T`-line is flat (torsion-free over ℚ[T]), finite type, with smooth geometric special fibre the tangent affine space and smooth nonzero geometric fibres; use the flat smooth-fibres criterion.
2. Give `T` the order “above all positive standard constants but below all old positive infinite elements of `F` and their positive rational powers.” For instance take a Gauss valuation on `F(T)` with ordered value group `Γ⊕lex ℤ`, old values `(γ,0)` and `val(T)=(0,−1)`; sign of a nonzero polynomial is the sign of the coefficient of the unique minimal-valued term, with `T>0`. All rational bounds refer to this ordered field. For a Laurent polynomial in `B`, the coefficients of `T^(−i)` are in `m^i`, `i>0`. A nonzero such coefficient is infinite (no nonzero rational belongs to `m`). A positive-degree term has a nonzero rational or an infinite coefficient. Distinct powers have distinct valuation, even when coefficient values have the same first coordinate. Thus the only bounded elements are the old rationals. Primality of `p` survives: since `p−1∈m`, `T` becomes invertible modulo `p` (`p−1=Tg`), and `(B/(p))[1/T]=(A/(p))[T,T⁻¹]` is a nonzero domain.
3. Let `k=min(ord_m(a₀),ord_m(b₀))`; finite by separation of the regular local ring. Then `u=a₀/T^k`, `v=b₀/T^k` are coprime in `B`: they are coprime after inverting `T` and not both divisible by `T`. At least one image in `B/(T)` is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial, so at some **rational** tangent vector it is nonzero. Take this vector as the new rational landing `e_B`. It extends `e`. It is in the same semialgebraic component of `p>0` as the new ordered generic point. Indeed over `T>0` the old component times `(0,∞)` is connected and contains the generic point. At `e`, use smooth real local coordinates to trace an arc with that tangent, `x(t)=e+t v_tan+O(t²)`, `T=t→0+`. The lifted arc has limits `g(x(t))/t=dg_e(v_tan)` for generators `g∈m`, so lands at `e_B`; small points along it project to the old component since it is open at `e`. This argument may first be made over ℝ (all stage data are rational) by the implicit function theorem, then transferred: connected components are semialgebraic and closure membership is first order over the real algebraic numbers. Adding a point in the closure preserves semialgebraic connectedness. In particular `e_B(p)=1`.

**The row operation.** Take `E=B[U,V]/(uU+vV−1)` (zero entries or unit ideals can instead be skipped). It is covered by the charts `u≠0`, `v≠0`, each a polynomial line over the corresponding localization of `B`. Both charts are domains with dense common open, so `E` is a domain; it is smooth. For any prime factor `s` of `u`, the special quotient `E/(s)=(B/(s))[1/v][U]` is a nonzero domain as `s∤v`. After inverting `u` the ring is factorial; Nagata's criterion gives factoriality of `E`. For the distinguished `p`, if `p|u` use this same calculation; otherwise after reducing mod `p` the two line charts over the domain `B/(p)` (omit any empty chart) have dense overlap if both are nonempty. At least one is nonempty, since `u,v` are coprime. Hence `p` stays nonunit prime.

Order `Frac(E)=Frac(B)(V)` by taking `V` positive larger than **every** old field element. In the chart over `u` any genuinely nonconstant polynomial in `V` with old fraction-field coefficients is infinite. Further, `E∩Frac(B)=B`, since both charts give membership in `B[1/u]∩B[1/v]=B` (factoriality and coprimality); hence bounded elements of `E` are precisely ℚ. Extend the landing by any rational point on the line `e_B(u)U+e_B(v)V=1`. For connectivity, the common zero locus of coprime `u,v` on the smooth irreducible variety `Spec B` has codimension at least two (or is empty); dimension does not increase under field extension and its real dimension is at most its algebraic dimension. Its removal from a semialgebraically connected open smooth real component cannot disconnect it: triangulate compatibly, or perturb a semialgebraic path off a finite smooth stratification of codimension at least two. This statement for each fixed finite collection of polynomial equations and its components transfers from ℝ to any real closed extension. Over the complement the real fibre is a line; the section `(u,v)/(u²+v²)` and the kernel direction `(−v,u)` give a semialgebraic product with ℝ. Both points are therefore still in the same component of `p>0`.

In `E`, the ideal `(a,b)` has generator `d T^k` (just `d` if separation was skipped), with `aU+bV=dT^k`. No assertion that *old* gcds stay gcds is needed. This addresses precisely the issue at a common zero of `a,b`, where naively inverting a member or imposing a unit row would lose the landing.

## Limit and the ES sentence

Iterate over countably many stages. Every pair at each stage and every member of its countable ordered real closure is eventually scheduled (e.g. service each pair “stage index, enumeration index” in diagonal order). Ordered real closures may be chosen compatible. At the union `A∞` every finitely generated ideal is principal and has a Bézout certificate: binary ideals suffice by iteration. Elements bounded in the union are still exactly ℚ, and `p` is still a nonunit prime, since each putative factorization, inverse or violation of primality occurs already at a finite stage. Write `R=e∞⁻¹(ℤ)`. Its units are ±1, and it is discretely ordered; `Frac(R)=Frac(A∞)` by clearing the rational value of each element with an integer multiplier. It is an integer part of the real closure: any algebraic `α` is in a finite-stage real closure, and eventually has bounded distance from some `g`. Then `g−e∞(g)∈R` still has bounded distance from `α`. For a bounded element of any ordered extension of ℚ there is a standard integer floor (choose a finite enclosing interval), so this gives an actual `R`-floor. Shepherdson's criterion applies.

For Bézout in `R`, if `a,b∈R` not both zero, take `a=h u,b=h v` with `(u,v)=1` in `A∞`. The rational numbers `e(u),e(v)` are not both zero. Write `γℤ=e(u)ℤ+e(v)ℤ`, `γ>0`, and `γ=i e(u)+j e(v)` for integers `i,j`. Then `γh∈R`, and `u/γ,v/γ∈R`. For `Uu+Vv=1` in `A∞` and `δ=γ−iu−jv`, one has `e(δ)=0`, so `i+δU,j+δV∈R`, and these give `γh` as an `R`-linear combination of `a,b`. For the semiring (nonnegative-coefficient) form of Bézout used in the language of IOpen, take `a,b>0` and the identity just obtained as `d=σa+ρb` with `d=±γh>0` dividing both (negate the coefficients if needed); with `k=|σ|+1` one has `d=(σ+kb)a−(ka−ρ)b`, where `σ+kb≥1` and hence `(σ+kb)a≥a≥d`, so `ka−ρ≥0`. (z74 Opus xreview fix) Since `e(p)=1`, primality of `p` descends to `R` by evaluating a quotient by `p`. Also `(p−1)/k∈R` for every standard `k≥1`.

If there were a positive ES triple at `p`, the three coordinates would occur at one finite stage. The cross-multiplied identity holds in that coordinate ring. Its positive locus on the real component of `p>0` is both open and closed: wherever all are positive, `1/x+1/y+1/z=4/p` gives each coordinate `≥p/4`. Thus no boundary point in `p>0` can have a zero coordinate. It contains the generic point, so contains the landing, where `e(p)=1` and all three coordinates are positive integers. But three positive integer reciprocals sum to at most 3, not 4. This contradiction rules out **all** positive triples in this model.

The prime is infinite, not a standard prime at which someone could check denominators. The standard model satisfies the axioms of the theory but its ES status is not inferred from constructing this nonstandard model. In particular this is **one-sided nonprovability**, not two-sided independence or a resolution in ordinary arithmetic. The square/numerator-8/composite/restricted-zero numerical screens do not test this model-theoretic claim.
