R(n) = Σ_{u≥1} D(n+u; 4u) = Σ_{C≥1} D(4Cn+1; 4C), D(N; m) := #{d | N : d ≡ −1 (mod m)}.
For a *slice* (N, m) (u-slice N = n+u, m = 4u; C-slice N = 4Cn+1, m = 4C) let P = prime factors of N coprime to m,

(−1 ∉ H·G_m², not B); **Q** only non-real odd characters kill H (−1 ∈ H·G_m² \ H); **S** starved (−1 ∈ H \ Div).
B, T, Q = *structured* failure (−1 ∉ H); S = *random* failure. χ_p = (·/p). 𝔐 = Mordell's six classes mod 840.

## 3. Proved statements

**Lemma 1 (slice dictionary via the Jacobi transfer; PROVED, elementary; the transfer itself is CAP_BOUNDED Lemma 1.1 /
(J)83).** Let p ≡ 1 (mod 4) be prime and ℓ an odd prime.
(a) If ℓ | p + u and ℓ ∤ u, then χ_{−4u}(ℓ) = χ_p(ℓ). If ℓ | 4Cp + 1, then χ_{−4C}(ℓ) = χ_p(ℓ).
(b) Hence the u-slice (resp. C-slice) is a B-kill iff every odd prime factor of p + u coprime to u (resp. every prime
factor of 4Cp + 1) is a quadratic residue mod p.
(c) A B-killed u-slice with u < p has χ_p(u) = χ_p(2)^{v₂(p+u)}, so χ_p(u) = +1 when p ≡ 1 (8). For C-slices, (b)
imposes no condition on χ_p(C).
*Proof.* (a) ℓ | p + u gives −u ≡ p (mod ℓ). For odd ℓ, χ_{−4u}(ℓ) = (−4u/ℓ) = (−u/ℓ) = (p/ℓ) = (ℓ/p) by reciprocity,
since p ≡ 1 (4). Likewise 4Cp ≡ −1 (mod ℓ) gives (−C/ℓ) = (−4Cp/ℓ)(p/ℓ) = (p/ℓ).
(b) This is the definition of B together with (a). Every odd prime factor of p + u is coprime to u when u < p,
because ℓ | u and ℓ | p + u would give ℓ | p. Every prime factor of 4Cp + 1 is odd and coprime to C.
(c) The Legendre symbol ((p+u)/p) = (u/p) factors as ∏_ℓ (ℓ/p)^{v_ℓ(p+u)}, and the odd factors are +1 by (b). Also
((4Cp+1)/p) = 1 identically. ∎

**Lemma 2 (the exponent-2 slices are exactly the purely structured ones; PROVED; = Z54_HCKILL Lemma 1.2(a), restated for
the u/C slicings; the converse is Lemma 1.2(c) there).** For m ∈ 4ℕ, G_m has exponent 2 iff
m ∈ {4, 8, 12, 24}, i.e. u (or C) ∈ {1, 2, 3, 6}. For those slices Div = H, so failure ⟺ −1 ∉ H ⟺ some odd real
character mod m is trivial on P: the type is B or T, never S or Q.
*Proof.* λ(4) = λ(8) = λ(12) = λ(24) = 2. For every other m ∈ 4ℕ, either 16 | m, or 9 | m, or a prime ℓ ≥ 5 divides m;
these give λ(m) ≥ 4, 6 and ℓ − 1 ≥ 4 respectively. In an exponent-2 group, ⟨P⟩ = {∏_{ℓ∈S} ℓ : S ⊆ P}, and each such
product is the residue of the squarefree divisor ∏_{ℓ∈S} ℓ of N. So H ⊆ Div ⊆ H. All characters of an exponent-2 group
are real. ∎

For every other m ∈ 4ℕ, type S is possible (Z54_HCKILL Lemma 1.2(c)). Directly: take g of order ≥ 3 in G_m and
N = ℓ₁ℓ₂ with ℓ₁ ≡ g, ℓ₂ ≡ −g. Then −1 = ℓ₂ℓ₁⁻¹ ∈ H, but Div = {1, g, −g, −g²} ∌ −1.
* EVIDENCE: in the census, S occurs at every u and every C ≤ 256 outside {1, 2, 3, 6}.
* Example (CERTIFIED): p = 409, u = 4, N = 413 = 7·59, m = 16. Here H = ⟨7, 11⟩ = G₁₆ ∋ 15, but
  Div = {1, 7, 11, 13} ∌ 15.

**Corollary 3 (what R(p) = 0 forces exactly at the exponent-2 slices; PROVED).** Prior art: the C = 1 instance,
4p + 1 ∈ S, is one of the two S-conditions of Z54_HCKILL Lemma 1.1. The other, p + 4 ∈ S, belongs to the HC line-4
family and is *not* forced by R(p) = 0 (e.g. p = 409, where 413 = 7·59). The u = 1 instance (p+1)/2 ∈ S is its
R-analogue. Let p ≡ 1 (mod 24) be prime with R(p) = 0. Then:
(i) every prime factor of (p+1)/2, of p + 2 and of 4p + 1 is a quadratic residue mod p (u = 1, u = 2, C = 1: the BM
character is the only odd real character that can kill);
(ii) u = 3: all odd prime factors of p + 3 are QRs mod p (equivalently ≡ 1 mod 3), **or** all are ≡ 1 (mod 4);
(iii) u = 6: all prime factors of p + 6 are QRs mod p, **or** all are ≡ 1 (mod 3);
(iv) C = 2, 3: all prime factors of 8p+1 (resp. 12p+1) are QRs mod p, or all are ≡ 1 (mod 4);
(v) C = 6: all prime factors of 24p + 1 lie in the kernel of one of χ_{−4}, χ_{−8}, χ_{−3}, χ_{−24}; the last one
means "QR mod p".
*Proof.* Lemma 2: the slice fails iff some odd real ψ mod m is trivial on P. That forces ψ(N′) = 1 for the m-coprime
part N′ of N.
* The odd real characters are: mod 4, χ_{−4}; mod 8, χ_{−4} and χ_{−8}; mod 12, χ_{−4} and χ_{−3}; mod 24, χ_{−4},
  χ_{−8}, χ_{−3} and χ_{−24}. The BM character χ_{−4u} is χ_{−4}, χ_{−8}, χ_{−3} (= χ_{−12} on odd ℓ) and χ_{−24}
  respectively.
* Residues at p ≡ 1 (24): p + 2 ≡ 3 (mod 8), so χ_{−4}(p+2) = −1. p + 6 ≡ 7 (mod 24), so χ_{−4}(7) = χ_{−8}(7) = −1.

  p ≡ 1 (mod 16). For p ≡ 9 (16) the second alternative in (ii) is excluded, and the u = 3 slice must be a B-kill.
  The listed alternatives are necessary conditions.
* Translate "BM-trivial" by Lemma 1(b). ∎

**Prop 4 (mimicry of 1² at every bounded slice scale; CONDITIONAL on Dickson's conjecture; construction of CAP_BOUNDED
Thm 1.3, extended to both slicings).** Fix K ≥ 1. Let L be the least common multiple of
840, of 4u(u+1) for u ≤ K, and of 4C+1 for C ≤ K.
Under Dickson, there are infinitely many primes p ≡ 1 (mod L) (so p ∈ 𝔐) such that, for every u ≤ K and C ≤ K, the
u-slice and the C-slice of p have *the same divisor-residue set as the corresponding slice of n = 1*. All of them are
B-kills.
Unconditionally (Dirichlet), every certificate whose validity at p depends only on p mod q with q | L and which is
T1-obstructed at 1 fails at all p ≡ 1 (mod L). An example is the Type I triples (d, t, f) of board item fable-5 with
4dt | L.
*Proof.* Put p = 1 + Lt, q_u(t) = 1 + (L/(1+u))t and r_C(t) = 1 + (4CL/(4C+1))t. These are integer linear forms,
because (1+u) | L and gcd(4C+1, 4C) = 1. They are pairwise distinct: 4C(1+u) > 4C+1. All take the value 1 at t = 0,
so the family is admissible. Dickson gives infinitely many t with all of them prime. Then:
* p + u = (1+u)·q_u with q_u ≡ 1 (mod 4u), since 4u | L/(1+u). Similarly 4Cp + 1 = (4C+1)·r_C with r_C ≡ 1 (mod 4C).
* So Div of the u-slice is the set of residues of the odd divisors of 1 + u, which is the u-slice of n = 1. None of
  them is ≡ −1: every such divisor is ≤ 1 + u < 4u − 1, so its residue mod 4u is itself.
* For C-slices, d ≡ −1 (mod 4C) with d | 4C+1 forces d = 4C − 1 | 4C + 1, which is impossible.
* Each slice is a B-kill: χ_{−4u}(ℓ) = (−u/ℓ) = (1/ℓ) = 1 for ℓ | 1+u, and χ_{−4u}(q_u) = χ_{−4u}(1) = 1. The C-slices
  are analogous.
* The Type I clause: f | 4dt² + 1 forces χ_{−4d}(f) = +1 (for each prime factor ℓ of 4dt² + 1, −d is a square
  mod ℓ). But f ≡ −p ≡ −1 (mod 4dt) gives χ_{−4d}(f) = −1. ∎

**Lemma 5 (transfer dimension: primes versus composites; PROVED, elementary).** Let n be odd, and ℓ ∤ 2u an odd prime
with ℓ | n + u. Then χ_{−4u}(ℓ) = (n/ℓ) (Legendre).

discriminant r (if r ≡ 1 (4)) or 4r (if r ≡ 3 (4)), induced to modulus 4u. Its conductor divides 4u iff r | u; assume
this. Then ψ = χ_{−4u}χ^{(s)} is an odd real character mod 4u. For every prime ℓ | n+u with ℓ ∤ 2us (automatic for
relevant factors), χ^{(s)}(ℓ) = (r/ℓ) = (s/ℓ)·(square) and ψ(ℓ) = (ns/ℓ) = ((n/s)/ℓ).
(Cross-model fix: for non-squarefree s the literal χ_s / χ_{4s} has extra zeros, e.g. s = 9, u = 1 gives χ₉(3) = 0, so

analogous statement holds for C-slices.

*Proof.* As in Lemma 1(a): −u ≡ n (mod ℓ), so (−u/ℓ) = (n/ℓ), and multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol does the
