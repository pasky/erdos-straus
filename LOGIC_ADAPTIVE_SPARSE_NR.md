# Adaptive norm tower: what the sparse-prime randomness proves

**Verdict (scope).** The adaptive sparse argument works for universal NR, conditional on constructing the other local evaluations compatibly, preserving the rational-stage invariants and ensuring restricted adelic integrality. It does **not** require a Chebotarev distribution for the primes in the sparse sequence. It does require defining prefix-consistent, prime-local kernels *before reading the acceptance decisions*. An inadmissible hypothetical branch may fail to admit even a field point at a place; the repair is a cemetery symbol, not a fictitious field embedding. There is also an arithmetic descent needed to skip Gaussian-split TS tasks. Here are the details. This is not an unconditional joint-model theorem about the ordinary-prime anchor construction.

### 1. Choose the prime laws without the tower

Start with all `P_l=1` and survival mass one in the complement `U` of the ET cylinders. Fix `0<α<1/5`. Recursively release an unused arbitrarily large `q_j≡3 mod 4`, `q_j∤840`, with normalized Haar on all units, then the least still-unused prime deeply, with `1+l^{K_l}ℤ_l` respecting `840` and `K₂≥3`. All choices of primes and depths here are deterministic and depend on the **finite previous set of releases**, not on samples, tower, or sign tasks. As in `LOGIC_UNIVERSAL_NR_AUDIT.md` §2, a new hit on whole release has `q_j|N` and costs at most

    C_previous · q_j^(−α)/(1−q_j^(−α)).

The constant includes the previous finite smooth Euler product and previous class-probability prefactors. The new factor from unit Haar is `q_j/(q_j−1)≤2`. For the deep release the finite smooth tail tends to zero as `K_l→∞`. Spend less than `2^{−j−3}` on each of the two releases in round `j≥1`; also require `q_j≥2^{2j}` and strictly increasing. In particular `Σ_j 1/q_j<∞`. Closedness and finite-law convergence give positive survival probability in the infinite product. This is a construction of a *particular* sparse set, not a bound for an arbitrarily prescribed sparse set. The infinite laws are not globally conditioned on survival during subsequent computations. Every `P_l` is almost surely transcendental.

### 2. A fixed finite node has a uniform-open law

Use the countable tree of rational polynomial, row, direct-circle `x²+y²=s`, and skip operations. Rational stage presentations and rational element labels have only countably many finite descriptions. Fix any choices of order extensions and scheduling labels per node; no continuum of extra random real parameters is needed for the local laws. Probability statements below concern **nodes**, not all infinite branches as a countable set. Give each sparse prime its own independent random seed comprising the Haar `P_q` and enough extra independent coordinates for all tree transitions. Siblings share the entire realization of their parent; a child uses fresh coordinates. A skip copies the parent. Never reject or resample old sparse-prime data to accept a TS task.

At each fixed finite node `v` in the geometrically integral part of the tree, use a finite-type integral model, ignoring finitely many bad *reduction estimates*. For the residue observation use the point in `V_v(𝔽_q)` if the model coordinates are integral, a special value otherwise. The following unconditional statement is sufficient:

    TV(law of observation at q, uniform on V_v(𝔽_q)) = O_v(1/q).

The uniform measure has mass zero at the special value. One can replace the full variety by any fixed nonempty open defined over ℚ, changing this estimate by `O_v(1/q)`; by spreading and Lang–Weil a proper closed complement has `O_v(q^{d−1})` points against `~q^d` total. This last ratio, not the `O(q^{−1/2})` error of a Lang–Weil main term, suffices for summable chart failures. Proof by distributional induction:

* At the norm-plane base `P_q mod q` is uniform on nonzero residues. Each fiber has **exactly** `q+1` points, since `q≡3 mod 4`. Uniform choice in the fiber gives uniformity on `D(P)`. Lift with exact norm `P_q` using a nonzero partial derivative and a Haar choice of the other coordinate in its residue ball. It is transcendental over the previous countable field almost surely; the independent pair consisting of `P_q` and this coordinate guarantees injection of the plane algebra.
* For a row restrict the old space to a fixed dense pivot chart where the pivot is integral and a residue unit. Above it every residue fiber has exactly `q` points. Sample its free coordinate uniformly, solve for the other in the field. A free polynomial step has the same count without the pivot. For `Q=a+mY`, `m≠0`, restrict to the old open where `m` is a unit on reduction; above it sample uniformly excluding `−a/m`. **The unit safeguard is active at most places, not finitely many.** It is the distortion from sampling the full line that is only `1/q`; on `D(mQ)` this is uniform with fiber size `q−1`. Elsewhere enforce the original conditional unit rule if its inputs activate it, and always keep `Y` integral.
* For a circle restrict to `D(s)` on the old space. There are exactly `q+1` pairs in every residue fiber. Sample uniformly, lift by Hensel to the **actual evaluated** right-hand side, choosing one free lift transcendentally over the prior countable local field. This gives an embedding of the circle function field: the conic with nonzero right-hand side is geometrically integral and the free lift has relative transcendence degree one. It is unnecessary to parametrize this conic over the old global field. The local decisions in this transition must test local data only, not whether `s` is a unit at *all* primes.
* In each of these good-open transitions, uniform old open + uniform constant-size fibers gives uniform new open. Losing the parent open costs `O_v(1/q)` by induction; removing its new complement costs `O_child(1/q)` by the same dimension bound. Formally, total variation contracts under a probability kernel, including on the special value. A finite number of errors accumulate with a node-dependent constant only. We use dense opens and exact fiber counts rather than inferring total variation from agreement on a list of definable sets.

Separate a **missing reduction** from a **missing field embedding**. At a row with two nonunits one can still solve in the field (a nonzero pivot stays nonzero); keep the actual field embedding, even if a coordinate is nonintegral. On a hypothetical circle branch with no admissible integral lift at a sparse prime whose evaluated `s` is a nonunit, one may send the branch there to an absorbing cemetery state. Indeed one can do this on *any* nonunit `s` for a circle edge requiring `s` to be everywhere a unit. Do **not** kill the branch on an unrelated earlier bad reduction if the new circle is required and its evaluated `s` is a unit: then smooth integral lifts exist and must be taken. Likewise retain legitimate local embeddings at bad model primes. Arbitrary treatment of these cases is allowed only for *estimating the residue law*, not in place of a required embedding in the final model. Hensel/root choices by congruence and Haar coordinates can be taken measurably; exact transcendence failures have probability zero (countably many nodes, places, coordinates). No part of these fixed-node sparse kernels reads ordinary-prime choices or another sparse column.

### 3. Countable probability-one event, then path selection

For each fixed node and fixed proper exceptional locus, the probability of missing its good open or of a missing reduction is `O_v(1/q_j)` for large `j`. Borel–Cantelli gives only finitely many such exceptions. This holds simultaneously at all finite nodes and for all of their countably many specified opens. In particular each fixed generator of each realized stage is integral almost everywhere **on the sparse set**. No uniform finite exceptional set over all stages is claimed.

For a nonunit prime element `r` in a geometrically integral, geometrically normal finite stage, `r` is nonsquare in the geometric function field. Indeed the geometric ambient ring is normal and `(A/r)⊗ℚbar` is nonzero and reduced (characteristic zero). A putative square root in the field is integral in the ring and gives a nonzero nilpotent modulo `r`. Equivalently the geometric divisor has multiplicity one on its components, even if it splits. On a dense open `O⊂D(r)`, the étale cover `z²=r` is geometrically integral. For all sufficiently large `q` Lang–Weil for `O` and its fixed cover gives

    #sign-negative points = #O(𝔽_q) − #cover(𝔽_q)/2 = q^d/2 + O_{v,r}(q^{d−1/2}).

This argument is for elements, not an assertion that arbitrary geometric primes have rational defining equations. Rational scalar associates are all included, since the same geometric nonsquareness holds. The TV bound therefore gives probability tending to `1/2` of an honestly realized negative unit value at this node. For a **fixed node and element** these events are independent over `q_j`. Second Borel–Cantelli gives infinitely many hits; even frequency `1/2` follows by the independent bounded-variable strong law, since `Σ j^(−2)<∞`. This does **not** apply to arbitrary joint sign patterns without checking geometric independence of the square classes: `r` and `−r` have opposite signs at every sparse prime. Even a constant such as `2` could have oscillating mean depending on the selection of primes `3,7 mod 8`. No prime-Chebotarev assertion of that kind is needed.

Intersect these probability-one assertions over **all finite nodes and all element labels there**, then intersect with the positive-probability event `P∈U`. The resulting intersection has the original survival probability. Take one outcome and follow any fair admissible path using the stored sparse columns and compatible ordinary-prime evaluations. Tests may inspect infinitely many places (e.g. all valuations zero); no independence *after* path selection or conditioning on `U` is invoked. The sign and integrality statements are already true for every possible visited node of this outcome. Countable nodes suffice although there may be uncountably many infinite paths. On an accepted path no sparse place hits cemetery: a circle is taken only for an everywhere-unit right-hand side, and the other edges always produce honest field extensions. This reasoning needs that the actual sparse choices are exactly the stored ones. A later construction on ordinary places may select a path but must not overwrite already sampled sparse lifts.

### 4. Which primes and which TS tasks?

Assume all `A`-elements in the realized union have evaluations integral almost everywhere **across both sets of places**, the rational finite stages are factorial, units are constants, and old nonunits persist. Then an infinite `R`-irreducible `p` has every valuation zero (else a standard prime divides it). A factorization into nonunits at a finite containing stage would have opposite local valuations in its two factors; integrality almost everywhere gives them finite support, so opposite rational rescalings would factor `p` in `R`. Thus `p` itself is prime at every containing finite stage, including the exact rational scalar. Its sign hit at such a stage supplies a standard `q≡3 mod 4` with `χ_q(p)=−1`, hence standard `x` with `q|x²+p` and an inverse residue giving Bézout with `q`, as in the previous audit. Subsequent embeddings extend the old evaluations. Ordinary primes `≡1 mod 4` have ordinary NR and TS witnesses.

For TS of such `p≡1 mod 4` in `R`, `v₂(p−1)≥2`, and the ordering makes it positive. Schedule all scalar representatives fairly. Units and the 2-adic congruence for an *old* element never change with later extensions. If `p` is still Gaussian-inert, a direct circle supplies the required integral norm by construction: at every odd place a unit has a smooth residue norm representation; at 2 choose the free `y≡0 mod 4` for a right-hand side `1 mod 8`, `y≡2 mod 4` for `5 mod 8`, making `p−y²≡1 mod 8`. Ensure both coordinates are integral even at the exceptional places. At service time, test splitting **then**, not only at the node where the label was first enumerated.

**Split case — skipping needs proof.** Assume `A_v[i]` too is factorial with scalar units. For a rational-stage prime `p` split over `ℚ(i)`, `p=c h h̄` with `c∈ℚ^×`, `h∈A_v[i]`. Indeed `A_v[i]/(p)` embeds in the finite étale constant extension of `Frac(A_v/p)`; in the split case the latter is a product of two fields with conjugate factors, giving two minimal primes and no multiplicity. The UFD factorization and conjugation give the displayed identity. Positivity implies `c>0` in ℚ. For each inert ordinary `l≡3 mod 4`, evaluate in `ℚ_l(i)`; `v_l(Nh)` is even and `v_l(p)=0`, so `v_l(c)` is even. Thus `c=N(k)` for `k∈ℚ(i)^×` by the rational two-square criterion (or factor the numerator and denominator in the Gaussian integers). Replace `h` by `kh`, so `Nh=p` exactly.

Writing `h=a+ib`, `a,b∈A_v`, these two coordinates need not yet be in `R`. At every nonsplit prime, including 2, a norm unit makes `h` a unit in the local Gaussian integers; `ℤ_l[i]` is the ring of integers there, so both coordinates are integral. At each split prime the two projections have opposite integral valuations `(e_l,−e_l)`; only finitely many are nonzero, by almost-everywhere integrality of `a,b` and the unit product. Choose a Gaussian prime `λ_l` above each such `l` oriented so `λ_l/λ̄_l` has projection valuations `(1,−1)`. Multiplying `h` by the product of `(λ_l/λ̄_l)^{−e_l}` keeps the norm unchanged, repairs those places and changes no valuations elsewhere. At a split prime `l≠2`, integral split projections are equivalent to integral coordinates. The resulting two coordinates now belong to `R`. This arithmetic correction uses **ordinary-place restricted integrality** as well as the sparse conclusions. It is not justified to skip split tasks merely because a rational-stage norm factorization exists.

### Boundary

The sparse mechanism neither makes the ordinary `P≡1` fibers generic in the full space nor proves that an adaptive circle extends the ordinary anchor invariants; that separate issue cannot be hidden in a probability-zero exception. The positive-probability proof here needs an ordinary-place construction on the **same** outcome (or a full-measure event), not just a second unrelated positive-probability event. Also a norm-circle step is not a line stage. For ES rigidity along such a circle one can use a different simple check: over the old ordered field `K`, complexify and put `u=x+iy`, so the generic circle algebra becomes `K(i)[u,u⁻¹]` with conjugation `u↦s/u`. A real nonconstant regular function then has both positive and negative Laurent degrees, hence its reciprocal vanishes at `u=∞`. In an ES identity with old numerator `P`, taking this Laurent limit leaves just the constant denominators' reciprocals summing to `4/P`. Positivity at the ordered realization precludes any further reciprocals. Descent from `K` to the old ring here uses the monic basis `A[x]⊕y A[x]`. This check does not supply the disputed ordinary-place integrality.
