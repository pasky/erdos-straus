**Verdict (PROVED, using the floor and sphere lemmas cited below).** There is a countable normal discretely ordered domain `R`, satisfying IOpen and Lagrange, with an infinite **prime element** `P≡1 mod 840` at which ES fails. It has positive Pell for every positive nonsquare. It has both negative Pell and an integral two-square representation for every positive nonsquare `s` such that `P∤s`, all its marks are units, and `s₂≡1 mod 4`. The two representations also hold at `P` itself. The restriction on the marks is a predicate on values in the ordinary local fields; it does not mean `s` is a unit of `R`. No NR requirement at `P` or elsewhere, PID, or Bézout assumption. The independent-edge construction even supports unbounded Dirichlet for **irreducibles** and no-common-nonunit-divisor inputs. It does **not** support unbounded Dirichlet for **prime elements**. This distinction survives even for the unimodular progression `1 mod 4` (§4).

## 1. Initial prime, including its entire fiber

Use `C=u²−v², D=2uv, Y=u²+v²`, `E=ℚ[u,v,a,b]/(Da+Cb−1)`, `P=a²+b²`, `X=Ca−Db`. Then `X+i=(a+ib)(u+iv)²`; in particular `X²+1=PY²`. The real row is smooth (the row makes `C,D` unimodular upstairs) and integral by the linear primitive relation. Modulo `P`, the identity `1=(Da+Cb)²=b[(C²−D²)b+2CDa]` makes `b` invertible. With `j=a/b`, the row reads `b(u+jv)²=1`, `j²=−1`; explicitly

```
E/PE = ℚ(i)[t,t⁻¹,v],     i↔j,  t↔u+jv,  b=t⁻²,  a=it⁻²,  u=t−iv.
```

This proves prime, not geometrically prime. In particular do **not** add an unrestricted signed edge at `s=P`: it splits there. The root order can put `v,u,a` successively positive above the preceding field and use `b=(1−Da)/C`. The `C,D` charts show that the subring constant in `a` is `ℚ[u,v]` (intersect the two localizations there). Dominance gives bounded elements exactly `ℚ` and positive infinite `P,X,Y`. For local embeddings use independent `u,v,X` in small integral opens about `(1,0,0)`; on `Y≠0`, `a=(CX+D)/Y², b=(C−DX)/Y²`. Thus all coordinates are integral, `P=(1+X²)/Y²` is a square unit and can meet the required congruence at each place. Perturb *transcendently*, not by specialization to the center. No almost-everywhere **unit** test for arbitrary elements is needed.

For root ES descent, first work over `F₀=ℚ(a,b)` in the row equation `bu²+2auv−bv²=1`. The leading form is irreducible (discriminant `4P` nonsquare in `F₀`); inverting `b`, or inverting `a` and setting `v=u+w`, gives monic charts, so `E∩F₀=ℚ[a,b]`. Then view the plane as a circle over `ℚ[P]` and descend again by its irreducible leading form. Details of these two projections: `LOGIC_NEGATIVE_PELL_ROOT_AUDIT.md` (commit `bf5a3f343`). End point is `ℚ[P]`, not merely `E`.

## 2. Signed edges over a normal finitely generated ordered ℚ-domain A

At service time require `s>0` nonsquare in `F=Frac A`, `s∉PA` and the stated local predicates. Such an `s` is infinite: a bounded `s` in `A` is rational, and a positive rational with all valuations zero is 1. Do **not** infer `s∉PA` from the marks, since `P` itself has unit marks. Use both edges below (even if witnesses already exist); no square enters an old field via either geometrically integral generic curve. Each ring is monic free over a one-variable polynomial ring. Thus it is faithfully flat over its old ring, contracts to `A` inside `F`, and after reduction at `P` injects into the algebra over `Frac(A/PA)`.

* **Negative Pell:** `B=A[x,y]/(x²−sy²+1)`. Smooth over `A`: the relative partials `2x,−2sy` cannot vanish together anywhere on it. Its integral generic fiber and smoothness over normal `A` give a normal domain. For `s̄≠0` in `Frac(A/PA)`, the generic residue conic is smooth and geometrically integral (projective rank three); hence `P` remains prime and nonunit. This proof does not assert that the map has integral fibers over zeros of `s`. Order with `y>F^rc`, `x=y√(s−1/y²)>0`. In `f(y)+xg(y)` the top coefficient is `f_d+√s g_{d−1}≠0` at `d=max(deg f,1+deg g)`; the error after dividing by `y^d` is infinitesimal relative to every old positive real-closure element. Positive degree implies infinite magnitude, preserving bounded elements. In particular `x,y>0` are nontrivial.
* **Circle:** `B=A[x,y]/(x²+y²−s)`. Here relative smoothness can **fail** over `s=0`. Over `D₀=A[i]`, a normal domain (finite étale; the ordered field excludes `i`), use `U=x+iy,V=x−iy`. In `Frac(D₀)(U)` one has the exact equality

  `D₀[U,V]/(UV−s) = D₀[U,U⁻¹] ∩ D₀[V,V⁻¹]`, with `V=s/U`.

  Compare Laurent coefficients: a coefficient at `U^n`, `n≥0`, is unrestricted in `D₀`; at `U^(−m)`, `m>0`, it must be in `s^m D₀`. The Laurent normal form also proves injectivity. This intersection is normal **for every nonzero `s`**, including repeated factors and codimension-one zeros. The real ring is normal by descent: an integral element of its fraction field lies in the normal Gaussian ring and is fixed under conjugation, whose fixed ring is `B`. Over `Frac(A/PA)`, `s̄≠0` makes `UV=s̄` smooth geometrically integral; monicity again preserves `P`. Only the *generic* fiber over `A/PA` is asserted smooth. For the order take `x=√s z`, `y=√s√(1−z²)`, `1/3<z<1/2` of fresh transcendental natural residue over `F^rc`. Scale the monic form `f(x)+y g(x)` by the largest coefficient magnitude weighted by the corresponding power of `√s`. Its residue cannot cancel, since `1−z²` is nonsquare in the residue rational function field. Any positive-degree term gives infinite scale: no nonzero old ring coefficient is infinitesimal. This is the argument of `LOGIC_CIRCLE_AUDIT.md`, independent of irreducibility of `s`.

**ES and factorizations.** In each generic ring the total-degree graded ring is a domain: its defining leading form is `x²−sy²` or `x²+y²` over `F`. Thus degrees add and degree zero means `F`. In a positive reciprocal sum with old nonzero right side, divide the cleared identity by the leading product: in top degree only constant denominators contribute and they already account for the entire sum. Positivity excludes any omitted denominator. Monic contraction gives descent to `A`. Likewise a product in `A` forces **both factors in `A`**, since their degrees are nonnegative. Factorial closure is not factoriality, nor preservation of prime ideals.

**Exact local choices.** At odd `ℓ` and unit `b=φℓ(s)`, `x²−b y²=−1` has a smooth residue point: it is a nonzero norm in the quadratic finite field, or a product of two units in the split case. Likewise for `x²+y²=b`. At 2 the necessary negative-Pell unit condition is `b≡1 mod 4` (reduce modulo 4). It suffices: take `x` in `4ℤ₂` if `b≡1 mod 8`, in `2+4ℤ₂` if `b≡5 mod 8`. Then `(1+x²)/b≡1 mod 8` and its square root `y` is a unit. For the circle choose the same cylinders for `x`, giving `b−x²≡1 mod 8`. In all cases vary one coordinate transcendentally over the old countable image in a small open, solving for the other at a nonzero derivative (Hensel/implicit function over the *field*, even at 2). This gives an embedding of the generic field by the nonsquare, simple-root radicand over the free coordinate. Keep both integral **at every place**. At the second edge the free coordinate must be transcendental over the field *already extended* at the first.

## 4. Dirichlet: what actually persists

**At birth.** If `(a,m)A=A`, `m≠0`, then `A[Z]/(a+mZ)≅A[1/m]` (use a Bézout identity to see `m` invertible in the quotient). Thus `Q=a+mZ` is genuinely prime at birth. A Bézout identity in the *final pullback* is visible at a large enough stage, so schedule the actual tuple again. But “no common nonunit divisor” cannot substitute for this identity, nor for height-one primitivity, in a normal domain. For example `D=ℚ[U,V,W]/(UV−W²)` is normal (the Laurent intersection above); `U,W` have no common nonunit divisor (`U` is irreducible by the standard grading and `D₀=ℚ`). Yet modulo `U+WZ`, `W(VZ+W)=0` with both factors nonzero: height-one ideal `(U,W)` supplies a vertical component. No Gauss-lemma inference from normality suffices.

**Unbounded irreducibles (PROVED).** Add a fresh polynomial `Z` above the entire old field, `m>0`, so `Q>H` and positive infinite. Schedule all integral pairs `a,m` locally primitive at **each** prime (plus bounds `H`), without requiring `(a,m)A=A`. Choose `Z` integral and transcendental locally with `a+mZ` a unit: exclude at most one residue when `m` is a unit, none otherwise. If `a,m` have no common nonunit divisor in the final `R`, they are locally primitive: a common positive valuation at `ℓ` would give the divisor `ℓ` in `R`. In the birth stage they have no common nonscalar divisor `d` either. Indeed `d,a/d,m/d` are integral almost everywhere, and local primitivity forces `vℓ(d)=0` almost everywhere. Multiply by a rational to make `d` an all-place unit in value, hence an actual `R`-divisor of both inputs. It is a nonunit: the stage units are rational (factorial closure, starting from the two row charts of `E`, whose units are scalar); the same holds at the union. Contradiction. Any factorization of the linear polynomial in `A[Z]` has a constant factor dividing both coefficients, hence is trivial. The factorial closure on **every** later edge preserves this irreducible element, including when `Q|s` on a signed edge. A factorization in `R` could then only split off a rational scalar in `R`; the all-unit marks of `Q` force it to be `±1`. Finally `Q−a=mZ` is divisibility in `R`. This proves the full progression statement if arithmetic `Prime` means *no nontrivial factors*. It does not make these irreducibles prime in the divisibility-of-products sense.

**Strong prime outputs fail on the independent edges (PROVED).** Let a putative final prime element `q>P` lie in `1 mod 4`. It is infinite, all-place unit (else some standard `ℓ` divides it), nonsquare, and not divisible by `P`. In `A∞=R⊗ℚ` it would still generate a proper prime ideal: it divides no standard nonzero integer in `R`. On the actual tower its signed tasks at `s=q` are both serviced. If `q` is still prime before the first, the negative-Pell zero fiber is `x²+1=0`, and the circle zero fiber is `x²+y²=0`. Either already splits, or its domain has `i` in its fraction field (`x`, respectively `x/y`). In the latter case the *second* zero fiber splits generically. Equivalently, localizing its monic nonzero free module over the old residue domain exhibits zero divisors, which precludes a domain before localization as well. Intervening edges inject old quotients by flat contraction, so cannot erase a split or a fractional root. Thus `q` is not prime in `A∞`, contradiction. This rules out strong unbounded Dirichlet even for `a=1,m=4,H=P`, in the version that adds both independent edges for every eligible `s`. A multiple of a previously assigned Dirichlet output is a real issue, not fixed by having made the linear output prime at birth. In contrast `P` gets its *tied* witnesses at the root and **all multiples** of `P` are excluded from these two edges.

**Multiples, not just the output itself.** In the irreducible-Dirichlet version fix one of the fresh outputs `r=1+4Z`. It is field-nonsquare (at birth a simple linear factor, and the later generic fields are regular, or use final normality and irreducibility). It is not divisible by `P` and has all-unit values. For *any* proposed positive infinite final prime element `q` not associated to `P`, the target `s=r q²` is now positive, field-nonsquare, prime to `P` in the sense `P∤s`, all-unit in value, and `1 mod 4` locally. Both of its independent signed edges have the same zero-fiber obstruction modulo `q`. Thus, with these tasks unconditionally adjoined on the stated domain, **the only positive infinite prime element is `P`**. Exempting just the Dirichlet outputs as targets, rather than handling their multiples, would not justify their persistence.

**Scope.** This is not a logical incompatibility of strong Dirichlet with the signed axioms: different, coordinated signed witnesses or a different persistence construction would require new proof. Nor is `R` asserted to be a PID, to satisfy IE₁ or universal NR, or to produce an ordinary ES counterexample. F1: the square-class obstruction tests only descended denominators. F2–F4: no assertion on numerator 8, ordinary composites or restricted zero counts.
