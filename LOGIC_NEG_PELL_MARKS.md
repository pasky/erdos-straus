# Square-unit marks and the residue field for the negative-Pell row

**Verdict (proved relative to the existing joint tower).** The new base supports the same parsimonious finite-tree marks, joint PID axioms (`IOpen`, internal Dirichlet, TS, NR, Lagrange), and exact field prescription at the ES-failing prime. In the resulting ring the negative-Pell equation at that prime has a positive solution but **no solution with any nonzero standard integer second coordinate**. This is stronger than merely exhibiting a large power of a unit at `1+Z²`. It concerns one nonstandard prime, not a universal negative-Pell axiom.

Put `S=2uv, D=u²−v², Y=u²+v²`, and `E=ℚ[u,v,a,b]/(Sa+Db−1)`. Set `P=a²+b², X=Da−Sb`. Then `X+i=(a+ib)(u+iv)²`, so `PY²=X²+1`. On `Y≠0` the inverse is `a=(S+DX)/Y², b=(D−SX)/Y²`. In particular `Frac E=ℚ(u,v,X)`.

**Ordinary places and tree prefixes.** For an empty-prefix place take `u,v,a` in small integral cylinders about `1,0,0`, successively transcendental over previous choices, and solve `b=(1−Sa)/D`. The pivot D and Y are units and b is close to 1. Require `P≡1 mod q` at odd q and `P≡1 mod 8` at 2; this ensures local unit squarehood and the required components of `1 mod 840` at `2,3,5,7`. Exclude the latter primes from the large-prime regime. These are embeddings of the root **field**, not evaluation at the rational center.

For a large odd q use the square cover `w²=P`, **not** a square specialization of all later witnesses. On `Y≠0` write `t=wY−X`; then `wY+X=t⁻¹`, `X=(t⁻¹−t)/2`, `w=(t+t⁻¹)/(2Y)`. The cover chart `Yw≠0` is exactly an open in affine `(u,v,t)` given by `Yt(t²+1)≠0`. It is geometrically integral and dominates the original root. Over `𝔽_q` it has `q³+O(q²)` points: omit the zeros of these fixed polynomials from `q³`. Each root image has at most two preimages. Sampling uniformly on the chart and forgetting w therefore gives mass `O(q⁻³)` per root atom, the correct bound for the dimension **three**. One can lift a selected chart point by choosing `u,v,t` algebraically independent in their cylinders and using the displayed formulas. This gives an embedding after restriction, with all four base coordinates integral and `P` exactly a unit square locally. The local roots w are not global stage elements.

Use this root law in `LOGIC_JOINT_PID_TREE_AUDIT.md` §§2–3. Along each path the generic fibers remain geometrically integral (line, NR chart, circle at nonzero generic target, rank-four quadric); thus a nonzero descendant test stays nonzero generically even after the root cover. Equivalently work on the original charts after forgetting w: the live atom bound starts at `O(q⁻³)` and multiplies by `O(q^(−relative dimension))` at each edge. A proper bad locus in dimension d then costs `O_prefix(1/q)` by the dimension upper bound after spreading. Charge dead transitions to the live parent's bad pivot or target. Sharing a single draw per common ancestor and union-bounding over the **whole finite tree** gives simultaneous avoidance, not just separate good paths. Lift each child at its actual lifted parent as in the cited proof. Taking growing prefix thresholds before branch selection gives unit valuation eventually for each fixed nonzero element on every potential history. This does not impose an infinite list of avoidance constraints at a fixed q.

**Residue at P.** The row is a UFD with constant units over ℚ and over ℚ(i): localize at S to get a polynomial UFD, while the distinct factors u,v of S remain prime and nonunits (at either factor D becomes a signed square of the other variable, which becomes invertible). Nagata gives factoriality, and a unit in the localization can only be a scalar times powers of u,v, whose orders must vanish globally. The Gaussian factors `a±ib` of P are comaximal by the row. Each is prime: e.g. setting `a=ib` gives `b(u+iv)²=1`. Thus `E/(P) ≅ ℚ(i)[u,v,1/(u+iv)]` with `i` corresponding to **X**. Indeed in `E/(P)` one has `X²=−1`, `a=Xb` by `a−Xb=S(a²+b²)` on the row, and `b(D+XS)=1`. The inverse sends `b↦(u+iv)⁻², a↦ib`; in particular there are no missing components at `Y=0`. The two Gaussian primes are distinct and simple. The initial residue fraction field is exactly `ℚ(i)(u,v)`, not an extension requiring a further algebraic root.

Hence the prescription of `LOGIC_PRESCRIBED_FIELD_AT_P.md` applies to every countable characteristic-zero field k of infinite transcendence degree containing i: choose a sign for the image of X and two independent images for u,v. Later residue extensions and their embedding/enumeration tasks are unchanged. In particular the algebraic-prescription edge `f(V)=PH` is a free line on `P≠0`, which includes all ordinary marks. It neither removes the square-cover chart nor demands an extra local root of f. This is field prescription for `R/PR`; it does not assert that the images of X and the four base coordinates can be independently prescribed there.

**Order, ES, and genuinely infinite Y.** One suitable order takes `u>ℚ`, `v>ℚ(u)`, then the row's free coordinate a above `ℚ(u,v)`. Interpreting 'above' as larger than every element of the indicated ordered field, nonconstant polynomials in a are infinite in magnitude. Elements of E constant in a lie in `ℚ[u,v,1/S]∩ℚ[u,v,1/D]=ℚ[u,v]` by the two row charts. Hence bounded elements are ℚ and `Y` is positive infinite. Use `|X|` for a positive Pell witness. Both `a,b` are algebraically independent: the defining equation is a nonconstant primitive quadric in `u,v` over `ℚ[a,b]`, with no nonzero constant in its ideal. To check base ES descent, work over `F=ℚ(a,b)` and filter the conic ring by total `(u,v)` degree. Its graded ring is `F[u,v]/(bu²+2auv−bv²)`, a domain since the binary discriminant `4P` is nonsquare in F. Minus degree is therefore a valuation trivial on F, degree zero meaning F. For a positive ES triple at P the reciprocals of positive-degree coordinates vanish in the residue; the constant coordinates already sum reciprocally to `4/P`, so positivity forces all coordinates to be constants. Their intersection with E is exactly `ℚ[a,b]`: on `b≠0` the conic is monic up to a unit in u; on `a≠0` substitute `v=u+z`, giving leading coefficient `2a` in u. The monic bases put a generic constant in each localized coefficient ring, whose intersection is `ℚ[a,b]`. The binary descent in `LOGIC_ES_RIGIDITY_NR.md` §2 now gives `ℚ[P]`. Together with the joint tower's stage descent and polynomial square-class obstruction this proves ES failure with these marks.

For **each** `0≠c∈ℚ`, `c²P−1=(c²X²+c²−Y²)/Y²` is nonsquare in `ℚ(u,v)(X)`: the numerator is a nonconstant squarefree polynomial in X since `Y²≠c²`. This is not just a fact about the initial ring. The actual tower has geometrically integral generic fibers (including the prescribed-field edge on inverting P), so its old fraction fields are relatively algebraically closed in the new ones. A missing square root cannot enter at any stage or at the union. In particular `P−1` has no square root even in the final fraction field, hence no negative-Pell unit with `y=1` can account for the witness by taking powers. More strongly any nonzero integer y of bounded magnitude in the final model is standard by discreteness, and the same argument excludes it.

**Ordinary-prime comparison (classical, no ordinary ES consequence).** Every ordinary prime `p≡1 mod 4` already has a negative-Pell solution. Indeed take the least `x>1` in a positive Pell solution `x²−py²=1` (ordinary Pell theorem). Then y is even and x odd by reduction mod 4. The coprime factors `(x−1)/2,(x+1)/2` of `p(y/2)²` are a square and p times a square. If the *larger* factor is the square, their difference yields a smaller nontrivial positive Pell solution; the other orientation yields norm −1. Conversely any ordinary negative-Pell witness `X²+1=pY²` at such p has X even, Y odd. In `ℤ[i]`, `X±i` are coprime (their difference divides out all but the prime above 2, excluded by parity). Each rational prime occurring in the norm is split and only one conjugate prime above it occurs in `X+i`: an inert factor, or a conjugate pair, would divide its imaginary part 1. All exponents are even except at the single factor above p. Thus `X+i=(a+ib)(u+iv)²` with `a²+b²=p` (absorb the unit into the first factor), so the row itself is not an extra ordinary-prime restriction. For instance `p=888721≡1 mod 840`, `(u,v,a,b)=(4,3,264,−905)` gives `(X,Y)=(23568,25)` with `p−1` nonsquare. This prime has the ordinary ES triple `(222183,17950790754,10098403901231006322)`. Neither an ordinary ES counterexample nor simultaneous negative Pell for all TS primes of the model follows.
