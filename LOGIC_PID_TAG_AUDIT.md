**Verdict.** The PID part of `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md` works with the finite tree prepared before the marks. Its `P=X²+Y²` can be square at **every standard place**, `1 mod 840`, prime, and ES-failing in a countable model of IOpen + external PID + full TS + NR + internal Dirichlet. Here full TS concerns positive primes `1 mod 4`. At `P` the witness modulus for NR **must be nonstandard**. In fact this model violates even the standard-prime reciprocity instance at 3. The following checks concern the PID construction, not sparse-prime sampling.

### Which choices can be synchronized?

Fix a finite rooted prefix and **finite** pole/zero lists per node. Start the diagram on the smooth open `t²=X²+Y² ≠ 0`: geometrically integral, as the quadratic form in three variables has rank three (or use `uv=t²`, `t≠0`). It dominates the original base. This cover is only for choosing local marks; adjoining `t` to the rational stage would make `P` reducible. Add one edge of the diagram at a time by fiber product over its parent. A smooth dominant chart with geometrically integral generic fiber retains that property after extension to the function field of the joint diagram. Thus its horizontal component is geometrically integral and dominates the old diagram **and the new child** (at the generic point of the parent, scalar extension is faithfully flat). One can restrict to a smooth nonempty open; earlier dominance is not lost. This proves the tree assertion even with nonrational circles. It does not require every circle fiber at every residue to be integral. A finite union of pulled-back divisors/poles is still proper. Spread that open out; Lang–Weil supplies a smooth point over every sufficiently large prime field. No uniformity in the size of the finite tree is used.

At such a place Hensel lift on the **joint** open, choosing a point avoiding the countable collection of proper rational zero loci: an étale-coordinate ball is locally analytic, and a proper algebraic zero locus has empty interior there. The actual embeddings agree on parents as field embeddings, not just mod the place. At successive cutoffs list the first `n` nonzero field elements at **each** of the nodes of depth at most `n` (or any equivalent exhaustive nesting). The finitely many exceptional places for a fixed prefix do not need integral rows. They do need integral Dirichlet `Y`, unit cone `D` (`3 mod 4` at 2), integral cone `V`, and integral admitted circle coordinates. For small places the initial base has an integral open about `(1,0)` giving square unit norm, with the prescribed congruence at primes of 840. For an actual circle the target is an everywhere-unit; an odd-place unit has integral norm lifts by surjectivity of the finite-field norm or the split product, and smoothness. At 2 subtract `0` or `4` from a target `1 mod 4` to obtain an odd square `1 mod 8` in `ℤ₂`. The smooth local conic in characteristic zero has a generic point in its integral open. An infeasible circle on a hypothetical branch may be left undefined, with no effect on a sibling. Select the actual child from the evaluations of its **parent**. Induction makes every actual tail feasible. This gives unit values almost everywhere even for *ratios*, not just integrality of finitely many generators.

### Primes, orders and norms

* For `B=A[D,H,V]/(DH−V²−s)` the chart `D≠0` gives `A[D,D⁻¹,V]`; the whole ring embeds there by monicity of `V²+s` and saturation at `D`. This works modulo **every old prime**, including `s`, so old rational primes stay prime. The coefficient of `D^(−j)` in the Laurent expansion in `D`, `j>0`, is divisible by `(V²+s)^j` in `A[V]`. Thus `B∩F(D)=A[D]`, `F=Frac(A)`. Order `D` positive above `F`, then `V` above `F(D)`; a bounded element is first constant in `V` and then constant in `D`. Positivity of `s` makes `V²+s` irreducible over `F`; it is irreducible over `F(i)` too since neither `s` nor `−s` is square in `F` (prime valuation and sign). Hence `D` itself is prime over both constant fields, and Nagata gives both UFDs. No unit in either Laurent localization except a scalar times `D^j` can be a unit of `B`; the primality of `D` forces `j=0`. In particular `D` is a positive infinite prime, not just a denominator. If the stage prime has nonintegral scalar normalization, do **not** demand integral `H` at its bad places: `H=(V²+s)/D` is integral wherever `s` is, enough for every actual NR task.
* At an inert admissible circle target the Gaussian chart has `U W=s`, with `U` prime by Gaussian inertness, giving the Gaussian UFD and only constant units. Normality descends; an invariant principal divisor over the finite étale Gaussian extension descends with a generator by Hilbert 90 on its constant units. In particular a rational height-one prime, extended with multiplicity one, is principal downstairs. Rational primality of the target survives the degenerate circle fiber **only because** `i∉Frac(A/s)`; other old rational primes have nonzero conic generic fiber. A second circle on a now split associate is not admissible. For the order choose `x=√s t, y=√s √(1−t²)` with fresh angular residue in `(1/3,1/2)` over the residue of the old real closure. In the normal form `a(x)+yb(x)` divide the scaled coefficients (including their powers of `√s`) by one of largest magnitude. On reduction the surviving terms cannot cancel: `1` and `√(1−t²)` are linearly independent over the rational function field in the residual `t`. Thus boundedness forces each scaled coefficient bounded; since `√s` is infinite and nonzero old ring coefficients are not infinitesimal, only the bounded old constant can remain.
* Finite valuation support permits normalization of every rational-stage prime by one rational scalar. A nonstandard positive prime in the PID has valuation zero at each ordinary place (otherwise divisible by the corresponding ordinary prime); two positive everywhere-unit rational associates are equal. One must schedule **all positive rational rescalings**, not just choose one per stage prime ideal. For a Gaussian-split prime write `s=c h h̄` using the two conjugate multiplicity-one factors; `c∈ℚ_{>0}`. At every `ℓ≡3 mod 4` the norm valuation is even, so `v_ℓ(c)` is even by the unit mark. This is precisely the positive rational norm criterion, hence absorb `c` into `h`. At nonsplit places including 2, norm a unit implies `h` a unit of the Gaussian integers locally. At split places the two component valuations are `(n,−n)`, and need not separately vanish. Correct each exceptional `ℓ=ππ̄` by a power of `π/π̄` with the opposite imbalance. Only finitely many corrections occur. This norm-one multiplication gives integral **real coordinates** at all places, including at 2 where the local integer basis is `1,i`. Norm equality in `A` without this correction is not the claimed TS in `R`.
* With permanent primes, finite support, the scheduled rows and floor approximations, the content/CRT argument of `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §2 gives external PID and IOpen; it does not require a purely transcendental ambient field. Unit output at every locally primitive Dirichlet input and integral `Y` yield the progression in `R`. At an actual cone target `s`, the nonassociate unit-mark primes `s,D` are coprime and `DH=V²+s` gives the NR witness in `R`. Positive standard primes use ordinary witnesses. ES descent is algebraic, **not** a claim that these NR witnesses specialize at integer squares: let a fiber coordinate tend to infinity in the rational function identity. Reciprocals of nonconstant polynomials vanish, so the constants already sum to `4/P`; positivity in the given order prohibits any omitted reciprocals. For the cone do `V` first and use `B∩F(D)=A[D]` before doing `D`. For the circle, a nonconstant rational-real element is a Laurent polynomial over `F(i)` in `U` fixed by conjugation acting as `U↦s/U`. Its exponents have both signs, so its reciprocal vanishes at infinity too. Constants descend by the monic normal form. The norm-plane rigidity and primitive square class obstruction now apply exactly to the descended triple (`LOGIC_NORM_BASE_NR_AUDIT.md`).

### Residue-field kill tests

1. **No standard NR witness at `P`.** Each standard `d>0`, `d≡3 mod 4`, has a standard prime factor `l≡3 mod 4`. Since `P` is a square unit mod `l`, `v²+P≡0 mod l` has no solution. This uses only the actual ordinary residue field (already forced at a standard prime in a discretely ordered integer part), not a reciprocity law of the model.
2. **One genuine tagged test.** At a cone on an actual nonstandard positive prime `s`, the field of the divisor `D=0` is `F(√(−s))(H)`. On old squareclasses its quadratic step kills exactly `[−s]`: squaring `a+b√(−s)` gives an old value only if `ab=0`. In particular `−1` is not a square there (`s` nonsquare in `F`), while `−s` is. Later steps over this divisor have geometrically integral generic fibers: rows, free lines, cone charts even when their target vanishes, and circles at *nonassociates* of `D`. There can be no admitted circle on an associate of `D`: the only positive everywhere-unit one is `D` and `D≡3 mod 4`. Thus all these nonsquares persist. The final residue field `R/DR` equals `Frac(A/DA)` for the union `A`: `R/DR` is a field and localization at standard nonzero integers is already trivial there. Consequently `s` really is a nonsquare mod `D`. For distinct old prime ideals, `−s'` does not become square mod `D` by the old prime valuations. This is a tag for negative-square **solvability**, not an assertion that the residue field has just two squareclasses.
3. **The reversed tag and a failure of reciprocity.** Modulo `s` at this particular cone the residue fraction field adjoins free `D,V`; even over the Gaussian constants `D` is not a square. Subsequently rows, cone charts and circles off `s` give regular function-field extensions there; an admitted circle on an associate of `s` itself can add only `i` and a free parameter at the generic divisor (`x²+y²=0`). Thus `D` is still nonsquare mod `s` in the limit. This verifies both nonsquares on this pair when `s≡1 mod 4`; it is **not** a quadratic reciprocity axiom. Indeed the initial divisor `P=0` has field `ℚ(i)(Y)`. No circle on an associate of `P` can be admitted, since the Gaussian factors of `P` remain nonunits. Every later step over its generic point has geometrically integral generic fiber; in particular a cone on `P` has rational chart `D≠0` even at `P=0`. Hence the only algebraic constants in `Frac(A/PA)` are `ℚ(i)`. In `R/PR` there is no root of `−3`, whereas `P` is a nonzero square mod `3`. This explicitly refutes `QR_std(3)`, despite full TS at `P` and the NR tag. (z74 Opus xreview fix: this concerns the joint model **as built** in `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md`. The modifications in `LOGIC_QR_AT_P.md`, and the pseudofinite case of `LOGIC_PRESCRIBED_FIELD_AT_P.md`, make every standard `ℓ*` a square mod `P`, so `QR_std` holds at `P` there.)
4. **Impossible stronger readings.** A tag cannot distinguish `s` from *every future prime* by a characteristic residue test mod that same `D`: internal Dirichlet produces arbitrarily large primes `Q≡s mod D`, even `Q≡s mod 4D` when `s` is odd and coprime to `D`. It does not rule out pair separation using multiple tags. Indeed repeat the cone tasks (z74 Opus xreview fix: this repetition is a harmless *modification* of the construction of record, since each cone is an ordinary L1 edge of the tree; it is not part of `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md` as written): for any two positive nonassociate nonstandard primes, take a cone on the first **after both already exist**. Item 2 separates them for the test “is the negative a square?”, and repetition gives infinitely many distinct such `D`. This is not arbitrary independent Legendre signs as over finite fields. Also, demanding TS of *all* positive nonstandard primes regardless of residue is incompatible with internal Dirichlet and the mod-4 square table in IOpen: the positive nonstandard primes `3 mod 4` cannot be sums of two squares. Nor can every nonstandard prime in this Dirichlet model fail ES: for `p=4k−1` one has `4/p=1/k+1/(2kp)+1/(2kp)`. Finally, square *unit* at every ordinary place does not mean `P≡1` at all those places: the latter would make `P−1` divisible by infinitely many standard primes, incompatible with an external PID. None of these restrictions invalidates the stated TS or NR sentences.
