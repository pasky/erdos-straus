**Verdict.** The same external PID model of IOpen as in the interval construction can be built to satisfy the single internal sentence `∀a,m,H (m>0 ∧ gcd(a,m)=1 → ∃Q>H (Prime(Q) ∧ m | Q−a))`. Here `gcd=1` can be expressed by a Bézout identity. No size bound on Q in terms of the inputs is asserted. The additional primes need not fail ES. The earlier square-marked ES failures in every interval of externally infinite length persist.

**Stages.** For every triple from a finite rational stage A with m>0 and a,m relatively prime in the UFD A, schedule a fresh polynomial variable Y above the entire current ordered fraction field, in particular above `(H−a)/m` and `(2−a)/m`. The element Q=a+mY is primitive linear over A, hence prime in A[Y] by Gauss's lemma and factoriality. If a=0 then m is an A-unit and this is just an associate of Y. All future stages preserve old prime elements and nonunits (`Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §1). This is an ordinary polynomial stage, not an extra non-square cover or deletion of `Q=0` from the stage variety. Choose Y itself as a basis coordinate. Keep three disjoint types of basis coordinates: square marks, Dirichlet variables, and unmarked coordinates. The expressions for a,m in the basis are rational functions only of earlier variables. Schedule externally over A before specifying R; the final R-inputs are thereby covered.

**Simultaneous embeddings: prefix and tail.** For a Dirichlet variable Y at each ℓ insist Y∈ℤ_ℓ. If both local input values are integral and at least one is a unit, also insist `a+mY≠0 mod ℓ`. No unit condition is imposed for this task at this prime otherwise. All basis coordinates can be chosen integral. The construction cannot simply make arbitrary tail residue choices first and impose the Dirichlet conditions afterwards.

* Enumerate the basis in stage order and all nonzero integer polynomials by any countable enumeration. For level n use a finite **initial segment of the basis** containing the first n coordinates and all variables of the first n polynomials; make segments nested. Add to the finite polynomial avoidance list the denominators for a,m and the numerator of `a+mY` for every Dirichlet variable in that segment, clearing rational constants. These are nonzero (m≠0, Y fresh), only involve this segment, and no zero numerator of a alone need be added. Invert the finitely many scalar denominators. For all sufficiently large ℓ the fixed list has nonzero reductions and its union of zero sets occupies `O_n(ℓ^(d−1))` points in d coordinates. For k square marks in the segment the allowed square residues occupy `((ℓ−1)/2)^k ℓ^(d−k)` points away from their prescribed congruence moduli. Thus a prefix tuple exists where every listed polynomial is nonzero. In particular all prefix Dirichlet outputs have unit value there, independently of the conditional rule. Choose thresholds increasing to infinity so each ℓ handles a finite level tending to infinity with ℓ; if necessary start with an empty prefix.
* At this ℓ choose actual prefix lifts successively in the selected cylinders, each transcendental over the countable field generated over ℚ by the previous values. Use square-unit subcylinders for square marks. The chosen residues ensure the prefix conditions on evaluating rational expressions at these lifts. Then for **each tail coordinate in chronological order**, evaluate old a,m on the actual lifts first. If the local unit condition is active and m is a unit, exclude `−a/m mod ℓ`. Even at ℓ=2 an allowed residue remains. If m is a nonunit then a is a unit and none is forbidden. If the condition is inactive any integral residue is allowed. Square marks are distinct coordinates, and their prescribed finite class and square-unit cylinders are always compatible. Choose each next lift transcendental over the previous field in the allowed open set. This gives joint algebraic independence over ℚ at every ℓ. Each nonzero integer polynomial is eventually in every handled list, so all nonzero rational functions still have valuation zero almost everywhere. No assertion that valuations of a polynomial vanish at *every* prime is needed.

**Return to R.** Form the adelic pullback R as in `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §2; its PID and floor arguments use just this finite valuation support, the embeddings, and the rational stage construction. Let now `a,m,H∈R`, m>0 and `(a,m)=1` in R. At any finite common stage no nonconstant prime can divide both: it would remain prime in the rational union, dividing the final Bézout identity for 1. At each ℓ the evaluations are integral and at least one is a unit, by that same identity with coefficients in R. Therefore the task applies and its Y∈R and Q=a+mY has valuation **zero at every ℓ**. Q is a permanent rational-stage prime, so Q is prime in R without rational rescaling (which could lose its progression). The exact identity Q−a=mY yields divisibility **in R**. It is positive infinite and >H. For the final exceptional case a=0, necessarily m=1, consistent with this proof.

**NR check.** The Dirichlet tasks change neither the finite-tower geometry nor the arithmetic specialization lemma for a square mark P: the new Y is just a product-line coordinate. The conditional unit rule governs the adelic embeddings, not the ordinary integer specializations. Indeed NR from `Z65_LOGIC2.md` §1 **fails** at P. If there were witnesses `d=4k+3>0`, `x²+P=dy`, `ud+vP=1` in R, mark all of them for integral specialization and pull back by P=t². On the connected real locus t>0, `dy=x²+t²>0` so d never vanishes; since it is positive at the ordered point, it is positive at the integer specialization. There one has a positive integer d≡3 mod 4 coprime to t, and d divides x²+t². Some ordinary prime l≡3 mod 4 divides d, hence l∤t and −1 is a square mod l, impossible. Thus Dirichlet alone, even here with PID and open induction, does not give NR. The classical derivation from Dirichlet chooses a quadratic nonresidue at P and invokes reciprocity to produce a root mod the auxiliary prime; the bare internal progression axiom does not assert those extra steps. This argument does not identify which individual additional reciprocity or nonresidue principles fail. No simultaneous prime-tuple claim, PA independence, or standard-prime ES failure follows.
