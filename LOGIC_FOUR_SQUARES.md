**Question.** The square-specialization method in `Z66_IE1.md`, Thm K, cannot produce an ES countermodel satisfying Lagrange's theorem. Does that exclude *all* such countermodels? No: it excludes the specialization of arbitrary certificates. In the joint PID construction (`LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md`) one can instead preserve ES failure by descending its three positive coordinates at each stage. The following is the additional operation and its proof. No claim about the induction axioms IE₁ or IΔ₀ follows from adding Lagrange's theorem as an axiom to IOpen.

Given a positive infinite element `s` of a rational stage `A` with old ordered field `F=Frac(A)`, set

```
B=A[u₁,u₂,u₃,u₄]/(u₁²+u₂²+u₃²+u₄²−s).
```

This operation does **not** require `s` to be prime, a Gaussian norm, or an adelic unit. Give the first three scaled coordinates `u_j/√s` algebraically independent residues in a small open ball inside the real unit ball (fresh over the countable natural residue field of `F^rc`); set the last to the positive square root of the remaining expression. This is an order embedding. Indeed use the unique normal form of degree at most one in `u₄`. After scaling, divide the resulting expression by the largest absolute value of its coefficients, each coefficient including its factor `(√s)^degree`. At the residues the two blocks become `f(z₁,z₂,z₃)+√(1−z₁²−z₂²−z₃²)g(z₁,z₂,z₃)`, with `f,g` not both zero. They cannot cancel: the polynomial under the radical is squarefree and nonsquare in the residue rational function field. Thus any monomial of positive degree with nonzero old coefficient makes the value infinite (nonzero old stage elements are noninfinitesimal). Bounded stage elements remain exactly ℚ.

**The descent.** On the generic fibre, filter by total degree. Its associated graded ring is `F[u₁,…,u₄]/(Σu_j²)` since the leading ideal of the principal quadric relation is generated by its quadratic part. This is a domain: a quadratic of rank four is not a product of two linear forms, even over the algebraic closure. Hence degrees of nonzero products add and define the negative of a valuation at infinity on the function field. Degree zero on the coordinate ring consists only of the old constants `F`. If `Σ1/x_j=4/n` with `x_j∈B` positive and `n>0` old, take residues at this valuation. A nonconstant `x_j` has strictly positive degree, so `1/x_j` disappears. Constants already have reciprocal sum exactly `4/n` (the residue field contains `F` faithfully). Returning to the given ordering, **every omitted summand would be strictly positive**, so no term was omitted. Monic normal form gives `B∩F=A`, proving descent to the old ring. This is an identity of functions, not a statement about the number of integral points on an individual sphere.

**Factoriality and primes.** Over the Gaussian stage `C=A[i]`, put `U=u₁+iu₂,V=u₁−iu₂`; the equation is `UV=s−u₃²−u₄²`. The right side is prime in `C[u₃,u₄]`: monic division embeds its quotient into the generic conic algebra, which is geometrically integral because `s≠0` in the field (over an algebraic closure the curve is `xy=s`). Thus `U` is prime and inverting it yields a Laurent polynomial UFD; Nagata gives factoriality of `B[i]` and its only units are Gaussian rational constants. The finite étale Gaussian descent by Hilbert 90 on constants in `LOGIC_CIRCLE_AUDIT.md` gives factoriality of `B`. For *each* old rational prime `r`, `B/rB` is a nonzero domain by monic embedding into the generic quadric over `Frac(A/r)`: also if `r|s` its homogeneous rank-four quadratic is still irreducible. Therefore no old prime has been factored by this operation.

**Integral witnesses.** At any prime `ℓ`, every `a∈ℤ_ℓ` has a four-square representation in `ℤ_ℓ`: sums of four squares form a compact subset containing the nonnegative ordinary integers by the classical theorem, so contain their closure. For `a∈ℚ_ℓ` of negative valuation multiply by an even power of `ℓ` to make it integral before representing it, then scale back. At the image of an actual nonzero stage element the fibre is smooth over `ℚ_ℓ`, including at `ℓ=2` (no assertion of a smooth *reduction* at 2). In the desired integral neighbourhood the local implicit function theorem gives a generic point over the old countable field; taking an open domain for three local parameters and avoiding countably many proper zero sets suffices. Thus in the tree construction always add this step for each old positive infinite element, insisting that its four local coordinates are integral at every mark where the old element is integral. At the good prefixes the generic fibre is geometrically integral and the same finite-product Lang–Weil choice works; it does not prescribe the already fixed local values a second time. At the finitely many uncontrolled tail places use the local procedure just described. The ring is unchanged on bounded integers, for which Lagrange's theorem already holds. Every positive element of the final integral pullback, not just elements at any prescribed standard size, is therefore represented there. Zero has its usual representation.

**Consequence in scope.** Combined with the joint PID construction, a model of open induction can simultaneously have Bézout, internal Dirichlet, universal NR, Fermat's prime two-squares theorem, and Lagrange's four-squares theorem, yet fail ES at a nonstandard prime. The prime is already a norm in the initial two-variable ring; an ES triple there would descend to a polynomial identity on a square class. Adding four-square identities does not produce such a triple. The construction has stopped using the operation ruled out by `Z66_IE1.md`: the four-square witnesses themselves need **not** admit integral odd-square specialization. This is a nonprovability result for the specified axioms, not a disproof of ES in the integers or independence from PA or ZFC.
