# A fixed finite menu fails at ordinary odd prime powers, for every choice of its Pell witnesses

(§0 block: z74 Opus xreview fix)
```
Result: every fixed finite menu of rational ES formulas in r integral negative-Pell witnesses fails at n=p^(2a+1)
        for every prime p≡1 mod M (menu-dependent, 4|M) and all a≥a₀(p); infinitely many such p have ES true at n.
Label: PROVED (unconditional; Dirichlet for the last clause).
Scope: ordinary prime POWERS, not primes; fixed number of integral witnesses; not a statement about ES.
```

**Audit: PROVED.** Fix `r<∞` and a finite menu of triples of rational functions over `ℚ` in `n` and `r` integral solutions `x_i²−n y_i²=−1`. Success means *some* ordered tuple of signed witnesses makes a defined triple a positive integral ES solution. There is a menu-dependent integer `M` divisible by 4 such that for **each** prime `p≥5` with `p≡1 mod M`, all sufficiently large `a` give failure at `n=p^(2a+1)`. The cutoff may depend on `p`. All those `n` admit negative Pell witnesses. An infinite subprogression of primes can additionally be chosen with ES already true at `p`, hence at every such `n`. The conclusion is over ordinary integers, not a theorem of noncoverage at ordinary **prime inputs**. No bound for regulators at varying prime discriminants is assumed.

**1. Exact order in the suborder.** Work in `ℤ[√p]`, not the possibly larger ring of integers. It has a unit `η=x₀+y₀√p>1` generating its positive-real units. Indeed these units are discrete in `ℝ_{>0}` (on a bounded interval away from 0 both embeddings are bounded, hence so are the two integral coordinates); the positive Pell theorem gives nontrivial ones. Norm `−1` is attained for a prime `p≡1 mod 4`: in the least positive norm-one solution `A²−pB²=1`, `A` cannot be even since `A±1` would be coprime odd factors, each a square or `p` times a square, one `3 mod 4`. Otherwise the coprime consecutive `(A±1)/2` have product `p(B/2)²`; putting the square on the larger side gives a smaller nontrivial positive Pell solution, so putting it on the smaller side gives negative Pell. Therefore the generator `η` has norm `−1` and `x₀,y₀>0`. All signed units are `±η^j`, `j∈ℤ`.

Write `Y_j=[√p]η^j` and `w=v_p(y₀)`. For positive `j` the first term of the odd binomial expansion is `j x₀^{j−1} y₀`, with valuation `v_p(j)+w` since `p∤x₀`. At index `2i+1≥3`, use `binom(j,2i+1)=j binom(j−1,2i)/(2i+1)`: the valuation exceeds the first by at least `i+2iw−v_p(2i+1)>0` (`p^i>2i+1` for `p≥5`). No cancellation. Conjugation/inversion gives the same valuation at `−j`. Hence membership in the suborder `ℤ[p^a√p]` is exactly `p^max(0,a−w)|j`, including the trivial exponent separately. Its smallest unit above 1 is

`E=η^(p^max(0,a−w))`.

It still has norm `−1` since this exponent is odd. For fixed `p` and every fixed `C`, `E/n^C→∞` as `a→∞`. **Every** witness `t_i=x_i+√n y_i` is `σ_i E^{j_i}`, `σ_i∈{±1}`, `j_i` odd and possibly negative. This simultaneously proves existence and handles arbitrary signs, including units extremely close to zero. Using the positive norm-one generator instead would miss the odd coset.

**2. Collapse of bounded integer outputs.** Write `s²=n`. The substitutions `x_i=(t_i−t_i⁻¹)/2`, `y_i=(t_i+t_i⁻¹)/(2s)` turn each fixed slot into a quotient of Laurent polynomials in `t₁,…,t_r` with coefficients in `ℤ[s]`, after clearing a common denominator. Use only presentations whose denominator is nonzero at the witness. This is automatic for evaluation of a fixed quotient. If “defined” means anywhere on the regular locus of a rational map, finitely many presentations suffice there: the domain is Noetherian and local quotient presentations cover that open locus. For sorted positive ES slots `d₁≤d₂≤d₃`, clearing gives `d₁≤3n/4`, `d₂≤2n d₁`, `d₃≤n d₁ d₂`; thus *each* output `m` satisfies `1≤m≤2n⁴`, without prespecifying its position.

On putting `t_i=σ_i E^{j_i}` into `F−mG`, partition the finite set of monomial indices `v` by equality of the **integers** `v·j`. For each block `J`, the coefficient at that exponent is `c_J=A_J(s)−m B_J(s)`, where `A_J,B_J` are the corresponding signed subset sums of numerator and denominator coefficients (pad missing terms with zero). There are only finitely many candidate polynomial pairs **before choosing `p`**. For all blocks, `|c_J|,|c_J'|≤n^C` for a fixed `C` and all large `n`, with prime denoting quadratic conjugation. If nonzero then `|c_J|≥n^(−C)` by its nonzero integral norm, regardless of any cancellation within a block. If any nonzero block existed, divide the identity by `E` to the power of its largest nonzero exponent: the remaining terms have total magnitude at most `N n^C/E`, where `N` is the fixed number of monomials. For sufficiently large `a` this is less than `n^(−C)`. Thus *all blocks vanish*. Since `G` is defined and nonzero at the tuple, at least one block has `B_J(√n)≠0`; so `m=A_J(√n)/B_J(√n)`. Equal exponents, opposite exponents, unrelated indices in different slots and choices of branch depending on the witnesses cause no problem. Neither the identity on the surface nor an upper bound for `|j_i|` is needed.

**3. The modulus is noncircular.** Form the finite ratio list in §2 *formally*. Each ratio in `ℚ(s)` has the unique form `u(n)+s v(n)`, `u,v∈ℚ(n)`. For nonsquare integer `n`, rational values require `v(n)=0`, outside the finitely many coefficient poles. Thus a ratio not in `ℚ(n)` has only finitely many eligible `n` (even though along a fixed prime tower the quadratic field is constant). For each remaining `u=H+J` by polynomial division, `H∈ℚ[n]` and `J` proper rational. If `J≠0`, integer values are possible only at finitely many positive integers: eventually `0<|J(n)|<1/D` for a fixed coefficient denominator `D` of `H`. Keep all the surviving polynomials, even ones which will not be realized as slot functions. Choose `M` divisible by 4 and **all** their coefficient denominators, or a multiple thereof such as `lcm(M,840)`. All exceptions so far, other than the growth threshold for `E`, are independent of `p`.

There are only finitely many triples of these polynomials. For a nonidentity triple, its cleared ES equation can hold only at finitely many integers; discard identically zero slots. For an identity triple `H_i`, its positive locus in `(0,∞)` is open and closed: wherever all are positive their reciprocals sum to `4/n`, hence each `H_i(n)≥n/4`, which prevents a zero on the boundary. Thus a single positive success implies `H_i(1)>0` for all `i`. When `p≡1 mod M`, integer values at `p^(2a+1)` imply integer values at 1 by the coefficient congruence. The identity at 1 now yields `4=Σ1/H_i(1)≤3`. This last step does not invoke ET or exclude arbitrary positive ES triples at a square.

**4. Actual ES successes.** Choose any fixed prime `q≡3 mod 4` not dividing `M`; take primes `p≡1 mod M`, `p≡−4 mod q`, of which infinitely many exist by CRT and Dirichlet. With `c=(q+1)/4`, `b=(1+pc)/q` positive integral, `4bc=1+b+pc` gives the ES denominators `(b,cp,bcp)` at `p`. Multiply all three by `p^{2a}`. Thus ES actually holds at these ordinary inputs on which every menu output fails. This does not assert ES at *all* primes `1 mod M`. Extra witnesses other than the fixed number of integral units, arbitrary divisions with remainder, extractions of coordinates from rational inputs of unbounded denominator, and menus growing with `a` are not covered.

**Exact regression, not a proof:** `uv run --offline --no-project python scripts/audit_ramified_pell.py` tests the lifting law at signed indices including ramified base coordinates and tests nontrivial positive and negative exponent collisions in two slots of input.
