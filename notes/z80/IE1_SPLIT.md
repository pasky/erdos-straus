# Splitting every translate without a common landing kernel

## 1. What is forced by parameter-free induction?

**PROVED (rel. the block of `notes/z79/IE1_BASE.md` §4).** Say that a test is *two-sided* over a theory if it and its negation have bounded-existential open-matrix definitions provably equivalent to them there. We use the already established theorems of `IE₁⁻` as base; for open tests no prior induction is needed beyond totality of the unary decoder. In particular the base proves totality of the alternatives `V` and its positive certificate of failure. In `IE₁⁻`, ordinary successor induction **with arbitrary number parameters** holds for every two-sided test `V(i,a)`. This is not full parameterized `IE₁`, strong induction, or least search with parameters. In particular Boolean combinations of polynomial divisibilities and comparisons are covered (quotient/remainder for the negative sign; include the zero-divisor-of-a-number convention `0|x ↔ x=0`).

For clarity, the block is an induction on the one free variable `y=m²+j`, `0≤j≤2m`. Encode the parameter tuple, desired last index `H` and padding in `m` by nested `(s+t)²+s` pairing. Bounded square-root/remainder decodes and a test for invalid code are open after existentially specifying their unique results. Root totality is itself parameter-free `E₁` induction. In a valid block put `i=min(j,H)` and ask for

```
 V(i,a)    or    ¬V(0,a)    or    ∃k<H [ V(k,a) ∧ ¬V(k+1,a) ].                 (1)
```

This is a **single parameter-free `E₁` formula** after expansion; all guards/auxiliaries are polynomially bounded in `1+y`. Invalid code succeeds. At index zero use excluded middle at zero. On a successor carry the exception or use excluded middle at the new index: a newly false test at `i+1≤H` supplies the certified exception `k=i`. At a square start over. Instantiating a sufficiently padded block at `j=H` gives the claimed parameterized induction, even the local counterstep below a supplied missed `H`. The negative step certificate, not just the pairing, is essential. No least counterstep was located.

**Factorial test (PROVED).** Suppose the signed ring of an `IE₁⁻` model contains a signed ordered PA subring `B` which is not initial. Fix `1<y<b` with `y∉B`, `b∈B`; by enlarging `b` arrange `y+ℓ<b` for every *standard* `ℓ`. Let `h=b!` in `B`. For **every** standard `L≥1` there is `t∈R\B`, `1≤t≤y`, such that **all** of `t,t+1,…,t+L−1` divide `h` in `R`. For `V(i)` the conjunction of these `L` divisibilities starting at `i`, apply (1) to the shifted test `V'(i)=V(i+1)` with `H=y−1`: `V'(0)` holds by old factorial divisibility. If it holds at `y`, take `y`. Otherwise some `1≤i<y` passes and `i+1` fails. Such an `i` cannot be in `B`: then every one of `i,…,i+L` is an old positive integer below `b` and divides `h` already in `B`. We use the very same factorial for all these formulas. With `L=1` this forbids keeping all divisors of all PA factorials in `B`. In a factor-conservative extension over `B` the test at `y` fails, yet **there is no certified counterstep** on `[1,y]`: this gives a particular parameter-free induction lock, not merely an unverified necessary condition.

**Geometric consequence (PROVED).** Put `K=Frac B`, `A=(B\{0})⁻¹R`. Then `A* / K*` has infinite rational rank; in fact the subgroup generated by *arithmetic divisors of this single h*, modulo scalars, has infinite rank. Every divisor of `h` becomes a unit. Every `t∈R\B` is transcendental over `K`: `B` is an integer part of its ordered real closure by PA/Shepherdson, so no new algebraic integer of the discretely ordered extension can be inserted in that ordered field (here “integer” means member of `R`, not integral over `B`). In `K(t)` the distinct linear factors `t+j` are multiplicatively independent modulo constants. Arbitrarily large finite independent systems suffice; no infinite string starting at the same `t` is claimed. In particular it does not suffice to preserve scalar factors and split **every** horizontal translate, nor to unfreeze finitely many independent units upstairs. This concerns units over the **cofinal coefficient field**, distinct from the constant multiquadratic height hyperplanes over a PA cut in z77. A PA *initial* subring alone does not trigger it. **Corollary (PROVED):** for a non-initial ordered PA subring `B`, if `((B∖{0})⁻¹R)*=K*`, then `R` fails `IE₁⁻`.

## 2. The arithmetic model: two kinds of factors

**Theorem (PROVED rel. §§3–6).** There are countable signed ordered rings `D ⊂ B ⊂ R`, `D,B` models of PA, `D` nonstandard and initial in both, `B` cofinal but not initial in `R`. The normal discretely ordered `R` satisfies IOpen, signed Bézout, and the full row-coprime (Sep) of z79 §8. There is a ring-theoretic positive prime `P>D`, `P≡1 (d)` for every `d∈D⁺`, with no positive ES triple. All the cofinal-PA applications **1–3** of z79 §3 hold together. This gives, for example, the arithmetic intermediate theory

```
 T = IOpen + Bézout + (Sep) + ∀n W(n;2,3),        T ⊬ ES,        IE₁⁻ ⊢ T,
 W(n;d,a) : ∃u,v≤(2a+1)(n+1) [u≥n ∧ u²=1+dv²].
```

For the last inclusion use §1 on open tests for IOpen, z79 §§4,8 for rows/splitting and the `3+2√2` successor for the window. The subtheory without the window already has a simpler proof: an external IOpen PID in z76 §3 of `QR_TS_BCM.md` satisfies (Sep) by finite prime factorization. That PID is **not** the model here. We obtain the field at every prime, not finite external factorization of every element. No true-universal-induction transfer from `B` is used.

**Idea (Assessment).** Work over the *field* `K=Frac B` for horizontal factors, and maintain arithmetic content by a family of valuations above the **internally indexed** primes of `B`. A content is a single fraction of `B`, not an external product over its primes. Ordinary Gauss content on a new line would introduce transcendental residue even modulo 2. Instead magnify a succession of residue discs, until the content of each individual polynomial stabilizes. The substitutions must be integral simultaneously at a nonstandard number of places. We use CRT **in PA on the coefficients**, never CRT on an externally given arbitrary set of primes. A descending reserve of cuts lets all the magnifications for one task fit *below* its order scale. No unital specialization of the completed arithmetic ring to `B` is used.

**Reserve (PROVED rel. compactness).** Choose a countable `B` with PA elementary end-initial subrings `C₀ ⊋ C₁ ⊋ ⋯`, `B ⊋ C₀`, and a common nonstandard elementary end-initial `D` strictly below all of them. Use predicates, relativized PA, elementary end-inclusion schemes and strictness; each finite list is realized by a finite iteration of MacDowell–Specker starting with nonstandard PA. Compactness and countable downward Löwenheim–Skolem suffice. One can impose `B ≡ ℤ` in signed arithmetic by starting with a nonstandard true-arithmetic model. This argument supplies **some** such diagram, not a prescribed copy of an arbitrary initial `D`. At step `n` the current reserve is `C=C_n`, the next is `C'=C_{n+1}`. Use `J=T!` for `T∈C` above all of `C'`. All powers `J^m` for standard `m` belong to `C`; so do all products of any fixed finite data of `C`. At each `ℓ∈B` prime dividing `J`, `v_ℓ J≥1`; for standard small `ℓ` it exceeds every standard number. Every `d∈C'⁺` divides `J` (in fact `d^k|J` for every standard `k`). Here and below `v_ℓ` on `K` is the internal PA exponent with value group the signed additive group of `B`, **not** a rank-one discrete valuation with standard exponents.

**Invariants (PROVED below).** At a finite step `A` is an affine factorial `K`-algebra with units `K*`, field `E`, with an order extending `K`. Every B-prime valuation extends to `w_ℓ` on `E`. We require

1. For each `x∈E*` there is a positive `c(x)∈K` with `w_ℓ(x)=v_ℓ c(x)` for **all** B-primes. It is unique by PA factorization. Write `R_A={0} ∪ {x∈A\{0}: c(x)∈B}`. Thus `A=(B\{0})⁻¹R_A`. If `c(x)=1` call `x` primitive. This includes primitive fractions, not necessarily in `A`.
2. For an integral-valued field element (`w_ℓ(x)≥0` at every place) and every `d∈C⁺`, there exists `z∈B` such that `w_ℓ(x−z)≥v_ℓ(d)` for `ℓ|d` (valuation of zero is infinity). In particular `R_A/dR_A=B/dB` at these moduli. The assertion is about congruences modulo **powers**, not just geometric residue points.
3. `S=(C\{0})⁻¹R_A` meets the convex hull of `Frac C` in `Frac C`. In the ordered real closure of `E`, relative sizes modulo `Frac C` units are represented by `K` and relative residues embed over `rc(Frac C)` in its real-closed Cauchy host as in z79 §1. Start with `A=K`, `R_A=B`; for the residue embedding approximate bounded elements to each `Frac C` precision by scaling and taking a `B`-floor, which is in `C` when `C`-bounded. `B` also supplies representatives of sizes using floors/inverses.

**Factor calculus (PROVED).** From 1, every `x∈R_A`, up to sign, has a unique divisor description: its positive PA scalar `c(x)` and an ordinary finite multiset of horizontal primes (irreducibles of `A` modulo `K*`). Normalize one generator for each horizontal prime to content 1 by dividing by its scalar, and make it positive. The factorization is literal up to sign because the remaining scalar is a `B`-unit after extracting `c(x)`. Divisibility of positive entries is exactly coordinatewise PA divisibility and multiset inclusion. This gives gcds, *before* installing Bézout rows. At all our edges old horizontal primes stay prime and distinct, contents restrict exactly. The same description therefore works at the union, where “finite” still means standard finite only for the horizontal part. No PA coding of a varying list of horizontal primes.

## 3. Simultaneous dilution lemma (including a row)

**PROVED (relative to 1–2 at the old field).** Extend the field by one independent coordinate. On a free line write it `Y` at every prime. On a row `au+bv=1` with nonzero `a,b∈R_A` of gcd 1 use `Y=u` wherever `w_ℓ(b)=0`, `Y=v` on the other primes (where `w_ℓ(a)=0`). Both are transcendental coordinates for the *same* field. This partition is PA-definable from `c(b)`, even though the ring elements need not be coded in `B`. The other coordinate is integral if the free one is integral. Ordinary Gauss in each selected coordinate, of value zero and transcendental residue over the old valued field, will be called depth zero. There is an extension preserving 1, shrinking the reserve for 2 to `C'`, leaving both row entries / the line coordinate integral, and having this additional property:

```
 any element integral at the final valuations at this step is integral at
 the depth-zero Gauss valuations after multiplication by some J^N (N standard),
 for polynomials in the selected coordinates over the old field.
```

Here and in the proof the polynomial assertion allows two different polynomial expressions for the same element, according to chart. On the line the final ring of polynomials with coefficients in `K` and nonnegative values has, for each polynomial, an actual integral presentation in `B[(Y−s_m)/J^m]` for some standard depth `m` (on a free line).

**Construction.** At depth `m` keep Gauss in `Z=(Y−s_m)/J^m`, using `s₀=0`, `s_{m+1}=s_m+J^m z_m`, `z_m∈B`. Away from `J` this is the original Gauss valuation at all depths. For a polynomial in `Y` the Gauss value at each place is nondecreasing with depth. We can freeze the values of each of the countably many nonzero polynomials in an independent coordinate over `E`, viewed in each chart. More precisely schedule polynomials in **each** of `u` and `v` where appropriate (equivalently schedule the two numerator/denominator expressions of fractions on their two charts, not only globally regular functions). A task for one chart polynomial may choose the digit using all places and impose its conclusion only on that chart's places. Preservation once frozen is independent of all future digits.

Write the scheduled polynomial at depth `m` as `Σ f_i Z^i`. Divide the nonzero coefficients by their common valuation content: choose `γ∈K⁺` whose exponents are `min_i v_ℓ c(f_i)` by PA fractional gcd. Then `g_i=f_i/γ` are integral-valued, at least one of them a unit at each `ℓ`. Use invariant 2 with `J` to get representatives `b_i∈B` modulo `J`. For **all** `ℓ|J` some `b_i` is a unit there. If degree is `e` let `V_e` be the nonzero integer Vandermonde determinant at `0,…,e` (degree zero: 1). We have `v_ℓ V_e < v_ℓ J` for every `ℓ|J`. For each such `ℓ` one of the `e+1` values `Σ b_i j^i` has valuation `≤v_ℓ V_e`: otherwise the adjugate matrix gives a contradiction at a unit coefficient. In PA select the first such `j` for each internal prime power exactly dividing `J` and impose `z_m≡j` modulo that full prime power by **internal** CRT. This requires one internally coded list of integer coefficients and the standard degree, not access by PA to any `w_ℓ` or to the old field. In `Σg_i(z_m+J Z')^i` the constant term has value `≤v_ℓ V_e`, and each positive-degree coefficient has value `≥v_ℓ J`. Thus the constant term is the unique leading residue, even after further integral substitutions. The same holds for constant polynomials without a task. This is also why simply taking `z_m=0` forever would not work for `Y` itself.

For an arbitrary fraction in the new field choose numerator and denominator polynomials over `E` in each chart. After their tasks all four values have stabilized **simultaneously** at all places of the corresponding charts. Their stable differences define valuations: the valuation rules follow by comparing common sufficiently late Gauss valuations for the finitely many expressions in any field identity. All values at a fixed depth of each chart polynomial are PA scalar profiles by taking minima of profiles of its coefficients there. The two profiles patch on the PA prime partition given by `c(b)` to a single fraction of `B`: split numerator and denominator products in their internal factorizations and take the prescribed parts. Explicitly on one polynomial chart at its freezing step let `N=Σ b_i z_m^i`. At `ℓ|J` the frozen value of the normalized polynomial is exactly `v_ℓ N<v_ℓ J`, so the scalar for the *original* polynomial there is `γ·gcd(|N|,J)`. Off `J` take the original depth-zero coefficient minimum; restricting these fractional factors to their designated prime sets in PA gives the common content. Thus 1 holds for the whole field, including fractions for the next step. This argument also shows the Gauss values off `J` are unchanged. For a polynomial of degree `e` in a chart, changing coordinates back from depth `m` can lower the coefficient minimum by at most `me v_ℓ J`, since substitution `Z=(Y−s_m)/J^m` has integral numerator. This gives the `J^N` bound for polynomials in the two charts. On a free line over `K` at a common stabilizing depth, nonnegative value at all primes says **every** coefficient in `Z` is integral in `B`.

**Full-modulus reduction at the new reserve.** Fix `d∈C'⁺`. For a single nonzero chart polynomial as above take a freezing step, and first divide by the scalar `γ` used there. At primes dividing `d` its frozen value `e_ℓ` lies between 0 and `v_ℓ V_e`. Divide again by the scalar with these exponents on `ℓ|d` and zero elsewhere (computed from the scalar profile of the frozen polynomial there). In the resulting expression all nonconstant terms contain a factor of valuation at least `v_ℓ J−v_ℓ V_e ≥ v_ℓ d` for `ℓ|d`: `d V_e|J` since `C'` is an initial PA cut. The constant term, after this division, is a unit on the selected places. It has a **single B-residue modulo d there**: a field element integral only on a specified PA subset of `ℓ|d` may be made globally integral by multiplying by the part of its scalar denominator supported *outside* that subset, invertible modulo the corresponding part of `d`. Apply 2 in the old field to this integral multiple and invert by PA CRT. This proves the required reduction for each polynomial normalized by its positive scalar profile on the selected places. Restoring any normalization changes only by a scalar unit at those places. For a field fraction apply this statement to the numerator and denominator: after normalization the denominator's residue is a unit at each selected place. Their residual quotient and the ratio of their scalar contents compute the residue of any integral-valued fraction, including a fraction with excess numerator valuation. Patch at the two PA parts of `d` by PA CRT. This proves 2 at `C'`. In particular the construction deals with all translated inputs, not just values of `Y` whose first digit was nonzero.

## 7. The least-bad-prime question and reciprocity

**Parameter-free least descent (PROVED).** For a genuinely unary parameter-free `E₁` hit `G(x)`, `IE₁⁻` proves that if it has a hit then it has a least hit. Here is a proof without using parameterized least induction. Suppose globally every hit has a smaller one. Pack `s` **and a certificate of `G(s)`** in the block; openly invalid certificates pass. At index `j≤s` require `∃r≤s−j G(r)`. At 0 use `s`; on a successor, either keep the witness or lower the positive last permissible one by the *global* descent hypothesis. Freeze the index at `s` thereafter. Decoding at depth `s` forces `G(0)`, impossible under that hypothesis. This cannot be used with a single fixed foreign parameter carried by `G`: then other blocks might not have the same descent hypothesis. In particular it does not imply `L(Div)` with arbitrary parameters.

**PROVED (rel. Smith, NDJFL 1993, §§2–3).** At a fixed standard odd prime modulus, exact finite residue tables give Euler's criterion (fixed exponent), both squareclasses and the least nonresidue already by division. For distinct primes `p,q` with **fixed standard odd `p`**, the *one direction* of Smith Thm 3.11 also holds in `IE₁⁻`: if `p` is a square mod `q`, then `(q/p)=+1` except for two `3 mod 4` primes, where `(q/p)=−1`. Indeed the only minimizations in Smith Lemmas 3.2–3.10 here are of unary parameter-free `E₁` sets: a finite table on `m mod 24,8,4` or `m mod p` and a bounded root of the fixed numerator modulo `m`. Use quotient/remainder witnesses for the residue table including its negative entries, and the unary least lemma. The other steps of the cited proofs are standard-input checks, complementing a root `b` by `m−b`, and the stated lower-modulus congruences for a cofactor of `b²−p` or of the fixed supplement polynomial; they need division and the displayed finite table, not arbitrary-parameter minimization. Compare the archived text `data/z66/ie1/smith1993_NDJFL_quadres_fulltext.md`, especially the direction of the implication in §3. No converse producing roots, arbitrary-prime Euler exponentiation, general QR, or nonresidue at each arbitrary prime.

**PROVED (prime reduction, not ES).** Universal ES is equivalent over `IE₁⁻` to ES on the primes. Use polynomial bounds, e.g. for a sorted positive triple `x≤3n/4, y≤2nx, z≤nxy` (so a guard `2(n+1)^4` suffices; one need not assume every `z≤n²`). Assume *globally* ES at each prime. In the unary block for `s≥2`, at depth `j` exhibit ES at `s` or a divisor `r≥2` of `s` with `r+j≤s` (freeze at `j=s`). Initialize at `r=s`. If necessary split a composite witness into two factors >1 and keep the smaller; if irreducible then it is prime by Bézout and has an ES witness by hypothesis. Scale its three denominators by `s/r` and use the polynomial bounds afresh. No primality test in the induction matrix. This gives ES at all `s`. Contrapositively *some* prime fails in every `IE₁⁻` model with a failure. It need not be the named generic prime, or a definable element by this proof.

**Assessment (invalid least-counterexample shortcut).** Failure `n≥2 ∧ ¬ES(n)` is `U₁`, not a certified `E₁` hit. The initial-success-segment formula `∀2≤m≤n ES(m)` has a bounded universal followed by the existential witness block. The number of denominators in a certificate for *each individual* `m` is fixed; concatenating a table for nonstandard `n` is not a polynomially bounded tuple of a fixed number of integers. Neither unary least descent nor (1) makes that formula `E₁`. Even for parameterized induction Wilmers distinguished `LE₁` from the *additional* `LU₁` question (1985 open-problem quotation in `data/z66/ie1/lit_ie1.md` §1); it would be a mistake to cite `IE₁=LE₁` as `IE₁⁻ ⊢ LU₁⁻`. This is a gap analysis, not a separation theorem for the latter schemes. With an actual least failure the scaling argument makes it prime, and its unique definition places it in definable closure; even then not **every** failing prime need be fixed. If failure had a parameter-free positive `E₁` characterization under a given global hypothesis, the unary least lemma would apply under that hypothesis. No such characterization or inductive ES step is obtained here. The existence of an unbounded progression of primes at even one fixed standard modulus would imply unbounded primes, an old problem already for `IΔ₀` (PWW, see `data/z66/ie1/lit_idelta.md`). Do not confuse this with supplying a standard prime in every *standardly* truncated progression.

 A' = A[x₀,x₁,x₂,x₃]/(Σxᵢ²−s).

### 9.2 Simultaneous avoidance on small quaternion rotations

**PROVED (approximate centres).** Work in the henselization of each old valued field at `ℓ|J`; no completeness, archimedean value group or choice of a simultaneous algebraic field extension is required. A system of tuples at these places will be called approximable if all its local coordinates are integral and, for each standard `N≥1`, one tuple in `B` agrees with the local tuples modulo `J^N` simultaneously at those places. This holds for globally integral old field coefficients by invariant 2 at reserve `C` since `J^N∈C`; it holds on PA partitions by CRT as in §3. Sums, products and division by units at the selected places preserve this property. Also, to divide by an element of known valuation bounded by `k v_ℓ J` for standard `k`, use `k` extra digits for the numerator and denominator (or more than `2k` extra) and PA division of the unit parts of their scalar representatives on each prime power. This last observation is used only for integral quotients and with the bound holding at every selected place.

There is an approximable centre `U` with exact norm `s` at each place. Take `a>0` in `B` representing `s` modulo `J⁴`, and a PA four-square vector of norm `a`. Each `ℓ|J` has `v_ℓ a≤1`, so some coordinate is a unit; select its index by a PA partition. Hold the other three fixed and lift the unit coordinate by Hensel, since the error has valuation `≥4v_ℓ J >2v_ℓ(2)` and the chosen derivative has valuation `w_ℓ(2)`. Choose the root on this Hensel branch. It is approximable to every `J^N` uniformly: Newton corrections preserve a unit coordinate, and if the error has value `r` the next error has value at least `2r−2v_ℓ(2)` (infinite error valuation needs no correction). Thus standardly many iterations attain `J^N` precision. Each iterate uses old coefficients, is integral on its PA part and admits a simultaneous `B` approximation there by 2 / the partition argument; the finitely many parts patch. No reduction of `s` all the way into `B` by a ring homomorphism was needed.

For a normalized polynomial `F`, in monic form of total degree `e`, assume all old coefficients integral-valued and of **joint minimum zero at every place** (divide through by their PA fractional gcd). Fix a centre `U` as above at some step. For vector `T` of three variables let

```
 Q(T)= (1−T·T, 2T)/(1+T·T),           U_new = U Q(J^k t) ,        k≥1
```

using ordinary quaternion multiplication (scalar coordinate first). These rotations preserve norm, integrality and approximability at `ℓ|J` when `t∈B³`: the denominator is a unit there and the coordinate change has value `≥k v_ℓ J`. For fixed `k` and `F` we can choose **one** `t∈B³` for which

```
         0 ≤ w_ℓ F(U_new) < M v_ℓ J    for all ℓ|J,                (3)
```

for an ordinary `M=M(e,k)` independent of centre and place. Here the value is taken on the actual evaluated polynomial; no parameter-count heuristic on a residual quadric suffices.

**Proof of uniformity in (3).** Replace `x` by `UG` on the unit quadric `N(G)=1`, reducing to monic normal form. This is a linear map on polynomials of degree at most `e` and does not increase degree. The inverse replaces `G` by `conjugate(U)x/s` and reduces using `N(x)=s`; its coefficient denominators are at worst `s^e`, the reductions integral. Therefore some coefficient in `F(UG)` has value `≤e w_ℓ(s)≤e`, since a coefficient before substitution had value zero. On the unit quadric the linear map obtained by substituting `Q(T)` and multiplying by `(1+T·T)^e` is a **fixed standard integer matrix** on these monic coordinates, injective over ℚ (stereographic map with rational inverse `G_vec/(1+G₀)`). A nonzero maximal column-rank minor `D_e` bounds the increase in the minimum coefficient value by `v_ℓ D_e` via its adjugate. Its output has degree ≤`2e`. Scaling `T=J^k t` increases the minimum by at most `2ek v_ℓ J`. Now use the tensor grid `t∈{0,…,2e}³`: a fixed nonzero integer tensor-Vandermonde determinant bounds the further loss in the minimum over grid values. Since `D_e` and the grid determinant are fixed standard nonzero integers, their valuations plus `e` are ≤ an ordinary constant times `v_ℓ J` at each `ℓ|J`. This proves a uniform strict upper bound in (3) for at least one grid value **at each** place. All grid values have simultaneous `B` representatives to precision `J^M` by approximability. In PA, for each prime power of `J` select the first grid tuple whose representative is not zero modulo that prime power to the power `M`. CRT patch their three coordinates to that same precision. Values and inequalities (3) are retained; the denominator of the rotation is a unit. When `e=0` the normalized polynomial is already a unit locally. This treatment includes dyadic depths, singular reductions and nonstandard B-primes, without counting points over an arbitrary residue field.

**Valuation extension and (2) (PROVED).** Schedule all nonzero polynomials in monic normal form over `E`, normalized as above. At step `i` apply (3) at `k_i`, then pick `k_{i+1}>max(k_i,M_i)` so every subsequent rotation preserves its value/leading residue, since coordinates move by at least `k_{i+1}v_ℓ J` and normalized coefficients are integral. Thus the stable values give a valuation on the fraction field (nonzero monic forms had finite frozen value after normalization). If one desires residue precision `J^N` rather than just the frozen value, take any later stage with `k_{i+1}>N+M_i`; all subsequent changes at those places vanish to that precision before dividing by the frozen scalar part. ## 11. A height test on each individual primary axis of a prospective BCM repair

**PROVED (rel. z79 §5's primary-hit lemma).** In *any* discretely ordered `IOpen + Bézout + BCM + L(Div)` ring, for **each** positive prime `p` and `x≥1` there is `u` with

 x < u ≤ px,       every divisor d>1 of u is divisible by p.                (PWdiv)

This is not the Pell test PW and does not assume Lag, TS, QR, ES failure or a PA subring. Call such a positive integer (allowing 1) *pure at p*. If a pure `s` lay strictly between `x` and `u`, it would divide `u` and then `t`.

**Group and height consequences (PROVED).** The pure integers form a multiplicative monoid `M_p`: a divisor `g` of a product `ab`, prime to `p`, has `(g,a)=1` when `a` is pure, so `g|b` and `g=1` when `b` is pure. Start with the countable reserve diagram of §2 of true arithmetic and let `B₀` be its top PA ring. Take an elementary extension of the whole diagram inserting a new `δ` strictly between `a/2` and `a` for one fixed infinite positive integer `a` of its bottom PA predicate, with `δ` different from **each** element of `B₀`. Any finite subset of this type is realized in the original interval, so compactness applies; take the extension countable. In the extended top ring take the signed convex part bounded in absolute value by members of the diagonal `B₀`, calling this `B₁`. It is elementary in the extended arithmetic reduct: for each fixed arithmetic formula the least-witness function (0 if absent) is PA definable and has a maximum on every internally bounded box, with the bound on its values computable *by the same PA definition* from the box endpoints. For endpoints in `B₀` that bound is in `B₀` by elementarity. Tarski–Vaught applies. In particular `B₀≺B₁`. Truncate the reserve predicates similarly. Their inclusions and PA elementarity survive: each subpredicate was end-initial and for a proper old reserve there is an old `B₀` bound above its entire extension (choose an old point in a higher predicate but outside the lower one). These lower predicates in fact need no truncation. `δ` lies in the extended bottom PA cut, still nonstandard, `δ∉B₀`. Use this flag to construct §9 with top coefficient ring `B₁` and bottom cut the extended bottom predicate. The former arithmetic, valuation and ES arguments all apply to this actual coefficient field, not to a scalar-content computation over `Frac B₀`.

For `y∈B₁\B₀`, take `t=y`. For positive `y∉B₁` the cofinal approximation lemma gives `v∈B₁` strictly between `2y/3` and `3y/4` (apply it to `2y/3` with precision `<1/8`). Since `y` exceeds the extended bottom cut, `δ<y/8`, so both `v,v+δ` lie in `(y/2,y)`. At least one is outside `B₀`. In both cases the claimed divisibilities hold by PA in `B₁`, since `b!` there agrees with the original computation in `B₀`. Relative to `Frac B₀` these are transcendental coordinates by discreteness as in §1, and all finite subsets of the linear factors give independent units there. All positive and negative ring-language conclusions of §9 are retained, including no least failure conditional on standard ES. This is a single model, not a union of the conclusions of z76 and z79.

**Ladder: an actual positive reduction fragment (PROVED).** In this same model all positive definite forms with coefficients in `B₁` are properly reducible over `R` by PA, with the full `|B'|≤A'≤C'` inequalities. More intrinsically, any form in their `SL₂(R)`-orbits is properly reducible, even if none of its three coefficients is scalar. This is not an argument for *arbitrary* forms. Independently in this same ring the Gelfand/CRT separation, stable range one, primary isolation, continuum of minimal primes over some nonzero principal ideal, and conditional Hurwitz **existence** all hold by §§6,9–10. The still-unverified assertion from z79 §9 is full proper reduction; the first *proved failure* among the §1 factorial-string assertions is the one for the true coefficient ring `B₁` (not for `B₀`). The general supplied-unit PW and two-sided factorial induction definitely fail. Absence of a verification is not a counterexample to Gauss reduction. The flag answers a quantifier test (one named PA subring versus every non-initial PA subring), not an advance in the induction scheme satisfied by the arithmetic reduct.

## 14. Cofactor reachability at two moduli of the same model

**PROVED (model-specific).** Read `notes/z80/ES_JUGULAR.md` Thm A (`side-agent/z80-es-jugular`, commit 93e2729db) literally: vertices are the nonsquares among `0<x<P` and arrows are positive divisors `d|P+x` that are vertices. At §9's original `P` the least vertex is 2, and **every odd vertex has a path of length one to it**, simply because `2|(P+x)`. No class-character multiplicativity, coding of a long path, or orbit-finiteness is needed. More generally this holds at any odd prime in a discretely ordered ring with division where 2 is a nonsquare. In fact at this `P` the full reachability criterion holds with at most two arrows. For even `x`, a factorization `P+x=ef` with `e,f>1` has both factors odd and `<P`, and at least one is nonsquare since their product is `x mod P`. Take it as an intermediate vertex. If `P+x` is prime there is no out-arrow. The zero-length exception `x=2` creates no extra case here because `3|P+2` and `P+2>3`. The graph on squares versus *all other classes* at our `P` is **not** the graph of a two-class character: `2,3,6` are all nonsquare by §10. Thus the nontrivial part of the cited Theorem A at ordinary primes `p≡1 (8)`, where 2 *is* square, has not been formalized by this observation. Nor does 2 have to have an out-arrow just because it is the least vertex; paths of length zero have their ordinary graph-theoretic meaning.

**PROVED (conditional graph lemma within the ordinary ring language).** For any two nonresidue vertices `x,q` at an odd prime `p`, if `p+x` has a nonresidue divisor `s<p` with `q|p+s`, then there is a positive path of length at most two from `x` to `q`. This just uses the two actual divisibility witnesses. It is useful to distinguish this first-order fixed-length assertion from either an external finite path (union over all standard lengths) or a specified coding of paths of possibly nonstandard length. A proof by finite doubling cycles does not decide either latter assertion in §2's `T = IOpen + Bézout + (Sep) + IW₂`. For the *external finite-path* interpretation there is in fact a counterexample, even with 2 square, as follows.

**Theorem (PROVED rel. §5 seed and §§6,9–10 persistence).** In the very same §9 model, alongside the ES-failing `P=1+JX`, consider the **different modulus**

```
 π = P²−8J²,      x_j = π − 3J²/2^j      (j=0,1,2,… ordinary).
```

Then `π≡1 (8)` is prime, 2 is a square mod `π` and the least positive nonsquare mod `π` is 3. The `x_j` are distinct increasing odd nonsquares in `(3,π)`. The reachable vertices from `x₀` by **externally finite** positive cofactor paths are exactly the `x_j`, so there is no such path to 3. In particular no fixed standard bound for these paths can be proved from §2's `T`, even with the cut, windows, Lag and internal Dirichlet. Such uniform bounds might well fail in ℕ too (Assessment); the gain here is unconditional failure of *external finite reachability*, without any Dickson/Schinzel prime-tuples hypothesis. Neither ES failure at `π` nor exactly two squareclasses there is asserted.

In `K[X]` the modulus is a quadratic prime since `8` is nonsquare. Each `x_j` is also a quadratic prime: `8+3/2^j` is not a square in `K`. For odd `j` its 2-adic order is odd; for even `j`, multiplying by the square `2^j` gives `8·2^j+3≡3 mod 4`, which cannot be square even in `Frac B` (a rational square integral in `B` is an integer square by PA coprimality). Coefficients `J²/2^j` are in `B` and divisible by every prime of `J` for every standard `j`, including at 2 since `v₂J` is infinite. Hence all these quadratics have residue 1 at primes over `J`, and coefficient minimum zero at other places by the seed Gauss rule and the unit leading coefficient there. They are therefore primitive positive arithmetic primes by the factor calculus; the old-horizontal-prime persistence in §§2,4,9.1 covers every later edge. Positivity and `x_j>3` use `X>C₀`, not a guess about the order of the roots of the quadratics at their fibres.

The base horizontal field at `π` is `K(√2)` (`P/(2J)` is a root of 2). It remains relatively algebraically closed in the directed-union fibre by exactly §10's geometric-integrality proof, including the rank-four cone. In this formally real coefficient field neither 3 nor any negative scalar is a square: for 3, square `a+b√2`, compare coefficients and exclude `3` and `3/2` as scalar squares by `v₃`. Since `2=(√2)²`, `J²/2^j=(J/(√2)^j)²` in this field; thus `x_j≡−3J²/2^j` has the nonsquare class of `−3`, as required. Finally `π+x_j=2x_{j+1}`. The only positive divisors are `1,2,x_{j+1},2x_{j+1}` by Bézout and primality, and among these only `x_{j+1}` is an allowed nonsquare vertex. External induction on ordinary path length gives the assertion. Here `x₀` is odd **and** `π+x₀` is composite, so both sufficient conditions of the cited Theorem A apply syntactically. It is the cycle step, before its gap-covering argument, that fails externally: this is exactly its odd first-return halving map, with `ν₂(π+x_j)=1` at every standard step and no finite return.

**Assessment (logic of the counterexample).** For each standard length bound the disjunction over those lengths is an ordinary first-order predicate refuted here. Existence of a path with *some externally finite* length is a semantic countable disjunction and is false here. A specific formula asserting an *arithmetically coded*, internally finite path of possibly nonstandard length is a third meaning and is **not refuted** by the chain. The chain does not establish a negative-character counterexample for a residue field with exactly two squareclasses; the vertices in this statement are all nonsquares. Conversely if one wants a multiplicative sign merely selecting some nonsquares, the class of 3 and that of `−1` are independent in `K(√2)` modulo squares (use its order and the above nonsquareness of 3) and remain so at `π`. An external character with sign `+` at `−1` and `−` at 3 exists by extending a functional over `𝔽₂`; it puts `2` in the kernel and all `x_j` in its negative part. The same missing finite path holds on that external character graph, **not** an internal Legendre law with squares as kernel.
