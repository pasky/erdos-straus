# Splitting every translate without a common landing kernel

## 1. What is forced by parameter-free induction?

**PROVED (rel. the block of `notes/z79/IE1_BASE.md` §4).** Say that a test is *two-sided* if both it and its negation are provably bounded existential with open matrices. In `IE₁⁻`, ordinary successor induction **with arbitrary number parameters** holds for every two-sided test `V(i,a)`. This is not full parameterized `IE₁`, strong induction, or least search with parameters. In particular Boolean combinations of polynomial divisibilities and comparisons are covered (quotient/remainder for the negative sign; include the zero-divisor-of-a-number convention `0|x ↔ x=0`).

For clarity, the block is an induction on the one free variable `y=m²+j`, `0≤j≤2m`. Encode the parameter tuple, desired last index `H` and padding in `m` by nested `(s+t)²+s` pairing. Bounded square-root/remainder decodes and a test for invalid code are open after existentially specifying their unique results. Root totality is itself parameter-free `E₁` induction. In a valid block put `i=min(j,H)` and ask for

```
 V(i,a)    or    ¬V(0,a)    or    ∃k<H [ V(k,a) ∧ ¬V(k+1,a) ].                 (1)
```

This is a **single parameter-free `E₁` formula** after expansion; all guards/auxiliaries are polynomially bounded in `1+y`. Invalid code succeeds. At index zero use excluded middle at zero. On a successor carry the exception or use excluded middle at the new index: a newly false test at `i+1≤H` supplies the certified exception `k=i`. At a square start over. Instantiating a sufficiently padded block at `j=H` gives the claimed parameterized induction, even the local counterstep below a supplied missed `H`. The negative step certificate, not just the pairing, is essential. No least counterstep was located.

**Factorial test (PROVED).** Suppose the signed ring of an `IE₁⁻` model contains a signed ordered PA subring `B` which is not initial. Fix `1<y<b` with `y∉B`, `b∈B`; by enlarging `b` arrange `y+ℓ<b` for every *standard* `ℓ`. Let `h=b!` in `B`. For **every** standard `L≥1` there is `t∈R\B`, `1≤t≤y`, such that **all** of `t,t+1,…,t+L−1` divide `h` in `R`. Apply (1), starting at 1, to the conjunction of these `L` divisibilities at `i`. If it holds at `y`, take `y`. Otherwise some `1≤i<y` passes and `i+1` fails. Such an `i` cannot be in `B`: then every one of `i,…,i+L` is an old positive integer below `b` and divides `h` already in `B`. We use the very same factorial for all these formulas. With `L=1` this forbids keeping all divisors of all PA factorials in `B`. In a factor-conservative extension over `B` the test at `y` fails, yet **there is no certified counterstep** on `[1,y]`: this gives a particular parameter-free induction lock, not merely an unverified necessary condition.

**Geometric consequence (PROVED).** Put `K=Frac B`, `A=(B\{0})⁻¹R`. Then `A* / K*` has infinite rational rank; in fact the subgroup generated by *arithmetic divisors of this single h*, modulo scalars, has infinite rank. Every divisor of `h` becomes a unit. Every `t∈R\B` is transcendental over `K`: `B` is an integer part of its ordered real closure by PA/Shepherdson, so no new algebraic integer of the discretely ordered extension can be inserted in that ordered field (here “integer” means member of `R`, not integral over `B`). In `K(t)` the distinct linear factors `t+j` are multiplicatively independent modulo constants. Arbitrarily large finite independent systems suffice; no infinite string starting at the same `t` is claimed. In particular it does not suffice to preserve scalar factors and split **every** horizontal translate, nor to unfreeze finitely many independent units upstairs. This concerns units over the **cofinal coefficient field**, distinct from the constant multiquadratic height hyperplanes over a PA cut in z77. A PA *initial* subring alone does not trigger it.

## 2. The arithmetic model: two kinds of factors

**Theorem (PROVED rel. §§3–6).** There are countable signed ordered rings `D ⊂ B ⊂ R`, `D,B` models of PA, `D` nonstandard and initial in both, `B` cofinal but not initial in `R`. The normal discretely ordered `R` satisfies IOpen, signed Bézout, and the full row-coprime (Sep) of z79 §8. There is a ring-theoretic positive prime `P>D`, `P≡1 (d)` for every `d∈D⁺`, with no positive ES triple. All the cofinal-PA applications **1–3** of z79 §3 hold together. This gives, for example, the arithmetic intermediate theory

```
 T = IOpen + Bézout + (Sep) + ∀n W(n;2,3),        T ⊬ ES,        IE₁⁻ ⊢ T,
 W(n;d,a) : ∃u,v≤(2a+1)(n+1) [u≥n ∧ u²=1+dv²].
```

For the last inclusion use z79 §§4,8 and the `3+2√2` successor. The subtheory without the window already has a simpler proof: an external IOpen PID in z76 §3 of `QR_TS_BCM.md` satisfies (Sep) by finite prime factorization. That PID is **not** the model here. We obtain the field at every prime, not finite external factorization of every element. No true-universal-induction transfer from `B` is used.

**Idea (Assessment).** Work over the *field* `K=Frac B` for horizontal factors, and maintain arithmetic content by a family of valuations above the **internally indexed** primes of `B`. A content is a single fraction of `B`, not an external product over its primes. Ordinary Gauss content on a new line would introduce transcendental residue even modulo 2. Instead magnify a succession of residue discs, until the content of each individual polynomial stabilizes. The substitutions must be integral simultaneously at a nonstandard number of places. We use CRT **in PA on the coefficients**, never CRT on an externally given arbitrary set of primes. A descending reserve of cuts lets all the magnifications for one task fit *below* its order scale. No unital specialization of the completed arithmetic ring to `B` is used.

**Reserve (PROVED rel. compactness).** Choose a countable `B` with PA elementary end-initial subrings `C₀ ⊋ C₁ ⊋ ⋯`, `B ⊋ C₀`, and a common nonstandard elementary end-initial `D` strictly below all of them. Use predicates, relativized PA, elementary end-inclusion schemes and strictness; each finite list is realized by a finite iteration of MacDowell–Specker starting with nonstandard PA. Compactness and countable downward Löwenheim–Skolem suffice. One can impose `B ≡ ℤ` in signed arithmetic by starting with a nonstandard true-arithmetic model. This argument supplies **some** such diagram, not a prescribed copy of an arbitrary initial `D`. At step `n` the current reserve is `C=C_n`, the next is `C'=C_{n+1}`. Use `J=T!` for `T∈C` above all of `C'`. All powers `J^m` for standard `m` belong to `C`; so do all products of any fixed finite data of `C`. At each `ℓ∈B` prime dividing `J`, `v_ℓ J≥1`; for standard small `ℓ` it exceeds every standard number. Every `d∈C'⁺` divides `J` (in fact `d^k|J` for every standard `k`). Here and below `v_ℓ` on `K` is the internal PA exponent with value group the signed additive group of `B`, **not** a rank-one discrete valuation with standard exponents.

**Invariants (PROVED below).** At a finite step `A` is an affine factorial `K`-algebra with units `K*`, field `E`, with an order extending `K`. Every B-prime valuation extends to `w_ℓ` on `E`. We require

1. For each `x∈E*` there is a positive `c(x)∈K` with `w_ℓ(x)=v_ℓ c(x)` for **all** B-primes. It is unique by PA factorization. Write `R_A={0} ∪ {x∈A\{0}: c(x)∈B}`. Thus `A=(B\{0})⁻¹R_A`. If `c(x)=1` call `x` primitive. This includes primitive fractions, not necessarily in `A`.
2. For an integral-valued field element (`w_ℓ(x)≥0` at every place) and every `d∈C⁺`, there exists `z∈B` such that `w_ℓ(x−z)≥v_ℓ(d)` for `ℓ|d` (valuation of zero is infinity). In particular `R_A/dR_A=B/dB` at these moduli. The assertion is about congruences modulo **powers**, not just geometric residue points.
3. `S=(C\{0})⁻¹R_A` meets the convex hull of `Frac C` in `Frac C`. In the ordered real closure of `E`, relative sizes modulo `Frac C` units are represented by `K` and relative residues embed over `rc(Frac C)` in its real-closed Cauchy host as in z79 §1. Start with `A=K`, `R_A=B`; for the residue embedding approximate bounded elements to each `Frac C` precision by scaling and taking a `B`-floor, which is in `C` when `C`-bounded. `B` also supplies representatives of sizes using floors/inverses.

**Factor calculus (PROVED).** From 1, every `x∈R_A`, up to sign, has a unique divisor description: its positive PA scalar `c(x)` and an ordinary finite multiset of horizontal primes (irreducibles of `A` modulo `K*`). Normalize one generator for each horizontal prime to content 1 by dividing by its scalar, and make it positive. The factorization is literal up to sign because the remaining scalar is a `B`-unit after extracting `c(x)`. Divisibility of positive entries is exactly coordinatewise PA divisibility and multiset inclusion. This gives gcds, *before* installing Bézout rows. At all our edges old horizontal primes stay prime and distinct, contents restrict exactly. The same description therefore works at the union, where “finite” still means standard finite only for the horizontal part. No PA coding of a varying list of horizontal primes.

## 3. Simultaneous dilution lemma (including a row)

**PROVED (relative to 1–2 at the old field).** Extend the field by one independent coordinate. On a free line write it `Y` at every prime. On a row `au+bv=1` with nonzero `a,b∈R_A` of gcd 1 use `Y=u` wherever `w_ℓ(b)=0`, `Y=v` on the other primes (where `w_ℓ(a)=0`). Both are transcendental coordinates for the *same* field. This partition is PA-definable from `c(b)`, even though the ring elements need not be coded in `B`. The other coordinate is integral if the free one is integral. Ordinary Gauss in each selected coordinate, of value zero and transcendental residue over the old valued field, will be called depth zero. There is an extension preserving 1, advancing 2 to `C'`, leaving both row entries / the line coordinate integral, and having this additional property:

```
 any element integral at the final valuations at this step is integral at
 the depth-zero Gauss valuations after multiplication by some J^N (N standard),
 for polynomials in the selected coordinates over the old field.
```

Here and in the proof the polynomial assertion allows two different polynomial expressions for the same element, according to chart. On the line the final ring of polynomials with coefficients in `K` and nonnegative values has, for each polynomial, an actual integral presentation in `B[(Y−s_m)/J^m]` for some standard depth `m` (on a free line).

**Construction.** At depth `m` keep Gauss in `Z=(Y−s_m)/J^m`, using `s₀=0`, `s_{m+1}=s_m+J^m z_m`, `z_m∈B`. Away from `J` this is the original Gauss valuation at all depths. For a polynomial in `Y` the Gauss value at each place is nondecreasing with depth. We can freeze the values of each of the countably many nonzero polynomials in an independent coordinate over `E`, viewed in each chart. More precisely schedule polynomials in **each** of `u` and `v` where appropriate (equivalently schedule the two numerator/denominator expressions of fractions on their two charts, not only globally regular functions). A task for one chart polynomial may choose the digit using all places and impose its conclusion only on that chart's places. Preservation once frozen is independent of all future digits.

Write the scheduled polynomial at depth `m` as `Σ f_i Z^i`. Divide the nonzero coefficients by their common valuation content: choose `γ∈K⁺` whose exponents are `min_i v_ℓ c(f_i)` by PA fractional gcd. Then `g_i=f_i/γ` are integral-valued, at least one of them a unit at each `ℓ`. Use invariant 2 with `J` to get representatives `b_i∈B` modulo `J`. For **all** `ℓ|J` some `b_i` is a unit there. If degree is `e` let `V_e` be the nonzero integer Vandermonde determinant at `0,…,e` (degree zero: 1). We have `v_ℓ V_e < v_ℓ J` for every `ℓ|J`. For each such `ℓ` one of the `e+1` values `Σ b_i j^i` has valuation `≤v_ℓ V_e`: otherwise the adjugate matrix gives a contradiction at a unit coefficient. In PA select the first such `j` for each internal prime power exactly dividing `J` and impose `z_m≡j` modulo that full prime power by **internal** CRT. This requires one internally coded list of integer coefficients and the standard degree, not access by PA to any `w_ℓ` or to the old field. In `Σg_i(z_m+J Z')^i` the constant term has value `≤v_ℓ V_e`, and each positive-degree coefficient has value `≥v_ℓ J`. Thus the constant term is the unique leading residue, even after further integral substitutions. The same holds for constant polynomials without a task. This is also why simply taking `z_m=0` forever would not work for `Y` itself.

For an arbitrary fraction in the new field choose numerator and denominator polynomials over `E` in each chart. After their tasks all four values have stabilized **simultaneously** at all places of the corresponding charts. Their stable differences define valuations: the valuation rules follow by comparing common sufficiently late Gauss valuations for the finitely many expressions in any field identity. All values at a fixed depth of each chart polynomial are PA scalar profiles by taking minima of profiles of its coefficients there. The two profiles patch on the PA prime partition given by `c(b)` to a single fraction of `B`: split numerator and denominator products in their internal factorizations and take the prescribed parts. Thus 1 holds for the whole field, including fractions for the next step. This argument also shows the Gauss values off `J` are unchanged. For a polynomial of degree `e` in a chart, changing coordinates back from depth `m` can lower the coefficient minimum by at most `me v_ℓ J`, since substitution `Z=(Y−s_m)/J^m` has integral numerator. This gives the `J^N` bound for polynomials in the two charts. On a free line over `K` at a common stabilizing depth, nonnegative value at all primes says **every** coefficient in `Z` is integral in `B`.

**Full-modulus reduction at the new reserve.** Fix `d∈C'⁺`. For a single nonzero chart polynomial as above take a freezing step, and first divide by the scalar `γ` used there. At primes dividing `d` its frozen value `e_ℓ` lies between 0 and `v_ℓ V_e`. Divide again by the scalar with these exponents on `ℓ|d` and zero elsewhere (computed from the scalar profile of the frozen polynomial there). In the resulting expression all nonconstant terms contain a factor of valuation at least `v_ℓ J−v_ℓ V_e ≥ v_ℓ d` for `ℓ|d`: `d V_e|J` since `C'` is an initial PA cut. The constant term, after this division, is a unit on the selected places. It has a **single B-residue modulo d there**: a field element integral only on a specified PA subset of `ℓ|d` may be made globally integral by multiplying by the part of its scalar denominator supported *outside* that subset, invertible modulo the corresponding part of `d`. Apply 2 in the old field to this integral multiple and invert by PA CRT. This proves the required reduction for each polynomial normalized by its positive scalar profile on the selected places. Restoring any normalization changes only by a scalar unit at those places. For a field fraction apply this statement to the numerator and denominator: after normalization the denominator's residue is a unit at each selected place. Their residual quotient and the ratio of their scalar contents compute the residue of any integral-valued fraction, including a fraction with excess numerator valuation. Patch at the two PA parts of `d` by PA CRT. This proves 2 at `C'`. In particular the construction deals with all translated inputs, not just values of `Y` whose first digit was nonzero.

## 4. Why normalization does not spoil the order

**Depth-zero row lemma (PROVED).** Suppose `a,b` are as above, and `A'=A[u,v]/(au+bv−1)`. At the unspecialized Gauss valuations the subring of `A'` with all values nonnegative is exactly `R_A[u,v]` (actual subring of the row field). Indeed in the `a`-chart expand `x∈A'` as a polynomial in `v` over `A[1/a]`. At `w_ℓ(a)=0` its coefficients are integral-valued if `x` is: if `b` is also a unit the two chart coordinates differ by an integral affine change with unit slope. At `w_ℓ(a)>0` the `u`-expansion has nonnegative coefficient values, since `b` is a unit; substituting `u=(1−bv)/a` gives a lower bound `−e w_ℓ(a)` on the `v`-coefficient values for `e` the degree. Thus after multiplying by `a^N` for a single ordinary `N` the `v`-coefficients belong to `A` (clear the chart denominators) and have all values nonnegative. So `x∈R_A[1/a][v]`. The other chart is identical with the two letters exchanged. Since `a,b` generate 1 in the row ring, intersection of its two localizations gives the assertion. For a free line it is just the Gauss coefficient test. In particular at the **final**, diluted valuations we have

```
 R_A[u,v] ⊆ R_{A'} ⊆ R_A[u,v,1/J],       respectively R_A[Y] ⊆ R_{A[Y]} ⊆ R_A[Y,1/J].
```

The upper bound is all we need for the hull test, the lower for the promised row/floor. Inverting `J` here does **not** authorize `1/J` in the discretely ordered arithmetic ring.

**Order at a row (PROVED rel. z79 §7's Gauss order).** `S=(C\{0})⁻¹R_A` is a gcd domain by the factor calculus and localization. Coprime `a,b` stay gcd-coprime there. A homogeneous form over `S` vanishing at `(b,−a)` is divisible over `S` by `au+bv` (Gauss's lemma over a gcd domain; equivalently the leading monomial coefficient test using that `a` is a non-zero-divisor modulo `b`). Thus a representative of least total degree `k>0` modulo `au+bv−1` has top form `f_k(b,−a)≠0`, or subtract a multiple to lower the degree. Over the preceding real closure order `u=b Hη`, `v=1/b−a Hη` with `H∈B` above `C` and a fresh positive relative residue `η` as in z79 §1. The top `η`-coefficient is `H^k f_k(b,−a)`; the old algebra `S` has no nonzero relative infinitesimals and Gauss forbids cancellation. This proves injectivity and that the localized row meets the hull only in `Frac C`. The two row charts are polynomial over localizations of the old normal domain; over `K` the row is affine factorial: each old prime persists since the charts modulo that prime give an integral nonempty affine-line bundle over an open of the old integral quotient (at least one of `a,b` survives; test also when one is zero there). Localize at `a` and apply Nagata to its finitely many old prime factors in the Noetherian UFD `A`. The generic line forces any unit to be old: degree zero on it contracts to `A[1/a]∩A[1/b]=A`. For a free polynomial edge these assertions are immediate. The new Gauss relative residue embeds in the same Cauchy host, and no relative size is added by a unit of fresh residue or by real closure (old relative value group divisible).

**Floors (PROVED rel. the relative floor lemma).** For a real-closure target `α`, first test if `α−s` is in the `Frac C` convex hull for some `s∈S`. If so, write `s=r/d` with `r∈R_A`, `d∈C⁺`; by 2 choose `b∈B` with `r≡b (d)` and reduce `b` modulo `d` in `B` so `0≤b<d`. Now `(r−b)/d∈R_A` is still a bounded-error approximant. Use the old residue embedding/density of `Frac C` in its Cauchy host and the `C`-integer part to correct the bounded difference to within a unit interval, as in z79 §1. If not, take a free line `Y=α+η` with a fresh relative residue `1<η<2`. Then `Y−2` is a floor. The Taylor-coefficient proof of `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §2 gives `S[Y]∩hull(Frac C)=Frac C`: if a positive-degree polynomial were bounded, its leading coefficient would be a nonzero `Frac C` scalar and the next Taylor coefficient would put `α` within the hull of a member of `S`. Dilute this line by §3; `Y` remains integral. Its normalized arithmetic ring is still inside `S[Y]` since `J∈C`. No recurring division by extremely large cofinal-PA scalars is silently assumed possible in this order.

**Changing reserve (PROVED).** After an edge the arithmetic ring localized by positive `C` still passes the old hull test by the sandwich above, and its intersection with `K` is `B` by valuations. At the smaller reserve, if `r/d` is bounded by `Frac C'`, `d∈C'⁺`, then `r` is `C'`-bounded, hence first belongs to `Frac C`, then to `B`, then to `C'`. For real-closure elements, every `C`-relative unit is approximable to any positive `Frac C` precision by `Frac C` scalars on residue. In particular every nonzero positive size, after choosing a `K` representative up to `C` units, can be approximated within a factor 2 by a `K` element. Its class thus still has a `K` representative for the finer relative scale at `C'`. For a `C'`-bounded element and positive precision in `Frac C'`, approximate first by a `Frac C` scalar and then, by scaling and using `C` floors and endness, by one in `Frac C'`. Such approximations at all precisions give the ordered-residue embedding into the `C'` Cauchy host, exactly as at initialization. This proves 3 for the next edge. A skip of a task merely shrinks the reserve; 2 and 3 then restrict. All orders extend the preceding orders, not the arithmetic endness of a changing reserve. Only the bottom `D` must remain initial forever.

## 5. A nonleaking square specialization

**PROVED (rel. z79 §11 inside PA).** At the first edge use a free `X=Hη`, `H∈B` above `C₀`, fresh relative residue `1<η<2`. Put `P=1+JX` using its factorial slope. ## 6. Completion, splitting and the exact failures

**PROVED (rel. §§2–5).** Alternate a fair queue of all real-closure floor targets and all pairs from all arithmetic stages. Divide each pair by its gcd from the factor calculus, then give its two nonzero cofactors a row; handle zeros trivially. Nonzero scalar normalization, divisibility and old geometric primes are permanent through every edge. Every requested floor and row arrives after finitely many tasks (countable dovetail). The union has all integer parts and is discrete by 3, so Shepherdson applies; `D` stays initial. Normality follows either from Bézout or from intersection of normal upper rings with valuation rings stagewise. At the union, if positive `p,q` generate 1, their scalar contents are coprime in `B` and their finite horizontal supports disjoint. For `h`, split `c(h)` in PA into its part supported on primes of `c(p)` with full multiplicities and the rest. Put the full powers of horizontal factors shared with `p` on the first side, the rest on the second. This gives actual positive `t,b` with `h=tb`, `gcd(t,q)=gcd(b,p)=gcd(t,b)=1`; all these are row-coprimalities by the **global** Bézout result. No cutoff for the translates, degree bound on the switches, or prime restriction. Every ambient prime is either a `B` prime or a primitive horizontal prime. At any `h`, its entire prime-primary part is obtained by PA extraction from the scalar or by taking the corresponding horizontal multiplicity; hence the primary hit `t|h ∧ pt∤h` has a least solution and `(p)` is isolated in `X_h` whenever `p|h` (z79 §8). This model has isolation but **not** full `L(Div)`.

For `x>D` in the real closure and `0<ε<1` in `Frac D`, there is `x<m<(1+ε)x` in `B`. At a containing finite stage use the scale and relative-residue approximations to approximate `(1+ε/2)x` in `K` to relative error `<ε/8`; this works also if `x` is already `C`-bounded, after first matching its nonzero size. Taking a `B`-integer part contributes error `<1` and `εx` exceeds every standard integer. This proves cofinality and gives precisely the hypothesis used in z79 §3 for simultaneous Pell windows of **nonsquares in B**, the parameter-free balanced ray windows in every standard number-field order (including the odd `C_k` tests), and primitive height windows on every standard positive-rank short integral elliptic curve. At old heights use the direct PA witnesses. Exact abscissa/height bounds and the relative interval transfer are those of §3 there; it is not enough just to have abstract units or unbounded rational points. We do not insert the separate general-variety edges of z79 §2.

**Failures (PROVED).** Factorizations of nonzero `B` scalars in `R` are exactly the ones in `B`, by the factor calculus. Thus §1 at `y=P`, `b∈B` above `P+P` gives a single explicit missed parameter-free induction, even with global (Sep). For a least-divisibility failure, minimize `t|b! ∧ t>P`: a hit `t` is old; if `t>b` use `b`, otherwise `t−1>P` is another divisor (the alternative `t−1=P` would make `P` old). Nonempty, no minimum. BCM fails: e.g. all `X+j` for positive standard `j` are nonassociate as horizontal linear factors, are arithmetic, and are bounded by `2X`; a nonzero common multiple has only finitely many horizontal factors. The residue of the geometric ring at `P` remains formally real: start with `K`, and each prime-preserving line/row gives a rational function field on the generic point modulo `P` (at least one row coefficient nonzero). The arithmetic quotient embeds there since `P` is primitive. A norm of four squares equal to `P` would give zero modulo `P` in every coordinate and imply `P²|P`. Thus **not Lag**. The z79 cofinal-PA + Lag model is separate; it has no global (Sep).

**A coupled window lock (PROVED).** The full supplied-unit PW of z75 `IE1_PUSH2.md` *is* forced by `IE₁⁻`: pack `d,a,b` as well as the target/padding, accept openly invalid `a²≠1+db²` or invalid inequalities, otherwise use norm-one multiplication to pass from a witness at `n` to one at `n+1` if needed (`u=n`, new abscissa `au+dbv≤2au` for nonnegative solutions). Our new model fails at `d=P²−1` with supplied `(P,1)`. Any polynomial-line or row edge over the preceding field has no new units there after adjoining `√d` as a coefficient. More explicitly the generic line after this quadratic field extension has only scalar units; `u+v√d` of norm one must therefore have `u,v` in the preceding fraction field, by independence of `1,√d` over the rationally extended field. Coefficient contraction puts both back in the preceding algebra. Induct to `K[P,√(P²−1)]=K[z,z⁻¹]`, `z=P+√(P²−1)`, conjugation `z↔z⁻¹`. The only norm-one units are `±z^j`, standard integral `j`. Their abscissae are bounded by standard powers of `2P` (include 1). A `B`-height above all these powers exists: take a `B`-integer above `2P` and its PA exponential at a still larger height. Hence PW fails although *all* §2 cofinal-PA windows hold. This polynomial-unit obstruction likewise applies to a factorial-slope seed with post-seed edges still contracting such units after the indicated quadratic base change. It is not a no-go for torus adjunctions that actually add units over that quadratic extension.

## 7. The least-bad-prime question and reciprocity

**Parameter-free least descent (PROVED).** For a genuinely unary parameter-free `E₁` hit `G(x)`, `IE₁⁻` proves that if it has a hit then it has a least hit. Here is a proof without using parameterized least induction. Suppose globally every hit has a smaller one. Pack `s` **and a certificate of `G(s)`** in the block; openly invalid certificates pass. At index `j≤s` require `∃r≤s−j G(r)`. At 0 use `s`; on a successor, either keep the witness or lower the positive last permissible one by the *global* descent hypothesis. Freeze the index at `s` thereafter. Decoding at depth `s` forces `G(0)`, impossible under that hypothesis. This cannot be used with a single fixed foreign parameter carried by `G`: then other blocks might not have the same descent hypothesis. In particular it does not imply `L(Div)` with arbitrary parameters.

**PROVED (rel. Smith, NDJFL 1993, §§2–3).** At a fixed standard odd prime modulus, exact finite residue tables give Euler's criterion (fixed exponent), both squareclasses and the least nonresidue already by division. For distinct primes `p,q` with **fixed standard odd `p`**, the *one direction* of Smith Thm 3.11 also holds in `IE₁⁻`: if `p` is a square mod `q`, then `(q/p)=+1` except for two `3 mod 4` primes, where `(q/p)=−1`. Indeed the only minimizations in Smith Lemmas 3.2–3.10 here are of unary parameter-free `E₁` sets: a finite table on `m mod 24,8,4` or `m mod p` and a bounded root of the fixed numerator modulo `m`. Use quotient/remainder witnesses for the residue table including its negative entries, and the unary least lemma. The other steps of the cited proofs are standard-input checks, complementing a root `b` by `m−b`, and the stated lower-modulus congruences for a cofactor of `b²−p` or of the fixed supplement polynomial; they need division and the displayed finite table, not arbitrary-parameter minimization. Compare the archived text `data/z66/ie1/smith1993_NDJFL_quadres_fulltext.md`, especially the direction of the implication in §3. No converse producing roots, arbitrary-prime Euler exponentiation, general QR, or nonresidue at each arbitrary prime.

**PROVED (prime reduction, not ES).** Universal ES is equivalent over `IE₁⁻` to ES on the primes. Use polynomial bounds, e.g. for a sorted positive triple `x≤3n/4, y≤2nx, z≤nxy` (so a guard `2(n+1)^4` suffices; one need not assume every `z≤n²`). Assume *globally* ES at each prime. In the unary block for `s≥2`, at depth `j` exhibit ES at `s` or a divisor `r≥2` of `s` with `r+j≤s` (freeze at `j=s`). Initialize at `r=s`. If necessary split a composite witness into two factors >1 and keep the smaller; if irreducible then it is prime by Bézout and has an ES witness by hypothesis. Scale its three denominators by `s/r` and use the polynomial bounds afresh. No primality test in the induction matrix. This gives ES at all `s`. Contrapositively *some* prime fails in every `IE₁⁻` model with a failure. It need not be the named generic prime, or a definable element by this proof.

**Assessment (invalid least-counterexample shortcut).** Failure `n≥2 ∧ ¬ES(n)` is `U₁`, not a certified `E₁` hit. The initial-success-segment formula `∀2≤m≤n ES(m)` has a bounded universal followed by the existential witness block. The number of denominators in a certificate for *each individual* `m` is fixed; concatenating a table for nonstandard `n` is not a polynomially bounded tuple of a fixed number of integers. Neither unary least descent nor (1) makes that formula `E₁`. Even for parameterized induction Wilmers distinguished `LE₁` from the *additional* `LU₁` question (1985 open-problem quotation in `data/z66/ie1/lit_ie1.md` §1); it would be a mistake to cite `IE₁=LE₁` as `IE₁⁻ ⊢ LU₁⁻`. This is a gap analysis, not a separation theorem for the latter schemes. With an actual least failure the scaling argument makes it prime, and its unique definition places it in definable closure; even then not **every** failing prime need be fixed. If failure had a parameter-free positive `E₁` characterization under a given global hypothesis, the unary least lemma would apply under that hypothesis. No such characterization or inductive ES step is obtained here. The existence of an unbounded progression of primes at even one fixed standard modulus would imply unbounded primes, an old problem already for `IΔ₀` (PWW, see `data/z66/ie1/lit_idelta.md`). Do not confuse this with supplying a standard prime in every *standardly* truncated progression.
