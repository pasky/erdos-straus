### 1. What is signed in the $6/19$ problem?

**Assessment.** There is no Kloosterman sheaf *in* (11) of z79: the trace was removed by a periodic shift and Cauchy before the positive enlargement to all conductors. Formula (1) below is a rewriting of the existing z79 identity; the new quantitative test is (2), not a second trace completion.

**PROVED (relative to `notes/z79/ANALYTIC_PUSH.md` §7, Poisson and smooth one-dimensional stationary phase).** To test a signed Poisson in the long radical variable, put $C=(n/M)|\ell|,\ J=C/W^2,\ T=WJ/Q$ as in (10). Conjugate the entire inner sum of (10), which preserves its squared modulus. Write $b(v)$ for the conjugated smooth critical amplitude (conductor and frequency parameters suppressed). The positive difference $a$ in the off-diagonal now contributes exactly the following to the main expression of (10):

$$ {W\over J}\sum_{|m|<H}(1-|m|/H)\int_{\mathbb R} b((t+aQ)/J)\overline{b(t/J)}\,e\big(f_a(t)-mt\big)\,dt,\qquad
 f_a(t)={2\sqrt C\over Q}(\sqrt{t+aQ}-\sqrt t). \tag{1}$$

The sum on the residue $c$ has reconstructed the full lattice $j$; substitute $t=j-u$ and apply Poisson to its translate. The multiplier is the exact Fourier coefficient of the Fejér probability, not a derivative estimate on a sharp cutoff. Outside the positive support define the integrand to be zero. The opposite difference gives the conjugate. Here and below $e(v)=\exp(2\pi i v)$.

**Explicit coherent stationary-lift example (PROVED; lower bound on an absolute majorant only).** Take $a\asymp A=cHJ/W$, with fixed sufficiently small $c>0$, $H\to\infty$, $HQ/W=o(1)$, $A\to\infty$. Choose a fixed smooth primal bump equal to one on a positive interval about the point $x/W=1$, supported inside $(1/2,2)$, and a strip bump of positive integral. Take $|\ell|$ a small fixed positive fraction of the Poisson length $L$ (not the logarithm of §3), in a whole interval of such fractions. For $M=4$ the primal amplitude at $y=x/W,s=Q/N$ is $\psi(y)\widehat\Phi(\ell/(4Lsy))/(4sy)$ (z78 §7); at the critical point it is multiplied by a unit phase and the positive factor $(2/y^3)^{-1/2}$. By continuity of $\widehat\Phi$ at zero, $|b(v)|\gg1$ on a fixed interval about $v=1$ (equivalently the stationary point $v^{-1/2}$ is on the plateau), with all scaled derivatives bounded. The same choice works for a compact range of $Q/N$ in $[1,4]$. Conjugation of the critical amplitude does not affect this assertion. On the rescaled band $t=Jy$, exactly

$$ f_a(Jy)=aW\, {2\over\sqrt{y+aQ/J}+\sqrt y} . $$

The scaled function after $aW$ tends with all fixed derivatives to $y^{-1/2}$, since $aQ/J=O(cHQ/W)=o(1)$. Its second derivative is positive and bounded away from zero on fixed positive compact sets. There are $\gg aW/J$ **negative** integers $m$ in the derivative image of an interior plateau subinterval, all $|m|<H/2$: that image has length $\asymp aW/J\asymp cH$. For each such integer there is a unique nondegenerate stationary point on the band; there the amplitude has modulus bounded below. Smooth stationary phase (Hörmander I, Thm 7.7.5, the compact family used in z79 §2) gives integral modulus $\gg J/\sqrt{aW}$, with error $O(J(aW)^{-3/2})$. Thus, even **after** signed summation on the full $j$ lattice and exact Fejér integration, taking absolute values of its individual stationary integrals in (1) costs at least

$$ \gg {W\over J}\sum_{a\asymp A}\sqrt{aW}\asymp H^{3/2}\sqrt J. \tag{2} $$

At the z79 fixed-$M$ test vertex $W\asymp n^t$, $r=1-3t$, $H=n^{2r+o(1)}$, $Q=n^{2r+o(1)}$, $|\ell|=n^{r+o(1)}$, the absolute cost relative to the needed $O(W n^\epsilon)$ has exponent $(1+7r-4t)/2$. At $t=6/19$ this is $1/19$. **Assessment:** if these same scalings are continued in $t$, the cost comparison needs $t\ge8/25$: absolute lifts do not even recover the existing $6801/21497<8/25$ bound. This does not claim optimality over choices of parameters. For example $n=4P^{19}$, $W=P^6$, $Q\asymp P^2$, $H=P^2$, $|\ell|\asymp c'P$ realize these coherent lifts at an integer sequence (one may use dyadic integer $P$); none requires $n$ to have a word on the test block. This is not an extremal lower bound for the signed energy or an exclusion of signed two-dimensional large sieves. ### 1.1 Split-prime differencing before enlargement: two different diagonal costs

**PROVED (relative to finite Cauchy–Schwarz and stationary phase; another scoped test).** Now go back *before* z79 (9) and retain an actual square-sieve conductor $q_1 q_2$, distinct primes $\asymp P$, and a primitive $\ell$. The periodic part after dividing by $\sqrt{q_1 q_2}$ factors as two bounded functions $g_i(x\bmod q_i)$ (Weil and CRT including the complementary-prime twists, z78 §6). A natural $q$-van der Corput variant removes **one** prime at each of two successive periodic shifts, rather than treating $Q$ as atomic. Write $S=W^{-1}\sum_x g_1(x)g_2(x)b(x/W)e(F(x))$, $F(x)=-C/(q_1 q_2 x)$ with $C=(n/4)|\ell|$, conjugating as necessary, and take $1\le H_i$, $q_i H_i\ll W$. Two separate shift identities give

$$ |S|\ll H_1^{-1/2}+H_2^{-1/4}+\left({1\over H_1 H_2}\sum_{1\le h<H_1,\,1\le k<H_2} |S_{hk}|\right)^{1/4}, $$

where $S_{hk}$ is the $W^{-1}$-normalized $x$ sum with phase $\Delta_{q_1 h}\Delta_{q_2 k}F(x)$ and the four translated smooth factors of $b$. Indeed averaging translates spaced by $q_1$ and applying Cauchy removes $g_1$; expanding the translated square leaves $g_2(x+q_1h)\overline{g_2(x)}$, periodic mod $q_2$ and bounded. Shift by $q_2$, Cauchy again removes *this product*, leaving the four factors of the primal amplitude. The support in each undifferenced Cauchy is $O(W)$, so the normalized diagonal terms are $O(1/H_i)$ inside their respective squares; take absolute values of the nonzero correlations and use Cauchy to average the second square roots. This test keeps the genuine, possibly coincident twists within a prime rather than asserting their geometric independence; it then loses them by Cauchy. It does not apply to methods retaining their signed cross-conductor contributions.

At $n=4P^{19}, W=P^6$, $|\ell|\asymp c'P$ as in §1, the normalized target is $|S|\ll P^{-1+o(1)}$ (the removed scale is $W\sqrt{q_1q_2}$); one may take the interval for $\ell$ below either detector prime. The diagonal costs need $H_1\ge P^{2-o(1)}, H_2\ge P^{4-o(1)}$. Even granting a $W^{-1/2}$ normalized majorant for each $S_{hk}$, plugging in that **positive cost** leaves $W^{-1/8}=P^{-3/4}$, too large by $P^{1/4}$. This last comparison is not a lower bound on the true $S_{hk}$. There is also an actual absolute-lift obstruction, not just exponent arithmetic: at the minimal power choices $H_1\asymp P^2,H_2\asymp P^4$ and $h,k$ in their middle dyads, $q_1h/W,q_2k/W=o(1)$. The exact double difference is

$$ -2C hk\int_0^1\int_0^1(x+s q_1h+t q_2k)^{-3}\,ds\,dt. $$

On the plateau from §1 the derivative is positive of size $Y_2=C hk/W^4\asymp P^2$, with image interval of length $\asymp Y_2$, second derivative negative of size $\asymp Y_2/W$. All four factors are bounded away from zero there. Smooth Poisson in $x$ followed by absolute values has $\gg Y_2$ stationary integrals of modulus $\gg W/\sqrt{WY_2}$ at interior critical points, by the same compact stationary-phase test as §1 (scaled phase, after division by $WY_2$, tends in smooth norms to $-2y^{-3}$ before the linear term). Their normalized absolute sum is $\gg\sqrt{Y_2/W}\asymp P^{-2}$ against the needed fourth-power budget $P^{-4}$. In particular the period split changes both the derivative scale and the number of Cauchy losses; it cannot simply be counted as saving a prime in the old single-shift bound. **Assessment.** There is no exclusion of Kuznetsov on a genuine signed modulus sum, and no generic involution on the real radical phases follows from Katz correlations at prime conductor.

### 1.2 A genuine mixed trace theorem and a sharp Fourier $\ell^1$ norm

**PROVED (rel. Katz's Kloosterman monodromy theorem and Deligne's RH over finite fields).** Work at $M=4$ **before** removal of the arithmetic traces. For a detector prime $p\ge5$, $v,w\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, let $T_v(c)=T_p(c;v)$ of z78 (6): explicitly $p^{-1/2}\sum_{k\bmod p}\big(\frac{(4ck-n)^2-4k}{p}\big)e_p(vk)$ with the Legendre symbol. For $s,t,b\in\mathbb F_p$ one has uniformly in $n\bmod p$

$$ \sum_c T_v(c+s)\overline{T_w(c+t)} e_p(bc)=p\,1_{s=t,\ v=w,\ b=0}+O(\sqrt p). \tag{K} $$

Also $\sum_c T_v(c+s)e_p(bc)\ll\sqrt p$ at every $b$. Here the implied constants are absolute. This includes the correlation of the *remaining* prime trace after the first split shift of §1.1. The determinant of the **actual twisted trace**, not projective independence of two abstract Kloosterman sheaves, rules out even the $v=-w$ exception for $s=t$. This is an application of the classical Katz/Deligne correlation method (compare Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel, *A study in sums of products*, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 373 (2015)); the specific determinant check below identifies the exceptions, not a new general trace-function estimate. These statements are only at primitive detector frequencies. The original $n$ is not required to be prime or a unit mod the detector.

On $c\ne0$, $4nc+1\ne0$, the formula in z78 §6 writes $T_v(c)$, up to an overall constant sign of the normalized Kloosterman trace, as the trace of

$$ \mathcal F_v=\mathcal L_{\psi(v A(c))}\otimes [B_v(c)]^*\mathcal{Kl}_2,\qquad A(c)=n/(4c)+1/(8c^2),\quad B_v(c)=v^2(4nc+1)/(256 c^4). $$

Indeed rescale the interior Kloosterman variable in (6) there to give argument $v^2 d(c)/4$. Use the pure weight-zero normalization (a constant field twist); the geometric group of $\mathcal{Kl}_2$ is $\mathrm{SL}_2$, tame at zero, Swan 1 at infinity (Katz, *Gauss sums, Kloosterman sums, and monodromy groups*, Annals Studies 116, Chs. 7,11, in particular Thm 11.1 for the geometric monodromy). The nonconstant rational map $B_v$ has degree four even for $n=0$ and is separable for $p\ge5$. After deleting branch values, a finite étale cover induces a finite-index subgroup of the fundamental group, and deleting finitely many points of the target of the lisse sheaf retains its geometric monodromy (surjection on geometric fundamental groups). A finite-index subgroup in a Zariski-dense group with connected closure is still dense. Thus each pullback is geometrically irreducible of rank two with trivial geometric determinant. The rank-one twist preserves irreducibility.

A geometric isomorphism $[c\mapsto c+s]^*\mathcal F_v\otimes\mathcal L_{\psi(bc)}\simeq[c\mapsto c+t]^*\mathcal F_w$ would force the rank-one Artin–Schreier character for $2(v A(c+s)-w A(c+t)+bc)$ to be geometrically trivial. Such a rational function must be $H^p-H+$constant for some $H\in\overline{\mathbb F}_p(c)$; any pole has order divisible by $p$. If $s\ne t$ there is an uncancelled double pole at $-s$. If $s=t,v\ne w$ there is still a double pole there; otherwise $b\ne0$ leaves a simple pole at infinity. All are impossible. Schur's lemma therefore removes $H_c^2$ of the mixed tensor on the common lisse affine open off this diagonal (by duality); on the diagonal its only geometrically invariant endomorphisms are scalars with Frobenius acting trivially, giving the term $p$. One can equivalently split off the trace-free endomorphisms there. In the single-trace case irreducibility of rank two excludes invariants after every rank-one twist.

For precision, the conductor and Euler characteristic in this argument are $O(1)$ uniformly: there are boundedly many removed points (translations of zeros/poles and infinity). The degree-four maps are tamely ramified ($p>4$), so pullback multiplies local wild breaks by ramification index $\le4$; after each rank-one twist the largest break is at most the maximum of the old largest break and 2. The tensor/dual has bounded rank and breaks. Grothendieck–Ogg–Shafarevich thus bounds $\dim H_c^1$ absolutely and $H_c^0=0$ on the affine open. Trace formula and Deligne (*Weil II*, weight bound for compactly supported cohomology) give the errors; complex conjugation of pure normalized local traces corresponds to the dual. The omitted actual values, including $c=0$ and $4nc+1=0$, are bounded by the explicit Gauss/degenerate evaluations of z78 §6, so cost only $O(1)$ in the **unnormalized** sum. No Fourier decay over a real arc was assumed.

**Consequence (PROVED relative to (K)).** For two primitive indices of z77 (1), let $m_0=[Q_i,Q_j]$ and $g(c)$ be the actual product of normalized CRT traces (complementary-prime rescalings included). The normalized coefficients $c_b=m_0^{-1}\sum_c g(c)e_{m_0}(-bc)$ satisfy $|c_b|\ll\sqrt{e_b/m_0}$, where $e_b$ is the product of the shared primes with coincident local twists, matched shifts (if translated), and $p\mid b$. This follows by CRT and (K) or the single-trace bound, at at most four primes. In particular for disjoint detector pairs $\sum_b|c_b|\asymp\sqrt{m_0}$ at all sufficiently large detector primes, not merely $\ge1$: Plancherel gives $\sum_b|c_b|^2=m_0^{-1}\sum_c|g(c)|^2=\prod_{p|m_0}(1+O(p^{-1/2}))$ by (K) on its diagonal. Use the sup bound for the lower $\ell^1$ estimate, Cauchy for the upper.

On the positive-proportion disjoint primitive pairs and nonvanishing smooth plateau of z78 §6 (7) this proves a lower bound $\gg\sqrt{m_0 UW}$ on its absolute completed kernel. Indeed $T\asymp UW$, stationary dual interval length $\asymp m_0 T/W$, each residue occurs $\gg T/W$ times and each corresponding stationary integral has modulus $\gg W/\sqrt{T}$ as proved there. For instance at $U=W=P^6,n=4P^{19}$ the old $\gg P^6$ becomes $\gg P^8$ with $m_0\asymp P^4$. This is the cost *without* taking the minimum with the trivial $O(W)$ bound; no lower bound on the signed sum. In particular all the local square-root bounds may hold at once **and** the absolute-lift argument fails: the Fourier spectrum is spread out, not sparse. **Assessment.** What is missing is a signed sum of these actual coefficients against the oscillating phases at repeated lifts of each residue. In fact even granting a square-root bound on each **single residue's train of lifts** is not sufficient if one then sums these majorants absolutely: at $U=W=n^t,\ P=n^{1-3t}$, the train length is $\asymp U$ and the stationary amplitude $\asymp W/\sqrt{UW}\asymp1$. Inserting the positive cost $\sqrt U$ separately for every residue pays $\asymp\sqrt{m_0 U}$ by the two-sided $\ell^1$ estimate. Compared with the z77 (1) bracket budget $W/P^2$, this loses exponent $4(1-3t)-t/2$, or a factor $P$ at $t=6/19$. This is a comparison of the square-root *majorant*, not a lower bound on the signed individual trains. Equating these real-integral weights with smooth weights on an ordinary Kuznetsov modulus sum would require a separate transform and uniform spectral estimates.

### 1.3 Round 2: signed pooling in $D$ and the common-integer term

**PROVED (rel. Poisson, smooth stationary phase and §1.2; a barrier for the absolute common-integer treatment, not the signed sum).** Work at the explicit $n=4P^{19}, U=W=P^6,V=P^7,E=P$ sequence of §1. Take disjoint prime pairs $Q,R\asymp P^2$ and $m=QR$. Smoothly localize two frequency windows to $\delta E<h<2\delta E$ and $-2\delta E<h'<-\delta E$ with fixed small $\delta>0$ and smooth nonnegative bumps with positive integrals. These are primitive, including at every local prime. Set $D=hR-h'Q>0$; there is at most one ordered $(h,h')$ in the windows for each $D$ by coprimality and width $<Q,R$. Retain the actual normalized periodic factors $T_Q(x,h),\overline{T_R(x,h')}$ of z77 (1); write $c_k(D)$ for the normalized Fourier coefficients of their product in $x\bmod m$ (hence $m$-periodic in $k$, including the CRT signs). Denote the product of the smooth factors by $b_D(x/W)$, including the two frequency cutoffs and a common nonnegative $x$ cutoff with an interior plateau. They factor into one local amplitude per window and that common cutoff, with the z78 §7 bounded derivative estimates. The following is the **signed pooled** form of the normalized disjoint block:

$$ \frac1{E^2}\sum_{k}\sum_D c_k(D)\,I_D(k),\qquad I_D(k)=\int b_D(x/W)e\!\left(-\frac{nD}{4mx}+\frac{kx}m\right)dx. \tag{S} $$

Indeed expand the actual periodic factor and Poisson in $x$; summing $D$ and the lifts in either order is allowed by the smooth tails. For $k<0$ in the band $|k|\asymp m T/W$, $T=nD/(4mW)\asymp P^{12}$, the critical action in $I_D(k)$ is exactly $-\sqrt{nD|k|}/m$. The integral is its $W/\sqrt T$ leading smooth amplitude times this exponential plus $O(W T^{-3/2})$ uniformly (interpreting the amplitude as zero if the critical point misses the support); outside an enlarged band integration by parts applies. Thus (S) with the square-root actions and smooth Jacobians differs from exact (S) by $O(P^{-4})$: use $\ll P^{10}$ lifts, (K)'s $|c_k(D)|\ll P^{-2}$ and the frequency normalization. This error is not why pooling fails.

There is a way to do **both** signed sums of (S), not just apply a square-root majorant to each $D$. Fourier-invert the periodic coefficient in its primitive frequencies and undo the $k$ transform (all signs retained); now sum the $D$ samples in their two independent window coordinates by Poisson. Explicitly put $u_0=n/(4x)$, write $w_{Q,x}(h/E)$ for the single-window smooth factor and $\psi(x/W)$ for the common cutoff. Since $T_Q=Q^{-1/2}\sum_{j\bmod Q}\chi_Q(f(x,j))e_Q(hj)$ with $f(x,j)=(4xj-n)^2-4j$, Poisson in $h$ gives

$$ {1\over E}\sum_h w_{Q,x}(h/E)T_Q(x,h)e_Q(-hu_0)=Q^{-1/2}\sum_{u\in\mathbb Z}\chi_Q(f(x,u))\widehat w_{Q,x}((u_0-u)E/Q). $$

Use the conjugate formula for $R$ with its own negative window. Hence (S) is exactly

$$ {1\over\sqrt m}\sum_x\psi(x/W)\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb Z}\chi_Q(f(x,u))\chi_R(f(x,v))\,\widehat w_{Q,x}((u_0-u)E/Q)\overline{\widehat w_{R,x}((u_0-v)E/R)}. \tag{S'} $$

This inverse computation explains a surviving common-integer term: there are $\asymp P$ integers in the overlap of both dual windows, not just one nearest $u_0$. It is not orthogonality of Kloosterman sheaves over two independent integer arguments. In particular consider the class of estimates which, after the exact signed pooling (S′), sum absolute summand sizes on $u=v$, even if **all $u\ne v$ cost nothing**. On the plateau $\widehat w_{Q,x}(0)$ and $\widehat w_{R,x}(0)$ are bounded away from zero: the original strip Fourier bump is uniformly near its positive value at 0 on the two small $\delta$-windows (the reciprocal $x,Q$ prefactors are positive bounded factors), and the window bumps have fixed positive integrals. Continuity is uniform on the compact parameter ranges, so both moduli are $\gg1$ whenever $|u-u_0|< cP$ for a small fixed $c>0$. For *each* of the $\gg W$ plateau integers $x$, at most $O(1)$ of these $u$ have a vanishing symbol: mod each of the four primes $f(x,u)$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most two, linear even when $x=0\bmod p$, and the interval has length $O(P)$. The other absolute symbols are 1. Thus the absolute common-integer cost is $\gg WP/\sqrt m\asymp W/P$, sharply so by Schwartz decay. This is an explicit coherent frequency window (the Fourier phases in each $h$-sum align on that overlapping set), holding for all disjoint conductor pairs in the range, while the required z77 bracket is $W/P^2$. A loss by $P=n^{1/19+o(1)}$ **after** doing the signed $D$ pooling survives this particular final triangle. At nearby binding shapes the same test has $E\asymp P$, $W=n^t$, $P=n^{1-3t}$ and the same ratio $P$ for $0<t<1/3$ in (S′). In comparing the stationary replacement in (S), $T\asymp n^{2t}$, its total in-band error is $O(m^{1/2}/\sqrt T)=O(n^{2-7t})$, and the budget is $n^{7t-2}$; the error is negligible against that budget for $t>2/7$. This is a lower bound on the absolute diagonal cost, not on (S) or on its signed $u=v$ component.

**Assessment.** Even square-root costs in the $P$ common integers separately at each $x$ would leave $W/P^{3/2}$ here. A way forward would have to handle their four-prime character **with its signs across $x$ or the prime pairs**, as well as the nonzero $u-v$ terms; ordinary complete finite-field RH doesn't turn a length-$P$ interval into an independent complete residue mod $P^4$. This test gives no new fixed-$4$ exponent and does not rule out a direct signed estimate for (S) without inverse Poisson.

### 1.4 Round 3: pool all four primes *before* common-integer completion

**PROVED (rel. §1.3 and the classical Weil bound for quadratic-character sums of polynomials, bounded degree).** At the same explicit $n=4P^{19}, W=P^6$, localize the common integer $u=v$ in (S′) by a fixed nonnegative $C_c^\infty$ bump in $t=(u-u_0)/P$ supported on a small interval about zero and nonzero inside. Form the signed mean over *all disjoint ordered pairs of detector moduli*, each an unordered product of two primes in $(P,2P]$. Do not take absolute values yet. At fixed plateau integer $x$ complete this one-dimensional $u$ sum by its periodic Fourier expansion. Among the resulting rational frequencies, group equal frequencies from **all** conductors first. The absolute majorant on just the near-zero primitive frequencies of the good four-prime moduli has size $\asymp1$ per $x$, i.e. $\asymp W$ over the plateau instead of the required $W/P^2$. This time the split by conductors **after** a triangle is not needed to exhibit the loss. It is the cost of a triangle on distinct primitive *rational frequencies* after pooling, still not a lower bound on the signed common-integer sum.

Here are the details. Call $p$ good at $x$ if $p\nmid x(4xn+1)$. Only $O(1)$ of the detector primes are not good, by their size and $x,n$ polynomial in $P$. For each good $p$ the quadratic $f(x,u)$ of §1.3 has leading square nonzero and two distinct roots over the algebraic closure (its discriminant is $16(4xn+1)$). Put $C_p(v)=p^{-1}\sum_u\chi_p(f(x,u))e_p(-vu)$. Then $|C_p(v)|\ll p^{-1/2}$ for every $v$ by z78 §6 or `Z53_SQSIEVE.md` §4.2 Lemma W (at $v=0$ the complete quadratic sum is $-1$ before normalization). For squarefree $m=p_1\cdots p_4$ with all primes good, let $C_m(b)$ be the corresponding Fourier coefficient of $\chi_m(f(x,u))$ modulo $m$ with $1/m$ normalization. Then $|C_m(b)|\ll m^{-1/2}$ by CRT. We need its *lower mass on a short frequency interval*, not just full-modulus Plancherel.

Define $G_p(v)=1_{p\nmid v}\,p|C_p(v)|^2$. Its complete mean is $1+O(1/p)$ by Parseval (at most two roots), and every nonzero normalized Fourier coefficient is $O(p^{-1/2})$: orthogonality reduces it, before removing $v=0$, to $p^{-1}\sum_u\chi_p(f(x,u) f(x,u+h))$, $h\ne0$. A nontrivial translation cannot preserve the two-element set of roots in characteristic $p>2$ (every nontrivial translation orbit has size $p$), so this quartic is not a square even up to scalar. Weil applies even if one root is shared, by removing the repeated factor at $O(1)$ cost in the unnormalized sum. Removing $v=0$ costs $O(1/p)$ or less in the normalized transform. By CRT the complete mean of $G(b)=1_{(b,m)=1}m|C_m(b)|^2$ is $1+O(1/P)$ and its other coefficients have modulus $\ll (h,m)^{1/2}m^{-1/2}$ (units rescaling at the primes is harmless). In an interval of length $D\asymp m/P$, the incomplete sum of $G$ thus differs from $D(1+O(1/P))$ by $O(\sqrt m\,\tau(m)\log m)$: sum the geometric bound $O(m/|h|)$ for $0<|h|\le m/2$ grouping by divisors of $(h,m)$. Since $m\asymp P^4$, this is $o(D)$. By the sup estimate and Cauchy this proves, on **any** such interval of fixed proportional length,

$$ \sum_{b\text{ in interval},\ (b,m)=1}|C_m(b)|\asymp D/\sqrt m. \tag{L} $$

In the pooled Poisson expression the frequency $b/m$ in lowest terms determines its **unique unordered set of four primes**. No other four-prime set has a denominator divisible by $m$. Write $w_{Q,R,x}(t)$ for the product of the two transformed weights in (S′) times the extra bump. At $t=0$ the original $h$-bumps are real nonnegative and the common strip transform is uniformly close to its real value at 0 for small fixed $\delta$. Hence, on a sufficiently small fixed $t$-interval, the product of the two transformed window factors is in a fixed sector $|\arg|<\pi/6$ and has modulus $\gg_\delta1$, for **every** pairing and ordering of the four primes. Take the additional nonnegative bump supported there. For $|b/m|\le c'/P$, $c'$ small fixed, the transforms $\widehat w_{Q,R,x}(-bP/m)$ still have real part bounded below by a fixed positive constant (integrate on that compact support). Shifting the interval center to $u_0$ contributes the same phase $e(bu_0/m)$ for every pairing. The other factors $P C_m(b)/\sqrt m$ are also **identical** over the pairings. Thus even after adding all terms at a given primitive rational frequency their absolute size, prior to normalization by the number of disjoint pairs, is $\asymp P|C_m(b)|/\sqrt m$ per pairing. If grouping modulo one rather than on the real line, the lifts $b/m+j$, $j\ne0$, contribute $O_A(P^{-A})$ relative to that scale for every fixed $A$, by the smooth localized bump. Apply (L) on e.g. $c'm/(2P)<b<c'm/P$. The normalized absolute cost is $\asymp1$ from this band, since all but $o(1)$ of the four-prime sets are good at each $x$. The proof supplies a coherent example with the actual trace and actual phases at the pooled frequencies, not a mock class-function majorant. It does **not** take account of cancellation between different rationals or plateau integers, nor the signs of the off-diagonal $u\ne v$; this is the precise limitation. Since primitive denominators from different four-prime sets never coincide, the nontrivial grouping here is only over the pairings of a given set. The resulting absolute cost $W$ is worse than $W/P$ in §1.3, not a new obstruction to a stronger signed method. No improvement of the fixed-4 exponent follows.

### 2. A separate large-numerator gain without exponential sums

**PROVED (relative to the divisor bound and Dirichlet's pigeonhole approximation).** For every $n\ge2,M\ge1$, let $D=n/M^2$. In addition to (12) of z79 one has

$$ R_M(n)\ll_\epsilon n^\epsilon(1+D^{1/2}M^{-1/6}). \tag{3} $$

This improves $D^{1/3}$ for $M>n^{1/3}$, and gives $O_\epsilon(n^\epsilon)$ throughout $M\ge n^{3/7}$. It is not an improvement for $M=1,4$, in particular not on the fourth-root balanced block of $N_2$.

Use a nonempty dyadic block $a\asymp U\le V\asymp b,\ d\asymp W$, with actual $a\le b$ by symmetry; write $B=V/M$, $K=UW$. By z78 §13 and z79 §8, $KB\asymp D$ and the block costs $\ll n^\epsilon\min(U,W,1+B)$ (with the usual change of the arbitrary $\epsilon$ for log factors). Hence we need only treat $M>D$, $D\gg1$, $U,W,B\gg t_0=\max(1,D^{1/2}M^{-1/6})$; fixed-large constants in these cuts are harmless. Put $n=MN+n_0$ with $0\le n_0<M$, $a+b=Mv-n_0$ so $v\asymp B$. The exact integer equation is

$$ N+v=ad(Mv-n_0-a). $$

Take a positive integer $S\asymp\sqrt{MK/(KU+B)}$, using the integer part with a lower cutoff of one. This scale is bounded below by a fixed positive constant since $U\ll D$ and $B\ll D$ here. There are $1\le q\le S$ and $g\in\mathbb Z$ with $|qn_0/M-g|\le1/S$ by pigeonholing multiples mod 1. Throughout the entire block,

$$ \big|ad(qv-g)-qN/M\big|\ll K/S+S(KU+B)/M\ll \sqrt{K(KU+B)/M}. $$

The left-hand integer product before subtraction is positive ($v-n_0/M=(a+b)/M\gg1$) and polynomially bounded in $n$ ($M\le n+2$ on a solution, or just use $M\ll n$ from z78 §13). Each such integer has $O_\epsilon(n^\epsilon)$ ordered factorizations, each determining $a,d,v$ and then $b$. Finally $K\ll D/t_0$, $U\ll D/t_0^2$, so the displayed width is $\ll D^{3/2}/(\sqrt M\,t_0^2)+\sqrt{D/M}\ll t_0$. This proves (3), including $D\ll1$ via the earlier fibre bound. Here and throughout, “improves” refers to the internal z79 bounds; no claim of external priority. The new ingredient is rational approximation to the **fixed fractional digit** $n_0/M$ across a whole block, not treating $n/M$ as an integer or restricting a detector to unit moduli.

### 2.1 Filling an intermediate numerator range by reciprocal discrepancy

**PROVED (relative to Erdős–Turán discrepancy, Bourgain's exponent pair as in Trudgian–Yang §1.1, and divisors).** In the same uniform minimum one may also include

$$ \max\{ D^{55/181},D^{1/2}M^{-1/4}\}. \tag{D} $$

That is, (D) is another choice **inside** the common $n^\epsilon[1+\min\{\cdots\}]$. It improves on $D^{1/3}$ in exponent for $D\to\infty$ and $M\ge D^{2/3+\eta}$ with fixed $\eta>0$, i.e. already past $M=n^{2/7}$ by a fixed power. At $M=D$ ($M=n^{1/3}$) it gives $D^{55/181}$, whereas (3) and the old bound give $D^{1/3}$. This is a result on effective volume for growing numerators, not a fixed-4 exponent or a fourth-root $N_2$ bound. The classical pair $(1/6,2/3)$ gives $4/13$ in place of $55/181$ **in (D) only**.

Indeed keep §2's notation and put $\alpha=n_0/M$. Here $M$ need not exceed $D$. Fix an exponent pair $(\kappa,\lambda)$ from the two just specified, ignoring arbitrary positive perturbations until the final $n^\epsilon$. Let $s_0=\lambda/(1+2\lambda-\kappa)$, $T_0=\max(D^{s_0},D^{1/2}M^{-1/4})$; omit $D\ll1$ and blocks with $\min(U,W,B)\ll T_0$ by the fibres. Then $B\asymp v-\alpha\gg T_0$, $K=UW\ll D/T_0$, $U\ll D/T_0^2$ and $B\ll D/T_0^2$. The equation of §2 gives **an integer within a thin window about a function of the one digit $v$ alone**:

$$ ad={D\over v-\alpha}+{a^2 d\over M(v-\alpha)}+{1\over M},\qquad \|D/(v-\alpha)\|\le\Delta\ll {KU+B\over MB}. $$

At each $v$ the total number of triples, not just the number of possible $ad$, is $O_\epsilon(n^\epsilon)$ since $abd=N+v$ is fixed. Thus it suffices to count $v\asymp B$ in a containing interval with $v-\alpha\asymp B$ meeting this necessary near-integer test. Its density term costs $B\Delta\ll (D^2/T_0^3+B)/M\ll 1+T_0$, using $T_0^4\ge D^2/M$ and, for the $B/M$ term, $B\ll D/T_0^2$, $D/(M T_0^3)\le1$ (from $T_0\ge D^{1/2}M^{-1/4}$). If $\Delta\ge1/4$ use the trivial length, absorbed by that cost. Below this threshold the Erdős–Turán upper inequality for an arc gives, for integer $H\ge1$, the density term plus $O(B/H+\sum_{h\le H}h^{-1}|\sum_v e(hD/(v-\alpha))|)$. On dyadic subintervals of this $v$-interval the reciprocal derivative after conjugation is $hD(v-\alpha)^{-2}$, differing from $hD v^{-2}$ and its fixed derivatives by relative $O_j(B^{-1})$. Its size is $\gg D/B^2\gg D^{4s_0-1}$ by the bound on $B$; $4s_0>1$. Thus TY (1.1)–(1.2) apply at $\sigma=2$, $y=hD$, giving $\ll_\epsilon n^\epsilon (hD/B^2)^\kappa B^\lambda$. The permissible small relative error is eventually met since $B\gg D^{s_0}$; bounded $D$ cases cost a constant by the fibres. The shifted pole is **not** a degree-two numerator perturbation or an unverified multidimensional pair.

Balance $B/H$ with the increasing discrepancy term by taking $H$ within a bounded factor of $(B^{1-\lambda+2\kappa}/D^\kappa)^{1/(1+\kappa)}$ when this is $\ge1$; if smaller, the optimized expression already dominates $B$ so use the trivial length instead. This leaves $D^{\kappa/(1+\kappa)} B^{(\lambda-\kappa)/(1+\kappa)}\le O(T_0)$ because $B\ll D/T_0^2$ and $T_0^{1+2\lambda-\kappa}\ge D^\lambda$. All arguments including $H$ have polynomial size in $n$, so perturbing the exponent pair in this application costs $O(n^{O(\epsilon)})$ with an arbitrarily small parameter. The classical pair is $A(1/2,1/2)$; for Bourgain $(13/84,55/84)+(\epsilon,\epsilon)$ see TY (1.3), cited in z79 §5. Summing blocks proves (D). In exponent notation $M=n^u$, the improvement of (D) over the minimum of (3) and z79 is strict for $2/7<u<213/607$; its two terms cross at $u=142/465$. At $u=2/7$ there is equality with the cube-root bound, not a power saving. These comparisons ignore the common $1+$ and epsilon losses, which do not change the stated strict interior range.

### 3. A level-one *upper transfer* for nonnegative statistics

**PROVED (relative to Mertens, elementary CRT and PNT).** Write $L=\log X$. Let $F(n)\ge0$ on all integers in $(X,2X]$, given as a finite real linear combination $\sum_i c_i 1_{n=r_i\ (l_i)}$ of **reduced** classes, including modulus 1. Write $\langle F\rangle_* =\sum_i c_i/\varphi(l_i)$ (Haar on units at the common period). Suppose, for *fixed* $\delta>0,b\ge0$,

$$ l_i\le X,\quad \sum |c_i|\le X^{1-\delta},\qquad \sum |c_i|/\varphi(l_i)\le L^b. \tag{4} $$

For every fixed $A>0$, uniformly for these statistics as $X\to\infty$,

$$ {1\over\pi(2X)-\pi(X)}\sum_{X<p\le2X}F(p)\ll_{\delta,b,A}\langle F\rangle_*+L^{-A}, \tag{5} $$

when $\langle F\rangle_*\ge0$ (as in our applications where $F$ is a square everywhere). This is **not** equidistribution of primes in arbitrary progressions to level one. There is no asymptotic in (5); a common majorant for the primes is summed against the *unexpanded nonnegative statistic* before evaluation, and (4) is needed. In particular bounding the signed coefficients one at a time by Brun–Titchmarsh would lose the centering.

This uses the classical **enveloping sieve** principle, not a new sieve for primes: compare Ramaré, *On Šnirel'man's constant* (1995), Ramaré–Ruzsa, *Additive properties of dense subsets of sifted sequences* (2001), and Green–Tao, *Restriction theory of the Selberg sieve, with applications* (2006). We prove the particular uniform signed-cell error estimate (5) here rather than assume it as a quoted theorem.

*Small-prime weight.* Take $z=L^C$ with $C$ a sufficiently large fixed constant specified by the errors below. On primes up to $z$ use inclusion-exclusion truncated at an even integer $k\asymp\log L$, the sum $S(n)=\sum_{e\mid n,\ e\mid\prod_{p\le z}p,\ \omega(e)\le k}\mu(e)$. It is nonnegative on integers (alternating binomial sum) and one at primes above $z$. There are $X^{o(1)}$ terms, since $e\le z^k$. On a reduced class $r\bmod l$, small primes of $l$ cannot divide $n$; the remaining small-prime expected multiplier under uniform integers in the class is

$$ S_l=\prod_{p\le z,\ p\nmid l}(1-1/p)+O_B(L^{-B}) $$

for each fixed $B$ (here $B$ is an accuracy parameter). Indeed the adjacent Bonferroni error is at most $(\sum_{p\le z}1/p)^{k+1}/(k+1)!$, with $\sum_{p\le z}1/p=O_C(\log\log L+1)$. This is simultaneous for every $l\le X$.

*Remaining weight.* Put $T=X^{\delta/8}$ (reduce $\delta$ to at most $1/2$ if necessary), $\mathcal D=\{d\le T:\mu^2(d)=1,\ p\mid d\Rightarrow p>z\}$. On this set there are real $\lambda_d$, $\lambda_1=1$, $|\lambda_d|\le1$, such that

$$ G=\sum_{d,e\in\mathcal D}{\lambda_d\lambda_e\over[d,e]}=1/\sum_{h\in\mathcal D}1/\varphi(h)\ll_\delta {\log z\over L}. $$

For completeness use $(d,e)=\sum_{h\mid d,e}\varphi(h)$ to diagonalize the quadratic form as $\sum_h\varphi(h)y_h^2$, $y_h=\sum_{d:\,h|d}\lambda_d/d$. Inversion gives $\lambda_1=\sum\mu(h)y_h$. The minimizer has $y_h=G\mu(h)/\varphi(h)$. Inverting yields $|\lambda_d|=G(d/\varphi(d))\sum_{k\le T/d,\,(k,d)=1,\,k\in\mathcal D}1/\varphi(k)\le1$: the factor $d/\varphi(d)$ restores all divisor choices from $d$, their products with $k$ lying in $\mathcal D$. For the denominator lower bound take the Euler product on $z<p\le T^{c_0}$ weighted by $1/\varphi(h)$; by Mertens it has mass $\asymp\log T/\log z$. In its normalized law the expected $\log h=\sum(\log p)/p\le2c_0\log T$ for large $T$, so with $c_0=1/8$ at least half the mass has $h\le T$. Use $G(n)=(\sum_{d\mid n,d\in\mathcal D}\lambda_d)^2$ as the large-prime weight. Its mean $G_l$ on a uniform-integer reduced class mod $l$ differs from $G$ by $O(L^4/z)$: deletion affects only pairs with a prime $p>z$ dividing both $l$ and $[d,e]$. Writing $d=j d',e=j e'$ with $j=(d,e)$ gives $1/[d,e]=1/(j d'e')$. The absolute sum over those triples with $p|jd'e'$ is $O(L^3/p)$ by the harmonic sum, even dropping coprimality. Sum over the $O(L)$ primes of $l$. This comparison of means uses signed (optimal) weights but bounds the deletion error absolutely.

*Interval and normalization.* The nonnegative product $S(n)G(n)$ is 1 on primes of the interval. In an individual reduced class its interval sum is $X S_l G_l/l+O(T^2 X^{o(1)})$ by expansion and one $O(1)$ rounding error per term. The small/large prime sets are disjoint, so their local averages multiply even after excluding primes of $l$. Put $P_z=\prod_{p\le z}(1-1/p)$. Since $\prod_{p|l,\ p>z}(1-1/p)=1+O(L/z)$, the main term divided by $X$ equals

$$ {P_zG\over\varphi(l)}+O\big(L^{-A-b-3}/\varphi(l)\big),\qquad P_zG\ll_\delta L^{-1}, $$

on taking $C>A+b+10$ and then $B>A+b+10$. The common factor is essential because $F$ is signed *after* expansion. Multiply by $c_i$, sum, and use (4) for both error terms. Dividing by the dyadic prime count $\asymp X/L$ proves (5). In particular no claim of an asymptotic at an exceptional character modulus, and no well-factorability/CRT compatibility of the class assignments across different cells is needed. The slack $\delta$ must be fixed first.

### 4.1 Arbitrary fixed factorial moments in **balanced** cubes: reverse the partial pin

**PROVED (rel. elementary CRT, the z79 §1 injection, §§6.1,11 local completions and §11 conic configuration count, and §3 for the prime corollary).** Let $v=2m\ge2$ be fixed. In each of the three chart constructions above one can fill the simplex $\sum t_i<1/v$ with narrow cubes **without a large-last-coordinate condition**. For the pair chart keep the cubes strictly off $t_1=t_2$ (on both sides); this sacrifices arbitrarily little volume and ensures a swapped atom is not in the same cube. For fixed $\xi>0$, outside $O_{v,\xi}(\pi(X)/\log^{sm}X)$ primes one has, for $s=2,3$ respectively,

$$ R(p)\ge (9/(2\pi^2v^2)-\xi)\log^2 p,\qquad f_{II}(p)\ge(1/(14\zeta(3)v^3)-\xi)\log^3p. \tag{7} $$

Thus for $v=4$ even the old $9/(32\pi^2)$ and $1/(896\zeta(3))$ coefficients (less fixed tolerance) have squared exceptional rates, $\log^{-4}$ and $\log^{-6}$. The $v=2$ endpoint is (6), not an arbitrarily high log saving **at** the full coefficient of (6). The new moment step uses no cancellation of signed mixed large-gcd errors: **expose the candidate at a divisor of its own period first**, not at the lcm of the earlier full pins. Mixed prime factors of many earlier periods need not divide a single small determinant.

**Haar lemma.** Fix a narrow log cube $J$ as above, free coordinates $\ge X^g$, width $w<g/100$, no base fibre ($Q=1$). Write $f_j,x_j$ for each atom's full period and unit target, including period $4ab$ for a pair atom. Let $Z$ be its *unweighted* count, and $M=K_s\sum_J 1/(4acd)$ (or $K_2\sum_J 1/(4ab)$ for the pair chart), harmonic sums over unrestricted coordinates, before gcd and parity tests. Only in this lemma $M$ denotes the harmonic center, not the numerator, and $b$ in factorial subscripts denotes the number of pins, not an outer coordinate or the log-loss in (4). For every fixed $0\le b\le v$, $\langle (Z)_b\rangle_*=M^b+O(X^{-\kappa})$ for some $\kappa>0$ depending on the fixed boxes and $v$. This is a log-growing mean, power-accurate factorial comparison in a single cube. It is **not** asserted uniformly over arbitrary increasing $v$. We prove it next; $L=\log X$ as in §3.

*Step 1: partial conditioning at one candidate.* For any $j$, divisor $k\mid f_j$ and $T'=X^\eta$ with fixed $\eta>0$, define
$$ B(j,k)=\sum_{i\ne j,\ (f_i,f_j)>T'}\Pr(i\text{ hits}\mid n=x_j\bmod k). $$
Then uniformly for the trial charts $B(j,k)\ll X^{-c}$ for some $c>0$. This is also true for the pair chart except on a set of $j$ of total **unconditional harmonic mass** $\sum_{j\ \rm bad}1/f_j\ll X^{-c}$. On all rows $B(j,k)\le X^{o(1)}$. In fact the sum of all hit probabilities under *any* one unit condition of polynomial modulus $u$ is $\le X^{o(1)}$: split by the exact gcd $k'=(f_i,u)$, use $\Pr(i\mid u)\ll X^{o(1)}(k'/f_i)1_{x_i=z\bmod k'}$, and the dyadic counts below with $h'=k'$; $k'\le f_i$ so the final term in each count is $O(1)$. The number of choices is $\tau(u)=X^{o(1)}$.

To prove the assertion about $B$, split by $h'=(f_i,f_j)>T'$ and $k'=(f_i,k)\mid h'$, both divisors of $f_j$ (primes on $h',k'$ here distinguish these exact gcds from the dummy indices in (P) below, not the removal of factors of 4 as in z79 §11). *Step 2: what is uniform before reversing the pins.* Expose $b$ distinct earlier hits with one compatible unit residue $z\bmod u$, $u=\operatorname{lcm}(f_{i_1},\ldots,f_{i_b})$. Let $j$ be a new candidate. Use exactly the conditional probability (6) of z79 §6.1, with $f=f_j$:
$$ {1\over f}\sum_{e\mid f,\,(e,u)=1}\mu^2(e)/\varphi(e)\, \sum_{k\mid h\mid u} c_u(h,k)\, k\,1_{h\mid f}\,1_{z=x_j\ (k)}. \tag{P} $$

**Transfer lemma.** On every such rectangle $\mathbb E_{X<p\le2X}(Z(p)-m_J)^2\ll_J L^2$, taking $m_J$ the *exact Haar mean*. As in §3 majorize the prime indicator on the nonnegative square by $S(n)G(n)$, here multiplied by a fixed $\Psi(n/X)\ge0$ smooth compactly supported on $(1/2,3)$ and $\ge1$ on $[1,2]$. For arbitrarily small fixed $\rho>0$ choose the large-prime sieve cutoff $T=X^{\rho/4}$. The small-prime weight has $X^{o(1)}$ divisors of size $X^{o(1)}$. On opening the product the number of terms with multiplicity is $\le X^\rho$, all coefficients in modulus $\le1$ before grouping and each condition is $t\mid n$, $t\le X^\rho$. The expected uniform-integer main term of a compatible reduced cell is unchanged except for $\int\Psi$: use the zero Poisson frequency. The comparison in §3 with $P_zG/\varphi(l)$ still holds with any desired inverse log error divided by $\varphi(l)$, since now $l\ll X^2$ still has $O(L)$ prime factors. The signed expansion costs only $O(L^4)$ in absolute Haar mass. Since $P_zG\ll_\rho1/L$, it is enough to make the **total nonzero-mode error** $O(X^{1-c})$ before dividing by $\pi(2X)-\pi(X)$, some $c>0$. The issue is no longer the zero-mode local factor. Below we always mean the same smooth $\Psi$ on all cells, including the constant; the local main term multiplies $\langle(Z-m_J)^2\rangle_*$, not $\langle Z^2\rangle_*$.

For the constant and one-cell terms one can use the ordinary $O_\Psi(1)$ per progression smooth counting error; there are $X^{y+O(w)+o(1)}$ atoms on a rectangle of log width $w$, paying a log power for the center. For two cells with the *same* $a$, the numerator of the bilinear estimate below would be zero. If $q=q'$ this is just one atom. For distinct $q,q'$ the actual interval sum *and* the zero-frequency main term cost $O_\Psi(X/[q,q'])$ per pair per sieve term: these hits have $[q,q']\mid N+a$ and $0<N+a\ll X$, so this bound holds even when the lcm exceeds $X$ (ignore the sieve divisibility for this positive upper bound). Write $g=(q,q')$. The distinct integers $q/g,q'/g$ lie in the same class $-\bar g\bmod 4a$. In particular $g\ll Q_{\max}/a$, with $Q_{\max}$ the largest period scale in this rectangle. At fixed $a,g$ there are $O((Q_{\max}/(ga))^2)$ such pairs, since the unrounded factor is then bounded below. Multiplication by $Xg/Q_{\min}^2$, summation over $g$ and then $a$ gives $O(X^{1-x+O(w)+o(1)})$. This same-$a$ error needs only $x>0$ and $w,\rho$ sufficiently small compared to $x$; the separate requirement $x>s$ ensures the point is in the central region. In these formulas $x,y$ now denote any representative exponents in the fixed rectangle, not an integration variable.

For $a\ne a'$ write $q'=4a'd'-1$, $g=(q,q')$, $q=gm$, $q'=gn$, $l=gmn$. The letter $n$ **only in this Poisson calculation** denotes the reduced second period; denote the input integer by $N$. Compatibility requires $g\mid a-a'$, so there are at most $X^{o(1)}$ choices of $g$ for each fixed pair $a,a'$ and $g\le X^{x+O(w)}$. Incompatible cells, including $(t,l)>1$, contribute zero. When $(t,l)=1$, the normalized CRT residue of $N/t$ mod $l$, with $u=(a-a')/g$, is

$$ -a/(tl)+ u\,\overline{tm}/n + a\,\overline{qn}/t\pmod1. \tag{CRT} $$

Indeed $N=-a+qj$ requires $j\equiv u\bar m\ (n)$ and $j\equiv a\bar q\ (t)$; divide the resulting expression for $j$ by $tn$. Bars are inverses modulo the indicated denominator, and the last term is zero mod 1 at $t=1$. Thus Poisson gives $X/(tl)$ times the sum over integers $k$ of $\widehat\Psi(kX/(tl))$ times the exponential of $k$ times (CRT) (or simultaneous conjugation with the opposite Fourier convention). Split $m,n$ by residue mod $t$, at cost $\le t^2$. The last phase is then a $k$-weight; the first phase and Fourier envelope are smooth. The genuine oscillation is $e(ku\overline{tm}/n)$, with $(tm,n)=1$. The congruences $gm\equiv-1\ (4a), gn\equiv-1\ (4a')$ cost no multiplicity at fixed $a,a',g$. In particular $g$ is invertible at each of these small moduli for nonempty sums. Keeping a parity restriction on $d,d'$ can only decrease the support norms.

**Trilinear calculation.** The precise input is Bettin–Chandee, arXiv:1502.00769v1, Thm 1 (archived PDF `sources/bettin-chandee-1502.00769.pdf`), with arbitrary complex coefficients on three dyadic intervals and $(m_1,n)=1$. Its bound, apart from the product of the three $\ell^2$ norms, for phase $e(\vartheta k\bar m_1/n)$, $k\asymp B$, $m_1\asymp M$, $n\asymp N_1$, is

$$ (1+|\vartheta|B/(MN_1))^{1/2}\big[(BMN_1)^{7/20+\eta}(M+N_1)^{1/4}+(BMN_1)^{3/8+\eta}(BM+BN_1)^{1/8}\big], $$

for nonzero integer $\vartheta$ and arbitrary fixed $\eta>0$. Use BC's frequency variable $a_{BC}=|k|$, length $A_{BC}=B$, and $m_1=tm$, $\vartheta=\pm u\ne0$. The zero mode was excluded and negative frequencies use the opposite sign, equivalently conjugation. In this calculation $t$ is the sieve divisor only; $B$ is the frequency length, not the marked divisor function in §5.4. Here $n=q'/g$ is a reduced period, not the input integer. In a dyadic pair of $q,q'$ put $Q_1,Q_2$ for their scales, $F=X^y$, $A=X^x$, $Y=F/g$, $H=tQ_1 Q_2/(gX)$. Write $\mathcal E=X^{O(w+\rho+\eta)}$ below (absolute implied coefficients, fixed cutoffs). Their exact dyadic scales are $M\asymp tQ_1/g$ and $N_1\asymp Q_2/g$, i.e. $tY$ and $Y$ up to $X^{O(w)}$, both between $Y/\mathcal E$ and $\mathcal E Y$. The product of their coefficient norms is $\ll\mathcal E Y/A$: each support in $m$ or $n$ has $O(1+Q_i/(g a_i))$ points since the residue is unique, and $Q_i\gg g a_i$ with power slack. Embedding $m\mapsto tm$ does not increase its norm. The $k$-norm is $O(\sqrt B)$. The factor $1+|u|B/(MN_1)$ is bounded for $B\le X^\eta\max(1,H)$: one has $x<1/40$, $y<1/2+1/106$, $g\le\mathcal E X^x$. Frequencies beyond this cutoff cost $O(X^{-10})$ overall by Schwartz decay of arbitrarily high fixed order.

To justify separation of all variables, extract $1/H$ from $X/(tl)$ on the dyad. As functions of $m/(Q_1/g),n/(Q_2/g),|k|/B$ in fixed positive compact sets, all derivatives of the remaining smooth weight, to any fixed order, are $O_j((1+B/H)^{-10})$. Indeed the argument of $\widehat\Psi$ is of size $B/H$ and the phase $ka/(tl)$ is $O((a/X)B/H)$ with the same scaled derivative bound; increase the Schwartz order to pay for differentiation. Extend with smooth cutoffs past the dyad and use the absolutely convergent Fourier series in these three real variables (hard rectangular endpoints stay on their coefficient supports). The separated terms have uniformly summable coefficients with the same decay; $e(k a\overline{qn}/t)$ stays on the $k$ side. No joint geometric smoothness of the CRT residues is presumed. The core norm bounds then give, per dyad per fixed $a,a',g,t$ and residue split, at most

$$ \mathcal E\, A^{-1}\big[Y^{39/20} H^{-3/20}+Y^{15/8}\big]. \tag{B} $$

Indeed $(1+B/H)^{-10} B^{17/20}/H\ll H^{-3/20}$ for the first term, and $(1+B/H)^{-10}B/H\ll1$ for the second. This is valid even if $H<1$; the factor $H^{-3/20}$ is kept, not replaced by an optimistic unit cutoff. The harmless $(BMN_1)^\eta$ costs $\mathcal E$.

Summing (B) over $a,a'$ costs $\mathcal E A^2$, over $g\mid a-a'$ only $X^{o(1)}$, over all $t$ and their residue splits $\ll X^{3\rho}$, and dyadic choices cost a log power. Since $H\asymp X^{2y-1+O(w)}t/g$, the net powers of $g$ in the two terms are $-39/20+3/20$ and $-15/8$, both nonpositive. The total powers of $X$ before the arbitrarily small overhead are therefore

$$ x+(39/20)y-(3/20)(2y-1)=39/40+x+(33/20)s,\qquad x+(15/8)y. $$

Both are strictly below 1 in (W) (the second is smaller throughout). On a fixed interior compact subset first fix its exponent slack, then choose $w,\rho,\eta$ sufficiently small, uniformly for its finitely many rectangles. This proves the transfer lemma. In particular the sparsity of the periods is paid for twice in the two $\ell^2$ norms, not treated as a positive-density set; forgetting the sparse norms would give a strictly smaller patch. No beyond-level prime theorem is used.

**Area and endpoint.** In the $(x,s)$ plane (W) is a triangle of base $1/40$ and height $1/106$ since $1+33/20=53/20$. The absolute Jacobian from $(\log a,\log d)/L$ to $(x,s)$ is one for exponent arithmetic. There are two orientations, each with normalized area twice its planar area, so their combined additional mass is $1/2120$. Fill by disjoint compact rectangles with total mass arbitrarily close to this, using the finite-tolerance convention in §4. Apply Chebyshev on the prime variance and use the already-disjoint corner bounds; for both parities use a finite union bound. This proves (BC), including its exceptional rate after dyadic summation. No joint moment between distant rectangles or the older corners is necessary for this lower bound. Equilateral boxes still fail the estimate: formally $x=1/3,s=1/6$ gives first-term budget $19/12>1$. This is not a lower bound on the true discrepancy.

### 5.4 What the untied shifted-divisor asymptotics do not estimate

**Exact reduction / CONDITIONAL on the marked estimate below (not just ordinary shifted $d_3$).** Use a fixed finite collection of interior log rectangles as in §5.1, now in the unfilled central part. Write $B_J(t,u)$ for the number of admissible ordered factorizations $abd=t$, $a+b=u$, with the two free letters in rectangle $J$ of the assigned chart; impose the same eligibility as on the atoms. For $N$ in the support of the test below, each hit gives one triple, and conversely. Indeed in a trial chart $b=(N+a)/(4ad-1)>0$; in a pair chart $d=(N+a+b)/(4ab)$ is positive integral. Even at composite inputs eligible trial atoms need not satisfy prime-only coprimalities of the recovered letter, so use *exactly* the chosen chart's predicates in $B_J$, not restrictions from a different chart. Let $W(N)=\Psi(N/X) S(N)G(N)$ as in §5.3, zero for $N\le0$. On its support $t\asymp X$; $a,b,d$ are automatically polynomially bounded (and we may restrict $t,u$ to the induced bounded ranges in the following sums). Put

$$ A_0=\sum_N W(N),\quad A_1=\sum_{t,u} B_J(t,u) W(4t-u),\quad A_2=\sum_{t,h,u} B_J(t,u) B_J(t+h,u+4h) W(4t-u). $$

All are finite after requiring both words to have their common input on the support. Then $A_2-2m_J A_1+m_J^2 A_0=\sum_N W(N)(Z(N)-m_J)^2$ exactly. Consequently, **if** for every such fixed rectangle this signed combination is $o(X L^3)$, the central remainder has the required concentration and $R(p)/M_{BM}(p)\to1$ in prime probability by §5.1, including its BT trimming for the upper direction. This is the composite-side version of (H) in §5.1 restated in marked shifted-divisor variables; §5.3 supplies it unconditionally on (W). This formulation permits an upper bound rather than an asymptotic for each moment separately. It needs *no* mixed estimates between distant corners and boxes: use the triangle inequality in $L^2$ on a fixed finite collection. Conversely, a unit-density statement transferring just the ordinary unweighted all-integer moment to primes without paying for their density and local factors does not prove this result. The $h=0$ term includes coincidences of product and outer sum, not just identical words without a separate argument.

At the equilateral dyad $|h|\ll X^{1/3}$; a single ordinary dyadic cube has bounded expected count, not a $\log^2 X$ mean. It is the *collection* of boxes (or a fixed-width log rectangle) that contributes a leading coefficient. Elsewhere the scale is $|h|\ll\max(a,b)$ up to box variation. To see the projection lost in the cited theorems, hold two physical boxes fixed, take a modulus $D_0$ larger than the full range of $|u'-u-4h|$, and insert $D_0^{-1}\sum_{j\bmod D_0} e_{D_0}(j(u'-u-4h))$. At an equilateral dyad one can take $D_0\asymp X^{1/3}$. The untwisted correlation is only $j=0$ (and even it inherits the box restriction and the $W(4t-u)$ weight). **Assessment (published comparison).** BBMZ, *Averages of shifted convolutions of $d_3(n)$*, arXiv:1101.5464, Thms 1–2, and Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao, *Correlations of the von Mangoldt and higher divisor functions I*, arXiv:1707.01315, treat products of ordinary divisor functions rather than these marked Fourier coefficients. The latter gets arbitrary fixed log saving on average for $H\ge X^{8/33+\eta}$ in its long-shift theorem; the room in $H$ does not estimate even one nonzero $j$ here. For BBMZ distinguish the signed mean of Thm 1 from the mean square in the longer range of Thm 2. The frequency sum requires control at total scale $o(X L^3)$ for the sieve-weighted, centered aggregate, not just any power of $\log X$ saved over $XH$ for unrestricted pairs.

**PROVED (scope: loss from discarding the marks).** Even exact unmarked correlations at *all* shifts do not give a uniform transfer to the tied count for arbitrary marks in the prescribed label interval. For example attach one mark to each integer $X<t\le2X$ in an interior interval of labels of length $4B$, $B\asymp X^{1/3}$. One choice has all marks equal; another takes $u(t)=u_*+4(t\bmod B)$. For $1\le h\le B/2$, the first gives zero ties, the second at least $(X-h)(1-h/B)-O(B)$, with identical unmarked autocorrelations. Thus the nonzero-frequency aggregate can be of order $XB$ versus the $O(X)$ scale after division of the unmarked aggregate by the label range. Narrowing the allowed label window only replaces $B$ by its new width in this example. These are mock marks, not factorizations of $t$ and not a disproof of the desired estimate for true $a+b$. The arithmetic solution on patch (W) uses the actual marked congruences, not artificial independence in this step.

**Chart O (outer letter small, trial period).** Improve (B) by grouping the variable $u=(a-a')/g$ with the Poisson frequency *before* applying BC. **Chart D (middle letter small, trial period).** One cannot use $-a$ as a small target when $d$ is small. Write $v=4d,w'=4d'$, so $q=va-1,q'=w'a'-1$. On the two cells $vN+1=qB_1, w'N+1=q'B_2$. ### 5.6 Conditional endpoint and the exceptional-rate limitation

**CONDITIONAL on Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłł, *The mean square of the product of the Riemann zeta-function with Dirichlet polynomials*, Conjecture 1 (1.4), arXiv:1411.7764v1.** This is a strong conjecture: by their Corollary 1 it implies the Lindelöf hypothesis, and the authors believe it strictly stronger; we are not deducing this from Lindelöf.
