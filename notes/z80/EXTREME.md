## 1. Kill a factor base, not a congruence menu

**Theorem (PROVED rel. ET, arXiv:1107.1010, Props 2.2, 2.6 and Lemma 2.8).** Write every positive solution in increasing order $x\le y\le z$. Let $P^+(x)$ be its largest prime factor, with $P^+(1)=1$. Given $A>0$, there exists $c_A>0$ such that, apart from $O_A(X/\log^A X)$ primes $p\in(X,2X]$, **every solution** has

$$P^+(x)> X^{c_A\log_3 X/\log_2 X}.$$

Here $\log_j$ denotes the iterated natural logarithm. One can insist on actual solutions: a relative-density-one set of primes $p\equiv1\pmod{840}$ has minimum, over its nonempty solution set, exceeding $\exp((\log p)^\kappa)$ for every fixed $\kappa<1$ (in fact the single displayed bound with $A=2$ works). For this intrinsic complexity the supremal stretched-log power is **1**, not $1/2$: $x\le3p/4$. This does not give $p^\delta$ for fixed $\delta>0$. It is a *multiplicative* obstruction for the actual denominators, not an improvement of z78's $1/4$ or z79's $1/3$ for their invariants. Primes in the empty set of solutions contribute no witnesses to the upper count in the proof.

**Proof.** Fix an integer $x$, and count the inputs for which it occurs as the smallest denominator, with multiplicity. ET with $a\le b$ has $x=acd$ in I and $x=abd$ in II. The former has $p=4acd-f,\ ef=4a^2d+1$; the latter has $p=4abd-e,\ ce=a+b$. Hence a majorant for the number of incoming sorted prime witnesses is

$$w(x)=\sum_{acd=x}\tau(4a^2d+1)+\sum_{abd=x}\tau(a+b). \tag{1}$$

No independence of solutions, primality of the incoming value, or local solubility is being assumed. In I a divisor $f$ fixes $p,e,b$; in II a divisor $e$ fixes $p,c$. Crucially it is $x$ rather than an unbounded larger denominator which indexes this *reverse* correspondence.

We give details of the only moment estimate needed: $\sum_{x\le Z}w(x)^2\ll Z\log^C(2Z)$, some absolute $C$. Cauchy on the factorisations costs $O(\tau_3(x))$ times the sum of the squared summands. Cauchy once more in the sum over triples separates this weight: its squared norm is $\sum_{x\le Z}\tau_3(x)^3\ll Z\log^{O(1)}(2Z)$. Indeed $\tau_3(n)^3\le\tau_{27}(n)$ (encode the three ordered factor allocations of each prime exponent by a 3-way table with those one-dimensional marginals, for example by aligning the three sorted lists of labels); sum the divisor convolution. For the remaining fourth powers partition the three variables into dyadic boxes of product $O(Z)$. For $\tau(a+b)^4$, average in the larger side $v\asymp V$, keeping the smaller fixed; the sum of shifted fourth powers is at most $\sum_{m\le4V}\tau(m)^4\ll V\log^{O(1)}(2V)$. For $\tau(4a^2d+1)^4$ average in the larger of $a,d$. This leaves a polynomial $P(v)=4dv^2+1$ or $4a^2v+1$, of size $O(V^3)$ and with at most two roots modulo *each* prime power (none at 2 or a coefficient prime; otherwise nonsingular at odd primes). Uniformity in the fixed coefficient matters here.

For clarity the elementary polynomial divisor bound is as follows (compare z78 WILD_ES §6.2). In a value $P(v)$ strip off individual primes $>V$, bounded in number with multiplicity. Greedily group the remaining prime factors in order into blocks of product $\le V$; successive blocks taken in pairs have product $>V$, so the total number of blocks is absolutely bounded for $V\ge2$. By submultiplicativity, for some absolute $k$, $\tau(P(v))^4\ll\sum_{h\mid P(v),\ h\le V}\tau(h)^k$ (take a block maximizing the divisor count). Count values per root by $O(V/h)$ and sum an Euler product with local coefficient at $q^j$ at most $2(j+1)^k$. This costs $O(V\log^{O(1)}(2V))$ including all prime powers, uniformly. The bounded side $V=1$ causes no problem. The $O(\log^3(2Z))$ boxes suffice. This proves the moment claim.

For any set $S\subset[1,2X]$, the number of solutions at primes $X<p\le2X$ with $x\in S$ is consequently $\ll (X|S|)^{1/2}\log^{C/2}(2X)$. This **sparse-set transfer** also allows $S$ chosen adversarially for the entire dyadic interval. It is not a per-$p$ estimate for sets $S_p$. Apply it to $Y$-smooth integers with $Y=X^{1/u}$, $u=K\log_2 X/\log_3 X$. Here is the smooth bound without short-interval inputs: set $s=1-\log u/(2\log Y)$. Rankin gives $|S|\le(2X)^s\prod_{q\le Y}(1-q^{-s})^{-1}$ for $S$ the smooth set. For large $X$, $s\ge3/4$, so higher powers in the log product cost $O(1)$. Splitting at $Y^{1/\log u}$, or partial summation with $\pi(t)\ll t/\log t$, gives

$$\sum_{q\le Y}q^{-s}\ll \log_2 Y+\sqrt u\log(2\log u).$$

Indeed below the split $q^{1-s}\le\sqrt{\mathrm e}$, above it $q^{1-s}\le\sqrt u$ and the reciprocal prime sum is $O(1+\log\log u)$. For fixed $K$ the second term is $o(\log_2 X)$. Thus $|S|/X\le\exp((-K/2+O(1)+o_K(1))\log_2 X)$. Take $K$ sufficiently large depending on $C,A$. The number of exceptional primes is at most the number of incoming witnesses. This proves the estimate. To see nonvacuity on density one one can use the classical almost-all ES result, or the following elementary contrast. For $p=1\ (840)$ write $x_0=(p+3)/4\equiv1\ (3)$. If $q\equiv2\ (3)$ divides $x_0$, take II with $(a,b,c,d)=(1,q,(q+1)/3,x_0/q)$: $x=x_0$. Conversely if none does, all divisors of $(px_0)^2$ are $1\ (3)$, whereas $3y-px_0\equiv-1\ (3)$ would divide it. For any finite list of such $q>7$ the proportion of primes in this progression missing them all tends to $\prod_q(1-1/(q-1))$ by fixed-modulus PNT; this tends to zero as the list grows. Thus **the same density-one set can have minimum additive gap $x-p/4$ exactly $3/4$ (`min_gap` in the CSV is $4x-p$, hence 3)**, and indeed this solution needs a large prime in $x_0$. No comparable claim follows about any supplied prime. ∎

**Dual, for a single intrinsic threshold (PROVED, same inputs).** There is a natural version measuring the trial-divisor range for the actual two-fraction test $( (4x-p)y-px)((4x-p)z-px)=(px)^2$. For $x\ge2$ let $B(x)$ be the least integer $B\ge1$ such that dividing $x$ by all prime powers supported on primes $\le B$ leaves remainder $\le B^2$ with no prime $\le B$ (thus 1 or a certified prime). Put $B(1)=1$. A factor-base certificate for the divisor test needs no factorization oracle at this height: factor out those primes; if the cofactor is nontrivial it is prime by size. This is the least such **trial-division certificate**, not the runtime of arbitrary factorization algorithms. We have $\sqrt{P^+(x)}\le B(x)\le\lceil\sqrt{x}\rceil$. The first bound holds whether the largest prime was removed or left, the second by the size of $x$. Therefore the least $B$ over all solutions, **the same statistic on both sides**, has the preceding density-one lower bound (halve $c_A$), but *every* ES prime has a witness with $B\le\lceil\sqrt{3p/4}\rceil$; indeed every witness obeys this. This $\theta=1/2$ upper bound just uses the size of $x$; it is far from the $X^{o(1)}$ lower scale and no optimality for the minimum is asserted. For the uncapped $P^+(x)$ itself we have not proved a uniform $\theta<1$. Knowing an almost-all upper bound would not rule out a $p^\delta$ lower bound on a thin sequence.

**Order-statistic warning (PROVED, ET).** The lane brief's alternative, the second largest $h$ of the six normalized ET coordinates (take $a\le b$) is automatically $\ge(p/4)^{1/3}$ at *every* solution. In I, $pa\le bf$; if both $b,f\le h$ then $p\le h^2$, otherwise $a,c,d\le h$ and $p<4acd$. In II, $p<ef$; either both are $\le h$ or $a,b,d\le h$ and $p<4abd$. This polynomial result does not make any particular prime extreme. The other extreme, the minimum of all six coordinates, is $1$ on the very endpoint in `notes/z78/WILD_ES.md` §6.1 ($a=c=1$); those primes cannot be used to prove the minimum grows. These observations and the short-gap example explain why one should not identify the advertised exponents for different heights. The cubic information/level barrier for *seven-period avoidance* in z78 §6.2 is untouched. No energy inequality or growing-modulus prime transfer occurs in the new theorem.

**Assessment (non-identifiability of the extreme tail).** No observed full zero informs a zero component. Set the weight zero through the verified ES range. This is a **sensitivity countermodel for count-only data**, not a calibrated discovery of an arithmetic mechanism; it is not conditioned on each prime's individually verified witnesses. In particular it cannot assign positive conditional failure probability once a witness is known. A positive or divergent expectation is easy to manufacture, whereas the rare-component weight and exponent are not measurable here. No $f=0$ forecast beyond the explicitly fitted coarse model is justified.

## 6. Actual additive windows: a joint tail for arbitrary fixed shifts

**Theorem G (PROVED rel. the elementary Selberg upper-bound sieve and Mertens in fixed progressions).** Fix any $J$ distinct positive integers $k_j=3\bmod4$. For $p\equiv1\ (4)$ form $x_j=(p+k_j)/4$. Apart from

$$ O_{k_1,\ldots,k_J}\bigl(X/(\log X)^{1+J/2}\bigr) \tag{2} $$

primes in $(X,2X]$, at least one $x_j$ is the smallest denominator of a **Type II** solution. In particular $g(p)>4J-1$ costs $O_J((\log X)^{-J/2})$ in relative prime density in 𝔐. The same bound holds for failure restricted to any $J$ selected shifts (e.g. avoiding holes), and therefore also on asking for unrestricted I or II. This turns the joint dimension bookkeeping in `POINTWISE_WINDOW.md` §6 (there an Assessment with possible log-log losses) into an upper bound for **actual** failures, including exponent-budget failures; it does not promote the opposite lower bound. Constants are not uniform in $J$; external priority for (2) is unverified.

*Proof.* We use only squarefree divisor shots. Fix $k$, and for $x=(p+k)/4$ color every distinct prime factor $\ell$ of $x$ either selected or unselected by an independent fair coin. We may keep only factors in a prescribed set, for instance $\ell\le z$ avoiding a fixed finite exceptional list. Independently repeat this to obtain divisors $d_1,d_2$. For $p>k$ coprime to $k$, these are units mod $k$. If $d_1/d_2=-1\bmod k$, divide both by their gcd; then their product divides $x$, and both remain positive. With $a=d_1/(d_1,d_2), b=d_2/(d_1,d_2)$, set $d=x/(ab)$ and $c=(a+b)/k$. This is a Type II point ($4abd=p+k$); in the range of (2), $k$ fixed, $p>x$ and the other denominators $pacd,pbcd$ exceed $x$. Thus if the window fails, $\Pr(d_1/d_2=-1)=0$ even for the truncated experiment.

For a character $\chi\bmod k$ the transform of the ratio distribution is a **nonnegative real** product $\prod_{\ell\mid x}^{*} h_\chi(\ell)$, with $h_\chi=(1+\Re\chi)/2$. The star denotes the retained prime factors. Inverting at $-1$ gives a sum of these products with signs $\chi(-1)$, divided by $\varphi(k)$. The principal term is 1, the other even terms nonnegative. Hence failure is majorized by $\sum_{\chi(-1)=-1}\prod^* h_\chi(\ell)$. This uses no relaxation from budgeted exponents to generators of an abstract subgroup. Because the majorants are nonnegative, multiply all $J$ inequalities and expand, choosing one odd character $\chi_j$ per window. All constants and numbers of choices may depend on the fixed shifts.

Here are details of the upper sieve for a resulting product, to avoid assuming independence at prime inputs. Sieve the **integers** in $(X,2X]$, dropping the base progression for an upper bound. For every $\ell\le z$ above a constant depending on the shifts, always forbid class 0 (primality), and independently forbid each class $-k_j\pmod\ell$ with probability $1-h_{\chi_j}(\ell)$. All these classes are distinct for large enough $\ell$; discard the smaller primes from both tests. At a prime input in the prescribed progression the expected survivor indicator is exactly the desired product (provided $z<X$). Let $\nu_\ell$ be the realized number of classes, between 1 and $J+1$. **Uniformly in the realization**, the upper sieve bounds survivors by

$$ O_J(X)\prod_{\ell}^{*}(1-\nu_\ell/\ell)+O_J(X^{1/2}). \tag{3} $$

For concreteness use Selberg weights supported on squarefree $d\le D=X^{1/8}$ with primes in the test. The upper bound is $X/G(D)+O(\sum_{d,e\le D}\nu([d,e]))$, with $G(D)=\sum_{d\le D}\mu^2(d)\prod_{\ell|d}\nu_\ell/(\ell-\nu_\ell)$ over the support primes; the optimal weights have absolute value $\le1$ (equivalently use the usual Selberg bound with remainder $\sum_{t\le D^2}3^{\omega(t)}|r_t|$, $|r_t|\le\nu(t)$ by CRT). This error is $\ll_J D^2(\log X)^{O_J(1)}$. The unrestricted Euler product for $G(\infty)$, normalized to mass one, includes prime $\ell$ with probability $\nu_\ell/\ell$. Its mean log divisor is at most $(J+1)\sum_{\ell\le z}(\log\ell)/\ell\le 2(J+1)\log z$ for large $z$. Take $z=X^{1/(64(J+1))}$; Markov gives $G(D)\ge G(\infty)/2$. This proves (3) without a uniform fundamental lemma. Averaging (3) over the independent *sieve choices*, not over residue symbols at the supplied $p$, gives factors $1-(1+\sum_j(1-h_{\chi_j}(\ell)))/\ell$. Each $\chi_j$ is nonprincipal, so $\sum_{\ell\le z} \Re\chi_j(\ell)/\ell=O_{k_j}(1)$ by fixed-modulus Mertens. The product is $\ll (\log z)^{-1-J/2}$. Summing over the finitely many tuples proves (2). ∎

## 10. Full-window failure: isolate the leading events, not just an upper sieve

**Theorem H (PROVED rel. the upper sieve of §6, fixed-modulus Mertens).** Fix distinct shifts $K=(k_1,\ldots,k_J)$, all positive and $3\bmod4$. Discard the finitely many $p\le\max k_j$. In this section $p$ is prime, $p\in\mathfrak M$, $x_j=(p+k_j)/4$. “Failure” means that **no** coprime pair of positive divisors $u,v\mid p x_j$ satisfies $u+v=0\bmod k_j$. Coprimality implies $uv\mid px_j$; by §7 this tests both types at the actual smallest denominator. For an odd quadratic character $\chi$ of the unit group mod $k$ define $E(k,\chi)$ by “every prime divisor of $x$ belongs to $\ker\chi$”. Then, for $X<p\le2X$,

$$ \#\{\text{all windows fail}\}=\sum_{\chi_1,\dots,\chi_J}\#\bigcap_j E(k_j,\chi_j)+ O_K\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1+J/2+1/12}}\right). \tag{4} $$

Each sum is over the *odd quadratic* characters, not every odd character. This is a fixed-shift asymptotic **reduction with additive error**, not an asymptotic evaluation of the sums on the right. For one prime window compare `notes.md` Thms 70.5, 70.7, 70.9: the secondary saving $1/12$ improves the exponent in (70.28) for primes $k\equiv3\ (4)$ with $k\ge13$. External priority for H and I unverified. Formula (4) allows composite windows, intersections, and multiple competing quadratic characters.

*Proof.* For one window put $G=(\mathbb Z/k)^\times$. All factors of $px$ are units. In the event $E$, $\chi(p)=\chi(4x)=1$; hence a ratio of divisors of $px$ cannot equal $-1$. Conversely call a residue plentiful if it contains at least $|G|$ **distinct** prime factors of $x$. The subgroup $H$ generated by plentiful residues is entirely realized by squarefree divisors built from the plentiful classes: reduce each generator exponent to $[0,\operatorname{ord}(g)-1]$. This is the finite generator argument of `POINTWISE_WINDOW.md` Lemma 6.1. At failure $-1\notin H$. If $[G:H]=2$ and a prime factor $b$ of $x$ lay outside $H$, some divisor $d$ built from plentiful classes would realize $-b^{-1}\in H$. Then $bd\mid x$ and $bd=-1\bmod k$, impossible. Thus an index-two case really is $E$, not “almost all factors in a kernel”. A nongenus failure has index at least 3. In every failing window at most $|G|^2$ distinct factors lie outside its plentiful subgroup. Consequently a joint failure not in the union of the genus tuples requires subgroups $H_j$ of indices $i_j\ge2$, one $\ge3$, and only boundedly many exceptions per window. Majorize this constraint by $t^{-\sum_j |G_j|^2}$ times a product of weights $t$ per **distinct** exceptional prime, with $t=1/(12(J+1))$. Repeat (3), now randomly forbidding $-k_j$ with probability $1-t$ when the prime is outside $H_j$. Density costs sum to $(1-t)\sum_j(1-1/i_j)\ge J/2+1/12$ by fixed-modulus Mertens. Only finitely many subgroup choices occur. Finally two different genus tuples overlapping require index at least 4 in a differing coordinate (two distinct nontrivial quadratic characters are independent there). The same bound, even with strict exclusion, absorbs multiple counting. ∎

Existence of solutions on infinitely many such primes follows by subtracting the classical density-exception estimate for ES, e.g. Vaughan. No $g\ge11$ infinitude follows: I cannot be applied to both forms on the same prime.

**Conditional construction; what the prime-tuple hypotheses say.** `POINTWISE_SIZE.md` Prop 8.4 gives qualitative escape on Dickson. Here is an explicit nonvacuous variant. Let $T=4J-1$, $j=1,\ldots,J$, $k=4j-1$. Choose $Q=3360\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,T)\prod_{\ell\le T}\ell$ (prime product) and a prime $2T<b<4T$, $b=3\bmod4$, for large $T$ by PNT. Write $p=1+Qu$ and require $p=-1\bmod b$ (so $(a,c,d)=(1,1,(b+1)/4)$ gives a positive Type II solution). Impose primality on $p$ and the $J$ linear forms $1+Qu/(4j)$. They have distinct rational roots, positive slopes and are admissible after substituting $u=u_0+bv$: every prime $\le T$ divides each slope and the values there are 1; at $b$ the constant terms are nonzero since $p+k=k-1\ne0\bmod b$; any other prime $>T$ divides at most $J+1$ classes, unless dividing a slope (then check the constant: outside $b$ every prime of $Q$ has the same safe value 1 before the substitution). Thus fixed-tuple Dickson gives infinitely many such $p$ for each $T$. In $x_k=j\cdot(1+Qu/(4j))$ every prime of $j$ has $(\ell/k)=1$ as in I. Also $(x_k/k)=(p/k)=1$ since $p=1\bmod k$. If the cofactor is prime its symbol must then be $+1$. Thus **$T<g(p)<\infty$**. There is no bound in Dickson on how far one must go for the first hit.

**CONDITIONAL on a stated growing-tuple BH bound (stronger than ordinary BH).** For the displayed $v$-forms assume that for fixed $\epsilon>0$ and all large $T$, at $V=\exp((1+\epsilon)J\log J)$ the simultaneous-prime count on $(V,2V]$ is at least half its BH integral times the singular series. Then $g(p)>(4-o_\epsilon(1))\log p/((1+\epsilon)\log_2 p)$ infinitely often (read $o_\epsilon(1)\to0$ for fixed $\epsilon$). Indeed slopes and offsets are $\exp(O(T))$ choosing $0\le u_0<b$, so the integral is $\ge V/(\log V+O(T))^{J+1}$. The singular series is $\ge\exp(-O(J))$: primes $\le T$ only help, and for $\ell>T$ the log local ratio is $\ge-O(J^2/\ell^2)$ since distinct-root count is $\le J+1<\ell/2$. The expected count grows as $\exp((\epsilon+o(1))J\log J)$. This is a rate under an **extra uniformity hypothesis**, not under Dickson alone. Fixed-tuple BH on these particular forms gives only a subset with count of order $X/\log^{J+1}X$ at fixed $J$ (with constants depending on $T$); it does not give the proposed joint $X/\log^{1+J/2}X$ asymptotic. At 3 and 7 the half-dimensional costs add to 1, **not** $1/2$; BV plus the linear lower sieve stops near $X^{1/4}$, not $X^{1/2}$. The leftover configurations can contain two large wrong primes in either shift after fixing parities. See `POINTWISE_WINDOW2.md` §7 and `POINTWISE_WINDOW3.md` §4 for the specific switching constant deficit; no impossibility theorem about other methods, nor an unconditional simultaneous lower bound, is asserted.

## 11. Constants: Euler products we can prove versus a shifted-prime assumption

**PROVED (integer Landau constants, relative to classical Selberg–Delange).** For odd quadratic $\chi\bmod k$, let $B_\chi=\{\ell:\chi(\ell)\ne1\}$ (including ramified primes). Uniform integers $x\le H$ supported off $B_\chi$ have density $C(k,\chi)/\sqrt{\log H}\,(1+o(1))$, where

$$ C(k,\chi)=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\left[L(1,\chi)\prod_{\ell\mid k}(1-1/\ell)\prod_{\chi(\ell)=-1}(1-1/\ell^2)\right]^{1/2}. $$

Square the Dirichlet series of the good-primes semigroup and divide by $\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)$: the remaining factors are exactly the two products at exponent $s$. This is the prime-modulus `notes.md` (70.9) with the imprimitive Euler factors retained. Selberg–Delange at exponent $1/2$ gives the stated gamma factor. Distinct odd quadratic characters are indexed by squarefree $f\mid k$, $f=3\bmod4$, primitive character $\chi_f=(\cdot/f)$ on the units (zero out **all** nonunits mod $k$). The finite formula $L(1,\chi_f)=-\pi f^{-3/2}\sum_{a=1}^f a\chi_f(a)$ follows from the odd primitive quadratic functional equation; remove the Euler factors at primes of $k$ not dividing $f$.

**CONJECTURE / EVIDENCE, not an application of ordinary BH.** The following specifies model constants for **any fixed tuple**; an asymptotic on primes is not proved even for $k=3$. Given $r\in\mathfrak M$ as a single residue and a tuple $\chi_j$, take independent local Haar **unit** residues $P_\ell$, conditional on $P=r\ (840)$. Put $X_j=(P+k_j)/4$ locally (integral at 2). On the finitely many conductor primes form the signs $S_j=(P/f_j)$ by CRT. Define

$$ A_r(K)=\sum_{(\chi_j)}\prod_j C(k_j,\chi_j)\;\frac{\mathbb E_r\big[\prod_j(1+S_j)\prod_{\ell}\mathbf1_{P_\ell\ \mathrm{allowed}}\big]}{\prod_\ell\prod_{j:\ell\in B_{\chi_j}}(1-1/\ell)}. \tag{5} $$

The ratio means the **limit of quotients** truncated at $Y\to\infty$, not a quotient of zero limits. Allowed means $\ell\nmid X_j$ for all forbidden $j$ at that prime. The factor $1+S_j$ models the exact global sign locking of a semigroup supported on a quadratic kernel: it is 2 on the required sign, zero otherwise (compare the single integer semigroup, with no prime biases). Do not resample the same $P_\ell$ at distinct shifts or resample the $S_j$ independently. For $\ell\nmid840\prod k_j$ away from shift differences, if $b$ characters forbid $\ell$ the ordinary ratio is $(1-b/(\ell-1))/(1-1/\ell)^b=1+O_K(1/\ell^2)$; for colliding shifts count **distinct** forbidden unit classes, and a conductor prime can enter both sign and divisibility tests. Thus (5) defines an explicit convergent modeled coefficient. It assumes local factors and the one-variable gamma factors suffice for the **shifted correlation**, an unproved sieve prediction. If one assumes this semigroup correlation asymptotic for each tuple with error $o(X/\log^{1+J/2}X)$, H transfers it to the full-window count. This assumption is not the Bateman–Horn conjecture on any fixed list of polynomials. The modeled $c_{K}$ in the user's normalization is $\sum_r A_r(K)/192$ (whereas dividing by 6 averages *within* 𝔐). In particular the BH-like factors are not derived by fitting $g$.

**EVIDENCE.** For each of the six hard residues alike we get $A_r(3)\approx2.2132$, $A_r(7)\approx2.2405$, $A_r(3,7)\approx4.9260$; in the all-primes normalization respectively 0.06916, 0.07002 and 0.15394. `data/z80/model/coeff.csv` gives the 88 individual constants through 351 and adjacent two-window blocks. In the prime-product calculation expand the signs over subsets of $\{1,\dots,J\}$ and average the Legendre symbol or 1 over each finite conductor's allowed residues. At 2 the bad-divisor test is $P+k_j=0\bmod8$, **not** mod 2. The code truncates absolutely convergent products at 30,000, sufficient for illustration, not certified accuracy of the displayed final digits. Example in $(10^7,2\cdot10^7]$: among 18,878 hard primes full failures vs genus failures are respectively 10567/10567 at 3, 10818/10818 at 7, 6263/5554 at 11, 9014/5751 at 19 and 6010/6010 jointly at 3,7. These data are not an extrapolation of small-modulus character uniformity to every supplied $p$. Finite-modulus effects at growing $k$ are substantial, even when the fixed-$k$ remainder in (4) has a log-power saving.

## 12. An unfitted record model, and why not to trust its tail

**EVIDENCE / sensitivity model only.** Use Poisson input locations from 1009 up with intensity $dx/(192\log x)$ for each hard residue $r$. At $t=\log x$ treat the first $j_0=\lceil t\rceil$ windows in disjoint adjacent pairs. A single window misses with probability $s_j=\min(1,A_r(k_j)/\sqrt t)$ (index $j$ starting at 1, $k_j=4j-1$). For an adjacent pair take the both-miss probability $\min(s_{j-1},s_j,A_r(k_{j-1},k_j)/t)$, at even $j$. Equivalently these are conditional survival probabilities for first-exceedances, multiplying completed blocks and using the single-window probability on an incomplete block. Only this **nested tail** is used; this construction need not realize all single-window marginals after a first hit. Different pairs are independent given $r$. This is a deliberately cheap approximation to (5), not a conjecture of uniformity in $J$.

Beyond $j_0$ use **one shared late search**: with probability $z=\exp(-3t^2/(4\pi^2))$ all remaining windows miss; otherwise one index is chosen uniformly from the remaining candidates up to $4x_{\min}-p\le2p$ (use $\lfloor(2p+1)/4\rfloor$ total indices). The scale of $-\log z$ is the Poisson zero model on *unordered* (★) words, mean approximately half the leading ordered scale $3t^2/(2\pi^2)$ (`STATUS.md` intro). It is an explicit ancillary assumption about the conditional large-window tail, **not** a conditional mean theorem. Beyond $j_0$ no independence of consecutive trials is assumed; there are not $2p$ independent half-dimensional sieves. The choices $j_0=\lceil\log p\rceil$ and uniform residual index are modeling conventions, not fitted parameters or derived cutoffs. As (★) is only sufficient, its zero probability is an upper proxy for failure; the chosen exponential is not a rigorous upper bound. The model has finite expected total failures; the predictions are far from identified by fixed-$J$ data. For example, after $t=86$ one can complete the model with the same rules and fixed-shift formula at all required $k$; the entire expected zero contribution there is bounded by $\int_{86}^\infty e^{t-3t^2/(4\pi^2)}dt/(32t)<10^{-200}$, regardless of prefix survival. The reported figure integrates from $10^{18}$ up to $e^{86}$; a midpoint quadrature is sufficient at its stated precision.
