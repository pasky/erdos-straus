# Polynomial pullbacks of the three-quarter exceptional-set bound

**Theorem P (PROVED rel. Paredes–Sasyk, Thorner–Zaman, Stark, Landau–Page, elementary function-field theory, and the internal z79 finite sieve below).** For each fixed $d\ge1$ there are positive $b_d,c_d$ such that for every nonconstant $F\in\mathbb Z[Y]$ of degree at most $d$, $\|F\|_\infty\le\exp(b_d(\log H)^{1/4})$, in every interval of $H$ consecutive integers,

$$\#\{j:F(j)>0,\ 4/F(j)\text{ has no positive integral triple}\}\ll_d H\exp(-c_d(\log H)^{3/4}).$$

Either leading sign; no separability or monodromy assumption on $F$ itself. In fact by a union bound the estimate holds for the **union** of exceptional parameters over that whole box (decrease $c_d$). At a rational scalar times a square polynomial we use scaling, not a genuine Type II hit at a square. Away from those polynomials the two word lower tails of `notes/z79/WILD_ES.md` (hereafter Z79) Theorem B hold with this $F$: at positive values, the exceptional count is $O_d(H e^{-c_d V})$ for fewer than $c_d V$ distinct periods in either row, $V=(\log H)^{2/3}/(\log\log H)^{1/3}$; restricting to prime values, $V=(\log H)^{2/3}$ (absolute count). The rows are $n=xyz-x-z$ with $(v_2(x),v_2(y))=(1,2),(0,3)$, respectively.

## 1. The only abelian subfield to test

**Lemma 1 (PROVED rel. Lüroth and quantitative HIT).** Use a prime block $(h,2h]$. With $e>0$ a sufficiently small constant fixed in §3, write

$$h^e<u\le h^{1.1e},\quad h^{.4e}<v\le h^{.5e}\quad\text{or }v=1,\qquad (u,v)=1.$$

Write $a=\operatorname{lc} F$, and assume $\|F\|_\infty\le h^{e/C_d}$ with $C_d$ sufficiently large throughout. For each $v$, outside $O_d(h^{.56e})$ choices of $u$, the root field $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$, $vF(\alpha)+u=0$, has degree $\deg F$. Its maximal subfield abelian over $\mathbb Q$ is $\mathbb Q$ unless there is a presentation $F=aG^2+b$ with $G$ monic in $\mathbb Q[Y]$, $b\in\mathbb Q$. In that case it is precisely $K_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{(-u/v-b)/a})$.

*Generic calculation.* Put $T=-vF(Y)$. $\mathbb Q(Y)/\mathbb Q(T)$ has degree $\deg F$ (equivalently $vF(Y)+T$ is irreducible). Infinity is totally ramified with rational residue. Its maximal abelian intermediate field, if nontrivial, is $\mathbb Q(Z)$ by Lüroth. The unique place there above the pole of $T$ is rational, so after a rational fractional-linear change of $Z$ it is the pole of $Z$. Now $Z=G_1(Y)$ and $T=P(Z)$ are polynomials: their sole poles are the indicated infinities. Automorphisms of $\mathbb Q(Z)/\mathbb Q(T)$ fix infinity and belong to $Z\mapsto a_1Z+b_1$ over $\mathbb Q$. A finite subgroup injects by $a_1$ into $\mathbb Q^*$ (no finite additive subgroup), hence has order at most 2. Thus any nontrivial such $P$ is quadratic; completing its square and normalising the leading coefficient of the inner polynomial gives exactly $F=aG^2+b$. Conversely this presentation exhibits the quadratic field in the generic extension. This calculation does not assert regularity of the *normal closure*.

*Specialisation.* Apply Paredes–Sasyk, **Effective Hilbert's Irreducibility Theorem for global fields**, arXiv:2202.10420v2, Thm 1.3, over $\mathbb Q$ to $vF(Y)+T$. Their fixed-bidegree bound for failure to preserve the arithmetic Galois group among integer $|T|\le U$ is $O_d(U^{1/2}(1+\log H_{\mathbb Q}(vF+T))^6)$ (also nonmonic); the coefficient height here is polynomial in $h$. Delete the $O_d(1)$ branch values too. At an unramified full-group specialization the decomposition group maps isomorphically to the generic group, with the same action on the distinct roots. In particular the subgroup fixing one root and the maximal abelian quotient killing that subgroup correspond on the two sides. Thus the abelian field is as asserted, not just an abstract list of possible group orders. With $U=h^{1.1e}$ the loss is $O_d(h^{.56e})$. This is an average in $u$ **for each** $v$, not in the small fibre.

## 2. Keep the cyclotomic part disjoint, even after a Page twist

**Lemma 2 (PROVED rel. Lemma 1 and Landau–Page).** Exclude for the moment scalar times square $F$, so if the presentation exists then $b=B/C\ne0$ in lowest terms, $C>0$. Write $q=4uv$ (ratio atoms) or $q=16w$, $u=2^i w$, $w$ odd, $i=0,1$ (word atoms, $v=1$). Choose a fixed small absolute $\eta>0$ such that among primitive real Dirichlet characters of conductor $\le h$ at most one has a real zero $>1-\eta/\log h$ (Landau–Page). Denote the corresponding quadratic field by $E_*$ and its conductor by $D_*$, putting $D_*=1$ and omitting $E_*$ when absent. If present choose the largest odd prime $p_*\mid D_*$; for large $h$ it exists and tends to infinity whenever present since real primitive conductors have odd part squarefree, 2-part at most 8, and no one fixed character has zeros approaching 1. Impose $p_*\nmid uv$ when present. Apart from that restriction and $O_d(h^{.60e})$ deletions per $v$, we have

$$K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)=\mathbb Q,\qquad E_*\not\subset K\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)\quad\text{if present}.\tag{1}$$

*Proof.* If $K$ has a quadratic abelian subfield its radicand has the square class of $-a C v(Cu+Bv)$. The numerator and denominator of $b$ are bounded by $(2+\|F\|_\infty)^{O_d(1)}$: recover the coefficients of monic $G$ successively from the highest coefficients of $F/a$, each step dividing by 2, and then subtract the squared constant term. We can require $|B|,C,|a|\le h^{.01e}$. Each prime dividing $Cu+Bv$ outside $2aBC$ divides neither $u$ nor $v$ by coprimality. Discard $Cu+Bv=0$ and those $u$ for which its signed squarefree part is supported on $2aBCD_*$. There are $h^{o(1)}$ possible such squarefree parts (uniform divisor bound on an integer $\ll h^2$). Since $|Cu+Bv|\ll h^{1.11e}$ there are $O_d(h^{.58e})$ choices of $u$ discarded. The surviving $K_0$ has a ramified odd prime outside $qD_*$, hence is not in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)E_*$, or in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)$ if no Page field. Together with Lemma 1 this proves disjointness of $K$ from that abelian compositum (intersection trivial, and the abelian extension is Galois). Since $p_*\nmid q$, $E_*$ itself is not in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)$. The degree formula now gives (1). If there was no generic quadratic field there is no square-part deletion to make. ∎

## 3. A Linnik-range dyadic lower bound in the root field

**Lemma 3 (PROVED rel. Thorner–Zaman and Stark).** On every retained pair and for **each** reduced class $s\bmod q$, there are $\gg_d h/(\varphi(q)\log h)$ primes $h<\ell\le2h$, $\ell=s\bmod q$, at which $vF(Y)+u$ has a root. The box constant $e=e(d)$ can be tiny but is fixed, independent of the block, fibre and coefficients.

*Proof.* Apply Thorner–Zaman, **A unified and improved Chebotarev density theorem**, arXiv:1803.02823v3, Thm 1.4, to the **abelian extension over $K$**, $L=K(\zeta_q)$, $G=H=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)=(\mathbb Z/q)^*$ by (1), and the singleton $s$. (Their base field denoted $F$ is our $K$ here.) For $x\ge Z^{c_2}$, $Z=|D_K|\,\mathcal Q\,[K:\mathbb Q]^{[K:\mathbb Q]}$, $\mathcal Q$ the largest norm of a finite Hecke conductor in this extension, their formula is

$$\pi_s(x)=\frac{\mathrm{Li}(x)-\vartheta\,\mathrm{Li}(x^{\beta})}{\varphi(q)}\left(1+O(e^{-c_3\log x/\log Z}+e^{-\sqrt{c_3\log x/[K:\mathbb Q]}})\right).$$

Here $\vartheta=0$ in the absence of the possible exceptional real Hecke character, else $\vartheta=\pm1$ with its zero $\beta$. The conductor of every norm-lift from $\mathbb Q$ divides $q\mathcal O_K$ at the finite places: on ray principals congruent to 1 mod $q$ and totally positive, the norm is 1 mod $q$ and positive (or use the local norm pullback of Dirichlet characters). All characters here are such lifts. Thus $\mathcal Q\le q^{[K:\mathbb Q]}$. For the discriminant, $va\alpha$ is integral with a monic polynomial of bounded degree and coefficients $h^{O_d(e)}$ obtained by clearing the leading coefficient. The field discriminant divides its nonzero polynomial discriminant. Consequently $Z\le h^{C'_d e}$ for large $h$.

Why the exceptional term does not spoil the **lower** bound: if $\beta>1-\eta/\log h$, it is a zero of $\zeta_{E}(s)$ for a field $E$ of degree $\le2d$, either $K$ (trivial character) or the quadratic extension of $K$ cut out by that character. In the latter case use $\zeta_E=\zeta_K L_K(\chi)$, and $|D_E|=|D_K|^2 N\mathfrak f_\chi$ by conductor–discriminant. So in both cases $\log(2|D_E|)\ll_d e\log h$ for large $h$. Stark, **Some effective cases of the Brauer–Siegel theorem**, Invent. Math. 23 (1974), Lemma 8: a real zero $\beta>1-1/(4[E:\mathbb Q]!\log|D_E|)$ comes from a quadratic subfield of $E$ having that zeta zero (see also Cho–Lemke Oliver–Zaman, arXiv:2510.02309v2, proof of Lemma 5.3). $E=\mathbb Q$ has no such zero. Shrink $e$ to enter Stark's range and have $|D_E|<h$. The quadratic conductor is then $\le h$ by discriminant divisibility, hence this field **must be** $E_*$, contrary to (1) since $E\subseteq L$. This use of Stark involves a factorial of a **bounded** degree, never $[L:\mathbb Q]!$.

Therefore the main term difference from $h$ to $2h$ has numerator at least $(1-e^{-\eta})\int_h^{2h}dt/\log t$ by differentiating the two logarithmic integrals. Choose $e(d)$ so that the relative errors at **both** endpoints times their main terms cost less than half this difference (each numerator before division is $O(h/\log h)$), and $Z^{c_2}\le h$. The prime ideals of degree $\ge2$ of norm $\le2h$ cost $O_d(\sqrt h)$: each lies above a rational prime $\le\sqrt{2h}$ with at most $d$ ideals there. Since $q\ll h^{1.6e}$ this is negligible even relative to the *single-class* count. Outside primes dividing the leading coefficient and polynomial discriminant, a degree-one ideal gives a root by reduction and its cyclotomic action is its norm mod $q$. Those excluded primes cost $O_d(\log h)$ by the same coefficient bounds. Divide by at most $d$ for multiple degree-one ideals per rational prime. This proves the lemma. No Artin holomorphy, Artin GRH, Chebotarev hypothesis on a family, or over-large discriminant $D_L$ error is used. ∎

## 4. Putting the root cells back in the parameter interval

**PROVED rel. Z79 §§1–3 (the internal TQ/AP2 lattice and upper-incidence bounds, BT, Mertens, Vaughan) and Lemmas 1–3.** We give the interface details since a density for fixed $u,v$ with an uncontrolled onset would not suffice. Take $t=\log X$, dyadic $(h,2h]\subset[\sqrt X,X]$, $K_1=\lfloor X^{e/1000}\rfloor$ (not the field $K$), an arbitrary set $J$ of odd multipliers $k\le K_1$, and one unit residue $c_0$ on their lcm. Keep $(u,v)=1$, $u\equiv-c_0v\ (k)$, $(v,k)=1$. A ratio atom has $f=k\ell=-1\bmod 4uv$, and $F(j)v=-u\bmod f$. For the two word rows put $v=1$, $u=2^iw$ ($w$ odd), $f=8w-1\bmod 16w$; thus $x=u,y=(f+1)/x$ have the required valuations. In every case $\ell$ lies in one reduced class mod $q$. Z79 §2's untwisted harmonic lattice lower bound for a single $k$ per block is $\gg_e t^r h(k)$ with weight $1/q$, $r=2,h(k)=\varphi(k)/k^2$ for ratios or $r=1,h(k)=1/k$ for words. The proof there holds with any sufficiently small fixed $e$ (the log lengths and $K_1$ scale with $e$).

Removing $p_*\mid uv$ still leaves this order, uniformly. For ratios, if $p_*\mid k$ it was already absent. Otherwise the loss from $p_*\mid u$, ignoring common-divisor exclusions but still imposing $(v,k)=1$, is at most $O((t/(kp_*)+1/U_0)(t\varphi(k)/k+\tau(k)/V_0))$ by harmonic partial summation on a progression mod $kp_*$ and the usual unit sum; $U_0=h^e,V_0=h^{.4e}$. The loss from $p_*\mid v$ is at most $O((t/k+1/U_0)(t\varphi(k)/(kp_*)+\tau(k)/V_0))$ by inclusion–exclusion on $k$. Both are $o(t^2h(k))$ since $k\le h^{.002e}$ and $p_*\to\infty$ when present. In a word row the corresponding sum over odd $w$ loses $O(t/(kp_*)+1/U_0)$. The Lemmas 1–2 exceptional $u$ discard only $O(K_1 h^{.60e-e}(1+t))=o(1)$ total weight $1/q$ per block even over all indices. In use $1\in J$. Applying Lemma 3 and $1/\varphi(q)\ge1/q$ gives, over all blocks, a conditional mean of large-prime root cells $\gg_d t^r\sum_{k\in J}h(k)$; on each block the conversion factor to reciprocal prime mass is $\gg 1/\log h$, and there are $\asymp t$ blocks. The upper bound by the same order uses at most $d$ roots per prime and Z79 §2's upper harmonic-incidence estimate and BT. Distinct active atoms at a pivot have disjoint parameters: different $u/v$ differ modulo $\ell$ by the small determinant, and for fixed $u/v$ the class mod $q>K_1$ pins $k$. Cells on different large primes are independent *in CRT*, not on the given interval.

For clarity on the rest of the transfer: use Z79 §3's full ES multiplier set supported on primes having an ES solution (its complementary prime reciprocal sum converges by Vaughan). The unit repair at each small prime dividing $F(j)$ changes it to 1 on that tower. A genuine ES failure is coprime to all the multiplier primes by scaling; a repaired hit would be a true hit, giving the positive triple of Z79 §1. The multiplier mass is $\asymp t$ so the repaired mean is $\asymp t^3$. For prime-value words use all odd $k$ with repair, mean $\asymp t^2$, and discard $0<F(j)\le2K_1X$ at cost $O_d(K_1 X)$ by the preimage bound. For all-value words use $y$-rough $k$ with $y=t^2/\log t$ as there, no repair, mass $\asymp t/\log t$ except on small fibres of probability $O_d(e^{-cy})$: content primes $p>y$ have reciprocal sum $o(1)$, the others satisfy $\Pr(p\mid F(j))\le d/p$ independently, and the exponential moment at $y$ of their weighted reciprocal sum is $O_d(1)$. Discard tiny positive values here too. If $m=t^3,t^2,t^2/\log t$ respectively, the conditional law gives $\mathbb E[2^{-Z}]\ll e^{-cm}$ and $\mathbb E{Z\choose i}\le(Cm)^i/i!$ for the hit count $Z$. As in Z79 §3 the even Taylor polynomial in ${Z\choose i}$ for $2^{-Z}$ of degree $\asymp m$ gives an interval upper bound $O(H e^{-cm}+e^{Cmt})$ for $Z<\delta m$ at sufficiently small $\delta>0$: each cell uses only $j\bmod k\ell$ even with repair, the absolute coefficient mass after writing elementary classes is $e^{O(mt)}$, rounding costs one per class. Take the three choices of $t$ in Z79 §3's table, small constant multiples of $(\log H)^{1/4},(\log H)^{1/3},(\log H\log\log H)^{1/3}$ respectively. Our common coefficient ceiling follows by taking $b_d$ sufficiently small.

If $F$ is a scalar times a square, write $F=cG_0^2$ with $G_0$ primitive integral by clearing denominators and Gauss's lemma, $c$ an integer. Only $c>0$ has positive values. An ES triple at $|G_0(j)|>0$ scales to $F(j)$ by the positive integral factor $c|G_0(j)|$. Apply the full-ES conclusion by induction on degree to $G_0$ and $-G_0$. The coefficient norm of $G_0$ is $(2+\|F\|_\infty)^{O_d(1)}$ (top-down square-root recursion after dividing by the leading coefficient; $|\operatorname{lc} G_0|\le |\operatorname{lc} F|^{1/2}$). Shrink the ceiling constant at each of finitely many degree steps. This proves Theorem P in all cases. In contrast to Z79's cubic proof, this does not require a fixed point for every odd permutation (false for 4-cycles), or even the existence of a group element with exactly one fixed point (false in a regular action of degree $>1$).

# A composite cofactor changes the cap deletion budget

**Theorem Q (CONDITIONAL on Dirichlet GRH).** With the capped relaxation of `EXPONENT_CAP.md`, for all $n>1$ and $R=\operatorname{rad}(n)\to\infty$,

$$\log(1+e^*(n))\le(2+o(1))\sqrt{\log_2 R\,\log_3 R}.$$

Here $\log_i$ denotes the $i$th iterated natural logarithm. This beats the internal $2\sqrt2$ coefficient, not the square-root order, and gives no cap-zero result. We do **not** prove the old $\max_a b_i(a)$ bound. That test counts, in one chosen class mod $D_i$, the distinct prime divisors of short nonzero squared-generator differences; the old proof needs this number below the count of admissible signed primes in that very class. Even a tiny proportion of classes failing that test does not suffice pointwise (z78 §15). We replace the necessity of working at a single $a=-R$ by using a composite window $qb$ where $b$ varies and **all its local equations are actually solved** with a common short exponent cost. Neither a composite with an unknown sign at one of its primes, nor replacing a conditional prime count by an average in $R$, is valid.

## 5. Many cofactor primes with one exponent

**Lemma 5 (PROVED rel. Bombieri–Vinogradov and fixed-ratio friable-number supply).** Fix $0<\eta<1/2$. Let $S$ consist of the first $k$ primes $3\bmod4$, $D=8\prod_{v\in S}v$, $s=\log D\sim k\log k$. For large $k$ there are a common multiple $L$ with $\log L=o_\eta(s)$ and a set $\mathcal P$ of primes $r=5\bmod24$, all $\le s^{O_\eta(1)}$ and with $r-1\mid L$, such that **any half** of $\mathcal P$ supplies $\ge D^{\eta}$ distinct squarefree products $b<D$, all of the same parity of number of factors. These products are units modulo $D$.

*Proof.* Choose fixed $\eta<\theta<1/2$, $A$ large with $\theta-1/A>\eta$, and put $y=s^A$, $z=\sqrt s$, $Y=y^\theta$, $L_0=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,\lfloor z\rfloor)$. There are $\gg_{A,\theta}Y$ integers $Y<d'\le2Y$ prime to 6 dividing $L_0$. Here are details: the fixed-ratio Dickman asymptotic $\Psi(Y,z)\sim Y\rho(\log Y/\log z)$ gives this positive density first for smooth numbers coprime to 6 in the dyad by inclusion–exclusion at 2 and 3 (with $Y$ replaced by $2Y,Y,Y/2,\ldots$). Nondivisors of $L_0$ among these have a prime-power divisor $p^j>z$, $j\ge2$; the cost up to $2Y$ is $\ll Y\sum_{p^j>z,j\ge2}p^{-j}=o(Y)$ by convergence. Also $\log L_0=O(z)$.

For each $d'$ count primes $y<r\le2y$ in the single reduced class $1\bmod d'$, $5\bmod24$. The sum of main terms is $\gg y/\log y$; indeed each is $\gg y/(Y\log y)$. BV at $24d'\le48Y$ bounds the sum of absolute errors by $O_{\theta}(y/\log^2 y)$ using the two endpoints. In the resulting pairs write $r-1=t_0d'$; there are $O(y/Y)$ candidate integers $t_0$. One common $t_0$ thus yields $\gg Y/\log y$ **distinct** primes (for fixed $t_0$, $d'$ determines $r$). Take these as $\mathcal P$, $L=t_0L_0$; $\log L=O(\sqrt s+\log y)=o(s)$. After halving $\mathcal P$, form all products of exactly $m=\lfloor (s-1)/\log(2y)\rfloor$ distinct members. They are distinct integers $<D$ and coprime to $D$, as the $r$ are $1\bmod4$. With $P$ the remaining size, $\log{P\choose m}\ge m\log(P/m)\ge(\theta-1/A-o(1))s$. This proves the lemma; $m\to\infty$. The multiplier $t_0$ is determined **before** selecting the half, not reselected for each $b$. ∎

## 6. Local simultaneous packet at $qb$

**Lemma 6 (PROVED, elementary).** It suffices to consider hard squarefree radicals $R$, all prime factors $1\bmod8$; the other radicals have an integral factor solution, and positive scaling by $n/R$ preserves the cap of a radical packet since its denominator is coprime to $R$. If $(R/r)=-1$ anywhere on $\mathcal P$, use the planted prime $r$ of `notes/z75/ANALYTIC_BREAK.md` §§1,4a: in the reduced class $q'=3\bmod4$, $q'=4r-R\bmod r^2$, Linnik gives a prime $q'\ll r^{O(1)}$, and $r\parallel(R+q')/4$, $(r/q')=-1$ by reciprocity. The single-generator cap is $O(q')$. This is far cheaper than the target. In the other case every $(R/r)=+1$ ($r\nmid R$ by its mod-8 class). Separate the primes according to the sign $((R/4)\bmod r)^{(r-1)/4}\in\{1,-1\}$ in $\mathbb F_r$; use a larger part. Thus all the $b$ just constructed use the same sign. Choose $\delta=0$ or 1 such that $(R/4)3^{2\delta}$ has quartic character **on squares**, i.e. its $(r-1)/4$ power, equal to $-1$ at each of these primes. This is possible because $(3/r)=-1$ at $r=5\bmod24$.

For each such $b$ impose on a prime $q>2y$:

$$qb=-R\pmod {D/8},\qquad qb=3\pmod8,\qquad (q/R)=-1.$$

This fixes a reduced class mod $D$ before the sign test and makes $q=3\bmod4$. Put $K'=(R+qb)/4$, an **odd** integer, coprime to $q b R$, divisible by all $v\in S$, including 3. Let $g=3^{2\delta}\prod_{p\mid K'}p^{v_p(K')\bmod 4}$ with remainders in $[0,3]$, and put $L_o=L/2^{v_2(L)}$. Thus $v_p(g)\le5$, $v_p(g)\equiv v_p(K')\bmod2$ on its support and indeed on *all* $p\mid K'$. Using $K'\equiv R/4$ at $r\mid b$, the quotient of $K'$ by the fourth-power-free product is a fourth power, so $g$ is a square of quartic sign $-1$. Since $v_2(r-1)=2$ and $(r-1)/4\mid L_o$, $g^{L_o}=-1\bmod r$. This holds simultaneously mod $b$ by squarefreeness. At $q$, $(g/q)=(K'/q)=(R/q)=(q/R)=-1$. Truncating valuations **mod 4**, rather than using all of $K'$ or just its squarefree kernel, is important both for a small cap and for the common colour.

Let $U=(\mathbb F_q^*)^2$ (odd order), $H_1=\langle v^{2L}:v\in S\rangle$, $j=[U:H_1]$ odd. Since $-g^{L_o}\in U$, $(-g^{L_o})^{-j}\in H_1$. Represent this by $\prod_{v\in S} v^{2L a_v}$ with $a_v\ge0$. Then

$$d_1=K' g^{L_o j}\prod_{v\in S}v^{2L a_v}\equiv-K'\pmod{qb}.$$

Indeed at $q$ the quotient by $K'$ is $-1$ since $j$ is odd. At $b$ it too is $-1$ since the $v^{2L}$ are 1. For $A_0=(K'+d_1)/(qb)$ use $(x,y_1,z_1)=(K',RA_0,RK'A_0/d_1)$; $1/y_1+1/z_1=qb/(RK')$. For $p^c\Vert K'$, write $w=v_p(d_1)\ge c$. If $w>c$, then $v_p(A_0)=c$, and otherwise there is no overflow. Thus the denominator exponent of $z_1$ is $(w-2c)^+$. The difference $w-2c=L_o j v_p(g)+2L a_p-c$ is even (set $a_p=0$ outside $S$). All overflow primes are odd, prime to $R$, and divide $y_1$. The cap is $O(L(j+\max a_v))$, **regardless** of the size or prime-power heights in $K'$. This is a capped rational identity, not a procedure for transferring to integral $z_1$. ∎

## 7. Pooled window count, deletion and optimisation

Our lost reciprocal mass is only a fixed log power, so the sum of main terms is $\gg_{B'} y/(\log y)^{C'_{B'}}$, larger than the sum of absolute errors at a higher fixed Maynard saving. Product lower bounds and $\tau(d)=y^{o(1)}$ still give $\ge y^{.504}$ distinct primes for some $t_0$, with $L=t_0 L_0^2$, $\log L\ll z+\log y$. Small-nonresidue log-cap $O(\log y)$ is smaller than this. The old $\log q\ll U$ and every subsequent step of §§10–11 are unchanged: $\log D_0\ll z+\log y=o(U)$ and all the large-sieve constants there are independent of the smoothness width. Since $B'$ can be any fixed large number, (15) yields (14). This is a rate improvement on QB, not an effective threshold (the arbitrarily high log-saving input is still ineffective). The effective Z75 bound is retained; specifically its explicit prime bound is $4e^*(p)+3\le\sqrt{4p/3}$ for every prime $p=1\bmod8$, not a bound with this constant for all radicals. Page deletion in Lemma 2 handled a bounded-degree field with a single exceptional zero, not every Siegel–Walfisz estimate inside the factorable-moduli dispersion theorem; we do not assert it makes the latter effective.

*Montgomery alternative.* Fix $0<a<1$ and $0<\gamma<.01$ such that $a(1-1/C)>1/2+5\gamma$, possible for $C>2$. Take $y=U^{A_0}$ for a fixed $A_0>20/\gamma$, $z=(\log y)^C$, one box not two. Let $m=\lceil a\log y/\log z\rceil$, $w=y^{a/m}$; to guarantee that all primes used divide $L_0$ take the prime interval $[w/2,w]$. Its lower endpoint to the power $m$ is $y^{a-o(1)}$, so **every** product is $y^{a+o(1)}$. Fixed dyadic PNT gives $\gg w/\log w$ primes there; removing the previously selected $m$ primes costs a negligible fraction since $w=(\log y)^{C+o(1)}$. The harmonic mass of the distinct products is at least $(c_0/\log w)^m/m!\ge y^{-a/C-o(1)}$: $m(\log m+\log\log w+O(1))/\log y\to a/C$. All products divide $L_0$ and are units at 6. For each such $d$, use the unique class $r=1\bmod d$, $r=5\bmod24$. At $x=y,2y$, $24d<x^{(1+a)/2}$ for large $y$, and the hypothesised error at $\kappa=(1-a)/8$ is $o(y/(\varphi(24d)\log y))$ uniformly. Thus the sum over $d$ of dyadic counts is $\ge y^{1-a/C-o(1)}$. There are $\le y^{1-a+o(1)}$ values for $t_0=(r-1)/d$; no product multiplicity for a common $t_0$ here. After halving for colour, one choice still provides $P\ge y^{1/2+4\gamma}$ distinct primes with $r-1\mid L=t_0L_0$. Since $\log L\ll z+\log y=o(U)$ the $q$ height and slot count $k=O(U)$ are as in QB. In §11 take $V=\exp(C_\gamma U)$ with $C_\gamma$ large enough depending also on $\gamma$ to supply $V^{1-\gamma}$ generators per slot. For $M=V^j$, $j\le k$, still $l\le U^3$, so $\log(P/(4l))/\log(2y)>1/2+3\gamma$ eventually. The count $O(M^2(4l/P)^l)$ is at most $M^{1-5\gamma}$, uniformly after absorbing the $O(\log y)$ rounding loss. The containment union bound $\sum_{j\le k}(kV^{-4\gamma})^j=o(1)$ and $P\gg k\log V$ complete the same full-support argument. The cap, including the small-nonresidue alternative, has logarithm $O(z+\log y)\ll_C(\log U)^C$. Montgomery supplies a *dyadic asymptotic in one sparse class per $d$*; GRH for signed $q$ alone does not supply this. ∎

**New barrier for a synchronised squarefree alphabet (PROVED, elementary).** If all $r-1\mid L$ and the cofactor is a product of a subset of the distinct $r$, covering even one fixed positive fraction of a unit group of size $G$ by such products needs $\tau(L)\ge (\log G)/(\log 2)-O(1)$. Indeed there are at most $\tau(L)$ eligible $r$ and at most $2^{\tau(L)}$ products. In the §11 scheme $\log G=\log\varphi(D')\gg U$ since at least one of the candidate primes is $>\sqrt V$. In fact $\log G\gg kU$, hence $\gg U^2$ when using a pivot with $\log q\asymp U$: the GRH choice is dyadic at that height, and in the unconditional Linnik choice one may additionally force $q=-1\bmod 3^b$ with $3^b\asymp e^U$, compatible with the original class mod 3 and $(3,R)=1$. The classical divisor bound $\log\tau(L)\ll \log(2L)/\log\log(3L)$ then implies $\log L\gg\log U\log_2 U$, not $O(\log U)$. Thus one cannot **certify** a polylog cap in $R$ (or $\log R$ on GRH) simply by substituting a better density theorem into §11's same full-support, common-exponent budget $e^*\ll L$. This is about that certificate, not a lower bound on $e^*$; no putative density of primes with $r-1\mid L$ evades the divisor bound. Also no impossibility for schemes using unbounded powers of cofactor primes, different generator lists, single-target hits, or a different way of paying for $L$.

## 13. Condition the exceptional-set sieve on *two-sided* balance

**Theorem UB (PROVED rel. PNT in the fixed class, Heath-Brown's quadratic large sieve (Cor. 2), quadratic reciprocity, moments and CRT, the ambient 3/4 theorem, and for the floor the internal unrefereed `paper/sieve-limits-note.tex`, Thms `noBcap`, `PLcap`).** Fix $A>2$, $0<\eta<1$. Write $Y=(\log N)^A$, $\mathcal T$ the primes $s=3\bmod4$ in $(Y,2Y]$, $m=|\mathcal T|\asymp Y/\log Y$. On all integers define $S(n)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal T}(n/s)$ (zeros allowed). Split the actual failures up to $N$ disjointly according as $|S(n)|\ge\eta m$ (biased) or not (unbiased). Then

$$ E_{\rm bias}(N)\le N^{1/2+o(1)},\qquad E_{\rm unb}(N)\ll N\exp(-c(\log N)^{3/4}).\tag{16}$$

Here the bias estimate counts even non-failures too. The second bound follows by containment, not a new ES exponent. On odd hard inputs $n=1\bmod8$, $(n/s)=(s/n)$, so outside the bias set a fixed positive proportion of $s$ have $(s/n)=-1$: there are at most $\log N/\log Y=o(m)$ zeros. $A=4$ is one explicit choice; the biased **population** (not necessarily failures) has order $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ in exponent, since it contains all the squares for large $N$. The floor below shows explicitly why restricting to balanced integers does **not** beat the ambient exponent via whole-avoider CRT majorants. This is not a claim on the true order of $E_{\rm unb}$.

*Two short moments.* Symbols reset: $k$ is the Haar moment's half-order, $j$ below the independent integer large-sieve order. Neither indexes slots or conductors in §11. Take $k=\lfloor\log N/(2\log(2Y))\rfloor$, $B(n)=(S(n)/(\eta m))^{2k}$. Under ordinary integer Haar on the CRT period (and likewise under unit Haar), the local signs are independent, symmetric and bounded by 1. The numerator expectation is at most $(2k)^k m^k$: each tuple of $2k$ primes whose product has even valuations everywhere admits a pairing. At all others the expectation is zero. Thus

$$\mathbb E B\le(2k/(\eta^2 m))^k=N^{-(A-1)/(2A)+o(1)}\quad\text{as an upper bound}.\tag{17}$$

For the actual integer estimate, write $n=t a b^2$, $t=1,2$, $a$ odd squarefree ($ta$ is the squarefree kernel). For fixed $t,b$, put $X=N/(tb^2)\ge1$. Deleting or reinstating the $s\mid b$ costs at most $\log N/\log Y=o(m)$ uniformly. Reciprocity at the $s=3\bmod4$ makes $(a/s)$ a fixed sign (depending on $a$ only) times $(s/a)$; twist the prime coefficients by $(t/s)$, of modulus 1. Hence a biased $n$ requires the absolute twisted sum for $a$ to be $\ge\eta m/2$. If $X\ge2Y$, put $j=\lfloor\log X/\log(2Y)\rfloor$, so the $j$th-power coefficients have length $\le X$. Use Heath-Brown, Acta Arith. 72 (1995), Cor. 2 for arbitrary coefficients, in the form of `EXSET_CHAR_FORCING.md` Prop. 5.1′: its energy sums over column pairs with square product. After expanding ordered tuples that energy is $\le(2j)^j m^j$ by the same pairing argument in absolute values. Markov gives $\ll_\epsilon N^{2\epsilon}X(8j/(\eta^2 m))^j$ such odd squarefree $a$. Since $j\le \log N/\log(2Y)$, $8j/(\eta^2 m)\ll_\eta (\log N)^{1-A}$; rounding $j$ costs at most $e^{O(\log Y)}$. Consequently the bound is $N^{2\epsilon+o(1)}X^{1/A}$ uniformly, absorbing even the $o(1)$ exponent loss at $X\le N$. For $X<2Y$ the same shape holds trivially with an additional $N^{o(1)}$. Summing $X^{1/A}$ over $b\le\sqrt N$ and $t=1,2$ costs $O_A(\sqrt N)$ as $A>2$. Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives (16). This counts biased **composites** directly, rather than treating their prime factors as if their characters all had the same bias.

*Balanced comparison (the sharpness example).* On any finite period containing the family and these primes, take the comparison law $\rho$ supported on the avoiders from `sieve-limits-note`, Lemma `compmeas`, at level $\lambda\asymp\log N$ large enough to include $2k\log(2Y)$. Include in its test span every projected modulus of the majorant **and every squarefree product of at most $2k$ of the bias primes**. Additional prime-power digits in a period need not enter the charged level. The polynomial $B$ is a nonnegative member of that span (expand its Jacobi characters into residue classes). Therefore $\rho(|S|\ge\eta m)\le\mathbb E_\rho B\le e^{C\lambda^{3/4}}\mathbb E B=o(1)$ by (17). Condition $\rho$ on $|S|<\eta m$: it is now supported on **balanced avoiders** and costs at most $1+o(1)$ times the old comparison factor for *all* nonnegative tests of that level. This is a simultaneous law, not a heuristic assertion that every hardest input is balanced. The same proof on units uses the reviewed prime-law factor $\exp(O(\lambda^{3/4}(\log\lambda)^{3/4}))$ for arbitrary mixtures or $\exp(O_B(\lambda^{3/4}))$ when each modulus $G$ satisfies $G\le P(G)^{1+B}$ for fixed $B$ as in `PLcap`; both absorb (17). Equivalently one can add $B$ to a nonnegative level-bounded majorant of the balanced avoiders to get a whole-avoider majorant, with the same conclusion for its Haar mean. Thus at polynomial level **even optimally weighted signed coefficients** of a nonnegative CRT test cannot yield exponent $>3/4$ from this bias conditioning in the audited class. This level statement does not claim that the expanded $B$ fits an absolute coefficient budget. **Assessment (separate word model).** For the same-band word-square model of Z79's `ANALYTIC_PUSH.md` §1 the same issue has an elementary fibre version: fixing $n\bmod\prod_{s\in\mathcal T}s$ leaves all fresh pivot primes $\gg2Y$ independent, and optimising a Selberg square on a unit multiplier fibre of mass $O(t^2)$ has the same $2/3$ floor. This last statement is only for that model and hypotheses, not a new word-limit theorem for arbitrary mixtures.

**Assessment.** Lane 1 (`notes/z80/ES_JUGULAR.md` Thm A on `side-agent/z80-es-jugular`) reaches the least nonresidue along equal-sign arrows, even when the basin is almost the entire negative half. No reverse divisibility at an opposite-sign pair is supplied by a large $\mathcal T$ of seeds. Lane 3 (`notes/z80/R_BELOW_619.md` §§3–4 on `side-agent/z80-r-below`) transfers fixed-order nonnegative centered moments with a greater level; the replacement $\lambda\mapsto C\lambda$ still does not beat the balanced comparison. An interval-specific **signed discrepancy** for the actual integral hit count, not such a test of low level, would be outside this theorem. It does not extend the cap to all possible non-CRT proofs on unbiased inputs.

For any fixed $C>0$, if $P_I\ge H/(\log H)^C$, the proportion among those prime arguments $q$ having $F(q)>0$ and no positive ES triple is $\ll_{d,C}e^{-c'_d(\log H)^{3/4}}$. The simultaneous coefficient-box conclusion also survives.

## 15. Single-target planting: how many of the requested slots can actually divide the one window?

**Theorem ST (PROVED rel. PNT, BT, Landau–Page and TZ Thm 1.4; BARRIER-SCOPED).** This concerns actual positive input primes, not random targets in a finite group. Consider squarefree cofactors $b$ supported on any primes $r$ with $r-1\mid L$, $1\le L\le U^C$ for fixed $C>0$. Both $L$ and the selected subset can depend on the entire input, pivot and target. Two distinct obstructions hold for the sizes in QB:

1. **Polynomial pivot height.** If $U=\log R$, $q\le\exp(C_0 U)$, the entire $K'=(R+qb)/4$ is smaller than the lower endpoint of the §11 lists $v>\exp(C_1 U/2)$ when $C_1>2\max(1,C_0)$. Thus even **one** nonempty target slot in those lists cannot be planted, with no distribution conjecture. Smaller generator primes are not covered.
2. **GRH pivot height.** Fix $D\ge2$ and use $U=\log_2 T$ on $T<R\le2T$. Along some sequence $T\to\infty$ there are primes $R=1\bmod 8$ such that all primes $s\le w$ are quadratic residues modulo $R$, $\log w=U/2+O(1)$, and the following holds. For each allowed prime $w<q\le e^{DU}$ and each allowed $L,b$, supply at most $e^{DU}$ menus per $(q,L,b)$, each with at most $U^D$ slots. Each slot allows at most $U^D$ unit residues for $v\bmod q$ and requires a different prime $w<v\le e^{DU}$ to divide $R+qb$. The list of menus and slots may vary with $T,q,L,b$ but is fixed independently of the particular $R$ in the interval; **the choice among them can inspect $R$ and all its data without restriction**. For any such system, all but a relative $o(1)$ of the primes on the profile $R\equiv1\bmod 8\prod_{3\le s\le w}s$ (product over primes) cannot satisfy even $t=\lceil 5D+10\rceil$ slots of one menu. The order of quantifiers is: choose the system, then choose $R$, not one $R$ for all possible systems. One can query fewer slots than the binary-basis length, and require just the actual class $-R/q\bmod \prod v_i$, not coverage of any other class. Still the obstruction applies as soon as $t$ slots are needed. Constants in polynomial/polylog heights can be enlarged into $D$. This second statement is **unconditional on a subsequence**, despite referring to the sizes used on GRH.

*Proof.* Uniformly in $L\le U^C$ there are at most $\tau(L)=U^{o(1)}$ distinct available cofactor primes and they are $\le L+1$. Thus $\log b\le U^{o(1)}=o(U)$, and over all these $L$ there are at most $\exp(o(U))$ pairs $(L,b)$. Part 1 follows from $\log K'\le(\max(1,C_0)+o(1))U$ for positive integral $K'$.

For part 2 use clean conductor bounds $Z\to\infty$ with no primitive real Dirichlet zero $\beta>1-c/\log Z$ of conductor $\le Z$, a fixed absolute $c>0$. Recall the Landau–Page argument (Z79 §8): use $Z_i=2^{2^i}$ and $c$ less than half the Page strip constant. If every sufficiently late $Z_i$ has such a zero then the one at $Z_i$ is still in the Page strip at $Z_{i+1}$. Uniqueness forces the same character throughout, impossible since it is nonvanishing in a neighbourhood of 1. Put $w=(\log Z)/3$, $T=\exp((\log Z)^2)$, $U=2\log_2 Z$. Then $M=8\prod_{3\le s\le w}s\le Z$ by PNT. TZ Thm 1.4 for the cyclotomic extension of $\mathbb Q$ of modulus $M$ (as in §11) gives $\gg T/(\varphi(M)\log T)$ primes $R=1\bmod M$ in $(T,2T]$. Indeed $\log T/\log M\to\infty$ so the relative errors at both endpoints are $o(1)$, and even a harmful exceptional term is negligible since $(1-\beta)\log T\gg\log Z$. At these inputs every odd prime up to $w$ has positive symbol by reciprocity, and 2 does too. So the QB pivot, requiring $(q/R)=-1$, is $>w$; the small-nonresidue fallback at $r\le L+1<w$ also cannot fire.

Fix $q,L,b$, a menu, $t$ chosen slots and a compatible list of distinct primes $v_1,\ldots,v_t$. If $\gcd(qb,\prod v_i)>1$ no $R$ in the profile can satisfy the divisibilities for large $T$ (indeed the $v_i<T$). Otherwise the requirement is a *single reduced class* mod $M\prod v_i$ by CRT since every $v_i>w$. The modulus is $T^{o(1)}$, even after multiplying by $(e^{DU})^t$. BT for the number of primes up to $2T$ and the preceding denominator lower bound show that the relative probability on the profile is $O(1/\prod(v_i-1))$. For a single unit residue mod $q$, the sum of $1/(v-1)$ in $w<v\le e^{DU}$ is $O(1/w+(1+DU)/q)$: keep the first term and sum the rest on an arithmetic progression, dropping primality. Consequently a slot costs $\exp(-U/2+o(U))$ even with $U^D$ allowed residues. Sum over distinct lists by the product of these bounds. Choosing the slots costs at most $U^{Dt}$; all menus, $q$'s and $(L,b)$ cost at most $\exp((2D+o(1))U)$. The resulting relative upper bound $\exp((2D-t/2+o(1))U)=o(1)$ proves the claim. ∎

**What this means for a single target (Assessment with the implication from ST proved).** One can index the menus by a generator of the pivot squares, the target element of those squares, and a choice of digits: there are at most $q^{O(1)}$ such descriptions for binary digits. Their computed roots under $v\mapsto v^{2L}$ number at most $2L$ per slot. Enlarge $D$ for the description size. Hence selecting $q$ *before or after* the target, adapting $b$ to the whole input, and prescribing only the nonzero digits do not justify a statement that any fixed number of large pivot slots (however large a fixed number is requested) can always be planted at $L\le U^C$. In particular a full new binary basis of length $\asymp\log q$ in the §11 large lists is impossible here, even if the cofactor test asks only for the **one right-hand side**. ST does **not** force the actual target $(-g^{L_o})^{-1}$ to have $t$ nonzero digits: one could design a basis/target together requiring $<t$ contacts, use unrestricted rather than prescribed prime residues, use small factors or change the cofactor mechanism. For example if the target is the identity, no pivot correction is needed at all. We have neither a polylog cap by this route nor a universal impossibility theorem for single-target constructions. ST is logically different from §12's entropy count: it bounds true divisibility for an adaptively chosen target menu at actual input primes, with no full-group coverage hypothesis.

## 16. Exact low-level marginals, including after removing an exceptional population cheaply

**Theorem LM (PROVED rel. finite linear programming and the indicated internal supply-cap theorems; not a barrier for arbitrary interval counts).** Fix $A_0>0$, $\lambda=\max(1,A_0)\log N$ and a finite audited family $\mathcal G$ of forbidden classes, with whole avoiders $\mathcal A$. There exists a **probability law** on a finite CRT residue space such that

* its mass on *each* class mod *every* $Q\le N^{A_0}$ is exactly $1/Q$, simultaneously; hence its expected count in $N$ draws is $N/Q$ with zero error;
* its mass on $\mathcal A\cap\{|S|<\eta m\}$, with $S,\eta,m$ from §13, is at least $(1-o(1))\exp(-F(\lambda))$.

For the four full-ES class types (including mixtures and selectors under the stated hypotheses) $F(\lambda)=C\lambda^{3/4}$ by `paper/sieve-limits-note.tex` Thm `noBcap` (Case A relative to the specified ET inputs). For the integer **word-class** universe with selectors, $F(\lambda)=C\lambda^{2/3}(\log\lambda)^{2/3}$ by `EXSET_BEYOND_CRT.md` Thm 2.1/Cor 2.5; this includes subfamilies of the word universe, e.g. the native (★) classes, not every possible representation of that equation. More generally use the proved majorant bound of that Thm 2.1 with its exact hypotheses (square-avoiding base, inflated first-mass bound with parameters $d,\gamma$, and uniform weighted cofactor second moment); then $F=C\lambda^{d/(d+1)}(\log\lambda)^{\gamma/(d+1)}$. For the full-ES prime-unit variant use $1/\varphi(Q)$ on unit classes, zero otherwise, and `PLcap` with the qualifications of §13. No general prime-unit word claim is taken from the integer word theorem.

*Proof.* Set the common period to a multiple of all family moduli, $Q\le N^{A_0}$, and the §13 bias period. In the finite space take the span $V$ of classes at all level-$\le\lambda$ divisors of this period (level as in `noBcap`: sum of log distinct prime factors above its fixed $W$). Minimise $\mathbb E_0 f$ over $f\in V$, $f\ge1_{\mathcal A}$ pointwise, where $\mathbb E_0$ is uniform. This forces nonnegativity, and the quoted theorem gives optimum $v\ge e^{-F(\lambda)}$. The dual LP has variables nonnegative point masses $\mu(x)$, constraints $\sum_x\mu(x)f(x)=\mathbb E_0 f$ for every $f\in V$, objective $\sum_{x\in\mathcal A}\mu(x)$ to maximise. The primal is feasible (constant one) and bounded below, so finite LP duality gives a feasible law attaining $v$ (normalisation comes from $1\in V$). This is the **unconditioned dual optimiser**, not the law obtained by restricting it to avoiders as in `compmeas`. Every cylinder in the first assertion belongs to $V$. So does the high-moment statistic $B$ in (17), giving $\mu(|S|\ge\eta m)\le\mathbb E_0 B\le N^{-c_A+o(1)}=o(v)$. Subtract this bad mass **without conditioning the law** to retain exact simultaneous marginals. The proof on units uses uniform unit expectation throughout. ∎

**Scope of the model (Assessment).** This uses the duality already present in `compmeas`, not a new proof of a product-sieve ceiling. What the formulation adds is simultaneous **exact marginals plus a balanced avoider atom of large total mass** in one law (also in the word universe). It disproves any implication, required to hold for arbitrary probability laws, from just those marginal equalities or tolerances $O(1/N)$ to a smaller void probability. It covers nonlinear postprocessing of joint probabilities, including a proposed Janson, cumulant or factorial-moment estimate **if all its premises are logical consequences of these marginals and the class descriptions**. Composite-modulus cylinders were already included in the parent theorem; no novelty is claimed for that part. Overlapping tuples cost the level of their lcm, not the product with multiplicity. The proof does not assume every method needs order comparable to the mean: that is a Bonferroni feature, not a necessary inequality for arbitrary estimates. Nor does an average witness count $\asymp\log^d n$ alone establish the required cap for a class family.

LM is a *real, positive measure*, not an $N$-point set with all empirical class counts within $O(1)$, and not a measure supported on $[1,N]$. Exact marginals are **expectations**, not concentration per draw. For example, a class $a\bmod Q$ with $N<a<Q$ has actual count zero on $[1,N]$, while the model has positive mass for it when $Q\le N^{A_0}$. Both obey the position-blind count with unit error. Actual integrality, support, dependence of signed rounding errors, or a dependency/conditional-independence inequality proved for the interval using more than those marginal data are additional inputs. Thus we do **not** claim the requested unrestricted assertion about every argument on $[1,N]$ using interval counts; it does not follow from an LP comparison. FKG is a positive-association property, not a theorem that applies below a modulus cutoff and stops above it. One possible escape is a bound at the **specified forbidden residues** for the true surviving integers, beyond what low-level equalities force; no theorem here says that this particular mechanism, or any specified band of moduli, is necessary. A proof could instead use non-CRT structure of the exceptional integers. In the existing triage this is BARRIER-SCOPED / consolidation, not a new BARRIER-BROAD against non-product-law methods.

**Final screens (Assessment).** ST constructs hard input profiles, not hard *ES instances*; they may all have integral triples. It does not exclude the $<t$-contact case at primes, say anything about Type II at 4545, or use a rational correction to revive any of F1–F4. LM uses the same low-cost bias test as §13, even though the underlying law can put mass on unit-square base digits. Neither the one-target trap nor the exact-marginal construction moves the ambient exceptional exponent or entails any cap-zero, numerator-eight, or single-prime claim.
