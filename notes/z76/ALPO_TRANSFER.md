## 2. A: the path norm and the arithmetic edge

**Exact norm test (PROVED).** For k≥2, q≥2, let Q=Σxᵢxᵢ₊₁ over ℤ/q. With unary weights tᵢ, the sum over h,x with phase e_q(h(Q−b)) is q times the weighted count at Q=b; its zero mode is ∏Σtᵢ. For h≠0 the absolute value of one mode is at most

    (q gcd(h,q))^((k−1)/2) ‖t₁‖₂ ‖t_k‖₂ ∏_{1<i<k} ‖tᵢ‖∞ .

Indeed the transfer matrix F(x,y)=e_q(hxy) has FF* equal to q on x≡x′ mod q/gcd(h,q), zero elsewhere. Contract the matrices F with the internal diagonal multipliers. In particular, at prime q, bounding each unary weight by 1 gives q^((k+1)/2). It is **attained with the same t=1 at every vertex**, for every odd k≥3, every h≠0: summing the even vertices enforces xᵢ₋₁=−xᵢ₊₁, leaving one free odd vertex. At b=0 all q−1 errors add with the same sign. This is a sharp obstruction to removing another power by repeated use of these norms; not a bound against estimates using a particular arithmetic t. Nonnegative tᵢ give N≥0, which does not give N>0. Signed tᵢ do not even give that sign by orthogonality.

At k=2 and b=1 one can compute the *aggregate* nonzero modes exactly. On the units, inversion is an involution. From each of its two-element orbits select one point; take their union A (discard fixed points). Then t=1_A gives N=0, nonzero-mode sum −|A|². For prime q≥5 the zero mode is ((q−3)/2)². Positivity of this path sum at one p cannot be obtained from the zero mode and a strict relative error valid for all such indicators. Projecting to xy≡1 mod 4p likewise discards the lift test: at an actual (★) pair, 4C=(xy−1)/p divides x+1 and y+1. Replacing x by x+4pj, keeping y, keeps the projected point but increases C by jy and eventually fails even divisibility of y+1. At odd squares the projected hyperbola still has units, whereas the original positive (★) count is zero (T1). Precise caveat: on **canonical** inverse pairs one could put the entire height test into a unary t(x), since y is then determined. This is a restatement of the test, not a proof that every separated expansion is impossible. For (S) one needs the positive word with the right sign, not the Legendre symbol somewhere on the full hyperbola. The uniform flat character m-jet cost there is ((p−1)/2)(2⌈m/2⌉−1), `notes/z75/PROOF_WILD2.md` §3 (m<p); it says nothing about a mask on a small specified set.

**Exact tied-edge test (PROVED).** For vertices u in (U,2U], give distinct u,v an undirected edge if uv|p+u+v. Every incident v is congruent to −p mod u, hence unique (interval length U<u). The symmetric adjacency matrix A is a direct sum of N₂ flip blocks and isolated zeros. Thus tr(A^{2j})=2N₂ for *every* j≥1; even closed walks of arbitrary depth just recount the matched vertices. The same holds with edges restricted to 4|(p+u+v)/(uv). In particular exact-edge iteration buys no new spectral gap by taking powers. Replacing the edge by an untied Fourier kernel is not an identity. TD(η) concerns phases on the thin strip, and NC(η) counts its near-squares (`Z54_BILINDIV.md` §4, `Z55_TDCAL.md`); even the strip with k unrestricted modulo 4 need not be empty at a square. The two tests do not estimate that strip or produce a new upper bound beyond `Z53_SQSIEVE.md`'s n^{12/37+ε}; nor a new exceptional-set sieve. This is a failure of the indicated transfers, not an impossibility theorem for Fourier methods.

## 3. B: exactly when positive moment data force an atom

**Missing-frequency lemma (PROVED).** Let H be a finite abelian group, a a prescribed atom of an H-torsor, and S a list of characters of H. Suppose the input is the Gram matrix of their evaluations for the *uniform probability* on the torsor (or a positive multiple of it). Set D=S S⁻¹. If D is the whole dual and S is nonempty, the data uniquely determine the mass of a. Otherwise, for nonempty S, there is a positive measure of the same total mass with **identical Gram matrix** and zero mass at a. Indeed pick γ outside D (also γ⁻¹ outside D). In coordinates x=ah, use the density 1−Re γ(h) against uniform. Orthogonality gives every moment indexed by D unchanged. In the full case use Fourier inversion. This is an iff for this moment problem, unchanged by taking *all* minors, tensor powers of the supplied matrix, or Schur complements when defined. New moments of products are new input, not tensor powers of supplied numbers. With a singleton torsor one character suffices. This argument is not an extrapolation of the quadratic-polynomial hypothesis (H) of `Z53_SQSIEVE.md` Lemma C3.

For each primitive (u,B) row q=4uB, target a=−u−B, the BM information selects a coset of ker χ₋q, the genus information selects a coset of G² in G=(ℤ/q)×. The corresponding uniform main-term density of the target in an active row is 2/φ(q) or [G:G²]/φ(q). On the selected coset the observed BM/genus characters are constants. No point mass is forced unless the coset is a singleton, even allowing arbitrary PSD tests of those moments. Every active BM row here has φ(q)>2. The lemma remains true if nonconstant auxiliary characters have been included **with uniform Gram moments**, until their differences span the dual as a *set*, not just as generators. This is a sharp information bound in a rowwise relaxation; the competing measures are not asserted to arise simultaneously from a common integer, or to reproduce actual nonuniform auxiliary periods. A cross-row identity or new signed arithmetic moments could change the answer. Likewise a Gram on actual certificate vectors has determinant nonnegative by Cauchy–Binet but positivity then requires an independent minor with nonzero determinant. `Z62_PARITY.md` §1 needs the nonzero index to be carried by certificates, `Z60_SIGNED.md` needs validity plus positivity, not just a nonnegative diagonal. Interlacing selection alone does not compute any such minor.

## 4. C: a higher-rank T1 test for *representation determinants*

**Common-square transport theorem (PROVED relative to Meyer's isotropy theorem and Witt extension).** Fix integral positive definite quadratic lattices Lᵢ, rational positive definite Gram shapes Bᵢ of size sᵢ≥1, rank Lᵢ≥sᵢ+3. A configuration at n is a finite tuple of integral frames Xᵢ with XᵢᵀLᵢXᵢ=n Bᵢ. Allow any prescribed joint residue conditions on their entries modulo M and any open condition on (Xᵢ/√n) at infinity. There is **one** common m, odd and arbitrarily large, giving a configuration at m² whenever there is one at a positive n≡1 mod 8M. In particular the theorem applies to nonvanishing of a joint determinant of continuous functions of the normalized frames (including determinants unchanged in nonvanishing by homogeneous rescalings). Repetition of the data, arbitrarily many stages, and arbitrary lengths of frames in this stable range are allowed. It extends to a Cauchy–Binet test det(Σ vv*)>0 on such configurations: select a full-rank minor first; transport all its finitely many columns at once, enforcing distinctness by open conditions for distinct frames if necessary. Here v depends continuously on normalized real data on each allowed residue class; masses of selected columns must stay positive. No uniform bound on m is claimed.

*Proof.* Diagonalize Bᵢ rationally. In a positive rational space V of dimension ≥4 each b>0 rational is represented over ℚ: apply Meyer to V⊕⟨−b⟩ (indefinite, dimension ≥5); the last coordinate cannot vanish. Iterating in the orthogonal complement gives a rational base embedding of Bᵢ into Lᵢ⊗ℚ. At infinity prescribe an embedding near Xᵢ/√n. At each ℓ|2M choose a local root c²=n with c≡1 mod ℓ^{vℓ(M)} and c a unit (odd ℓ by Hensel; at 2, n≡1 mod 2^{3+v₂(M)} suffices). Prescribe an embedding near Xᵢ/c, integral with the original residues. These local neighborhoods can be met by one rational embedding: by Witt extension the local special orthogonal group acts transitively on the embeddings (correct determinant by reflection in the complement). The rational group SO(V) has weak approximation here: the Cayley transform of rational skew adjoints for the form covers the open det(1+g)≠0 over every completion; it is dense locally since SO is smooth geometrically connected and the complement is a proper algebraic subset. Approximate simultaneously in the affine coordinates of this chart by rational numbers. Apply the resulting group element to the base embedding. Do this independently for the finitely many frames, taking a product neighborhood inside the joint open condition. In integral bases the resulting rational frames are integral at primes of 2M. Take a common multiple m of their denominators with m≡1 mod 2M (CRT), arbitrarily large. Scaling all frames by m preserves all conditions at m². ∎

Thus a fixed-level nonnegative stable-range frame proxy of this form that respects T1 at every sufficiently large odd square is identically sterile on n≡1 mod 8M, including infinitely many hard primes (Dirichlet, intersect with 1 mod 840). This extends the *termwise* ternary-recipe screen `Z59_WALDS.md` Lemma W0 in **scope of objects**, not its rank-3 proof: whole higher-rank positive minors and several Gram indices share the square. For a family indexed by depth j, fix j=j(p) first. If the j-th test is itself valid on all sufficiently large odd squares and p≡1 mod 8M_j, it cannot fire at p. There is also a nested version: if acceptance by test j implies acceptance by every later test at the same input, and j(m²)→∞, then a positive at p would transport to arbitrarily large squares m² accepted first by the frozen test and hence by the selected test. Without either frozen square-validity or persistence the argument does not apply to adaptive depth.

**Why this is not an all-towers Rallis theorem.** Items 8–16 do not give a kernel where n occurs as a scalar Gram index nB with independent frames. Signed sums *before* squaring, theta lifts of nontrivial automorphic test vectors, coupled equations between stages, Bessel periods with n-dependent data, variable levels and divisibility of a global sum are not termwise open tests of normalized frames. A seesaw can identify two periods (or Rallis relate a norm to L-data); if the common period is zero, changing the length does not prove it nonzero. Positive divisibility D|I and |I|<D force I=0, not an ES hit, without an additional identity. Even fixed-rank mixed-nebentypus signed vanishing as a universal claim is false: `Z60_SIGNED.md` §4.1 gives an explicit rank-5 difference negative on all odd squares and positive on primes ≡17 (24). Nor does T1 imply arbitrary period nonvanishing is impossible at nonsquares: a coefficient sequence killed at squares can be nonzero elsewhere. There is no specified arbitrary iterated-tower coefficient to which one could apply a universal vanishing theorem in this announcement. Escape E3 is **not closed** in general. To apply the positive theorem, exhibit its frame model; to escape it, one must at least leave those hypotheses and prove validity independently.

## 5. D: the full genus asymptotic at one prime

**Theorem G (CONDITIONAL on GRH for Dirichlet L-functions).** For p→∞ through primes ≡1 mod 4, with the ordered-pair normalization of `Z56_FAMDESIGN.md`,

    M_gen(p) = (3/(2π²) + o(1)) log² p ∼ M₀(p).

This sharpens the unconditional one-sided factor 5/6. `Z56_FAMDESIGN.md` §5 already anticipates admissibility of the open triangle; taking a supremum of compact subregions alone gives *only a lower bound*. Here both the upper bound and edge control are part of the theorem. This is purely asymptotic; we have no usable numerical onset. It has no implication R∼M_gen. All estimates through the end of this proof are **CONDITIONAL on GRH**. We give details because an unquantified conductor-o(1) error would not justify the upper bound.

Write L=log p, T=exp(L^{1/3}), κ=1/100, K=⌈10 lg L⌉ (lg is base 2). The cutoffs and contour parameters in this proof are local to §5, not z56's Burgess parameters. Write W(o,e) for the extended genus weight of the **two** orders, odd o, e=2^k e′ even; this is exactly the algebraic formula in z56 Lemma 3.2 with no height cutoff, zero on nonprimitive pairs and at p|oe. Impose 4oe−o−e≤p at the end. Drop k>K at total cost ≪2^{-K}(1+L)^4=o(1) in the height range: W≤4·2^{-k}2^{ω(o)+ω(e′)}/(φ(o)φ(e′)) and Σ_{n≤X}2^{ω(n)}/φ(n)≪log²(2X) by its positive Euler product up to X (Mertens). Both entries of a contributing pair are ≤p.

**Uniform Mellin input.** For nonprincipal primitive ξ of conductor ≤p^4, on 1−κ≤σ≤2 and |t|≤C L (any fixed C), |L(σ+it,ξ)|≤exp(O_C(L^{1/8})). Indeed the explicit formula gives Σ_{ℓ≤x}ξ(ℓ)log ℓ≪√x log²(p x) over primes. In log L truncate the prime sum at L^{10}: the initial sum is O(L^{10κ} log L) and the tail by partial summation is O((1+|t|)(L^{10})^{-1/2+κ}log²(pL^{10})); higher powers cost O(1). This converges in the strip in question. For omitted Euler factors at primes of an integer b≤p^{O(1)}, on that strip their product of absolute upper bounds is ≤exp(O((1+log b)^κ log(2+log b))) (split primes at log(3b), bounding the tail by their number times (log(3b))^{-1+κ}). For principal ζ on the shifted line 1−κ polynomial-in-height bounds suffice. In particular all these factors at bounded powers of p fit the exp(O(L^{1/8})) budget. At σ=1 the sharper bounds |L(1,ξ)|≪log L and ∏_{ℓ|b}(1+1/ℓ)≪log(2+L) follow from the same truncation and Mertens. Smooth shifts below have Gamma decay; tails |t|>C L are negligible (polynomial-in-conductor/height bounds on fixed strips, C large enough), with the pole of a principal factor accounted for. All implicit constants may depend on fixed smoothing functions.

**Slice estimate.** Partition into dyadic boxes (O,2O]×(E,2E] in o,e. Free the coordinate of larger dyadic scale Y=max(O,E); freeze the smaller coordinate b at scale B=min(O,E) (so b≤2Y). One may start the dyadic grid at 1/2. Use a fixed smooth weight w(z), a finite real linear combination of e^{-az} (fixed positive integer a) with w(0)=0, in the free variable z=o/Y or e/Y. Its Mellin transform is Γ(s)Σ c_a a^{-s}, regular at 0, with ŵ(0)=∫w(z)dz/z. The character expansion is z56 Lemma 3.3 with this test in place of the interval indicator, justified simply by the pointwise character identities; the extension W does not impose height. For a frozen o one has free odd variable m=e/2^k at scale Y/2^k for each k≤K. For frozen e the free odd m=o has scale Y. Each twisted Dirichlet series for the weight in m, before its factor 1/m, is

    L(s,η)L(s,ηχ) H(s),    χ=χ_{D_o} or χ_{E_e}.

This is z56 Prop 3.4(1) with h twisted by η. At good primes H has first coefficient O(1/ℓ) and second O(1), hence bounded product on σ≥1−κ; at bad primes ℓ|2bp its factors are products of at most two omitted Euler factors (z56 §3.4). The same bounds hold for imprimitive ηχ. Its primitive conductor always contains p, since p∤b. Thus only η=1 gives a pole, simple, at 1. Shift from Re s>0 to −κ in the Mellin integral with series evaluated at 1+s. Each error is ≪ (Y/2^K)^{-κ}exp(O(L^{1/8})). In the row expression the sum of absolute outside coefficients is ≪ (1/b)·exp(o(log(2b))) up to powers of L: by z56 Lemma 3.3 these cost τ(rad b), b_odd/φ(b_odd), and O(K) choices, with 2^{-k} in the frozen-even row. The little-o bound is for b→∞; bounded b is harmless. As b≤2Y, uniformly for Y≥T/2 the resulting error per frozen b is O(b^{-1}Y^{-κ/2}) for all large p. In particular no p^ε swamps the smaller scales.

After summing the principal residues, the smoothed row is

    (1−1/p) ŵ(0) t_b ℒ(χ;2bp)/(2b) + O_w(b^{-1}Y^{-κ/2}),

where 1−2^{2−K}≤t_b≤1 for frozen odd b (sum over its allowed k) and t_b=1 for frozen even b with v₂(b)≤K. Here ℒ=L^{(2bp)}(1,χ) Σ_{(d,2bp)=1} μ²(d)χ(d)/d², exactly z56 Lemma 3.5. For tiny frozen b we have the uniform bound ℒ≪(log(2+L))² by the preceding Mellin input. All of this holds without averaging over p.

**Outer dyadic average.** Uniformly at B≥T/2 the sum of ℒ/b on a frozen odd dyadic interval is (6/π²)log 2+o(1); the same holds on the frozen even interval with k≤K. Here is a short-scale justification rather than invoking z56 Prop 3.6 down to an unproved endpoint. For odd b write V=B; for even b=2^k b′ do this separately at V=B/2^k in odd b′. In both cases V≥T/(2^{K+1}). The Dirichlet series at 1 defining ℒ has coefficients 1[(n,2bp)=1]β(n)χ(n), β(n)=∏_{ℓ|n}(1+1/ℓ), before division by n. Its Euler product at s is L^{(2bp)}(s,χ) times the absolutely bounded product of 1+χ(ℓ)ℓ^{-1-s} over good primes. Smoothly truncate at D=V^{1/8} with e^{-n/D}: Mellin-shifting Γ past its pole at 0 costs O(D^{-κ}exp(O(L^{1/8})))=o(1), uniformly. Terms n>D² are negligible even absolutely (β(n)≤τ(n)). For nonsquare odd n the average over odd b′∈(V,2V] with weight 1/b′ is O(n/V) by complete periods 2n and partial summation. Even using τ(n) in place of β(n) this costs o(1) after summation to D². The resulting convergent diagonal tends uniformly to (log 2)/2 times Σ_{s odd}β(s²)φ(s)/s³ = (log 2)/2 times ∏_{ℓ odd}(1+ℓ^{-2})=(6/π²)log 2, by dominated convergence as D→∞. For even b restore the factors 2^{-k}, with Σ_{k=1}^K 2^{-k}=1−2^{-K}. This proves the outer estimate.

**Sharp boxes and the axes.** For both scales ≥T the mass in a full sharp box for extended W (k≤K) is (3/π²)(log 2)²+o(1) uniformly. To justify unsmoothing, for each fixed τ>0 bound 1_{(1,2]} above and below by w of the indicated class with ∫w(z)dz/z within τ of log 2. First use compact continuous upper/lower approximants. The number of bulk boxes is L²/(2(log 2)²)+o(L²) by the area of the log triangle, since log T=o(L). Summing proves G. ∎

## 6. D: the square-root boundary of (PC)

**Transfer lemma (PROVED).** In `Z68_PC2.md` Thm B(i), at fixed p the size restriction F(y)<p can be replaced, for any fixed δ>0 and all sufficiently large p, by F(y)≤p^{2−δ}. Choose the unit profile equal to p away from p and choose its p-component by Haar measure on ℤ_p^×. Even at p≤y the odd nonresidue count away from p suffices. Bad classes are those of modulus m≤F(y) divisible by p. There are O_ε(m^ε) ET classes per modulus, *uniformly*: for I-1 split m/4=ad then f|4a²d+1; for I-4/II-1 split m/4=ab then e|a+b; for II-2 split (m+1)/4=acd; for I-2/I-3/II-3 split m/4 into the three modulus slots. I-3 has ≤2^{ω(f)} roots since the target is a unit mod odd f. Use the divisor bound on numbers ≪m² (ET Prop 1.9; primitive classes only). Each bad class catches a fraction at most 1/(p−1) of p-components, irrespective of its prime-to-p restrictions. The union has mass ≪_ε F(y)^{1+ε}/(p(p−1)), hence <1. Choose a component avoiding it. If PC(F,η) holds and the nonresidue count is sufficient, a class catches this profile, is prime to p, and hence contains p. ET validity (`Z68_PC2.md` Lemma V) finishes. No GRH in this lemma.

**Endpoint bridge (CONDITIONAL on GRH for quadratic Dirichlet L-functions, AND on PC(F,η)).** Given fixed 0<η<1/2, there exists c_η>0 such that log F(y)≤c_η√y eventually implies ES for every sufficiently large prime. In particular log F(y)=o(√y) suffices, without the log y loss in z68's unsmoothed criterion. No specific constant such as 0.9 is asserted.

*Proof.* Reduce to p≡1 mod 4. Take w∈C_c^∞((0,1)), 0≤w≤1, ∫w>1−η/2. The classical smoothed Dirichlet explicit formula for primitive quadratic χ=(·/p) gives ΣΛ(n)χ(n)w(n/y)≪_w √y log(2p) for y≥2: the nontrivial-zero term is −Σ_ρ y^ρ ŵ(ρ), with rapid Mellin decay and O(log(p(j+2))) zeros (with multiplicity) in each unit height interval j≤|Im ρ|<j+1. The gamma/trivial terms are O_w(log(2p)+1), e.g. shift to Re s=−1/2 using the functional equation there and account for s=0. GRH is used in |y^ρ|=√y only. The untwisted sum is y∫w+o_w(y) by PNT. At y=C_η(log p)² discard prime powers at cost O(√y log²y)=o(y), and the prime 2 at O(1). Orthogonality of the two signs and log ℓ≤log y now give at least (1/2−η)π(y) odd nonresidue primes by taking C_η sufficiently large (y<p, reciprocity). With c_η=3/(2√C_η) the modulus bound is p^{3/2}; apply the transfer lemma with δ=1/2. The nonresidue input has no odd-square analogue. This proves an implication, not PC itself or eventual ES from a standard conjecture alone. A fixed small coefficient at exponent 1/2 is an improvement even using F<p (smaller c_η). Neither the p²-scale transfer nor the smoothed error changes the power-law comparison when log F(y)=y^{c+o(1)}, c>1/2. The error envelope then need not be small relative to y. This is not an impossibility theorem for ERH.
