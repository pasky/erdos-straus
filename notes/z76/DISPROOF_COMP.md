# Track 3: which prime features survive the local match?

## 1. Record and independent checks

For **p=1,590,284,203,561 ≡ 361 (840)** there is no positive solution of `8uBC−u−B=p`. At numerator 4 there are 26 solutions with `u≤B`, hence 52 ordered (u=B is impossible by parity). One is `(15,26984,982239)`. This raises the repository lower bound on X for any putative “R₈>0 for all in-𝔐 primes p>X” from 1,511,088,831,001; it does **not** refute eventual R₈ positivity. Full **8/p** already has a solution: in `8abcd=a+b+pc` take `(2,101507502355,3,1)`; denominators are `abd,pacd,pbcd`. We do not claim a count of *all* ES or all 8/p solutions. The new record is neither a smooth nor a square-shift prime: `p−1=2³·3²·5·13·211·1033·1559`; `12p+1=7·13·209707807063`.

Discovery used a translated progression sieve with a complete ordered-coordinate fallback. Independent of the sieve, `z75_push2_prime_factor.c` constructs the primes through √(p+u_max), factors **every** p+u with `8u²−2u≤p` (plus end padding), and enumerates every divisor f: test `8u | f+1` and `(p+u)/f ≥ u`. Completeness follows by swapping u,B to put u first. The unmodified `z75_obstruct_check.py` instead trial-divides p up to √p, sorts u≤B, partitions by `u≤B≤C`, `u≤C<B`, `C<u≤B` and tries every feasible smaller pair, at k=4 and 8. `z58_disproof_eng.c` with `EXACT=1` counts the k=4 solutions and their C parities directly, also yielding (52,0). Outputs `factor.txt, pycheck.txt, z58.txt` in `data/z76/disproof/comp/`. This verification does not assume that failure of a truncated detector is a zero, or that the AP sieve missed no other primes.

## 2. Exact-residue matched panels, not a zero-rate estimate

Endpoint inherited unchanged from `notes/z75/DISPROOF_PUSH2.md` §3: no R₈ witness with first word modulus `f=8uC−1 ≤ 1000`. Testable as `r=−p mod f`, `r | (f+1)/8`, since the positive u is less than f. **Match modulo M=2⁵3³5²7²=1,058,400**, in the same trillion bin in `[10¹²,9·10¹²)`, with `|control−anchor|≤anchor/1000`. This fixes the exact residue, *not* valuations above the specified exponents. All anchors and controls are 1 mod 840. Only z75's anchor IDs are used: 2,048 smooth/shift-prime non-square-shift anchors (256 hash-ranked per trillion), plus all 720 primes `p=m²+1, 420|m` in the range. Reserve anchors before any matching. In anchor order choose nearest eligible unused `p±jM` within caliper, ties up. Non-square anchors must have **all three** other cells of the smooth × shift-prime table; square anchors must have a nonsquare-shift control with the same two statuses. Distinct everywhere. Drop unmatched units before reading the outcome: **883/2048** quartets and **21/720** pairs lacked complete overlap (`match.txt` logs these). Every quartet dropout lacked a smooth/composite-shift neighbour in this match; the farthest such neighbour used is 999.97 ppm away. The finite modulus does not fix valuations above its own exponents. The selected common-support sample, especially the quartet, is not representative of all 1 mod 840 primes; these are not new independent data for the z75 anchors.

**Interpretation.** The square shift shows the largest difference: 299 pairs late only at the square shift vs 60 late only at control. Smoothness retains a smaller excess at fixed shift status (214:170 and 212:153 discordances); shift primality does not show a comparable, consistent excess at fixed smoothness (194:190 and 171:152). For scale, two-sided normal McNemar comparisons for those two smoothness contrasts give nominal p≈0.03 and 0.002, respectively. These are post hoc (z75 informed the design) and the contrasts use the same quartets, not two independent replications. We cannot rule out small effects or infer causation. A smooth p−1 also changes factors >7. For an outcome not based on first f, choose **eight complete sets per trillion bin in the first six bins** by SHA256(anchor), not by outcome. `subfactor.txt` contains exhaustive sorted solutions for all 288 primes, checked for distinctness and the defining identity. Square shifts had fewer/equal/more **total** ordered R₈ witnesses than their controls in **40/2/6** pairs; medians 32 vs 51. The two smooth comparisons gave 25/2/21 and 30/2/16, the shift-prime comparisons 22/1/25 and 23/3/22 (`subreport.txt`). No zero here. These central counts do not measure the extreme zero tail.

**Why the cheap detector changes.** For p=m²+1 each candidate `(f,u)` needs `m²≡−(u+1) mod f`. This is a necessary condition beyond matching p modulo M. In particular `u=1` is impossible: for f≡7 mod 8, `(−2/f)=−1` (Jacobi). Dropping just these shots from *both* arms changes the late counts to 367:164. Dropping all shots for which `−(u+1)` has no square root mod f changes them to **367:522**: the residual comparison actually reverses. `z76_disproof_report.py` enumerates every shot ≤1000 and every root residue mod f. The filtered comparison is not like-for-like: square roots can have multiplicity, so compatible shots can be more frequent at square shifts. The review’s `shot_expect.py` counts roots directly, including nonunit targets. Summing each shot's marginal probability at uniform m mod f conditional on gcd(m²+1,f)=1 gives 0.824 expected hits; at uniform units p mod f the sum is 2.891 (empirical means 0.670 and 2.030). This elementary local-density difference accounts for the direction of the gap in mean hits, without predicting the probability of *no* hit or controlling the selection of the matched primes. Neither of these two models conditions on the fixed CRT class. In a post-review refinement (`scripts/z76_disproof_crt_shots.py`), for each anchor class p₀ mod M take the first model uniform on residues m mod lcm(M,f) with 420|m, m²+1≡p₀ mod M and gcd(m²+1,f)=1; take controls uniform on units mod f compatible with p₀. Weighting by the 699 actual anchor classes gives **0.615 vs 2.018 expected hits**, compared to 0.670 vs 2.030 observed. This calculation of marginals still doesn't model smoothness, joint hits, or the chance of a genuine zero.

## 3. Targeted searches and limits

* Use the square-shift excess directly: enumerate even m≤6,000,000, p=m²+1 prime above the old floor in **any** of the six Mordell classes. 5,258 primes (only residues 1 and 121 are possible: membership in 𝔐 forces 60|m and m≡0,±1 mod 7). 2,840 had first R₈ f>1000; 8 exceeded the cap 2,000,000, but all 8 had genuine R₈ triples on exact fallback. * Also search the five remaining Mordell residues 121,169,289,361,529 above the old floor up to 3.1·10¹² (z75 had already scanned 1 mod 840 in this interval). Solve the two fixed-coordinate congruences for R₈ up to pair product 40,000: `n≡−u−B (8uB)` or `n≡−u (8uC−1)`. Either supplies a positive third coordinate at these heights.
