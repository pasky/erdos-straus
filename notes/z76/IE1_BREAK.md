# Least nonresidues, filtered Pell minima and twisted Pell coordinate rings

## 1. Target 1: confine the exceptional least-nonresidue problem to one prime

**Theorem 1.** The `BCM4_QR.md` + `BCM4_PUSH2.md` §5 external PID with full prime QR, both supplements, internal Dirichlet, Lag, `L(D)` and `¬ES(P)` can be made to satisfy: **for each positive nonstandard prime other than P, infinitely many standard positive primes are quadratic nonresidues modulo it.** Hence every odd prime other than P has a least positive nonresidue. In particular the `g(t)=ct`, `0<t<q` search from z75 has a minimum whenever nonempty, at all these q: in a two-class field it selects either everything (c=0 mod q), the squares (first at 1), or the nonsquares. The exceptional prime still satisfies `P≡1 mod 840`; in fact every positive standard integer is square there.

**Prescribing a mark rather than a vertical character.** Prepare the z75 finite-branching ordered tree *before* choosing any characters. On each finite depth of it use the smooth geometrically integral joint chart, with auxiliary square cover `T²=P`, from `BCM4_QR.md` §4. Dovetail obligations (node, positive rational scalar representative h of a prime divisor other than P at that node, repetition index); include all positive representatives, since final normalization uses **all** primes. For an obligation take a fresh arbitrarily large ordinary prime ℓ≡1 mod 4 with a good prefix containing the node. Require the reduction of h there to be a nonsquare unit. At other places impose only the original prefix requirements. More precisely:

* h is geometrically nonsquare on the auxiliary joint chart. At its node the affine domain is factorial and geometrically integral in characteristic zero. The divisor of h has multiplicity one on each geometric component: after constant extension the principal quotient is reduced, and the ambient remains normal (geometric normality over the perfect constant field). Likewise P and h have no common geometric component (flat algebraic constant extension preserves codimension, or descend a common height-one prime). Thus neither h nor h/P is square in the geometric function field. Adjoining √P cannot kill h. The geometric joint field over this square-covered node is regular: assemble the finite tree starting with the path to the node, then attach the other children in ancestor order. Each such generic fibre is geometrically integral over its parent, stays so over the current function field, and the old diagrams are dominated. On the initial path the square cover is just base change of the original tower, whose generic edges are geometrically integral. This proves the claim for the compositum actually used, not just for independent function labels. Deleting finitely many divisors for chart and prefix tests does not change it.
* Spread the fixed smooth joint open and its connected double cover `V²=h` (shrink to h invertible). Both are geometrically integral; at every sufficiently large ℓ both have `ℓ^d+O(ℓ^(d−1/2))` points with the same d. The latter counts **twice** the number of square hits on the former. Hence a nonsquare unit hit exists with all current tests. Choose ℓ≡1 mod 4 by ordinary Dirichlet, and take a generic smooth lift there. Only one sign is prescribed on each such prefix. In the jth obligation take depth at least j (and sufficient for the node) and tests exhausting those at each fixed node, with all the z75 Dirichlet unit conditions. Require the assigned primes strictly increasing, above the relevant good bounds. All leftover primes get ordinary good prefixes with depths tending to infinity off finite sets. Lifts, tails, subsequent decisions and the extension lemma for (R) are exactly as before. There is no genericity claim after conditioning on the eventual branch; the mark is already on the whole finite tree. Each nonzero function still has finite prime support.
* For an actual positive normalized nonstandard prime q≠P, take its label at a containing node. At its assigned places the mark of q is a nonsquare unit, and ℓ≡1 mod 4 gives `χ_q(ℓ)=χ_ℓ(q)` by full prime QR. Since ℓ is standard and q infinite, ℓ itself is a positive reduced representative. Externally scanning `[1,ℓ]` gives the minimum, not just an upper bound. Standard odd q are ordinary. No sign is forced on P by this argument: its double cover is *split* after the auxiliary pullback.

**Exact exception variant.** Include the free-line task of `IE1_PUSH2.md`, Theorem B, for each infinite positive h: `Z=√h θ` with fresh positive residue between 1 and 2. These are deterministic polynomial-line edges on the pre-marked tree. Include integral Z in the presentations (also choose an integral Z on the tails). The old sign obligations above apply even to primes born on those lines; they only concern ordinary marks at nodes where those primes already exist. At the actual line edge set `χ_P(Z)=1`: Z is a free parameter over the old vertical field and (R) permits this extension for the preassigned marks. New horizontal bits are *computed*, not prescribed. All previous constraints at P are unchanged. Thus each positive infinite t has an integral nonsquare strictly below t, while all positive standard integers are squares at P. This gives the claimed unique exception **with** Dirichlet, Lag, QR and L(D).

**Threshold audit.** There is no least nonstandard positive prime in *any* such external PID: for infinite prime q, `(q−1)/2` is infinite and <q; finite external factorization forces a nonstandard prime divisor. This does not rule out a least prime of **negative character**. More precisely at a positive nonstandard `b<P` the condition for b to be least nonresidue, given two classes, is exactly that b be prime of negative character and every positive prime below b have positive sign (external finite factorization, not a theorem of IOpen alone). The necessary linear-algebra compatibility for forcing this by a stage extension at P is that the prescribed signs on the classes of all requested small primes and of b respect **all relations with old classes** modulo squares in the enlarged vertical field. The (R) extension lemma supplies the horizontal characters *after* this compatibility, not before. Theorem 1 gives no such compatibility across future floor requests. Leastness at the square-marked P, and general linear/affine residue-root minimization in unrestricted t, remain open here.

## 2. Target 3: fixed-modulus Diophantine filters **on Pell witnesses**

Use Theorem A of `notes/z75/IE1_PUSH2.md` with exactly one height generator for each standard squarefree d>1. This is the same construction: its natural size group is `ℚ·[P]` in additive scale notation (the one-dimensional vector space with basis the **class** of P), so one nonzero generator spans it. Write A for the final rational algebra and R for its all-place integral subring. At each ordinary place P has mark 1. The modulus m may be **any** parameter in R, including a nonstandard modulus with zero image in some or all ordinary marks. It must not vary with the searched coordinates. Thus the schema below is in the ordinary arithmetic language without naming a marked element.

**Theorem 2.** In that countable `IOpen + Lag + PW + ¬ES(P)` model, for arbitrary `D≥2` and parameters, minimize `u≥0` satisfying `∃0≤v≤u [u²=1+Dv² ∧ Φ(u,v)]`. Here Φ is **any Boolean combination** of polynomial comparisons and fixed-modulus polynomial congruences `m | f(u,v)` with arbitrary fixed moduli and polynomial coefficients in R. Use absolute values for signed moduli; `0 | f` is just equality to zero. Every nonempty such set has a least member. This is a restricted bounded existential least-number principle: on signed polynomial inputs, both divisibility and its negation have bounded existential definitions over IOpen. Using congruence notation does not literally keep the number of witnesses at one in the original ring language. This is not minimization of `∃v<P: v²≡g(t) mod P` without a Pell equation.

**Proof.** For infinite D the bounded part of the positive branch is externally finite by packing in the rank-one order (`IE1_PUSH2.md`, growth lemma): ratios >1 of distinct units have nonzero positive integral ordinate, hence size at least √D. Truncate at a member. For finite square D the branch is trivial. For finite nonsquare `D=b²d` work over `k=ℚ(√d)`; let T be the single pure torus generator for d (size H>1 infinite), and let `e>1` be the ordinary least positive nontrivial norm-one unit of the order `ℤ[√D]`. The integral positive units with signed ordinates are **exactly**

` s=e^j T^r,      j∈ℤ (standard), r∈ℚ. `

Indeed the units of `A_k` are `k^× T^ℚ` by the unit calculation in z75. Every pure pair has marks `(1,0)`, hence coordinates integral even on dividing the ordinate by b. Removing the pure factor leaves a standard-field pair with rational coordinates integral at every ordinary mark, so both are ordinary integers (in the basis `1,√D`). Conversely each displayed product has integral coordinates by multiplication of pairs. Retain `s≥1` for the nonnegative branch. Its abscissa increases strictly with s.

**Finite denominator lemma.** For any nonzero m in R and a Laurent polynomial `F(S)=Σ c_i S^i ∈ A_k[S,S⁻¹]` obtained by substituting `(S+S⁻¹)/2,(S−S⁻¹)/(2√D)` into f, if F has a coefficient outside `mA_k`, the condition `F(s)∈mA_k` has **externally finitely many hits on every bounded positive branch segment**. This is a necessary condition for R-divisibility, sufficient only after checking integral marks on the quotient. For coefficients all in `mA_k` we will instead obtain a periodic test.

Here are details justifying the use of root-level independence even when **m and f have parameters from later floor or sphere edges**. Work in a finite prefix of the construction over `k`, containing all the data and the d-task. After the d-task the base is Laurent in `T^ℚ` over the preceding extended algebra. Replace at this task the exponent group by `(1/N)ℤ`; then replay the subsequent finitely many edges in that prefix, increasing N to clear the finitely many d-denominators in sphere targets and other defining coefficients and in the data. A free floor line is a polynomial algebra, not a base change of *its ordered point*. Other constant tori tensor with their whole constant coordinate algebras independently of T; spheres have monic equations; skip edges do nothing. Thus as **algebras**, at the end of the prefix the full ring is the base change of this smaller algebra B by `k[T^ℚ]` over `k[T^(±1/N)]`. This can equivalently be proved inductively taking the union over N, since every expression uses finitely many root exponents. The full algebra is a free B-module with basis any Laurent monomial representatives of `ℚ/((1/N)ℤ)`, including 1. The remaining edges to the final algebra are faithfully flat (free polynomial or monic sphere, or tensoring with a nonzero constant torus algebra over ℚ); contraction of the ideal `(m)` is therefore exact. We may test in the prefix modulo `mB` with the **same free basis**, even for composite m. This is not an irreducibility assumption on any quotient at a later stage.

Let h be the difference between largest and smallest exponents appearing in F, omitting zero coefficients (F=0 trivial). If `r` has order >h in `ℚ/((1/N)ℤ)`, the surviving terms of `Σ c_i e^(ji) T^(ri)` are in distinct basis slots, with unit monomial/constant multipliers. They cannot cancel modulo m. For h=0 the nontrivial test has no hits. Otherwise r belongs at a hit to `(1/(N·lcm(1,…,h)))ℤ`. On `1≤s≤B₀` for a field bound B₀, r ranges over a bounded rational interval by rank one, independently of standard j. Thus just finitely many r can work. At fixed r, `Σ (c_i T^(ri)) X^i` remains a nonzero Laurent polynomial modulo m. At most h distinct `X=e^j` can annul it: shift to a polynomial of degree h and use the Vandermonde determinant, a nonzero element of k and therefore invertible even over the possibly nonreduced coefficient ring modulo m. This proves the lemma.

In the other case write `F=mG` **coefficientwise in the algebra**, before taking marks; never divide by the image of m. On the integral pairs `G(s)=f(u,v)/m` belongs to A: it belongs to `A_k` and is fixed under the constant conjugation by the displayed equality and `m≠0`. Choose one ordinary positive integer C clearing the denominators of all the coefficients of G in the basis `1,√D`, i.e. `CG` has coefficients in `R[√D]`. This exists by `R⊗ℚ=A`, including when the marks have kernels. Integrality of the marks of `G(s)` is automatic away from the ordinary primes dividing C. At `ℓ^a‖C` test the scalar coordinate of `CG(s)` modulo `ℓ^a`. In the free quadratic algebra over `ℤ/ℓ^a`, the pure pair is the identity and e is a unit, so the evaluation, also for negative Laurent exponents, depends only on j modulo the (finite) order of e there. Its other coordinate is zero. Hence each such filter is a union of phases `j mod L` with a positive standard period, and finitely many tests have a common period. This proof includes `m=P−1` (zero at every mark), not just all-place units. In each permitted phase j₀, ordinary translates by `e^L` bracket **every positive endpoint ≥1 in the real closure**: at an infinite endpoint choose its scale `r[H]` and compare to `T^r e^j₀`, at a bounded endpoint use r=0. The ratio is bounded above and below by positive standard constants, so standard powers suffice. Between consecutive phase translates no element of that phase can lie, by equality of scales and the displayed normal form. Open polynomial filters cut the branch into finitely many intervals/points by Laurent substitution. After truncating at any member, the nonconstant nonstandard-modulus tests change their default (false) values only at finitely many points by the lemma. Split off those points and cut the remaining intervals there. For each phase each remaining interval has a first point if nonempty by bracketing its inclusive or exclusive left endpoint. Finite union proves Theorem 2. ∎

## 3. Target 2: all-order twisted roots really can be factorial on the Pell torus

**Theorem 3 (geometric, over ℚ).** For the norm-one torus `U: x²−2y²=1` and `e=3+2√2` there is a countable tower of copies of U with transitions `w_j=e^(b_j) w_(j+1)^(n_j)` in the split coordinate `w=x+√2 y`. All these maps descend to ℚ. The products of the positive standard degrees are cofinal for divisibility; the direct limit **of rational coordinate rings is an external PID** (and the split limit over `k=ℚ(√2)` is a PID). Thus all standard orders occur with twists from a *cyclic* constant group. We use this as an algebraic lemma; priority not established for the twisted PID assertion. A compatible ordering is given by a positive infinite w₀ and positive roots. The rings here contain ℚ: this alone is **not** an IOpen model.

Choose `α∈Ẑ` with the property that each nonzero affine form `aα+c`, `a,c∈ℤ`, `(a,c)≠(0,0)`, has finite divisibility: only finitely many primes divide it and every local valuation is finite. Enumerate the forms. For each prime p choose a residue avoiding the zeros of the first `J(p)` forms, with `J(p)→∞` and `J(p)<p`; decrease J as necessary to avoid including a form identically zero mod p (only finitely many bad primes for each finite list). Pick an irrational p-adic lift. This gives the property, including constants. Let `M_j=(j+1)!`, `n_j=M_(j+1)/M_j`, choose residues `s_j≡α mod M_j`, `s₀=0`, and `b_j=(s_(j+1)−s_j)/M_j`. At a fixed level i the accumulated twist exponent `s=(s_j−s_i)/M_i` modulo `N=M_j/M_i` is the reduction of `β=(α−s_i)/M_i` in Ẑ. Every nonzero affine form in β has the same finite-divisibility property by clearing M_i.

**Norm bound.** Above a closed point at level i in the split torus take a root a of its polynomial, in the number field `E=k(a)`. At level j the geometric lifts have coordinate z with `z^N=a e^(−s)`. In the group of S-units of E modulo torsion, a free finitely generated abelian group (take S containing all primes of a and e), choose an integer coordinate functional λ with `v=λ(e)≠0` since e is nontorsion. Put `u=λ(a)`. If `t=[E(z):E]`, the norm of z is an S-unit (all valuations away from S are zero upstairs), and

` N · λ(Norm z) = t(u−sv). `

Consequently `t≥ N/gcd(N,u−sv)≥N/C` with C the finite product of prime powers dividing `u−vβ` in Ẑ. This holds for **every** z over a, including when a is a power of e or a root of unity. There are at most C irreducible factors over E. Thus the number of primes over the starting closed point is uniformly bounded at all levels: its fibre irreducible factors correspond to the factors over E after fixing a. The same boundedness downstairs follows by the finite constant extension k (at most two initial split closed points per rational closed point, surjectivity under scalar extension). These are finite étale transition maps, hence no ramification, disappearance or merger of branches. A fixed nonzero function has finitely many zeros at birth, and after some finite level none of their branches split further. Their multiplicities remain constant. This handles the identity divisor, precisely where untwisted roots fail.

**Principalization downstairs.** At each level the rational affine conic compactifies to ℙ¹ over ℚ (rational identity point) minus one degree-two point at infinity. Its coordinate ring is Dedekind and its class group is `ℤ/2` by degree and restriction of divisors. Translation by e followed by the power map as displayed extends to a map of compactifications of degree n_j (split Laurent coordinate); pullback multiplies degrees by n_j and the boundary maps to the boundary. An even total degree therefore kills the ideal class of *every* finite-stage ideal. For each pair, extend its ideal from a common finite level to such a level: it becomes principal, with a row and two cofactors, permanently so. Hence the union is Bézout. For any nonzero f the sum of multiplicities of closed points in its divisor is bounded at all subsequent levels by the norm bound and étaleness. A factorization into nonunits in the union has at most this bounded length: put it at a common level where each nonunit must have a zero, and add orders. An element without zeros at its level was already a unit. Thus the union is atomic, and atomic Bézout is PID (choose a finite subideal of minimal nonzero generator length in any nonzero ideal). Over k the finite levels are Laurent PIDs; the rest is identical. ∎

**Arithmetic gap.** The compatible local pairs `w_j=e^((α−s_j)/M_j)` exist at every ordinary prime in the profinite closure of the ordinary integral norm-one cyclic group: interpret the exponent on every finite quadratic algebra mod `ℓ^t` by reducing modulo the order of e there. This specifies integral coordinates, and the twist equation holds level by level. It does *not* imply that arbitrary functions of those pairs are units at almost all ℓ; the proof of the norm bound counted closed points **over characteristic zero**, not the prime divisors of their specializations. Nor does the one-dimensional ring yet have floors, Lag, or the necessary heights for a full arithmetic construction. Untwisted identity marks on multiple tori have a different problem: for coprime row inputs vanishing at all the marks the row `aU+bV=1` has no lift there. Merely putting the z75 rank-one tori into the row queue is not a Bézout proof.

## 4. Which PID route to induction is actually ruled out?

**Corollary (relative to Wilmers and Kaye).** No nonstandard external IOpen PID has a nonstandard PA initial segment. Thus none satisfies `IE₁`. Moreover **any** such PID satisfying `IE₁⁻` would satisfy *every* true `∀E₁` sentence. In particular an external-PID model of `IE₁⁻ + ¬ES(P)` would imply an **ordinary counterexample** to ES, not just one-sided nonprovability. This consequence requires no assumption that ordinary ES is true.

Indeed take an infinite t in a proposed PA cut and form t! **inside that cut**. It is nonzero and divisible by every ordinary prime, with the witnesses in the cut, hence also in the parent. Standard primes stay pairwise nonassociate primes in the PID by division with standard remainders. This contradicts finite factorization. More generally, lack of a nonzero multiple of **all standard positive integers**, e.g. `⋂_{n≥1} 2^n R=0` (external intersection), suffices to exclude any nonstandard PA cut. Use Wilmers' PA-cut theorem for `IE₁`, and for parameter-free induction use the stronger structural statement explicitly attributed to Kaye in D’Aquino, NDJFL 1997, proof of Thm 3.3: an `IE₁⁻` model not satisfying `∀E₁(ℕ)` has a nonstandard PA initial segment. This cites the **ordinary-language** input to her proof, not her smash-language nonrecursiveness conclusion. Universal ES on inputs ≥2 is itself `∀E₁` using the polynomial bounds on a sorted triple (`LOGIC_BEZOUT_NORM_COLLECTION.md` §4). If true it holds in that PID; by contraposition a countermodel of the indicated kind entails its falsity in ℕ. This does **not** rule out `IE₁⁻` alone in all nonstandard external PIDs, still less `IW₂`. If ordinary ES is true, *every* `IE₁⁻` countermodel must have a nonstandard PA cut, hence a nonzero common multiple of all standard integers; in its signed ring the dyadic kernel `J=⋂_{n≥1} 2^n R` is nonzero, and if it also has IOpen + Lag, `J²=J` by `BCM4_QR.md` §1. This is the proposed FRONTIER §3/§4 barrier. It does not imply BCM at arbitrary internal cutoffs. It concerns external structure, not the first-order Bézout axiom. The same PA-cut exclusion holds for the rank-one model by growth: for infinite t in such a cut `2^t` exceeds every standard power of t, impossible there.

Smith, NDJFL 1993, §§2–4 (full text in `data/z66/ie1/`) gives `IE₁=LE₁`, maximal elements of bounded nonempty E₁ sets, the field quotient at primes, and one direction of reciprocity for a standard prime. It does not establish existence of a nonresidue at every prime. With two squareclasses and **given** a nonsquare c, LE₁ does minimize `ct` square in `0<t<q` and hence does give a least nonsquare. Neither c nor the two-class statement follows from the least axiom alone. We have not obtained arbitrary one-witness induction, or separated `IE₁⁻` from ES. All new ES countermodels use prior positive reciprocal descent at an infinite square-marked prime, not a specialization of four-square witnesses and not a counterexample in ℕ.
