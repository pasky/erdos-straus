## 1. A negative answer to the bounded-obstruction question

This answers the final question of `notes/z76/DISPROOF_DEEP.md` §3 negatively for the simultaneous coordinate covers, and more generally for finite ℚ-group torsors of bounded geometric monodromy exponent. Use the conventions of `notes/z75/DISPROOF_OBSTRUCT.md` §§1–3. In particular D means integral on the **original** affine equation at every finite place, in the coordinate open at every place, positive at infinity, and integral on the **open** at almost all places. Write s for the product of the primes with odd valuation in n (its squarefree kernel). Assume s≠1 for k=4 and s∉{1,2} for k=8. Take V to be the coordinate-deletion open in any of

    S: k u B C = n+u+B,     X: k a b c d = a+b+nc,     U: k x y z = n(xy+xz+yz).

**Theorem.** Fix m,M≥2. Adjoin m-th roots of the arrangement units (z75 §2), or equivalently of the original coordinates, and allow rational twists. There exists a single rational twist π:W→V and a point T∈W(𝔸) with π(T)∈D and T orthogonal to Br(W)[M]. In fact for S **every** z75 repair with q^{mM} works provided q≡−1 mod k, q∤mMn(n+1), and −n is an mM-th power mod q. This means existence of a *choice of local lifts*, not that arbitrary lifts give the same evaluation. The construction is uniform in the classes and their conductors. Equivalently it handles order dividing M modulo constants: subtract the value at the rational base point. It does not assert existence of a point on each twist. At a failed input the set in question is the **union** of the images over twists; one twist suffices.

In particular no such obstruction explains 4545 on X (4), 131 or 241 on U (8), or any of the nonsquare odd R₈ zeros on S (8). For the cited failed inputs the global negative decisions are from the independent exhaustive checks in z75–76, not from the theorem. ES in ℕ remains open.

### A local evaluation fact

Let O=ℤ_q, K=ℚ_q, F=𝔽_q, q∤M. On a smooth finite-type O-scheme 𝒴, take two O-sections whose reductions lie in one split-torus component Y≅𝔾_m^r of the special fibre. Suppose the reductions differ coordinatewise by M-th powers in F^×. Then **every** β∈Br(𝒴_K)[M] has equal values in Br(K) at the two generic points of the sections.

Here and below Br is the cohomological Brauer group. Lift β to H²(𝒴_K,μ_M) by the Kummer sequence. Removing the other components in the special fibre if necessary, the localization boundary and codimension-one absolute purity give a residue in H¹(Y,ℤ/M). Pullback by a section sends this to the residue in H¹(F,ℤ/M) of β evaluated on its generic point: the section meets the smooth special fibre with multiplicity one (transverse base change for purity). The residue detects the class in Br(K)[M], since Br(O)=Br(F)=0. Finally, for every γ∈H¹(Y,ℤ/M), evaluation is unchanged between the stated reductions. Translate the first point to the identity, then pull the translated character back by coordinatewise [M]. Its geometric H¹ class is zero, since geometric prime-to-q H¹ of a torus is generated over the algebraic closure by the coordinate Kummer characters, killed by [M]. Thus this pullback comes from F by Hochschild–Serre and is constant on Y(F). No assumption that F contains μ_M is needed. This is the usual smooth-locus residue method (cf. M. Bright, *Bad reduction of the Brauer–Manin obstruction*), with [M] on a torus. It compares even classes with **nonzero vertical residue**; compactifying and declaring the evaluations zero would not do that.

### Proof of the theorem

Choose q as stated (infinitely many by `notes/z75/DISPROOF_OBSTRUCT.md` §3, applied with exponent 2mM if necessary). Set B₀=(n+1)/q and C₀=(q+1)/k. The rational point P=(1,B₀,C₀) on S is positive and integral outside q. Normalize the twist by P. With x,y,z all invertible, its equation over ℚ is

    n+x^m+B₀(y^m−(q+1)z^m)=0,                 (1)
    u=x^m,   B=B₀ y^m,   C=C₀(z/(xy))^m.

Indeed this adjoins the m-th roots of the normalized u,B and uBC. The point above P is P̃=(1,1,1). Over K choose α^m=−n with α mod q an M-th power (Hensel applied to an mM-th root). Choose λ^m=(q+1)⁻¹ with λ≡1 mod q. A useful **line on the local twist** is

    L: t ↦ (α,t,λt),      t∈𝔾_m.                          (2)

The repaired point downstairs has C′=C₀, B′=B₀ q^{mM}, u′=(n+B′)/(k B′ C₀−1). Here B′ has valuation mM−1 and u′ reduces to −n. There is a lift

    T_q = (w, q^M, w q^M),     w^m=u′,   w≡α mod q,

again by Hensel. All other components are P̃. We compare values at P̃ and T_q for any β∈Br(W_K)[M] in **three equalities**:

1. **P̃ to L(1).** Use the model in the three-dimensional split torus over O given by q(n+x^m)+(n+1)(y^m−(q+1)z^m)=0. The special fibre has y^m=z^m; the component y=z is a split two-torus with coordinates (x,y). Smoothness there follows from the unit z-derivative by the relative Jacobian criterion. Both sections reduce on this component, to (1,1) and (α mod q,1). By the local fact their evaluations agree.
2. **L(1) to L(q^M).** Restrict β to (2). On 𝔾_{m,K}, every Brauer class of order dividing M is a constant plus (χ,t) with Mχ=0. Indeed the residue at 0 gives χ, the character symbol splits the residue map, and the kernel extends over 𝔸¹_K and is constant by homotopy invariance. At 1 and q^M the values agree. This step crosses a real change of valuation; it is not local constancy on a ball.
3. **L(q^M) to T_q.** Set Y=y/q^M, Z=z/q^M in the torus. The second model is n+x^m+(n+1)q^{mM−1}(Y^m−(q+1)Z^m)=0. The special fibre has x^m=−n, smooth by its x-derivative. The component x=α mod q is a split torus with coordinates (Y,Z). Our points reduce to (1,1) and (1,α mod q). The same local fact applies.

It follows that β(T_q)=β(P̃) locally, **for the full local M-torsion**, a stronger equality than for restrictions of global classes. The two smooth models include the needed sections; their generic fibres are both (1). We never suppose the diagonal projective Fermat twist has good reduction at q or that its boundary residues vanish. Returning to global classes, all other local values are unchanged; the diagonal evaluation sums to zero by reciprocity. The image lies in D by construction.

For X use (a,b,c,d)=(u,B,1,C); for U use (x,y,z)=(uBC,nuC,nBC). In each case twist the target coordinate cover by the image of P. The normalized target units pull back to monomials in the normalized source coordinates, so the map lifts over ℚ from (1) by the same monomials in their roots (c-root=1 for X). Project T by this morphism. Integrality, positivity and the adelic restriction hold on the image; every target M-torsion class pulls back to M-torsion, so there is no computation of the target group to assume. ∎

### Sharpness: exactly which jumps can pass?

Keep a prime q as in the theorem. Keep the same P away from q; at q take B′=q^E B₀, C′=C₀ and solve for u′, where E≥1 is integral and the resulting coordinates define a point of the open. For the simultaneous level-m cover on S the resulting adelic point passes the union-over-twists M-torsion test **if and only if mM divides E**. For sufficiency, in the proof replace q^M in the coordinates and line parameter by q^{E/m} (still an M-th power), and mM−1 in the second model by E−1. Nothing else changes.

For necessity put N=mM. Choose a finite Dirichlet character ψ of order N, unramified at q, whose value on Frobenius at q has order N. To justify this even for composite N: the prime q∤N has valuation one at every place above q in F_N=ℚ(μ_N). Eisenstein gives [F_N(q^{1/N}):F_N]=N; the extension is cyclic Kummer over F_N and normal over ℚ. By Chebotarev pick a rational prime ℓ outside Nq with Frobenius conjugate to a generator over F_N. Then ℓ splits in F_N and q^{(ℓ−1)/N} has order N mod ℓ (reduce the Frobenius action on q^{1/N} at a place over ℓ). Composing t↦t^{(ℓ−1)/N} on 𝔽_ℓ^× with an identification μ_N(𝔽_ℓ)≅(1/N)ℤ/ℤ gives ψ. At q the cyclic algebra (ψ,B) evaluates differently at the patched and diagonal adeles by invariant ±Eψ(Frob_q); units do not contribute at this unramified prime. All other places agree. If N∤E the point is therefore already rejected by Br(V)[mM] downstairs. But on **each** rational twist there is a unit b with B=a b^m, a∈ℚ^×, so π^*(ψ,B)=(ψ,a)+(mψ,b). The first summand is constant and the second is killed by M. Thus even if a local lift exists everywhere on that twist it cannot be orthogonal to Br[M]. The same necessity applies on the indicated X and U images using respectively b downstairs on X and z=nBC downstairs on U (its ratio is again q^E); sufficiency is by the morphisms already given. This optimality concerns this one-coordinate valuation jump, not a lower bound on all possible repairs.

### Consequences and checks on the scope

* There **are** genuinely non-pullback classes. For example, on (1) for even m, the quaternion (x,y) is defined on W (two units), belongs to Br(W)[M] for even M, and is geometrically nonconstant. On the smooth affine diagonal surface with y=0 restored, over ℚ̄ its residue at y=0 is the squareclass of x in the function field of the curve n+x^m=(q+1)B₀ z^m. This is a smooth geometrically irreducible affine Fermat curve (smooth projective closure, irreducible by Bézout); x has simple zeros at x=0 there, so is not a square. These zeros may be used to test its function field even though x=0 is not on the original open. In contrast every class from Br(V) on V=S° has geometrically trivial pullback for even m: z75 §2 generates modulo constants by character classes (geometrically zero) and quaternion symbols of pairs of arrangement units (whose entries become squares over ℚ̄). Thus already m=M=2 really tests more than inherited classes, including if pullbacks of unbounded-order downstairs classes are allowed. The calculation does not require a description of the entire Fermat-surface Brauer group.
* **Beyond the displayed Kummer torsor.** At given V,m,M the same downstairs adelic point passes the following test for **every** finite ℚ-group torsor 𝒯→V of geometric monodromy exponent dividing m: some rational twist of 𝒯 admits an adelic lift orthogonal to its entire M-torsion. Indeed normalize 𝒯 by its fibre at P (the image of P on the target if V≠S). Pull this twist back to the normalized level-m cover W of V. Its geometric monodromy is trivial because the image of π₁(W̄) is mπ₁(V̄), with π₁(V̄)=Ẑ(1)^r, as computed in z75 §2. Twisting by constants does not change this kernel. Since W is geometrically connected, this finite étale pullback descends from a finite étale ℚ-scheme by the arithmetic π₁ sequence; it has a rational point over P̃ by normalization. It therefore has a section. Under the resulting W→𝒯^tw morphism, M-torsion pulls back to M-torsion. No assumption of abelian **arithmetic** monodromy is made. In particular finitely many covers and finitely many prescribed exponents can be handled simultaneously by taking common multiples. This still does not treat a class of unbounded order or a single adelic point for the unbounded tower: q, P and W may change. The unrestricted coordinate-reconstruction result in z76 §3 stands.
* **One twist for all bounded dyadic tests (different adeles).** At any fixed level m choose q just once for exponent 2m. Since q≡−1 mod k, v₂(q−1)=1. In the cyclic group 𝔽_q^× the image of [2^j m] is therefore exactly the image of [2m] for every j≥1, so the prime conditions in the theorem hold for every M=2^j with the **same q, P and twist W**. On that W each Br[2^j] set has a restricted lift, although the full 2-primary subgroup has no restricted orthogonal point at a failed input (z76 §3). There is no compactness contradiction: in this sequence the projected B-coordinate at q tends to 0, on the deleted boundary. This corollary does not interchange “for every j” and “there exists a point”.
* Numerical calibration is not needed for the local proof: e.g. at m=M=2, q=11 for n=4545,k=4 (−n≡9≡5⁴ mod 11), q=7 at n=131,k=8 (−n≡2≡2⁴ mod 7), q=7 at n=241,k=8 (−n≡4≡3⁴ mod 7). Unlike q² (rejected by the explicit quaternion in `notes/z76/DISPROOF_DEEP.md` §3), the q⁴ repair always admits an orthogonal lift at this level under the displayed prime conditions. At squares the Chebotarev step fails: a square root of −n is incompatible with q≡−1 mod 4, q∤n. At k=8 the same issue arises for twice a square ((−2/q)=−1). This is a constraint on the construction, not a claim that these all obstruct on U.

## 2. Fixed fibres and thin sequences

`KUMMER_FIBRES.md` proves the bounded-height polynomial dichotomy, including the requirement c|C missing from a direct application of `Z60_MS105.md` Thm 3. For every bounded selector of c the non-BM sterile parameters of a polynomial family have density zero; this is **not finiteness**. The Pell and exponential composite tests there distinguish arbitrarily long finite-modulus exclusion from a true zero: the same exclusion occurs on an explicit exponential control with a guaranteed imprimitive R₈ witness at every index. Neither an infinite family of genuine nonsquare zeros nor a new deterministic small-c sterile family was obtained.
