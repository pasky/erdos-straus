## 3. Counting P: two short-shift bounds and an integer-centre cap

Write M=(N+e)/4. PL showed each oriented centre comes from h=Ma/b | M² in one unit class modulo e (gcd(e,M)=1). For any g|M this costs ≤τ((M/g)²) if g²≤e, ≤2τ((M/g)²) if g≤e. These count *centres*, not all factorizations.

**Small-side lemma.** For t≤e<2t and a≤b the number of data at fixed e is at most

    τ(M) [1+2 ceil(sqrt_floor(M/t)/t)],   sqrt_floor(x)=floor(sqrt(x)).

Indeed if a<t fix ad|M: b=M/(ad), a≡−b mod e gives at most one possibility in [1,t). Otherwise fix a|M. The smaller of b,d is at most floor(sqrt(M/t)); each is in one specified residue class mod e since b≡−a and bd=M/a. Even the unit class containing 1 has at most the displayed ceiling choices per side. Double for orientations. The bound remains valid if t is just a lower cutoff on e (the upper cutoff was unused). When t³≥M the sharper **4τ(M) oriented** bound works: a<t gives ≤τ(M); for a≥t, a≤b, one has d≤M/t²≤t, so only the small-d alternative is needed. This permits pooling many e-bands rather than paying a separate endpoint error in each.

On a shift interval the M's occupy at most H consecutive integers ≤m. Bound Στ(M) by the smaller of (i) the elementary hyperbola bound 2[H(1+bit_length(⌊√m⌋))+⌊√m⌋] (sum H/d+1 for d≤√m; use harmonic ≤1+log₂⌊√m⌋) and (ii) PL §2's positive Dirichlet convolution with local running maximum of (j+1)/p^{j/k}, not (2j+1)/p^{j/k}, 3≤k≤12. This gives m^(1/k)(HA+B) with normalized local Euler products A,B as there. All root and product roundings in `z77_bound.py` are upward; by adjacent-term ratios primes ≥2^k never increase the local factor. `small(n,j)` sums the per-band minimum of this lemma and PL's wheel bound. In `pooled` take one interval [2^i,2^j) with 2^{3i}≥m, use 4Στ(M) there and `small(n,i)` below.

**Cube sieve.** Rather than use any moment bound, for K=3 and r≤50000 we sieve each individual M=(r³+e)/4, 1≤e<32r, by all primes p≤r. Greedily assign prime factors to g while the product stays ≤e. Unassigned factors of p^j contribute 2j+1 to τ((M/g)²). The residual x has no prime factor ≤r and x≤M<(r+1)³. If x can be put into g, absorb it all; otherwise its τ(x²) costs 1 for x=1, ≤3 for x<(r+1)², ≤9 otherwise. The contribution is multiplied by 1 or 2 according as g²≤e or not. We may include nonunits in the arithmetic sum: only eligible shifts require a bound. `z77_p3.c` records cumulative counts for e<2^j r, j=0,…,5. All sizes fit uint64: M<(50001)³ and τ(M²)≤1000√M: the local ratio (2j+1)/p^{j/2} is ≤4 for p=2, ≤3 for p=3, ≤2 for p=5,7, ≤1 for p≥11. This last bound also bounds the partially factored cost up to the extra factor 9. Even 32r times this bound times 18 is <2^64.

For the complementary shifts e≥E, 4acd=(N+e)(1+a/b)/e ≤2(N/e+1), so 1≤acd≤T=⌊(⌊N/E⌋+1)/2⌋. The first centres lie in the integer list 1−4t, t=1,…,T. ## 4. Higher P levels: determinant in both directions of a pair row

Fix an upper bound n for N. Use half-open integer boxes in a,c,d, successive edges E,E+max(1,⌊E/s⌋), s=2 or 5. At fixed pair the possible f are in a unit class modulo u=4 times the product of the pair; each is parametrised by an integer in an interval of width w (number of consecutive integers).

PL §3's evaluation determinant works for **any** fixed unit class mod u: f=uZ+v, gcd(u,v)=1, f|V. Remove from V factors over primes dividing u, take the root −v/u modulo what remains. For l distinct candidate Z in a subinterval of diameter ≤2^h, the Vandermonde determinant times V_reduced^{m(m+1)/2} is a nonzero integer divisible by the product of f_i^m (row divisibility), for 1≤m<l. Hence at most l−1 candidates if

    V_bound^{m(m+1)/2} 2^{h l(l−1)/2} < f_lower^{ml}.

Partition the w consecutive indices into chunks of **2^h+1** integers, and take the minimum over l=2,3,4,5,6,8,12 and m. `cap` rounds log₂V upward and log₂f downward on an exact quarter-bit grid. The unfactored V itself can depend on the fixed pair, but its bounding value does not depend on any of the three variables in the box. Multiply by the number of fixed pairs, then take the minimum over routes.

In a fixed (a,d) row, e=V/f lies in a unit class modulo 4ad and falls as c increases. For c in a box of wc integers its entire range in **progression steps** has diameter ≤V₁(wc−1)/f₀², by subtracting the two reciprocals. Hence there are at most 1+⌊V₁(wc−1)/f₀²⌋ consecutive possible index positions. Here we may apply the determinant **again, to the cofactor e** with f_lower replaced by max(1,⌊(n/2)/(4A'C'D')⌋) when n/2<N≤n.
