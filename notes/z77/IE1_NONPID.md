# Above a nonstandard PA cut

Use nonnegative cones for induction, signed rings for fraction fields. An end-initial signed subring means its cone is initial in the larger cone. A cut below means a substructure, not an interval with a last member. All induction and least principles in the ambient arithmetic language are for each **standard formula**, with internal number parameters where indicated.

## 1. Transfer the pair, not the axioms of its two reducts separately

**Theorem.** There are countable end-initial inclusions `C ⊊ D ⊊ R` such that:

* `C⁺ ≡ ℕ` is nonstandard. `R` is IOpen + Bézout + BCM + Lag + full prime QR (two nonzero classes, both supplements). A prime `P∈D` greater than all `C` has `¬ES(P)` in `R`.
* `D` satisfies IOpen + Bézout + internal Dirichlet + full prime QR + Lag + universal TS + `L(Div)` (`L(D)` of z75, arbitrary polynomial divisibility/comparison filters). TS at these primes holds also in `R`. There are primes of `D` beyond every particular bound of `D`, not just the primes of `C`. Neither `D` nor `R` is an external PID. In fact some positive element of `R` is divisible by **every nonzero element of `D`**.
* For every positive `m∈C`, `P` is a **unit square mod m** in `R`, with a root representative in `C`. Conversely every positive `a∈C` is a square modulo `P`. There is no claim `P≡1 mod m` for every `m∈C` in this construction.

Start with the actual z76 §3 model `D_std` of `QR_TS_BCM.md`, not merely any model of its elementary theory. Feed it as bottom PID to §§4–5 there: the input is exactly the Lag Bézout PID package of `BCM4_PUSH2.md` and the bottom (B) identity, which follows from (R) *also* on the circle/Dirichlet tower. Its old primes have the required characters with realized kernels. It has the discrete support/CRT and the squareclass supply at the ordinary primes; no Gaussian invariant is demanded of its successors. No change is made to an upper edge. Let `R_std` be the resulting ordinary countable union. Endness of each inclusion and the polynomial bounds for an ES tuple ensure `¬ES(P)` in it. The first axis `h` is divisible by every nonzero bottom element. In the bottom the square-unit marks at all ordinary primes imply the unit-square condition mod every standard `m` by finite CRT and division with standard remainder. At the reverse place use full QR for ordinary primes and the supplement for 2, then ordinary factorization of the given positive integer.

Expand the *one structure* `R_std` by predicates for its signed ordinary integers and its actual bottom ring, and constants for `P,h`. Take a countable elementary extension of this expanded structure containing an infinitely large member of its first predicate (elementary diagram, compactness, downward Löwenheim–Skolem). The two predicates give `C,D`. Initiality, properness, divisibility by the whole bottom, all the first-order properties of the relativized theories, the sentence `P>C`, the two uniform square assertions, and the **ambient** failure of ES transfer. No endness of the elementary extension over the original countable structure is used. Alternatively enlarge by an ultrapower of the *flag* before taking a countable elementary substructure. The varying finite moduli and root representatives are then sequences, not just constant ordinary primes. Remainder rings at `m∈C` in each end extension are precisely the cut remainder rings by division. A constant ordinary mark by itself would miss the varying primes. We construct **a** countable `C`, not a prescribed presentation or an arbitrary completion of PA.

One must not transfer external PID or insist on literal congruence 1 in the seed: any nonzero element in the bottom PID has finite support, so `P−1` cannot be divisible by all ordinary primes. Indeed in the transferred flag some `m∈C` still violates that congruence. A unit-square profile alone is not an obstruction to arbitrary triples even in a model of true arithmetic. Here the actual obstruction to *all* tuples is already proved in the seed by reciprocal descent; it, not an attempted external enumeration of polynomials with cut coefficients, transfers. Every code in the cut which really evaluates to an ES witness in the ring would give an actual tuple. No general operation evaluating nonstandard-degree polynomials on `P` is assumed. A nonstandard factorial in `C` lies in the external dyadic kernel `J` of both bigger rings and is divisible by every standard prime. The finite-factorization test of `IE1_BREAK.md` §4 thus excludes external PID; `J²=J` by `BCM4_QR.md` §1. Nothing uses the truth of ES in ℕ.

## 2. Which induction holds above the cut? Which definitely fails?

Write `W(n;d,a) := ∃u,v≤(2a+1)(n+1) (u≥n ∧ u²=1+dv²)`. Then in the theorem **`C⁺` is exactly `W(n;2,3)`**. More generally for every nonsquare `d≥2` in `C`, every ambient integral Pell pair `u²−dv²=1` lies entirely in `C`.

Here are the seed unit checks needed for this assertion. For each fixed positive ordinary nonsquare `d`, put `k=ℚ(√d)`. In the bottom PID rational algebra after tensoring by `k` the only units are `k*`. Start at `k[P]`. The generic line, row and root have only scalar units; contraction back to the old algebra is as in `IE1_PUSH2.md` §4 (or tensor the old coefficientwise contraction by the flat constant field). The four-square affine quadric has only scalar units (graded top form integral, monic normal form); so does an inert-circle edge after this **real** constant extension: at the ordered old extended fraction field the top form `X²+Y²` remains irreducible. Integral graded degree and monicity give unit descent, independently of how the target factors after extending constants. All generic field extensions are geometrically integral, so the fixed real quadratic constant extension stays a field on fractions with its compatible real order. Units and inverses occur together at a finite prefix. Consequently an integral norm-one pair in the bottom gives coordinates in ℚ, then in ℤ by discreteness.

In a successor over an old field `F`, the *unpunctured* twist base after this scalar extension is a union of polynomial rings `F(√d)[x_j]`, with only `F(√d)*` units. Here and at later edges we can tensor over ℚ: the old fraction fields are regular over ℚ (at the twist use the directed union of rational fields), and all rational generic lines and sphere edges preserve regularity. The same generic-line/quadric unit-descent argument applies to the unpunctured algebra of §4 of z76. In detail contraction for rows is established over the rational unpunctured ring there by the two punctured charts and the nonnegative axis-value test; after constant extension contraction is coefficientwise in `1,√d` by independence over the new fraction field. For the other edges contraction is polynomial or monic. Thus a norm-one pair in the arithmetic successor has coordinates in `F` and, by `D'∩F=D_old`, in the old arithmetic ring. No use of external factorization after the pullback. Induct down finitely many successors in the seed. Ordinary Pell supplies the windows on the ordinary cut (successive powers of a norm-one unit); above it no pair brackets a nonstandard height for `d=2`. These facts for **all ordinary nonsquares at once** say a single first-order sentence relative to the first predicate (square in it is the same as square in the ambient ring). Hence transfer gives the assertions, not just one check per standard numeral.

The usual base/step for `W(n;2,3)` holds in IOpen: if a witness does not already reach `n+1` it has `u=n`, and multiplication by `3+2√2` gives a witness in the next bound. Thus even a parameter-free two-witness bounded existential induction axiom **fails**. Adding a PA cut, even with the trace properties below and with `J²=J≠0`, is not a converse to Kaye. D'Aquino, NDJFL 1997 Thm 1.2 and proof of Thm 3.3, cite implications for models of parameter-free induction violating true `∀E₁` sentences, not a sufficient condition for that induction. This argument does not use induction in the smash language.

**Positive global schemes.** In this very same BCM model the following are true, with `t` searched over the **whole** cone:

1. For each `d∈C` nonsquare, `∃v≤u [u²−dv²=1 ∧ Φ(u,v,b)]` admits a least `u` if nonempty, with *arbitrary* ambient parameters and indeed arbitrary first-order filter `Φ`. For open filters this is single-witness bounded existential minimization. It does not fill the missing windows: the set of heights stops at the cut. On `C` even subsets defined with outer parameters and quantifiers in the pair satisfy full induction: in the seed every nonempty trace on ℕ has a least member, a sentence for each defining formula. Apply this to the projection of the filtered pairs.
2. **Cut-coefficient elimination with unbounded witness values.** Consider Boolean combinations of polynomial comparisons in `t,b` and inequalities/equalities `c_i v ⋈ f_i(t,b)` with all `c_i∈C` signed, arbitrary polynomial right sides, and arbitrary ambient coefficients/parameters `b`. Permit the moving guard `0≤v≤B(t,b)`. The projection `∃v` with that guard admits least search in `t≥0`, as does its complement. Hence the associated ordinary successor induction holds. No cut bound on `b,t,v,f_i` or on `B` is imposed. This includes linear equations with cut leading coefficient and side conditions of the same kind. The conclusion is pure arithmetic by replacing the cut guards by `W(|c_i|;2,3)`; for `c_i` numerals the guards are unnecessary.

   Proof: work **before transfer** with any tuple of ordinary coefficients `c_i`. Include coefficients `1` for the bounds. Pass to a finite disjunction of systems of weak inequalities (strict comparisons adjusted by 1 using discreteness; either sign of equality handled). Deal with zero coefficients separately. Take a positive ordinary common multiple `m` of all nonzero `|c_i|`. On each residue class of `t mod m`, every `f_i(t,b) mod |c_i|` is fixed, also for infinite `b`, by Euclidean division. The lower/upper integer endpoints for `v` given by floor/ceiling division by `|c_i|` are therefore rational polynomials in `t` with fixed corrections on that class. Pairwise comparisons of the lower and upper endpoints decide whether there is a witness, since both bounds are present. Clearing denominators gives open comparisons, including for arbitrary `B`. Substitution `t=r+mk`, `0≤r<m`, gives an open set of nonnegative indices `k` in the sense of a quantifier-free formula. Nonempty such sets have first members by IOpen (alternatively integer-part floors of real-closed cell endpoints). Complements on each class are also open formulas. There are externally finitely many classes **only in the seed**. Transfer the resulting least-number sentences with universal relativized coefficient tuple; no external scan up to a nonstandard modulus in `R`. For instance in the model one may find the first `t>P` for which `c v=t²+B₀`, `2v≥Pt`, `v≤t²+B₀` has a solution, if nonempty, for nonstandard `0<c∈C`, arbitrary `B₀≥0`.

   Also, for any fixed finite quantifier pattern with all its number searches bounded by `b∈C` and open matrix with arbitrary ambient parameters, both least schemes for its unary projection hold. In the seed expand each quantifier over the *actual finite interval* before using IOpen; transfer the schema, not this expansion. The same proof allows such a bounded outer block on the formulas of item 2: for the finitely many specializations at a seed `b`, use a common ordinary modulus and the same finite Boolean algebra of residue-cell tests. These are fragment statements, not `IE₁(1)` for open equations nonlinear in the witness or arbitrary witness coefficients. They claim no separation from all conceivable alternative axiomatizations of these fragments.

## 3. A second, incompatible use of the flag

Instead start with the z76 §2 `IE1_BREAK.md` filtered-Pell model and name only ℤ and `P`. Exactly the pair argument of §1 gives a nonstandard true-arithmetic cut `C`, **literal** `P≡1 mod m` for every `0<m∈C`, ambient Lag, `PW` (all radicands with supplied unit), the filtered least scheme there with arbitrary ambient fixed moduli, and `¬ES(P)`. Every nonsquare `d≥2` of `C` has a unit already in `C` by elementary Pell there. Thus the windows now reach **all** heights for **all** those `d`, including nonstandard ones. The external rank-one packing estimates used to prove the scheme in the seed are not themselves claimed in the extension. No Bézout or BCM claimed here. Likewise transfer of just the bottom flag gives a non-PID model with TS + full QR + `L(Div)` on its whole cone, but **without** BCM: in the bottom seed ∀H>0 ∃d≤P, d>0, d∤H, by finite factorization, and that sentence transfers. These examples distinguish being non-PID from being BCM.

## 4. An exact simultaneous Pell test: the missing height vectors

Let `R` now be **any** integrally closed signed IOpen ring properly end extending a signed PA cut `C` (the ordinary cut works too). Put `F₀=Frac C`, `K=Frac R` and `L=rc(K)` with its chosen ordering. Let `Γ=L_{>0}/U` additively, where `U` is the group of ratios bounded above and below by positive `F₀` constants. This ordered group is divisible, torsion-free and nonzero (use any positive ring element above the cut). Write `λ(x)` for the size of `|x|`. For a finite list of positive cut elements `d_i≥2` with independent squareclasses in `F₀`, write `G=(±1)^r` and `d_I=∏_{i∈I}d_i` for nonempty subsets. Inside `L` put `A=R[√d_1,…,√d_r]` with positive square roots. For each `I` choose the positive norm-one Pell unit `e_I=a_I+b_I√d_I>1`, with `a_I,b_I>0` in the cut, e.g. the least one there. PA proves the usual existence theorem (one may instead assume the units supplied in the cut). No positivity of all conjugates of a general unit is required. Define

```
        H(z) = (λ(σz) : σ∈G),  z∈A*,       Γ⁰[G] = {x ∈ Γ^G : Σσ xσ=0}.
```

**Height criterion.** The following are equivalent:

(i) for all `I≠∅` and all ambient `n`, `W(n;d_I,a_I)`;

(ii) `H(A*) = Γ⁰[G]`.

In particular parameter-free `IE₁⁻` in such a ring forces **the entire augmentation hyperplane** at every multiquadratic system of independent *standard* primes. `IE₁` forces it also for independent nonsquares in the PA cut. This is not just the existence of a PA cut or of abstractly many units at some larger stage. It is a test of the **joint signed sizes of conjugates**, with no PID or finite-support assumption and no assumption on the truth of ES.

Proof. `F₀` is relatively algebraically closed in `K`: clear denominators and multiply an algebraic element by a suitable cut integer so it is integral over `C`, thus integral over `R`, hence in `R`. A polynomial root bound puts the multiple in the convex hull of `F₀`, hence in `C` by endness. It follows that the multiquadratic constant field is linearly disjoint from `K` (characteristic zero, regular extension). The radical monomials form a free `R`-basis of `A`, the sign flips act, and `A^G=R`. The product of all conjugates of a unit belongs to `R*={±1}`. Thus the image always lies in the indicated hyperplane.

For positive `γ∈Γ` take a positive representative and floor it in `R` by Shepherdson. It is above the cut and the floor has the same size. A window at this height puts the positive Pell unit `s=u+√d_I v` in size `γ` since `u≤s≤2u` and its upper bound divided by the height is bounded over the cut. Its conjugates are `s` or `s⁻¹` according to the nontrivial sign character `χ_I`; hence `H(s)=γ χ_I`. Inverses give negative sizes. The sign characters span the augmentation hyperplane over any uniquely divisible group: the coefficient at character `χ` of an arbitrary vector `y` is `2^(−r) Σσ χ(σ)yσ`; orthogonality gives the inverse formula and the constant coefficient is zero. Multiply the corresponding units. This proves (ii).

Conversely, for target `γ` choose a unit `z` with vector `(γ/2^r)χ_I`. The product `s=∏σ σ(z)^{χ_I(σ)}` is fixed by `ker χ_I`, inverted by the other coset, and has size `γ` at the identity. The monomial basis gives `s∈R[√d_I]` (not just in its fraction field). It has quadratic norm one; change its overall sign to be positive. Thus every relative size is obtained by an actual positive integral norm-one unit. To get the **stated numerical window** rather than a cut-sized factor, for `n≥1` obtain `s` of size `λ(2n)` and put `y=2n/s`. This ratio and its inverse are bounded by cut constants. The powers `e_I^j` with *signed cut exponents* bracket every such positive `y` between consecutive powers. In detail for `y≥1` floor it in `R`, getting `m∈C`. In the cut use PA with the ordinary two-coordinate power recurrence to bracket `m` by consecutive powers (compare the quadratic numbers in its ordered real closure). The gap between successive powers on `[1,∞)` is >1 since `a_I≥2`; check also the next power against `y∈[m,m+1)`, requiring at most one adjustment. For `y<1` invert. These powers are computed as pairs **in the cut**, not by exponentiating an ambient pair by a nonstandard index. Multiply `s` by the bracketing upper power (use equality if appropriate), obtaining `s'∈[2n,2e_I n]`. Then `u'=(s'+s'⁻¹)/2` and `v'=(s'−s'⁻¹)/(2√d_I)` are its integral nonnegative coordinates; `v'≤u'`, `n≤u'≤e_I n+1≤(2a_I+1)(n+1)`. Use `(1,0)` at zero. This proves (i). Finally, for numerical `d_I,e_I` the base/step proof of §2 uses no parameters; for cut inputs apply the same proof with parameters. Do not replace a nonstandard `e_I` by a numeral in the parameter-free argument.

**Obstruction in geometric form.** Let `Ē` be the real algebraic closure of `F₀` in `L`. Suppose an upper algebra containing `R` after adjoining `Ē` has relative unit group modulo `Ē*` of finite rational rank `b` (rank means dimension after tensoring with ℚ; take the algebra inside `L`). No such realization can satisfy `IE₁⁻`: the character units at one positive `γ` for `r` independent standard primes give `2^r−1` independent units modulo constants. Indeed if a product is constant, apply the sign flips on the smaller multiquadratic field: the product belongs to `K(√d_i)∩Ē=F₀(√d_i)` by regularity, so all its conjugate sizes vanish. Character orthogonality gives all exponents zero. Standard primes stay independent in `Frac C` by PA (equivalently ordinary divisibility/parity at each fixed prime). More locally (ii) rules out even infinite-rank groups missing one required sign-height vector. For example, an upper algebra contained after real-constant extension in one normal geometrically integral affine finite-type coordinate ring over `Ē` has the finite-rank obstruction: units modulo constants inject via divisors into the finite free group on the codimension-one boundary components of a normal projective compactification. A union of such algebras need **not** have finite unit rank. This is a restriction on bounded unit supply, not a claim that arbitrary towers of tori fail. It explains why installing a tower for just `d=2` cannot by itself certify parameter-free induction.

## 5. Limits and screens

`TS` here means every positive prime `1 mod 4` is a sum of two integral squares. Starting the BCM tower with the simultaneous `QR + TS` PID preserves TS on that entire old interval, but the circle argument of z76 §§1–3 is for a rational-stage algebra whose units are `ℚ*`; it reduces obstruction parameters to rational squareclasses and detects them by ordinary-place rotations. In the punctured upper algebra the units also include the whole preceding field and the axis monomials. A positive old nonstandard prime, for instance, can have zero valuation at every ordinary mark while nonsquare in the old field. The parity-injection argument cannot just be quoted with the same codomain. Moreover new marks are embeddings of the **axis residue field**, and an integral circle point at an old axis must have residues in its prescribed pullback ring. Field points alone do not check that condition. No proof of universal TS in the BCM model from the transfer or the height criterion.

Comparison: `IE1_PUSH2.md` §4 has the single-radical absolute-size necessary condition for `IW₂` and its profinite consequence under an injective ordinary mark. The relative converse with cut exponents and the multiquadratic image test above neither assume that mark nor deduce its injectivity. The flag construction is elementary model theory, not a priority claim for ultrapowers, for the character decomposition, or for Pell theory. F1–F4: the square profile is used only with the existing positive reciprocal descent; there is no claim about ES at ordinary odd squares, the C-even/numerator-eight sub-line, composite avoiders, or the restricted-zero census.
