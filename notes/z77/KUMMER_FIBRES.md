## 1. Fixed c: a polynomial-family barrier with the actual fibre BM exception

Write Y_{n,c}: 4abcd=a+b+cn. A point means a,b,d positive integers with **no** primitivity constraint. For odd n this regular fibre passes its positive-integral BM test iff s(n)∤c, where s denotes squarefree kernel (`Z58_STRUCT2.md`, Prop F). Here is an extension of the fixed-c=1 polynomial theorem in `Z60_MS105.md` §4; one must force **c | C** in the solution at input cn. The old c=1 theorem at cn does not do that.

**Theorem.** Fix c≥1 and a nonconstant f∈ℤ[T] of positive leading coefficient which takes odd values. Exactly one of the following applies:

1. f=s g² for positive squarefree s|c and g∈ℚ[T]. Every positive odd f(t) then fails the BM test at c.
2. At a density-one set of positive integers t, Y_{f(t),c} has a positive integral point, even with **d even**. This remains true in each fixed progression of parameters.

Consequently, for any bounded positive-integer selector c(t), even chosen adversarially, the parameters t with f(t) positive odd and Y_{f(t),c(t)} **non-BM sterile** have density zero. In particular there is no everywhere-sterile non-BM bounded-height family along all parameters of a nonconstant polynomial with positive odd values. This does **not** settle a sparse sequence of t, or even one prescribed unbounded height c(t).

*Proof.* A rational-polynomial square factorization of f up to a constant can be written f=s g² with s squarefree positive. If s were even, then all nonzero integer values would have odd 2-adic valuation, so none would be odd. At any positive odd value the squarefree kernel equals s, regardless of the denominator convention for g. This gives case 1. In case 2 we have the following uniform non-inclusions. Put m=8c, u an indeterminate, α a root of cf(T)+u, and K=ℚ(u)(α)=ℚ(α), a rational function field (the polynomial is irreducible over ℚ(u) by linearity in u and Gauss). No nontrivial constant quadratic field lies in K. For every quadratic squareclass D of ℚ(ζ_m) (and D=1), we have √(−Du)∉K: otherwise Dc f(T) is a square in ℚ(T), hence a polynomial square in ℚ[T]. This is impossible for f not a constant times a square. In the remaining case f=s g² of case 2, some **odd** prime dividing s does not divide c; it is unramified in ℚ(ζ_m), hence has even valuation in D and odd valuation in Dcs. This proves the assertion even if 2 | c. This unramified-prime step is where non-BM rather than just nonsquare enters.

Apply Hilbert irreducibility with the quantitative thin-set bound of S. D. Cohen as in `Z60_MS105.md` Thm 3, now to the normal closure of K(ζ_m,√−u) over ℚ(u). Outside the thin exceptional set the specialised Galois group and the subgroups for the root and each quadratic field are identified up to conjugacy. Thus the field K_q of a root of cf(T)+q contains neither a quadratic subfield of ℚ(ζ_m) nor any √(−Dq) as above. The finitely many branch/bad values and O_{f,c}(√X log X) exceptional positive integers ≤X cannot exhaust the primes q≡3 mod 4, q∤m. Fix one such good q. Since (q,m)=1 and −q is the unique quadratic class contributed by ℚ(ζ_q), this excludes **all** quadratic subfields of ℚ(ζ_{mq}) from K_q. Consequently F=K_q∩ℚ(ζ_{mq}) is abelian of odd degree (a finite even-order abelian group has a quadratic quotient). In particular F is real. This deals also with large cyclic 2-power factors from c: merely excluding imaginary quadratic fields would not give this argument for arbitrary c.

In the normal closure of K_q(ζ_{mq}), lift complex conjugation on ℚ(ζ_{mq}) to an element fixing K_q, possible because it fixes F. Chebotarev gives a positive-density set of good primes ℓ≡−1 mod mq with cf(T)+q having a root t_ℓ modulo ℓ (discard ramified primes, the leading coefficient and discriminant). For t≡t_ℓ mod ℓ with f(t) sufficiently large, take

    a=q,  b=(cf(t)+q)/ℓ,  d=(ℓ+1)/(4cq).

Then d is even, (4acd−1)b=cf(t)+a, and all coordinates are positive. The divergent sum of 1/ℓ and the finite CRT product ∏(1−1/ℓ) give the asserted density, as in MS105. Affine substitution preserves each case (the inverse is rational affine); a finite union over heights proves the selector statement. ∎

**Collapse/descent check.** More generally if n=c^j s z² is odd, its kernel outside c is exactly the part of s outside c (s squarefree). If that part is 1, the proposed square-kernel mechanism already fails BM, even for arbitrarily large j. If c is fixed and that part is nontrivial, case 2 applied in z instead supplies points almost always. For growing c, neither claim gives a non-BM sterile family. The trial n=c^k+1 is not sterile for all even heights at k=3,4,5: respective (c;a,b,d)=(22;6,148,3),(2;1,5,1),(2;3,3,1). The parameter reflection at k=2 in `notes/z76/DISPROOF_DEEP.md` §1 uses a quadratic in c; it does not take integral complementary roots in general degree k. These counterexamples do not say anything about eventual or sparse subfamilies.

## 2. Pell/exponential locking is possible, but not a zero certificate

For K=4,8 use n=KaBh−a−B (K=8 is the even-C part of R, C=2h). For D≡−1 mod K let r=(−n mod D)∈[0,D−1]. A D-hit is precisely r>0 and Kr | D+1, since a=r, h=(D+1)/(Kr), B=(n+r)/D; conversely a=(D+1)/(Kh)<D. All hits have gcd(D,n)=1. This criterion does not test gcd(a,B)=1. Odd squares have no hits (T1).

Consider three explicit odd composite sequences at positive indices t:

* Exponential fixed kernel: E_t=752257·11^{2t}, where 752257=6217·11² is a known nonsquare R-zero coprime to 105.
* Pell fixed kernel: P_t=4545 x_t², x_t+y_t√2=(3+2√2)^t. This multiplies the actual non-BM failure at 4545 by an odd square; it is not a polynomial in t.
* Moving positive control: G_t=g(256g−33), g=2·3^t−3. At t≥1, g≥3 is odd, and (16g−2)²<G_t<(16g−1)². **Every** G_t has an R₈ witness (a,B,h)=(g,32g,1). It is imprimitive with gcd(a,B)=g. Indeed the primitive point (1,32,g) of G_t/g scales to this point; treating only primitive witnesses loses this proof. We do not claim the point is the only one. At the nonpositive seed g=−1 (index 0), the same parametrization gives a signed point over the **positive odd square** 289, not a positive point.

**Proposition.** For every bound H≥2, in each of these three sequences an infinite arithmetic progression of indices has no D-hit for D≤H for either K. For the moving positive control this gives infinitely many actual finite least-hit moduli D_min for R and R₈, both ≫ log log G_t along a common subsequence. Thus forbidding every fixed bounded scan, even on a single exponential sequence of nonsquare composites, need not produce a single zero.

*Proof.* For each p^e≤H take the order p^{4(e−1)}(p²−1)(p²−p) of GL₂(ℤ/p^e). The field formula counts independent columns; passage up one power multiplies the order by p⁴. Let T_H be the lcm of these orders. Every invertible 2×2 matrix modulo D≤H raised to T_H is the identity by CRT and Lagrange. This also applies to scalar units. At positive t≡0 mod T_H the Pell matrix for multiplication by 3+2√2 (determinant 1) therefore returns to identity modulo every D≤H. Thus P_t≡4545. Likewise E_t≡752257 for (D,11)=1 and G_t≡289 for (D,3)=1. The omitted D cannot be hits: when 11 | D for E_t, or 3 | D for G_t, one has gcd(D,n)>1. The three seed inputs have no hits for any D. In particular an arbitrarily large bound does not obstruct locking. For size, T_H divides lcm(1,…,⌊H⌋+1)^7: bound p^{4e−3},(p−1)²,p+1 separately. Hence log T_H≪H by the Chebyshev bound. At t=T_H, log log G_t=log t+O(1)≪H; these indices are unbounded. The explicit point makes the positive control's least moduli finite. ∎

**A stronger hard-class positive control (direct primorial indexing).** In the same identity set g=8∏_{p≤H}p−1 for H≥7. Then g is odd, n=g(256g−33)≡289 mod 840 is in 𝔐, has the displayed nonprimitive R₈ witness, and is a nonsquare composite. Every prime p≤H sees the square 289 modulo p. Any hit D satisfies (n/D)=(−a/D)=−1: D≡−1 mod 4a gives (a/D)=1 by reciprocity (`Z60_MS105.md` Claim 0). Thus **every** witness modulus, not just the one we supplied, has a prime factor >H≫log n. This is a construction of late *positive* inputs coprime to 105, not a new general bound for pseudosquares.
