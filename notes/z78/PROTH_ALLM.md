### 1. A programmable ladder of binomial ratios

**Theorem 1 (PROVED).** Let h≥5 be odd, (3,h)=1, and suppose 2^b≡−1 mod h for some b>0. Set m=(h+1)/2, H=2^h−1, k=2H+1, u=2H. There exists an effectively computable P=P(h)>0 such that **every** positive s divisible by P gives an interior good word, and hence ES, at n=k·2^s+1. Examples with unbounded h: h=2^(2i)+1, i≥1. Each of these *restricted* families already admits no finite ET/divisor covering, even eventually.

Proof. Write y_a=1+2^{a mod h} in Z/H. All are units: any odd prime common to H and 1+2^a would give −1 in the odd-order group generated by 2 at that prime. For any unit source exponent j mod h and any finite list of ratios y_v/y_w with **both indices units mod h**, we can realize their product as a single product of DISTINCT cyclotomic factors of X^E+1, at X≡2^j mod H, for some positive odd E. Start at e=1. For a desired ratio advance e to an odd multiple e' with j e'≡w mod h (ignore this step's factors); advance to an odd multiple e'' of e' with j e''≡v mod h. Both multipliers can be chosen positive odd and prime to h because e and the indices are units. Take ALL factors of (X^{e''}+1)/(X^{e'}+1) and continue from e=e''. This quotient has precisely the factors Φ_{2d}(X) for d|e'', d∤e'. No factor is reused. Modulo H the quotient is the prescribed ratio. Dummy or equal endpoints cause no problem.

The key to leaving the ratio subgroup's apparent degree-zero restriction is the **integer evaluation** y_3/y_1=9/3=3=y_1 (h≥5); also y_1/y_{−1}=2. Thus each y_a with unit a is itself represented by two ratios, (y_a/y_1)(y_3/y_1). Telescoping now gives

    2 ∏_{i=0}^{b−1} y_{2^i} = 2(2^(2^b)−1) ≡ −1 mod H.

Use the ladder for this list, once for each unit j, with final degree E_j. Let L=lcm_j E_j and P=4L. For s a positive multiple of P strip from s/L all prime factors dividing h, leaving r; r is a multiple of 4, and j=r mod h is a unit. X=2^r satisfies X^L+1 | 2^s+1 since s/(rL) is odd. All the selected factors divide X^L+1 jointly. Their product A equals −1 mod H and 1 mod 16 (constant terms 1), i.e. A≡7u−1 mod 8u by CRT. This uses u≡14 mod 16. Therefore t=(A+1)/u≡7 mod 8 and positive, and v=(n+u)/A is integral since n+u=k(2^s+1); v≡7 mod 8 and v≥2. The word n=tuv−u−v is good (8|n+t). Directly, D=(tv−1)/4 gives ES denominators ADn, uD, AuD: multiplying through by AuDn gives 4AuD=u+(A+1)n. For any menu modulus take arbitrarily large s divisible also by every unit order of 2 at the odd part: n is 4^m there and 1 at the 2-part, a square unit at both. Apply EXPONENT_COVERING Thm 1.4 (square-unit part). ∎

**Independent result and density refinement (PROVED rel. Mertens in progressions).** Lane 6 independently found this group identity and a different factor supply in `450d0e304:notes/z78/WILD_ES.md` §1 (sibling lane; not a second all-exponent result), with the stronger density-one assertion, even within any fixed exponent progression: take sufficiently many distinct prime divisors of s in each unit class mod h, supplying ratios Φ_{2ℓ}(X)=(1+X^ℓ)/(1+X) and leaving a spare prime ≥5 to ensure r≥4. See its Corollary A.1 for the CRT/variance proof. This does NOT settle every exponent.

**Assessment.** This uses the specialization X↦2 and is not claimed as an identity of abstract cyclotomic units. P is not necessarily practical: the reviewer's literal ladders at h=11,17 give L with 23 and 27 decimal digits. In particular the theorem does not say that the complement of 0 mod P is finitely coverable, much less exhibit such a cover.

### 2. Cubic families within the square classes

**Theorem 2 (PROVED rel. quadratic reciprocity and the square-unit obstruction).** Let h=7^i with i≥1, H=2^h−1, m=(h+1)/2, k=4^m−1. There is an explicit nonempty progression of positive y for which every n=ky³+1 lies in the single hard class 1 mod 840, has an interior good word, and gives ES. For each such k these n have order of growth Θ_k(N^{1/3}) up to N and admit no finite ET/fixed-divisor cover, even eventually. The progression can be taken as y≡0 mod 1680 and y≡z mod H, with z ANY root of z²−z+2=0 mod H.

Proof. For p|H the order of 2 mod p is a nontrivial power of 7, so p≡1 mod 7, p∤1680, and (−7/p)=(p/7)=1. The two distinct roots lift at all powers of p, and CRT gives z. Let u=2H, A=y²−y+1. Then A|y³+1, A≡−1 mod H and A≡1 mod 16. The same congruences as in §1 give the good word and direct triple. The counting assertion follows by monotonicity on an arithmetic progression. For unit-square closure use the profinite y with p-component z (any integral lift of the selected residue) at p|H and zero at every other prime. Mod p|H we have k≡1 and 1+z³≡(z−1)²≠0 (z=1 would imply 2=0). Thus n is a square unit in Z_p by Hensel; elsewhere n=1. This y respects the progression; arbitrarily large representatives match it at any finite modulus by CRT. The square-unit criterion applies. No prime-value assertion. The ES denominators are polynomial in y on its progression (v=k(y+1)), not polynomial in n. Noncovering here concerns fixed congruence classes of n; it is not a lower bound on the difficulty of solving an individual instance. ∎

### 4. Exact computable core criterion, no hypothesis on the doubling orbit

**Theorem 4 (PROVED rel. Dirichlet).** Fix any odd h≥3, H=2^h−1, B_a=1+2^a (indices mod h). Let R be the subgroup of the unit group mod H generated by B_a/B_b with gcd(a,h)=gcd(b,h). Define E_d=Φ_{2d}(2) mod H. At unit source j=1, the set of attainable residues of products of distinct Φ_{2d}(2) (arbitrary odd degree) is **exactly**

    R · { ∏_{d∈S} E_d : S ⊆ {d:d|h} }.

At every other unit j it is the same set (actual witnesses may differ). Thus checking finitely many subsets, c|k with c≡7u−1 mod 16, and subgroup membership of −1/(c∏E_d) decides the z77 congruence for unit sources after stripping h-primes, c|k, and products of distinct Φ_{2d} of arbitrary odd degree; this says nothing about other divisors of 2^s+1. A positive decision gives an effectively computable common period. A negative decision would exclude only THIS congruence recipe.

Proof. All B_a are units by odd order. In the quotient by R they depend only on gcd(a,h). If d∤h choose a prime p with v_p(d)>v_p(h). Write d=p^a b, p∤b. In the Möbius product for Φ_{2d}(2^j), terms pair as (B_{j p^a e}/B_{j p^{a−1}e})^{μ(b/e)}, e|b. The paired indices have identical gcd with h, so the class is trivial. For d|h and unit j the same Möbius formula gives exactly the class of E_d, since multiplying an index by j preserves its gcd. This proves necessity even with arbitrary repeated primes in the degree. For sufficiency first take the desired exceptional factors, once each. For g|h, the ratios B_{j g ℓ}/B_{jg} as ℓ ranges over reduced classes mod h cover every numerator in the gcd-g orbit: reduction from units mod h onto units mod h/g is surjective. Each ratio is the evaluated quotient (X^{gℓ}+1)/(X^g+1) at X=2^j with ℓ a fresh odd prime in its class, ℓ∤h. Its constituents are exactly Φ_{2eℓ} for e|g; they neither repeat exceptional factors nor overlap the constituents for any other fresh prime. These generators give all of R; negative powers may be replaced by nonnegative ones modulo the order. Dirichlet supplies all fresh primes. Use an odd common multiple of all chosen indices, across all j, then strip the h-part as in §1. This proves realizability, not just membership in the abstract group generated by all factors. ∎

### 5. What fails off the core; odd offsets

**Proposition 5 (PROVED, scoped obstruction).** Let B be a fixed positive odd integer and h≥3 odd with 2^B+1<2^h−1. At infinitely many s=B·2^v the unshifted z77 cyclotomic construction does not even achieve A≡−1 mod H, H=2^h−1, for ANY c|k and ANY permitted odd degree. Take v a large multiple of ord_h(2). If X=2^r, X^e+1|2^s+1 via s/(re) odd integral, then r=2^v b, be|B. Every Φ_{2d}(X), d|e, reduces mod H to Φ_{2d}(2^b). Their subproduct F at this latter integer is a positive odd divisor of 2^B+1, strictly below H. If cF≡−1, multiplying by C=k/c gives F+C≡0 mod H (k≡1). Since F+C is even and H odd, C≥2H−F>H. This forces c=1: every proper divisor of the odd k is at most k/3<H. Then F≡−1 is impossible: F<H and F is odd. In particular a FIXED core period P for all sufficiently large h cannot work by this method: choose B equal to its odd part. This does not preclude ES on a single one of these rows, and does not obstruct the h-dependent periods in §1.

If v₂(A)=1 then 4|v and t odd. If v₂(A)=2 then 2|v and t≡3 mod 4. If 8|A then t≡7 mod 8. In every case 8|(n+t)tuv. For ES invoke the good-word theorem at n≡1 mod 4. For general a=1,2 use the exact criterion ν₂(n+t)+ν₂(t)+a−ν₂(A)≥3 (u is odd). Merely choosing a≡s mod L does NOT make this a cover: the effective period depends on the resulting u.

    c · 3^|T0| · ∏_{a∈(Z/h)^×} (B_a/B_1)^{e_a} = −1 (mod H).

### 7. A divisor of the multiplier pays for a negative doubling orbit

**Theorem 8 (PROVED, density rel. Mertens in progressions).** Suppose h≥5 odd, 3∤h. If **some divisor a≥4 of h+1** satisfies −a∈⟨2 mod h⟩, then for m=(h+1)/2 there is an effective positive good-word progression with square-unit closure, and good words on a set of density one even relative to any fixed exponent progression. In particular take every h=2^{12t+6}+13, t≥0. These h are outside §1, not merely reparametrizations of its hypothesis.

Proof. Choose 2^b≡−a mod h, b≥0. The positive integer d=2^a−1 divides k, and c=k/d≡1 mod 16 since k≡d≡−1 mod 16. Modulo H we have k≡1, so the target −1/c is 1−2^a. But

    2^a ∏_{i=0}^{b−1} B_{2^i} = 2^a (2^(2^b)−1) ≡ 1−2^a (mod H).

Every factor on the left belongs to the subgroup generated by unit-index ratios by §1 (including 2=B₁/B₋₁). This is an actual unit-ratio product, not a test with just one quadratic character. Use the disjoint factors, period and density argument of §§1,6 with prefactor c. The closure argument of §1 is unchanged. For the example, 2⁶≡−1 mod 13, so 13|h+1. Also h≡2 mod 3 and 7|h since 6|12t+6; −1 is not a power of 2 mod 7. Use a=13. Taking a=h+1 recovers §1 when its doubling condition holds. ∎

### 8. Initial-segment size obstruction, allowing arbitrary shifted divisors

**Theorem 9 (PROVED).** Write n=k2^s+1, k=4^m−1. No interior word n=tuv−u−v with the outer letter u=k2^a−1 (including a=0), for ANY 0≤a≤s, exists whenever u>2(2^{s−a}+1). In particular ALL such shifts and ALL divisor choices fail simultaneously for m≥2, 0≤s≤m. No complexity bound on the rule choosing a or on the degree of a moving divisor.

Proof. Put L=2^{s−a}, so n=L(u+1)+1. The word equation forces

    (tv−1−L)u=L+1+v,     v=(L+1)(u+1)/(tu−1)≤L+1

since t,u≥2. The right-hand side in the first equality is a positive multiple of u, but at most 2(L+1). For s≤m and m≥2, u≥4^m−2>2(2^m+1). ∎

**Corollary 9.1 (PROVED; lower bound along an explicit sequence of m).** Fix any B≥1. Consider certificates for all sparse exponents s=B·2^v (v≥1) using (i) arbitrary direct solutions below a cutoff S, (ii) any fixed congruence classes of n, of modulus at most R, each avoiding square units modulo its modulus (e.g. the ET and fixed-divisor menu), and (iii) arbitrary shifted-divisor words exactly as in Theorem 9. Rules, divisors and offsets can depend on m, and the number of rules is unrestricted. Let R≥2 and let m be a multiple of lcm(1,…,R) with m≥max(2B,2⌈log₂ R⌉,2). Such a certificate requires S>m/2, or else a fixed modulus exceeding R. Thus bounded cutoff and bounded fixed moduli cannot work for **all sufficiently large m**, even on this one fixed sparse family. More quantitatively at m_R=lcm(1,…,R) for all sufficiently large R with m_R≥max(2B,2⌈log₂ R⌉,2), either the cutoff exceeds m_R/2 or the largest fixed modulus exceeds R=(1+o(1))log m_R (the asymptotic only is rel. PNT). This does not demand monotone complexity in m, rule out higher-degree words with OTHER outer letters, or apply to an arbitrary m-dependent eventual threshold without charging its size.

Proof. Choose the largest s=B2^v≤m, so s>m/2. For every odd q≤R its order ord_q(2)≤R divides m (omit q=1); hence k≡0 mod q. At every power of 2 at most R, n≡1 as s≥log₂ R. Thus n≡1 modulo every menu modulus, excluding (ii); this congruence works at every s≥log₂ R, not specifically sparse s. All (iii) fail by Theorem 9 on this initial segment. The only sparsity input is a term in (m/2,m]. Only (i) remains. Use log lcm(1,…,R)=ψ(R) for the asymptotic. ∎
