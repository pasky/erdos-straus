## 2. Exact scope claimed in the manuscript

- Main stated setting: **all number fields F and all continuous unitary Hecke characters** (Introduction §1.2, PDF pp. 12–16; §1.6 and the letter excerpts §§1.7–1.8, pp. 33–56). The desired conclusion is that nontrivial zeros of the completed Hecke L-function lie on the central line Re(s)=1/2. In the introduction the claimed route is the trace formula and its deduction that the shifted finite logarithmic derivative is holomorphic in the right half-plane, apart from the stated polar exception, which then excludes off-line zeros of the completed L-function (PDF pp. 29–31, especially §1.4.5).
- The actual trace formula is stated as Theorem 12.5.1.1, PDF pp. 519–520. The subsequent zero-location theorem is not fully stated in this draft: Theorem 13.3.1.2 is an unfilled placeholder at p. 522. Section 14.1.2 states a related multiplicity bound for noncentral nontrivial zeros of Hecke L-functions over F, at most `[F:Q]`; the central zero is expressly not bounded by this argument (pp. 528–529). However, §1.7.3 (pp. 37–38) says the multiplicity analysis is not fully worked out, is reserved for a sequel, and its details are only sketched. Treat that multiplicity discussion as an announced expectation, not a completed by-product. The manuscript itself distinguishes this from zero-location.
- Thus the advertised scope includes Dirichlet L-functions (F=Q), Dedekind zeta as the trivial Hecke character, and degree-one Hecke L-functions over general number fields. It is **not** a theorem for arbitrary Artin L-functions or arbitrary automorphic GL_n L-functions. Artin-Weil L-functions are discussed as context/complements (§14.1.1); the manuscript invokes conjectural Langlands/Artin-Weil properties, not a general proven Artin-GRH conclusion. The higher-rank discussion §14.3, p. 536, says the analogous proposed method would give a generic-zero multiplicity bound `n[F:Q]`; it does not announce GRH for all GL_n. In §14.1.2 the author separately labels GRH/GSH for cuspidal GL_n as conjectures (pp. 529–530).
- Effective/quantitative: no effective zero-free-region constants, explicit zero-density theorem, explicit least-prime bound, or effective threshold is stated as a by-product of the central claim. The trace formula is an identity for test functions; the announcement does not provide usable explicit error terms of the kind needed for effective GRH consequences. It does not announce Siegel-zero exclusion as a separate theorem, Elliott–Halberstam/Montgomery-type distribution, character-sum estimates, least quadratic nonresidue bounds, Linnik bounds, effective Chebotarev, small-prime class-group generation, or new explicit `L(1,χ)` bounds. Standard consequences one could derive from GRH are not separately established quantitatively in this draft.
- The author claims a multiplicity bound, not nonvanishing or a positive arithmetic counting lower bound. The manuscript specifically says the central zero can remain mysterious (p. 529).

## 3. Method architecture (≤40 lines)

1. **Adelic/idele harmonic analysis:** global and S-local theta functions, idelic Mellin transforms, and Hecke L-functions (Chs. 2–3; §§1.1–1.2, pp. 11–16).
2. **Topological functional analysis:** new Hardy–Sobolev completions and orbital Schauder bases for theta-function spaces; divisor exact sequences encode zero divisors as evaluation/derivative functionals (Chs. 4–9; §1.2, pp. 12–21).
3. **Trace construction:** a nuclear convolution operator has a spectral trace expressed through zeros; split its geometric kernel into orbital pieces indexed by finite places and compute the non-S-local pieces as zero, reducing to an S-local trace calculation (Introduction §§1.2–1.3, pp. 14–16; spectral trace Ch. 10, geometric Chs. 11–12).
4. **Arithmetic/geometric side:** an adelic Fourier transform and local calculations yield Theorem 12.5.1.1 (pp. 519–520). The exact half-line Sobolev weight is said to force the `1/2` in the critical line (§1.2.3, p. 16; §1.4.8, pp. 31–32).
5. **Zero-location deduction:** approximate a holomorphic family of test functions, recover `L'/L`, then use holomorphy and the functional equation to exclude zeros off the central line (§1.4.3–1.4.5, pp. 29–31).
6. **Key announced input:** the trace formula valid on test functions vanishing on the norm-one subgroup, plus the claimed continuity/nuclearity/orbital-basis and kernel-deconstruction machinery. It is explicitly *not* Weil positivity: §1.4.6 (pp. 30–31) says Weil’s positivity criterion is not used. Hence no advertised “positivity ⇒ a nonzero atom/count” mechanism.
7. **Caveat from the manuscript itself:** the corresponding function-field trace method is said not to constrain zeros; overlapping Euler-factor poles obstruct the Sobolev trace construction (§14.2, pp. 534–535).
