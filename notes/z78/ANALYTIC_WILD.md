### 1. A lower bound for the *signed* dispersion norm (PROVED)

Use z76/z77 notation: an interval of integers $W<C\le2W$, nonnegative smooth $\Phi$ supported in $[1/2,5]$ and one on $[1,4]$, $D=V/W$, primes $P<q\le2P$. In this section assume $2P\le W\le n$, $D\to\infty$, $10D\le P$, $\log P\gg\log n$ (fixed positive implied constant). The detector diagonal in the original argument requires $P\ge V n^{-\theta-o(1)}$. We compare with normalized *signed* Cauchy, not a sum of absolute values. For local real rows $u_q(c,k)$ periodic mod $q$ in both variables put $v_q=u_q-q^{-1}\sum_{k'\bmod q}u_q(c,k')$. Write

$$A(C)=\mathop{\rm avg}_{q<r}\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\Phi((4Ck-n)/V) v_q(C,k)v_r(C,k).$$

Poisson in $k$ identifies this **exactly** with the prime-pair average of all primitive frequencies in z77 §1, with the mock rows in place of Legendre rows: centering removes precisely the modes divisible by either prime, before any inequalities. The two local frequencies are $h/r\pmod q$ and $h/q\pmod r$. In particular a single row table per prime must serve *all* pairs and *all* frequencies. The signed Cauchy majorant for the primitive part is $\sqrt{\#\{C\}\sum_C |A(C)|^2}$; the squared norm includes all three types of products, with all their signs.

**Theorem 1.** For every such set of parameters there are shared real row tables with $|u_q|\le1$, unnormalized nonzero complete Fourier coefficients $O(\sqrt q)$ with an absolute constant, and **exactly the genuine zero-mode values** of z76 §2, for which $\sum_C |A(C)|^2\gg P D^2$. Thus this signed Cauchy majorant itself is $\gg D\sqrt{PW}$; the construction does not assume or count resonant rational frequencies. At $\alpha=\gamma=\theta$, $\beta=1-2\theta$, $\rho=1-3\theta$, taking $P\asymp D$ with a sufficiently large constant, its exponent is $(3\rho+\theta)/2$. It exceeds $\theta$ for $1/4<\theta<3/10$ and equals it at $3/10$. (The range ensures $P=o(W)$.) Logarithmic enlargements of $P$ do not change exponents. The nearby strict interior $\alpha=\gamma>\theta$ gives the same obstruction to a strictly sub-3/10 target by continuity. There is no barrier here **above** 3/10, or to *attaining* 3/10 with epsilons.

**Proof.** Build a real sign sequence with all prefix polynomials of length $m$ bounded by $O(\sqrt m)$ everywhere on the unit circle. The Rudin–Shapiro recursion starts with $F_0=G_0=1$ and takes $F_{j+1}=F_j+z^{2^j}G_j$, $G_{j+1}=F_j-z^{2^j}G_j$. The sum of squared moduli doubles on $|z|=1$. The coefficients of the nested $F_j$ define the sequence. Every aligned dyadic block of length $2^j$ in that sequence is a signed copy of $F_j$ or $G_j$, by descent. Decomposing a prefix in binary and summing the geometric series proves the bound, with no logarithmic loss.

Choose any consecutive patch of $\lfloor P/2\rfloor$ values of $C$. For each prime its residues are distinct there. At $c=C\bmod q$ in that patch translate the cyclic row so that position zero corresponds to $k_0(C)=\lfloor n/(4C)\rfloor$. For ordinary $c$ the new row in cyclic order consists of the first $(q-1)/2$ signs, their termwise negatives in the same order, and a final $-1$. Hence its sum is $-1$, its nonzero transforms are $O(\sqrt q)$ by the prefix bound, and the translation changes only phases. Off the patch ordinary rows can use the same pattern with origin zero. At $c=0$ use the genuine affine Legendre row $( (n^2-4k)/q)$ (mean zero, Fourier modulus $\sqrt q$ at nonzero modes by the quadratic Gauss identity). At $c\ne0$, $4cn+1=0\pmod q$, use the genuine double-root row (one zero, all other entries $+1$). This also checks the rare large DC values; we did not hide them in a mean-square hypothesis.

For each patch $C$ all contributing $k$ have $0\le k-k_0\le1+5D/4 <(q-1)/2$ for large $P$. Thus *every* ordinary prime uses exactly the same real sign at that $k$. There are only $O(1)$ other primes, as they divide $C(4Cn+1)$ (here $W\le n$, as on the ES blocks). For two ordinary primes the product of centered values is $1+O(1/P)$; all other products have magnitude at most four. By the dyadic prime number theorem the average is $1-o(1)$ uniformly on the patch. The bump sum is $\asymp D$ since its plateau has $\gg D$ integers. Consequently $A(C)\gg D$ throughout the patch, giving the stated lower bound by **positivity of the squared norm** off the patch, not by discarding signed terms in its expansion. ∎

**Scope.** This is not z77's $P W^{3/4}$ majorant. It rules out a bound below 3/10 obtained by keeping the *signed* norm but using only the indicated row-size, complete Fourier and factorisation information. The fake rows depend on $n$ (actual rows do too); they need not be traces of bounded conductor sheaves. Genuine complete *mixed correlations in $c$* or a bound before the outer Cauchy remain possible. In particular this is not a no-go for Deligne or for the actual $R(n)$, and it says nothing about a lower tail.

### 2. Adaptive alphabets in the cap argument: sharp supply and a minimax floor (PROVED)

Let $1<R\le C_0 X$, $q\le X$ and $K=(R+q)/4$ integral. Give the selector every positive integer window, not just primes or those with a prescribed quadratic sign, and allow it to inspect **all** factors of $K$. If it insists on at least $k$ distinct primes of $K$, the population $M$ satisfies

$$ M\le C_1 X\,(\log\log X+O(1))^k/k!. $$

Indeed $K\le C_1 X$, the map $q\mapsto K$ is injective, and each retained $K$ has some squarefree $d\mid K$, $\omega(d)=k$. Drop the shifted interval and bound the number of multiples up to $C_1 X$ by $C_1 X/d$, with **no per-modulus rounding error**. Expanding an elementary symmetric sum gives the display, using Mertens' prime harmonic estimate $\sum_{s\le y}1/s=\log\log y+O(1)$. No small-product cut, primality, or preassigned alphabet is needed. Write $u=\log X$. In particular for fixed $\eta>0$, $u^\eta\le k\le u$, one has $\log(X/M)\ge(1-o_\eta(1))k\log k$ (infinity if $M=0$). This completes the adaptive high-product range of the z75 BT objection when $R\ll X$. It is an upper bound on the number of **windows**, not on the number of labelled alphabets.

The coefficient one cannot be increased in this integer-window statement at its binding rank. Take $k\asymp\sqrt{u/\log u}$ and the product $D_k$ of the first $k$ odd primes. Whenever $q\equiv -R\pmod {4D_k}$, $D_k\mid K$, and there are $X/(4D_k)+O(1)$ such $1\le q\le X$. By the ordinary prime number theorem $\log D_k=(1+o(1)) k\log k$. This lower example, for *every* shift here, gives $\log(X/M)\le(1+o(1)) k\log k$ for the unrestricted integer selector. It does not give free carriers, good lattices or any admissible prime at all.

**Certificate comparison.** Formalize the z75 short-relation deletion + Minkowski-II certificate in its *optimistic*, constant-free budget. With $k\ge2$ inputs, height $X$, and $M\le X$ eligible windows it certifies absence up to a threshold $B$ by charging $B^{k+1}\le M$, and pays $X/B^{k-1}$ for diameter. These are the monomials in z75 §4 (its constants/logarithms worsen this budget). Even grant a **single** deletion charge for an adaptive choice among all alphabets: this is only a comparison of numerical certificates, not permission to use z75's union bound for changing generators. The best log cost in this comparison is at least

$$ {2u\over k+1}+{k-1\over k+1}\log(X/M). $$

If $k<u^{1/3}$ the first term alone dominates $\sqrt{u\log u}$. Otherwise the supply inequality applies (no window has more than $O(u)$ distinct primes), and $k\log k$ dominates that scale if $k\gg\sqrt{u/\log u}$. At $k\asymp\sqrt{u/\log u}$ write $k=b\sqrt{u/\log u}$; the normalized cost is at least $2/b+b/2-o(1)\ge 2-o(1)$, equality at $b=2$. For $k=o(\sqrt{u/\log u})$ the first term again dominates. Thus the **optimistic certificate** has floor $(2-o(1))\sqrt{u\log u}$, attained to leading order in that numerical model by the integer selector above and $B=M^{1/(k+1)}$. This does *not* bound $e^*$ from below, or prove attainable constants for genuine prime windows. It isolates a deletion/diameter bottleneck, not a least-prime exponent. Changing $X=R^A$ to another fixed power $A\ge1$ cannot change this order. For the Dirichlet-GRH argument at $X=(\log R)^8$ the shifted interval cannot be dropped at cost $O(X)$: the single-divisibility argument does not apply. For z75's *actual square-divisibility* there is a different argument.

### 3. Sharp window supply at GRH height (PROVED, unconditionally)

Fix $A>0$; $p\in(T,2T]$ prime, $X=(\log T)^A$, $u=\log X$. Let $M_2(p,k)$ count **integer** windows $1\le q\le X$ with $K=(p+q)/4$ integral and at least $k$ distinct odd primes whose squares divide $K$. No restriction on the size of any square divisor. Prime windows, or *exactly* valuation two at each of $k$ places, are subsets. Uniformly for integers $u^{1/3}\le k\le 2u$, outside a **single** exceptional set of relative density $o(1)$ among these primes, one has

$$ M_2(p,k)\le X\exp(-2k\log(k\log k)+O(k)). \tag{3} $$

This also holds relatively almost everywhere among $p\equiv1\pmod8$ by the fixed-modulus PNT. In the binding range $k\asymp\sqrt{u/\log u}$, (3) gives the true logarithmic supply loss up to additive $O(k)$ for integer windows: for **every** prime input the progression $q\equiv-p\pmod {4D_k^2}$ has $X/(4D_k^2)+O(1)$ members, and $\log D_k=k\log(k\log k)+O(k)$ by $p_j\asymp j\log(2j)$. A second consequence is an actual maximal-rank law: among all integer windows in this short interval, the maximum number of distinct odd prime squares dividing $K$ is $(1+o(1))u/(2\log u)$ for relatively almost all prime inputs, by rounding $k=(1\pm\delta)u/(2\log u)$, then taking any fixed $\delta>0$. This is a statement about factorisation in short intervals, not about ES or correction success.

**Proof.** Fix $q$. Sum over squarefree odd $d$ with $\omega(d)=k$, $4d^2\mid p+q$. Necessarily $d\le\sqrt T$ for large $T$, and $(q,4d^2)=1$ whenever the progression contains an odd prime $p>T$ (such a $q$ is odd and no prime $>T$ divides $d$). For $d\le T^{1/8}$ use the classical Brun–Titchmarsh inequality $\pi(y;m,a)\le 2y/(\varphi(m)\log(y/m))$ for $(a,m)=1$, $1\le m<y$ (Montgomery–Vaughan); $y=2T$, $m=4d^2$, so the count is $\ll T/(\varphi(d^2)\log T)$. For $T^{1/8}<d\le\sqrt T$ use integer counting, $O(T/d^2+1)=O(T/d^2)$, and sum over *all* these $d$ to get $O(T^{7/8})$. The cost of this tail divided by the number of primes is $O(T^{-1/8}\log T)$, negligible compared with $\exp(-2k\log(k\log k))$ uniformly in the stated range. This cut is made in the **varying prime input**, not at the much smaller window length $X$.

For $z\ge3$, positivity gives

$$ \sum_{\omega(d)=k}^{d\ {\rm squarefree}} 1/\varphi(d^2)\le z^{-2k}\prod_s(1+z^2/(s(s-1)))\le z^{-2k}\exp(O(z/\log z)) $$

where $s$ runs over primes. For $s\le z$ sum $O(1+\log(z/s))$; this is $O(z/\log z)$ using $\pi(y)\ll y/\log(2y)$ and partial summation. For $s>z$ use $O(z^2/s^2)$ and dyadic summation with the same prime bound. Take $z=k\log k$. Summing the fixed-$q$ estimate over $q\le X$ proves (3) **in expectation** for uniform prime $p$. Markov at $e^k$ times that upper bound, followed by $\sum_{k\ge u^{1/3}} e^{-k}=o(1)$, proves the simultaneous assertion. At the top of the range (3) gives zero, so larger ranks cannot help on those same inputs. ∎

**Sharp numerical corollary.** Keep the optimistic z75 comparison of §2 but allow even input-dependent $k$ and adaptive square-dividing alphabets in this GRH-height interval. Uniformly off the exceptional set, the best comparison cost in logarithms is $(2\sqrt{2}+o(1))\sqrt{u\log u}$ if all integer windows are granted and $M=M_2(p,k)$. For a lower bound any restriction to admissible prime windows only worsens it. Indeed the binding scaled cost is now $2/b+b$ (minimum at $b=\sqrt2$); outside that compact range one of the two terms dominates, as in §2. The progression lower bound for $M_2$ attains the numerical optimum. No assertion about an upper bound on the actual cap constant follows. This rules out a **uniform** little-oh improvement *by that certificate* even granting perfect distribution for the eligible primes. It does not rule out switching to another certificate on typical inputs (in particular almost-all cap results already exist), using valuation one with a different parity correction, estimating bad relations **inside** the admitted population, or choosing a new height.

### 4. Bypassing the square-rank barrier: single planting (PROVED local lemma; pointwise theorem CONDITIONAL on Dirichlet GRH)

Let $R>1$ be squarefree with every prime factor $1\bmod 8$. Other radicals give cap zero by the classical identities for a factor $2$, $3\bmod4$ or $5\bmod8$ (z75 §1), scaled to $n$. Take a prime $q\equiv3\pmod4$ with $(q/R)=-1$, $K=(R+q)/4$. All primes of $K$ are coprime to $Rq$. The product of the odd primes occurring in $K$ to odd order has symbol $-1\pmod q$: indeed $(K/q)=(R/q)=(q/R)=-1$; if $K$ is even then $q\equiv7\pmod8$, $(2/q)=1$.

**Parity packet.** Let $S$ be *any* set of $k$ distinct odd prime divisors of $K$, valuations $b_v$ arbitrary. Take $V_0=\prod_{v\in S,\ b_v\ {\rm odd}}v$. If $(V_0/q)=-1$ set $g=V_0$; otherwise adjoin to $V_0$ one negative-symbol odd prime outside $S$ of odd valuation, to obtain $g$. The preceding product sign guarantees that extra prime exists in the latter case. Thus $(g/q)=-1$ and $g$ contains exactly once each odd-valuation member of $S$. Let $H=\langle v^2:v\in S\rangle$ in $U=(\mathbb F_q^*)^2$ and $j=[U:H]$, odd since $q\equiv3\pmod4$. Since $-g\in U$, $(-g)^{-j}\in H$. Choose $a_v\ge0$ representing it by $\prod v^{2a_v}$. Then

$$d=K g^j\prod v^{2a_v}\equiv-K\pmod q,\qquad A_0=(K+d)/q.$$

With $(x,y,z)=(K,R A_0,R K A_0/d)$ direct addition gives $4/R$. At a prime $v\mid K$ of valuation $b$, write $w=v_v(d)\ge b$. If $w>b$, then $v_v(K+d)=b$ and the reduced denominator exponent of $z$ is $(w-2b)^+$; if $w=b$ it is zero. At every $v\in S$ the difference $w-2b=j1_{v\mid g}+2a_v-b$ is even; at the possible exterior prime it is $j-b$ even. There is no overflow elsewhere, in particular none at 2, and every overflowing prime divides $A_0$. The reduced denominator is therefore an odd square with root prime to $R$, supported on $y$, of cap at most $j+\max_v a_v$. Scaling by $n/R$ preserves the denominator root and the support. In particular the negative-symbol carrier can be **shared** with the planted alphabet without requiring any valuation modulo $v^2$ or $v^3$.

**Window count.** Put $t=\log_2 R$, $k=\lfloor2\sqrt{2t/\log(2t)}\rfloor$, $S$ the first $k$ primes $3\bmod4$, $D=4\prod_{v\in S}v$. Thus $\log D=k\log(k\log k)+O(k)$ by the fixed-modulus PNT. Impose $q\equiv -R\pmod D$ (this is $3\bmod4$). Use $X=(\log R)^2D^4(1+t)^{10}$, so $u=\log X=(2+o(1))t$ and $k=(2+o(1))\sqrt{u/\log u}$. Under Dirichlet GRH there are $M\gg X/(D\log X)$ such primes $q\le X$ with $(q/R)=-1$.

Here is the analytic input precisely. For fixed nonnegative $w\in C_c^\infty((1/2,1))$ with positive integral, the classical Weil explicit formula for primitive Dirichlet $L$-functions and the unit-height zero bound $N_\psi(T+1)-N_\psi(T)\ll\log(2m(T+2))$ for conductor $m$, $T\ge0$ (Davenport, *Multiplicative Number Theory*, chapters on the number of zeros and explicit formula), give under GRH

$$\sum_{q\ {\rm prime}} w(q/X)\psi(q)\log q=1_{\psi=1} X\!\int w +O_w(\sqrt X\log(2mX)+\sqrt X\log^2(2X)).$$

Indeed the nontrivial zeros contribute $-\sum_\rho X^\rho\widehat w_M(\rho)$, absolutely $O_w(\sqrt X\log(2m))$ by Mellin decay on $\Re s=1/2$. The pole of $\zeta$ gives the main term; the trivial zeros/archimedean terms are harmless for this smooth bump away from zero (equivalently move the Mellin contour to $\Re s=-1/2$ and use the functional equation there; a possible pole of the logarithmic derivative at zero contributes $O_w(1)$). Prime powers cost $O_w(\sqrt X\log^2(2X))$. Imprimitive lifts at the common modulus $RD$ cost $O_w(\log(RD))$ for primes, not a multiple of the family size. In fact $1_{q=-R(D)}(1-\chi_R(q))/2$ on units has coefficient norm one and mean $1/(2\varphi(D))$ by $(R,D)=1$ and the nontrivial primitive quadratic character of the squarefree $R$. Each primitive conductor is $\le RD$. These estimates at the displayed $X$ give error $o(X/D)$ uniformly, proving the count. No GRH for a Kummer field or exceptional-set inference in $R$ is used.

**Deletion and optimization.** For a shared fixed $S$, a nonzero relation for squared generators of sup norm $\le B$ forces $q$ to divide a nonzero difference of two products (separate positive and negative coordinates). There are $\le (3B)^k$ vectors for $B\ge1$, each difference has $O(B\log D)$ prime divisors by its log height. With $B=(cM/(3^k\log D))^{1/(k+1)}$ for an absolute small $c>0$, $B\to\infty$, at least one admissible $q$ survives. The kernel lattice has determinant $|H|$, first sup minimum $>B$, largest minimum $\lambda_k\le |H|/B^{k-1}$ by Minkowski's second theorem ($\prod_i\lambda_i\le\det\Lambda$ for the unit sup cube). Box injection on $[0,\lfloor B\rfloor]^k$ gives $j\le X/B^k$. Rounding in a real basis of $k$ independent lattice vectors of length $\le\lambda_k$ gives a representative of every coset of norm $\le k\lambda_k$ (no integral-basis assumption). By shifting the target coset by the constant vector $-(L,\ldots,L)$ first, $L=\lceil k\lambda_k\rceil$, the final representative has entries in $[0,2L]$. Thus the cap is $O(kX/B^{k-1})$. Its log is at most

$$ {2u\over k+1} + {k-1\over k+1}(\log D+O(k+\log u))+O(\log k)
       =(2+o(1))\sqrt{u\log u}. $$

Since $u=(2+o(1))t$ this gives the §0 constant $2\sqrt2$. This is below the optimized cost of the **square-dividing** architecture from §3 at the same $X$: that comparison alone costs $2\sqrt2\sqrt{u\log u}$. Neither comparison proves $o(\sqrt{t\log t})$; the little-oh target remains open. A polylogarithmic cap in $R$ is of course *larger* than the existing GRH bound; the nontrivial polylogarithmic target on this scale would be polynomial in $\log_2 R$.
