## 2. The limit set at 17 (and at every odd prime)

**PROVED (relative to Dirichlet's theorem).** For any odd prime r, every nonsquare cylinder u mod r^j contains P-centres of unbounded level, already with a=c=1. Take a prime ℓ≡−u mod r^j, ℓ≡3 mod 4 (Dirichlet). By reciprocity (r/ℓ)=(−ℓ/r)=−1. Therefore N=r^K≡−1 mod ℓ for K=(ℓ−1)/2 (odd) and for every K in this class modulo ℓ−1. Put d=(ℓ+1)/4 and b=(N+1)/ℓ. Then 4bd=N+e for e=b+1, all parameters are units at r (in particular r∤d as u≠1 mod r), and the oriented P-centre is −ℓ. Take K arbitrarily large so the box is inside the prescribed cylinder. Distinct ℓ may be used as j grows. Thus the nonsquare part of the sequential limit set as K→∞ is the *entire* nonsquare locus. The union of the positive-radius boxes is open dense there; any sterile subset is nowhere dense, even when positive in Haar measure. This is not a finite covering or a disproof of sterility at 17.

**PROVED (obstruction to the most direct lifting rule only).** For the fixed prime key f=ℓ above the admissible odd exponents must satisfy r^K≡−1 mod ℓ. The periods ord_ℓ(r) are unbounded even with the same prescribed low digits of the centre: all ℓ of order ≤H divide the fixed nonzero product of r^i−1, 1≤i≤H. Consequently a fixed phase K mod M cannot decide presence of *these individual prime keys* (otherwise take a period >2M and compare K with K+2M). The auxiliary datum is divisibility in ℤ by a unit prime to r, not Hensel solvability in ℤ_r. This does not exclude a finite automaton for existence of some other surviving child; it shows precisely what information an exact key-by-key Hensel recursion misses.

**PROVED (nonregularity of exact-level box recognition, all r≥17).** Write centre words least-significant digit first, to the *actual box depth*. The language of all exact-level nonsquare P words is not regular; neither is the one for all P and Q together. Indeed every word with a nonsquare first digit has an extension in the language, by the prime-key construction. In a deterministic finite automaton with s states such a word would have an accepting continuation of length <s (erase loops in a shortest continuation). Thus at least (r−1)r^{L−1}/2 distinct accepted words would occur across depths L,…,L+s−1 for every L. But each level L has o(r^L) words: apply the ZL tails to K=2L−1 for P and k=L for odd Q. The normalised bounds decrease geometrically for this fixed r, by §1. The sum over any *fixed* number of following depths is still o(r^L), contradiction. The argument even gives unbounded minimum additional depth to the next P/Q box word over all prefixes. Inversion is harmless (language is already inversion-invariant); sign or rescaling by r² was not assumed to preserve data. For every r certified by the positive-measure test, the exact language of cumulative alive prefixes is nonregular as well: a finite safety automaton accepting a positive-measure set of infinite paths has a reachable closed live component, else from every live state a killing word of length ≤s exists and survival is at most (1−r^{−s})^k after ks more digits. A closed live component gives an entirely sterile cylinder, impossible by density. At 17 the latter implication is conditional on positive measure. **Neither theorem excludes a one-sided regular safe subtree, nor proves that any sterile point exists at 17.**

**Fractional-knapsack lemma.** At one e≥t≥1, put M≤m<2^b and let θ=i/b, t=2^i. Among h|M² in the PL unit class there are ≤8 τ(M²)^(1−θ). A greedy optimum takes whole items and at most one fraction β of a boundary p^v. Put those whole powers in g, and floor(βv)=u copies of the boundary prime; g≤t. In exponents of h at this last prime, use **consecutive blocks of 2u+1 exponents**. Factoring out the block base power leaves an exponent in [0,2u]. It has at most two values since at least one of h₀ and g²/h₀ is ≤g≤e (each in its specified class).
