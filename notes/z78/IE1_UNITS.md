# Circle controls and simultaneous Pell heights

## 1. What survives both components at a pivot?

**PROVED (rel. z76 §§1–2).** Let `A°` be a punctured factorial stage of a twisted successor over the constant field `F`, `E=Frac A°`, `w` its axis. Its units are `F* t(ℤ[1/2])` as in z75 `BCM4_PUSH2.md`. At an inert positive prime target `s` of axis value zero form `X: x²+y²=s`. Here inert means `−1` is nonsquare in `k_s=Frac(A°/s)`. Use the obstruction space `W` of z76: horizontally unramified classes in `Br(X)[2]` whose vertical residues all come from the respective old squareclasses. The parameter map on `W/im Br(E)[2]` embeds this space **modulo sign** in `F*/(±F*²)` (view the constant classes in `E`), and every representative `a∈F*` so obtained becomes a square in `k_s(i)`.

Indeed over `E(i)` a lift is `γ+(a,U)` with `U=x+iy` and `γ∈Br(E(i))[2]`. As in z76 the variable `U` in each good vertical Laurent fibre, and in the component where `U` is a unit at `s`, detects the parity of the valuation of `a`. Gaussian base change is étale, so all punctured parities downstairs vanish. Factor in the punctured UFD. Its units mod squares are constant classes: divide any monomial by the square of one of half its value; the resulting monomial unit of value zero is a field scalar. The stages are regular field extensions of `F` (directed rational base, then geometrically integral generic edges), so constants inject on squareclasses. The sign-only kernel on `W` is zero modulo old 2-torsion by precisely the order-four residue argument of z76 §2: a complete-conic lift not from old 2-torsion doubles to `(-1,s)` on constants and restricts at `k_s(i)` to a character not descending from old 2-torsion at `k_s`.

For the additional assertion, compare the **two** components above `s` over the Gaussian base. The Gaussian old valuation has residue `k_s(i)` and uniformizer `s`. On the first component `v(U)=0`; on the other `v(U)=1` since `U conjugate(U)=s` and the other factor is a unit. The residues of the same constant `γ` are the same by restriction, while the residues of `(a,U)` differ by `[a]`. Since the residue downstairs is an old `k_s` squareclass, its restrictions on *both* components are the same class from `k_s`, using the identity on the Gaussian coefficient field on each component. Injection of constant squareclasses into a rational line now gives `[a]=0` in `k_s(i)`. In particular at a fresh proxy pivot `s=hV+b` on a new line with nonzero axis residue of `b`, `k_s` is the preceding fraction field (invert the punctured unit `h`). It is regular over `F`; hence **`W=im Br(E)[2]`** there. There is no unchangeable constraint on the local choices for this proxy circle. This comparison of the two components does *not* say that every parameter in the kernel occurs.

## 2. First successor: the rotation calculation

**PROVED (rel. z76 §2 and the z76 TS+QR bottom PID).** Suppose the target has been positively normalized in the first successor: its axis reduction is a unit at every old place, and its dyadic image is 1 mod 4. On the residual circle, integral rotations at all old places suffice to solve (B) on `W`. The signless parameter space embeds via valuation parity in the direct sum of bits over the **entire** bottom PID prime set, including 2. This is ordinary external PID factorization of a fraction with unit group `±1`, not factorization of the later arithmetic ring. At an odd split completion use a unit with nonsquare norm in the split Gaussian algebra. At an odd inert completion with residue field `l`, `l*/l*²` has order two generated by `−1`. The Gaussian norm represents this generator: old Lag on a positive generator of value one gives a nontrivial isotropic four-vector mod the prime, and grouping its squares in two pairs presents `−1` as a quotient of two nonzero Gaussian norms (anisotropy of two squares). This proves surjectivity on unit classes, hence on residue units, even at the infinite fields. Lift unit norm points by Hensel. These arguments also supply an integral initial point for the given unit target. At 2 use the dyadic unit-circle criterion and the norm 5 rotation of z76. Hilbert 90 on the torus gives the change `(a,Nv)` under multiplication by `v/conjugate(v)`, as a Brauer class, not just a local-field sign. After the odd tame residue followed by the old character, a unit nonsquare norm tests exactly the parity of `a`, in residue characteristic zero as well as at finite fields. Changes in the real bit are zero. At the axis these formulas apply on reduction: the parameter can be taken constant after dividing by a square, or equivalently reduce the Gaussian Laurent symbol and the translated torus torsor on the unit-target smooth model (see specialization discussion below).

Extend the defect functional from the embedded subspace to the direct sum, and use its coordinate bits. Changing infinitely many local choices is legitimate **provided** the candidates have uniform finite-support marks as in z76; for each parameter the sum is finite. The rest of the vertical and horizontal extensions are the quotient-space and purity arguments in z76, in that order. At the example of z77 §5 the bit at `P` is necessary; here it is available because the residue is split Gaussian and has a nonsquare unit. It changes the embedding of a *new* residual coordinate, not the old character, the old residue arithmetic or the marks of `P`. This is not the adjunction of a second independent valuation on the upper field.

**PROVED (specialization detail).** One can justify the parameter used after axis specialization without assuming that a global Azumaya representative has good reduction. Over the Gaussian field the class is `γ+(a,U)`; at the generic axis `U` is a unit with residue the residual Laurent variable. On a Gaussian residual circle, specialization is thus `sp(γ)+(a,Ū)` by the square-normalized specialization of z76. To exclude a hidden Gaussian-kernel term *varying on the circle*, use the generic torus action: in `Br(E(X×T₁))[2]` one has `m*β−pr₁*β=(a,δ(t))`, by the translated torus argument of z76 applied over `E(X)` with the first generic point as origin. Here `δ(t)` is represented generically by `N(1+t)` using `t=(1+t)/(1+t̄)`. Specialize this identity at the generic axis of the product (same finite-level prime-divisor construction as for the circle, both reduced factors smooth and geometrically integral). All three dominant maps preserve the Gauss axis and its group: on the axis the corresponding maps are dominant, so a normalized nonzero residue stays nonzero. Thus the identity holds rationally on the residual product with parameter `[a]`. Evaluation on a *generic pair* in a local ball gives exactly the asserted difference; to prescribe the difference choose `b` and the square-perturbation in the rotation independently generic in their balls. This avoids special evaluation of a ramified representative at an exceptional point.

## 3. Finite-dimensional obstruction at *every* successor

**PROVED (relative to the finite-presentation model of the twisted base).** At any finite circle prefix `W/im Br(E)[2]` is finite dimensional, even over a countable constant field of infinite squareclass rank. Place the coefficients of the stage, its normalized pivot and its presentations at a common level `j` over `F[x_j,1/x_j]`. The transition on finite presentations is finite free and faithfully flat; quotient by the pivot is a domain at every such level since the direct limit is a domain and the maps are injective also on quotients. The pivot is nonunit throughout. Consequently `k_s` is the union of fields `K_n=K_j(x_n)` with `x_n^{2^{n−j}}=d_{jn} x_j, d_{jn}∈F*` and `K_j` finitely generated as a field over `F`. This concerns one prefix, **not** the pivot residue after all upper edges. The kernel on `F*/2` into `K_j` is finite dimensional because the algebraic constants there form a finite extension of `F`. Adjoin all 2-power roots of unity to `K_j`. Its Galois group over `K_j` is a closed subgroup of `ℤ₂*≅{±1}×ℤ₂`, hence has mod-two character rank at most two (project to `ℤ₂`, with cyclic kernel). Over this cyclotomic field the successive composita with `K_n` are nested cyclic Kummer extensions, each obtained by **one** radical. The union has at most one independent quadratic subextension since every finite piece is cyclic. The squareclass kernel into `k_s(i)` therefore has dimension at most the constant-field kernel at `j` plus three. Apply §1. In particular there is no countable-saturation requirement at the single old axis mark: we need prescribe only a functional on a **finite** space.

**PROVED (parity coordinates).** In the input field at each finite-depth successor, sign together with the parity coordinates indexed by *all birth layers' positive normalized arithmetic primes*, including 2, describes every squareclass uniquely. First factor in the top punctured PID, using normalized positive axis-value-zero arithmetic generators. The remaining unit is an old scalar times a monomial; the monomial modulo squares is a positive old scalar. Descend on the field scalars; terminate in the bottom PID, with units `±1`. Independence follows by successively taking punctured parities and then using relative algebraic closedness of each old scalar field; sign by the order. No claim of external unique factorization in the arithmetic ring. The lower parities may equivalently be read by square normalization down the axes, passing through the residual marks as in z76 §5: a factor born above has unit images at every lower evaluation (including dyadic), and one born below is still a field scalar. For this equivalent parity test one must descend using `H`-mark and reduction between successive punctured layers. The factorization proof only uses a finite chain, and gives finite support. Thus any functional on the signless finite obstruction space is the restriction of one with **finite support** among all layers (coordinate restrictions span the dual since they separate points of the finite space). Sign is irrelevant for allowed norm rotations.

## 4. A parameter-free cubic test beyond all the quadratic windows

**PROVED (explicit bounded formula).** Put `t=∛2`, `e=1+t+t²`, of norm one since `(t−1)e=1` and `N(t−1)=1`. In the free cubic ring on `t³=2` write `N(x,y,z)=x³+2y³+4z³−6xyz`. Over any discretely ordered ring use the following parameter-free `E₁` predicate:

```
C₃(n): ∃x,y,z≤5(n+1) [N(x,y,z)=1 ∧ n≤x+y+z≤5(n+1)].
```

On the cone, the base and ordinary successor implication already hold: at 0 use `(1,0,0)`. If the sum already exceeds `n` retain the witness. Otherwise multiply by `e`; the new coordinates are `(x+2y+2z,x+y+2z,x+y+z)`. Their sum is between `3n` and `5n` and the norm is still one, an integral polynomial identity. The case of zero sum cannot occur. Thus `IE₁⁻` forces `∀n C₃(n)`. More generally `1+t+…+t^{k−1}` for odd standard `k≥3` in `t^k=2` has norm 1, and multiplication on the nonnegative coefficient cone raises its coefficient sum by a factor between `k` and `2k−1`; the same proof gives one window per such k. This uses only polynomially bounded witnesses of fixed dimension per axiom. In particular the cubic test uses no parameters, existential power relation, minimization, or bound on the number of prime factors of an ambient integer.

**PROVED (unit-descent barrier, not finite rank).** Let `D` be a normal discretely ordered signed IOpen domain with an end-initial signed PA cut `C` (or the ordinary cut); let `F₀=Frac C`. Suppose `D` is inside an ordered upper algebra `B` over `F₀` and its fraction field is regular over `F₀`. Suppose `B` is obtained, starting with a regular algebra having only scalar units after adjoining `F₀(t)`, by a directed tower of the following kinds of edges:

* polynomial/axis twist levels; lines, row and polynomial-chart root edges with old-coefficient contraction, and monic four-square or inert Gaussian circle edges as in z75–76;
* translation-twisted power systems on *quadratic* norm-one tori for positive or negative radicands in `F₀` nonsquare there, with arbitrary available twists, root orders, multiplicity and interleaving. Use the unpunctured affine torus at each level. These are edges over the preceding algebra, with the defining quadratic still nonsplit over its fraction field after adjoining `F₀(t)`. No restrictions on the unit rank over the splitting fields.

Then `C₃(n)` fails at every `n>D`'s cut (i.e. every `n∈D` above `C`), provided that `D` properly end extends `C`. Indeed `F₀(t)` is real of degree three over `F₀` by PA (or ordinary rational irreducibility), hence contains no nontrivial quadratic constant extension. Regularity at all stages follows for these blocks from geometric integrality and passage through directed unions. After this cubic extension every generic quadratic torus still has only scalar units: in the split Laurent presentation a unit has one exponent and conjugation negates it. The same holds on a Gaussian circle whether or not it is a trivial torsor, using the ordered real cubic embedding. A rank-four sphere has unit descent by the domain top form. The generic polynomial charts have constant units. Contraction is exact on the affine conics/spheres by monicity (or their monomial basis), over lines by coefficients, over root charts by even/odd polynomial degrees, and on the z75 rows by the stipulated unpunctured old-field contraction before base change, then comparing coefficients in the cubic basis. Scalar extension is over the constant field, not along a splitting map at a marked prime. Any unit and inverse in a twisted limit already occur at a finite level; the twist does not make that level's torus split. Thus `B[t]*=F₀(t)*`, even if after real **algebraic closure** there are infinitely many independent units. A norm-one solution produces an actual unit with its inverse given by the integral adjugate. Independence of `1,t,t²` over `Frac B` now confines all coordinates to `D∩F₀=C`. This barrier also applies with a base relative to a preceding field, provided the cubic-extended units there descend to the old field; in an arithmetic pullback its old coordinates contract to the old arithmetic ring. Alternatively a failure at an old height simply persists under arithmetic end extensions because all quantifiers are bounded. No unspecified mixing of old-field scalar and arithmetic units is needed.

**PROVED (counterexample to sufficiency, rel. z77 §3, z76 `IE1_BREAK.md` §2, z75 `IE1_PUSH2.md`).** There is one non-PID normal IOpen + Lag + supplied-unit PW model with a nonstandard true-arithmetic cut, `J²=J≠0`, `H(A*)=Γ⁰[G]` for **every finite independent multiquadratic cut system**, and the full fixed-modulus polynomial-congruence/comparison least scheme **on Pell witnesses** of z76, but `C₃` defines exactly that cut and `¬ES(P)` holds. In particular even *all these simultaneous properties* do not imply parameter-free bounded-existential induction. **No Bézout, BCM, TS or QR assertion for this model.** Take the actual rank-one seed of z76 §2. Its extra initial negative-Pell edge at `P` still has only scalar generic units after the real cubic constant extension: `X²−PY²` remains irreducible over `ℚ(t)(P)` by the valuation at `P`, and monic contraction gives unit descent. The barrier applies to all later edges. Hence every ambient norm-one triple is ordinary, whereas in ℕ the base/step proves `C₃` at every n. Transfer the *pair* including this defining equivalence, normality (the monic-equation scheme), PW, the filtered schemes and ambient ES failure as in z77 §§1,3. All nonsquare radicands in the cut have supplied positive units there by PA, so z77 §4 applies to all the asserted hyperplanes. The cut factorial of an infinite cut element is nonzero in `J`, preventing PID by finite factorization, and Lag gives `J²=J` by z75. This realizes the requested height condition and nonzero idempotent kernel together, **not** by joining the two axiom packages. The separation from `IE₁⁻` is witnessed by one displayed cubic induction instance, despite all the heights and the Pell-filtered least scheme.

**Assessment (generic construction).** A Fraïssé construction taking only the listed extensions cannot be existentially closed even for this one parameter-free window at an infinite height: every finite witness would already occur at a node where unit descent forbids it. The obstruction is the Galois action on exponents after an *odd-degree* constant extension, not countability or lack of unboundedly many quadratics. Twisted rather than untwisted roots, Bézout rows satisfying the contraction condition, and more dyadic-axis halvings do not repair it. This proves a no-`IE₁⁻` theorem for a tower shape, not an implication from Bézout/Lag/idempotence plus the height equality alone, and not `IE₁⁻ ⊬ ES`.
