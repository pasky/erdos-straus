## 4. A parameter-free cubic test beyond all the quadratic windows

**Theorem B — PROVED (relative to z75–77; a different model).** All multiquadratic cut height hyperplanes, nonzero idempotent dyadic kernel and filtered Pell least search hold together in a normal IOpen + Lag ES-countermodel that fails the following parameter-free norm-form induction. The first theorem is not an input to this one.

**PROVED (explicit bounded formula).** Put `t=∛2`, `e=1+t+t²`, of norm one since `(t−1)e=1` and `N(t−1)=1`. In the free cubic ring on `t³=2` write `N(x,y,z)=x³+2y³+4z³−6xyz`. Over any discretely ordered ring use the following parameter-free `E₁` predicate:

```
C₃(n): ∃x,y,z≤5(n+1) [N(x,y,z)=1 ∧ n≤x+y+z≤5(n+1)].
```

On the cone, the base and ordinary successor implication already hold: at 0 use `(1,0,0)`. If the sum already exceeds `n` retain the witness. Otherwise multiply by `e`; the new coordinates are `(x+2y+2z,x+y+2z,x+y+z)`. Their sum is between `3n` and `5n` and the norm is still one, an integral polynomial identity. The case of zero sum cannot occur. Thus `IE₁⁻` forces `∀n C₃(n)`. More generally `1+t+…+t^{k−1}` for odd standard `k≥3` in `t^k=2` has norm 1, and multiplication on the nonnegative coefficient cone raises its coefficient sum by a factor between `k` and `2k−1`; the same proof gives one window per such k. This uses only polynomially bounded witnesses of fixed dimension per axiom. In particular the cubic test uses no parameters, existential power relation, minimization, or bound on the number of prime factors of an ambient integer.

**PROVED (unit-descent barrier, not finite rank).** Let `D` be a normal discretely ordered signed IOpen domain with an end-initial signed PA cut `C` (or the ordinary cut); let `F₀=Frac C`. Suppose `D` is inside an ordered upper algebra `B` over `F₀` and its fraction field is regular over `F₀`. Suppose `B` is obtained, starting with a regular algebra having only scalar units after adjoining `F₀(t)`, by a directed tower of the following kinds of edges:

* polynomial/axis twist levels; lines, row and polynomial-chart root edges with old-coefficient contraction, and monic four-square or inert Gaussian circle edges as in z75–76;
* translation-twisted power systems on *quadratic* norm-one tori for positive or negative radicands in `F₀` nonsquare there, with arbitrary available twists, root orders, multiplicity and interleaving. Use the unpunctured affine torus at each level. These are edges over the preceding algebra, with the defining quadratic still nonsplit over its fraction field after adjoining `F₀(t)`. No restrictions on the unit rank over the splitting fields.

Then `C₃(n)` fails at every `n∈D` above `C`, provided that `D` properly end extends `C`. Indeed `F₀(t)` is real of degree three over `F₀` by PA (or ordinary rational irreducibility), hence contains no nontrivial quadratic constant extension. Regularity at all stages follows for these blocks from geometric integrality and passage through directed unions. After this cubic extension every generic quadratic torus still has only scalar units: in the split Laurent presentation a unit has one exponent and conjugation negates it. The same holds on a Gaussian circle whether or not it is a trivial torsor, using the ordered real cubic embedding. A rank-four sphere has unit descent by the domain top form. The generic polynomial charts have constant units. Contraction is exact on the affine conics/spheres by monicity (or their monomial basis), over lines by coefficients, over root charts by even/odd polynomial degrees, and on the z75 rows by the stipulated unpunctured old-field contraction before base change, then comparing coefficients in the cubic basis. Scalar extension is over the constant field, not along a splitting map at a marked prime. Any unit and inverse in a twisted limit already occur at a finite level; the twist does not make that level's torus split. Thus `B[t]*=F₀(t)*`, even if after real **algebraic closure** there are infinitely many independent units. A norm-one solution produces an actual unit with its inverse given by the integral adjugate. Independence of `1,t,t²` over `Frac B` now confines all coordinates to `D∩F₀=C`. This barrier also applies with a base relative to a preceding field, provided the cubic-extended units there descend to the old field; in an arithmetic pullback its old coordinates contract to the old arithmetic ring. Alternatively a failure at an old height simply persists under arithmetic end extensions because all quantifiers are bounded. No unspecified mixing of old-field scalar and arithmetic units is needed.

**PROVED (counterexample to sufficiency, rel. z77 §3, z76 `IE1_BREAK.md` §2, z75 `IE1_PUSH2.md`).** There is one non-PID normal IOpen + Lag + supplied-unit PW model with a nonstandard true-arithmetic cut, `J²=J≠0`, `H(A*)=Γ⁰[G]` for **every finite independent multiquadratic cut system**, and the full fixed-modulus polynomial-congruence/comparison least scheme **on Pell witnesses** of z76, but `C₃` defines exactly that cut and `¬ES(P)` holds. Thus this is a model with *all these simultaneous properties* in which parameter-free bounded-existential induction fails; the height groups and external intersection `J` are not themselves ring-language axiom schemes. **No Bézout, BCM, TS or QR assertion for this model.** Take the actual rank-one seed of z76 §2. Its extra initial negative-Pell edge at `P` still has only scalar generic units after the real cubic constant extension: `X²−PY²` remains irreducible over `ℚ(t)(P)` by the valuation at `P`, and monic contraction gives unit descent. ## 5. A single character module for all number fields

**Theorem C — PROVED (relative to finite Galois descent, the rank-one floor/Lag lemmas of z75 and polynomial ES obstruction).** In a *third* countable normal IOpen + Lag ring with `¬ES(P)`, every ordinary number-field order simultaneously has all its archimedean norm-one height vectors (defined below). In particular `C_k` of §4 holds throughout the cone for **every standard odd k≥3**. This is not the Bézout/QR tower of §§1–3, nor does it have the cubic failure of §4. The construction does not rely on taking a product of models.

**The module.** Fix an algebraic closure `Q̄⊂ℂ`, its absolute Galois group `G`, and complex conjugation `c`. Let `M` be the additive rational vector space of locally constant rational functions on `G` that are right-`c`-invariant and have Haar mean zero. Equivalently take the union of finite-level spaces on finite Galois groups modulo the right action of conjugation; the mean is an ordinary finite average at any level. `G` acts by left translation. Let `X^m` be the basis of the group algebra, with `X^m X^l=X^(m+l)`. Over `ℚ[P]` use the algebra

```
       A=(Q̄[P][M])^G,       g(X^m)=X^(gm),     g(P)=P.
```

Here `P` is an indeterminate. Every finite list is contained after extension to a finite Galois field `L` in `L[P][Λ]` for a `Gal(L/ℚ)`-stable finite free lattice `Λ⊂M` (take the integer span of finitely many finite orbits). Descent gives an affine torus over ℚ with geometric character lattice `Λ`, times the `P` line, and recovers that Laurent algebra after adjoining `L`. This proves coefficientwise that `A⊗Q̄=Q̄[P][M]`; injections and unions agree by this faithfully flat base change. Finite pieces are smooth geometrically integral normal domains. Each positive-degree fibre of the `P` line used below, in particular the fibre at `P=0`, is a geometrically integral torus. Countability uses only functions on finite quotients, not all functions on `G`.

**Order and cancellation lemma.** Assign `X^m` size `P^{m(1)}` at the identity place with `P` positive infinite. To do this simultaneously and honestly in an ordered field, take the complex Gauss field of `Q̄[M]` with its semilinear involution `c`, realized as follows. For the fixed and anti-fixed rational subspaces of `c` on `M` choose countable rational bases. To a fixed basis element assign a positive real in `(1,2)`; to an anti-fixed one a unit complex number on the circle. Choose the real parameters and the circle parameters (e.g. rational slope on the circle) algebraically independent over the real algebraic numbers. Together the corresponding complex coordinates are algebraically independent over `Q̄` (rational circle parametrization for the unit coordinate and its inverse parametrization over `Q̄`). Choose compatible rational powers, positive on the real generators and on the unit circle for anti-fixed ones. This gives an injective Laurent evaluation `m↦u_m` on group-ring monomials with `u_{cm}=conj(u_m)`: no finite Laurent sum with algebraic coefficients cancels, by taking common roots and algebraic independence. Now put `X^m=P^{m(1)}u_m` in a complexified real ordered field of rational powers of `P`, ordered at infinity with these archimedean constants. This intertwines conjugation since `(cm)(1)=m(c)=m(1)`. Coefficients at a given power in any Laurent sum over `Q̄[P]` cannot cancel except by coefficientwise cancellation in the group algebra. The real invariant algebra thus embeds with its intended order and every field size is in `ℚ·[P]` (mod standard bounded ratios). A nonconstant term of an invariant element has its entire coefficient-conjugate orbit. If it has exponent `m≠0`, some conjugate has `(gm)(1)>0` by mean zero and nonconstancy, and all polynomial coefficient degrees are nonnegative. If all exponents are zero only ordinary polynomials remain. Thus the bounded elements of `A` are exactly `ℚ`. Compatible powers and a mean-zero module, not independently added variables at duplicate heights, are important for this conclusion.

**Cubic test of the proposed adjunction.** For `K=ℚ(t)`, `t³=2`, put `H=Gal(Q̄/K)`. Functions fixed by `H` are functions on `H\G/⟨c⟩`: identify the place at `Hg` with the embedding `g⁻¹|K`, modulo complex conjugation. At the real place prescribe `a>0`, at the complex place `−a/2`, `a` any positive rational. The resulting function `m` has mean zero. `z=X^m` lies in `A⊗K`; its three conjugates have product one since the sum of the three exponents is the zero function (each value at a group element sums over all embeddings). Thus its coefficients give an actual point on the cubic norm-one torus. In fact for this profile the orbit of exponents spans a lattice of rank two with only the sum relation: viewed on the three cosets before identifying complex places the three left translates are the three conjugates of a special-coset indicator minus its mean (the real embedding corresponds to `H` and `c∈H`). The generated algebra at this one orbit is the generic norm-one torus, not just a curve on it. Its positive real coordinate dominates at height `P^a` (choose the square of the monomial of half the profile for positive sign), and all three power-basis coefficients are positive there by Fourier inversion as in §6. This works for any prescribed seed size class `a[P]`, not for an arbitrary pre-existing algebra with competing units. Normality and bounded contraction are as above. Use the same `M` to prescribe all subsequent sizes without opening a new independent angular variable at an already represented profile.

**Positive reciprocal descent, including the non-hypersurface case.** In the generic group algebra over `Q̄(P)` use the weight filtration `deg X^m=m(1)`, *giving all coefficients degree zero*. The largest degree is additive under multiplication (the leading homogeneous pieces in a group domain multiply nontrivially). Restrict this valuation `−deg` to the invariant field. Every invariant Laurent polynomial with a nonconstant exponent has a pole, since its conjugate term with positive weight occurs. The intersection with constants is `ℚ[P]` in the unlocalized algebra. In any positive reciprocal identity at `P`, reduction at this valuation retains just the denominators in `ℚ[P]`, which already supply the entire sum; positivity forces no discarded term. This is an ES descent valuation, **not** the valuation that orders the ring. More generally this suffices on an ordered algebra edge over an old domain: a valuation trivial on the old fraction field at which all elements of the new algebra outside the old algebra have poles. For this test it is the orbit of each character, not inertness of `P` in a norm extension, that matters. At an edge relative to old coefficients one needs such a filtration plus constant contraction, and a separate order check.

**Arithmetic completion.** Mark the whole torus algebra by its augmentation `X^m↦1` and `P↦1` at every ordinary prime (fixed elements map to rational values). Dovetail the rank-one floor and positive-infinite four-square tasks as in `IE1_PUSH2.md` §1: floor variables near a real-closure approximand with a fresh bounded transcendental residue, and sphere variables scaled by `√s` with independent bounded angular residues. Each countable stage's natural residue field is countable; choose fresh real residues over it. The cited order argument preserves bounded elements `ℚ` and puts the new sizes in the divisible hull of `ℚ[P]` in the size-group sense, i.e. `ℚ·[P]`; algebraically use polynomial or monic sphere stages, keeping normality via finite-type subsystems as in `IE1_ES.md` §3. Over a field with algebraically closed constants these later generic extensions are rational (the split four-form has a hyperbolic chart); modulo `P` the sphere is geometrically integral even at zero by rank four. Extend the marks at a floor by zero, at a sphere by ordinary local Lag, integrally whenever its target is integral. Each element has poles at most at finitely many ordinary places by the finite expression test. In the final union take the simultaneous integral subring `R`. Standard denominator clearing, rational CRT translation of floor approximants, the old bounded-element calculation and Shepherdson yield IOpen and `R⊗ℚ=A_final`. Normality descends by integrality first in the upper algebra, then at each `ℤ_l` on the marks; Lag witnesses are integral by scheduling, and `P` stays prime by the prime fibre and unit marks. All of these are union statements, not floor or Lag claims at each prefix.

**ES check.** The floor and sphere edges have ordinary constant contraction and negative degree valuations on their generic coordinate algebras by z75.
