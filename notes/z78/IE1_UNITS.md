## 5. A single character module for all number fields

**The module.** Fix an algebraic closure `Q̄⊂ℂ`, its absolute Galois group `G`, and complex conjugation `c`. ```

```

Here `P` is an indeterminate. **Order and cancellation lemma.** Assign `X^m` size `P^{m(1)}` at the identity place with `P` positive infinite. To do this simultaneously and honestly in an ordered field, take the complex Gauss field of `Q̄[M]` with its semilinear involution `c`, realized as follows. Now put `X^m=P^{m(1)}u_m` in a complexified real ordered field of rational powers of `P`, ordered at infinity with these archimedean constants. This intertwines conjugation since `(cm)(1)=m(c)=m(1)`. ```
∃z of norm one with signed O-coordinates,   D⁻¹ n^{r_j} ≤ |σ_j z| ≤ D n^{r_j}   (all j).
```

Here `D>1` is a sufficiently large ordinary integer depending only on `O`, and `r` is the fixed ray, not a parameter. At 0 set the formula true. ## 7. Beyond tori: an elliptic doubling window

**Theorem D — PROVED (rel. the duplication law, homogeneous Bézout identity and no rational curves on an elliptic curve).** There is a parameter-free bounded-existential induction instance in the original arithmetic language, with a fixed elliptic curve over ℚ and a **polynomial-width** height window, whose base and step hold in every normal signed IOpen ring. In the model of §§5–6 its truth set is exactly the ordinary cut before transfer and the nonstandard true-arithmetic cut after transfer. Consequently even the simultaneous norm-window theorem C, including all the `C_k`, is not `IE₁⁻`. The obstruction is geometric constancy of elliptic **field points**, not a failure of the tori to have enough units.

Take `E: y²=x³−2`. For a primitive affine rational point use `x=a/b`, `b>0`, `H=max(|a|,b)` and `c_y=y b²`; require the integral equations

```
      c_y²=b(a³−2b³),        u a + v b = 1.
```

At input `n` ask for such `a,b,c_y,u,v` with `n≤H≤K(n+1)^4`. All are signed except `b,n,H`; take `|c_y|≤2H²`, `|u|,|v|≤2(H+1)`. This is a single parameter-free `E₁` predicate `Ell_E(n)` after the usual sign and max case splits; the standard numeral `K` is fixed below. The row bound costs no solutions with `(a,b)=(1)`: reduce the coefficient of `a` mod `b` by IOpen division and bound the other coefficient by the equation, including `b=1`. Conversely the row forbids arbitrarily inflating a constant field point by a common coordinate. A naive height of arbitrary projective triples would not have this property.

**Uniform successor.** On the x-line duplication is given by the homogeneous quartics

```
       F(a,b)=a⁴+16ab³,       G(a,b)=4b(a³−2b³).
```

They have no common projective zero in characteristic zero. Fix nonzero standard integer `D₁>0` and integer homogeneous identities putting both `D₁ X^m` and `D₁ Y^m` in the ideal `(F,G)` for some fixed `m` (resultant, or the two Euclidean polynomial identities on the standard line charts, homogenized). By the old row, `(a^m,b^m)=(1)` via binomial expansion of `(ua+vb)^{2m−1}`. Thus `(F(a,b),G(a,b))` contains `D₁`. It is principal generated by an **ordinary positive divisor** `d` of `D₁`: reduce the two entries mod `D₁` by division, take the ordinary gcd with `D₁`, then use the just-proved ideal containment. No global Bézout property of `R` or external factorization of coordinates is invoked. After division one has a primitive pair. On the real square perimeter `max(|a|,|b|)=1`, `max(|F|,|G|)` is bounded below by a positive ordinary rational `η` (compactness/no common zero) and above by an ordinary constant `B`. These homogeneous inequalities hold in the real closure of every ordered field by real-closed transfer. Therefore the new primitive height satisfies

```
        (η/D₁) H⁴ ≤ H' ≤ B H⁴.
```

Choose an ordinary threshold `H₀>1` such that the lower bound exceeds `H+1` for `H≥H₀`, using only the displayed lower bound. If `G=0` at such a point the pair after division would have height 1, impossible. The doubled point is thus affine, with `b'>0` after simultaneous sign adjustment. Its field ordinate `y'` gives integral `c'_y=y'b'²` by the monic square equation and normality. Its size and the row coefficients satisfy the specified bounds. These arguments apply equally at infinite `H`. They do **not** say the canonical height increases by a fixed additive amount, or that there is a point in every fixed-*ratio* window.

To initialize, `(3,5)` is nontorsion, for example its double has x-coordinate `129/100` while rational torsion on this integral short Weierstrass model has integral x-coordinate by Nagell–Lutz. The infinitely many standard doubles have unbounded primitive height (finitely many ordinary pairs below each ordinary bound). Choose one standard point of height `H_*≥H₀+1` and fix `K≥max(H_*,B,1)` an ordinary integer. This choice of numerals specifies one ordinary formula, with no parameters. For `n≤H_*` this point works. Otherwise if an old witness exceeds `n` retain it at `n+1`; if `H=n`, double as above, which lands between `n+1` and `K(n+2)^4`. Thus base and successor hold. Ordinary induction in ℕ proves the predicate for every standard `n`.

**Failure despite the norm windows.** Over `Q̄` each finite torus piece is rational, and after finitely many floor/sphere extensions the generic function field is still purely transcendental over `Q̄` at finite data: finitely many old coefficients/monomials fit in a finite lattice and one replays the edges in that finite-level presentation. For one variable its rational map from `ℙ¹` extends by properness and is constant by Riemann–Hurwitz in characteristic zero, also after enlarging the coefficient field to an algebraic closure. For a rational `x=A/B` in lowest ordinary terms, an integral representation with the displayed row and `b>0` has `a=A,b=B`: the common multiplier belongs to `R` by the ordinary row for `A,B`, and its inverse belongs by the given row; positive arithmetic units are 1. More generally if a normal IOpen proper end extension of a PA cut has `E(Frac R)=E(Frac C)` for this curve, the same primitive-coordinate argument, with the Bézout row *inside PA*, rules out `IE₁⁻`. This is a no-`IE₁⁻` theorem for geometrically rational towers and many more towers with no new maps to the given elliptic curve, irrespective of their toric ranks or `J`. It imposes no fixed-ratio or full Mordell–Weil height lattice theorem, and it does not imply ES. Finitely many integral solutions of a Thue equation would not give this test: projective rational heights and controlled primitive duplication are essential.

## 8. No-gap criteria: the quantifiers matter

**PROVED (counterexample to the per-window cofinality test).** Work in the §§5–7 model, including its nonstandard-cut transfer. In the same parameter-free language form

```
    S(n) : ∃h,v≤n [ h²+v=n ∧ Ell_E(v) ].
```

Witness bounds for the substituted formula remain polynomial in `n+1`. This set contains 0, is successor closed by §7, and is **cofinal**: every square is a hit. It contains the whole PA cut. Nevertheless it misses `n=t²+t` for every `t` above the cut: if `h²≤n` then `h≤t`, and `n−h²≥t` cannot be in the cut. Thus cofinality of even a parameter-free, successor-closed bounded algebraic projection with cofinal cut trace does not verify its induction instance. Adding an alternative “or has a maximum” does not repair the cofinal branch. A nonempty successor-closed set cannot itself have a maximum. If one takes its *downward closure* instead, the example's downward closure is the whole cone and loses precisely the gap. This refutes the suggested *pointwise* replacement, not necessarily every imaginable equivalence of whole schemes via extra test sets.

**PROVED (exact bounded hypersurface scheme).** For an IOpen ring, with or without a PA cut, `IE₁⁻` is equivalent to the following test on parameter-free nonnegative **bounded fibres of quartics**. For every fixed polynomial `Q(X,Y₁,…,Y_m)` over the ordinary integers which is a sum of squares of polynomials of total degree at most two, and every standard bound `B(X)=K(X+1)^b`, let `D_Q(x)` assert that the integral fibre `Q(x,y)=0`, `0≤y_i≤B(x)`, is nonempty. If `D_Q(0)` and `D_Q(x)⇒D_Q(x+1)` throughout the cone, the fibre must be nonempty at **every** x. In particular relative to a normal IOpen proper end extension of a PA cut, this implies surjectivity (hence cofinality) of any such bounded successor-closed window map containing 0. Merely requiring an unbounded projection over cut points, or putting no bound on the lift coordinates, does not establish the base/step hypotheses or bounded quantifier complexity. Over a named cut no cut predicate is allowed in the formula.

One direction is ordinary induction on this `E₁` predicate. For the other, take a bounded existential block with open matrix. Successively expand its finitely many term bounds into a *common dominating polynomial* in `x+1` (parameters absent); keep the actual, possibly dependent, bounds as inequalities. Put the quantifier-free tests in disjunctive normal form, replacing negated equalities by strict inequalities either way. Represent each arithmetic operation in the polynomial terms by a gate equation of degree ≤2; coefficients of signed polynomials can be moved to the positive side. Represent weak or strict comparisons by nonnegative slack with offset 0 or 1. Each resulting finite branch has a sum of gate/slack squares `Q_j`; its vanishing is exactly success of that branch. For the disjunction compute each `Q_j` from nonnegative polynomial expressions via further arithmetic gates (or signed positive-minus-negative pairs) and multiply the branch values, requiring the product zero. Unchosen branches need not satisfy their tests; set unconstrained slacks zero there and just evaluate their gate expressions. All auxiliary gate values and slacks used for this verification are bounded by some fixed `K(x+1)^b` since circuit size and degrees are fixed. Conversely the final multiplication/output equations force at least one tested branch to succeed since the signed ring is a domain. Combine *all required* gate equations and the zero-output equation by one sum of squares, of degree ≤4. This proves exact equivalence per instance, pointwise in the ambient ring. These are affine algebraic sets with an orthant/box guard; they need not be smooth, irreducible, or groups. This is a sufficient presentation class, **not** a lower bound of four on the minimal possible degree. Theorem D rules out substituting the archimedean norm windows alone. No characterization by all commutative-algebraic-group windows or MRDP coding with unbounded auxiliary witnesses follows.

## 10. Side tests and limits

**PROVED (exact divisor test).** In a discretely ordered Bézout IOpen domain, for `h>0` set `U_h={u>0:u|h, 2u∤h}`. This set has a least element iff there is a factorization `h=t b` with `b` odd and `t>0` having no positive odd divisor except 1. In fact the least `t` then divides **every** hit. Necessity: divide a least hit by any nontrivial odd divisor to contradict leastness. Conversely for another hit `h=u b'`, `b'` odd, cancel `g=gcd(t,u)` to see by the Bézout row for `t/g,u/g` that `t/g` divides `b'`. As a positive divisor of an odd element it is odd, hence 1. This does not require that `t` be an internally coded power of two. In particular suppose a proper nonzero prime ideal `I` satisfies `I⊂2R` and every `0<t∈I` has a nontrivial odd divisor (e.g. `I=3I`). For `0<h∈I` every hit is in `I` since its odd cofactor cannot be there. No least hit exists. This rules out `L(Div)` for constructions realizing BCM by successive common-multiple axes **with such a prime conductor**, even if the value group or the relative order was changed. In the old-field-algebra pullback division of an axis-positive element by 2 or 3 preserves the axis ideal, so `I=2I=3I`; the criterion is independent of rotations or of 2-divisibility of the axis **value group**. No theory-level incompatibility `BCM + L(Div) + TS` follows (PA itself has these); the problem at an ES-failing prime outside this shape is not decided.

**PROVED (row and two-chart obstruction).** If a unital mark on a candidate upper algebra sends two inputs into the maximal ideal of its valuation ring, no comaximal row on those inputs can be realized with integral coefficient marks. More sharply, in the §5 seed take positive ordinates `f,g` of norm-one monomials for `√2,√3`, each at real height `[P]`, using the standard quadratic profiles `+1,−1` (these are distinct nonproportional functions). Their identity marks are zero; augmentation kills both. The ratio `g/f` is bounded above and below by ordinary positive constants but is nonrational: after splitting, `g` and `f` are nonproportional binomials `const·(X^m−X^(−m))` with linearly independent exponents, so neither quotient is constant. Consequently **neither** standard affine principalization chart `A[g/f]` or `A[f/g]`, with the chart coordinate retained as an arithmetic integer, can preserve discreteness in the given order: the new coordinate would have to be a bounded nonstandard integer. Even allowing a rational shift does not make it an integer. In particular this cheap replacement of the infeasible comaximal row fails; it is no theorem against a new fractional common generator with two unbounded cofactors. No solution of Bézout + variable supplied-unit windows with `¬ES(P)` is claimed.

**Assessment (scope and next target).** The actual no-gap instances solved here are all standard fixed norm orders with the balanced windows of §6, the positive odd `C_k`, and in §9 a specified elliptic window. They are compatible with `¬ES(P)`. This does not say that a tower satisfying *all* bounded projections of §8, or all commutative-group windows, preserves `¬ES(P)`. Comparison: z75–77 treated quadratic units, not the absolute-Galois module of §5 or the projective height test of §7. The inputs on archimedean logarithmic lattices, elliptic heights/duplication and affine tori are classical; no priority claim for a general theorem about induction and algebraic groups. ## 11. Genericity: two extension relations, two different theorems

**PROVED (freezing lemma).** Fix a countable signed normal IOpen base `D₀` with prime `P` failing ES and a named end-initial PA cut `C` if desired. Let `𝒦` consist of countable normal IOpen proper end extensions **of `D₀`** (optionally all satisfying Lag). Require inclusions to respect the named data and order. There are two choices of arrows. With **end arrows** between all stages, a bounded formula with parameters in `R` is absolute along `R⊂_end R′`: induct on its quantifiers, since the full interval up to every value of a bounding term is unchanged. In particular for ANY bounded existential instance `(φ,h)` in `R`,

```
    fillable by an end arrow from R   ⇔   R ⊨ φ(h).
```

Allow identity arrows; if a *strict* continuation is required add the hypothesis that some proper continuation exists. There is no witness to install later, at **any** height already born, not just at heights in `D₀`. Even a smooth positive-dimensional real branch with points of correct size cannot change this. The two-instance joint-fillability statement for end arrows is true trivially, not an amalgamation theorem for the extensions themselves. In §§4,7,9 every missing cubic or elliptic instance above the indicated cut remains missing even under **all** end arrows, independently of the allowed edge types. R2 constructed other models ab initio; it did not extend their failed windows by end arrows. Proper extension of every member with any chosen extra properties is not assumed.

**PROVED (relative existential closure with weak arrows).** Alternatively allow all ordered inclusions `R⊆R′` of members, requiring endness only over `D₀`. Now call `φ(h)` *possible* over `R` if `R′⊨φ(h)` for some such inclusion (possibly identity), and *unfillable* if none exists. For a bounded existential block with open matrix, truth with old inputs persists upward, by retaining the witnesses; this need not hold for arbitrary bounded formulas under weak arrows. Impossibility also persists upward by transitivity. Starting from **any** member, build an increasing chain by fairly queuing all such formulas and all tuples in each stage, and at service take one possible extension realizing the instance if it exists, else do nothing. This is a set-theoretic construction, not an algorithm for deciding possibility. Countability causes no loss if using larger witnesses: take a countable elementary substructure of the witnessing ring containing the whole countable input and witnesses. The union is discretely ordered and an end extension of `D₀`; it is normal by placing the integral equation and fraction together in one stage, IOpen by compatible real closures and integer parts, Lag by retaining representations. It still has the same bounded intervals below `D₀`, hence `P` is prime if tested by irreducibility/factorizations as appropriate (see prime qualification below), and `¬ES(P)` by the sorted bounds of §3. A possible final-instance extension would already be possible at the time of service, since it extends that stage as well. Thus the union realizes **exactly all instances possible over the union**. In particular if an induction axiom fails there, its base and step hold there and some missing instance is *absolutely unfillable over the union in this category*. The converse is immediate if base/step hold and an instance is unfillable. No amalgamation, recursive saturation, quantifier elimination, or assertion that possibilities of the **starting** ring are preserved was used. The empty class of proper extensions gives no construction. This is generic only in the relative existential-closure sense, not uniqueness, homogeneity or independence of choices.

**Prime qualification and ES.** In a discretely ordered IOpen end extension even ring-theoretic primality of an old prime is preserved: by division every residue mod old `P` is old, and the old residue ring injects (if an old element is a multiple then its unique old division remainder is zero). Thus the old domain quotient remains a domain without assuming Bézout. Both the Type I and Type II positive witnesses at this `P`, indeed all triples, would have all three coordinates bounded by old polynomials and hence would be old. Changing arrows among upper stages never changes this argument over the fixed base. This freezes the failure of any old elliptic or cubic instance *in `D₀`* also with weak arrows. To preserve **failure of induction** one also needs persistence of the base and universal step; for the examples these are theorems of normal IOpen. Arbitrary universal steps need not persist.

## 12. Two individually fillable unary windows that cannot be filled together

**PROVED (weak arrows, even with normality and Lag; relative to the one-successor floor/sphere lemma `LOGIC_LAGRANGE_END_TEST.md` and the z76 bottom PID).** There are countable fixed data `C⊂_end D₀`, `P∈D₀`, with `C` a nonstandard true-arithmetic cut, and `R,R₁,R₂∈𝒦` with inclusions `R→R_i` over `D₀`, such that two parameter-free bounded-existential instances at the **same** `h∈R` are individually realizable in `R₁,R₂` but incompatible in *any* discretely ordered ring extending `R`. In particular weak-arrow possibility is not generally preserved by making a different possible choice. This example concerns all bounded-existential instances as in the proposed lemma, not specifically ones with universally valid successor step.

Start **before cut transfer** with the actual countable z76 §3 Lag + TS + QR IOpen PID as `D₀` and `F=Frac D₀`. For the common upper seed start with `F[a,b]`, two successive largest-scale positive variables. At every PID mark send both `a,b` to 1. Complete the relative floor and sphere tower as in `LOGIC_LAGRANGE_END_TEST.md` §§2–3 (no rows). Write `A` for the unpunctured upper union and `R` for its integral pullback. The algebras and their pullbacks are normal. For the **row branch**, take `A[u,v]/(au+bv−1)` with `u` a largest-scale parameter and `v=(1−au)/b`. The generic line is integral; localization at `a,b` covers the row and its two charts are normal polynomial localizations. Equivalently there is injection into the generic line by saturation (as in z75 §2). A relatively bounded element must be constant in that line. It then belongs to `A[1/a]∩A[1/b]=A` since `a` is prime and does not divide `b`; hence it is in `F`. The marks extend by `(u,v)=(1,0)` and the residue host for the order persists. Dovetail the floor/sphere completion again over `F`, obtaining `R₁∈𝒦` extending `R` with the integral row. Normality uses the same charts and flat-fibre lemma, or finite pieces followed by union. Reduce `u` mod `b` to get an equivalent row with `0≤u<b`, `|v|≤a+1` by division. The initial largest-scale row was not itself a bounded witness; this normalization supplies one.

For the **divisor branch** take an intermediate positive scale `T>F` with `T^j` smaller than every positive old field element outside `hull(F)` for every positive standard `j` (the intermediate-scale type of `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §1). In `A[T,T⁻¹]` use `A[T,a/T,b/T]`. For a negative exponent `−j` its coefficient lies in `(a,b)^j A`, so, if nonzero, cannot be a scalar in `F` and is outside the relative hull. Such terms remain outside after division by `T^j`; positive exponents with nonzero coefficient are also outside. Terms of different powers cannot cancel in relative magnitude by the intermediate-scale test on their coefficient ratios. Thus the bounded intersection is still `F` and the relative residue host is unchanged. Normality here does **not** require an associated-graded calculation at some alleged smooth point of `Spec A`. The algebra is the base change of the entire floor/sphere presentation from `F[a,b]` along `a↦T A₁,b↦T B₁` into `F[T,A₁,B₁]`: after inverting `T` this is the old Laurent algebra, and before inversion there is no `T`-torsion by flatness of `A` over the original base. Its image is exactly the displayed subalgebra. Replay the polynomial/monic sphere edges as above to get normality (generic targets stay nonzero by injection); the marks extend by `T=1, A₁=B₁=1`, compatible with *all* old marks. The next relative floor/sphere completion gives `R₂∈𝒦` extending `R` and containing `1<T` and positive integral `a/T,b/T`. Existence of such completions over the countable upper algebras uses only the bounded intersection, residue host, local lifts at the old PID places and finite pole support of the marks: the DVRs and CRT are still those of `D₀`, not of `R`. Every new target or formula uses finitely many entries, as in the cited one-successor proof. Neither branch is asserted end over `R`.

**Unary packaging.** Put `h=(a+b)^2+a`. Over any discretely ordered extension this code has at most one decoding into nonnegative `a,b`: the code lies in `[(a+b)^2,(a+b+1)^2)` and the residual part determines `a`. Both entries are bounded by `h+1`. One window decodes and asks for `au+bv=1` with signed coefficients bounded by `h+2`; the other decodes and asks for `a=tx,b=ty`, `1<t≤h+1`, `0≤x,y≤h+1`. These are fixed parameter-free `E₁` formulas. They cannot hold together by substituting the common divisor in the row; `t>1` cannot divide 1. Thus neither already held in `R`. Each alternative is possible even with Lag. Name the whole span as a **many-sorted** structure, with the two embeddings of the common `R`, the old `D₀` and the ordinary integers therein named; take a countable elementary extension with infinite element in the ordinary-integer predicate. This produces the stated nonstandard `C` and preserves all individual endness, witness and non-witness assertions, Lag, normality and ES failure. No amalgam of the two codomains was used to transfer the span. The sorted triple bounds at the old `P` apply in all three pullbacks and transfer. In particular even with the base fixed, the weak-arrow generic is not a construction realizing every initially possible request. The example uses a choice of base, not a failure theorem for every prescribed base.
