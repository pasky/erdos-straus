## 1. Which interval information to test

**PROVED (rel. the positive two-fraction identity).** Write $n=4t+1$, $q=3\bmod4$ prime, $h_q=(q+1)/4$ and $m=t+h_q=(n+q)/4$. In $G_q=\mathbb F_q^\times$ put

$$D_q(m)=\{d_1/d_2\bmod q: d_1,d_2\mid m,\ q\nmid d_1d_2\}.$$

Equivalently take signed exponents at each $r^v\Vert m$, $r\ne q$, in $[-v,v]$. If $-1\in D_q(m)$, choose the two positive divisors. The denominators $m$, $nm(d_1+d_2)/(qd_1)$ and $nm(d_1+d_2)/(qd_2)$ are positive integers and give $4/n$. This works even if $q\mid m$. For $n$ prime with $q<n$ and $n\equiv1\pmod4$, $m<n$ is prime to $n$, and the other two denominators are multiples of $n$: a Type II solution. Thus on the *entire interval* every failure lies on an arithmetic diagonal of incomplete divisor-ratio sets. This uses the actual prime factors of $m$ and a full bounded exponent box, not merely the subgroup they generate. See `EXSET_CHAR_FORCING.md` §2 for the window dictionary. Even integers $n\ge2$ are soluble (write $4/n=1/(n/2)+2/n$); for $n=4t-1\ge3$, split $4/n=1/t+1/(nt)$ into three. We may therefore treat just $n=4t+1$.

## 2. A logarithmic window, unconditionally

**Theorem W (PROVED rel. the inputs in §0).** Let $L=\log X$, $l=\log L$, $I(a)=1-a+a\log a$. For definiteness take $0.9\le\beta<\log 3$ fixed, and let $q$ be any prime in $[L^\beta,2L^\beta]$. For $X\le m<2X$ the proportion for which $D_q(m)\ne G_q$ is

$$L^{-I(\beta/\log 3)+o(1)}.\tag{1}$$

The error in the exponent is uniform in these $q$ for fixed $\beta$. In particular the *specific* $-1$ is present except on a proportion $\le L^{-I(1/\log3)+o(1)}$ throughout the band $[L,2L]$. Here $I(1/\log3)=0.00415475\ldots>0$. For $q$ in the corresponding band at fixed $\beta>\log3$, almost every $m$ has **incomplete** coverage. This last assertion does not imply that $-1$ is usually missed.

**Proof (the finite group step).** In a cyclic group of order $M=q-1$, take $k$ i.i.d. uniform group elements (not necessarily primitive roots). Give their $3^k$ signed products with exponents in $\{-1,0,1\}$ equal weight and let $P_g$ be the resulting masses. If $d$ is the missing fraction, then $d\le M\sum_g(P_g-1/M)^2$. In the squared norm, exponent pairs with an odd difference in some coordinate agree with probability $1/M$. There are $5^k$ pairs with all differences even (on a coordinate: $(0,0)$ or both nonzero), including $3^k$ identical ones. Each nonidentical all-even pair agrees with probability at most $2/M$. Consequently

$$\mathbb E d\le M3^{-k}+(5/9)^k.\tag{2}$$

The second term matters: distinct ternary vectors need **not** give pairwise independent group elements. For $s=\lceil (\beta+4)\sqrt l\rceil$ additional independent uniform elements, the extended support contains the $s$ single translates of the old support. Conditional on that support the probability of any hole is at most $M d^s$. Split at $d=e^{-\sqrt l}$ and apply (2). With a total of $j$ elements, for $s\le j\le4l$ the probability of incompleteness is at most

$$\min(1,e^{O(\sqrt l)}M3^{-j})+e^{O(\sqrt l)}(5/9)^j+O(L^{-3}).\tag{3}$$

For $j<s$ use 1. This is already **all** targets in the given group, not a hypothesis about the randomness of $-1$.

**Proof (factor exposure in one actual integer).** Fix $0<e<1-\beta/\log 3$ small. Use only primes $R<r\le Z$, where $R=\exp(L^e)$, $Z=X^{1/l^2}$. Their reciprocal mass is $H=(1-e)l-2\log l+O(1)$. By Siegel–Walfisz and partial summation, for *every* class $a\in G_q$ its mass is $H/M+O(L^{-5}/M)$: $q\ll (\log R)^{2/e}$, and the usual SW log-saving exponent can be arbitrarily large for this fixed $e$ (or use its square-root-log exponential error). There is no assertion uniform as $e\downarrow0$. Ignore integers with a repeated band prime: their proportion is $O(1/R)$ by summing $\lfloor2X/r^2\rfloor$. With $J$ distinct band primes, $\mathbb E 2^J\ll \prod(1+1/r)\ll e^H$ by expanding the divisors and counting multiples up to $2X$. Thus $J>4l$ costs $L^{-1.7}$ or better.

For an unordered tuple of $j\le4l$ distinct primes in the band let $b$ be their product. On the nonrepeated integers with exactly this band part, $m=bu$ with $u$ free of band primes. Since $b\le X^{4/l}$, Shiu's upper bound (Thm 1, modulus 1) for the multiplicative indicator of that condition gives $\ll(X/b)e^{-H}$ such integers in our interval, with an absolute constant: the band is included below $X/b$, the reciprocal prime sum in Shiu's exponent is $\log\log(2X/b)-H+O(1)$. This is a one-sided domination, not an independent distribution of the factors of an interval tuple. If the actual ratios are incomplete then the $j$ signed directions from this tuple alone do not cover. Sum over ordered tuples and divide by $j!$; adding repeats only increases this upper bound. The harmonic prime residue law differs *multiplicatively* from uniform by $1+O(L^{-5})$ in each entry, so on these products domination costs $1+o(1)$. The total upper bound is a constant times the Poisson$(H)$ mixture of (3) (with the stated cutoffs) plus the errors just removed. Only a single ordinary integer was factored at this step; no $j$-window correlations were evaluated.

Write $a=\beta/\log3$, $h=1-e$. The mixture of the first term in (3) costs $L^{-h I(a/h)+o(1)}$. For details, absorb the $e^{O(\sqrt l)}$ in a threshold $j_0=al+O(\sqrt l)$, use the Poisson lower-tail bound through $j_0$, and above it tilt by $3^{-j}$ (mean becomes $H/3$). Since $h/3<a<h$, the upper part has its largest summand at $j_0+O(1)$ and is bounded by a geometric series times that boundary term. Stirling gives the displayed exponent on both sides. The $j<s$ contribution is $L^{-h+o(1)}$; the $(5/9)^j$ contribution is $L^{-4h/9+o(1)}$. Both are negligible for $0.9\le\beta<\log3$ and sufficiently small fixed $e$. Let $e\downarrow0$ *after* taking the limsup of the log-probability exponent. For the matching lower bound on incompleteness use $|D_q(m)|\le\prod_{r^v\Vert m}(2v+1)\le3^{\Omega(m)}$. The single Sathe–Selberg layer $\Omega(m)=\lfloor\log(M)/\log3\rfloor-1$ in this dyad has proportion $L^{-I(a)+o(1)}$ (the parameter tends to $a<1$, well inside the classical $<2$ range for $\Omega$). For $\beta>\log3$ the same size obstruction uses just the normal order $\Omega(m)\sim l$ in probability. ∎

**Theorem S (specific, PROVED rel. Shiu, Sathe–Selberg and W).** Throughout the band of W, the proportion with $-1\notin D_q(m)$ is also $L^{-I(a)+o(1)}$, $a=\beta/\log3$. For $\beta>\log3$ the proportion *hitting* $-1$ tends to zero. Here size alone would say nothing about this particular target, an involution.

*Proof.* Reduce a ratio representing $-1$ to coprime positive $A,B$; then $AB\mid m$, $q\mid A+B$, $q\nmid AB$. Write $ABC=m$. When $A,B\le q^2$, the union bound costs $\ll (\log q)^2/q$ in probability: at fixed smaller label $B$ sum $1/A$ over $A\ge\max(B,q/2)$ in one class $\bmod q$ up to $q^2$; double for order and sum $1/B$. For the other pairs order $A,B$ so $A\ge B$, $A>q^2$. Uniformly at fixed $0<v<1$, the weighted sum of $v^{\Omega(ABC)}$ over these triples with $X\le ABC<2X$ is $\ll_v X L^{3v-1}/q$. Indeed on dyads of sizes $D,E,F$ for $A,B,C$, fix $B,C$ to impose the reduced class $-B$ on $A$. Shiu for $v^{\Omega}$ on that class supplies $\ll_v D(1+\log D)^{v-1}/q$ uniformly since $D>q^2/2$. On $B,C$ use the analogous unrestricted bound. Relax all further constraints, retaining $DEF\asymp X$. Summing the three log weights gives $\ll L^{3v-1}$: the largest log is $\asymp L$ and has $O(1)$ dyadic indices for fixed other two, whose weights sum to $O(L^{2v})$. The finitely many bounded dyads use the trivial estimate with an absolute constant.

Choose fixed $\alpha<a$ arbitrarily close to $a$, and $v=\alpha/3$. The proportion with a hit and $\Omega(m)\le\alpha l$ is at most
$$O_v\big((\log q)^2/q+ L^{-I(\alpha)+\alpha\log3-\beta}\big).$$
This is $o(L^{-I(\alpha)+o(1)})$ in the sense of a strict power gap since $I(\alpha)<\beta$ here. The single Sathe–Selberg layer $\lfloor\alpha l\rfloor$ costs only $L^{-I(\alpha)+o(1)}$, so almost all *on that layer* miss $-1$. Send $\alpha\uparrow a$ for the lower bound and apply W for the upper. Above $\log3$, take $1<\alpha<\min(3,\beta/\log3)$ close to 1 and discard $\Omega(m)>\alpha l$ by normal order; the displayed hit bound tends to zero. Thus a single prime window has a sharp log-exponent transition for the **true prescribed residue**. This is not a transition for ES failure. ∎

**Corollary (PROVED rel. W).** Let $t$ be uniform on the integers $X\le t<2X$, $X$ integral, $\mathcal Q=\{L<q\le2L:q=3\bmod4\text{ prime}\}$, $J=|\mathcal Q|\sim L/(2l)$. For all but $O(XL^{-I(1/\log3)/2+o(1)})$ values of $t$, all but an $O(L^{-I(1/\log3)/2+o(1)})$ fraction of these $q$ give a positive two-fraction completion at $m=t+h_q$. Sum (1) and use Markov; moving the dyadic endpoints costs $O(L/X)$ per marginal. This is for all integer arguments, **not** a relative prime-population estimate by inclusion. In the ET coordinates this actually puts witnesses in a definite large-modulus band. Cancel the divisor ratio to coprime $a,b$; $c=(a+b)/q$, $d=m/(ab)$ give $4abcd=a+b+nc$. The two oriented moduli are $4acd-1$, $4bcd-1$; their minimum equals $(n+q)(a+b)/(q\max(a,b))-1\in[n/q,2n/q+1]$. Conversely any such ET tuple with $(a+b)/c=q$ has $m=abd$ and $-1$ represented by $a/b$ if $q\nmid ab$ (automatic if $q\nmid n$ since $4m=n+q$, or if $(a,b)=1$ since $q\mid a+b$). In particular for a successful band window at $q\asymp\log n$ a *specific-class* Type II modulus of order $n/\log n$ really occurs on the row. This uses the full $4acd-1$ family, not necessarily the $d=1$ slice. The two fractions were integral before any averaging. The corollary improves the *single-window input* in `EXSET_CHAR_FORCING.md` §4.2: at $q\asymp\log X$ one now has a log-power failure envelope even without imposing a negative Legendre symbol. The issue is a growing number of different shifts, not a shortage of separately useful windows.

**Theorem WP (PROVED rel. W and the fixed-dimension upper-bound sieve).** In the $q$-band of W with $q=3\bmod4$, among primes $4t+1$ with $X\le t<2X$, the proportion missing even **complete** ratio coverage at $t+h_q$ is $\le L^{-I(\beta/\log3)+o(1)}$. Thus the Corollary holds relatively among these primes with the same Markov exponents: almost all have positive Type II solutions at almost all $q$ in the logarithmic band, with the indicated physical modulus $\asymp n/\log n$. Different $q$ mean different first denominators, not necessarily different trial periods. This does not follow by dividing W by the prime density. `FORCED_WINDOW.md` Thm 8.5 has a different strength (every prime window in smaller bands at most of the input primes); WP does not assert that simultaneous conclusion here. *Proof.* The factor exposure needs just an upper bound for the number of $u$ with $m=bu$ in the dyad (translated by $h_q$), no band prime dividing $u$, and $4bu-q$ prime. This bound is $\ll X e^{-H}/(bL)$, uniformly for all the retained $b$ of W. For clarity use the classical fixed-dimension upper-bound form of Brun's sieve on this **one cofactor**, not on any ES witness classes. Take a fixed sufficiently small $\kappa>0$, sieve all primes $p\le X^\kappa$ against $4bu=q\ (p)$ and all band primes also against $u=0\ (p)$ ($Z<X^\kappa$ eventually). There are $\nu(p)\le2$ deleted residues, never all residues: at 2 there is no deletion, $q<R$, and $(4b,q)=1$. For $2<p\nmid b$ there is a single deletion outside the band and two in the band, since there $p\ne q$; for $p\mid b$ only the band deletion remains. The dimension bound $\prod_{w\le p\le z}(1-\nu(p)/p)^{-1}\ll (\log z/\log w)^2$ is uniform by Mertens. At level $D=X^{1/3}$ all intersection counts have error $\le2^{\omega(d)}$ on this cofactor interval. The dimension-two upper-bound sieve therefore costs interval length times $\prod(1-\nu(p)/p)$ up to an absolute constant, plus $D\log^{O(1)}D$ (the fixed-dimensional sieve remainder weights cost at most a fixed divisor power; take $\kappa$ with $\log D/\log(X^\kappa)$ sufficiently large and fixed). By Mertens the product is $\ll L^{-1}\exp(-H+O(\sum_{p\mid b}1/p))\ll L^{-1}e^{-H}$ since $j\le4l$ and $p>R$ at $p\mid b$. The remainder is negligible since $b\le X^{4/l}$. This accounts for the prime condition *before* dividing by the prime count $\asymp X/L$ (fixed-modulus PNT). Repeats and $j>4l$ cost at most $L$ times W's unconditional tails, still negligible; a translation by $O(L^2)$ costs negligible endpoint errors. Apply the identical group calculation and mixture upper bound. No lower Sathe–Selberg layer count for shifted primes is assumed. ∎

## 3. A specified high-dimensional restriction, with sharp scale of loss

**CONDITIONAL on the rank-one diagonal estimate (DR) below.** Use the integer cyclic group $\mathbb Z/X$ with representatives $[X,2X)$ and set $F_q(x)=1[-1\notin D_q(x)]$ on these representatives. This asks about the specified involution at that window, not ES. Alternatively one may use the stronger test $1[D_q(x)\ne G_q]$ throughout (a target-blind sufficient condition with the same sharp marginal exponent). Put $I_*=I(1/\log3)$, choose a fixed $0<\sigma<I_*$, and write $z=L^\sigma$, $G_q^*(x)=1+(z-1)F_q(x)$. Their means $m_q=1+(z-1)L^{-I_*+o(1)}$ satisfy $\sum_q\log m_q=o(J)$ by S (by W for the all-target test). The ordinary product mean is $\prod m_q=X^{o(1)}$. For the specified offsets $h_q$ define $K(h)=X^{-1}\sum_x\prod_q G_q^*(x+h_q)/\prod_q m_q$. Our explicit **diagonal restriction hypothesis** is that, for some fixed $0\le\lambda<\sigma/2$, all sufficiently large $X$ have

$$K(h)\ll X^\lambda.\tag{DR}$$

This is a positive tensor of *univariate arithmetic functions*, restricted to a particular translated diagonal in $(\mathbb Z/X)^J$. Equivalently, with $\widehat g(k)=X^{-1}\sum_xg(x)e(-kx/X)$, the signed hyperplane sum

$$\sum_{\sum k_q=0\ (X),\ \mathbf k\ne0}\ \prod_q \frac{\widehat G_q^*(k_q)}{m_q}\ e\!\left(\frac{\sum_q k_q (q+1)}{4X}\right)\tag{4}$$

is $\ll X^\lambda$ in absolute value (or just from above). Orthogonality gives exactly $K(h)-1$. No denominators of *products* of $J$ witness moduli have been assumed accessible. For independent, uniform *offsets* $h$ the mean of $K(h)$ is exactly 1 by Fubini, so $K(h)>X^\lambda$ has proportion at most $X^{-\lambda}$ for $\lambda>0$. In (DR) the offset vector is not random. All simultaneous translates of it have exactly the same $K$; replacing its slope by an average over arbitrary offsets is invalid. The smoothing $1+(z-1)F_q$ charges rows with only *some* holes as well; it is not the joint indicator of ES failure.

**Proof of the conditional consequence.** An actual $n=4t+1$ failure contributes $z^J$ to the unnormalised product unless its offsets wrap at the upper endpoint, which costs $O(L)$ possible $t$, not $O(L)$ per tuple. Therefore

$$E(8X+1)-E(4X)\ \ll\ L+ X^{1-\sigma/2+o(1)} K(h),\tag{5}$$

using the elementary soluble classes and ignoring at most constant endpoint errors. In particular (DR) gives $E(N)\ll_\delta N^{1-\delta}$ for every $0<\delta<\sigma/2-\lambda$, by dyadic summation. For the subpower target it suffices that $\log K(h)\le J\log z-\sum\log m_q-c L^\theta$ for some $\theta>3/4$; here retain the *exact* $J$, not just its asymptotic. These are one-sided restrictions on an interval. They allow exceptional primes at arbitrarily large heights, even failure of all window certificates there. They assert neither (★) at prescribed primes nor uniform lower bounds for $R(p)$. Conversely, pointwise positivity using some *other* windows need not bound this tensor; we do not assert formal implication from a pointwise B2 conjecture for a different family. (DR) is an explicit, unproved input, not new in kind: the $C^k$-multiplicativity contemplated in `EXSET_CHAR_FORCING.md` Calculation 4.2, if available in the corresponding event family uniformly through $k=J$, gives (DR) with any fixed $\lambda>0$. We need only the *single scalar sum*, permitting polynomial losses smaller than the specified exponent, not that stronger uniformity. W/S provide the missing log-power envelope at this band. **Calibration (PROVED rel. the fixed-$A$ version of W/S justified here and PNT; ES consequences CONDITIONAL on the stated interval estimates).** The subpower criterion following (5) can also be made with no growing tilt. A claim merely $K=X^{o(1)}$ for this **fixed** weight would be useless: it already holds trivially since $2^J=X^{o(1)}$.

The mixed estimate is purpose-built, not a standard analytic conjecture; neither its growing-dimensional part nor the needed lower-tail Laplace cancellation on actual primes is proved here.

**Sharpness of the exponent budget (PROVED for offset-correlation data, not ES).** At fixed coordinate marginals $u=L^{-I_*+o(1)}$ one can mix a mass $v=X^{-d}$ on the all-one Boolean row with mass $1-v$ of independent coordinates of parameter $(u-v)/(1-v)$. For any fixed $d>0$ this is a probability law eventually. Its unnormalised tensor mean is $v z^J+(1-v)(1+(z-1)(u-v)/(1-v))^J$. Dividing by $(1+(z-1)u)^J=X^{o(1)}$ gives the exponent $\max(0,\sigma/2-d)+o(1)$ for $\log K/\log X$. This explains the threshold $\sigma/2$, and the unit coefficient of $\lambda$ in (5), for this scalar statistic. The law is neither [1,X] nor an arithmetic counterexample. One can likewise place an atom of size $\exp(-C L^{3/4})$; it has the top exponent $\sigma/2$. A two-cell variance estimate cannot distinguish the required exponentially small atom from zero at inverse-log precision.

## 4. Two concrete limits on other routes

**Theorem P (PROVED rel. Shiu, Mertens, SW, Turán–Kubilius and W's group lemma; a phase boundary on actual integer rows).** Let $0<b<\log3$, $q\in[L^b,2L^b]$ with $q=3\bmod4$ prime, and retain the notation $h_q$. Give a witness at this window its *certificate divisor* $d_q\mid t+h_q$ with $-1\in D_q(d_q)$; one may always take the product of the two coprime ratio labels. Allow choosing both the windows and the certificates **after seeing the integer**, subject only to $\prod d_q\le X^C$ for fixed $C>0$. Write $b_*=\log3/(1+\log3)=0.52349\ldots$.

* For fixed $b>b_*$, the maximum fraction of the band certifiable this way tends to **zero in probability**. This is for the single target $-1$.
* For fixed $b<b_*$, it tends to **one in probability**, even demanding $D_q(d_q)=G_q$ at the selected windows and $\prod d_q=X^{o(1)}$. The boundary at equality is not settled.

*Upper side.* Pick $0<u<1$ with $u>1-b$ and $u\log3<b$. Set $Y=\exp(L^u)$. At most $C L^{1-u}=o(L^b/l)$ certificates can exceed $Y$. For a single window with a certificate $d_q\le Y$ there must be $A,B$ as in S with $AB\le Y$. The pairs $A,B\le q^2$ again cost $O((\log q)^2/q)$. Let $K=\Omega_{\le Y}(m)$ count prime factors with multiplicity, $m=t+h_q$. The population $K>(u+\epsilon)l$ is $o(X)$ uniformly: its mean is $u l+O(1)$ and its mean square deviation is $O(l)$ on comparable dyads by Turán–Kubilius (the standard prime-power form). For $K\le(u+\epsilon)l$ weight a hit by $v^{K-(u+\epsilon)l}\ge1$ at $v=1/3$. At fixed $A,B$, the remaining cofactor $c=m/(AB)$ contributes at most $\ll X(\log Y)^{v-1}/(AB)$ with its weight $v^{\Omega_{\le Y}(c)}$: Shiu on a length comparable to $X/(AB)\ge X/Y$, and $Y<X/Y$. All primes in $A,B$ are $\le Y$. For $q^2<A\le Y$, $B\le Y$ the dyadic argument of S, now with reciprocal weights on two *independently* bounded variables, gives $\sum_{A,B}v^{\Omega(AB)}/(AB)\ll (\log Y)^{2v}/q$ on $q\mid A+B$, $q\nmid AB$. Thus the number of small-certificate hits divided by $X$, after the atypical-$K$ and short-label costs, is $\ll L^{u(3v-1)-(u+\epsilon)\log v-b}$. At $v=1/3$ the exponent is negative for small $\epsilon$. Average the $o(1)$ over the actual shifted windows, then apply Markov. No independence across $q$ is assumed. This improves the crude $3^K$ size obstruction for full bases to the **actual class**.

*Lower side.* Choose $b/\log3<u<1-b$ and $0<e<u-b/\log3$. Repeat the one-integer proof of W with band $\exp(L^e)<r\le\exp(L^u)$, harmonic mass $(u-e)l+O(1)$ and $M\asymp L^b$. With $j\le4l$ the entire band divisor has log at most $4l L^u=o(L)$ on nonrepeated rows. The (one-sided) Poisson mixture now gives incompleteness $o(1)$: below $((u-e)-\epsilon)l$ use its lower tail; above that apply (3), with $\beta$ replaced by $b$, taking $((u-e)-\epsilon)\log3>b$. Repeats and the $j>4l$ tail remain negligible. Averaging in $t$ shows $1-o(1)$ of the windows work in probability. The product of the chosen divisors has log $\ll (L^b/l) l L^u=o(L)$. ∎

**Radical strengthening (PROVED rel. P, Mertens and the private-prime observation).** P's upper side still holds charging only $\operatorname{rad}(\operatorname{lcm}_q d_q)\le X^C$. Put $T=4L^b$; each prime $p>T$ divides at most one $t+h_q$, because the shifts are distinct and closer together than $T$. Except at an $o(1)$ fraction of all pairs $(t,q)$, no square $p^2$, $p>T$, divides this window (count multiples up to $3X$ and sum $O(X/T)$ per $q$). At the remaining windows the rough parts of $d_q$ are squarefree and pairwise coprime, with total log $\le C L$. At most $C L^{1-u}$ have rough log $> L^u$; at all the others the **entire** $d_q$ is $\le\exp(2 L^u)$. The single-class bound in P still gives $o(1)$ at this cutoff (the factor 2 changes $\log\log Y$ by $O(1)$ only). P's lower side already has a small product, so the same strict phase boundary holds under the radical/lcm tariff. No requirement to find *which* small certificate to use is placed on the selection rule. ∎

## 6. Round 2: joint misses at actual shifts

**Theorem J (PROVED rel. SW, Mertens and elementary factor exposure, the fixed-dimensional upper sieve on primes, and Henriot and Shiu for the fixed-$k$ matching lower bound).** At any fixed $k$ distinct prime offsets from W, the proportion of $X\le t<2X$ with **all** prescribed involutions missed is $L^{-kI(a)+o_k(1)}$, $a=\beta/\log3$. The very same exponent holds for joint *incompleteness*, uniformly in the offset choices. For growing $k$ there is an explicit upper-bound tradeoff. Write $k=L^{\kappa+o(1)}$, $h=1-\kappa$. If $0.9\le\beta<\log3$ and $0\le\kappa<1-a$, the joint proportion is at most
$$\exp\{-[hI(a/h)-o(1)]k l\}.\tag{6}$$
In particular the full marginal exponent per window survives for all $k=L^{o(1)}$. For any fixed $0<\beta<\log3$, $\kappa<1-\beta/\log3$, the same proof gives $\exp(-c_{\beta,\kappa}k l)$, without claiming that precise rate function over the extended range. We always require enough distinct $q$ in the band. The estimates apply to absence of the specific $-1$, not merely absence of quadratic nonresidues.

*One interval coupling with a growing number of translated integers.* Fix $0<e<h$, and take band primes $R=\exp(L^e)<r\le Z=\exp(L/(Bkl))$, with $B$ sufficiently large absolute. Then $H=\sum 1/r=(h-e+o(1))l$. Record at $r$ either the index $i$ for which $r\mid t+h_{q_i}$, or no index. Since $r$ exceeds the diameter of the shift set, these categories are mutually exclusive (this is **not** an assumption of independence among the integers). Compare with a model independent over $r$, with probability $1/r$ per index. Test any $[0,1]$-valued function of this marked set. Its multilinear inclusion expansion on subsets of marked hits has coefficients bounded by $2^j$ at degree $j$, by Boolean Möbius inversion (extend arbitrarily in $[0,1]$ on impossible configurations). Truncate through degree $s=\lceil Ckl\rceil$. At a row with $d\ge s$ hits the truncation and true test differ by at most $(1+3^s){d\choose s}$, since ${d\choose j}\le {d\choose s}{s\choose j}$ for $j\le s\le d$; below $s$ they agree. For both laws the $s$-th binomial moment is $\le(kH)^s/s!+(kZ)^s/X$: a compatible monomial fixes one class by the elementary division algorithm and CRT, an incompatible one always vanishes. The errors for transferring the polynomial are at most $(1+2kZ)^{s+1}/X$. Choose e.g. $C=200$, $B=10^5$. For $k=L^{\kappa+o(1)}$, $\kappa<1$, we have $s\log(2k)=o(L)$, and the whole comparison costs $O(e^{-10kl}+X^{-1/2})$. This is for a **one-interval exposure of divisors**, not a signed estimate on the full $J$-tuple beyond its product cost. It does not bypass LM.

*The law actually used in the retained band.* For an exact marked configuration its model probability is $\prod_r(1-k/r)\prod_{r\text{ marked}}1/(r-k)$. Drop configurations with $>s$ marks at cost $e^{-10kl}$ by the same binomial bound. For the others replace $1/(r-k)$ by $r^{-1}$ at total relative cost $\exp(O(sk/R))=1+o(1)$, retaining $\prod(1-k/r)\le e^{-kH}$. For an upper bound on joint incompleteness one can now drop the disjointness condition between the $k$ prime lists, and sum lists as in W (ordered lists divided by each list length factorial). This gives at most $(1+o(1))$ times the product of the $k$ independent Poisson$(H)$ mixtures of the group miss probabilities, plus the additive transfer error. Each prime gives just *one* direction: no squareful-row deletion with a large additive error is needed. SW as in W makes the harmonic class probabilities multiplicatively $1+O(L^{-5})$ times uniform, costing $1+o(1)$ on at most $s$ marks. After transferring the marks to uniform residues, extend the list sums to all lengths. The group proof (2)–(3) works for larger list lengths too, using the first $j-s_0$ directions and $s_0=\lceil(\beta+4)\sqrt l\rceil$ reserve translates (here $s_0$ is not the truncation degree $s$).

For $0.9\le\beta$ and $h-e>a$, one has $(h-e)/3<a<h-e$; the Poisson tilt in W costs $(h-e) I(a/(h-e))l$, with the other terms negligible. Let $e\downarrow0$ after the limit. For smaller positive $\beta$ select any fixed $a<a'<h-e$, split at $j=a'l$, and (3) and the Poisson lower tail each give some fixed power saving. If $k\to\infty$ the corresponding *upper* log-rates also hold relatively among input primes merely by division by their density ($L=e^{o(kl)}$).

*Prime transfer at fixed $k$ without signed overlap.* Use a direct one-sided computation. At a fixed list of $j\le s$ marked primes, count rows with exactly these marks and all other band primes unmarked **and** $4t+1$ prime. On the pinned progression (modulus $b\le Z^s\le X^{.01}$) use the ordinary fixed-dimension $k+1$ upper sieve. At primes not dividing $b$ delete the $k$ mark roots if in the band and one extra prime root if $p\le X^{\epsilon_k}$ odd. All roots are distinct where both rules apply; $Z<X^{\epsilon_k}$ eventually. At pinned primes no additional deletion is necessary. Dimension $k+1$ is fixed here, with uniformly bounded sieve constant for that $k$ since band primes exceed $R$. *Matching lower bound for fixed $k$, on integers, not just in the independent model.* We give details since joint rarity of numbers with few factors does not by itself imply that they miss this target. Let $m_i=t+h_i$, $h_i=(q_i+1)/4$. An input used below is the following consequence of Henriot, *Nair–Tenenbaum bounds uniform with respect to the discriminant*, **2014 erratum, New Thms 5 and 6**. For fixed $k$, fixed real $0<u_j\le1$, distinct nonproportional primitive linear forms of polynomially bounded height on a dyad of variable length $T\ge X^c$ ($c>0$ fixed),
$$\sum_{x\asymp T}\prod_{j=1}^k u_j^{\Omega(|P_j(x)|)}\ll T L^{\sum_j(u_j-1)+o_k(1)}.\tag{7}$$
Here use ordinary dyads bounded away from 0, disregard possible zero values, and the $o$ can depend on all fixed parameters. Polynomial contents may be stripped for this upper bound. For the forms $m_i$, on a suitable fixed-modulus subprogression (depending on offsets), the corresponding lower bound $\gg X L^{\sum(u_j-1)-o_k(1)}$ holds. Indeed the hypotheses on $\|\prod P_j\|$ in New Thm 5 hold with a sufficiently small fixed height exponent $\delta$ and fixed dimension. The bounded multiplicative weight belongs to $\mathcal M_k(1,1,\epsilon)$; for New Thm 6 it is bounded below by $(\min u_j)^{\Omega(\prod n_i)}$. Away from primes dividing the leading coefficients or pairwise resultants, the product of the local sieve factor and local sum is $1+(\sum(u_j-1))/p+O_k(p^{-2})$ by the $k$ disjoint roots. At bad primes the New Thm 5 local sum is at most $1+k/p$: the nonconstant terms of its normalized *exact*-valuation densities are disjoint, and their union requires one primitive linear root modulo $p$ (erratum (0.1), including zero valuations in the other coordinates). The product over bad primes of $(1+O_k(1/p))$ for the upper bound and its reciprocal for the lower bound is $L^{o_k(1)}$, since their product divides a nonzero integer of size $X^{O_k(1)}$ and $\sum_{p\mid d}1/p=O(1+\log l)$ there. For New Thm 6 use the constant term of its exceptional local sum for the lower bound, and $p>k$ in its outer sieve product. To ensure no fixed prime divisor select $t\bmod 2^D$ avoiding $-h_i$ there, $2^D>k$, and strip the fixed powers of 2. At every odd $p\le k$ the residue $4t=-1\pmod p$ avoids all $m_i$ as $q_i>k$; primes $>k$ cannot cover the line. The stripped dyad loses only a $k$-dependent constant. This establishes exactly the lower estimate used here.

Tilt by $\prod_i\alpha^{\Omega(m_i)}$ with fixed $\alpha<a$ close to $a$. The resulting finite measure (normalized by $X$) has mass $L^{k(\alpha-1)+o(1)}$. Under its probability normalization, with probability $1-o(1)$ all $|\Omega(m_i)/l-\alpha|<\varepsilon$: for either tail tilt just that coordinate by $e^{\pm\tau}$, $\alpha e^\tau<1$, and (7) gives Chernoff exponent $\alpha(e^{\pm\tau}-1)\mp\tau(\alpha\pm\varepsilon)<0$ at small fixed $\tau>0$. Otherwise set $v=\alpha/3$ at this coordinate. The total $v^{\Omega(m_i)}\prod_{j\ne i}\alpha^{\Omega(m_j)}$ mass of marked triples with $A>q_i^2$, divided by $X$, is
$$\ll L^{3v-1+(k-1)(\alpha-1)+o(1)}/q_i.\tag{8}$$
On the dyads of S use (7) in the **largest** variable (so length $\gg X^{1/3}$ before progression). If $C$ is largest, fix $A,B$, use (7) then Shiu on the reduced class $A=-B\ (q_i)$ in summing $A>q_i^2$. If $A$ is largest put $A=q_i s-B_0$ with $B_0\equiv B\pmod{q_i}$ in $[0,q_i)$, and use (7) in $s\asymp A/q_i$. Here the forms are $A,BC A+h_j-h_i$; they are pairwise nonproportional by distinct shifts. Likewise in $C$; in either orientation contents only help, height is polynomial in $X$, and $\log T\asymp L$. The other two independent dyadic weights sum as in S. This gives (8), including the $1/q_i$ density loss in either orientation. To restore the original tilt on the box multiply by at most $3^{(\alpha+\varepsilon)l}$. Relative to the tilted mass the exponent is $(\alpha+\varepsilon)\log3-\beta<0$ for small $\varepsilon$. Sending $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, then $\alpha\uparrow a$, gives $L^{-kI(a)-o_k(1)}$ on actual joint specific misses. ∎

## 7. What multiplicative correlation would suffice?

**PROVED (finite Fourier inversion).** Let $N_q(m)$ count ordered pairs of divisors prime to $q$ with ratio $-1$ in $G_q$. Then
$$N_q(m)=\frac1{q-1}\sum_{\chi\ (q)}\chi(-1) f_\chi(m),\qquad f_\chi(m)=\Big|\sum_{d\mid m}\chi(d)\Big|^2.$$
Each $f_\chi$ is nonnegative multiplicative, with $f_\chi(p^a)=|1+\chi(p)+\cdots+\chi(p)^a|^2$, in particular $1$ at $p=q$. The **zero indicator** is not multiplicative: e.g. $q=11$, coprime $m=3,7$ separately miss but $3\cdot7=-1\ (11)$. Bounds for the positive correlations of $f_\chi$ do not survive signed subtraction as a lower bound for $N_q$. The $\chi$ are neither all nonpretentious nor are these $f_\chi$ 1-bounded. Known log-averaged correlations for nonpretentious multiplicative functions and shift-averaged correlations in short intervals do not supply a uniform unweighted growing-dimensional estimate of these products on the prescribed shifts. **Assessment:** no implication from Matomäki–Radziwiłł, Chowla or EH to the requested exceptional exponent established here. The fixed-$k$ miss power savings of J are unconditional but use factor exposure, not an estimate of the raw signed correlations of the $N_q$.

**CONDITIONAL on the following growing-dimensional shifted Shiu upper bound (M), not a named conjecture.** One can get $E(N)\ll N\exp[-c(\log N)^{0.751}]$ with *no witness count or specified target in the hypothesis*. This trades (DR)'s power-saving scale for a slightly super-$3/4$ exponent. It is not claimed that a bound for a specific tensor implies the general hypothesis or conversely. For $\beta=.751$, choose the complete prime band $L^\beta<q\le2L^\beta$, $q=3\ (4)$, of size $k\sim L^\beta/(2\beta l)$. Let $e=10^{-8}$, $\mathcal B=(\exp(L^e),X]$ (primes only). At each shift $h_i=(q_i+1)/4$ take a nonnegative multiplicative function $f_i$ equal on prime powers to $f_i(p)\in[0,3]$ and equal to 1 off $\mathcal B$. Put $M(f_i)=\exp\sum_{p\in\mathcal B}(f_i(p)-1)/p$. Write $\mathcal T_i$ for the finite list of scalar/function pairs $(c,f)$ in items 1–3 below. The primary hypothesis is the single summed comparison, with e.g. $\eta=10^{-7}$:
$$X^{-1}\sum_{X\le t<2X}\prod_i\sum_{(c,f)\in\mathcal T_i}c\min(L^2,f(t+h_i))\le e^{\eta kl}\prod_i\sum_{(c,f)\in\mathcal T_i}cM(f).\tag{M}$$
A cleaner but stronger variant, $(M_{\rm unif})$, asks that for every one of the stated bounded prime weights per coordinate, the mean of $\prod_i\min(L^2,f_i(t+h_i))$ be $\le e^{o(kl)}\prod_i M(f_i)$. Positive expansion implies (M). No arbitrary-function uniformity is required in the result. All small-prime and shared-prime singular factors have been removed: the prime supports exceed the shift diameter. No asymptotic, cancellation against Möbius, or lower bound is requested. The clipping is important: a single huge divisor product is harmless for a void. This is a high-dimensional **upper** correlation problem for truncated multiplicative functions, not a consequence of fixed-dimensional Shiu/Henriot. The change from J is the aggregate harmonic mass tested: the factors now extend to $X$ instead of $\exp(L/(Bkl))$. No full-law coupling to that height is presumed; only the stated capped positive correlation is needed. The permitted log error in (M) is numerically very small.

*Proof (improve the one-window Fourier loss before multiplying).* Write $J_i=\omega_{\mathcal B}(t+h_i)$, $\alpha=.999$, $h=1-e$. At each of the $J_i$ distinct factors independently draw exponent $0,+1,-1$ with probabilities $.4,.3,.3$. Each product is an actual divisor ratio. Its normalized Fourier factor is $.4+.6\Re\chi(p)$; write $g_\chi(p)=|.4+.6\Re\chi(p)|$. A miss implies $\sum_{\chi\ne1}\prod g_\chi(p)\ge1$ by inversion. The continuous mean of $|.4+.6\cos\theta|$ is $\mu=.4(2\theta_0/\pi-1)+1.2\sin\theta_0/\pi=0.47052864\ldots$, $\theta_0=\arccos(-2/3)$. By the Lipschitz Riemann-sum estimate, for all orders $d\ge10^5$ the discrete mean is $<.4707$. For $2\le d<10^5$ it is $\le\sqrt{.4^2+.6^2}<.73$ by Cauchy, and there are at most $10^{10}$ such characters per prime. SW and partial summation yield each discrete mean times $hl+O(1)$ in harmonic sum, with $o(1)$ discrepancy, uniformly in characters (including order 2). Use three sorts of nonnegative majorant terms per coordinate, each a scalar times a multiplicative function on the band:

1. Charge $J_i<\alpha l$ by $w^{-\alpha l}w^{J_i}$ with $w=\alpha/h<1$; coefficient times $M(f)$ equals $L^{-h I(\alpha/h)+o(1)}$.

3. For order $\ge10^5$ on $J_i\ge\alpha l$, charge by $z^{-\alpha l}\prod zg_\chi$ at $z=2.12$. Summing the scalar times $M(f)$ costs at most $L^{\beta-\alpha\log z+h(.4707z-1)+o(1)}$. The exponent is $<-.0017$ (in particular negative); this small margin is why the choice of ternary probabilities matters.

In each term $c f$ we may replace $f$ by $\min(L^2,f)$ without losing the majorization by their sum: whenever a term is shortened its remaining value is already at least 1 since $c L^2\ge1$. Use this positive product majorant in (M). Summing the coefficients times Euler means gives $L^{-c_0+o(1)}$ per shift with $c_0=hI(\alpha/h)\simeq5.0\cdot10^{-7}$ (the smallest of the three rates). Hence the $\exp(10^{-7}kl)$ loss is absorbed; a per-window log loss as small as $10^{-6}l$ would already exhaust this budget. Since $kl\asymp L^{.751}$ the asserted integer bound follows. ∎
