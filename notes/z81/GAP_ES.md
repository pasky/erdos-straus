## 2. What simultaneous seeded misses cost

**PROVED (rel. finite cyclic groups and z80 `EXTREME.md` §6 upper sieve).** Fix a QNR seed b and any J distinct **prime** levels k=3 (4) in the class −p mod 4b, larger p understood.     ≪ X (log log X)^{O_K(1)} / (log X)^{1 + Σ_k(1−1/(2 r_min(k)))} ,

where r_min(k) is the least prime divisor of h. The implied constants can depend on the fixed levels. This sharpens the fixed-seed exponent assessment in `POINTWISE_SIZE.md` §9 (not the prediction that the exponent is exactly 1).

*Proof.* The seeded factor b is a nonsquare in the cyclic group G of order 2h, h odd. Let H be generated by residue classes occurring at least |G| times in x. At a miss the product over all windows costs at most z^{-Σ(k−1)²} in majorizing the indicator. ## 3. Exactly what fails at 3 and 7

**PROVED (elementary).** For prime p≡1 (4), k<p, set S=px. The window succeeds iff −1 is a ratio of divisors of S mod k, with all exponent differences in [−vℓ(S),vℓ(S)]. At k=3 failure means all factors of S are 1 mod 3, equivalently all factors of x are: p≡4x. For k=7, count prime-factor **occurrences of S, including p**. Failure is equivalent to:

* no 6 mod 7 factor, and
* either no factors in {3,5}, or only one or two such occurrences and **no** factors in {2,4}.

## 6. Two zero-fit exponent-budget forecasts

**EVIDENCE (out-of-population tests; not a blind discovery exercise).** The observed answers were supplied in the task. No probability or coefficient was estimated from prime outcomes. We separate a composite-control population model with real factors from an analytic letter model with fictitious factors: neither is a certified prime probability.

**Controls.** `design.md` describes the composite engine; it is not a separately committed preregistration. No numerical outcome was used to optimize a parameter. The architectures, clipping and roughness rules are modeling choices. In each hard residue and height bin sample *composites only*, with least prime factor >min(123,log(upper endpoint)), excluding nonunits for both levels of an adjacent pair. Replace the centre by one formal generator, but keep the **complete actual factorization** and ordinary divisor set of each consecutive side. Ordinary divisor residues intersecting their negatives give the exact exponent-box predicate with that formal centre; this is intentionally *not* the full ES box for the composite. Multiply the disjoint adjacent both-miss fractions within the same residue, ending with a single miss probability, then average the six residues. This preserves all prime-size, valuation and subgroup effects and small-prime collisions *within* a pair, not across pairs except for the 840 fibre. It also admits centres divisible by ℓ between the roughness cutoff and 123 outside the pair's unit tests: the baseline local probability ℓ|x_k there is approximately 1/ℓ, not the prime-centre 1/(ℓ−1) (removing prime centres is a further finite bias). For cumulative counts since 10⁶ use four stratified blocks of width ≤2²⁰ per bin and integrate dx/(32 log x). The bulk panel instead uses the entire control interval. See `bulk_pairs.txt`, `forecast.txt` for denominators and complete results; no claims for early records from bins with sparse controls.

**Letters.** A second, entirely synthetic model includes correlations **across all windows**. At height X let t=log X, y=max(7,⌊(log t)³⌋). Draw one uniform hard class and independent local Haar units P_ℓ conditional on that class. For each k=4j−1, take their exact s_j=∏_{ℓ≤y}ℓ^{vℓ((P_ℓ+k)/4)} (at odd ℓ divide by the local unit 4); use their CRT residue P mod k. Put H=log(X/(4s_j)), λ=max(0,log(H/log y)) when H>0, else 0. The count of large formal primes is a Poisson(λ) clipped to [1,max(1,⌊H/log y⌋)]. Their residues are independent uniform units mod k **except** the last, which enforces the exact total (P/4)/s_j. Add P once and compute the full signed box. Unknown letters at different windows are independent only **conditional** on the shared local data. This is a finite-size Sathe–Selberg surrogate, not an assertion that conditioned large primes have this law. It ignores repeated primes above y, and always introduces at least one cofactor letter, even if there is no admissible integer of that size. This modeling convention is potentially material near records. No fit or added late Poisson ES rescue. For finite integration of very small probabilities use a sequential particle filter: draw missing CRT digits and large letters, retain misses, multiply by the survival fraction and uniformly resample survivors; 12 runs of 8192 particles. It estimates a nested survival law (linear expectation propagation by induction); the bootstrap does not certify rare-event probabilities. Monte Carlo standard errors in `letters.txt` exclude both discretization and model errors.

### Asymptotic scale *inside the analytic model*

**PROVED (relative to the specified random law only).** Put u=1/log 3, I=1−u+u log u≈0.00415475. For J∼c t/log t the modeled probability of J leading misses is exp(−(I+o(1))J log t). With independent Poisson input locations of intensity dx/(32 log x), this gives a modeled running maximum of **(4/I+o_P(1))log X/log log X** (4/I≈963), not log X. To specify a finite maximum truncate the prefix length at ⌈(log x)²⌉ at each location; right censoring is **not** declaring success there. No statement of this order, let alone this implausibly large asymptotic coefficient in the measured range, is proved for real primes.

*Uniform random box bound.* In a finite abelian G and n independent uniform letters, count nonzero exponent vectors in {−1,0,1}ⁿ hitting −1. Mean (3ⁿ−1)/|G|. Distinct nonempty supports give independent values (some 2×2 minor of the exponent matrix is ±1). Same support gives at most 2·3ⁿ diagonal/opposite pairs, and ≤5ⁿ pairs altogether; if not opposite/equal there is a minor ±2 with determinant-map kernel size |G[2]| (use a unit coefficient to eliminate one variable). Chebyshev gives miss probability ≤min(1,O(|G|3^{−n}+|G[2]|(5/9)ⁿ)) for n≥1. This uses actual bounded subset products, **not** 3ⁿ independent shots.

*Upper bound.* With local data fixed, all but π(y) of the first J windows have s_j≤J^{π(y)}: above the first ℓ-power >J at most one j can vanish for each small ℓ, by consecutiveness. Thus H=t−o(t), λ=log t+o(log t), and the upper count clip ≫t/log y for all other windows. Keep the d−1 independent letters, dropping the forced last letter and all small factors, for an upper bound on misses. Uniformly for J/(log t)²≤j≤J we have |G|=φ(k)=t^{1+o(1)}, |G[2]|≤2^{ω(k)}=t^{o(1)}. For N∼Poisson(λ), d₀=log |G|/log 3=(u+o(1))log t, Stirling/ratio bounds give Pr(N≤d₀)=t^{−I+o(1)}. In |G| E[3^{−N};N>d₀] the ratios beyond d₀ are ≤λ/[3(d₀+1)]<1 bounded away from one; the first term is t^{−I+o(1)} or smaller. E[(5/9)^N]=exp(−4λ/9), negligible even with the 2-torsion factor. Poisson upper-clip tail is superpolynomially small in t; the d−1 offset only costs constants. Multiplication on the retained J−o(J) windows gives the stated upper. Constants in this argument are at a *moving* k in a random group-box experiment, not z80 H with constants uniformized.

*Lower bound.* With probability exp(−O(J)) all CRT units through 4J (including the 840 data) are 1 modulo the first power of each such prime exceeding 16J; indeed the log modulus cost is O(π(4J) log J)=O(J). On that event P=1 mod k, s_j is exactly the y-smooth part of j because (1+k)/4=j. Every factor of j is Jacobi-positive at k. If there is just one cofactor letter its residue is j/s_j, so the window certainly misses. If there are d letters and their product is prescribed, any nonconstant vector of signed exponents has, after eliminating the last one, a nonzero coefficient ±1 or ±2 and hence point mass ≤|G[2]|/|G|. Constant vectors give only the Jacobi-positive deterministic factors. With the small letters there are ≤τ(j²)3^d candidate ratios (P=1). For j≥J/(log t)² choose d=⌊(1−η)log |G|/log 3⌋, fixed small η>0. Since τ(j²)|G[2]|=t^{o(1)}, a union bound gives conditional miss ≥1−o(1) there. For this d away from both clips the Poisson mass is t^{−I_η+o(1)}, I_η=1−(1−η)u+(1−η)u log((1−η)u). Earlier windows may use N=0 at cost at most exp(O(log t)) per window in inverse probability. Conditional independence, then η↓0, proves the logarithmic equivalence. This coherent squarelike CRT fibre costs exp(−O(J)), subleading versus exp(−I J log t), and does not condition the actual p to be a square.

*Extremes.* For thresholds (1±δ)t/(I log t) of missed windows, the mean number past the lower threshold on (X/2,X] diverges exponentially and its Poisson void probability tends to zero. The upper-threshold mean at smaller heights x=e^s is bounded by integrating O(exp(−δs/2)) from any sufficiently large fixed s₀, since s/log s eventually increases. Below s₀ the right-censored maxima are bounded. Let s₀→∞. This proves the in-probability model statement; the copious real dependencies not encoded by the independent formal large letters are precisely why it is not an ES conjecture with this constant.
