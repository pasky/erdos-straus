## 2. What simultaneous seeded misses cost

**PROVED (rel. finite cyclic groups and z80 `EXTREME.md` §6 upper sieve).** Fix a QNR seed b and any J distinct **prime** levels k=3 (4) in the class −p mod 4b, larger p understood. *Proof.* The seeded factor b is a nonsquare in the cyclic group G of order 2h, h odd. Let H be generated by residue classes occurring at least |G| times in x. ## 3. Exactly what fails at 3 and 7

**PROVED (elementary).** For prime p≡1 (4), k<p, set S=px. The window succeeds iff −1 is a ratio of divisors of S mod k, with all exponent differences in [−vℓ(S),vℓ(S)]. At k=3 failure means all factors of S are 1 mod 3, equivalently all factors of x are: p≡4x. For k=7, count prime-factor **occurrences of S, including p**. Failure is equivalent to:

* no 6 mod 7 factor, and
* either no factors in {3,5}, or only one or two such occurrences and **no** factors in {2,4}.

**PROVED (relative to the specified random law only).** Put u=1/log 3, I=1−u+u log u≈0.00415475. For J∼c t/log t the modeled probability of J leading misses is exp(−(I+o(1))J log t). *Uniform random box bound.* In a finite abelian G and n independent uniform letters, count nonzero exponent vectors in {−1,0,1}ⁿ hitting −1. Mean (3ⁿ−1)/|G|. Distinct nonempty supports give independent values (some 2×2 minor of the exponent matrix is ±1). Same support gives at most 2·3ⁿ diagonal/opposite pairs, and ≤5ⁿ pairs altogether; if not opposite/equal there is a minor ±2 with determinant-map kernel size |G[2]| (use a unit coefficient to eliminate one variable). *Upper bound.* With local data fixed, all but π(y) of the first J windows have s_j≤J^{π(y)}: above the first ℓ-power >J at most one j can vanish for each small ℓ, by consecutiveness. Thus H=t−o(t), λ=log t+o(log t), and the upper count clip ≫t/log y for all other windows. Keep the d−1 independent letters, dropping the forced last letter and all small factors, for an upper bound on misses. Uniformly for J/(log t)²≤j≤J we have |G|=φ(k)=t^{1+o(1)}, |G[2]|≤2^{ω(k)}=t^{o(1)}. For N∼Poisson(λ), d₀=log |G|/log 3=(u+o(1))log t, Stirling/ratio bounds give Pr(N≤d₀)=t^{−I+o(1)}. In |G| E[3^{−N};N>d₀] the ratios beyond d₀ are ≤λ/[3(d₀+1)]<1 bounded away from one; the first term is t^{−I+o(1)} or smaller. E[(5/9)^N]=exp(−4λ/9), negligible even with the 2-torsion factor. *Interval comparison lemma (PROVED, elementary).* Use n≡1 (4) uniform in the dyad before imposing primality. Mark a window at each band prime R<r≤Z if r|(n+k)/4. Assume R>2K, so at r at most one window of the prefix is marked. Compare to independent uniform CRT digits n mod r. For any m specified windows and any [0,1]-valued function F of their allocation of marks, if D≥1 the expected values in the two laws differ by at most

    O(3^{D+1}(e m H/(D+1))^{D+1} + (C m Z)^{D+2}/X),    H=Σ_{R<r≤Z}1/r.

To see this, index labels by pairs (r,k). For a configuration S, expand F(S) by Möbius inversion over subsets, with coefficients bounded by 2^{|T|}; evaluation on incompatible subsets may be extended arbitrarily into [0,1]. Truncate after degree D. The error vanishes when |S|≤D; otherwise its absolute value is ≤(1+3^{D+1}) binom(|S|,D+1), by comparing each j-subset with its (D+1)-supersets. A specified compatible T has probability ∏_{(r,k)∈T}1/r in the CRT law, and differs by O(1/X) in the interval (one progression, including n≡1 mod 4). Incompatible T always costs zero. Thus the factorial moment of order D+1 is ≤(mH)^{D+1}/(D+1)! plus O((mZ)^{D+1}/X) in the interval. Comparing the means of the polynomial itself termwise costs at most O((4mZ)^{D+1}/X); combining the two bounds gives the displayed error with an absolute C (e.g. 20). There is no averaging over a vector of offsets.

*Dependence and group estimates (PROVED).* For one r its indicators at different windows are negatively associated: increasing functions of disjoint sets of coordinates cannot simultaneously receive nonzero increments above their all-zero values. Independence over r preserves negative association: condition successively on independent rows for two increasing tests on disjoint coordinate sets, and apply the same inequality to their conditional means. The same covariance inequality holds for two decreasing tests by negation. By induction products of positive monotone tests on disjoint windows are bounded in expectation by products of their means (all tests increasing or all decreasing). Independent thinning coins can be included. This statement is for the CRT comparison law only.

For d independent uniform directions in G=(ℤ/k)× the number of nonzero signed exponent vectors hitting −1 has mean (3^d−1)/|G|. As in §6, pairs with different supports have an invertible 2×2 minor; identical/opposite pairs contribute O(3^d/|G|) to the variance, other same-support pairs O(5^d |G[2]|/|G|²). Consequently if d=⌈log K/log 3⌉, K/2≤k≤K→∞, the hit probability is bounded below by an absolute constant, by the second moment inequality: |G|≤K and |G[2]|≤2^{ω(k)}=K^{o(1)}. Composite k are useful here: there are ≍K windows rather than ≍K/log K. This is about an actual signed box, not independent shots or the group generated by its directions.

*Harmonic directions.* In the CRT law for one window the band part is Bernoulli per prime with probability 1/r, independently. A d-element subset T has probability ∏(1−1/r)∏_{r∈T}(r−1)^{-1}; the first factor is exp(−H+O(1/R)). Equivalently for events on these subsets one can use d iid primes of mass proportional to 1/(r−1), discard repeats, and multiply by exp(−H+O(1/R))(Σ1/(r−1))^d/d!. For d=O(l) the iid repeat probability is O(d²/(R H)) when H≫1. If each residue class mod k has harmonic mass (H+O(l^{-2}))/φ(k), the iid residue law for those d directions has likelihood ratio 1+o(1) to uniform when H≍l (replacing 1/r by 1/(r−1) changes any class sum by O(1/R)).

For part 1 take K=L^β, β=1 or log 3, R=L^{20}, log Z=L^u, with u=1/(e log 3) or 1/3 respectively. Here H=u l−log l+O(1).     ≥ c L^{−u I(t)} l^{1/2−t} .

*Rare-hit transfer.* Thin each retained hit with a fixed independent coin so that in the CRT law its retained probability is μ/J, J the number of retained levels. **PROVED (algebraic obstruction, not a sieve impossibility theorem).** Use the necessary binary norm identities p+3=A²+3B² and p+7=C²+7D² on simultaneous confinement (the primitive norm forms of discriminants −3,−7 have class number 1; multiply by 4). This forgets some local/primitivity conditions, so showing that an algorithm reaches the quadric would not alone suffice. With h=C−A the h≠0 chart has three free *rational* variables (h,B,D),

    A=(4+3B²−7D²−h²)/(2h),   C=A+h,   p=A²+3B²−3.

At h=0 the equation is 7D²−3B²=4 (Pell type), not a second independent linear direction. The h-dependent denominator is genuine: modulo h the numerator of 4h²p is (4+3B²−7D²)², not the zero polynomial. Its degree-four homogeneous part even equals N_{ℚ(√−3,√−7)/ℚ}(h+B√−3+D√−7); the *polynomial itself* has lower-degree corrections, and when h is a nonunit at an integral point one needs a prime **quotient**, not prime numerator.

For **every nonzero rational fixed h**, the resulting bivariate polynomial P_h(B,D) is absolutely irreducible. Its zero fibre over ℂ, before eliminating A by the displayed linear relation, is B²=(3−A²)/3, D²=(7−(A+h)²)/7. The two quadratics have simple, disjoint root sets (equality would require rational h=±√3±√7). Thus their squareclasses in ℂ(A) are independent. The coordinate ring is free of rank four over ℂ[A] by the two monic equations; its localization is a field, hence it is a domain. Eliminating A gives the asserted irreducibility. It cannot equal on the full chart a nonconstant field norm of polynomial arguments over a field of degree ≥2, since the conjugate factors of such a norm are nonconstant over ℂ. This rules out direct identification with x²+y⁴, x³+2y³, binary norms at prime coordinates or Maynard's incomplete norm. Restricting the resulting variables further need not preserve absolute irreducibility, but would require a prime theorem on that restriction.

**Converse obstruction for full families (PROVED, rel. Lang–Weil for the local consequence).** For any product P of d *distinct* affine-linear factors over ℂ whose hyperplane intersection graph is connected, P+c is absolutely irreducible for nonzero c. If P+c=FG nontrivially, on each hyperplane FG=c, so F is a nonzero constant there (polynomial units). These constants agree across intersections. Their common value a makes F−a divisible by P, impossible at deg F<d. In Maynard 1507.05080v2 Thms 1.1–1.2 the embeddings have distinct primitive-element coefficients (power-basis variables 1,ω,…), hence pairwise nonparallel hyperplanes whenever there are ≥2 such coordinates. The argument also works for affine sections preserving these hypotheses. Thus neither P+3 nor P+7 can be a polynomial norm identity there. More generally by good reduction and Lang–Weil such a hypersurface has residue points mod every sufficiently large prime ℓ, including primes inert at the attempted shift. A full-dimensional parameter progression therefore cannot confine that shift on *all* its values. Compare `Z55_P415.md` §3 for the homogeneous generic-plane proof on the 4,15 pair. For Friedlander–Iwaniec, x²+y⁴+c is absolutely irreducible for c≠0 since −(y⁴+c) has simple roots and is not a square in ℂ(y). Heath-Brown's binary cubic and Green–Sawhney's norm at prime coordinates are not claims about two translated norm fibres. No theorem here about confinement just at the prime outputs or about all nonlinear reparametrizations.
