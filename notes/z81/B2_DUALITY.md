# Can two different windows repair one another?

## 1. A sign constraint on actual alternating returns

Use the ET ratio windows (`LONGSHOT_PIGEONHOLE.md` Lemma 1.1), not a character proxy. For an odd prime $p=1\bmod4$, let $X_c=(p+c)/4$, $Y_q=(pq+1)/4$, with levels $3\bmod4$. At a visited II (resp. I) level test for two divisors of $X_c$ (resp. $Y_q$) opposite modulo the level. Dividing them by their common factor gives coprime witnesses when the level is a unit on the side integer. From II take prime factors of $X_c$ as new I levels; from I take prime factors of $Y_q$ as new II levels. The mod-4 restriction really matters: a nonresidue need not be an eligible successor. We tested both all eligible prime factors and nonresidues only. No heuristic assertion that the latter alternates *quadratic signs* is warranted.

**A (PROVED, quadratic reciprocity).** A two-cycle of distinct odd **prime** levels $c,q=3\bmod4$, both prime to $p$, has opposite $p$-characters. Indeed $q\mid p+c$ gives $(q/p)=(-c/q)=(q/c)$; $c\mid pq+1$ gives $(c/p)=(-q^{-1}/c)=(c/q)$. Their product is $-1$. A self-loop would imply $q\mid p$ from the first relation, impossible in the domain. Hence a walk confined to QNR primes cannot close a two-cycle. The proof works with Jacobi symbols for coprime odd composite levels and $n=1\bmod4$ prime to both; the two arrows imply coprimality. In particular at an odd square there are no two-cycles of these unit levels even without any prime or size filter. A two-cycle is not itself claimed to be an ES certificate.

## 2. Moving on the original cubic: two different composition laws

Write $h=n/M>0$, $S:\sum1/x_i=1/h$ on the nonzero chart. For a triple $P$ put $d(P)=[-x_1x_2x_3/h]\in\mathbb Q^*/\mathbb Q^{*2}$ and $\alpha(P)=(-x_1/x_3,-x_2/x_3)\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb Q)[2]$ (quaternion symbol, group law additive). For a direction $V$ with no zero entries use the same homogeneous formula for $\alpha(V)$, **not** evaluation of a smooth-locus Brauer class at a point of the surface at infinity.

**C (PROVED, elementary for $d$, rel. diagonal quadratic forms and the symbol identities for $\alpha$).** On a proper line $P(s)=A+sV$ with three finite rational intersections $P_1,P_2,P_3$ off the axes, counted with multiplicity,

$$d(P_1)d(P_2)d(P_3)=1.\tag{1}$$

For **distinct** intersections there is also a direction-dependent correction:

$$\alpha(P_1)+\alpha(P_2)+\alpha(P_3)=\alpha(V).\tag{2}$$

All $V_i$ are nonzero since the restriction has cubic degree. To prove (1), put $a_i=-A_i/V_i$. The monic intersection cubic is $g(s)=\prod_i(s-a_i)-h\sum_i V_i^{-1}\prod_{j\ne i}(s-a_j)$. The product of the $i$th coordinates at its three roots is $-V_i^3g(a_i)=hV_i^2\prod_{j\ne i}(a_i-a_j)$. Multiply: the three unordered pairs give three minus signs times a square. This also proves the tangent sign assertion for finite nonzero residual intersections. In particular closure of *one-negative* seeds under such chords/tangents and permutations stays one-negative: no positive rational output there, not merely a failure of integrality. This strengthens the real three-distinct-points calculation `BRAINSTORM/graph_cayley_moves.md` A.1 on this chart. Two-negative signed points are **not** in that closure.

Proof of (2). The matrix $F(s)=\operatorname{diag}(P(s))-h\mathbf1\mathbf1^t$ is singular at each root $s_i$, with kernel vector $w_i=(1/P(s_i)_j)_j$, whose coordinate sum is $1/h$. These are eigenvectors of $\operatorname{diag}(V)^{-1}F(0)$ with eigenvalues $-s_i$, hence a basis, orthogonal for $\operatorname{diag}(V)$ by symmetry. Write $\lambda_i=w_i^t\operatorname{diag}(V)w_i\ne0$. In this basis $F(s_i)$ has the two nonzero entries $\lambda_j(s_i-s_j),\lambda_k(s_i-s_k)$. On the plane of coordinate sum zero this rank-two form coincides with $\operatorname{diag}(P_i)$ restricted there; the plane complements the radical. For $\operatorname{diag}(P_i)$ the orthogonal complement to that plane is $w_i$, of norm $1/h$. Thus this ternary form is equivalent to $\langle h,\lambda_j(s_i-s_j),\lambda_k(s_i-s_k)\rangle$. Its even Clifford class is $\alpha(P_i)$: for $\langle a,b,c\rangle$ it is $(-ac,-bc)$, invariant under common scaling.

For clarity write $H_j=-h\lambda_j$, $d_{ij}=s_i-s_j$, and denote the quaternion by braces. Each summand on the left of (2) is $\{H_j d_{ij},H_k d_{ik}\}$. On expanding their sum, the constant terms give $\sum_{j<k}\{H_j,H_k\}$, the mixed terms $\sum_j\{H_j,-1\}$ since $d_{ik}d_{ki}$ is minus a square, and the pure difference terms $\{-1,-1\}$. For the latter put $d=s_1-s_2$, $e=s_2-s_3$, $f=d+e$: the discrepancy from $\{-1,-1\}$ is $\{d,e\}+\{-de,f\}=0$ by Steinberg $\{d/f,e/f\}=0$. The sum is exactly the even Clifford class of $\langle H_1,H_2,H_3\rangle$, a scalar multiple of the form with matrix $\operatorname{diag}(V)$ in the $w$-basis. This proves (2).

**Corollary (PROVED rel. C, Bright–Loughran Lemmas 3.1,3.5,3.8 and Hilbert reciprocity).** A proper chord through two signed integral points and one positive integral point at $M=4,n=p$ odd prime, with distinct finite points off the axes, must have a definite direction (all coordinates of the primitive integral direction have the same sign), and the direction quaternion is ramified **precisely** at $p,\infty$. Indeed at infinity the positive point alone has nonzero invariant. At every finite $\ell\ne p$ all three points have trivial evaluation; at $p$ use reciprocity. In particular $p\mid V_1V_2V_3$ in the primitive direction (two $p$-units define a split Hilbert symbol at odd $p$ if both entries are units). Modulo $p$ the direction has a zero coordinate, and also its unit parts/valuations must give the nonsplit symbol. For $M=8$ doubling the coordinates gives a point at $M=4$ with unchanged $\alpha$. This is a necessary direction test, not a blanket obstruction to mixed chords at primes.

## 3. Transverse weighted sum involutions and rank on a sterile fibre

**D (PROVED, elementary; the orbit description itself is tautological).** Use projective denominator coordinates $[x:y:z]$: the unique normalized triple is $(n/M)(\sum 1/x_i)(x_i)_i$ when the sum is nonzero. On the weighted sum pencil $(\sum1/x_i)(\sum a_ix_i)=N$ the reciprocal map $I_a:[x_i]\mapsto[1/(a_ix_i)]$ is a fibrewise involution for nonzero weights. Two transverse such involutions compose to $[x_i]\mapsto[(a_i/b_i)x_i]$. Thus the subgroup generated by **all** signed $S$-unit weight pencils and coordinate permutations acts by diagonal $S$-units, possibly followed by inversion and permutation. Conversely every such transformation is a composition. Here actions are birational (projective torus); no claim of integral *intermediate* points. For rational projective data the orbit test is to remove all factors in $S$ and the signs in each coordinate of an integral representative, sort, and divide by the gcd; then identify this primitive triple with its lcm times its reciprocal. Necessity follows from common rescaling at places outside $S$; conversely equality of these normalized triples gives a common rational scalar times independent signed $S$-units. This explicitly permits sign- and level-changing jumps, more permissively than requiring the intermediate orbit integral.

**CERTIFIED (finite DATA, not a general barrier).** At both 2521 and 3361, **no** signed integral point has a positive integral point in its orbit even for $S=\{2,q\}$, $q=11$ the least nonresidue, or for $S=\{2,3,5,7\}$. For $S$ all primes through 19, respectively 5 of 9 and 11 of 15 positive points *are* accessible projectively. At 409, already $S=\{2,7\}$ reaches 7 of 15; the test uses all signed starting points, both sign sectors. In particular the added nonsquare weight is genuinely useful but not sufficient. **Postmortem:** bounded-support transverse inversion does not supply denominator control; taking all rational weights instead is transitive by diagonal rescaling and proves nothing.

**E (PROVED rel. Mazur's rational torsion theorem).** On the ordinary sum pencil $C_N:(\sum x)(\sum 1/x)=N$, $N\notin\{0,1,9\}$ rational, a rational point with nonzero coordinates is torsion, with origin $O=[1:0:0]$, **iff** two projective coordinates coincide or one is the geometric mean up to sign of the other two ($x_i^2=x_jx_k$). In particular **every positive integral solution at a prime $p=1\bmod4$ is a non-torsion point on its sum fibre**. This settles the all-prime P3 question of `BRAINSTORM/elliptic_fibration_es.md` (repository upgrade only). Probable prior torsion analysis: Bremner–Guy–Nowakowski, *Which integers are representable as the product of the sum of three integers with the sum of their reciprocals?*, Math. Comp. (1993), DOI 10.1090/S0025-5718-1993-1189516-5. Comparison of their precise torsion statements with E is owed; no external priority claim.

Indeed the projective cubic $(x+y+z)(xy+yz+zx)=Nxyz$ is smooth: on the torus a critical point of the scale-invariant function has $x_i^2=(\sum x)/(\sum1/x)$ for all $i$ when $N\ne0$; this gives $N=1,9$. The six points on the coordinate boundary (vertices and permutations of $[0:1:-1]$) are smooth by their first partials for $N\ne0$. The three-cycle has no fixed point over the algebraic closure (the three eigenlines would give $N=9$ or 0), so is translation by a rational 3-torsion point: an automorphism is translation times an origin-fixing automorphism, and a nonzero endomorphism $1-f$ is surjective. Inversion $I:[x:y:z]\mapsto[1/x:1/y:1/z]$ extends on this smooth complete curve, exchanges $O$ with $[0:1:-1]$ (tangent at $O$ is $y+z=0$), and similarly for the other vertices. A fixed torus point has all squares equal, excluded. Thus $I$ is translation of order 2. Their commuting translations generate a cyclic six-group $H$, exactly the six boundary points as translates of $O$. Swapping $y,z$ fixes $O$ and is negation (nontrivial origin-fixing involution in characteristic zero). Mazur gives $[E(\mathbb Q)_{tors}:H]\le2$. Thus $P$ is torsion iff $2P\in H$, i.e. iff $P$ is fixed by some translate of this transposition by $H$. Those maps are the three transpositions and their compositions with inversion, precisely the two displayed tests.

For the integral application, geometric progression implies after ordering $(x,y,z)=s(a^2,ab,b^2)$ for coprime $a,b$ and integer $s>0$. Then $Ms a^2b^2=p(a^2+ab+b^2)$, with the trinomial prime to $ab$, forcing $(ab)^2\mid p$, so $a=b=1$. Repeated entries are impossible at $M=4,p=1\bmod4$: writing $4/p=2/x+1/z$ gives $(2x-p)2z=px$. If $p\nmid x$ then $2x-p\mid p$, hence $2x-p=1$, forcing $x=(p+1)/2$ odd and $2z=px$ odd. If $x=pk$ then $(2k-1)2z=pk$ forces $2k-1=1$ or $p$; both give a nonintegral $z$. Strict AM–HM gives $N>9$.

**F (PROVED rel. E).** At **each prime $p=1\bmod4$, $p\ge13$**, there is an explicit smooth ordinary sum fibre with a signed integral point of infinite order, a positive real oval, **and no positive integral point at all**. Put $a=(p-1)/4$, $L=pa$. The signed point $P=(a,L(L-1),1-L)$ satisfies $4/p=1/a-1/L$. It is neither repeated nor a geometric progression (that would require $(L-1)^2=aL(L-1)$). Its sum $s=a+(L-1)^2$ has $N=4s/p>9$, so E gives infinite order. Normalizing positive coordinates to sum 1, $\sum1/x_i=N$ is a compact oval (strictly convex function with minimum 9 and divergence at the triangle boundary). It is disjoint from the boundary torsion points. As a smooth real elliptic curve has at most two components, the signed point and $H$ lie in the identity component: the subgroup $\mathbb Z P+H$ does not meet that oval. No assertion about division points or all rational points.

There is also an integral rather than component obstruction here. For a sorted positive solution, $3\le c=4x-p\le2p$, $S=px=p(p+c)/4$. The positive divisor split gives $y+z=(2S+D+S^2/D)/c\le(S+1)^2/c$. Thus the sum is at most $B(c)=(p+c)/4+[p(p+c)/4+1]^2/c$. This is convex in $c>0$ (linear plus positive constant$/c$), hence bounded by its two endpoint values. The differences $s-B(3)=(p^4-6p^3-19p^2-8)/24$ and $s-B(2p)=(2p^5-13p^4-14p^3-24p^2+24p-16)/(32p)$ are positive for $p\ge13$ by bounding the lower powers with $p\ge13$. This proves sterility of the *individual* positive-rank fibre, not of $S_p$. **Postmortem:** this one signed seed selects the wrong sum: it lies beyond the entire positive integral search range. This size argument says nothing about rank-based methods on other fibres.

### A fibre with an explicit jump to *positive rationals*, but none to positive integers

**H (PROVED rel. Bright–Loughran, Hilbert reciprocity and Nagell–Lutz).** For each prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$, the fixed-$p$ weighted sum plane

$$x+y-z=s_0=p(p^2+3)/4$$

cuts $S_p$ in a genus-one curve with a signed integral point, an explicit positive rational point of **exact joint denominator $p^2-1$**, and infinitely many positive rational points. There are **no** positive integral points on this curve. More precisely, given any finite set of primes, there are infinitely many positive rational points integral there; yet at every positive rational point with **odd** joint denominator the reduced denominator of $z$ is **3 mod 4**. In particular no odd square joint denominator works on this fibre, no matter how large. Unlike F's out-of-range ordinary sum, this is an arithmetic obstruction.

*The forbidden linear combination.* For any positive integral solution of $4/p$, at least one but not all coordinates vanish mod $p$ (cubic equation, then $4=\sum 1/k_i\le3$ if all are multiples). Let $w_i$ be $p$-units with all ratios quadratic residues at $p$. Then $p\nmid\sum w_i x_i$. This is immediate with a single unit coordinate. With exactly two unit coordinates $x,y$ and $p\mid z$, the equation forces $v_p(z)=1$. By (finite-place constancy and reciprocity as in C) $\alpha_p(P)=-1$ in Hilbert sign notation. By permuting the symmetric ternary form its symbol is $(-x/y,-z/y)_p=(-x/y\mid p)$, where the last expression is Legendre. If $w_xx+w_yy=0\bmod p$, then $-x/y=w_y/w_x\bmod p$ is a square, contradiction. In particular every positive integral point has $p$ as the **reduced denominator** of its ordinary sum parameter $N=4(x+y+z)/p$. The choice $w=(1,1,-1)$ satisfies the same hypothesis since $(-1/p)=1$.

*The sign-changing map.* Put $a=(p-1)/2$, $b=(p+1)/2$, $D=ab=(p^2-1)/4$. The point $P=(a,b,-pD)$ has reciprocal sum $(p^2-1)/(pD)=4/p$ and $x+y-z=p(1+D)=s_0$. On this weighted fibre $I_w$ of D sends it to positive projective coordinates $[1/a:1/b:1/(pD)]$. The normalized point is $Q=(p s_0/4)(1/a,1/b,1/(pD))$. The last entry is $p(1+D)/(4D)$, in lowest terms since $p$ is coprime to $D$ and $1+D$ odd. The other denominators divide $4D$, proving exact joint denominator. The symmetric construction for numerator $M$ would require both $2(p-1)/M$ and $2(p+1)/M$ integral, hence $M\mid4$: it does not give this signed integral seed for $M=8$. In particular the derivation does not assume a positive integral seed.

*Infinite order.* In projective denominator coordinates the pencil parameter is $N=4s_0/p=p^2+3$. Work on $z=1$, put $s=x+y$, $t=xy$, $T=(N+1)/s-1$. The equation $(s-1)(s+t)=Nt$ gives $t=s(s-1)/(N+1-s)$. With $X=4T$ and $Y=4(N+1)T(x-y)/s$, one obtains

$$Y^2=X\bigl(X^2+(N^2-2N+5)X-16N\bigr).$$

This is birational: $s=(N+1)/(1+X/4)$, recover $x-y$ from $Y$, then $x,y$. For $N>0$ the quadratic factor has two distinct real roots and nonzero constant, hence the model is nonsingular. The projective plane cubic is irreducible since the indicated open is birational to the elliptic curve and no excluded line is a component (coordinate lines and $s=0,N+1$ by substitution); it is therefore smooth by arithmetic genus one. After normalizing $P$ to $z=1$ we have $s=-1/D$, $(x-y)/s=-1/p$. Its image $(-p^2N,pN(N+1))$ has double with

$$X(2P)=(p^4-p^2+4)^2/(4p^2),$$

by the tangent formula. This has $p$ in its denominator. For a direct application of Nagell–Lutz, move $Y^2=X^3+A X^2+B X$ to an integral short model by $X'=9X+3A$, $Y'=27Y$; the denominator at $p\ge5$ persists. Therefore $P$ has infinite order. The weighted inversion is a fixed-point-free involution: it exchanges boundary vertices with the opposite edge points (all three weights nonzero), and a torus fixed point would require $1/(w_ix_i)=k x_i$ for all $i$, hence $N=(\pm1\pm1\pm i)^2\ne p^2+3$. So it is translation by rational two-torsion on the elliptic curve. Thus $Q$ too has infinite order. Its $X$-coordinate is $16/p^2$ (here $x+y=p^2$ in the $z=1$ chart). Thus $Q$ lies on the real identity component: the three real roots of the Weierstrass cubic are negative, zero and positive, and $X>0$ on the curve puts the point on the unbounded branch. The real multiples of $2P$ are dense in this component, a circle, since an infinite cyclic subgroup of a circle is dense.

*Keeping any finite set of places integral.* At the Weierstrass identity the weighted projective chart limits to $[0:0:1]$: $s=O(1/X)$ and $x-y=sY/((N+1)X)=O(Y/X^2)$, both vanish there. The weighted-plane normalization is $s_0(x,y,1)/(x+y-1)$, regular at this identity with value $(0,0,-s_0)$. Its three functions are therefore integral on a neighbourhood of the identity over every $\mathbb Q_\ell$. For a prescribed finite set $S$, choose a compact open subgroup in each such neighbourhood (the local elliptic formal group suffices); these subgroups have finite index by compactness. Some fixed positive multiple $m$ of $2$ then has *every* multiple of $mP$ in these subgroups at every $\ell\in S$. Their real points are still dense in the component containing $Q$; infinitely many therefore lie on its open positive arc. These points are simultaneously integral on $S$; the argument does not assert a point integral on all primes at once.

*Which primes must supply the denominator?* Let $K$ be the lcm of the three reduced denominators of any such positive rational point and suppose $K$ odd. Hilbert signs of $\alpha$ at infinity and 2 are respectively $-1,+1$ (the latter by 2-integrality and BL). At $p$, put $m=\min v_p(x_i)$. If $m\ge1$, divide all coordinates by $p$, obtaining an integral $p$-adic point on the good-prime model $4/1$; $\alpha$ is homogeneous and hence split there (BL Lemma 3.5). If $m<1$ then at least two coordinates have valuation $m$, because the weighted sum is $0\bmod p$. If all three do, both symbol entries are units. If exactly two do, $-x_i/x_j=w_j/w_i\bmod p$ is a square unit, again split. Thus always $\alpha_p=+1$. At an odd $\ell\ne p$ not dividing $K$ use good-prime constancy. For $\ell\mid K$, write $d=v_\ell(K)>0$. At least two coordinates have valuation $-d$ since the weighted sum is integral. If all three did, the reciprocal sum would be divisible by $\ell$, contrary to $4/p$ being a unit. The remaining coordinate must therefore have valuation zero. In the symbol presentation with the first entry the negative ratio of the two minimal coordinates, that entry reduces to $w_j/w_i=\pm1$, and the second entry has valuation $d$. The sign is $-1$ exactly when the two weights are opposite, $\ell=3\bmod4$ and $d$ is odd. The opposite-weight pair is exactly the case in which $z$ is one of the minimal coordinates; in that case $v_\ell(\operatorname{den}z)=d$, otherwise it is zero. Since $p=1\bmod4$ contributes trivially to the mod-4 character, reciprocity gives $\operatorname{den}z=3\bmod4$. Thus for every finite $S$ containing 2, infinitely many positive rational points are integral on $S$, and **each** has an odd-exponent prime $\ell=3\bmod4$ dividing $\operatorname{den}z$ outside $S$. This concerns joint denominators on this plane, not the unrestricted capped-third-denominator relaxation. **Postmortem:** the sign change and positive rank are real, but within this pencil no amount of rational addition or denominator descent can land on the positive integral locus, by the linear-combination test. No obstruction on a different weighted sum is asserted.

## 4. Rational middle letters: a uniform rigidity theorem, not a descent in disguise

**G (PROVED, elementary).** On $p=uvt-u-v$ allow rational $t$. The autonomous Lyness map $(u,v)\mapsto(v,(p+v)/u)$ preserves $K=(u+1)(v+1)t$ by substitution (already `Z69_IDEAA.md` idea 8). A nonintegral $K$ forbids any integral word on the *entire fibre*, regardless of intermediate denominators. More strongly, start at **any** prescribed positive integral outer pair $(E,B)$, $H=\max(E,B)$. When $p>30000000 H^9$, every positive **all-integral** word on this $K$ is, up to reversal, the seed or one of its two immediate Lyness neighbours, whether or not the orbit was allowed to move arbitrarily on the invariant curve. The seed and neighbours must still be tested for integrality and the 4-slot. This is fixed-$p$ and unrelated to varying the Type II parameter $c$ in z76.

Proof. Put $V=EB,W=(E+1)(B+1),L=E+B+1=W-V$. Equality of the two invariants for an integral word, with $u\le v$, gives

$$t[Luv-V(u+v+1)]=W(u+v-E-B).$$

If $u+v\le E+B$, $u\le2H$. Otherwise the bracket is positive and at most $W(u+v)$, giving $Lu\le 2(V+W)+V/v$, hence $u\ll H$ (in fact $u\le8(H+1)$). Put $A=Lu-V$, $D=Av-V(u+1)$, $R=WL(u-E)(u-B)$. Then $D(At-W)=R$. If $u=E$, the equation factors into $v=B$ or $t=(B+1)/E$, the latter giving $v=(p+E)/B$; likewise for $u=B$. An identical argument applies to $v$. If $A=0$, positivity forces $u+v<E+B$ and $t\le W(E+B)/(V(u+1))\ll H$, so $p\ll H^3$. Otherwise outside the seed and neighbours, $R,D\ne0$, $1\le|D|\le|R|\ll H^5$. The two reconstructed factors satisfy

$$tv=\frac{(W+R/D)(D+V(u+1))}{A^2}\ll H^8$$

by expansion. Explicitly $W\le4H^2,L\le3H,V\le H^2,u\le 8(H+1)\le16H$ (for positive RHS, $Lu\le 2(V+W)+V/v\le5V+2L$). Thus $|R|\le3072H^5$, $V(u+1)\le17H^3$. With $1\le|D|\le |R|$, the four terms in the expanded numerator after taking absolute values give $tv\le(4\cdot3072+4\cdot17+3072+3072\cdot17)H^8$. Multiplying by $u$ gives the advertised constant with room. In the $A=0$ branch $u+v<2H+1,t\le2HW/V\le8H$. We use $u\le v$ throughout by outer reversal. This is a bound on *all* integral points with integral middle on this rationally pinned pencil, not a count of small iterations. Note the branch condition: $2^{v_2(b)+2}\mid ebt$ means $4\mid et$, **stronger in general than $4\mid ebt$**. Symmetry of the equation does not make that oriented condition symmetric; a good word may also use $4\mid tu-1,tv-1$ (`DEDEKIND_ES.md` Thm 4.5). No unqualified equivalence to ES is used.

**Sharp exponent for the unrestricted rigidity bound (PROVED; not a prime-values claim).** Set $k\ge3$, $u=k^2-2$, $E=2u+1-k$, $B=2u+1+k$. Then $A=1$. Take $D=1$, $v=1+V(u+1)$, $t=W+R$. Both are positive integers and substitution proves equality of $K$ at $n=tuv-u-v$. Neither endpoint is a seed letter; $H\asymp u$, $v\asymp H^3$, $t\asymp H^5$, hence $n\asymp H^9$. This is distinct from merely bounding every word height by some polynomial in $p$. The examples need not be ES certificates or prime inputs. **Postmortem:** descending a rational middle denominator by walking on a fixed Lyness fibre cannot beat the two immediate divisibility tests from any fixed seed at large $p$; the failure is quantitative even for growing seeds. Changing the fibre independently of a known word is not ruled out.
