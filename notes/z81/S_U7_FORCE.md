## 4. What an arbitrarily large joint class menu can see

**Theorem B (PROVED rel. imaginary ring class field theory and Chebotarev).** Let L be the compositum of *any finite number* of ring class fields of imaginary quadratic orders, with arbitrary conductors and arbitrary base fields; subfields of this compositum are allowed as data. If instead these are Hilbert class fields of the maximal orders, every abelian subextension has exponent dividing **2**. More precisely the exponent-2 bound is already valid for the inertia image at each odd prime not dividing any order conductor, even in a mixed menu. *Proof of the 4-bound.* Put G=Gal(L/ℚ), embedded in the product of generalized dihedral groups Gᵢ=Aᵢ⋊⟨cᵢ⟩, where Aᵢ is the ring class group and actual complex conjugation c on L restricts to cᵢ, acting by inversion (Cox, *Primes of the Form x²+ny²*, Lemma 9.3). For any g∈G, **g² is in every rotation group** upon projection. Therefore c g² c⁻¹=g⁻² coordinate by coordinate, and g⁴ is a commutator. This bounds the *joint* abelianization and works without any disjointness or rank assumptions. It is not the incorrect assertion that exponent two is preserved in a subdirect product.

*Proof of the 2-bound.* If l is unramified in all the order conductors (i.e. l divides none), inertia in each class field restricts injectively to inertia in its quadratic base over ℚ: the relative extension is unramified there. In particular its image has exponent ≤2. Every element of inertia in the compositum thus squares to the identity. For a Hilbert-only menu this holds at **every finite prime, including 2**. The finite inertia subgroups generate the abelianization, since the remaining abelian quotient would be unramified at every finite prime of ℚ (trivial by Minkowski). ## 6. Unrestricted real quadratic menus break four; common-p menus do not

**Theorem C (PROVED rel. class field theory, Dirichlet's unit theorem, finiteness of class groups and Chebotarev).** Let L be a finite compositum of ordinary or narrow ring class fields of real or imaginary quadratic orders. Let B be the compositum of the quadratic bases, [B:ℚ]=2ʳ. Then:

1. The exponent of Gal(L/ℚ)ᵃᵇ divides **2ʳ**. This bound is attained, for each r≥1, using just **ordinary real** ring class fields with a common prime conductor. More strongly, for *any* totally real multiquadratic B of rank r, for a positive-density set of primes ℓ, the product of the conductor-ℓ ring fields of its 2ʳ−1 quadratic subfields contains the cyclic subfield of ℚ(ζ_ℓ) of degree 2ʳ. Here “product” means compositum. The class data determine this particular cyclotomic quotient on all unramified primes.
2. If a single σ∈Gal(B/ℚ) is nontrivial on every base in the menu, the exponent divides **4**. In particular this holds for any mixture of real/imaginary bases ℚ(√(±dp)) with p odd and p∤d in every base. It holds regardless of the ring conductors. For mixed **Hilbert-only** menus, ordinary or narrow, the exponent divides **2** without a common-base restriction.
3. Hence no menu in this theorem detects q≡−1 mod 7 or mod 9 on all unramified primes (both exponents have odd part 3). Unrestricted rank can give cyclic *octic and higher* cyclotomic information; this is not a proof that every prescribed 2-primary cyclotomic field such as ℚ(ζ₁₇) is available. For common-p and imaginary menus the necessary condition for detection of the full −1 mod 4C is still C|60; Hilbert-only gives C|6. Even where a cyclic layer is available it does not ensure that a prime factor in the desired class occurs at the given p.

**Upper bounds.** Each Lᵢ is Galois over ℚ: the conjugation of the base preserves the order and the principal subgroup, also for the totally positive principal condition. It acts on Aᵢ=Gal(Lᵢ/Kᵢ) by inversion, since an ideal times its conjugate is generated by its **positive rational** norm. Every element outside Aᵢ squares to 1: realize its conjugacy class by a rational prime inert in Kᵢ away from the conductor; the Frobenius squared is the Artin image of the ideal (q), ring-principal, totally positively generated. Thus each factor really is generalized dihedral, not quaternionic. Do not choose independent reflections and assume they lift to one global element.

Let V=Gal(B/ℚ), an F₂-space, and let χᵢ be the sign character cutting out Kᵢ. Embed G=Gal(L/ℚ) in the product of the individual groups. In the kernel of G→V every coordinate is a rotation. Multiplying the conjugates of h in that kernel by one lift of each v∈V gives 1: on coordinate i the sum of χᵢ(v) is zero. In Gᵃᵇ the product is h^{2ʳ}. For g mapping to nonzero w∈V, g² is a rotation tuple supported only on coordinates with χᵢ(w)=+1. On a linear complement of ⟨w⟩ in V, each such nontrivial χᵢ has sum zero. Multiply conjugates of g² over that complement; now g^{2ʳ}=1 in the abelianization as well. A common inverting σ gives the stronger g⁴∈[G,G] as in §4. For a common odd ramified p all Kᵢ/ℚ ramify quadratically at p. In the elementary abelian extension B/ℚ its inertia is cyclic of order two (tame), projecting onto inertia in *each* base. The same element works. Finally the Hilbert bound uses finite inertia and Minkowski exactly as in §4: allowing real places to ramify does not allow a nontrivial extension of ℚ unramified at every finite prime. The Chebotarev detector step is unchanged.

**Attaining the bound.** Put N=|V|=2ʳ. For a prime ℓ completely split in B and ℓ≡1 mod 2N, fix a character λ of 𝔽_ℓ^× of exact order N. We will make the *global cyclotomic character* λ factor through L, not just prescribe a local inertia quotient. Fix a prime of B over ℓ to label the N reductions by g∈V. For each nontrivial sign χ of V use K=B^kerχ, with its two reduction maps marked +,− by χ. On principal ideals of K prime to ℓ the character we want is

    θ_K((α)) = λ(α₊/α₋).

The two maps are normalized by the distinguished prime. If the unit ratios are killed, this is defined on principal ideals; it kills every ring-principal congruence (equal unit reductions mod ℓ). Equivalently it is a character on the principal-ideal image of the ordinary order class group, by the ring class field ideal description. Fractions are evaluated in the local units at both primes. The norm pullback to B of this character on a principal ideal (b) is ∏_{g∈V} λ(b_g)^{χ(g)}. Multiplying over **all nontrivial signs** gives λ(N_{B/ℚ}b)⁻¹: the sum of their values is N−1 at the identity and −1 everywhere else. Both exponents are −1 mod N; the absolute norm convention causes no sign since λ(−1)=1. This congruence, rather than a common complex conjugation, is the source of the new layer.

Here are the global obstructions in that calculation and how to remove them. All the following choices of ideals and elements are made **before choosing ℓ**. For each K choose ideals J_j giving cyclic generators of its ordinary class group in an invariant factor decomposition, with J_j^{e_j}=(u_j). Choose ideals b_i generating Cl(B). In each K write the ideals N_{B/K}b_i=(a_{Ki})∏J_j^{v_{Kij}}. Form a finite list of algebraic numbers consisting of fundamental units and torsion units of every K, the u_j, the a_{Ki}, and the positive rational norms N_{B/ℚ}b_i. Exclude all primes at which any ideal or element is not a unit. Take ℓ split completely in the Galois closure of B(ζ_{2N}, all N-th roots of the numbers in this list). This has positive prime density. Then every listed element at every indicated reduction is an N-th power. The θ_K above are well defined; extend them by θ_K(J_j)=1. These assignments respect *all* relations: any relation on the J_j has exponents multiples of e_j, and the resulting principal generators u_j have trivial value. The extensions are characters on ideals modulo the conductor-ℓ ring-principal subgroup, hence of the ordinary ring class fields.

On each b_i, both λ(N_{B/ℚ}b_i) and ∏θ_K(N_{B/K}b_i) are 1 by construction. The principal computation now gives equality, with the inverse on one side, on **all** ideals prime to ℓ (and it suffices to omit all ramified primes). By Artin functoriality this is the equality of characters on the absolute Galois group of B. Thus λ restricted to B factors through the compositum of the indicated ring fields. That compositum contains B, so the absolute character λ over ℚ is trivial on its fixing subgroup: the actual cyclic cyclotomic field is contained. There is no unaccounted unramified class-character twist. No external priority claim.
