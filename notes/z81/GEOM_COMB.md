## 2. Simultaneous bad exponent budgets, not just simultaneous congruence biases

**Theorem A (PROVED rel. Maynard's dense-cluster theorem, BV, PNT in fixed APs, Chebotarev, Dirichlet and QR).** For every J,B,W≥1 there are J distinct fixed positive shifts k_j≡7 (8) and infinitely many primes p≡1 (840) with the following joint profile. Put x_j=(p+k_j)/4. In every window:

* x_j has ≥W distinct prime factors; a specific prime q_j|x_j to exponent one is a quadratic nonresidue at p. Moreover **−1 is a power of q_j modulo the entire k_j**.
* Even allowing arbitrary integral exponents on all prime factors of x_j/q_j, every product of those factors, q_j^u and p^v equal to −1 mod k_j needs |u+v|>B. In particular the true full I/II box misses (choose the construction with B≥2). The q_j are distinct for different windows. The windows at 8 with denominator x_j/2 miss as well.
* Nevertheless p has an explicit positive (★) solution. The k_j are constants on a subsequence of primes, chosen by pigeonholing from a finite set, **not** a prescription for simultaneous failure at, say, 3 and 7. The largest prime factor q_j is ≍p with a constant depending on the constructed shifts; this theorem is not a smooth-x construction.

**Proof.** Choose an odd prime h>max(11,B,2) congruent to 1 mod 4. Choose a prime r≡3 mod 4 such that r≡1 mod h and 2,3,5,7 are all (2h)-th powers in F_r. Here are details of existence: let F=Q(ζ_h,√2,√3,√5,√7). It does not contain i: the only quadratic field of Q(ζ_h) is the real Q(√h), ramified at h whereas the multiquadratic extension on 2,3,5,7 is unramified there. Their intersection is trivial: any nontrivial cyclic subfield of exponent ≤2 would be this quadratic field. Quadratic characters of the direct product therefore all cut out real fields. Adjoining h-th roots of 2,3,5,7 to F gives an odd-degree Galois extension over F (roots of unity present) and the new field K is Galois over Q, still not containing i (adjoining i over F has even degree). Discard r∈{2,3,5,7,11,h}. Use Chebotarev on K(i) to split completely in K and conjugate i. At unramified r away from the bases this ensures squares and h-th powers, hence (2h)-th powers. Let H=(F_r^*)^{2h}, g primitive mod r. In G/H≅C_{2h}, −1 has class h in the g-coordinate because r≡3 mod 4. Enlarging the order of g without achieving this specific class would not suffice.

Take N so large that any fixed admissible N-tuple of distinct nonproportional positive affine forms has at least J+1 simultaneous primes infinitely often. This is the elementary fixed-form specialization of Maynard, *Dense clusters of primes in subsets*, Compos. Math. 152, Thm 3.1 (statement checked at `https://arxiv.org/html/1405.2593v2`). Use A=N (positive integers), all primes, B_sieve=1, α=1, θ=1/3, δ=1/2. For a fixed primitive form a n+b the AP modulus is aq, φ_L(q)=φ(aq)/φ(a); fixed a costs nothing in BV at this θ, using Siegel–Walfisz at a for the unconditioned count. The other distribution conditions are integer counts with O(1) remainders. The density input follows from fixed-modulus PNT. The threshold in the variable may depend arbitrarily on the forms; N is chosen before they are constructed. This does not demand that a designated form be prime.

Choose N positive distinct indices I such that **every i−j>0 divides j**, for i,j∈I. Induct: adjoin zero to a working set with the property, then shift all elements by a positive common multiple of the old elements and all nonzero differences. This retains divisibility of the smaller element by the difference for each pair including the new one. Denote the set of positive differences by Δ.

Let S be the product of 2,3,5,7 and ≥W further distinct primes ≡1 mod r, excluding 11,r. **Admissibility check (important).** Outside the AP modulus one may take t≡0. At r every form is g. ## 4. Geometry at the specified point, without paying for irrelevant characters

**Lemma C (PROVED rel. Poisson summation, Euclidean norm; classical Gaussian transference adapted to an index-two target, no novelty claim; cf. W. Banaszczyk, *New bounds in some transference theorems in the geometry of numbers*, Math. Ann. 296 (1993), 625–635).** In a product box on generators g_i of a finite abelian group A, suppose the target t has exact order two in A. Give coordinate i a positive budget b_i (integer). Let R be the least ‖(z_i/b_i)‖₂ among integral products g^z=t. Let S be the least `‖(b_i ||arg χ(g_i)/(2π)||)‖₂` over characters of A satisfying χ(t)=−1, where double bars inside are distance to an integer. Then

`1/2 ≤ R S ≤ (d log 2 + 2 log(1+√2))/π`,  d=number of generators.

This need not be cheaper to check than searching the box, and the sufficient threshold grows linearly with d. It is **single-target**, not worst-coset covering: even characters, however close to trivial on all inputs, cost nothing. An instance with S at least the displayed upper constant forces R≤1 and hence a genuine box hit. Failure of the native box implies R>1 and S less than the constant; this is not a converse. At actual windows use A=⟨l:l|px⟩, g_i=l mod k and b_i=v_l(px); if −1∉A this branch is inapplicable (a separate exact group obstruction). Compare `BALANCED_LATTICE.md` §1's min over all nontrivial characters for the **covering radius** and z80 `EXTREME` G's fixed-shift Fourier majorant.

*Proof.* In scaled Euclidean coordinates let L be the relation lattice, L'=L+Zv for any scaled exponent vector for t. Both are full rank; [L':L]=2. L* consists of (b_i θ_i) with θ_i lifts of phases of a single character of A: the phase homomorphism from Z^d trivial on the relation kernel factors through A, and conversely. The index-two dual difference L*\(L')* is exactly the odd part. Shortest norms in the two nontrivial cosets are R,S. Pairings between these cosets are half integers, giving the lower bound.

Write ρ_a(D)=Σ_{y∈D}exp(−π|y|²/a²). For a lattice M and a coset D of distance c, the ratio of shifted to unshifted masses at scale a is ≤2^{d/2} exp(−πc²/(2a²)): split the exponential in half, apply Poisson's formula at √2a to bound the shifted mass there by the unshifted, and use Poisson once more to bound ρ_{√2a}(M)/ρ_a(M)≤2^{d/2}. For M=L, D=v+L, denote the ratio by U, and denote its counterpart on (L')* for the dual odd coset at scale 1/a by V. Poisson gives **U=(1−V)/(1+V)**, since the pairing with v is + on the dual even coset and − on the odd one. Take a=√(R/S). Both ratios are ≤2^{d/2}exp(−πRS/2); they cannot both be less than √2−1, the unique positive fixed point of this decreasing fractional-linear involution. Rearrangement proves C. No claim of optimal dimension constant. ∎

**Application/limit (PROVED rel. A).** In each A window use the single character inflated from G/H that sends g to exp(2πi/(2h)). It is odd and has exactly two nonintegral phases (p,q_j), giving S≤√2/(2h) at either numerator. In particular R≥h/√2, unbounded at actual prime-event fibres with target in the generated group, independent of how many neutral primes have been planted. The signed *exponents* here are positive rational divisor ratios, not signed Egyptian denominators. Doubling x in B changes one budget, and may do nothing or may give a solution, at identical k and identical group index. Neither general transference nor an LLL dimension increase predicts a universal volume threshold.

## 7. A hypothetical difference analogue of Jain's sum theorem: testing the actual gap

**Theorem E (PROVED rel. the positive ET charts, elementary divisor bounds, Mertens and the polynomial divisor-moment argument of z80 `EXTREME.md` §1).** Fix α>0 and 0<δ≤1. In X<p≤2X the number of primes with **any** sorted positive 4/p solution whose gap k=4x−p at its smallest denominator x satisfies k≥X^δ and P⁺(k)≤(log X)^α is at most

`X^{1−δ min(1,1/α)/3 + o(1)}` for Type I, and `X^{1−δ min(1,1/α) + o(1)}` for Type II.

There is no smoothness or QNR assumption on x: requiring either only decreases the count. This is an upper bound for *actual solutions' gaps*, unlike the λ(k) envelope, and unlike the z80 result on factors of x itself. A hypothetical smooth-difference theorem supplying such a k could yield a witness at that decomposition for at most X^{1−η+o(1)} primes in (X,2X], η=δ min(1,1/α)/3. It does not obstruct the very frequent k=3.

**Variable-scale version (PROVED, same inputs).** Given A>0, there are constants B,L>0 such that, outside O_A(X/log^A X) primes in that interval, **every solution** has either k≤(log X)^B or

`P⁺(k)>k^{log₃X/(L log₂X)}`.

In particular this is a simultaneous conclusion over *all* actual denominators that occur as smallest denominators, not a statement about just the first successful shift. The small-gap alternative cannot be dropped. In particular, for each fixed α,A>0, outside O_A(X/log^A X) primes **every** polylog-smooth actual gap with P⁺(k)≤(log X)^α has log k < M_{A,α}(log₂X)²/log₃X: above this threshold k>(log X)^B and k^{log₃X/(L log₂X)}≥(log X)^α for M_{A,α}≥αL and all large X. *Proof.* Use the sorted ET charts exactly as in `EXTREME.md` §1 (p odd prime). In I, x=acd, k|4a²d+1. In II, x=abd, k|a+b.
