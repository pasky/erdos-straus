## 4. Geometry at the specified point, without paying for irrelevant characters

**Lemma C (PROVED rel. Poisson summation, Euclidean norm; classical Gaussian transference adapted to an index-two target, no novelty claim; cf. W. Let S be the least `‖(b_i ||arg χ(g_i)/(2π)||)‖₂` over characters of A satisfying χ(t)=−1, where double bars inside are distance to an integer. Then

`1/2 ≤ R S ≤ (d log 2 + 2 log(1+√2))/π`,  d=number of generators.

This need not be cheaper to check than searching the box, and the sufficient threshold grows linearly with d. It is **single-target**, not worst-coset covering: even characters, however close to trivial on all inputs, cost nothing. An instance with S at least the displayed upper constant forces R≤1 and hence a genuine box hit. Failure of the native box implies R>1 and S less than the constant; this is not a converse. *Proof.* In scaled Euclidean coordinates let L be the relation lattice, L'=L+Zv for any scaled exponent vector for t. Both are full rank; [L':L]=2. L* consists of (b_i θ_i) with θ_i lifts of phases of a single character of A: the phase homomorphism from Z^d trivial on the relation kernel factors through A, and conversely. The index-two dual difference L*\(L')* is exactly the odd part. Shortest norms in the two nontrivial cosets are R,S. Pairings between these cosets are half integers, giving the lower bound.

Write ρ_a(D)=Σ_{y∈D}exp(−π|y|²/a²). For a lattice M and a coset D of distance c, the ratio of shifted to unshifted masses at scale a is ≤2^{d/2} exp(−πc²/(2a²)): split the exponential in half, apply Poisson's formula at √2a to bound the shifted mass there by the unshifted, and use Poisson once more to bound ρ_{√2a}(M)/ρ_a(M)≤2^{d/2}. For M=L, D=v+L, denote the ratio by U, and denote its counterpart on (L')* for the dual odd coset at scale 1/a by V. Poisson gives **U=(1−V)/(1+V)**, since the pairing with v is + on the dual even coset and − on the odd one. Take a=√(R/S). Both ratios are ≤2^{d/2}exp(−πRS/2); they cannot both be less than √2−1, the unique positive fixed point of this decreasing fractional-linear involution. Rearrangement proves C. ## 7. Testing Jain's proposed smooth difference against the *actual gap*, not λ(k)

**Theorem D (PROVED rel. the positive ET charts, elementary divisor bounds, Mertens and the polynomial divisor-moment argument of z80 `EXTREME.md` §1).** Fix C,δ>0. In particular a hypothetical smooth-difference theorem that always supplied a k of polynomial size and only polylog-sized prime factors could not give an ES witness at that decomposition on a density-one set of primes. **Variable-scale version (PROVED, same inputs).** Given A>0, there are constants B,L>0 such that, outside O_A(X/log^A X) primes in that interval, **every solution** has either k≤(log X)^B or

`P⁺(k)>k^{log₃X/(L log₂X)}`.

In particular this is a simultaneous conclusion over *all* actual denominators that occur as smallest denominators, not a statement about just the first successful shift. The small-gap alternative cannot be dropped. No implication at an individual supplied p.

*Proof.* Use the sorted ET charts exactly as in `EXTREME.md` §1 (p odd prime). In I, x=acd, k|4a²d+1. In II, x=abd, k|a+b. Put γ=1/3 or 1 respectively and Z=c K^γ with a fixed c>0 small enough for Z≤V. If k is y-smooth with 2≤y<Z, stop multiplying its prime factors (including repetitions) just before exceeding Z. `≪ Σ_{Z/y<m≤Z, P⁺(m)≤y} 2^{ω(m)}/m ≤ (Z/y)^(−s) ∏_{l≤y prime}(1+2/(l^{1−s}−1))` (0<s<1).

This selects a divisor of the smooth **gap** only for an exclusion estimate. There is no necessity to exhibit a class divisor for a given p.
