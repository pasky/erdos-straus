# The size of the supplied unit, not the size of its radicand

## 1. Positive descent first

**PROVED (rel. z79 `IE1_BASE.md` §4 and the following elementary descent).** In ordinary `IE₁⁻`, let `p≡1 (4)` be prime. From a supplied `u>1,v≥0`, `u²−pv²=1`, one obtains `X,Y>0` with `X²−pY²=−1`, `X<u`. In particular `−1` has a root mod `p`, and the parameter-free Farey theorem gives two positive squares of sum `p`. This is Legendre's classical factor argument; the point here is the *parameter-free conditional*, without assuming a least Pell solution at a variable parameter. In particular any failing prime `p≡1 (4)` without a root of −1 cannot have a nontrivial norm-one Pell point at `p` in this theory. Supplying `(p,1)` at **another** radicand `p²−1` does not activate the implication.

First `v` is even, `u` odd, by the squares mod 4. The consecutive positive integers `s=(u−1)/2, t=(u+1)/2` multiply to `p(v/2)²`. Exactly one is divisible by `p`. Remove one `p` from that factor. The resulting two coprime factors of a square are squares, already in a signed Bézout domain: if `ab=z²`, `(a,b)=1`, then `gcd(a,z)²=gcd(a²,z²)=a` up to a positive unit, since coprime squares are coprime (expand a power of a Bézout row). If `s=X²,t=pY²` we are done. Otherwise `t=w²,s=ph²`, so `1<w<u`, `w²−ph²=1`. To iterate, package the proposed parameters **and the initializing solution** in the unary square block of z79 §4. Openly invalid inputs pass; a nontrivial factorization of the putative prime is another bounded existential escape. At index `0≤j≤u` exhibit either the negative solution with abscissa `<u`, or a nontrivial positive one with abscissa `w≤u−j`. Start with `u`; a step keeps the negative certificate or uses the just-proved split (keeping a positive one if it already fits). Freeze indices at `u`, and reset at the next block; heights, signs, quotient and invalidity witnesses have polynomial bounds in the code. At `j=u` only the negative certificate is possible for a prime. No list of nonstandard length and no least-`U₁` axiom. Applying short vectors to the residue `X mod p` gives the stated norm `p` by the strict `<2p` bound.

**Assessment (the extraction test).** This produces a sign and an exact small representation, but for the wrong congruence. For example apply proper reduction to `[p,2r,(r²+k)/p]` for a root of `−k`. One gets `AC=B²+k, 3A²≤4k` and `p` primitively represented by `[A,2B,C]` (z79 §9). At `k=1` there is no remaining choice of reduced form at all: `[1,0,1]`. In the other direction a specific 4-sensitive extraction at discriminant `−4kp` is z75 `CONJS_GEN.md` Prop. 3: a reduced `[a,b,c]` with `|b|=a−1`, `a≡−1 (4k)` gives a factor `a` of `4kp+1` with complementary factor `−1 mod 4k`, hence `(★)`. Proper reduction gives `|b|≤a≤c`, **not** the off-by-one equality or the moving residue. Reduction of the principal form `[1,0,kp]` does not even supply the congruence. At 409, for instance, `409=20²+3²` supplies a root and the shortest norm without *any* such `(★)` extraction at *any* k. Requiring k even for the same extraction would imply C-even `(★)`; the two in-𝔐 R₈-zero controls below forbid this. This tests two particular mechanisms, not a negative answer for all uses of reduction.

**Assessment (minimal failure).** Suppose even numerical leastness at `P`. The standard scaling map along `m|P` transports an old triple only in that direction; there is no smaller divisor ≥2 at a prime. For a witness at `m|P+a` the same map gives `4/(P+a)`, not `4/P`; the difference is `4a/[P(P+a)]` and all three terms may already be in use. Farey neighbours supply signed remainders, not an absorption rule for this difference. In particular a numerator-independent induction cannot repair `8/11` even with every *successful* lower input at its disposal, nor force the missing `8/241` by its `1 mod 24` class. No least-failure principle for `Bad` is inferred from the least descent on the **supplied** witnesses above.

## 2. The model theorem and cut lemma

**PROVED (rel. §§3–6).** There is a countable signed discretely ordered domain `T` satisfying `IOpen + Bézout + (Sep)` of z80, with a nonstandard prime `P≡1 (840)`, `¬ES(P)`, and the **full supplied-unit PW**: for `d≥2`, `a>1`, `a²−db²=1`, every `n≥0` has `n≤u≤(2a+1)(n+1)`, `0≤v≤(2a+1)(n+1)`, `u²−dv²=1`. Also z80 §1's factorial-string conclusion holds for **every** non-initial signed ordered PA subring of `T`. Such subrings can actually occur. Unlike z80 §9, there is no *cofinal* PA subring of `T`. No ES proof depends on specializing Pell witnesses to ordinary integers.

Here is the general cut lemma used to obtain these two assertions. Take a countable signed PA model `B`, positive infinite `H∈B`, and define the external convex subring

```
 C = {b∈B : |b|^j < H for every ordinary integer j≥1}.
```

Products and sums satisfy the test by using larger fixed powers (handle bounded inputs separately). Write `K=Frac B`, `F=Frac C`. Suppose `R` extends `B`, is an integer part of its ordered real closure, and `C` is initial in `R`. Suppose every positive element of the real closure above `C` has a `B`-point strictly between it and twice it, with the factor 2 replaceable by `3/2`. Let `T` be the signed convex subring of `R` bounded by some ordinary power of `H`. Then `T` is an integer part of the real closure of `Frac T`: that field's fractions are bounded by members of `T` by discreteness, its algebraic elements by the polynomial root bound, and their `R`-floors are therefore in `T`. Thus `IOpen`. Any positive `a∈T` outside `C` dominates a positive standard rational power of `H` **up to a constant factor**: choose a `B`-integer between `a` and `3a/2`, also outside `C`, and use the defining test. Hence the ordinary powers of `a` are cofinal in `T`.

If `a∉C`, put `e=a+b√d` with `b≥0`. For `n≥1` take the first ordinary power `e^j≥2n`; then `e^j<2en` (the `j=0` case is unnecessary), and its integral trace-half and ordinate give PW. If `a∈C`, then `d,b∈C` because `b≥1` and `db²=a²−1`. For `n∈C` bracket `2n` between consecutive powers in PA in `B`; the first power at or above it gives integral coordinates in `T` by the bound. For `n>C` bracket a `B`-point in `(2n,3n)` by these same internally computed powers in `B`, then compare the power immediately below that point with `2n`. Consecutive powers have ratio `e≥2+√3>3/2`, so at most one extra comparison gives `2n≤s≤2en`. These comparisons refer to actual pairs in `B` and the common ordered real closure; no transfer of PA induction to `T`. In either case `(s+s⁻¹)/2≤en+1<(2a+1)(n+1)` and the nonnegative ordinate is ≤ the abscissa. At zero use `(1,0)`.

Every PA subring `D` of `T` lies in `C`: a positive member outside `C` would have cofinal ordinary powers, but PA in `D` bounds all those powers by a single member (e.g. `a^a`). Given `0<y<b∈D`, `y∉D` and `y+j<b` for all standard j, we have `y∈C` by initiality. Let `h=b!` computed in `D`, **not necessarily** the ambient PA factorial. For fixed ordinary `L`, in `B` examine the divisibility predicate `V(i): ∀0≤j<L (i+j)|h` on `[1,y]`. True and false tests on entries of `C` agree in `B,T` since any successful quotient is bounded by `h∈C`. If `V(y)` use `y`; otherwise take a true-to-false counterstep before `y` in `B`, starting at 1. Its true index cannot be in `D`: all the next `L` factors there would divide `h` already in `D`. Thus an actual `t∉D` works for all `L` consecutive factors at once, with the same `h` across `L` (not necessarily the same `t`). This is the external factorial-string conclusion, **not induction for every divisibility formula**. For nonvacuity take `B` with a nonstandard initial PA cut `D₁` below `H` and containing a non-initial signed ordered PA subring `D₀`: take an elementary extension of a countable nonstandard PA model inserting a different integer in an old bounded infinite interval by compactness, then an elementary end extension by MacDowell–Specker. `D₁⊂C`. The unordered-embedding loophole is not used. The power cut retains these two actual subrings and their old gap.

## 3. A fixed cut for *all* the floor and row tasks

**PROVED (rel. z80 `IE1_SPLIT.md` §§2–4 for the row/hull lemmas).** The new ingredient is a dilution for which there is **no shrinkage of the reserve**. We only need line and row edges. In particular no rank-four sphere with these valuations is assumed. Maintain a finite-type factorial upper `K`-algebra `A` with units `K*`, `E=Frac A`, and valuations `w_l` at **all internal PA primes** `l` of `B`, extending `v_l` there. As in z80 we need:

1. Every nonzero field element has values `v_l γ` at all `l`, for a single positive scalar `γ∈K` (its content).
2. Every globally integral-valued element of `E` has a simultaneous `B` representative at full precision `d` on the primes of `d`, for **each** `d∈C⁺`. Let `R_A` be all integral-valued elements of `A`.
3. `S=(C∖0)⁻¹R_A` intersects the convex hull of `F` only in `F`. In the real closure, relative sizes (positive magnitudes modulo ratios bounded above and away from 0 by `F` bounds) are represented by `K`; relative residues embed compatibly in the Cauchy host of `rc(F)` of `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §§1–2.

Initially `E=K`, `A=K`, `R_A=B`. `C` floors its own real closure: embed that closure in `rc(K)`, use the polynomial root bound and the `B` floor. Approximating relatively bounded elements of `rc(K)` by scaling at each `F` precision and taking such `B` floors (then in `C`) gives the required ordered residue embedding, just as in z79 §1. Positive sizes in `rc(K)` have `K` representatives by floors and inversion. These arguments do **not** need `C` to be PA or factorial-closed. In fact a PA reserve coinitial with this subpower boundary is not what makes the construction work.

**Moving-modulus line lemma (PROVED).** For a free line use the coordinate `Y` at every prime; for a row `a u+b v=1` with nonzero, gcd-coprime arithmetic cofactors, use `Y=u` at primes where `b` is a valuation unit, `Y=v` elsewhere. This is a partition computable in PA from the scalar content of `b`; the other pivot is a unit on its chart. Below we describe a common sequence of substitutions on the selected coordinates, not a requirement that the two coordinates have the same value in one valuation. At each place preserve the old valuation. Off a current positive modulus `D_m∈C` use ordinary Gauss; in fact everywhere use Gauss in `(Y−s_m)/D_m`, `s_m∈B`; these coincide off `D_m`. Start `D_0=1,s_0=0`. At the next step choose a multiplier `M∈C`, integral digit `z∈B` and set `D_{m+1}=D_m M`, `s_{m+1}=s_m+D_m z`. These refinements can only increase values of chart polynomials.

Enumerate nonzero polynomials over the old **field** in a free variable, to be protected in each chart (schedule each chart expression; one may formally work at all places for a given chart and restrict at the end). Enumerate `C⁺` cofinally for divisibility, repeating every modulus arbitrarily late. For each scheduled polynomial require its Gauss value to be frozen at *all* primes from its service step onward, not just at old primes of `D_m`. Inductively the old tasks have stable constant term of strictly lower value than nonconstant terms on the already entered primes of their refinement, and have their **original** Gauss minimum off the entered primes. At a later newly entered prime we shall freeze them at this same minimum for good. The following is the digit choice in detail.

* At a step with new polynomial of ordinary degree `e`, include in `M` the current `D_m`, the next requested modulus, and a sufficiently large **ordinary** factorial factor. Require before this step (except at the first step) that `D_m` contains every ordinary prime ≤ the sum of degrees of the polynomial list through the **current** task. This one-step look-ahead is possible from the enumeration. At primes of `M` require `v_l M>v_l V_e` for the nonzero ordinary Vandermonde determinant on `0,…,e` (degree zero harmless): multiply by `|V_e|²`. All these are finite products in `C`, not a factorial of an arbitrary member of `C`.
* In each polynomial at the current depth divide by the scalar fractional gcd of its coefficients' contents. The resulting coefficients are globally integral-valued and have joint minimum zero at each place. Their representatives modulo `M` exist by invariant 2 **over the fixed cut**. For `l|D_m` select `z` at which the current normalized polynomial has value ≤ `v_l V_e`, possible by the Vandermonde adjugate. Previously frozen values there put no restrictions on the digit. For a newly entered `l|M`, `l∤D_m`, avoid **all** the residual zeros of previously served polynomials and of the current one; each normalized reduction is nonzero, and `l` exceeds the sum of degrees, so there is a residue outside their union. For the *first* step use just the Vandermonde test everywhere, no old constraints. Implement all choices by PA on integer representatives and CRT on the full prime powers of `M`; a chart partition, if used, is PA-coded. No CRT for a non-coded set of ambient elements.
* Substituting `Z=z+MZ'` makes each nonconstant coefficient, after the indicated normalization, divisible by the full local `M`; the constant term is strictly below this value at selected primes. All future integral substitutions there preserve the chosen value. Off `M` the Gauss valuation does not change. At newly entered primes the old values are indeed preserved: the old coefficient minimum there was still depth-zero (the shift/scaling since depth zero is an integral unit-slope affine change), and choosing a residual nonzero value realizes that minimum. In particular each polynomial's scalar profile is computed at its own service step, by the fractional gcd of coefficients in the new coordinate. It is unchanged thereafter. For the two charts patch the two profiles on the PA subset as in z80 §3.

Eventual comparison of any finite set of identities gives actual valuations on the fraction field, with invariant 1. For each polynomial, backwards substitution at its finite freeze depth clears its depth-zero coefficient denominators at a loss of at most the `degree`th power of `D_m` in value. Thus if integral at the final places, a polynomial in the respective charts becomes depth-zero integral on those charts upon multiplying by **some** member of `C⁺` (take both degree bounds). On a free line over `K` a finite list of finally integral polynomials actually belongs to `B[(Y−s_m)/D_m]` at a common late depth.

Here is a separate check of invariant 2; controlling only values at freeze time would not prove it. For a fraction and a requested `d∈C`, choose a common depth *after* numerator and denominator freezes and after `d|D_m`. At this step and on each chart divide each polynomial by its depth-`m` scalar coefficient minimum. All coefficients in `Z=(Y−s_m)/D_m` are now integral-valued and each normalized polynomial has value zero at **every** place there, also at all later depths. Choose a subsequent depth with `d|(D_j/D_m)`; the chain can include any given `d` arbitrarily late in a multiplier. Substitution in `Z` at that depth now gives a constant term (an integral old field member) representing the normalized polynomial to precision `d` at `l|d`. Its reduction there is a unit. Invariant 2 in the old field gives simultaneous integer representatives for these constants. Invert the unit denominator on the required prime powers and restore the scalar ratio of the original minima; for an integral fraction that ratio is integral on the chosen chart primes. Clear its PA scalar denominator prime by prime in lowest terms, or invert its denominator on the chart part of `d` where integral. Patch the two PA parts by CRT. This works also at nonstandard exponents. When using both symbolic charts one can run the single-variable construction at every place on old field polynomials, then pull it back along the chosen field identification at each place; thus normalization on the unused chart parts poses no problem.

## 5. What survives taking the power cut

**PROVED (rel. §§2–4).** `P` and the quotient certifying `P≡1 (840)` are in `T`. It remains prime: divisibility of bounded inputs by a nonzero element is absolute between the signed convex rings, and a failed existential ES assertion remains failed on restriction. Bézout rows compress into the cut: take the positive ambient gcd `g` (bounded by nonzero entries), divide the pair by it, reduce one Bézout coefficient modulo its opposite nonzero cofactor by division and solve for the other. Their magnitudes are bounded by a polynomial in the pair. Zero inputs are trivial. The positive `(Sep)` factors of `h` are bounded by `h`; all the required coprimalities remain row-coprimalities by the same compression. Thus this is one `IOpen + Bézout + (Sep) + PW` countermodel. §2 proves the universal external factorial test for this **same** model. For instance at `d=P²−1` the actual ordinary powers of `P+√d` now meet *every* ambient window for the supplied `(P,1)`; this is not preservation of a failed bounded instance of z80 in an end extension. It uses a power cut, excluding the superpower height, after a new fixed-cut construction. BCM fails, since the infinitely many `P+j`, ordinary positive `j`, are pairwise comaximal nonunits upstairs, still horizontal, all ≤`2P`, and no ambient nonzero common multiple can have infinitely many horizontal factors. No statement that a BCM witness in another model obeys this finite horizontal factorization.

**Assessment of scope.** The limit scalar-factor objection in z80 §16 assumes a PA coefficient union with a scalar above `P`. The surviving scalars here are **not PA**: `H` has cofinal ordinary powers. For actual non-initial PA subrings below `C`, there are divisors outside the subring, by the counterstep proof; for an arbitrary input `y>C` there is no PA subring containing a larger input. This does not build a cofinal PA subring with the factorial test over it. It also does not force a genuinely super-polynomial error relative to `P` in an order containing heights above *all* its powers. No determination here of full Farey, uniform prime short vectors, general proper Gauss reduction, or numerical leastness over `IE₁⁻`.
