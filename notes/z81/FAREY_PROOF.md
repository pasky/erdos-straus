**A degree obstruction (PROVED).** With Dirichlet lines the exact Thue cut proved below already refutes uniformity at `P`. The degree argument gives an explicit obstruction even for a shifted variant *without* those lines. For the new seed, put `β=η³=((P−H)/J)³`. For every nonzero arithmetic pair with `x,y≪_C J³`, `|x−βy|>1/c` for some `c∈C⁺`. At the seed both entries have degree ≤2 in `P−H`: degree ≥3 gives size at least `J³/c` by the preceding top-coefficient bound. In `η` the first entry uses degrees `0,…,2`, the second after multiplication by `η³` degrees `3,…,5`. These do not overlap. At least one nonzero coefficient has magnitude ≥`1/c` (weighted by nonnegative powers of `J`); all coefficients are scalars. The relative Gauss bound proves the assertion. At later free, row and sphere edges repeat the derivative/weighted coefficient proof of §8 with `J³` as height boundary and `β` as the fixed old-field slope. Dividing the top derivative by a scale ≥1 preserves the bound on its arguments; the induction hypothesis bounds their error below; restoring the scale cannot decrease it. Freshness is over old real closures, containing `β`. All small-denominator clearings are by members of `C`. As `√P` is comparable to `J²`, this obstructs a short congruence vector mod `P`: take `b=⌊β⌋`, `a'=⌊P(β−b)⌋`. Transcendence gives `0<a'<P` and the would-be pair gives `x=bY+(a'Y−X)/P, y=Y`, both `≪_C J³`, error ≤`2/√P`. Thus the named vector was fixed and another residue at the **same prime** fails. A level-`⌊√P⌋` Farey bracket fails there, and the form with `S=⌊√P⌋`, `v=⌊S²β⌋`, `Q=(S²U−vV)²+V²` fails proper reduction exactly as in §8. For any ordinary fixed `d` the same degree argument uses `η^d` against entries `≪_C J^d`; one chooses `d` above the relevant power scale. A large partial quotient for just one input does not settle the uniform problem.

**Late-edge version (PROVED, scoped order barrier).** More generally let an already constructed arithmetic stage have a prime `p` much bigger than `C` (so `√p>C`) and the relative hull/order invariant of §3. Suppose a later *scaled free line* `Y=B₀ζ`, positive `B₀` with `√p≪_C B₀`, `1<ζ<2` of fresh relative residue, has the §4 sandwich `R_old[Y] ⊆ R_new ⊆ S_old[Y]` with constant contraction. Then none of the entries of size `≪_C B₀` at that stage are new: a positive-degree polynomial has top coefficient of size ≥`1/c`, so Gauss gives size ≥`B₀/c'`. The old coefficients of a nonzero pair have size ≥1 in some entry; normalized by the larger, the residue polynomial at `ζ` cannot vanish. Thus (B) holds with slope `ζ` and boundary `B₀`, initially at that edge and permanently under any subsequent edges admitting the §§7–8 weighted-coefficient or leading-derivative estimates with scales ≥1, constant contraction and `C`-bounded denominator clearing. If `ζ` is in the final fraction field (as it is for arithmetic scalar `B₀`) and floors exist, the same `⌊p(ζ−1)⌋` input refutes short vectors **at `p`**. No transcendence assumption on the original seed ratio `p/H` and no tower-wide superpower scalar are needed. In particular, retaining a large independent Dirichlet line after an earlier convergent repair regenerates an obstruction. This is a no-go for the stipulated extension/order architecture, not for arbitrary extensions with the same abstract axioms, or an implication that an actually supplied short vector produces positive ES denominators.

**PROVED (exact Thue cut, all moduli).** In each of the Dirichlet-line constructions `T₊`, `T*`, `T_g`, `T_shift`, including the optional choices above, the parameter-free predicate

```
 Th(n):  for every 0≤a<n there are signed X and positive Y with
         X²≤n, Y²≤n, and X≡aY (n)                 [X=0 allowed]
```

satisfies `Th(n) ⇔ n∈C` **for n≥0**. For `n∈C⁺`, PA in `B` proves the assertion: order the `⌊√n⌋+1` multiples of `a mod n` with multipliers from 0 to `⌊√n⌋`; a repetition gives `X=0`, and otherwise the smallest cyclic gap is ≤`n/(⌊√n⌋+1)`. Conversely given any cut `n>C`, a later Dirichlet task with `N=n²`, `m=2`, `a=1` supplies the scaled fresh line with `B₀` within the power cut and `√n≪_C B₀` (§7). The late-edge gap above, applied to its slope and `a'=⌊n(ζ−1)⌋`, gives **no** short pair mod `n`; the short-vector-to-approximation direction did not use primality or reducedness. ## 15. Why a single power cut cannot repair the z78 BCM countermodel

**PROVED (rel. discrete order, integer division and ordinary finite Bézout identities).** Let `H` be infinite in a discretely ordered Bézout ring with division and let `I` be its power cut about `H` (more generally assume all positive elements of the ring are bounded by ordinary powers of `H`). There is no positive common multiple in `I` of all integers up to `H`. This is independent of finite horizontal support, of scalar contents, of how primes split, of TS, and of any ambient PA ring. For each fixed ordinary `k≥2` take `s=k!`, `u=⌊H/(2ks)⌋`, `d_i=1+i s u` for `i=1,…,k`. All lie between `H/(3k)` and `H` for infinite `H`. Any positive common divisor of `d_i,d_j` divides `j d_i−i d_j=j−i`; it is ordinary, divides `s` and so divides 1. Bézout gives pairwise comaximality, hence a common multiple is divisible by their product and is ≥`(H/(3k))^k`. Since `k` is arbitrary ordinary, no single cut integer works. Apply directly to a power cut in the z78 model (Bézout rows compress by §5): at an infinite generator the argument works even if the ambient structure has BCM, QR and TS. This does not rule out cuts with no such power-cofinal generator or a different non-power-cut model of BCM + TS + `L(Div)`. Compare the standard/PA instance and Parikh argument of z75 `IE1_ES.md` §5; here the bound is forced in the weak ring itself.

## 16. Another positive reduction implication in `IE₁⁻`

**PROVED (rel. z79 §9; classical discriminant −20 calculation, not an ES extraction).** For `p≠5` an odd prime with a supplied root of `−5 mod p`, `IE₁⁻` gives `p=x²+5y²` if `p≡±1 (5)`, and `2p=X²+5Y²` if `p≡±2 (5)`. In the latter case `p=x²+5y²` is impossible by the squares mod 5. Reduce `[p,2r,(r²+5)/p]` to `[A,2b,D]` primitively representing `p`, `AD=b²+5`, `2|b|≤A≤D`, `3A²≤20`. For `A=1` the form is `[1,0,5]` and its unit values mod 5 are squares. For `A=2` divisibility forces `b=±1, D=3`; twice its value at `(v,w)` is `(2v+bw)²+5w²`, so its unit values mod 5 are nonsquares. These exhaust the possible positive leading coefficients. At `p=5` one may use `x=0,y=1`. This separates the two reduction outcomes in the weak theory without postulating a class-number algorithm, exponentiation at `p`, or a variable-modulus QR converse. At `p∈𝔐` the class mod 5 is a unit square, but the root is a hypothesis; even granting the representation neither gives `|b'|=a'−1` on a `−4kp` row nor produces a divisor in the moving 4-class. The `m=2,3,7` one-class root criteria were already proved in z79 §9, not an increment here.
