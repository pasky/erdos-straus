# Pell windows without Farey: the size of the supplied unit, not its radicand

## 1. Positive descent first

**PROVED (rel. z79 `IE1_BASE.md` §4 and the following elementary descent).** In ordinary `IE₁⁻`, let `p≡1 (4)` be prime. From a supplied `u>1,v≥0`, `u²−pv²=1`, one obtains `X,Y>0` with `X²−pY²=−1`, `X<u`. In particular `−1` has a root mod `p`, and the parameter-free Farey theorem gives two positive squares of sum `p`. This is Legendre's classical factor argument; the point here is the *parameter-free conditional*, without assuming a least Pell solution at a variable parameter. In particular any prime `p≡1 (4)` without a root of −1 cannot have a nontrivial norm-one Pell point at `p` in this theory. Supplying `(p,1)` at **another** radicand `p²−1` does not activate the implication.

First `v` is even, `u` odd, by the squares mod 4. The consecutive positive integers `s=(u−1)/2, t=(u+1)/2` multiply to `p(v/2)²`. Exactly one is divisible by `p`. Remove one `p` from that factor. The resulting two coprime factors of a square are squares, already in a signed Bézout domain: if `ab=z²`, `(a,b)=1`, then `gcd(a,z)²=gcd(a²,z²)=a` up to a positive unit, since coprime squares are coprime (expand a power of a Bézout row). Openly invalid inputs pass; a nontrivial factorization of the putative prime is another bounded existential escape. At index `0≤j≤u` exhibit either the negative solution with abscissa `<u`, or a nontrivial positive one with abscissa `w≤u−j`. Start with `u`; a step keeps the negative certificate or uses the just-proved split (keeping a positive one if it already fits). Freeze indices at `u`, and reset at the next block; heights, signs, quotient and invalidity witnesses have polynomial bounds in the code. At `j=u` only the negative certificate is possible for a prime. No list of nonstandard length and no least-`U₁` axiom. ## 2. The model theorem and cut lemma

**PROVED (rel. §§3–5).** There is a countable signed discretely ordered domain `T` satisfying `IOpen + Bézout + (Sep)` of z80, with a nonstandard prime `P≡1 (840)`, `¬ES(P)`, and the **full supplied-unit PW**: for `d≥2`, `a>1`, `a²−db²=1`, every `n≥0` has `n≤u≤(2a+1)(n+1)`, `0≤v≤(2a+1)(n+1)`, `u²−dv²=1`. Also z80 §1's factorial-string conclusion holds for **every** non-initial signed ordered PA subring of `T`. Such subrings can actually occur. Unlike z80 §9, there is no *cofinal* PA subring of `T`. No ES proof depends on specializing Pell witnesses to ordinary integers.

Here is the general cut lemma used to obtain these two assertions. Take a countable signed PA model `B`, positive infinite `H∈B`, and define the external convex subring

```
 C = {b∈B : |b|^j < H for every ordinary integer j≥1}.
```

Products and sums satisfy the test by using larger fixed powers (handle bounded inputs separately). Write `K=Frac B`, `F=Frac C`. Suppose `R` extends `B`, is an integer part of its ordered real closure, and `C` is initial in `R`. Suppose every positive element of the real closure above `C` has a `B`-point in `(x,3x/2)`, where `x` denotes that element. Put `T={r∈R : |r|≤H^j for some ordinary j≥1}`. Then `T` is an integer part of the real closure of `Frac T`: that field's fractions are bounded by members of `T` by discreteness, its algebraic elements by the polynomial root bound, and their `R`-floors are therefore in `T`. Thus `IOpen`. Any positive `a∈T` outside `C` dominates a positive standard rational power of `H` **up to a constant factor**: choose a `B`-integer between `a` and `3a/2`, also outside `C`, and use the defining test. Hence the ordinary powers of `a` are cofinal in `T`.

If `a∉C`, put `e=a+b√d` with `b≥0`. For `n≥1` take the first ordinary power `e^j≥2n`; then `e^j<2en` (the `j=0` case is unnecessary), and its integral trace-half and ordinate give PW. If `a∈C`, then `d,b∈C` because `b≥1` and `db²=a²−1`. For `n∈C` bracket `2n` between consecutive powers in PA in `B`; the first power at or above it gives integral coordinates in `T` by the bound. For `n>C` bracket a `B`-point in `(2n,3n)` by these same internally computed powers in `B`, then compare the power immediately below that point with `2n`. Consecutive powers have ratio `e≥2+√3>3/2`, so at most one extra comparison gives `2n≤s≤2en`. These comparisons refer to actual pairs in `B` and the common ordered real closure; no transfer of PA induction to `T`. In either case `(s+s⁻¹)/2≤en+1<(2a+1)(n+1)` and the nonnegative ordinate is ≤ the abscissa. At zero use `(1,0)`.

Every PA subring `D` of `T` lies in `C`: a positive member outside `C` would have cofinal ordinary powers, but PA in `D` bounds all those powers by a single member (e.g. `a^a`). Given `0<y<b∈D`, `y∉D` and `y+j<b` for all standard j, we have `y∈C` by initiality. Let `h=b!` computed in `D`, **not necessarily** the ambient PA factorial. For fixed ordinary `L`, in `B` examine the divisibility predicate `V(i): ∀0≤j<L (i+j)|h` on `[1,y]`. True and false tests on entries of `C` agree in `B,T` since any successful quotient is bounded by `h∈C`. If `V(y)` use `y`; otherwise take a true-to-false counterstep before `y` in `B`, starting at 1. Its true index cannot be in `D`: all the next `L` factors there would divide `h` already in `D`. Thus an actual `t∉D` works for all `L` consecutive factors at once, with the same `h` across `L` (not necessarily the same `t`). This is the external factorial-string conclusion, **not induction for every divisibility formula**. For nonvacuity take `B` with a nonstandard initial PA cut `D₁` below `H` and containing a non-initial signed ordered PA subring `D₀`: take an elementary extension of a countable nonstandard PA model inserting a different integer in an old bounded infinite interval by compactness, then an elementary end extension by MacDowell–Specker. `D₁⊂C`. The unordered-embedding loophole is not used. The power cut retains these two actual subrings and their old gap.
