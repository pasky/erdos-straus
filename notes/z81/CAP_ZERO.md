# A single prime bearing the entire cap

**Assessment.** `CAP_ZERO.md` names the assigned target, not the result. No general cap-zero or bounded-cap proof is claimed.

**Assessment (preflight).** The proposed “pay for every digit by a congruence on b” does not license picking b as a free residue: the full product m=qb must also divide the numerator of the signed multiplicative target plus its denominator. We drop that forward search. First choose the *overflow prime* s (not the prime in the window), then choose a divisor m of s^h+1 in **one** residue class mod s. A further cyclotomic factor resolves the bad lift mod s²; no equidistribution of primitive characters at conductor s² is needed. There is no binary basis in the first denominator and no uncontrolled prime of a truncated fourth-power-free kernel. We still pay an odd common exponent h. Below, “root” always means the square root of the **reduced denominator of the rational denominator** z in 4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z, not a square root of its numerator.

## 1. The inverse packet

**Lemma I (PROVED, elementary).** Let p be prime, p≡1 mod 8, s≡1 mod 4 prime with (s/p)=−1, and h odd divisible by 3.     (x,y,z)=(K, pA, pKA/d)

has root exactly s^((h−1)/2). Indeed F|s³+1|s^h+1, F≡1 mod 4 and F≡1−s mod s²; multiplying changes the lift by −s m₀≠0 mod s². None of the t equals p, and p∤F: otherwise s^6=1 mod p, impossible for a nonresidue when 8|p−1 (the order of a nonresidue has full 2-part). Coprimality now follows from 4K=p+m. ## 2. What planting *all* the carrier power would cost

**Lemma H (PROVED, elementary, not a lower bound for e*).** Keep the one-positive-carrier pattern d=K s^h, with s odd prime, c=v_s(K)≥1, m>0 coprime to p, 4K=p+m, m|s^h+1. If c≤h, write b=(s^h+1)/m. Then s^c|pb+1, since (4Kb)=pb+s^h+1. If p<2s^c, then m≥4s^c−p>2s^c and b≤s^(h−c); hence s^c≤pb+1≤(p+1)s^(h−c), i.e. s^(2c−h)≤p+1. If p≥2s^c the same inequality holds since c≤h. Thus an exact bounded-overflow certificate of cap E in this pattern obeys

    h ≤ 4E + log_s(p+1).

Indeed c≥h−2E; if c>h then m≤s^h+1 gives p≥4s^c−s^h−1>s^h. At cap zero even 3s^h≤p+1 follows directly. In particular at a polynomial-height carrier s≥p^α the order h in a cap-C proof must be O(C+1/α), irrespective of how high a modulus is allowed in the planting step. In I's unconditional construction s>R^(D/2)>p+1 for large R (choose D>4), hence h≤4E+1: even at a different legal lift of the same (s,h), increasing c cannot lower the exponent by more than a factor two from (h−1)/2. ### A thin-profile test allowing *any* window factorisation, including prime powers

**Theorem T (PROVED rel. Landau–Page, TZ Thm 1.4, Brun–Titchmarsh; BARRIER-SCOPED).** Fix D≥2 and a nonnegative integer C. There are dyadic ranges (T,2T] tending to infinity and a profile p≡1 mod M there, of prime population ≫T/(φ(M) log T), with the following property for all but a relative o(1) of its primes. Write U=log₂ T, w=exp(U/4), Y=exp(DU), H=⌊exp(U/8)⌋. Consider **any** positive m≡3 mod 4 coprime to p, K=(p+m)/4, and **any** δ_p=0 cap-C exponent vector on K with signed multiplier u/v in lowest terms. Let k=ω(uv). If all its support primes are ≤Y, k≤U^D, and |δ_l|≤H, then

    log(uv) ≤ 100 (C+1)(D+1) k U.                          (T)

In particular Σ_{l>w}|δ_l|≤400(C+1)(D+1)k. No assumptions on the number of prime factors, exponents, order synchronisation or choice of m/q are needed. This is about **spending large exponents in a bounded-cap packet**, not a lower bound on e*(p). A vector with bounded exponents everywhere trivially satisfies (T) and is not excluded. Unlike z80 ST there need be no prescribed residue slots, large or small.

*Proof.* Use the clean scales Z→∞ of z80 §15 (Landau–Page), T=exp((log Z)²); set M=2^(H+3)∏_{3≤l≤w} l^H over odd primes (so 2^H|M). Then log M≪Hw=o(log Z) by Chebyshev, so M≤Z. Exactly the TZ dyadic lower bound in that section applies: even a real exceptional term at modulus M has (1−β)log T≫log Z on the clean sequence. Here the first H digits are frozen to 1 as a residue mod l^H, not just the square character mod l. All primes ≤w are quadratic residues mod p. Split the cost G=uv as follows: each prime l of exponent j=|δ_l| contributes a guaranteed exponent a_l=max(1,j−2C) to K (negative exponents actually require j). Let A and B be the products of these paid powers on l≤w and w<l≤Y respectively. Thus AB|K, A|M, and W=G/A≤B Y^(2Ck).

To bound the contribution from paid small primes even when A is large, put b=(u+v)/m, an integer by the class condition. As p≡1 mod A, m≡−1 mod A. Hence m≥A/2, even when A=1. Swap u,v in this **size calculation only** to have u≥v. From u+v≤2u and A=uv/W obtain bv≤4W. For A_u the part of A supported on u we have A_u|u and mb=u+v, so A_u|b+v. Now u≤W A_u (G/A contains the unpaid part of u and the paid part outside A_u cancels only from v). Thus u≤(b+v)W≤(4W+1)W, v≤4W, and G≤20W³. This forces B≥(G/20)^(1/3)Y^(−2Ck), even if nearly all the prime support lies in the deterministic digits. It handles negative exponents without interpreting a signed identity as a positive triple.

At fixed k there are ≤(2YH)^k signed candidates, counting each ordered list of signed prime powers. For each, at most τ(u+v)≪(G+1)^(1/12) choices for m; discard any not meeting the deterministic condition. The remaining test AB|K requires p≡−m mod B, a reduced class since (m,B)=1. We have (M,B)=1 and log(MB)≪Hw+kH log Y=o(log T). BT and the profile population give relative frequency O(1/φ(B))=O(1/B), since all primes of B exceed w and k≤U^D. Each candidate thus contributes ≪G^(−1/4)Y^(2Ck). For log G exceeding the right side of (T), summing this over candidates and over k≥1 costs O(e^(−U))=o(1). The proof concerns exceptions within the special profile; it does not construct an ES counterexample. ∎

**Assessment (application to controlled carriers).** If a few factors are planted to absorb a common exponent L_o, and all the *net* exponents obey the support/height ceilings above, at least one large nonresidue must still have an odd net exponent. If that exponent is ≥L_o, then (T) requires L_o=O_{C,D}(k). At prime inputs R=p, I's unconditional carriers lie beyond T's polylogarithmic support ceiling. The GRH height of I fits a sufficiently large D in T, but I does not have bounded cap.

## 3. Size exactly three, and a failure of local descent

**Proposition S (PROVED, elementary).** For p≡1 mod 12 prime put N=9p+4.
