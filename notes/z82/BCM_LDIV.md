## 7. A second-digit diagonal despite (C1)

**PROVED (rel. §6).** The same coordinated variant can be chosen to have **no least nonnegative hit** of the single atom

```
             (A⁴+u)² | h² + (A⁴+u)Y .                                (Q2)
```

Consequently (C1) is a genuine positive coordination theorem but **does not suffice for full quadratic leastness**. This test only uses a square of a scalar squarefree resultant. In particular there cannot even be a host code for the true residues of this one `Y` modulo all the `p²` in `h²`, in any elementary enlargement of this host preserving initiality of `C`: such a code would minimize (Q2) on its absolute PA divisor list. This is a sharp precision barrier for the stipulated model, not a theorem that simultaneous second-precision codes cannot be arranged in another model.

**PROVED (correlated word at the special line).** Use one unrestricted `Y=Hη` edge just after the seed; make the primes of §1's `S` its **first** coded cohort, before any nonconstant freeze on this edge. If `|S|` is odd discard its largest prime, and put `h` equal to the product of the retained primes, `N` their even cardinal. Use all the §6 machinery except for choosing this single first word. In PA take a uniform perfect matching of its vertices. If `p` is matched to `q`, prescribe

```
              Y_p = −(h/p)² / q²  mod p .                             (2)
```

All these digits are units. The desired stored word, and indeed its matching, will be host-coded. The use of a matching rather than independent digits leaves many candidate pairs to test at *second* precision. We check both its support and its entropy rather than assuming the product-word lemma for dependent coordinates.

For each standard band `AI_j` of §1 let `X_j` count matched edges wholly within it. With `n` band vertices, `E X_j=n(n−1)/(2(N−1))`. For two disjoint candidate edges the joint inclusion probability is `1/((N−1)(N−3))`, whereas overlapping distinct edges cannot both occur. Summing variances and the positive covariances gives `Var X_j=O(N)` uniformly. By PNT the relative band size tends to `2^(−j−1)` at each fixed j, so with probability tending to 1 the band contains at least `N/100^j` matched edges. These finite estimates transfer to true arithmetic (an integer threshold can use floors). Start the refinement on a **single** host-definable event of probability ≥1/2 imposing these bounds simultaneously for `1≤j≤J` with `J` infinite: in PA take the greatest `J≤⌊log₂ A⌋` for which the simultaneous event has that probability. Every fixed finite conjunction has it by the preceding bounds. Later refinements cannot destroy this property.

For a protected test with at most `e` forbidden digit values per vertex, at most `2e` partners per vertex are bad, by distinctness mod p in `(A,2A)` and the degree-two map. Root lists are supplied as in §6 by old field coefficient codes. If `2s` or more vertices are bad, there are ≥`s` distinct matched edges, each with a bad orientation. The number of unordered `s`-sets of prescribed oriented edges is ≤`(2eN)^s/s!`; overlapping ones have probability zero, and any fixed disjoint set has probability ≤`(2/N)^s` for `s≤N/4`. Thus the bad event has probability ≤`(4e)^s/s!`. At current density ≥`1/c`, `c∈C`, choose `s` the larger of `⌈√N⌉` and `⌈16 log(2c)/log N⌉` (integer binary logs/rounding with a fixed enlarged constant suffice). Then `s/N` is less than every positive standard rational since `log c=o(A)` and `N log N` is comparable to `A`. The bound is `<1/(2c)`: the last half of the factorial alone gives log-saving at least `s log N/5` eventually after the fixed `e` cost (or enlarge the ordinary constant). The product of bad primes when fewer than `2s` are bad is ≤`(2A)^(2s)∈C`. This proves exactly the bad-product requirement for every scheduled normalized polynomial, with Skolem refinements as in §6. Degree zero is immediate. The pairwise probabilities and estimates refer to PA counts, not to new choices on externally arbitrary sets of old indices.

**PROVED (lifting the word).** At each ordinary step `j` plant just one of the matched pairs wholly in band `j`, using two primes not yet entered, also avoiding any preliminary `C`-modulus of bad primes used at this step. Only `o(N)` vertices have been removed by that modulus and previous entry moduli (§1); the fixed band's `N/100^j` edges are disjoint. The match and its two scalar indices are old at these bounded indices (the *whole matching* need not be in `B`). At the two new primes prescribe

```
             Y ≡ −(h/(pq))²  mod p² and mod q² .
```

At **every other** first entry from `S`, including preparatory ones, lift (2) to a value mod `p²` avoiding all `−(h/(pr))² mod p²` for `r∈S\{p}`. At most two of these reduce to (2), so fewer than p lifts are excluded. Require `p²` precision in the multiplier at every such entry (square the new factors); for planted primes enlarge it by `(pq)²`. Old chart slope is a unit there, hence these are prescriptions on its next digit. The batch choices are executable in `B`: the first word restricted to the `C` radical has an old CRT representative, two planted vertices are named, and the forbidden second digits are calculated from old `h,S` in PA. The freeze constraints at new places concern only their first digit; a current test which vanishes there is pre-entered, then frozen by Vandermonde at the subsequent depth. Earlier tests never vanish at fresh entries unless already handled in their own pre-entry. All §6 exceptional-product patches and (C1) therefore remain valid on every edge. At a prime in a planted pair, the planted congruence to precision `p²` specifies the partner uniquely: equality for two possible partners implies `r₁≡±r₂ mod p²`, impossible for distinct positive values in `(A,2A)` as `p²>4A` and p is odd.

Put `t=A⁴+u`. A hit forces `t|h²`, so t is a scalar divisor. If a prime p has exponent exactly one in t, then `p|h²/t`, while `Y mod p` is nonzero; this is incompatible with `t|h²/t+Y`. Thus t has only exponents two, and in `(A⁴,16A⁴)` it must be `(pq)²` for distinct primes of the cohort. Its two tests are exactly the second-digit prescriptions. The hits in that interval are precisely the descending planted products squared. At `t=A⁴` there is none by coprimality; any other hit above the interval cannot be least. This finishes (Q2) and the claimed impossibility of a second-precision code (PA compares on the bounded interval and need not inspect larger hits).

## 8. Rank without any missing square root

**PROVED (rel. only the §3 integer-part and plain successor hypotheses).** Keep the notation of §3, `O` the convex hull of `F`, `𝔪` its infinitesimals in the real closure, `v` its order valuation. Then the positive principal units `(1+𝔪)∩U*` supplied by **normalized divisors of this one `H²`** have `v(g−1)` coinitial in the strictly positive value group; indeed strictly below any specified positive value. Consequently their classes in `U*/F*` have infinite rational rank, without `√2∉F` and even when §3's residue-field quotient offers no rank obstruction at all.

Take any `0<ε∈𝔪`. A least divisor `t` above `⌊H(1+√ε)⌋` exists (use `H²`). Every `H(1+1/d)`, `d∈D⁺`, is a larger-than-threshold divisor as in §3. Therefore `√ε≤t/H−1≤1/d` for all `d`, so `g=t/H` has `0<v(g−1)≤v(ε)/2<v(ε)`. The group is nonzero since `1/H` is infinitesimal. Choose an external sequence with distinct decreasing positive values in this way. For any nonzero fixed ordinary integer `a`, the first nonconstant term of `(1+x)^a` for infinitesimal x has value `v(x)`, by finite binomial expansion for positive `a` and inversion for negative `a` (residue characteristic zero). Thus in any nontrivial finite product of powers of these units the unique smallest value dominates the difference from 1. Such a product is not 1, but has residue 1, so cannot be in `F*`. This proves the rank and coinitiality tests. They rule out even **infinite rational rank** architectures whose units after localizing at `D∖0,H` are pure size monomials times scalars with injective size character on the monomial group, or whose nontrivial residue-one units all have `v(g−1)` bounded below by a fixed positive value. Arbitrarily high rank in *other* unit fibres does not supply any of these principal units. This is a necessary condition over the specified initial base, not an incompatibility of BCM+TS and plain successors in general.

**PROVED (why the proposed square-root shortcut needs a hypothesis).** In a discretely ordered Bézout domain the fraction field cannot contain `√2`: the domain is integrally closed (write a fraction in coprime terms and clear a monic equation), whereas a root integral in the ring would be strictly between 1 and 2 in absolute value. This applies if the *ambient* ring is Bézout as well. It is false for arbitrary discretely ordered domains: inside `ℝ[X]` ordered at positive infinity take `ℤ+Xℝ[X]`; its positive bounded integers are ordinary, but `(X√q)/X` belongs to its fraction field for every standard positive q. No induction or Bézout is claimed in this example. The new principal-unit proof needs neither integral closure nor exclusion of any real algebraic constant.
