## 1. A split/merge group with an exact obstruction at the overflow prime

**Theorem A (PROVED, elementary).** On the positive surface of `M/n`, put `h=n/M` and let `t_i=h/x_i` be the three shares, of sum 1. Pick any of the three shares `u`. Interchange it with the sum of the other two, preserving the ratio of those two:

    u ↦ 1−u,       t_j ↦ u t_j/(1−u)  (j not the pivot).

This is a positive rational involution; the three pivots and coordinate permutations may be chosen anew at every step, without a height or length bound. If `s∤Mn` is prime, and initially the share valuations are `(−a,−a,b)` up to order with `a≥0,b>0`, then in the **entire orbit**

    min v_s(lcm of the three reduced coordinate denominators) = max(0,b−a).

If `b>a` every orbit point having just one nonintegral coordinate at `s` has its *original* valuation `−b` there. This holds with `x` free to change at every move, not just at the original `x` or a bounded set of its neighbors.

*Proof.* Pivoting either of the first two shares leaves all three valuations unchanged: when `a>0` one has `v(1−u)=v(u)<0`; when `a=0` one has `1−u=t_j+t_k` of valuation zero because exactly one summand is a unit. Pivoting the positive-valuation share gives `(0,b−a,b−a)`. This proves the assertion when `b≤a` and the upper bound when `d=b−a>0`. In the latter case follow a word without adjacent repeated pivots (permutations can be commuted to the end by relabeling). Until the word exits `(−a,−a,b)`, pivots on its first two positions do not change those valuations. The exit gives one unit share and two shares of positive valuation `d,d`. The unit is precisely the last pivot, so pivoting it would undo the exit. Each other pivot, of valuation `i>0` with the other positive valuation `j>0`, gives valuations `(0,i,i+j)` up to order: the new unit is again precisely the last pivot. Induction to arbitrary length shows the two positive valuations always remain at least `d`; no reduced continuation brings the word back. Before exit there is exactly one positive share valuation, after exit exactly two. Since `v_s(h)=0`, positive share valuations are exactly denominator debts. ∎

**Integral-model explanation and extension (PROVED).** On the positive chart set `A_i=1/(t_j t_k)=x_j x_k/h²` (the complementary indices). These are on `W:(A₁+A₂+A₃)²=A₁A₂A₃`; conversely `t_i=A_i/ΣA`. Under the pivot at `i` the other two `A`'s are unchanged and

    A_i'=(A_j+A_k)²/A_i = A_j A_k−2A_j−2A_k−A_i.

The first equality follows by substitution, the second by Vieta on W. Thus the moves are polynomial involutions on this particular integral model, and even the three individual squareclasses `[A_i]` are preserved up to permutation. At `s∤Mn` the two lowest pair-product valuations in the trapped input are `a−b<0`. An integral endpoint on the original surface would give an integral point of W at `s`. Consequently **no** transformation on W with inverse defined there by `s`-integral polynomials can produce that endpoint: apply the inverse. This last test does not require classification of the automorphism group, positivity of intermediate points or that it be generated by these pivots. The obstruction is about extension over the valuation ring on **W**, not about every birational automorphism of the open rational surface. Regularity just over ℚ does not suffice. A supplies the stronger quantitative minimum for the three specified pivots.

**Archimedean normal form (PROVED, elementary Markoff reduction).** Repeatedly pivot the share `>1/2`, if present. This terminates for every positive real triple, and the sorted terminal shares are a complete orbit invariant for the pivots and permutations. In fact `r_i=√A_i>2` (the other two shares have product `<1/4`) satisfy `r₁²+r₂²+r₃²=r₁r₂r₃`. The pivot replaces `r_i` by `r_jr_k−r_i=(A_j+A_k)/r_i`, hence strictly decreases that coordinate iff the share exceeds half. Only one can do so. In an infinite descent the three labelled coordinates would have finite limits `R_i≥2`. At least two indices change infinitely often, since repeating an involution cannot decrease twice. At every such index `2R_i=R_jR_k` by convergence. Applying this at two indices forces equal limits there and the third limit 2, contrary to the Markoff equation at the limit (`4=0`). This proves termination without assuming the square roots are rational. On each pivot edge the higher endpoint reduces to the lower in one step; equality of heights means the pivot fixes the point. Thus normal form is constant on edges, and equality after sorting gives a word by reversing a reduction. This is a test for *orbit membership*, not an algorithm guaranteed to produce an integral point.

## 3. A height theorem for all linked halfway packets

**Theorem D (PROVED, elementary upper bound; prime-carrier sharpness CONDITIONAL on Schinzel H).** Let `n>1` be odd and `D>1` coprime to `2n`. In **every** positive packet `(x,y,Z/D)` of `4/n` with `x,y,Z` integral, `(D,Z)=1` and `D|gcd(x,y)`, one has `D=O(n⁴)` with absolute effective constant. By A this is **exactly** the stratum on which the pivot group can clear the original debt primes. All linked squarefree packets lie in it; an unlinked prime prevents local clearing. For `D=r²` on this stratum, `r=O(n²)`. No coordinate is held fixed and there is no restriction on which coordinates are divisible by a prime input. In contrast the *unlinked* prime `k` in the z81 jump is unbounded at each fixed prime. Conditional on H there are infinitely many primes `p≡1 (840)` with a linked **prime** `s=(1/44+o(1))p⁴`. This is sharpness for maximum achievable carrier size, **not** for the smallest carrier or any cap exponent.

*Proof of the upper bound.* Put `x=DA,y=DB`, interchanging them to have `A≤B`. The share difference gives

    DT AB = n Z(A+B),     T=4Z−nD>0,     D|A+B.

The last claim uses the coprimalities, not coprimality of `A` and `B`. Set `C=(A+B)/D≥1`. One obtains

    (4TA−n²)C D = nTC+4TA².                         (1)

Thus `4TA−n²` is a **positive integer**. It suffices to handle `D≥n`. From `T(2D−n)≤n²D` as in §1 we have `T≤n²` and `T/D≤n`. Also `A≤2nZ/(DT)`, hence `TA≤(n²+nT/D)/2≤n²`. Inserting these inequalities into (1), using `C≥1`, gives `D≤nT+4(TA)²/T≤n³+4n⁴`. ∎

**Sharp coefficient (PROVED, elementary).** At primes `p≡1 (840)`, (1) gives `D≤p⁴/44+O(p³)`. Put `k=4TA−p²`. For `D≥p`, expand (1) exactly as

    D = pT/k + p⁴/(4TCk) + p²/(2TC) + k/(4TC).

The non-leading terms total `O(p³)` uniformly by `T≤p²`, `TA≤p²`, `k≥1`. In fact `TCk≥11`. For a smaller product `k≡3 (4)` forces `k=3` or `7`, and `T` is odd. At `k=7`, `T=C=1`, but `7D=p+4A²` is impossible mod 7 (`p≡1`, `A≡2`). At `k=3`, `T=3` is impossible mod 3 from `4TA=p²+3`; else `T=1` and `A=(p²+3)/4` is odd and `1 mod 3`. Then `3CD=pC+4A²` forces `C≡2 mod 3`, so only `C=2` remains. But `3D=p+2A²` gives `D≡1 mod 4`, contrary to `4Z=pD+1`. This proves the coefficient. If `TCk>11`, the same proof gives `D≤p⁴/48+O(p³)`; equality of products `TCk=11` forces `T=C=1,k=11`. These are genuine restrictions on *all* the movable points in D, not merely on polynomial witnesses.
