## 2. A small-prime seed changes the log factors

**Theorem A (PROVED rel. the §0 inputs).** For $L=\log X$, $l=\log L$ and $K=L^\beta$, the prime count up to $X$ with $g(p)>K$ is

$$\ll X\exp(-c L^\delta/l^v),\qquad
(\beta,\delta,v)=\begin{cases}(1,1-1/(e\log3),1-1/(2e)),\\ (\log3,2/3,2/3).\end{cases}$$

Here $e=\exp(1)$. This improves $v=3/2$ in both cases of `GAP_ES` Thm C, not its primary exponents. There are two changes: supply a set of *distinct* small targets before conditioning the private letters, and at the endpoint use a whole tilted Poisson band, not its boundary layer.

**Proof (parameters and pin).** Work first on $X\le t<2X$. Set $a=\beta/\log3$, $u=1-\delta$, $s=a/u$ ($e$ or $3$ respectively), and
$$\log Z=L^u l^b,\qquad b=-1/(2s)\quad(s=e),\qquad b=-1/3\quad(s=3).$$
Use private primes $R<r\le Z$, $R=L^{20}$, of harmonic mass $H=u l+(b-1)\log l+O(1)$. Pin $t\bmod P$, $P$ the product of odd primes up to $y=K^{1/10000}$. Thus $\log P=O(y)=o(L)$. Only fibres with $(4t+1,P)=1$ need to be counted, since we eventually intersect with primes $4t+1$; no independence of primality and the fibres is used. On a fixed fibre $q\in[K/2,K]$, $q\equiv3\ (4)$, determines the radical $d$ of the odd $y$-smooth part of $m_q$ from the pin. Retain windows with $d\le K^{1/10}$, $\omega(d)\le 30\log l$. Then $(d,q)=1$: a common prime would divide $4t+1$. The $T_q=3^{\omega(d)}$ signed products of these primes are distinct units mod $q$: coprime numerator/denominator pairs have entries $\le d$, cross-products $\le d^2<q$, and unique reduced fractions. There are $T_q$ possible corrections for the rough signed product, translated by $-1$ and inversion.

**Seed supply uniformly in the pin.** With $s=e$ or $3$,
$$\sum_{q\ {\rm retained}} s^{\omega(d)}\gg K l^{s-1}.\tag{1}$$
To count $q$ at fixed $d$, write $q=4h-1$; $h$ runs over a consecutive interval of length $\asymp K$. The condition $d\mid t+h$ fixes one class. In that progression sieve out **one root** at each odd prime $r\le y$ not dividing $d$. No exclusions at primes of $d$: their higher powers do not alter the radical. The ordinary dimension-one lower-bound sieve gives $\gg K/(d\log y)$ rows, uniformly in the pin and in $d$. Explicitly use level $K^{1/3}$, $\log(K^{1/3})/\log y=10000/3$, the dimension bound from Mertens for the subset of primes, remainders $\le1$ per squarefree divisor and linear-sieve weights of absolute value $\le1$; the $O(K^{1/3})$ total remainder is negligible even at the largest $d$.

For the sum over $d$, the full Euler sum $\sum_d s^{\omega(d)}/d=\prod_{3\le r\le y}(1+s/r)\asymp l^s$ here runs over squarefree products of the odd primes indicated. Under its normalized weights, $\mathbb E\log d\le(s+o(1))\log y$, $\mathbb E\omega(d)\le(s+o(1))\log l$ by Mertens. Markov leaves a fixed positive mass under both cuts. The exact-radical events are disjoint; this proves (1).

**Uniform rough directions.** As in `GAP_ES` §8 (BV over dyads $[T,2T]$ for $T\ge R/2$, then partial summation), except at $O_A(K l^{-A})$ moduli, every unit class mod $q$ has band mass $(H+O(l^{-2}))/\varphi(q)$. Indeed the sum of maximum discrepancies is $O_B((\log R)^{1-B})$ for arbitrary fixed $B$, so discard at threshold $1/(K l^2)$. The loss to (1) is $O_A(K l^{-A+30\log s})$, negligible for fixed large $A$. These deletions are independent of the pin. On the CRT law the marks are independent across $r$, at any specified window with probability $1/r$. At exactly $j=O(l)$ marks the law can be summed by iid sampling primes with weights $1/(r-1)$ and discarding repeats, with prefactor $(1+o(1)) e^{-H}H^j/j!$. The replacement of $1/r$ costs $O(1/R)$ in each class; the residue likelihood ratio for the $j$ independent samples is $1+o(1)$ to uniform on the good moduli. The iid repeat probability $O(j^2/(RH))$ is negligible even against $1/K$.

**Group calculation for the seeded targets.** In $G=(\mathbb Z/q)^\times$, count all nonzero ternary exponent vectors on the $j$ uniform directions which land in the indicated set of $T_q$ classes. The mean is $(3^j-1)T_q/|G|=:\lambda$. Distinct supports have a unimodular minor and joint probability $(T_q/|G|)^2$. Equal or opposite nonzero vectors contribute at most $2\lambda$ to the second moment. For remaining pairs on the same support a two-by-two minor has determinant $\pm2$, hence an upper bound $|G[2]|(T_q/|G|)^2$; fix the other coordinates and use the Smith invariants $1,2$. There are at most $5^j$ such pairs. For $j\asymp l$, $(5/9)^j |G[2]|=o(1)$ since $q$ odd and $|G[2]|\le 2^{\omega(q)}=\exp(o(l))$. The second moment inequality gives hit probability $\gg\min(1,T_q3^j/K)$ on these layers; the constants are uniform over the seed sets. In particular the seed is not an unsupported correction in the generated subgroup.

**Layer calculation.** For $s=e$ take $j=\lceil al\rceil-\omega(d)$, positive eventually. Then $T_q3^j\ge K$ and $j=al+O(\log l)$. Stirling, with $I(z)=1-z+z\log z$, gives
$$e^{-H}H^j/j!\asymp L^{-u I(s)} l^{(b-1)(s-1)-1/2}s^{\omega(d)}.$$
The derivatives of the log mass in $H,j$ at $(ul,al)$ are $s-1$ and $-\log s$ up to $O(1/l)$; the quadratic Taylor error is $O((\log l)^2/l)$. Summing using (1) gives expected hits $\gg L^{\beta-u I(s)} l^{b(s-1)-1/2}$. Here $\beta-u I(s)=\delta$ and $b(s-1)-1/2=-1+1/(2s)$.

For $s=3$ use the layers $3H-2\sqrt{3H}\le j\le3H-\sqrt{3H}$, all with $T_q3^j\le K$ for large $X$: $3H=al+O(\log l)$ and $\log T_q=O(\log l)$. Tilting this time gives
$$\sum_j e^{-H}H^j 3^j/j! = e^{2H}\Pr_{J\sim{\rm Pois}(3H)}(3H-2\sqrt{3H}\le J\le3H-\sqrt{3H})\gg e^{2H}.$$
The last inequality is also an immediate Stirling lower bound on $\asymp\sqrt H$ layers. Thus total expected hits $\gg e^{2H}K^{-1}\sum T_q\gg L^{2/3}l^{2b}$. No $l^{-1/2}$ loss at this endpoint. These are means on each pinned CRT fibre, not on the actual interval.

**Transfer.** Thin the hits on each fibre by independent coins to have total CRT mean $\mu=c_0 L^\delta/l^v$ (a single common thinning probability per fibre suffices). The increasing success indicators on disjoint windows are negatively associated in the independent-prime digit model, as are the decreasing failures, by `GAP_ES` §8. Consequently its void is $\le e^{-\mu}$ and order-$j$ binomial moment $\le \mu^j/j!$. Transfer the even upper Bonferroni sum through order $m_0=2\lceil 5\mu\rceil$. For $m\le m_0$ specified windows the z81 Boolean–Möbius lemma, with depth $D=\lceil C m_0 l\rceil$, gives additive error
$$O\bigl(3^{D+1}(emH/(D+1))^{D+1}+(C' m Z)^{D+2}/\lfloor X/P\rfloor\bigr).\tag{2}$$
Indeed writing $t=t_0+Pj$, each compatible selection of distinct private primes fixes one class in $j$; $(P,r)=1$. This also works for fixed seed sets which change with the fibre and for tests averaged over coins. Choose a sufficiently large absolute $C$ so the first error summed over $\le(2K)^{m_0+1}$ tests is $O(e^{-2\mu})$. For the second, $D\log Z=O(c_0 L)$ since $1+b=v$, $D\log(2m_0)=o(L)$, $\log P=o(L)$, $m_0\log(2K)=o(L)$; take $c_0$ small to leave fixed power slack. The CRT truncation costs at most $(e\mu/(m_0+1))^{m_0+1}$. Sum the normalized bounds with the actual fibre lengths; no factor $P$ multiplies a probability. Now intersect with primes; a true void implies a thinned void. For $p$ dyads one can use $t$-dyads and replace $K$ by $(\log t_{\rm left})^\beta\le (\log p)^\beta$. For the cumulative statement sum these dyads up to $X$, discarding $p<\sqrt X$, which is negligible. The $3\bmod4$ prime case has $g=1$ (and bounded inputs are absorbed). ∎

## 3. Two sides of the private-letter truncation

**Theorem B (PROVED rel. BV, BT and (2)).** On integers $X\le t<2X$, use *only one copy* of each prime $L^{20}<r\le\exp(L^u)$ dividing $m_q$. Take all $q=3\bmod4$ in $[K/2,K]$, $K=L^\beta$, $0<u<1$, $0<\beta<2$ fixed. The same statement holds taking only primes among these $q$. Assume $a=\beta/\log3>u$. Define
$$D=\begin{cases}u I(a/u),&a\le3u,\\ \beta-2u,&a\ge3u,\end{cases}\qquad d=\beta-D.$$
If $d>0$ and $u+d<1$, the probability $V_X$ that **every one** of the specified private ternary boxes misses $-1$ satisfies
$$-\log V_X=L^{d+o(1)}.\tag{3}$$
Why test $u=.249$? Exposing $L^{.751+o(1)}$ windows at polynomial level permits $u\le1-.751=.249$ in the primary exponent. On this entire range $d\le2u\le.498$; at the largest feasible $u$, (3) gives $d=.495229\ldots$, not $.751$. Even with composite windows in that band, this gives a lower as well as an upper count of truncated avoiders, at the precise exponent (3). This is not a lower estimate on full misses, on primes, or on $E(X)$.

*One-window costs.* In the independent-prime CRT allocation, for good moduli the uniform-direction group bound of §2 with $T=1$ gives hit probability $\ge L^{-D+o(1)}$ by taking $j=\lfloor\min(a,3u)l\rfloor$: the layer costs $L^{-u I(j/(ul))+o(1)}$ and the hit within it $\gg\min(1,3^j/K)$. BV here also leaves $\gg K/l$ good prime moduli (discard $O(K/l^2)$). Conversely at **every** modulus the upper bound is $L^{-D+o(1)}$. Indeed BT and partial summation from $R=L^{20}$ give maximum harmonic class mass $\ll H/\varphi(q)$: on dyads $Y\ge R/2$, $\log(Y/q)\gg\log Y$. Conditional on $j$ iid directions, pin all but one nonzero component in any proposed ternary exponent vector. The last unit direction must equal a prescribed unit or its inverse, so the union bound gives $\min(1,C3^j/\varphi(q))$. Here $\varphi(q)=L^{\beta+o(1)}$ uniformly. Distinct-list summation is bounded by iid summation with the Poisson prefactor of §2.

To sum, discard $j>C_1l$ at arbitrary fixed log-power cost by taking $C_1$ large. Up to $al+o(l)$ tilt by $3^j$ (Poisson mean $3H$), above it use the ordinary upper tail. Equivalently maximize $-u I(x/u)+\min(0,x\log3-\beta)$ on $x\ge0$: the maximum is $-D$, attained at $\min(a,3u)$. Thus the sum of marginal hit probabilities is $L^{d+o(1)}$. This last calculation sharpens the rare-supply lower bound in `SPECIFIC_CLASS` J by an upper bound for the *specified* residue, not all-target incompleteness.

*The needed reverse inequality.* Negative association gives an **upper** void bound and factorial upper bounds but no lower bound. To get the latter, on each prime row $r$ start with $J$ independent Bernoulli indicators of parameter $1/(r-J+1)$, $J$ the number of windows. Conditional on at most one success at each $r$ this is exactly the exclusive-mark law: the odds of a specific mark to no mark at $r$ are $1/(r-J)$. All-window miss is decreasing, as is the conditioning event. Harris's inequality for the unconditioned independent bits therefore gives
$$\Pr_{\rm CRT}(\text{void})\ \ge\ \prod_{q}(1-p_q^+),$$
where $p_q^+$ is the marginal hit probability in the *intensified independent* system. Its exact-list odds are $1/(r-J)$ instead of $1/(r-1)$, relative factor $1+O(J/R)$ per prime; the no-mark probability per window is $\exp(-H+O(J/R))$ by expansion and $\sum_{r>R}1/r^2\ll1/R$. The preceding BT upper mixture and $j>C_1l$ tail thus still give $p_q^+\le L^{-D+o(1)}=o(1)$ uniformly. (It would be invalid to transfer an $O(J^2/R)$ total variation error to an exponentially small void.) Together with the negative-association upper bound, the CRT void is $\exp(-L^{d+o(1)})$.

*Two-sided transfer.* Fix any small $\epsilon>0$ with $d+u+2\epsilon<1$. Use the adjacent upper and lower Bonferroni polynomials of orders $m_0,m_0-1$, $m_0$ even of size $L^{d+\epsilon}$. In CRT the gap is its $m_0$-th binomial moment $\le(e L^{d+o(1)}/m_0)^{m_0}$, negligible **relative** to its void. In the interval transfer every term via (2) at $P=1$, $D$ there replaced by $\lceil C m_0 l\rceil$. Even after the $\exp(O(m_0 l))$ terms, the error is $O(\exp(-2m_0)+X^{-1/2})$, again negligible relative to the void. This proves (3) for the actual interval, not just a comparison distribution. ∎

**PROVED (scoped exponent budget).** In the CRT model the same BT upper bound for the expected *number of hits* from private letters is particularly transparent without a Poisson saddle: sum $O(e^{2H}/\varphi(q))$ over the band, obtaining $O(e^{2H})$ since $\sum_{K/2<q\le K}1/\varphi(q)\ll1$ (expand $q/\varphi(q)=\sum_{d\mid q}\mu^2(d)/\varphi(d)$). This is an upper bound on available **mean vs transfer** in that recipe, not an absolute minimum degree for every void proof. For the lower bound on the actual void use the strict hypotheses of (3), not an extrapolation through the endpoint. Multiplying the level by a fixed power under EH changes neither primary exponent. The extra seed of A repairs a logarithmic loss, not a primary exponent. Any use of a full-box comparison at $.751$ must not silently substitute these retained letters. In particular at fixed $.751$ the full void could be far smaller than the truncated one.

## 4. Pay separately for character energy and for the phase

**Proposition C (CONDITIONAL on (U) and (P), neither a standard conjecture).** We separate a positive energy estimate from an additional ES-adjacent phase-independence hypothesis. We have not widened the margin of z81 (M) itself. On the same prime band $L^\beta<q\le2L^\beta$, $q=3\bmod4$, $\beta=.751$, $k\sim L^\beta/(2\beta l)$, the following two interval comparisons suffice for $E(N)\ll N\exp(-c(\log N)^{.751})$. Their **combined** allowed loss is $\exp(.05kl)$. This is a trade, not a proof of z81 (M): we have added a phase test. Even the original $\exp(10^{-7}kl)$ tolerates $C^k$ for each fixed $C$ eventually; it does not tolerate $\exp(c k\log k)$ for general $c>0$.

For integral $X$, average on integers $X\le t<2X$. Use the band $\mathcal B=(\exp(L^{.01}),X]$ of primes. Set $b_i=\prod_{r\in\mathcal B,\,r\mid m_i} r$, $m_i=t+h_i$, $J_i=\omega(b_i)$ and $M_i=q_i-1$. Let $P_i(y)$ be the mass at the unit $y\bmod q_i$ of the random product of independent uniform ternary exponents on these primes (given $t$). Use the bounded nonnegative test $A_i(y)=\min(1,|1-M_iP_i(y)|^2)$ and $\bar A_i=M_i^{-1}\sum_y A_i(y)$. All these quantities depend on $t$. Write $1_U=1[(4t+1,\prod_i q_i)=1]$. Our phase comparison is, uniformly over **fixed** unit translations $c_i\bmod q_i$,
$$\mathbb E_t 1_U\prod_i A_i(c_i b_i/m_i)\le e^{\eta_P kl}\mathbb E_t 1_U\prod_i\bar A_i+e^{-kl}.\tag{P}$$
Inverses are used only on $U$. This asks whether the evaluation phases correlate excessively with the squared fluctuations of $k$ multiplicative convolutions. The test includes nonempty targets with nonuniform weight, and the right side randomises only the evaluation points, **not** the lists of factors. Although not written as an ES count, its left side majorizes the joint miss rate of these complementary-divisor trials on unit rows: a zero mass at the designated target costs one. In particular when $m_i=b_i$ the target is the fixed $c_i$ itself; with $c_i=-1$ this is precisely the window box test. The enlarged error budget rests on (P), not just on a better positive multiplicative weight. Uniformity in all $c_i$ is optional; sufficiency needs just $c_i=-1$. A complementary divisor explains that choice: if $b_i/m_i$ times $-1$ occurs, multiplying the ratio by $m_i/b_i$ produces $-1$ with signed exponents bounded by the actual valuations of $m_i$. Repeated factors cause no issue. This extra available direction is not a second independent integer.

Set $h=.99$, $a=\beta/\log3$, $w=a/h$, $z=3a/h<3$. Define $M(f)=\exp\sum_{r\in\mathcal B}(f(r)-1)/r$. A function listed by its prime weights is 1 off band and takes the same value on all positive powers in band.

At each $q_i$ form the following list $\mathcal T_i$ of scalar/function pairs:

* $(w^{-al},w^{J_i})$, where the second entry denotes the evaluation at $m_i$ of the multiplicative function with constant band weight $w$;
* $(1,g_\chi)$ for the single quadratic character, with band weights $g_\chi(r)=((1+2\Re\chi(r))/3)^2$;
* $(z^{-al},z^{\omega_{\mathcal B}}g_\chi)$ for each character of order $>2$.

Use the positive comparison
$$\mathbb E_t\prod_i\sum_{(c,f)\in\mathcal T_i}c\min(L^2,f(m_i))\ \le\ e^{\eta_U kl}\prod_i\sum_{(c,f)\in\mathcal T_i}c M(f).\tag{U}$$
Assume $\eta_U,\eta_P\ge0$, $\eta_U+\eta_P\le .05$. (U) alone has the shape of z81's (M), but different weights; the arbitrary-prime-weight variant there with $o(kl)$ loss would supply it. We do not have (P) as a consequence of (U).

*Proof.* Parseval before averaging the interval gives $\bar A_i\le\min(1,\sum_{\chi\ne1}g_\chi(m_i))$. For $J_i<al$ the first charge exceeds one. Otherwise the third charge dominates $g_\chi(m_i)$ and the quadratic term is unchanged. Clipping can only cut a charge to a value $\ge1$ since $c L^2\ge1$ for all these coefficients. Thus each sum in (U) majorizes $\bar A_i$. The exact group mean of a band prime weight $g_\chi$ is $1/3$ for order $>2$ and $5/9$ for order 2, by character orthogonality. SW and partial summation on $r>\exp(L^{.01})$ give these means times $hl+O(1)$ with $o(1)$ discrepancy in the weighted harmonic sums, uniformly on this fixed log-power band (arbitrarily strong log-power error in the prime estimate). The three Euler costs have log exponents respectively
$$-al\log w+h(w-1)l,\qquad -(4/9)hl,\qquad [\beta-a\log z+h(z/3-1)]l,$$
up to $O(1)$ per list after summing characters. First and third equal $-[h-a+a\log(a/h)]l$. This rate is $0.05324\ldots>.05$; the quadratic cost is smaller. The use of **squared** Fourier coefficients accounts for the change from the z81 high-order mean $.4707$ to $1/3$. For any failing prime $4t+1$ the row is in $U$ and every designated evaluation has $P_i=0$, giving a contribution of 1. (P) then (U) gives $\ll X\exp(-c kl)$ such primes. The positive margin survives multiplication over all $k$ windows. Sum dyadically and use the Rankin transfer of `paper/es-threequarter-note.tex` §“All denominators” with $3/4$ replaced by $.751$ (its inequality $L^{\theta-1}s\le s^\theta$ uses only $s\le L$, $0<\theta<1$). ∎

**PROVED (short-band phase check).** In the private-prime CRT model with independent digits also at the $q_i$, (P) is an equality before either loss: given all marks and $U$, the vector $(m_i\bmod q_i)$ is uniform in the product of unit groups. There is even an *interval* comparison with additive error $O(e^{-2kl})$ if one cuts $\log Z\le L/(Bkl)$, $B$ sufficiently large, and uses band primes larger than the shifts and all $q_i$. Pin $t$ mod $Q=\prod_i q_i$, $\log Q=O(kl)=o(L)$. In each pinned progression apply (2) with $m=k$, depth $Ckl$ ($C$ then $B$ sufficiently large), to the bounded product on the left or right; $H\le l+O(1)$. Averaging the pins with their actual interval weights differs from uniform weighting by $O(Q/X)$. The CRT equality proves the assertion. This does not prove (P) with the full band: the actual unpinned factor lists are not independent of the $q_i$ digits. Nor does a single order-$k$ Elliott or Chowla conjecture with fixed $k$ and unspecified $o_k(1)$ error yield either comparison at $k\asymp L^{.751}/l$. Clipping a multiplicative function generally destroys multiplicativity; bounded *prime weights* are not 1-bounded nonpretentious functions on integers.

**Proposition H (PROVED rel. the explicit shape of Henriot, 2014 erratum, New Theorem 5, and Mertens).** A growing-dimensional obstruction to applying the published theorem *as stated* is already visible at $F=1$. Take our $k$ distinct monic linear forms $t+h_i$, scale $X$, length $X$. Its displayed sieve product times its **product-truncated** local sum $n_1\cdots n_k\le X$ is at most
$$\exp[-k\log k+O(k)].\tag{4}$$
Consequently any uniform extension at these parameters retaining this right side needs a prefactor $\ge \exp(k\log k-O(k))$ (or an onset excluding these $X$). This is a lower bound on a necessary constant, not an upper bound on the unknown optimal one. It does **not** refute a differently normalized Euler-product or clipped comparison such as (U).

### 6.2 Rare matches, length control and collisions

**PROVED (one-window probability calculation).** In a comparison law take independent bits for the $J$ retained windows at each $r$ with parameter $1/(r-J+1)$; also independent across $r$. Conditioning on at most one mark at every $r$ gives exactly the honest categorical CRT law. This conditioning has probability $1-O(J^2/R)>1/2$ by a union bound, independent of the small pin. Thus *any upper bound for a nonnegative test* in the independent-bit law transfers to CRT up to factor 2. This use does not require monotonicity of the exact-list events.

Given $j$ iid uniform unit directions, one specified nonzero ternary vector has uniform product. Conditional on hitting a particular target, any other vector except itself or its opposite lands in $\mathcal A_e$ with probability at most $|G_e[2]|\,|\mathcal A_e|/\varphi(e)$, $G_e=(\mathbb Z/e)^\times$. Indeed there is a minor of determinant $\pm1$ or $\pm2$ (§2), giving the bound on the fibre of the joint homomorphism. The opposite cannot hit since inverse targets are disjoint. Thus the probability of a unique **active** match (unique even among inactive candidates) is comparable to $3^j N_e(z)/\varphi(e)$, uniformly: the extra conditional collision cost is at most
$$3^j |G_e[2]| K^{2\gamma}/\varphi(e)=o(1).$$
For the polylogarithmic case $3^j=L^{\log3+o(1)}$ since $3H=l+O(\log l)$; for $B=L^\alpha$ it is $\exp(O(l))=K^{o(1)}$. In both cases $|G_e[2]|\le2^{\omega(e)}=K^{o(1)}$. $$\sum_e p_e^+(z)\asymp e^{2H}\sum_e N_e(z)/\varphi(e)\asymp B T^2=:W.\tag{7}$$

### 6.3 A relative sieve for the list exclusions

$$\prod_b U_b-\sum_b(U_b-V_b)\prod_{j\ne b}U_j.$$

*Proof.* Revert to the small **physical pin** of §2, on $X\le t<2X$ with $(4t+1,P)=1$, and notation $q$ for windows. The iid/exact-list comparison works with the intensified odds of §6.2 just as with $1/(r-1)$. On a fixed pin, the acceptance and the target set are determined by $q$ and the one exact list. No unique-vector restriction is necessary: no residual small congruence needs to be expressed as a union of classes. Here these tests only use **band hits** on the chosen layer(s), not the increasing success indicators of A.

## 8. The three-quarter gap endpoint and its two charts

**PROVED (endpoint of G; direct variant, and provenance).** Take an internal cutoff $K_0=e^B$ with $B=c L^{1/4}$, $0<c<C$. ## 10. A small Parseval defect can coexist with the missing involution

**Lemma F (PROVED, finite extremizer and arithmetic realization).** Let $M$ be even, $P$ a probability on a cyclic group of order $M$, and $D=\sum_{\chi\ne1}|\widehat P(\chi)|^2=M\sum P(x)^2-1$, with unnormalized character sums of the probability. A specific missing cell forces $D\ge1/(M-1)$ by Cauchy on the other $M-1$ entries; equality is attained by uniform measure off that cell. This sharp factor $(M-1)$ in $1_{P(-1)=0}\le (M-1)D$ is not merely a shifted abstract hole. In a cyclic group of order $3^j+1$ take directions of discrete logs $1,3,\ldots,3^{j-1}$. Their independent uniform ternary digits produce every integer in $[-(3^j-1)/2,(3^j-1)/2]$ exactly once by balanced base 3. This is exactly the extremizer with the missing cell the involution. We do not assert primality of $3^j+2$ infinitely often.

There is a polynomial loss even for **actual prime window groups** with uniform independent ternary exponents. For sufficiently large $j$ take a prime $q=3\bmod4$ in $(3^j+2,3^j+2+3^{3j/5}]$ (fixed-modulus short interval PNT of Huxley as in `Z58_TRYO.md` §2.1; $3/5>7/12$). In $\mathbb F_q^\times$ take the same logarithms to a primitive root. Then the involution is absent while $D=(q-1)/3^j-1\ll q^{-2/5}$. These directions can be residues of distinct prime letters by Dirichlet. If an arithmetic complementary target as in C is desired, take $m=b$ equal to the product of those prime letters, so $-b/m=-1$; one can even locate $q$ in the band of C. Choose all letters in the dyad starting at $Y=\exp((q/1.5)^{1/.751}/j)$; SW gives primes there in each designated class since $q\ll(\log Y)^2$. Then $\log m=(q/1.5)^{1/.751}+O(j)$; with the dyad for $t=(4m-q-1)/4$ starting at $X=t$, the letters belong to the band of C and $q/(\log X)^{.751}\to1.5$. With $n=4m-q$, the window, factors and target are then on one integer (not asserted prime, or simultaneously hard at other windows). This construction demonstrates the failure of a **pointwise scalar Parseval replacement** for (P); it does not refute the averaged phase comparison or eliminate (P) in Proposition C. In particular translations of arbitrary phases cannot be averaged away merely because the individual convolution is close to uniform in normalized $L^2$. Lemma T gives a different, absolute-Fourier test limitation. Neither says that every possible positive bilinear statistic is phase blind.
