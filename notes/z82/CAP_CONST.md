## 1. The exact chart, including its composite-input trap

**PROVED (rel. `CAP_INTERPOLATION.md` §5, elementary).** Put \(f=4A-1\), \(n\equiv-4D\pmod f\), \(V=(n+4D)/f\), for positive integers \(A,D,n\), \(n\ge2\). Then
\[
 x=VA-D=(n+V)/4,\quad y=nA,\quad z=nxA/D.
\]
The identity follows from \(x+D=VA\), so \(1/y+1/z=V/(nx)\). Conversely a triple with this \(y\) gives the positive integer \(D=fx-nA\) and hence this same formula. In the **unit chart** \((n,Df)=1\), let \(a=v_s(A), d=v_s(D)>0\). Then \(s\nmid V\); if \(a<d\), \(v_s(z)=2a-d\); if \(a\ge d\), \(v_s(z)\ge a\) (equality is not needed or asserted at a tie). For cap \(E\), precisely the following exponents including zero are legal:
\[
 d=0\quad (a=0);\qquad d\in[0,2a]\ \cup\ \{2a+2,\ldots,2a+2E\}_{\rm even}\quad(a>0).
\]
At 2 delete the second interval. Indeed for \(a=0<d\) the prime of the reduced denominator is not in \(y\); otherwise the negative valuations must be even and at least \(-2E\). The root is odd, coprime to \(n\), with radical dividing \(y\). No primes outside \(D\) overflow. Write \(\mathcal C_E(f)\) for the resulting classes \(-4D\) at this \(f\), without imposing \(4D<f\). They are unit nonsquares: \((s/f)=1\) for each odd \(s\mid A\) by reciprocity, and \((2/f)=1\) if \(2\mid A\). Thus \((-4D/f)=-1\) (Jacobi). At prime inputs the chart covers all triples with \(p\mid y,\ p\nmid xf\); it does not classify triples with no such denominator.

**PROVED (V is a subchart, not a new coordinate).** In z81 Thm V, \(A=arvw\) and \(D=a^2r^4w\); then \(4Dv\equiv ar^3\pmod f\) and substituting \(S=(nv+ar^3)/f\) gives exactly \(x=arwS\), \(z=nvwS/r^2\), not just the same residue. ## 2. Upper exceptional count at every fixed cap

**Theorem H (PROVED, rel. inputs in §0).** Write \(b=E+2\), \(B=b+1=E+3\) for fixed integer \(E\ge1\). Then for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
 \#\{2\le n\le N:e^*(n)>E\}\ll_E N\exp[-c_E(\log N)^{B/(B+1)}].
\]
Constants/thresholds need not be uniform in \(E\); the proof does not take \(E\to\infty\) with \(N\).

*Proof.* Let \(G\) be the odd primes with a positive integral ES solution. Vaughan and partial summation give \(\sum_{p>P,p\notin G}1/p\ll_M(\log P)^{-M}\) for each fixed \(M>0\) (sum over odd primes). A failing input is odd and has no prime factor in \(G\), by **integral** scaling. Let \(Z=e^t\), use the disjoint full dyads \((T,2T]\) of large primes \(\ell\) inside \((\sqrt Z,Z]\). Put \(\delta=1/(100b^2)\), \(Y=T^\delta\), \(K_0=\lfloor Z^{\delta/10000}\rfloor\). Set \(j_1=1,\ j_i=2(i-1)\) for \(2\le i\le b\). With pairwise coprime odd squarefree \(Y<u_i\le Y^2\) take
\[
 Q=4\prod_i u_i,\qquad D=\prod_i u_i^{j_i},\qquad f=k\ell\equiv-1\pmod Q.
\]
Keep \(u_2,\ldots,u_b\) supported on \(G\); no such restriction on \(u_1\). The support restriction on \(u_2\) is harmless but optional: if \(s\mid(n,u_2)\) then \(s\mid x\) and \(v_s(n x A)\ge3>2\). We keep it for uniform bookkeeping. Then \(Q<T^{1/10},\ 4D<T^{1/10}\) eventually since \(\sum j_i=1+b(b-1)\). At a hit, a failure would satisfy \((n,\prod_{i\ge2}u_i)=1\); the exponent list at all such primes works for every \(v_s(A)\ge1\). Even if \(s\mid(n,u_1)\), then \(v_s(D)=1\le v_s(A)\), \(s\mid x\) and no overflow occurs there. This proves every genuine hit excludes failure. At a common \(\ell\), equality of classes forces equality of \(D<\ell\), hence of all labels by distinct exponents; \(k\ell\equiv-1\pmod Q,\ k<Q\) then recovers \(k\). There is no multiplicity factor for the number of factorizations of a root.

*Uniform supply.* Fix a large constant \(P=P(E)\), and let \(k\le K_0\) be supported on \(G\cap(P,\infty)\) with \(\sum_{p\mid k}1/p\le(\log P)^{-2b-4}\). Such \(k\) have positive lower density before truncation: avoidance of all primes \(\le P\) and the bad primes above \(P\) has density \(\gg1/\log P\) by the Euler product (tail error by counting multiples up to the endpoint). The reciprocal restriction removes upper density \(O((\log P)^{2b+4}/P)\). Thus \(\sum_k1/k\asymp_E t\). Pin *any* unit \(c\) modulo the lcm of these multipliers. Require \(4D\equiv-c\pmod k\) (“active”). Given unit \(u_2,\ldots,u_b\) this is **one reduced class** for \(u_1\). Uniformly in \(c,k,T\),
\[
 \sum_{\text{active at }k}1/Q\gg_E t^b/k,\qquad \sum_{\text{active at }k}1/\varphi(Q)\ll_E t^b/k. \tag{1}
\]
Here the sums are over potential labels before the prime test. To detail the lower bound, take full ordinary dyads of lengths \(U_i\asymp\) the corresponding lower endpoints within the boxes. Count the free coordinates \(i\ge2\) coprime to primes \(\le P\); delete multiples of primes of \(k\), a relative loss against unrestricted volume \(O_b((\log P)^{-2b-4}+\log k/Y)\). At these tuples the reduced class for \(u_1\), sieved only by the fixed primes up to \(P\), has \((\prod_{p\le P}(1-1/p)+o(1))U_1/k\) representatives by elementary CRT. This starts the count at \(\gg_b (\log P)^{-b}\prod U_i/k\). Relative to the *unrestricted* volume \(\prod U_i/k\): bad primes above \(P\) in any free coordinate cost \(O_b(\sum_{p>P,p\notin G}1/p)\) since at \(p\le2U_i\) the multiple count is \(O(U_i/p)\) and \(\#u_1\ll U_1/k\); squares in a free coordinate, or a common prime of two free coordinates, cost \(O_b(1/P)\) by the same full-interval bound. Squares \(p^2\mid u_1\) cost \(O(1/P+k/\sqrt{U_1})\) using the reduced progression and \(p\le\sqrt{2U_1}\). For common primes with \(u_1\), at fixed other labels the count for \(p\mid u_i\) is \(O(U_1/(kp)+1)\) for each \(p>P\); averaging over \(u_i\) costs \(O_b(1/P+k\log Z/Y)\). These losses total less than half the starting density by fixing \(P\) sufficiently large, then \(t\) large. Sum reciprocal weights over \(\asymp_b t^b\) dyad products. For the upper bound drop the restrictions after bounding \(Q/\varphi(Q)\le 2\prod u_i/\varphi(u_i)\); in one reduced class mod \(k\), \(\sum_{u\asymp U}u/\varphi(u)\ll U/k+\log(2U)\) by expanding in \(d\mid u\) with weights \(\mu^2(d)/\varphi(d)\), and \(\sum_{d\le x}1/\varphi(d)\ll\log(2x)\). The same bound at \(k=1\) handles unrestricted variables. This proves (1).

*Discrepancy with the pinned coordinate.* Let \(I_Q\) count active label/multiplier requests at \(Q\). Cauchy over the at most \(\tau_b(Q/4)\) factorizations gives
\[
 \sum_Q I_Q^2/\varphi(Q)\ll_E t^{O_E(1)}.
\]
Indeed expand joint activity in \(k,k'\), \(h=[k,k']\le K_0^2<Y^{1/10}\); given the free coordinates it is impossible or imposes a single reduced class on \(u_1\bmod h\), because both activations use the same pinned unit. Bound the factorization weight by \(\prod_i\tau_b(u_i)\) and \(Q/\varphi(Q)\ll\log\log(3Q)\). Shiu on each dyad of \(u_1\) in that class gives \(\ll_b U_1 t^{b-1}/\varphi(h)\); the free coordinates have means \(O_b(t^{b-1})\). Sum dyads and use \(\sum_{k,k'\le K_0}1/\varphi([k,k'])\ll t^4\) by writing \(k=ga_1,k'=ga_2,(a_1,a_2)=1\). BT gives maximal dyadic discrepancy \(\Delta_Q\ll T/(t\varphi(Q))\) and BV \(\sum_{Q<T^{.1}}\Delta_Q\ll_C T/t^C\) for any fixed \(C\). Hence \(\sum I_Q\Delta_Q\le(\sum\Delta_Q\sum I_Q^2\Delta_Q)^{1/2}=O_E(T/t^{10})\). The main count uses the reduced class \(-1/k\bmod Q\); (1) and the multiplier harmonic sum give active prime reciprocal mass \(\asymp_E t^b\) per large-prime dyad. Thus on every unit fibre the total mass is \(\asymp_E t^B\).

*Transfer.* Repair nonunit multiplier digits to 1 prime by prime as in `notes/z79/WILD_ES.md` §3 (whole tower at that prime). A genuine failure is unchanged there because the multipliers are \(G\)-supported. Conditional on the repaired digits in ordinary integer Haar, the large-prime digits are independent. The hit count is a sum of independent Bernoullis *over primes*, since the active targets are disjoint at one prime. For mean \(\mu\asymp_E t^B\), the even-degree truncation of \((1-1/2)^{J_{\rm hit}}\) in binomial coefficients of the hit count \(J_{\rm hit}\) majorises the no-hit indicator; its mean is \(\le e^{-\mu/2}+(\mu/2)^{J+1}/(J+1)!\ll e^{-c_E t^B}\) at a sufficiently large even \(J\asymp_E t^B\). The number of indexed cells and the class expansion of each repaired test mod \(k\ell\) are \(\exp(O_E(t))\), so ordinary interval CRT counting with at most one boundary error per elementary intersection contributes \(\exp(O_E(t^{B+1}))\) after expanding the polynomial. Taking \(t=\alpha_E(\log N)^{1/(B+1)}\) with a sufficiently small positive constant proves H. No growth theorem on \(E\) is inferred. ∎

\]

### Sharp growing-cap transition for the ordinary prime-modulus sieve

**Theorem L (PROVED, elementary upper bound on the sieve denominator).** This limit allows *all* supports, separately at every prime, and untruncated labels. In the ordinary Montgomery arithmetic large-sieve expression \((N+Q^2)/\mathcal L(Q)\), allow \(\omega_\ell\) forbidden classes from the full \(\mathcal C_E(\ell)\) if \(\ell\equiv3\ (4)\) is prime, zero otherwise. Write \(\lambda=\log\log N\). If \(E\le\lambda^{1+\eta+o(1)}\) for fixed \(0\le\eta<2\), then **uniformly in the sieve level** \(Q\ge1\),
\[
 \frac{N+Q^2}{\mathcal L(Q)}\ \ge\ N^{1-\eta/2-o(1)}.
\]
In particular at \(E=\lambda^{1+o(1)}\) no fixed power saving via this expression, not even by combining all the untruncated prime boxes. This settles the logarithmic-power *threshold of the expression*: on the positive side `EXSET_LINNIK_CAPS.md` Thms 4.2,4.5 already obtain fixed power savings with selected prime classes at every fixed \(\eta>0\) (PROVED rel. their inputs). This is not a barrier on the true void, on a large sieve with extra non-class information, or a variable-cap composite-modulus KARY theorem. At a given prime one may also add a single zero residue (for large \(\ell\) just changing an absolute factor below; omit any fully covered modulus).

*Proof.* The classes are in one nonsquare coset, so \(\omega_\ell\le(\ell-1)/2\); also \(\omega_\ell\le F_E((\ell+1)/4)\) with \(F_E(p^a)=2a+1+E\) as above. \[

## 4. What partial orders can supply at the primorial budget

**Theorem P (PROVED, elementary).** Write \(U=\log X\), \(Y=U^a\) for a fixed \(a>1\), and fix \(\epsilon_0>0\). Let \(H\) satisfy \(\log(2H)\le(\log U)^{C+o(1)}\), with fixed \(1\le C<a/(a-1)\). The number of integers \(1\le s\le X\) for which **some odd** \(h\le H\) has

\]

*Proof.* At \(t\) the number \(\rho_h(t)\) of roots is \(\gcd(h,t-1)\) for odd \(h\), by cyclicity (equivalently negate the roots of unity). Put \(M_h=\sum_{t\le Y}\rho_h(t)/t\) over odd primes. Expanding the gcd in totients and even dropping primality gives
\[
 M_h\le\sum_{d\mid h,d\le Y}\varphi(d)\sum_{1\le j\le Y/d}(1+jd)^{-1}\le(1+\log Y)\#\{d\mid h:d\le Y\}.
\]

Let \(j=\lfloor\log X/\log Y\rfloor\). A product of \(j\) such primes is \(\le X\); over this product every root class contributes at most twice its Haar main count on \([1,X]\). ## 8. A least-prime conjecture suffices for the polylogarithmic log-cap

**Theorem Q (CONDITIONAL on Chowla's least-prime conjecture, rel. the z81 I carrier and packet argument).** Assume that for each fixed \(\epsilon>0\) every reduced class modulo \(q\) has a prime \(\ll_\epsilon q^{1+\epsilon}\), uniformly in the class and modulus (the usual Chowla conjecture; see Li–Pratt–Shakan, *A lower bound for the least prime in an arithmetic progression*, introduction). For each fixed \(C>2\), on **all** inputs as \(R=\mathrm{rad}(n)\to\infty\) there is a packet with root 1 or one prime power, and
\[
 \log(1+e)\ll_C (\log U)^C,\qquad U=\log R.
\]
Additionally assuming Dirichlet GRH gives the same at \(U=\log_2R\). No AP asymptotic is assumed in the supply step: just **one prime** in one class for each smooth modulus. The first conclusion, \((\log_2 R)^C\) on Chowla alone, does not assume GRH. The second **recovers** the \((\log_3 R)^C\) rate of z80 §12 under weaker assumptions, rather than improving that exponent: z80's uniform Montgomery hypothesis implies Dirichlet GRH (fixed-conductor argument there) and Chowla. Indeed, for a reduced class mod \(q\), take a unit lift mod \(h=24q\) and \(x=h^{1/\theta}\) at any fixed \(\theta<1\) sufficiently near 1. *Proof.* Put \(y=U^A\), \(z=(\log y)^C\), with \(A=A(C)\) large. Make moduli \(d\) from \(v=\lfloor\log y/\log z\rfloor\) distinct primes of \((z/2,z]\). There are at most \(y^{2\epsilon+o(1)}\) possible odd quotients \(b=(q-1)/(2d)\). Keep the most frequent: its primes are distinct since \(q=1+2bd\) determines \(d\).
