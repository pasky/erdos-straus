This comparison is uniform in the arbitrary fixed digits, and does not condition k to be prime.

Put `j=⌊t log l⌋`. There are `≥K l^{-I(t)+o(1)}` levels having exactly j small marks, `I(t)=1−t+t log t` (constant factors absorbed in the o(1)). In the product law this is one factorial layer: replace exact Bernoulli weights by `∏(1−1/q)(∑1/(q−1))^j/j!` with relative `1−o(1)` for no repeats, since each q exceeds l². Stirling gives the exponent. The product b of these j primes satisfies `b²≤y^{2j}=K^{o(1)}<k`; also `(b,k)=1` since all n-digits were units. Their `3^j` signed rational ratios are distinct mod k: cross multiplication has two positive terms ≤b²<k and hence can be congruent only when equal as integers. They enlarge the target for the remaining factors to a definite set in `G=(ℤ/k)^×` of size m=`3^j` (negatives of the inverse ratios).

Take `R=L^{20}`, `log Z=L^u l^s`, where `s=−1/(2e)` at β=1 and `s=−1/3` at β=log 3. By the dyadic BV and partial summation of z81 §8 the maximum discrepancy of each harmonic residue mass in `(R,Z]` is `≤1/(K l²)` for all except `O_B(K l^{-B})` moduli k≤K, for each B. Discard those k: the rich-level lower bound still holds. The remaining large-band CRT allocation is independent by prime across rows of digits, negatively associated across windows (`R>2K`), unchanged by the small fibre. Its large-prime intensity at each retained level is `H=u l+(s−1)log l+O(1)`.

For d iid uniform directions in G, count the nonzero signed exponent vectors landing in the specified target of size m. Its mean is `(3^d−1)m/|G|`. Pairs with different supports are independent (a unit 2-by-2 minor); opposite/identical pairs contribute `≤2·3^d m/|G|`, and other same-support pairs `≤5^d |G[2]|m²/|G|²` in the second moment. Thus when `d≍l`, `|G[2]|≤2^{ω(k)}=L^{o(1)}`, Paley–Zygmund gives a lower bound `≫min(1,3^d m/K)` for the probability of a hit. In this *large band*, after conditioning on d distinct marks, iid residue probabilities have likelihood ratio `1+o(1)` by the harmonic BV bound. The mass of repeated primes in the iid law is `O(d²/(R H))`, negligible **also relative to the smallest hit probabilities used below** (`3^d m/K≥1/K`, β≤log 3). The exact layer probability times the hit bound is therefore `≫ exp(−H) H^d/d! min(1,3^{d+j}/K)` uniformly on the d-ranges used here. All these Bernoulli probabilities concern integer CRT digits, not primes independently sampled at the various shifts.

At β=1 keep `d=⌈l/log 3⌉−j`. The threshold term is 1. Stirling gives, uniformly at every retained level, hit probability at least

    L^{−u I(t)} l^{(s−1)(t−1)−1/2+t log t+o(1)}.

The new `t log t` is the gain from lowering the required number of long-band letters by j. Multiplying by the supply of rich k costs `l^{-I(t)+o(1)}`; since `t log t−I(t)=t−1`, the total mean is `≥L^δ l^{s(t−1)−1/2+o(1)}=L^δ l^{−1−s+o(1)}`.

At β=log 3 retain **all** d between `3H−2√(3H)` and `3H−√(3H)`. These are below `log K/log 3−j` since `3H=l+O(log l)` and `√l≫log l`. Tilting by `3^d` changes the Poisson mean to 3H, and the new distribution has positive mass on this interval (Stirling uniformly, or Poisson CLT). Hence the probability is `≫3^j e^{2H}/K`. Multiplying by the supply gives `≥L^{2u} l^{2(s−1)+3 log 3−I(3)+o(1)}=L^δ l^{2s+o(1)}`. There is **no** single-layer `l^{-1/2}` loss. Here `2s=−1−s`.

To transfer, select just the retained k in that fibre and thin their increasing *actual box-hit* indicators (using any number of long letters, not “exactly d”) by independent coins to common probability `μ/J`, with `μ=L^δ/l^{1+s+ε/2}` and J the retained level count. The uniform lower bounds justify thinning. z81 §8's rare-hit argument applies conditionally on the fibre: comparison to independent band digits for any m≤`M=10μ+O(1)` windows costs `O((3e mH/(D+1))^{D+1}+(20mZ)^{D+2}P/X)` at `D=⌈C M l⌉`, since P is coprime to all these primes and gives one progression. Choose C sufficiently large absolutely. The accumulated errors after at most `(2K)^{M+1}` terms are `≪e^{-2μ}`: the factorial term costs `exp(−Ω(C M l log C))`, while `D log Z=O(L/l^{ε/2})`. The upper Bonferroni truncation at even M and negative association give `O(e^{-μ})` for the transferred void. Every hit is an actual ratio of divisors at the same x=(n+k)/4, hence a solution there. Sum over the small-prime fibres **with their population weights**; the error used the relative progression length and no multiplication by P of a probability bound is needed. Nonunit n are irrelevant for prime counting. Summing dyads and absorbing constants in ε gives the statements. ∎
