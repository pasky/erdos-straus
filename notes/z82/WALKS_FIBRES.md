## 8. Positive supply at **exactly two** steps without prime tuples

**I (PROVED rel. Dirichlet/CRT, ordinary Bombieri–Vinogradov, the classical dimension-one lower sieve and upper-bound sieve).** For every sufficiently large fixed prime $q=1\bmod840$, there are $\gg_q X/\log^2 X$ primes $p\in(X,2X]$, $p=1\bmod840$, of least nonresidue $q$ with shortest depth **exactly 2**. The implied constant can be very small. This is a lower bound, not an asymptotic. The shift need not have a unique negative successor. The new ingredient is to count bad root divisors by their *smaller factor in at most four classes modulo $q$*, and to count bad successors by a second numerical divisibility. Do not apply Lane 1's §4.1 vector sieve to an unspecified set of shifts: presieving one second shift only to a power would not exclude all the exits here.

**Plant the exit.** Choose fixed primes $s>q$, $s=1\bmod12$, $(s/q)=-1$, and $v>s+q$, $v=3\bmod4$, $v=q/3\bmod s$ (Dirichlet). Take $M=4sv\prod_{3\le l\le q}l$ over odd primes. Work with $N=(p+q)/2$ in **one** fixed unit class $n_0\bmod M$; equivalently $p=a=2n_0-q\bmod 2M$. Prescribe $n_0=1\bmod4$ so $p=1\bmod8$; prescribe $a=1$ at $3,5,7$, and square units other than $-q$ at all further primes $l<q$. At $q$ take any nonsquare $a$. At $s,v$ respectively take $a=-q/3,-s$. These satisfy $(n_0a,M)=1$ and yield $q$ as asserted. From $q$ go to $N$ (negative since 2 is positive); $s\mid p+N=(3p+q)/2$ and $(s/p)=(-q/3\mid s)=-1$. Finally $sv\mid p+s+v$ and $(v/p)=(-s/v)=-(q/3\mid s)=+1$. This supplies a hit in at most two steps for every sufficiently large prime here.

**Rough supply with uniform constants.** Put $z=X^{1/100}$; retain primes for which $N$ has no prime factor below $z$. The count is at least
$$ c\,{X\over M}\left({\log q\over\log X}\right)^2,\tag{9}$$
with *absolute* $c>0$, for $X$ sufficiently large depending on $q,s,v,M$. Indeed on the primes in $a\bmod 2M$ divisibility by each $l<z$, $l\nmid M$, costs $1/(l-1)$; $l=q$ is excluded. For squarefree $d$ supported there the test is one reduced class mod $2Md$ by CRT. Apply the lower linear sieve with level $X^{1/3}$ (sifting parameter $>2$); BV for these moduli bounds the summed remainders by $O_{M,A}(X/\log^A X)$, harmless in this order of limits. The normalized lower sieve factor is bounded below by an absolute positive constant: the dimension-one interval-product hypothesis is absolute by Mertens on all primes $l>7$, and omission of primes only weakens the upper hypothesis. The local product is $\gg \log q/\log z$ since all primes up to $q$ are omitted. Also $1/\varphi(2M)\gg (\log q)/M$, and the dyadic prime mass is asymptotic to $X/(\varphi(2M)\log X)$. Constants here and in the upper main terms below are independent of $q$; the thresholds and discarded $o$ terms need not be.

**Subtract early hits.** A root exit needs a divisor $D=tq-1$ of $2N$, say $D=2^e d$, $e=0,1$ ($N$ odd). Write $N=df$. Neither $d$ nor $f$ is 1: $D=1,2$ cannot work, and $D=N$ would make $a=-2\bmod q$ a square, while $D=2N$ leaves endpoint 1. Both factors are therefore $\ge z$. The smaller factor $u=\min(d,f)$ lies in one of just four nonzero classes mod $q$: $d=-2^{-e}$, $f=-2^{e-1}a$. We may discard all other restrictions on $u$ except roughness and size $z\le u\le 2\sqrt X$. For each such fixed $u$ coprime to $M$, its complementary factor $r$ is confined to one class $r=n_0/u\bmod M$, in an interval of length $O(X/u)$. Sieve the two linear forms $r$ and $2ur-q$, deleting both roots at primes $q<l<X^{1/1000}$ outside $M$. They are distinct and units are not required for this integer sieve; $l\nmid u$ by roughness. This majorizes the true count since $2ur-q=p>X$. The dimension-two upper sieve gives
$$ \ll {X\over u M}\left({\log q\over\log X}\right)^2$$
uniformly for the stated $u$, once $X$ is sufficiently large at the fixed parameters. Explicit error bookkeeping: use level $X^{1/5}$, each compatible squarefree modulus has $2^{\omega(d)}$ roots, each with counting error $O(1)$ in the progression. The sieve remainder is $X^{1/5+o(1)}$, absorbed even at $u=2\sqrt X$. The product bound uses $\prod_{q<l<X^{1/1000}}(1-2/l)\ll (\log q/\log X)^2$; omitting $s,v>q$ costs a bounded factor. All normalized upper factors are absolute (fixed levels and sieve dimensions).

In each dyadic $U\le u<2U$, $U\gg z$, one residue mod $q$ has $\ll U/(q\log X)$ rough integers. Apply a one-dimensional upper sieve in that class using primes below $X^{1/1000}$ other than $q$ (including primes smaller than $q$!), level $X^{1/200}$ and integer remainders $X^{1/200+o(1)}$, absorbed by $U\gg X^{1/100}$. Thus the harmonic mass in all four classes is $\ll 1/q$ after summing $O(\log X)$ dyads. The entire root loss is $\le(C/q)(X/M)(\log q/\log X)^2$, with absolute $C$. This is why one first chooses a **sufficiently large fixed** $q$. No uniform BV in $M$ is needed. Ignoring the negative-character requirement in this upper bound only increases the loss.

Finally a depth-one success is at a negative divisor $d$ of $p+q$; necessarily $d\ge z$ (2 a residue). Its positive endpoint $v'$ satisfies $d\mid p+v'$, hence $v'=q+j d$ with $j\ge1$ since $d>q$ and $v'\ne q$. For $p=t d v'-d-v'$, $t\ge2$, one has $t d v'\le 2p$ since $d+v'\le dv'$. Thus $j\le4X/d^2$ and there are at most $4X/(j d^2)$ values of $t$. Summing over $d\ge z$ and $j$ bounds *all* such inputs, even integers and ignoring the AP, by $O(X\log X/z)$, negligible compared with (9). Subtract at fixed $q>C/c$, then take $X\to\infty$. This proves exact depth two on the survivors. It does not give unconditional unbounded delay.
