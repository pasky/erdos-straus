## 2. Pointwise saturation without a multiplicative-order hypothesis

**Theorem A — PROVED (rel. inputs in the header).** For every sufficiently large prime $p\equiv1\pmod {24}$, the one-way negative closure $R$ from the least positive quadratic nonresidue $q$ is *all* $(p-1)/2$ nonresidues, not just all the nonresidue primes below a power of $p$. The vertices with outgoing paths to every vertex are exactly the odd nonresidues and the even nonresidues with $p+x$ composite. The others are sinks. There is no bound here on path length useful for fast search.

**Integer rule and multiplier budget.** Write $[z]$ for the representative $1\le[z]<p$, $\chi=(\cdot/p)$. From $x$ adjoin $d$ with $1<d<p$, $d\mid p+x$, $\chi(d)=-1$. Never use an integer lift $x\ge p$ as a vertex. A product of two reached nonresidues is not even of the right sign. What *can* be multiplied freely are elements of the stabilizer $H=\{h:hC=C\}$ of the union $C$ of doubling orbits whose odd representatives can be reached. We use the following three facts of [z80 A](../z80/ES_JUGULAR.md#1-a-directed-descent-without-negative-shifts) and [z82 A](../z82/WALKS_FIBRES.md#2-what-a-closed-miss-would-have-to-contain):

1. $q\in C\subset R\subset\mathrm{QNR}$, $2\in H\subset\mathrm{QR}$; if $z\in C$, every negative divisor $d<p$ of $[z]$ **or** $p+[z]$ is in $C$. Recall why: odd representatives cycle under first return of modular halving, $x\mapsto\operatorname{oddpart}(p+x)$. To access an even representative $e$, use its odd predecessor $2^je-p$ with $j$ least such that $2^j e>p$. The same trick applied to $[z]$ gives the divisor rule for $[z]$ itself after stripping powers of 2 from $d$.
2. If *every* $H$-coset has a representative $<2p/m$ and $1<m<p$, $\chi(m)=1$, then $m\in H$. For $x\in C$ choose such a $y$ in $(x/m)H$; $[my]\in C$, and $y$ is a negative divisor of $[my]$ or $p+[my]$. Thus $x/m\in C$ and finiteness gives equality.
3. For each **fixed** $M_0$, all residues $m\le M_0$ lie in $H$ at sufficiently large $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$. Here $2,3\in H$ by z80's small-gap lemma, and the Furstenberg closed-set argument in z82 A gives least representatives $o(p)$ *uniformly in the coset*. No rate is needed. Fact 3 is used only once, not at a parameter growing with $p$.

**Scale improvement (PROVED rel. PNT and Erdős–Turán).** There is an absolute $M_0$ such that, for any integer $M\ge M_0$ with $M^{12}<p$, if $H$ contains all QR integers through $M$, every coset $zH$ has a representative in $(0,p/M^2)$. Consequently $H$ contains all QR integers through $M^2$. This is not an estimate of smooth numbers in a prescribed AP, and does not require reachability of arbitrary smooth products.

*Dense legal spectrum.* Put $L=M^{40}$, $Q=M^{12}$, $J=M^4$. At least half of the primes $\le\sqrt M$ have the same sign at $p$. From this half take the set $A$ of products of **80 distinct** primes. Unique factorization and PNT give $A\subset[1,L]$, $|A|\gg L/(\log M)^{80}$ with absolute constant. Each such product is in $H$ modulo $p$: pair the primes, even if they are all negative. Each pair is a QR integer $\le M$, in $H$ by hypothesis. Repetition of residues mod $p$ would cause no problem below; these are distinct integers, used as weights on the long interval. No equidistribution of $\chi$ on small primes is used.

*Escape from small rational arcs.* In each coset choose a point $\alpha=[2^i z]/p$ on the circle at distance $>1/(8Q^2)$ from every rational of reduced denominator $\le Q$. If no such point existed, each orbit point would be shadowed by a unique small-denominator rational (distinct ones have spacing $\ge1/Q^2$). The rational shadow at the next iterate must be twice the present shadow, since their distance is $\le3/(8Q^2)$. The signed small errors therefore double, without wrap, around a periodic orbit, an impossibility unless zero. No orbit point has denominator $\le Q$ since $p>Q$. By elementary Dirichlet approximation of this *single* $\alpha$ with height $8Q^2$, there are coprime $a,s$ with
$$Q<s\le8Q^2,\qquad |\alpha-a/s|\le1/(8Q^2s)\le s^{-2}$$
(using a lift on the real line). The selected point works for all frequencies; one must not select a different $\alpha$ at each frequency in a discrepancy estimate.

*Bilinear estimate at that point.* For $1\le j\le J$, put $S_j=\sum_{b,c\in A}e(j\alpha bc)$, $e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)$. Cauchy in $b$, completion of its resulting nonnegative square to **all** $1\le b\le L$, then a geometric sum give
$$|S_j|^2\ll L^3 + L^2\sum_{1\le d\le L}\min(L,\|j\alpha d\|^{-1}).$$
Here each nonzero difference of two $c$'s has multiplicity $\le L$. In blocks of length $\lfloor s/(2j)\rfloor$ the phases $j\alpha d$ are spaced by $\ge1/(2s)$: the numerator difference at $a/s$ is nonzero mod $s$ and the perturbation on the difference is $\le1/(2s)$. Ordering distances to the nearest integer thus bounds each block sum by $O(L+s\log(2s))$. Consequently
$$|S_j|^2/L^4\ll 1/L+(jL/s+1)(L+s\log(2s))/L^2\ll M^{-8}.$$
The last estimate uses $s>M^{12}$, $s\le8M^{24}$ and $L=M^{40}$. All bounds hold for an arbitrary set of coefficients $1_A$ at these sizes, not just typical $p$. Erdős–Turán for the multiset of $|A|^2$ phases bounds its interval discrepancy (normalized by $|A|^2$) by
$$O\big(1/J + M^{-4}(\log M)^{160}\log(2J)\big)=o(M^{-2}).$$
There is a phase in $(0,1/M^2)$, e.g. test a closed subinterval of half this length. Its representative is in $zH$, since $2,b,c\in H$ modulo $p$. Apply fact 2. This completes the scale improvement with a threshold independent of the number of iterations.

*Endgame.* Start at the absolute fixed $M_0$ of that estimate, using fact 3. Square $M$ repeatedly until $M\ge p^{1/12}$; the first such $M$ is $<p$ for sufficiently large $p$. Therefore all QR integers through $p^{1/12}$ belong to $H$. In particular products of **24 distinct** same-sign primes $\le p^{1/24}$ lie in $H$ by the pairing argument, and are distinct integers $<p$. Thus $I=[\mathbb F_p^\times:H]\ll(\log p)^{24}$. For *any* coset, orthogonality over the $I$ characters trivial on $H$ and Burgess ($r=4$) count its representatives up to $B=\lfloor p^{1/3}\rfloor$ as
$$ B/I + O_\epsilon(p^{21/64+\epsilon}),$$
with $21/64=1/4+5/64<1/3$. This is positive uniformly in the coset for large $p$. By fact 2 every QR integer through $B$ is in $H$ (indeed through $\lfloor2p/B\rfloor-1$). Any other coset inside QR would have a representative through $B$, contradiction. Hence $H=\mathrm{QR}$, $C=R=\mathrm{QNR}$. The last assertion about individual starts now follows from z80 A's exact criterion for reaching $q$. None of the proof uses the existence of a reciprocal pair or a previously found ES solution.
