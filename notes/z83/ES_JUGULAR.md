## 1. Pointwise saturation without a multiplicative-order hypothesis

**Theorem A — PROVED (rel. inputs in the header).** For every sufficiently large prime $p\equiv1\pmod {24}$, the one-way negative closure $R$ from the least positive quadratic nonresidue $q$ is *all* $(p-1)/2$ nonresidues, not just all the nonresidue primes below a power of $p$. The vertices with outgoing paths to every vertex are exactly the odd nonresidues and the even nonresidues with $p+x$ composite. The others are sinks. There is no bound here on path length useful for fast search. On $1\bmod24$ this improves z82 H's $O_\epsilon(X^\epsilon)$ possible nonsaturations up to $X$ to a **finite** exceptional set (none through $10^9$ by the coset census). The proof uses an ineffective compactness step; the census does not bridge the unknown threshold.

**Integer rule and multiplier budget.** Write $[z]$ for the representative $1\le[z]<p$, $\chi=(\cdot/p)$. From $x$ adjoin $d$ with $1<d<p$, $d\mid p+x$, $\chi(d)=-1$. Never use an integer lift $x\ge p$ as a vertex. A product of two reached nonresidues is not even of the right sign. What *can* be multiplied freely are elements of the stabilizer $H=\{h:hC=C\}$ of the union $C$ of doubling orbits whose odd representatives can be reached. We use the following three facts of [z80 A](../z80/ES_JUGULAR.md#1-a-directed-descent-without-negative-shifts) and [z82 A](../z82/WALKS_FIBRES.md#2-what-a-closed-miss-would-have-to-contain):

1. $q\in C\subset R\subset\mathrm{QNR}$, $2\in H\subset\mathrm{QR}$; if $z\in C$, every negative divisor $d<p$ of $[z]$ **or** $p+[z]$ is in $C$. Recall why: odd representatives cycle under first return of modular halving, $x\mapsto\operatorname{oddpart}(p+x)$. To access an even representative $e$, use its odd predecessor $2^je-p$ with $j$ least such that $2^j e>p$. The same trick applied to $[z]$ gives the divisor rule for $[z]$ itself after stripping powers of 2 from $d$.
2. If *every* $H$-coset has a representative $<2p/m$ and $1<m<p$, $\chi(m)=1$, then $m\in H$. For $x\in C$ choose such a $y$ in $(x/m)H$; $[my]\in C$, and $y$ is a negative divisor of $[my]$ or $p+[my]$. Thus $x/m\in C$ and finiteness gives equality.
3. For each **fixed** $M_0$, all residues $m\le M_0$ lie in $H$ at sufficiently large $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$. Here $2,3\in H$ by z80's small-gap lemma, and the Furstenberg closed-set argument in z82 A gives least representatives $o(p)$ *uniformly in the coset*. No rate is needed. Fact 3 is used only once, not at a parameter growing with $p$.

**Scale improvement (PROVED rel. PNT and Erdős–Turán).** There is an absolute $M_0$ such that, for any integer $M\ge M_0$ with $M^{12}<p$, if $H$ contains all QR integers through $M$, every coset $zH$ has a representative in $(0,p/M^2)$. Consequently $H$ contains all QR integers through $M^2$. This is not an estimate of smooth numbers in a prescribed AP, and does not require reachability of arbitrary smooth products.

*Dense legal spectrum.* Put $L=M^{40}$, $Q=M^{12}$, $J=M^4$. At least half of the primes $\le\sqrt M$ have the same sign at $p$. From this half take the set $A$ of products of **80 distinct** primes. Unique factorization and PNT give $A\subset[1,L]$, $|A|\gg L/(\log M)^{80}$ with absolute constant. Each such product is in $H$ modulo $p$: pair the primes, even if they are all negative. Each pair is a QR integer $\le M$, in $H$ by hypothesis. Repetition of residues mod $p$ would cause no problem below; these are distinct integers, used as weights on the long interval. No equidistribution of $\chi$ on small primes is used.

*Escape from small rational arcs.* In each coset choose a point $\alpha=[2^i z]/p$ on the circle at distance $>1/(8Q^2)$ from every rational of reduced denominator $\le Q$. If no such point existed, each orbit point would be shadowed by a unique small-denominator rational (distinct ones have spacing $\ge1/Q^2$). The rational shadow at the next iterate must be twice the present shadow, since their distance is $\le3/(8Q^2)$. The signed small errors therefore double, without wrap, around a periodic orbit, an impossibility unless zero. No orbit point has denominator $\le Q$ since $p>Q$. By elementary Dirichlet approximation of this *single* $\alpha$ with height $8Q^2$, there are coprime $a,s$ with
$$Q<s\le8Q^2,\qquad |\alpha-a/s|\le1/(8Q^2s)\le s^{-2}$$
(using a lift on the real line). The selected point works for all frequencies; one must not select a different $\alpha$ at each frequency in a discrepancy estimate.

*Bilinear estimate at that point.* For $1\le j\le J$, put $S_j=\sum_{b,c\in A}e(j\alpha bc)$, $e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)$. Cauchy in $b$, completion of its resulting nonnegative square to **all** $1\le b\le L$, then a geometric sum give
$$|S_j|^2\ll L^3 + L^2\sum_{1\le d\le L}\min(L,\|j\alpha d\|^{-1}).$$
Here each nonzero difference of two $c$'s has multiplicity $\le L$. In blocks of length $\lfloor s/(2j)\rfloor$ the phases $j\alpha d$ are spaced by $\ge1/(2s)$: the numerator difference at $a/s$ is nonzero mod $s$ and the perturbation on the difference is $\le1/(2s)$. Ordering distances to the nearest integer thus bounds each block sum by $O(L+s\log(2s))$. Consequently
$$|S_j|^2/L^4\ll 1/L+(jL/s+1)(L+s\log(2s))/L^2\ll M^{-8}.$$
The last estimate uses $s>M^{12}$, $s\le8M^{24}$ and $L=M^{40}$. All bounds hold for an arbitrary set of coefficients $1_A$ at these sizes, not just typical $p$. Erdős–Turán for the multiset of $|A|^2$ phases bounds its interval discrepancy (normalized by $|A|^2$) by
$$O\big(1/J + M^{-4}(\log M)^{160}\log(2J)\big)=o(M^{-2}).$$
There is a phase in $(0,1/M^2)$, e.g. test a closed subinterval of half this length. Its representative is in $zH$, since $2,b,c\in H$ modulo $p$. Apply fact 2. This completes the scale improvement with a threshold independent of the number of iterations.

*Endgame.* Start at the absolute fixed $M_0$ of that estimate, using fact 3. Square $M$ repeatedly until $M\ge p^{1/12}$; the first such $M$ is $<p$ for sufficiently large $p$. Therefore all QR integers through $p^{1/12}$ belong to $H$. In particular products of **24 distinct** same-sign primes $\le p^{1/24}$ lie in $H$ by the pairing argument, and are distinct integers $<p$. Thus $I=[\mathbb F_p^\times:H]\ll(\log p)^{24}$. For *any* coset, orthogonality over the $I$ characters trivial on $H$ and Burgess ($r=4$) count its representatives up to $B=\lfloor p^{1/3}\rfloor$ as
$$ B/I + O_\epsilon(p^{21/64+\epsilon}),$$
with $21/64=1/4+5/64<1/3$. This is positive uniformly in the coset for large $p$. By fact 2 every QR integer through $B$ is in $H$ (indeed through $\lfloor2p/B\rfloor-1$). Any other coset inside QR would have a representative through $B$, contradiction. Hence $H=\mathrm{QR}$, $C=R=\mathrm{QNR}$. The last assertion about individual starts now follows from z80 A's exact criterion for reaching $q$. None of the proof uses the existence of a reciprocal pair or a previously found ES solution.

**Beyond the hard classes (PROVED rel. the same inputs).** For all sufficiently large $p=1\bmod8$ with **even** $\operatorname{ord}_p2$, no power-size lower bound on the order is needed either. Indeed $-1\in H$. Each $\langle-1,2\rangle$-coset has a positive representative $<2p/9$: otherwise take folded distances to zero divided by $p$ in its orbit under doubling. They are all $\ge2/9$ and $\le7/18$, since their successors are $\le1-2d$ at distance $d\in[0,1/2]$. If any $d\le1/4$, its successor $2d\ge4/9$ would be too large. Thus the branch is always $d\mapsto1-2d$, whose only periodic point is $1/3$, impossible at a prime $p>3$. Fact 2 now adjoins 9, and Furstenberg's multiplicatively independent $\times2,\times9$ gives fact 3 by the identical compactness argument. The scale improvement and endgame are unchanged. There is an exact large-prime dichotomy in the remaining case. Let $p=17\bmod24$ and let $m$ be the first quadratic residue in the list $5,7,9$ (3 is negative, 9 always positive). Then **for all sufficiently large such primes**, $R\ne\mathrm{QNR}$ iff some negative doubling coset lies entirely above $2p/m$. Indeed if no such coset exists, fact 2's proof for this $m$ only uses the cosets $(x/m)H$ with $x\in C$, all negative. Thus $m\in H$. Now $2,m$ are multiplicatively independent as integers and there are just three choices for $m$; apply the Furstenberg compactness argument for each fixed pair, taking the largest of the three thresholds, followed by the same scale improvement. Conversely for a negative orbit above $2p/m$, any arrow $z\to y$ into it has positive-character cofactor $(p+z)/y<m$. Every QR integer below $m$ is a power of 2 here (including 1). Hence $z$ belongs to that same doubling orbit. It is inaccessible from $q=3$ for $p>17$. This obstruction is one-way **predecessor closure**; not all missing nodes need lie in these high orbits. It does not exclude other accessible reciprocal exits. We do not know whether such high orbits occur at unbounded prime denominators.

**Exit (PROVED rel. DEDEKIND_ES Theorem 4.5; scope boundary).** At such $p$ a hit from any non-sink is equivalent to the *existence* of an interior (S) word. ## 2. An explicit window-31 split on a failed-jump family

Separately the signed seed of z81 can be transported via $W$ and three adaptive rank-at-least-two planes to that carrier; a pivot then gives $T$. Even restricting to the failure of the z82 ratio-$p$ jump, this latter family has $\gg_M X/(\log X)^{3/2}$ primes up to $X$ with $p\equiv1$ mod any prescribed **odd** $M$. In fact it has relative density one on that failure set in the given progression. Not a claim of new coverage of an entire Mordell class, or that the minimum linked prime is exactly 31.

**The budget (PROVED, elementary).** In the following cyclic quotient it is enough to reach the target, not cover the entire group: in a cyclic group of order $2m$ with $m$ odd, *any $m$ odd letters* (discrete logs odd) have a signed subsum equal to $m$, coefficients $-1,0,1$. Start with the singleton zero, grow the set $S$ of ordinary subset sums. If adjoining an odd letter fails to increase its size, $S$ is invariant under that letter, whose subgroup contains $m$. Otherwise eventually $|S|>m$. Either way $S-S$ contains $m$ (in the second case by pairing the elements at separation $m$). Each occurrence is used at most once after cancellation. All identical generators show sharpness for arbitrary letters. This differs from requiring that the entire exponent box equal the generated group, cf. `JOINT_BUDGET.md` §3.2.

More generally let $k$ be a prime $7\bmod8$, $h=\operatorname{ord}_k(2)$, $m=(k-1)/(2h)$ (both odd). At an integral window $K=(p+k)/4$ prime to $k$, if $v_2(K)\ge(h-1)/2$ and $K$ has $m$ negative prime-factor occurrences mod $k$, there exist coprime $A,C$ with $AC\mid K$, $k\mid A+C$. Apply the lemma in the quotient by $\langle2\rangle$, preserving the parity character: the quotient involution is the image of $-1$. Repair the lift using a signed power of 2 of exponent at most $(h-1)/2$. Coprime numerator and denominator of the resulting ratio give $A,C$. For $m=1$ this kind of dyadic repair is old (`Z73_PROBE_FORCING.md` §5); here no one odd letter need lie in $-\langle2\rangle$. At 31 use $h=5,m=3,4\mid K$. No primality of the final unmarked cofactor or equidistribution of its residues is assumed.

For the transpose family, work instead in $\{u\in(\mathbb Z/124)^\times:(u/31)=(-1)^{(u-1)/2}\}$. Projection mod 31 identifies this group with the cyclic group of order 30. Its involution is $-1$ modulo **124**. The 15 marked letters are odd in its cyclic logarithm. Thus there are *odd* coprime $A>C\ge1$, $AC\mid K$ with $124\mid A+C$; orient the ratio toward the larger term. Each nonidentity product uses a prime $>31$, hence $A>31$. ## 7. R2: an unconditional prime trap for the rational Euclidean and digit gates

**Theorem C (PROVED, elementary relative to Dirichlet for prime infinitude).** Consider an ordinary finite straight-line program with finite open case splits, rational arithmetic when defined, signed quotient/remainder, normalized integer gcd and Bézout, compatible CRT, level-$Q$ Farey/regular convergents on **rational** targets, proper reduction of positive definite integral binary forms (including a reducing matrix), and Lane 1's `DL` gate at arbitrary input-dependent integer $a,b,h>0$ (also signed $a,b$, $h>0$), fixed-degree polynomial $G$ with register coefficients. No unrestricted algebraic floors, Lagrange oracle or variable-length register lists. Each partial call must respect its domain on the actual branch (or branch to failure on invalid input). For each such program there is a reduced progression inside $1\bmod840$ on which, for **every sufficiently large prime**, there is a valid run with no positive ES triple among its finitely many outputs. Unique gates have their actual canonical outputs; nonunique gates, including the divisor escape, have adversarial witnesses. The same result holds with finite-valued advice by building all finitely many branches. This is a standard-input result, not the interpretation of Herbrand's theorem over one nonstandard model. It excludes programs working under **every** valid choice, not success under a lucky choice that encodes advice in an unbounded output. It assumes neither prime values of nonlinear polynomials nor factorization of a formal cofactor.

*The local semantics.* Fix a profinite unit square $\alpha$ equal to 1 modulo 840, with each $\alpha_l$ transcendental over $\mathbb Q$ (choose squares of transcendental $l$-adic units, close enough to 1 at primes of 840). A register is a rational-coefficient polynomial in the input $N$ on the tail of **one** congruence neighborhood of $\alpha$. Refine this neighborhood at finitely many primes at every step. A comparison of polynomials stabilizes by leading signs and identities. There is also an exact extension test for a fixed algebraic floor on such arguments: all **positive-power terms** in the one-variable Puiseux expansion at $+\infty$ must form a polynomial in $N$ over $\mathbb Q$ (the constant may be any real algebraic number). This is sufficient: freeze the rational polynomial's fractional part on the progression, then the constant and the eventual sign of the infinitesimal decide the floor. It is necessary even for a finite polynomial menu on an infinite subsequence: the approximating integer polynomial differs from the target by less than one there, so every unbounded Puiseux term must match. This test, relative to the usual real Puiseux theorem, is branchwise, not a claim about all real-closure gates; failure of this particular semantics does not prove ability to solve ES. A rational quotient with nonzero denominator has exact floor after polynomial division: the proper part tends to zero with fixed sign or is zero, and the fractional part of the polynomial quotient is constant after refining at the fixed coefficient denominator. Rational registers themselves can be stored as numerator/denominator pairs; this polynomial assertion concerns the integer registers. Domain and normalization inequalities are checked in the same way.

*Euclidean gates.* Run ordinary Euclid symbolically on two positive registers, treating zero and signs first. A degree drop puts the next dividend at smaller degree. For a pair of equal degree, ordinary integer quotient steps on their positive rational leading coefficients terminate by the rational Euclidean algorithm. The only subtlety is an exact integer limit approached from below: the quotient is one less, leaving a remainder with the same leading coefficient as the divisor, followed by a quotient 1 and a drop in degree (or use exact division when equal). In an unequal-degree division the remainder has degree at most that of the divisor, since its ratio to the divisor is in $[0,1)$. These observations prove termination **of this formal run**, not a uniform bound on Euclid for arbitrary numerical pairs. Back substitution yields rows; division reduces either coefficient to its canonical range. CRT uses a gcd compatibility check and such a row. A rational target's entire list of ordinary regular convergents on this branch has fixed finite length by the same argument, including all denominators and signed numerators. At bound $Q$ select the last eligible one; a Farey neighbor truncated within a partial quotient uses one floor to select its last eligible semiconvergent (alternatively solve the determinant-one row for the last convergent and take the neighbor nearest on the required side by a division). Exact targets have their usual neighbors from the determinant-one row. Endpoints, signs and integer translations require only comparisons and division.

For a positive definite form $A(x-\theta y)^2+D y^2/(4A)$, $\theta=-B/(2A)$, $D=4AC-B^2>0$, a shortest vector can be selected from $(1,0)$ and the convergents of the rational slope (include both roundings at denominator 1). Indeed any competitor with $y>0$ is dominated in both $y$ and $|y\theta-x|$ by some convergent: use the best-approximation-of-the-second-kind property up to that denominator, including the exact final fraction. Minimize the displayed quadratic among this finite list. Complete the primitive vector to a determinant-one basis, shear its second column by a nearest integer to make $|B'|\le A'$, where $A'$ is the shortest norm; then $C'\ge A'$. Equalities with a prescribed sign convention are handled by swaps/signs of determinant one. Thus even the matrix, not just the reduced class, can be supplied on the branch. This step does not replace an algebraic target by a rational one in an unrestricted floor call.

*Digit gates (the moving modulus).* For fixed standard $J$ with $h\mid b^J$, iterate $x_0=a$, $x_{s+1}=a-bG(x_s)$. Consecutive differences are divisible by successively higher powers of $b$, starting with $b$; hence $x_J+bG(x_J)=a\pmod h$. The root is unique mod $h$: $F(x)-F(y)=(x-y)(1+bK)$ with $1+bK$ a unit mod $h$. Reduce the polynomial iterate by division. This covers $h=P^j,b=P$ without a field assumption on $P$, even after specialization of a *formal* $P$ to $m^2$ (there is of course no unital map $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ changing an ordinary prime). It also covers any certified relation $h\mid b^J$ after specialization. Arbitrary radical support in integers does **not** give a universal ordinary $J$, e.g. $h=2^s,b=2$. In a nonstandard ring Lane 1's Jacobson-radical formulation need not mean nilpotent.

Here the finite program makes such an exponent available *locally* on every nonescape branch. For nonzero polynomials $b,h$ write $h=cSD$ over $\mathbb Q[T]$ with $S,D$ primitive integral products of powers of irreducibles, those in $S$ dividing $b$, those in $D$ not; $c$ a nonzero rational constant. Then $S\mid b^j$ over $\mathbb Q[T]$ for a fixed $j$, and $D,b$ have nonzero resultant after clearing denominators (use $D=1$ for an empty product). Exclude a finite set $L$ of rational primes from the following two good-place facts: $v_l(S(N))\le jv_l(b(N))$, and $l\mid D(N)\Rightarrow l\nmid b(N)$; include the primes of $c$ and all coefficient denominators in $L$. At **each** $l\in L$ the valuations of all nonzero register polynomials and $D,S$ can be fixed, finitely, on a smaller neighborhood of $\alpha_l$, since none vanishes there. If $D$ is nonconstant its absolute value divided by its now fixed $L$-part is eventually $>1$, a polynomial register dividing $h$ and coprime to $b$. Supply this escape and its compressed row. If $D=1$, we have $h\mid b^J$ everywhere, so use the iterate. Constant polynomials, $b=0$ and $h=1$ cause no difficulty ($F=X$ at $b=0$). This treats *all* moving moduli on this branch, not just powers of registers. Avoidance of local polynomial zeros matters: on the ordinary integer sequence $N=1+2^s$ take $h=N-1,b=2,G=X^2,a=2$. The unique digit inverse cannot agree with a finite menu of rational polynomials in $N$ on an infinite subsequence. A repeated polynomial tends $2$-adically to its rational value at 1; the limit would solve $2x^2+x=2$ with nonsquare discriminant 17. This is not an assertion that infinitely many of those $N$ are prime. A number-theoretic example is $h=\gcd(u^2,v^3), b=uv$; no syntactic power requirement applies. A gate returning a freely chosen answer even when an escape is available need not have *all* its outputs polynomial. More generally a supplied simple root modulo the radical lifts on this class: assume $\operatorname{rad}(h)\mid f(s)$ and $(f'(s),h)=1$ for a fixed-degree polynomial. Apply the same support test to $b=f(s)$, so $h\mid f(s)^J$ locally, or $f(s)=0$ already. A modular Newton update $s'=s-f(s)u$, $uf'(s)=1\bmod h$, gives $f(s')\in(f(s)^2,h)$ and preserves the derivative unit since $s'=s$ at every prime of $h$. After $2^j\ge J$ the residual vanishes modulo $h$. Each inverse here is one normalized row; no named prime factors in the program. This concerns a *supplied seed*, not root generation on an arbitrary fibre.

*Termination and prime conclusion.* Intersect the finitely many congruence neighborhoods on the chosen formal path. Repeated calls of one choice symbol on the same tuple reuse the earlier answer: equality of polynomial arguments stabilizes off finitely many inputs. For finite advice take a common refinement of the finite tree. If a leaf triple succeeds for infinitely many inputs, the cleared polynomial identity holds identically and its three integer-valued denominators are positive eventually. The primitive unit-square progression then gives the strict-square contradiction of `LOGIC_SPLIT_SQUARE_TEST.md` (or §8 below): choose an odd $m$ with $m^2$ in the progression avoiding the common coefficient denominator and every nonzero cleared constant term. All $G_i(m^2)$ with zero constant term are multiples of $m^2$, and the others are units modulo $m^2$. At least one constant term vanishes by the identity at zero. This excludes every candidate at every sufficiently large prime on the progression. Dirichlet supplies infinitely many such primes. On polynomial registers the normalization gates really do evaluate on the same formulas at the square and the prime; for the arbitrary four-square and algebraic floor gates §6 explains exactly why the proof stops.

**Host version (PROVED, rel. Łoś).** Choose increasing ordinary primes $p_i$ tending to $\alpha$ profinitely by Dirichlet on a nested countable basis. In any nonprincipal ultraproduct of these pointed integer rings take the evaluations at $P=[p_i]$ of polynomials $f\in\mathbb Q[T]$ integral on some neighborhood of $\alpha$ (discard finitely many initial evaluations). These are exactly the $f$ with $f(\alpha_l)\in\mathbb Z_l$ at every prime: only coefficient denominators need testing. This ordered subring is closed under the integer-valued rational Euclid/Farey/Gauss/DL witnesses just proved, and has no positive ES triple at $P$ by the polynomial identity argument. Even the internal support guard for DL causes no new case: when $h\nmid b^J$ for all standard $J$, the construction gives an actual divisor escape and row *in the subring*. $P$ is prime there: if $T\mid f g$ in the ring, then $T\mid f$ or $T\mid g$ over $\mathbb Q[T]$; division of the corresponding polynomial by $T$ preserves integrality at $\alpha$ because every $\alpha_l$ is a unit. This illustrates the desired PA-host hull without assuming ES in the host. It is **not** an `IOpen` hull: no polynomial has values $\lfloor\sqrt{p_i}\rfloor$ on an unbounded subsequence. Nor is it the model $T_\Pi$ used for the fragment corollary. Comparison with concurrent Lane 8 `U_TRAP.md` §§1,4 at `ef857a629`: its parsimonious host ring, Euclid and digit gates, and progression misses overlap ours; it additionally treats joint linear witness blocks via parametric Presburger. Here the resultant argument does not require parsimony, and proper Gauss matrices and supplied-seed Newton lifts have explicit branch-local realizations. Neither host construction gives closure under unrestricted nested algebraic floors.

One can even take this *particular* true-arithmetic host subring to be an external PID. For each sufficiently large prime $l$ choose a nonzero square modulo $l$ avoiding the roots of an increasing initial list of nonzero primitive polynomials in $\mathbb Z[T]$: let the sum of their degrees grow to infinity slowly, always $<(l-1)/2$. Lift to a transcendental $l$-adic square. At the finitely many small primes retain the unit and 840 conditions. Each $g\in\mathbb Q[T]$ then has valuations $v_l(g(\alpha_l))=0$ almost everywhere unless $g=0$. Scale each rational irreducible polynomial by a rational constant so all its valuations are zero; such a factor divides every polynomial in the subring that it divides over $\mathbb Q[T]$. Thus it is prime there. Likewise rational primes are prime by evaluation modulo $l$ (kernel $lR$, with $R$ the subring). Every nonzero nonunit factors finitely into these primes by taking the rational polynomial factorization followed by its remaining scalar, an integer by integrality. Together with the rows already proved, atomicity gives an external PID (an ideal's nonzero member has finitely many prime factors; successively taking gcds with other members must stabilize at a generator). This is a refinement of a polynomial seed, **not** an external PID model of $IE_1^-$ or even `IOpen`.

## 8. R2: specialization lemma for the logic lanes

**Lemma D (PROVED rel. ordinary strict T1 in `LOGIC_SPLIT_SQUARE_TEST.md`).** In a source ring record $4xyz=P(xy+yz+zx)$, and mark each denominator either by a record $d=P w_d$ or by $u_d d+v_d P=1$. At least one of each sort is required. At a positive prime of a discretely ordered Bézout domain these records exist for any actual positive triple: primality forces a divisible coordinate, all three divisible contradict $4\le3$, and a nondivisible coordinate generates the unit ideal with $P$. A unital specialization of **just the finite identity and these records** to integers taking $P$ to an odd square $m^2>1$ cannot give three positive denominator images. No preservation of the whole order, irreducibility, internal Legendre symbol, factorization, primality, or primitivity of the cofactors $w_d$ is needed. More intrinsically, quotient/row records may be omitted from the diagram if the resulting divisibilities and gcd-one tests are established in the target directly. This is the exact target predicate for the strict dichotomy, not an equivalence between existence of a ring map and absence of ES. A sufficient way to transmit the three positives without preserving the order globally is a continuous specialization path on which all three denominator functions are finite, $P>0$, and the identity holds. Starting with positive denominators, none can reach zero: on the positive part each is at least $P/4$. This is the connected-component argument of `LOGIC_SPLIT_SQUARE_TEST.md`, not a claim that an oracle diagram admits such a path. In Type I the image pattern is $(m^2w,X,Y)$ with $(XY,m)=1$; in Type II it is $(w,m^2X,m^2Y)$ with $(w,m)=1$. The cited two-term argument gives $(-g/m^2)=-1$ for a unit $g$ in either case, impossible. The same interface works verbatim for a map into a signed PA scalar ring $B\equiv\mathbb Z$ at an **internal**, not necessarily standard, $m$: nonexistence of a positive strict triple at an odd square with those divisibility/unit records is first-order and transfers. More generally the target need only satisfy this T1 sentence and the stated positivity tests. There is no need to transport a Jacobi function or a particular CT prime. A signed strict specialization is possible: $((n-1)/2,(n+1)/2,-n(n^2-1)/4)$ always satisfies the identity for odd $n>1$. Dropping the unit gate allows even positive nonstrict square triples, e.g. $(3,10,90)$ at 9.

If there is a genuine row, **the exceptional set for its gcd under any unital specialization is empty**, also for nonscalar inputs. If there is only coprimality over $\mathbb Q[T]$, gcd failure at $F(m^2),G(m^2)$ for integral coprime polynomials is exactly the union over primes of an integral nonzero Bézout constant of the common-root residue classes of $m^2$ modulo that prime; a resultant suffices for nonconstant inputs. These are finitely many congruence tests, not necessarily a thin or even a zero-density set (e.g. $T,T+2$ at even $m$, or $T,T+3$ at odd multiples of 3). Without a row or one-parameter polynomial hypotheses, two coprime *elements* in the gcd sense need not be comaximal, e.g. $T,U$ in $\mathbb Z[T,U]$. There is no unrestricted one-dimensional thin-set claim for that situation.

For an attached **universal** digit presentation $R[x,y]/(F(x)-a-hy)$, any base specialization with $\phi(h)>0$, $\phi(h)\mid\phi(b)^j$, extends by the canonical residue of the iterate and the integral quotient. The scalar case $h=P^j,b=P$ specializes modulo $m^{2j}$ exactly as requested. A source Jacobson-radical guard alone is not a polynomial divisibility record; do not assume that arbitrary base maps transmit that universal guard. Additional equations or saturated denominators in an actual extension still have to be checked; a canonical residue in the source need not specialize into its normalized interval if the map is not ordered. Similar distinction for adjoining a normalized Bézout row: the raw equation extends where solvable, but inequalities are extra conditions. The tower's geometric descent avoids assuming integer maps on arbitrary finite prefixes (§6).

**Control (PROVED in `IOpen`, hence in $IE_1$).** At $n=3k-1>1$ the denominators $(n,k,nk)$ give $4/n$ by $k+n+1=4k$; this is a polynomial identity with the displayed parameter. A positive prime in this progression has a strict triple, but **no integer square is 2 mod 3**. There is no compatible twin, even before lifting. The hard classes enter the trap in the opposite direction: unit-square congruences must survive *all* the finitely many congruence tests, not just primality and not merely $1\bmod4$. ES in ordinary arithmetic and its provability in $IE_1$ remain separate open questions.

## 9. Free sprint: which polynomial residue fibres can actually carry every prime?

**Initial falsifier (PROVED rel. O140).** In the campaign model (`FUNCTIONFIELD_ES.md` Definition 1.1) the denominators are **monic**, of degrees at least $n-k$, where $n=\deg P>k=\deg A>0$ and $A,P$ are monic. In characteristic $\ge5$, `BRAINSTORM/ff_erdos_straus.md` Thm 2.1 bounds all successful irreducibles by $O_n(q^{n-k})$. Thus the suggested all-$P$ large-$q$ statement is already **false**. Averaging in this regime does not solve the lottery even in the polynomial model. We instead classify the exceptional **fibres** in its fixed-degree estimate. This concerns only the stated sign and degree normalization.

**Theorem E (PROVED, elementary relative to the monic two-term split; Weil's prime polynomial AP bound only for the last relative comparison).** Take $A$ as above and a unit residue $R\bmod A$. Put
$$\mathcal E_A=\{-2\}\ \cup\ \{-D\bmod A: D\text{ a monic divisor of }A+1\text{ in }\mathbb F_q[T]\}.$$
* At $R\in\mathcal E_A$ every irreducible of degree $n$ in the fibre succeeds. This positive part is an explicit linear certificate.
* At **each** other $R$, at most $O_n(q^{n-k-1})$ irreducibles succeed. The saving of a factor $q$ relative to O140 is a bound on the worst nonexceptional fibre, not an averaging argument; it is uniform in $A,R$ and the characteristic $\ge5$. The exponent is sharp for a natural family of nonexceptional fibres when $n>2(k+1)$, as below.
* For fixed $n>2k$ and sufficiently large $q$ the displayed list is consequently the **exact** list of all-prime fibres among unit classes mod $A$. Indeed prime polynomial equidistribution gives $q^n/(n\varphi(A))+O_n(q^{n/2})$ irreducibles in each such class, of order $q^{n-k}$. This use of RH is just to count prime inputs *after* proving a uniform bound at a prescribed residue. There is no RH analogue here forcing a divisor at an arbitrary single $P$.

For a positive certificate take $X=(P+D)/A$; with opposite sides $P,X$ in the divisor split, $D\mid P+X$ since $P\equiv AX\bmod D$ and $D\mid A+1$. The leading coefficient is 1. For $R=-2$ take $X=(P+2)/A$ with equal sides $1,1$. This is `FUNCTIONFIELD_ES.md` §1, not the new exclusion. In contrast let $L$ be monic linear coprime to $A$ with $R=-L\bmod A$ nonexceptional. Impose the extra digit $X=-1\bmod L$ on $X=(P+L)/A$. Then $L\mid X+1$ gives a same-side split with divisors $X,1$ (distinct degrees). Equivalently the primes are in one unit AP modulo $AL$: $P=-L\bmod A$, $P=-A\bmod L$. For $n>2(k+1)$ the same prime polynomial estimate gives $\gg_n q^{n-k-1}$ such primes, attaining the upper order. For $A=T$ this covers **every** nonexceptional constant $b$ via $L=T-b$; for $A=T^k$, $k\ge2$, all but $O_k(1)$ choices $L=T-b$ yield distinct nonexceptional unit fibres. This sparse but positive supply does not turn those fibres into all-prime fibres.

*An eliminant uniform in the fibre.* Write $d=n-k$ and $X$ monic of degree $d$. In the split lemma a witness $E=AX-P$ must have $E\equiv-R\bmod A$. We may overcount, treating $P$ as a single prime block. If the two monic divisors of $PX$ both omit $P$ or both contain it, cancel any common $P$ (coprime to $E$). For each pair $a,b\mid X$, $E$ must then be the leading coefficient $\lambda$ of $a+b$ times a monic divisor of $(a+b)/\lambda$; $\lambda=1$ or 2. On a mixed pair $Pa,b$, the leading coefficient is 1 by degree, and $E$ is a monic divisor of $AXa+b$. Each choice determines $P=AX-E$; we may discard the degree and irreducibility restrictions for the upper bound. There are at most $O_n(1)$ choices per $X$.

Construct a polynomial in the $d$ free coefficients of $X$ and an indeterminate $z$: take the product over all these $E$ of $\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}z^j[T^j]((E+R)\bmod A)$. Enumerate monic factors by subsets of *labelled* roots over an algebraic closure, repeating values for multiplicities. First take products over the submultisets from each $(a+b)/\lambda$ or $AXa+b$; these are symmetric polynomials in that polynomial's roots. Then take the product over the two subsets of the roots of $X$; it too is symmetric. Symmetric-polynomial descent gives a polynomial, of degree bounded solely in terms of $n$: the construction for each pair of subset sizes has bounded many operations at bounded degrees, division only by the constant 2 or by monic $A$ with remainder. Equivalently perform it universally over $\mathbb Z[1/2]$ with coefficient indeterminates and then specialize; for equal degrees the sum has leading coefficient 2 throughout. In particular formation commutes with **colliding roots**. The product vanishes identically in $z$ at every successful $X$.

*Test the exceptional locus, not just its expected dimension.* Put $X=(T-u)^d$ over $\overline{\mathbb F}_q(u)$ with $u$ transcendental. If any factor vanishes identically, $E\bmod A=-R$ over that function field's algebraic closure. Fix a root $\beta$ of $A$; $E(\beta)=-R(\beta)$ is a nonzero constant. On a same-side pair the polynomial to split is a sum of two powers of $T-u$; after dividing by its leading coefficient it is a translate of a polynomial over the constant field. Every root is $u+$constant, so every nonconstant subproduct evaluated at $\beta$ has positive $u$-degree. Only $E=1,2$ remain.

On a mixed pair with $a=(T-u)^i$, $b=(T-u)^j$, the polynomial to split is $(T-u)^j C$ where $C=A(T)(T-u)^e+1$, $e=d+i-j\ge0$. No factor $T-u$ can enter $E$: all roots of the monic $C$ are integral at $u=\beta$ so at a place over that point such a factor would give $E(\beta)$ positive valuation. If $e=0$ only divisors of $A+1$ occur. For $e>0$ extend the valuation $v(u)=-1$ to the splitting field of $C$. An unbounded root $w$ has $v(w)=-1$ since $A(w)(w-u)^e=-1$: for $v(w)<-1$ the product would have negative value, as also for $-1<v(w)<0$. Every bounded root is close to some root $\gamma$ of $A$; if its multiplicity in $A$ is $k_\gamma$, then $v(w-\gamma)=e/k_\gamma$ since $v(w-u)=-1$. Counting roots with multiplicity, write $t$ for the number of unbounded roots of $C$, $s_\gamma$ for the numbers close to $\gamma$. From $C(\gamma)=1$ one gets $s_\gamma e/k_\gamma=t$ for each $\gamma$, and from $\deg C=k+e$ it follows that $t=e$, $s_\gamma=k_\gamma$.

Use the same value equation for a selected factor $E$: if it contains $t'$ unbounded roots and $s'_\gamma$ close roots at $\gamma$, then $s'_\gamma e/k_\gamma=t'$ for **every** $\gamma$. A proper nonempty selection must therefore have $0<s'_\gamma<k_\gamma$. In $E(\gamma+V)/E(\gamma)$ the coefficient of $V^{s'_\gamma}$ is an elementary symmetric sum of inverses of root differences. It has strictly negative value: exactly one term takes *all* the $s'_\gamma$ negative-value inverse differences, all other differences having inverse of nonnegative value. This uniqueness holds for labelled roots with multiplicities. But $E\equiv-R\bmod(T-\gamma)^{k_\gamma}$ makes that coefficient constant. This contradiction excludes the selection. Empty or full gives residue 1. Finally a proper factor of $A+1$ of degree $<k$ which is $-R\bmod A$ has coefficients in $\mathbb F_q$ by unique reduction; the full factor has residue 1. This proves the product test does not vanish identically for $R\notin\mathcal E_A$. Some nonzero coefficient in $z$ is a bounded-degree polynomial in the $d$ coefficients of $X$. Its zero set has size $O_n(q^{d-1})$ by the elementary polynomial zero bound, proving E. Neither separability of the intermediate binomials nor a choice of an irreducible component over the ground field is assumed.

**Effective first-degree check (PROVED; CERTIFIED in `r2-ffib.txt`).** For $n=k+1$ on a nonexceptional fibre there are at most **eight** successful primes, uniformly even in $k$. Write $P=A(T)(T+a)+R$, $X=T+x$ ($\deg R<k$); the divisors of $PX$ have distinct degrees and are $1,X,P,PX$. An equal pair requires constant $E=2$ by coprimality; otherwise $E$ is monic. Thus the only candidates are $x=a+1$, $E=A-R$, or $x=a$, $E=-R$ monic. Constant $E=1,2$ is excluded. For a nonconstant candidate $E$, besides $E\mid A+1$ (excluded by the fibre), one of $1+X,1+AX,1+AX^2$ must vanish at every root of $E$ (cancel unit factors). At any one such root, $A$ is a unit, giving at most $1+1+2$ possibilities for $x$, hence for $a$. For $A=T$, $n=2$, $P=T^2+aT+b$, this is exact in the following sharper form: either $b=-1,-2$, or, with $v=b+a+1$, one of $v,bv,bv^2$ equals $-1$. The test is unchanged on any constant-field extension in which $P$ stays irreducible. For example $T^2+2$ stays irreducible and fails over **every** $\mathbb F_{5^{2j+1}}$. Eight is only an upper bound in the general case. In fact at $\deg E\ge2$ the first condition is impossible and the quadratic condition has at most one scalar solution $x$ (two distinct solutions would force $E\mid 2T+x+x'$). At $\deg E\ge3$ the linear and quadratic conditions cannot both occur: subtracting after cancellation of the unit $A$ would give $E\mid(T+x)^2-(T+x')$. Thus the three bounds on contributions of a nonconstant $E$ are 4, 2, 1 at degrees 1, 2, $\ge3$. The two candidate $E$ have distinct degrees, so six suffices for all $k$. No integer ES claim follows from this polynomial degree obstruction.
