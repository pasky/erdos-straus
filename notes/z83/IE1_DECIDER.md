**PROVED (local input versus ES transport).** At a prime `p≡1 mod 8`, Π supplies `r²≡p (h)` for every supplied `D₂(h)` by taking `a=(p−1)/8`. **Assessment (attempt to use the root).** Writing `r²=p+ht`, obtaining the needed moving odd residue from `r` by cancelling the `ht` term at a divisor of `4Cp+1` would require divisibility there of `ht`; `h` is a unit at every such odd divisor under `D₂(h)`. Alternatively taking an approximate square root `r` as a small denominator would require an order bound much stronger than `r<h`; sorting a Farey bracket does not impose the modular square relation on its endpoints. We obtain no ES, `(★)`, `(S)`, `U₇` or new hard prime class by these implications.

**PROVED (a positive small-remainder test, not just a sign objection).** Suppose `p≡1 mod 8`, `h` is supplied pure dyadic with `√p/2<h<√p`, and we have a square root of `p` modulo `4h`, where `h` is even. The radicand, size comparison and congruences can all be written without radicals. Modulo `4h` either sign and translation by `2h` preserve squaring; reduce modulo `2h` and reflect to obtain an odd `0<r≤h` with the same square mod `4h`. Thus

```
            p=r²+ht,        0<t<2√p,        4|t,        0<r<√p.
```

In particular for `p>4` this supplies a genuinely **smaller successful denominator** `t`, with no leastness assumption at all: for `t=4u`, `4/t=1/(2u)+1/(3u)+1/(6u)`. Even `8/t=1/u+1/(2u)+1/(2u)` succeeds under the unrestricted ES convention allowing repetitions. In §3.1 such a dyadic `h` exists at `p=Q`: approximate `√Q` by a scalar within factor `3/2`, use PA powers of two to bracket that scalar and at most one further adjacent power to bracket `√Q`. Strict endpoints follow since `Q` is prime and not the square of a dyadic integer. Transporting by positive scaling fails because `t∤Q` (here `1<t<Q`). At the ordinary F1 square `225=1²+8·28` the same successful small remainder cannot force a strict triple. The square bound therefore does not supply a 4-specific lift of the smaller triple. This rules out the indicated use of a smaller success, not a proof involving simultaneous success at *all* smaller inputs.

**Assessment (least-counterexample descent).** Divisibility-minimality stops scaling at a prime.
