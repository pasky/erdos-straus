## 10. The induction rule: hereditary closure and Kaye's known bounded conservation theorem

**PROVED (ordinary `+,·,<,0,1`; rules on theorems, not open deductions).** For a deductively closed theory `S` extending `IOpen` in this language let `IR` be the Hilbert rule: from the two theorems `∀p V(0,p)` and `∀i,p (V(i,p)→V(i+1,p))` infer `∀i,p V(i,p)` for a bounded existential open-matrix `V`. A tuple of parameters can be universally closed here. Equivalently ask for the endpoint `t(p)` with bounded progress up to that endpoint: cap the index and include a target among the parameters. The **hereditary** characterization is exact:

```
 S contains IE₁⁻  ⇔  every same-language sentence extension S+σ (deductively closed) is closed under IR.
 R ⊨ IE₁⁻         ⇔  Th(R) is closed under IR              (R ⊨ IOpen).
```

In the forward direction work in the extension proving the premises. In a valid square block pack `p,H` and ask for `V(min(i,H),p)`; reset at each square. Every block's initializer and nonreset step follow from those **universally closed** theorems. At the target block's `i=H` get the arbitrary conclusion. In the reverse direction for each genuinely unary `V` use the *single sentence* `σ=V(0)∧∀i(V(i)→V(i+1))`, apply the rule there and discharge `σ` by deduction in first-order logic. For the semantic statement, in the complete theory either the two unary premises hold or at least one is false; conversely use the same parameter-packing proof. This does not say that such a ring has parameterized induction with an arbitrary tuple of *named* parameters held fixed in its premises.

**PROVED (known conservation, credited to Kaye, *Diophantine induction*, APAL 46 (1990), by Jeřábek 2020 §5; original unseen, exact formulation directly verified here via free-cut elimination).** Let `U` be the least deductively closed extension of `IOpen` closed under `IR` on **theorems**, iterated finitely often in each derivation. Then `U ⊆ IE₁⁻ ⊆ IE₁`, and these three theories have the same `∀E₁` consequences. In particular `IE₁⁻ ⊢ ES` **iff** `IE₁ ⊢ ES`; ES is `PA⁻`-provably equivalent to its `∀E₁` form: sorting and successive positive subtraction give `x≤3n/4`, `y≤2nx`, `z≤nxy`, hence all ≤`2(n+1)^4` (z76 IE1_BREAK §4 citing LOGIC_BEZOUT_NORM_COLLECTION §4). Neither side of the biconditional is asserted. This is not equality as arbitrary first-order theories. No priority claim: compare the ordinary rule-versus-axiom conservation work of Parsons and Beklemishev and Jeřábek's bounded free-cut argument cited below. Details of the nontrivial upper bound:

1. Use the ordinary two-sided sequent system with induction inference `Γ,V(i,p) ⇒ V(i+1,p),Δ` over `Γ,V(0,p) ⇒ V(t,p),Δ`, fresh `i` not free in the conclusion/side parameters. This presents full parameterized `IE₁` over the discretely ordered semiring base: the axiom gives the inference by applying induction under the side conditions, and the inference yields each axiom by ordinary logical rules. Discretely ordered semiring axioms can be given universal-existential open matrices (including differences for order), treated as eigenvariable left rules: from `Γ,D(a,w)⇒Δ` infer `Γ⇒Δ` for `∀a∃w D(a,w)` in the base. Alternatively `IOpen` may be included by such axioms: each open induction instance with parameters is equivalent *as a scheme* to local countersteps `V(0,p)∧¬V(H,p)→∃i<H(V(i,p)∧¬V(i+1,p))` by capping at `H` in open induction. No axiom of bounded universal collection is used.
2. Strip the leading universal closure from the goal and use free-cut elimination for induction inferences (Buss, *Handbook of Proof Theory*, free-cut elimination; see the specific application in Jeřábek Thm 5.1 below): retain cuts on subformulas of the induction formulas or base-rule auxiliaries (up to term substitution); there is no need to eliminate those cuts. This is the usual partial cut-elimination argument for induction sequents: nonlogical inferences carry a cut on a side formula past the inference with a fresh eigenvariable; the only new principal cases at which elimination may stop are instances of the induction formula or base axiom auxiliaries. Use bounded existentials as quantifier rules with their bound guards, or expand a block to nested existentials with conjunctions of open guards. All surviving formulas and their subformulas are `E₁` or equivalent to such (a residual conjunction of guards and a suffix of the block is still bounded existential; choose the bounded rules so no isolated *unguarded* existential suffix is introduced). It is this subformula property, with an `E₁` end sequent, `E₁` induction matrices and open base auxiliaries, that excludes `¬E₁` side formulas. In particular no negation of an `E₁` step-failure predicate is needed on the right. This is the free-cut argument of Jeřábek, *Induction rules in bounded arithmetic*, Arch. Math. Logic 59 (2020), Thm 5.1 proof, applied to the indicated bounded-existential/open sequent class, not its smash-language conservation theorem taken verbatim.
3. Translate each sequent in the resulting finite derivation to its universally closed implication and induct **externally on the derivation**. Only the induction inference needs a new rule application. Give all `E₁` antecedents in `Γ` and `V(0,p)` fresh witness variables, and let `W` be the open conjunction certifying them, with all bounds. With `δ` the disjunction of the right side formulas in `Δ` (false if empty), use the rule on `χ(i)=¬W ∨ δ ∨ V(i,p)`, parameters including the fresh witnesses. Its initializer holds by the open certificate for `V(0,p)`. Its step follows by the already translated premise under `W∧¬δ`; there are no free occurrences of `i` in `W,δ`. It is genuinely `E₁` since `¬W` is **open**, not the negation of an existential antecedent. Its conclusion at `t` yields the conclusion sequent after existentially restoring the antecedent witnesses. Logical rules and the retained cuts just compose first-order theorems. This proves the upper bound. For the lower inclusion `IOpen ⊆ IE₁⁻` use z80 IE1_SPLIT §1 (open tests are two-sided), then apply the block proof under already proved theorems.

## 12. Why specializing the *whole ordered diagram* at odd squares fails

**PROVED (one forced IOpen call; absolute obstruction).** At a nonsquare positive prime `P` in a discretely ordered integer part take `r=⌊√P⌋`. The single open formula

```
                    r² < P < (r+1)²
```

holds, but has **no** integer realization with `P=m²`, for any `m`. This is independent of the branch dimension, primes, denominator bound or how the other diagram parameters are chosen. Even without order symbols, in a Lag ring append witnesses to `P−r²−1` and `(r+1)²−P−1` being sums of four squares. The resulting finite diagram of polynomial equalities also has no integral square-`P` specialization. In particular the problem with the suggested specialization/compactness induction occurs *at the floor stage*, before the first nontrivial `IR` iteration. No Ramsey refinement, FIP ultrafilter or infinite diagonal selection can make an empty cylinder thick. Conversely an ultraproduct with every marked `P_i` an integer square makes `P` a square; even in an arbitrary subring with integer-part floors there, the host's square root must equal the subring floor (use the displayed adjacent-square bracket in the host). Changing the witness for IOpen is not an available adversary.

This concerns **one common ordered specialization of the tuple**, unlike z81/z82 and §§3.1,7–8. There negative-degree descent first removes the floor coordinates from a *hypothetical reciprocal tuple*. The positivity of the remaining polynomial denominators then propagates on the positive real ray and only those polynomials are specialized arithmetically on squares. It was not necessary or generally possible to extend every order inequality to the square evaluation. Even z64 §2.8 proves universal **ring** theory, not this pointed square specializability. If both Lag and universal ring theory hold in a signed model then every finite **ordered** diagram is indeed integrally realizable (replace positive strict inequalities by `z−1=Σw_i²` using Lag), but that gives no right to impose the extra condition `P=m²`. Imposing order only on final denominators *after* descent is a genuine asymmetry of the existing construction. A Hilbert tenth obstruction or a failure of the integer-part order can neither be removed by discarding vanishing polynomials nor by choosing generic points of the same Zariski closure.

**PROVED (universal host theory alone is insufficient, rel. z64 BO model and z75 IE1_ES §2).** In the particular z64 §2 model, the arithmetic elements lie in `ℚ[β₁,β₂,…]` with algebraically independent generators and rational constants integral everywhere exactly `ℤ`. Every integral solution of `u²−2v²=1` has *standard* coordinates: extend constants to `ℚ(√2)` in that polynomial ring, and `u+v√2` is a polynomial unit, hence constant. Yet the one-rule `U` theorem `∀n ∃u,v≤7(n+1) [u≥n ∧ u²−2v²=1]` has independently proved base and step, by multiplying by `3+2√2` when `u=n` (zero handled by `(1,0)` and then the first positive step). It fails at `n=β₁`. The conclusion that z64 does **not** get `U` for free already follows from its recorded failure of Bézout (§2.6 there). The Pell control additionally exhibits the tameness obstruction below. Elementary copies/ultrapowers of that model still fail this sentence by Łoś. Its embedding as a subring of an ultrapower of the integers may be unordered; existential closure even for this window does not follow. A full elementary true-arithmetic host would instead satisfy all true `∀E₁`, *including ES if true*. Taking the entire host is not a countermodel construction. For an *ordered* subring of a PA host with full Farey, §6 of z82 FAREY_IE1 gives, conditional on host ES at `P`, a missing host denominator `j≤√P` with explicit separation of `1/j` from the subring's real closure. Adding all bounded host witnesses indiscriminately closes the wrong gap.

**PROVED (depth-one failure of a proposed tameness condition).** Even on the square seed the same Pell loop has no eventually quasipolynomial witness section, on any infinite subfamily on which the outputs are finitely many prescribed rational polynomials in `m`: for `n=m²`, one such pair on an infinite subset gives the identity `u(m)²−2v(m)²=1` in `ℚ[m]`. Polynomial units over `ℚ(√2)` are constants, contradicting `u(m)≥m²`. This covers finite residue-class piecewise rational polynomials and even rational *functions* with rational coefficients taking integral values there: a nonzero remainder after polynomial division eventually has size less than the reciprocal of a common fixed coefficient denominator, so integrality forces the remainder to vanish. It does not rule out algebraic functions or all real-semialgebraic sections on arbitrarily thin subsets (e.g. a Pell coordinate may itself be chosen as a parameter). If a **single real-semialgebraic section on a tail** gave integral Pell pairs for every integer `n` there with the stated bounds, its unbounded abscissa is eventually increasing by o-minimal monotonicity and not eventually constant. Distinct successive positive Pell units differ by a factor at least `3+2√2` in unit size, so such a section would grow exponentially along integers, contradicting its linear bound. This does not contradict the actual PA-scalar window supply in `T_Π`; dense *algebraic-family sections in the variable prime* are stronger than the presence of individual arithmetic witnesses. Thus tame sections in either of these senses are not an `IR`-stable sufficient hypothesis covering all of `U`.

## 13. A restricted witness class which can be eliminated exactly

**PROVED (rel. Hilbert irreducibility on an integral arithmetic progression).** Consider any true sentence `∀n∈ℕ ∃y≤t(n) [F(n,y)=0 ∧ θ(n,y)]`, with `0≠F∈ℤ[N,Y]` and `θ` open (no free auxiliary parameters). There is a successful **finite rational-polynomial menu**, selected by a fixed residue table and eventual signs, for every `n`. In particular this whole possibly non-effective *true fragment* is satisfied in every model of `IOpen`. The single algebraic witness may be highly nonunique; it need not originally be presented with an explicit section. The exception of finitely many small standard `n` uses their actual integer witnesses.

Proof: factor over `ℚ(N)`, discard finitely many poles/zeros of scalar content and leading coefficients, and distinguish roots in `ℚ(N)` from irreducible factors of degree ≥2 in `Y`. A rational root `g/h` in lowest terms admitting integral values at infinitely many increasing integers is in `ℚ[N]`: divide, take a fixed common denominator `D` of the quotient polynomial; the difference between an integer and the quotient lies in `(1/D)ℤ` but the proper fraction tends to zero. A nonzero remainder contributes only finitely many candidates. Each polynomial root has a fixed denominator, making its integrality periodic, and each open bound/comparison on it stabilizes by eventual polynomial signs. If some residue class is still uncovered, specialize `N=Mk+r` on that class. The finitely many higher-degree irreducibles remain irreducible over `ℚ(k)` by this invertible linear substitution; simultaneous Hilbert irreducibility gives arbitrarily large positive ordinary integer specializations with no rational root of any of them. Contradiction to totality (if there are no higher factors it is immediate). All tests for an actual menu value are identities, fixed integer divisions/congruences and eventual polynomial signs valid also in a discretely ordered ring with division. No transfer for a nonstandard polynomial degree, multiple varying inputs, positive-dimensional generic fibre or merely modular zeros. In particular a square-root **floor** oracle is not an instance of this equality lemma.

**PROVED (finite traces with these extra oracles, rel. §11 and z53).** Any finite menu nesting such calls has a standard adversary that picks only from these tables, as a function of each input, consistently across repeats. After representative selection it compiles to ordinary boundedly many polynomial arithmetic operations, fixed-modulus division and comparisons. For any remaining calls one must still produce valid answers in the allowed formal instruction class.

**CONDITIONAL on H for the associated finite family and the stated formal-tree hypotheses.** If all calls have such implementations and the resulting correct ES program and its augmented factorization tests meet `Z53_NONCRTLOGIC` Thm A(e)/T's defined finite-tree and trap hypotheses, then there are infinitely many primes at which every compiled checked output misses (combine the menus in one program, checking triples before returning SUCCESS). This does not say that the program meets the finite-tree hypotheses merely because its Skolem syntax has finite depth. In particular `g` for Pell, or for Euclidean/Farey with arbitrary nonstandard trajectories, need not fit. Nor do ILP/parity decisions in a fixed p-adic language give integrality simultaneously in an ordered number model. This is a fragment for which existential oracle quantification adds nothing to a covered program, not a separation of a full iterated rule theory.

**PROVED (finite-cover induction).** The unary-input lemma holds also for a fixed tuple of witnesses when the accompanying polynomial equalities cut out a zero-dimensional generic fibre over `ℚ(N)`. Indeed discard nontotal components and invert one nonzero polynomial in `N` so the reduced generic finite algebra spreads out as a product of finite separable field algebras over the open base (clear denominators in the primitive-element presentations, discriminants, idempotents, and the expressions for the coordinates). Nilpotents do not affect field-valued points. Away from the finitely many excluded fibres, a component of degree >1 has no rational point at any specialization where its primitive polynomial stays irreducible. Degree-one components give rational functions for **every** coordinate. Apply the same arithmetic-progression argument simultaneously to all of them. Consequently `IOpen` already proves **every parameter-free successor induction axiom** for such finite-cover predicates in the single induction variable. If the predicate is total in ℕ the finite menu proves its totality; otherwise its base fails or it has a true-to-false step at an ordinary index, verifiable by bounded exhaustive search on that standard box. This is not an induction theorem for an arbitrary family at arbitrary *parameters*. In particular a bound on the number of **integer** points per fibre does not imply geometric fibre dimension zero. Encoding parameters into a square block by floor inequalities does not give this dimension for free.

## 14. A stronger positive loop fragment in T_Π, and a finite negative control

**Assessment (open hypothesis: standard nested-loop trap, `NT(U)`, not established by H).** For every finite list of `∀E₁` consequences with finite `IR` derivations over `IOpen` and every finite menu in their witness symbols, the bounded trace predicate `∃z Miss(n,z)` of §11 has a standard hit `n≥2`. **PROVED (a sufficient simultaneous `Unif` condition on viable prefixes).** A specialization-based version may work over ordinary input **indices** rather than one evaluation of all stage variables at squares.
