# What a standard adversarial witness would have to defeat

**Assessment.** There are two distinct questions: does every proof give a finite menu, and does every countermodel give a *standard* trap for every menu? Yes to the first. For the second, the diagram of the countermodel has to be locally realizable in ordinary **ordered** arithmetic. That extra requirement also changes the one-witness extension criterion. Below, “host” means a model of true arithmetic, not just a PA coefficient field. We give two actual trap constructions in hosts (§§4–5), with different closure properties. They must not be combined.

## 1. Herbrand and the cost of insisting on a standard input

**PROVED (rel. Kaye 1990; direct proof `IE1_DECIDER` §10, branch `side-agent/z83-ie1-decider` at `bb06ac1bc`; see §7).** Work in the finite ordinary language of ordered arithmetic. The theorem-rule closure `U` of `IOpen` adds the universally closed bounded-existential conclusions of the induction rule, where both base and step are **theorems** of an earlier level. Use this axiom list, not all sentences of its deductive closure as witness relations. One may instead list all its `∀E₁` consequences without changing the theory. `U`, `IE₁⁻`, `IE₁` have the same `∀E₁` consequences. The sorted positive ES predicate `Sol(n,x,y,z)` can incorporate `x≤y≤z≤2(n+1)^4` (z76 `IE1_BREAK` §4); its equation is `4xyz=n(xy+yz+zx)`. Write `E` for `∀n≥2 ∃x,y,z Sol`. Switch between the semiring and its signed difference ring by sign splitting; for host embeddings use the inclusion of ordered rings and their positive cones, not an unordered ring embedding. All witnesses, including divisibility quotients or sign coordinates as appropriate, are part of the open matrix `W` **with** its bounds.

**PROVED (rel. Shepherdson, real-closed-field QE).** A universal expansion for the base uses symbols `F_f(ā)=⌊f(ā)⌋` for each (parameter-free over the rationals) semialgebraic function, arities arbitrary, defined in the real closure of the ordered fraction field. Totalize a partial graph by zero off its domain. The cut `y≤f(ā)<y+1` has an open ordered-ring translation by RCF quantifier elimination, evaluated at the ring entries; clear numerical denominators. It defines at most one integer in a discretely ordered ring. Existence of all these floors is precisely the integer-part property: each real-closure element is an indexed real root of a polynomial over the ring (or of one after clearing fraction denominators), hence covered. Shepherdson identifies this with open induction on the cone. Thus the base symbols are *definitional* on models of `IOpen`. An alternative is ordinary nonunique local open countersteps for induction as in `IE1_DECIDER` §10, in which case treat their witness choices like any others. Open conditionals in terms are just definitional abbreviations.

**PROVED (Herbrand).** For each extra axiom `σ=∀a ∃b Wσ(a,b)` introduce a tuple-valued choice (coordinate functions taking the **same input tuple**). The expanded universal theory `T*` has precisely expansions of models of `U` as its models; choose a whole valid tuple on every input. The following are equivalent:

1. `U ⊢ E`.
2. Some finite axiom list, a finite list of triples of terms `τ_j(c)` in the expanded language, and the base force `c<2 ∨ ⋁_j Sol(c,τ_j(c))` in **all** their expanded models, with fresh arbitrary `c`.

Indeed if `T* ∪ {c≥2} ∪ {¬Sol(c,τ(c)): all term triples}` is consistent, close `c` under **all** functions/constants in a model. The substructure still satisfies all universal axioms, and the open misses are absolute, so its reduct refutes `E`. Conversely a countermodel of `E` expands with a failing `c`, refuting every such menu. Compactness gives (2), with only finitely many axioms used; add the witness axioms for any additional choice symbols actually mentioned. Soundness/completeness gives the reverse implication also for the unexpanded theory. One bad standard interpretation defeats **one menu**; defeating all menus using the given fragment suffices for nonprovability there. It is not enough to exhibit one bad computation for one candidate proof algorithm.

**PROVED (standard validity theorem).** Write `DC=Th_∀(ℤ,+,−,·,<,0,1)` (equivalently use the cone with order and differences supplied). Replacing “all their expanded models” in (2) by “every valid interpretation on the standard integers” gives an equivalence with **`U+DC ⊢ E`**, not by Herbrand with `U ⊢ E`. Proof of the key direction: flatten a finite failing evaluation into variables naming the values of all subterms. Prescribe ring computations, open floor graphs, tests, misses, bounds, the matrices at all oracle calls and, for each pair of calls of the same symbol, equality of their entire answer tuples if the input tuples coincide. This is a finite existential-open sentence without choice symbols. A realization in a model of `DC` implies one in ℤ, since its negation is universal. Extend the consistent finite partial choices to total choices on ℤ (all listed axioms hold there); floors already have their unique interpretation. Conversely a standard realization is such an evaluation. Apply the preceding equivalence over `U+DC` (the `DC` axioms are universal). This argument does **not** assume that arbitrary functions on a nonstandard ring extend elementarily to a standard-function expansion. In particular, standard ADV-TRAP is a sufficient, possibly stronger, attack on the original question.

**PROVED (no general converse).** In a true recursively axiomatized theory such as `U`, some true universal ordered polynomial statement `∀ā ψ(ā)` is not provable: otherwise by enumerating proofs one could enumerate integer polynomial nonexistence, as well as existence by direct search, contradicting the negative solution of Hilbert's tenth problem. The bounded goal `∀ā ∃b≤0 [b=0 ∧ ψ(ā)]` then has a constant standard menu and yet is not provable. No assertion that ES itself separates the two equivalences. `Z64_UNPROV` §2.8 gives all universal **unordered ring** facts; `Z65_LOGIC2` §2 explicitly leaves full ordered correctness of those models open. They do not supply the missing implication.

## 2. Hulls, prime points, and two different extension criteria

**PROVED (diagram criterion).** For unpointed inputs `n≥2`, the following are equivalent: standard ADV-TRAP for `U`; `U+DC ⊬ E`; and existence of an ordered subring `R ⊆ M ≡ ℤ` with `R ⊨ U` omitting all solutions at some `P∈R`, `P≥2`. Indeed `DC` axiomatizes structures embeddable in models of `Th(ℤ)` by compactness of the complete atomic and negated atomic diagram (each finite piece is an existential-open test). No elementarity of `R⊆M`. Inside a host, being `U`-closed means containing all base floors and **at least one** valid tuple for each additional instance over `R`; this is precisely satisfaction of those axioms by open absoluteness. With a fixed standard counterexample, `P` need not be nonstandard. Assuming standard ES, `P` must be nonstandard, by the actual numeral certificates, not by compactness alone.

**PROVED (pointed version).** The exact standard version for a nonstandard **host-prime** `P` is: for every finite list of axioms and menus and each ordinary `B` there is a **prime** `p>B` with simultaneous standard misses under consistent valid choices. One can include any fixed congruence diagram, imposing finitely many of its constraints at a time. In the forward construction take the expanded true-arithmetic theory of the **reduct** (i.e. `Th(ℤ)` in its own language), all witness axioms, `Prime(P)`, `P>B` for each `B`, and all term misses. Finite satisfiability by the stated tests gives a host, and the term hull is the desired `R`. Conversely choose the functions inside `R` on inputs from `R` and arbitrarily in `M` outside. The finite transcript of §1 together with `Prime(P)` and `P>B` is first order in the host reduct, so it transfers to ℤ, and its choices extend there. Primality in a subring does not in general imply primality upstairs. A failing prime *internally* by prime reduction, when available, does not supply that transfer. Neither bare `U ⊬ E` nor a failing standard prime by itself gives arbitrarily large standard prime traps by this reasoning.

**PROVED (one-step, fixed host type).** Write `A=Hull_F(r)` for the subring generated over finitely many generators `r` (including `P`) under the base floor functions. The host in this paragraph is always expanded by the unique base functions, a definitional expansion; put `T₀=Th(ℤ,F)`. Let `a` be a term in `r`, fix `σ`, and for each triple of base terms `t` put `H_t(r,w)=Sol(P,t(r,w))`. In an elementary extension of the **given pointed host** one can choose a tuple `w` meeting `Wσ(a,w)` such that `Hull_F(r,w)` still misses `Sol(P,−)` iff

```
for every finite J,  M ⊨ ∃w [Wσ(a,w) ∧ ⋀_{t∈J} ¬H_t(r,w)].                 (S)
```

This is compactness with the elementary diagram; (S) is necessary by elementarity. Failure is equivalently a finite cover by the `H_t` for all valid `w` at the *actual* `r`. One may express it uniformly as `T₀ ⊢ ∀r,w (δ(r)∧Wσ(a,w) → ⋁_{t∈J}H_t)` with `δ` from the **complete** type of `r` (the cover itself, universally quantified in `w`, will do). There is a different, weaker demand: keep only an isomorphic copy of the generated stage. Use the **quantifier-free** type `q(r)` instead of the elementary diagram. Then the identical compactness proof gives exactly the obstruction with some **open** `δ∈q` (finite conjunctions permitted); an arbitrary model of `T₀` suffices. To retain host-primality in this second test add `Prime(P)` to the type/background explicitly. One cannot both silently weaken the diagram and promise an elementary extension over the old generators. For the elementary issue already in the pure ordered language, an infinite prime and an infinite composite have the same unary open type (eventual signs), but at the former **every** witness to “dummy zero or nontrivial factorization” takes the dummy branch, unlike the latter. This illustrates the logical distinction, not a claim about q.f. types with all floors.

**PROVED (sufficient stage invariant; and its limit).** In the space of finite generated-stage open types realizable in hosts, a family of safe types which (i) starts at an allowed type of `P`, (ii) is safe for *all* base terms, and (iii) has, for each member and each requested instance, a jointly realizable witness-extension type in the **same family**, suffices. Dovetail countably many requests by order of introduction, concatenate the types, use compactness and take the union of the generated stages. Condition (iii), not just (S) for the first step, is essential. The condition is necessary in the existential sense too: take the types of all finite tuples extending `P` in an actual safe closed hull in a host, with their own allowable continuations. In the complete-type formulation the same construction uses elementary extensions. No assumption of homogeneity for types from unrelated hosts.

**PROVED (automatic thick and scalar cases).** If a semialgebraic part of the real witness fibre over `A` contains a product of closed intervals of length ≥1 in every witness coordinate, it already has an integer point in `A`. Write the existence of such a box by an ordered-field formula; real-closure elementarity descends it to `rc(Frac A)`. Round its left endpoints upward there, using the integer-part property; rounding remains in the box. The same holds after a given affine unimodular change over `A` with inverse over `A`. For *one scalar witness* to an open matrix, whenever the host has a witness, so does `A`: semialgebraic cells in one variable have endpoints in the old real closure or at infinity. An interval meeting the host integers has an old integer by taking an appropriate rounded endpoint and adjusting by 1 for open endpoints (or use the other end for a left-unbounded interval); an integer singleton must equal its old floor. Cf. S. Raffer, [*Diophantine correct open induction*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3798), Thm 1.1: `IOpen+DC` is exactly all standard true `∀x̄ ∃y` open-matrix sentences with **one** `y`. Neither result applies to an encoded tuple whose decoding uses extra existential witnesses.

**PROVED (thick lattice fibres really suffice).** More generally suppose that in the host the open matrix holds at a fraction ≥ε (a fixed ordinary ε>0) of the integer points of an axis-parallel integer cube of infinite side length `L`. The cube need not be based at old parameters; its **full-dimensional** density suffices to get the preceding real unit box. Proof: put the cube on `[0,1]^d` by an affine translation and dilation in the host real closure. For each of the finitely many nonzero resulting atom polynomials divide by the maximum absolute coefficient. The coefficientwise standard parts give nonzero real polynomials (some coefficient is ±1). Identically zero atoms are already decided. The union of their real zero sets has measure zero. Partition the unit cube into finitely many equal rational subcubes so that the closures meeting this union have total volume `<ε/2` (compactness and Jordan measure zero). On the closure of each other cell all normalized signs are constant, including for points in the host real closure: their standard parts lie in the cell closure and each polynomial has a strictly positive minimum absolute value there. The discarded cells, including overlaps at faces, cover fewer than ε of the host integer grid for infinite `L`: each ordinary rational subcube of side `1/k` has `(L/k+O(1))^d` points and there are only ordinarily finitely many. Counting is in true arithmetic; there is a good cell with a witness. The entire real cell now satisfies the same Boolean matrix, yielding a box of side `L/k>1`. By compactness (or an ultraproduct of proposed finite counterexamples) there is also a finite threshold depending only on the fixed formula and ε for this implication in standard arithmetic. This is **not** an assertion about positive density relative to a thin variety. It handles such full-dimensional requests without enlarging the stage and therefore preserves every safe-stage invariant.

**PROVED (why cardinality is insufficient).** Pad any witness relation `W(a,u)` by freely adjoining coordinates `0≤v_i≤a`. The padded relation is total iff the original one is; projection extracts `u` from *every* reply regardless of the padding. In particular this gives arbitrarily many free dimensions across the scheme and arbitrarily many witnesses already at each infinite input, without removing a single forced coordinate. As a concrete true goal, pad the unique square root floor: projecting any witness still solves the square-root-floor problem. A corresponding ES-specific decoder would survive padding unchanged. Even a single-variable bounded fibre is finite at every standard input; “finitely many” in that sense imposes no restriction. Thickness in **all** real coordinates as above is a sufficient hypothesis, not generic freedom on a thin projection.

## 3. Forced functions: what is forced and what the rule can certify

**PROVED (host semantics).** In a fixed host let `D(P)` be its base term closure of `P` iterated with the functions having an open bounded certificate `W` with `U ⊢ ∀a ∃b W` and **one** valid tuple on each standard input. Standard uniqueness transfers to the host even when not proved in `U`. Every `U`-closed subring containing `P` contains `D(P)`. For this deterministic closure alone the standard cofinal-prime finite-menu test of §2 is *exact*, with just the unique functions, by the same proof. A fibre of size two does not define a function. Even constant output multiplicity is different from an identically finite set of possible *values*: two possible values may both grow. If exactly `k` distinct answers are forced to exist by `U` and at most `k` occur in ℕ for an ordinary fixed `k`, a sorted exhaustive list of the `k` tuples is itself a unique bounded tuple, by adjoining `k` copies of the certificate and lexicographically ordering them. This argument uses the lower bound theorem, not a guessed maximum in ℕ.

**PROVED (examples, rel. z79 `IE1_BASE` §§4,9 and `IE1_DECIDER` §§1,6).** For `a,b>0`, certify `d,u,v,s,t` by `d>0, a=du, b=dv, su+tv=1, 0≤s<v`; handle zero and signs by cases. This bounded row, including the positive generator and reduced pair, is unique by the row identity, common divisibility and uniqueness of the residue representative. It computes canonical CRT for a coprime pair by division. In the Farey test the reduced exact point or the unimodular bracketing pair is geometrically forced: no reduced fraction of denominator ≤k is in the strict interior (its positive coordinates in the unimodular basis force denominator ≥ the sum). The raw row certifying an exact fraction need not be unique. For binary reduction the change-of-basis matrix is not unique (even negating both columns preserves it). For `DL` the escape and the root alternatives are not globally disjoint. If no nontrivial divisor of `h` is coprime to `b`, the root modulo `h` is unique: `F(v)−F(u)=(v−u)(1+bQ)`, the second factor is a unit modulo `h` by Bézout. Do not declare a particular root total on arbitrary inputs without the escape. These facts distinguish a forced projection, a unique full certificate, and multiple choices.

**PROVED (logic versus search).** Full parameter induction `IE₁` *does* prove that every `n>1` has a least divisor greater than one, which is prime: minimize the nonempty `E₁` set `{d>1:∃e≤n, de=n}` by `IE₁=LE₁` (Smith, NDJFL 1993, Thm 2.3 and the least-divisor argument in §4). Divisibility has bounded quotient certificates; minimality and primality in this proof are **universal** checks, not additional coordinates of an open certificate for a prime-factoring Skolem function. For a supplied proper divisor, factoring off the cofactor is of course bounded. “Some divisor >1” alone permits `n` itself. Neither an open certificate for primality of polynomially bounded *magnitude* nor its impossibility follows from its usual `U₁` definition. Likewise a compatible target in a residue class with no supplied divisor, or a QNR at an arbitrary odd prime, does not follow by saying to search a polynomial box. Smith's Lem. 2.7 gives inverses, Thm 4.2 identifies two squares of an odd prime with solubility of `x²≡−1`, not the solubility itself. We derive no total E₁ QNR or ES-divisor oracle.

**Assessment (the induction bottleneck).** A derivation of the successor premise can be Herbrandized with an *old* arbitrary valid state given as extra input: `∀i,p,w [W(i,p,w) → ∃w' W(i+1,p,w')]`. This yields a finite menu using lower-level choices over that theory; it does not identify a particular terminal witness. Iterating even a deterministic selector `t` times is not a term of ordinary fixed length. Conversely one need not present a coded run of the loop to use the endpoint axiom. Knowing the lower-level replies on a square twin for a finite list of queries does not ensure that the endpoint relation has *any* reply compatible with that twin and the new list of tests. A forced floor already distinguishes an exact square from a prime by comparing its square to the input; §5 gives a less elementary failure of flattening. There is no prohibition on induction proving genuine search progress: Farey and `DL` are counterexamples to that prohibition.

## 4. A nested host fragment with no nonrational floors

**PROVED (host-prime fragment, unconditional).** Let `L₀` consist of the discretely ordered ring base, floor division, bounded normalized Bézout and coprime CRT, the bounded rational-target Farey axiom of z79 §4, `(Sep)` of z79 §8, and every ordinary fixed-degree `DL` of Lane 1 §6. Include also **every `∀E₁` axiom of `U` whose matrix, including bounds, is piecewise linear in the *joint witness block*, with polynomial functions of all inputs as coefficients** (Boolean combinations of such comparisons). Denote this fragment `U_lin`. It is a subtheory of `U` under the signed translation; no integer square root axiom is included by fiat. There is an ordered subring of a true-arithmetic host containing an infinite **host prime** `P≡1 (840)` where `U_lin` holds but `ES(P)` fails. This theorem gives *nested* standard adversaries for `U_lin`, by §2 applied without the base algebraic floors. In particular no Schinzel H is needed for these oracle calls. The ring is **not** an `IOpen` model.

**PROVED (construction).** Choose `α∈Ẑ` a parsimonious unit-square profile: each `α_l` is transcendental over ℚ and, for every nonzero `f∈ℤ[X]`, `f(α_l)` is a unit for almost all primes `l`. To construct it enumerate primitive polynomials, and for increasing `l` avoid on the nonzero squares all roots of an initial segment of length tending to infinity (sum of degrees `<(l−1)/2` and nonzero reductions). Lift within each prescribed square neighbourhood to a transcendental `l`-adic square. Impose `α≡1 mod 840` at its finitely many places. Dirichlet and compactness realize `P` prime infinite of these residues in `M≡ℤ`. In `Frac M` set

```
A = ℚ[P] ∩ M = {f(P): f∈ℚ[X], f(α)∈Ẑ}.
```

Indeed `P` is transcendental over ℚ by its size; integrality of a rational-coefficient polynomial is tested at its fixed common coefficient denominator. Order is eventual leading sign and `A∩ℚ=ℤ`. Each rational polynomial factor can be scaled by a positive rational to have unit value at **every** place: parsimony makes the valuation correction finitely supported. Thus a nonzero nonunit in `A` is a product of finitely many standard prime integers and normalized irreducibles of `ℚ[P]` (up to sign); each normalized irreducible is prime since a polynomial quotient by it preserves local integrality. Each ordinary prime is prime by residue evaluation with kernel exactly `lA`. This is ordinary external factorization, not an internal code in `A` or the assertion that these polynomial primes remain prime in `M`.

**PROVED (the linear witness edge, rel. Bogart–Goodrick–Woods, [*Parametric Presburger arithmetic*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.08520), Thm 1.15, Property 3).** Substitute the finitely many rational polynomials of `P` serving as inputs into the family and clear numerical denominators, treating the new variable `X` as **one** parameter. The formula in the unknowns is now one-parametric Presburger (multiplication of unknowns was forbidden; multiplying them by integer polynomials in `X` is allowed). On the positive integers its nonempty fibres admit a selector with eventually quasipolynomial coordinates. Choose the residue of `P` modulo the ordinary period. The chosen rational polynomials give a valid integral tuple at `P`, by true-arithmetic transfer of the eventual theorem on this class; if the predicate is guarded, require the inputs integral/valid, which they are at `P`. Hence the tuple lies in `A`. In fact for any fixed such parametric Presburger fibre nonempty in `M`, the selector belongs to `A`; we only include open-matrix `U` axioms in the stated fragment. This proves closure with *arbitrary* inputs from earlier query stages. It includes ordinary variable floor division, which is linear in quotient and remainder at fixed inputs.

**PROVED (remaining edges).** For `a,b>0` use finite factorization for `d` and divide to coprime `u,v`. One can equivalently produce the gcd by the ordinary Euclidean algorithm: floor-divide in `A`; for polynomials of equal degree the quotient is an ordinary integer, and at that degree the positive leading coefficients have a common ordinary denominator inherited from the entering pair. Each successive remainder has strictly smaller leading numerator, except possibly equality with the divisor's leading numerator, in which case both have the same leading term and the **next** remainder has smaller degree (quotient 1 for two ordered positives with ratio tending to 1 from above). When degrees are unequal the first remainder has at most the divisor degree; after at most one such step one either drops degree or enters an equal-degree run. There are only finitely many degrees; the constant tail is ordinary integer Euclid. Back-substitute and reduce the row coefficient modulo `v` for the bounded normalized row; solve CRT with this inverse. For `(Sep)`, split the finite prime powers in `h` between the two factors so that each avoids its designated coprime input, with Bézout rows as certificates. For `DL`, use a prime divisor of `h` coprime to `b` as escape if possible. Otherwise `h|b^k` for some **ordinary** `k≥1`, including `b=0`. Starting at `x=0` iterate `x←a−bG(x)` externally: successive differences gain one power of `b` by the polynomial difference identity. After `k` updates the defect is divisible by `b^k` (for `b=0` the update gives `a`). Reduce modulo `h`. All asserted certificate bounds hold by reduction/row compression, not by bounding that external iteration count uniformly.

For Farey start at `0/1,1/1` for a strict target `a/b` with denominator bound `k≥1`; equality at either endpoint is immediate. With adjacent endpoints `u/v,w/z` use positive errors `L=av−bu`, `H=bw−az`. Advancing the left endpoint by `j` right columns subtracts `jH` from `L` and adds `jz` to `v`; use `j=min(⌊L/H⌋,⌊(k−v)/z⌋)` when `L≥H`; swap sides for `H>L`. If the budget truncates the Euclidean quotient (even at zero moves), the denominator sum now exceeds `k` and both errors are positive. Otherwise there is equality or an ordinary Euclidean remainder step. Finitely many such steps by the preceding argument. Determinant one gives a row for exact endpoints (compress to the stated bounds). No internal continued-fraction list. No use of the square-root scale of Thue's theorem.

**PROVED (ES miss and a sharp failure of closure).** `P` is prime in both structures. In `A` every denominator of a putative positive triple is a rational polynomial `f_i(P)` with numerator identity. Since `P` divides the product in `ℚ[P]`, some are divisible by `P`; not all (their integral quotients by `P`, a unit at all places, would have three positive reciprocals summing to 4). For a common ordinary coefficient denominator `D` include the coefficients of these quotients and enlarge to a multiple of 840. Choose a large odd `m` with `m²≡α mod D` and coprime to the nonzero cleared constant terms of all `P`-free denominators: at primes of `D` enforce the unit square-root residue of `α`, at other finitely many primes avoid zero. Evaluation at `m²` is integral, positive for large `m`, and the `P`-divisible coordinates are multiples of `m²` while the others are coprime to `m`. This is the forbidden **strict** odd-square Type I or II, z64 §2.5 / Elsholtz–Tao Prop. 1.6. Thus `¬ES(P)` in `A`. But `⌊√P⌋∉A` by polynomial degree growth. In fact a finite nested menu has standard traps on **every sufficiently large prime of a menu-dependent reduced progression**: choose replies in `A`, represent all input, answer and computed coordinates by their rational polynomials at `P`, and restrict to the residue of `α` modulo a common coefficient denominator. Integrality then holds throughout the class, and signs, equalities and inequalities of polynomial evaluations agree with the finite transcript eventually. Include argument-tuple equality/disequality for repeated calls; the finite table extends to total choices at each prime, with no assertion that one interpretation works for all primes at once. This produces an adversarial transcript, not a uniform evaluator of every possible oracle answer in the z53 instruction set. The fragment and the prime/host interface, not an improvement of the general-model `IOpen` record, are the point. For comparison, among the named `IOpen`-containing fragments one may take `U_X=IOpen+Bez+CRT+(Sep)+Farey+proper binary reduction+DL`; this is contained in `U` by z79 §§4,8,9, Lane 1 §§6,10, and `U_X ⊬ E` follows **unconditionally, in unrestricted models** from `T_Π` in Lane 1 §§6–8. We have not embedded `T_Π` as an ordered subring of true arithmetic.

## 5. The first algebraic-floor layer, including exact rational output

**PROVED (flat-floor theorem).** Fix a finite list of parameter-free semialgebraic functions `f_i(X)` over ℚ at infinity, and a finite menu of triples of rational functions with rational coefficients in `p` and `⌊f_i(p)⌋`. Poles, nonintegers or nonpositive entries are not solutions. There is a fixed `D` divisible by 840 such that successes have **relative prime density zero** on `p≡1 mod D`. Finite selectors, open branches and any indexed selection from this fixed menu do not matter. Here is a host strengthening: for some infinite host-prime `P≡1` modulo every standard integer, let `K` be the field generated over ℚ by `P` and **all** these first-layer floors (all unary semialgebraic functions, not just a prescribed finite tuple). Then `K` is relatively algebraically closed in `Frac M`, `K∩M` omits ES at `P`, but **does not even have Bézout for the pair** `⌊√P⌋,⌊P^(1/3)⌋`. In particular one coordinate of the unique normalized Bézout data at that pair is *transcendental over the flat field* inside the host. Algebraic closure here is relative to the field of host **rationals**, not its real closure. This exhibits a provably necessary new source of data in `D(P)`; it does not prove success or failure of ES in `D(P)`.

**PROVED (adelic distribution, rel. real Puiseux expansion; Bergelson–Kolesnik–Son, [*Uniform distribution of subpolynomial functions along primes and applications*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.04960), Thm 1.6 = 3.1).** Choose a ℚ-basis of the real algebraic numbers containing 1. Use the countable list of distinct monomials `h_j(X)=c X^r`, `c` a basis element, `r>0` rational, omitting `c=1` at positive integer exponents. Write `Y_j(p)=⌊h_j(p)⌋`. For any finite sublist, nonzero integral vector `k`, rational number `s` and integer `m>0`, the Hardy-field function `Σk_j h_j(X)/m+sX` differs from every rational polynomial by a leading nonzero positive power of `X`.
