## 1. A uniform no-minimum test, without prescribing any digits

**PROVED (rel. the following class hypotheses).** Let `B` be a signed model of true arithmetic, `H=2^L` for an infinite `L∈B`, `C={a∈B : |a|^j<H for every ordinary j≥1}`, and `R` an uncut discretely ordered domain extending `B`. Then there exists a positive squarefree scalar `h≤H³` (constructed below) for which the set of nonnegative `u` satisfying the one atom

```
            h² | (1+u) ((1+u)² − P)²                               (1)
```

is nonempty and has no least element. Thus even unary least search at **constant divisor and polynomial dividend**, with neither a negation nor an existential projection, fails. **PROVED (sparse modulus).** In `B` put `m=⌊log₂ L⌋`, `x_i=2^(2^i)`, `0≤i≤m`, and choose the first prime `p_i` in `(x_i,2x_i)` by Bertrand. They are distinct increasing odd primes. Form `h=∏p_i` **in PA**. We need three estimates, using only internally finite products:

* `∏_{i≤j} p_i ≤ 2^(j+1+2^(j+1)−1) ≤ x_j^3` for `0≤j≤m` (since `j≤2^j`). Thus `h≤H³` and the prefix up to **any** `q=p_j∈C` has product in `C`.

**PROVED (lower bound for *every* hit).** Write `t=1+u`. At each listed `p>C` the second factor is a unit modulo `p`, so `p²|t` from (1) (cancel twice at a prime). **PROVED (a smaller hit).** Given this `c` take a listed `q∈C`, `q>c`. Write `M=(h/q)²`. **PROVED (an overspill consequence of the quintic, without assuming the large-prime profile).** In fact one can extract a *greatest rooted prime* on sufficiently sparse scalar lists. ### 2.1 Why a bounded algebraic repair at the large primes is still insufficient

**PROVED (relative to §1's ordered ring, power cut and scalar divisor/gcd hypotheses; elementary Kummer theory and finite fields).** In fact replace §1's square-tested seed by **any** cut parameter `Y` with scalar residues at the small listed primes and with `s=Y mod p` transcendental over `k_p=B/pB` at every large listed prime. In particular an immediate henselization not altering these residue fields cannot suffice. This bounded-degree extension theorem does **not** require the seed to reduce to squares in the reserve.

Here is the polynomial for an ordinary `N≥2` with `2^(N−1)>d`. At an indeterminate `S` write `u_i²=S+i` for `1≤i≤N`, `G={±1}^N` acting by sign changes. Over *every* field of characteristic zero the classes of these `N` linear polynomials in its rational function field are independent, by their distinct simple zeros. The compositum thus has degree `2^N`, group `G`, and basis `u^e` for `e∈{0,1}^N`. For each nonidentity `σ∈G` pick a binary basis `E_σ` of the character hyperplane trivial on `σ`, and put `θ_σ=Σ_{e∈E_σ}u^e`. Its stabilizer is precisely `{1,σ}` by linear independence of the basis monomials. Form

```
    F₀(X,S) = ∏_{σ≠1} ∏_{g∈G/⟨σ⟩} (X−gθ_σ),
    F(X,S)  = F₀(X,S) F₀(X−1,S).
```

The orbit products have coefficients in `ℤ[S]`: expand in the free module with monic relations `u_i²=S+i` and use sign invariance over the torsion-free coefficient ring. Each individual orbit polynomial is irreducible of degree `2^(N−1)` before specialization at a transcendental `S=s` (or after that substitution over `k_p`). Hence neither it nor its translate has a root at the large primes under the degree bound. This is a restriction on relative **algebraic degree**, not transcendence degree of a field of words in the order host.

At a small listed prime `q`, evaluate `S` at the scalar residue of `Y`. Choose all `u_i` in the *internal* degree-two finite-field extension of `B/qB` (all scalars there are squares of elements of this extension). Frobenius on the chosen tuple changes signs by some `σ`, with arbitrary compatible signs on zero entries. If the action is trivial choose any nonidentity sign for the orbit polynomial: the tuple is then already scalar. Otherwise the specialization of `θ_σ` is Frobenius-fixed, hence scalar. Thus an orbit polynomial has a scalar root. One of that root and its translate by 1 is nonzero, a root of `F`. Precisely, for the fixed ordinary polynomial the assertion “for every odd prime `q` and integer `y` there is `0<x<q` with `q | F(x,y)`” just proved is a single true arithmetic sentence (primality as usual, with no standardness predicate). At large primes no roots forces `p²|t`, and the small-prime competitor uses a nonzero scalar root. Both bounds in §1 remain unchanged. ## 4. The BCM successor: a test without prescribed monomials

**PROVED (relative to discrete order and Bézout).** Let `D` be a proper end-initial signed subring of `R`, `H>0` divisible in `R` by every `d∈D⁺`. Put `F=Frac D`, `A=(D∖0)⁻¹R`, `U=A[1/H]`. Use the convex hull `O` of `F` in the ordered real closure. Then `A∩O=F` (clear the one old denominator; initiality). Suppose merely that `H²` has a least positive divisor `t>H`. Then there are **two coprime positive divisors `a,b` of `H`, both outside `D`**, with `H=lab`, `t=la²` for `l∈R⁺` and `a/b>1` of residue one for this order valuation. In `A` there are distinct maximal ideals over `H`, one containing `a`, the other `b`. In the local valuation rings of the Bézout domain `A` there, `a/b` has opposite strictly signed values. In particular the obstruction is on the puncture at **this H after inverting every old scalar**, not merely the countless ordinary prime ideals of `R` below a BCM bound.

Indeed `H>D` on positives, and for every `f=c/d>0` in `F` the pair `Hf,H/f` is integral with product `H²`. Thus `1<t/H≤1+1/d` for all `d∈D⁺`, by the leastness hypothesis. `t/H` cannot be in `F` by the midpoint between 1 and itself. Reduce `t/H=a/b` to coprime positive terms by the row gcd: `t=ga,H=gb`; from `t|H²` obtain `a|gb²`, hence `a|g`. This gives the displayed expressions. If either reduced coordinate belonged to `D`, the other would be bounded by an old integer, hence also old. Neither is an `A`-unit: an inverse would make it divide an old nonzero integer after clearing denominators, hence be in `D`. The coprimality row persists in `A`, proving the maximal-ideal and sign assertions. Replacing the threshold by the floors in z82 `BCM_LDIV` §8 forces normalized units at coinitial `v_order(g−1)>0`; for the units in that proof the **same coprime opposite-sign calculation** applies, since they too are nonscalar, relatively bounded with bounded inverses, and come from divisors of this `H²`. No assumptions on total rational rank.

**PROVED (rank must survive deletion of any one place).** Suppose now plain successors hold at every threshold for `H²`, in an integer part as in z82 §8. For each maximal ideal `M` of `A` containing `H` let `w_M` be the group valuation of the valuation domain `A_M` (localization of a Bézout domain). Here assume `R` is Bézout and take valuations on `Frac R`. Put `V=(1+𝔪_O)∩U*`. The image of every nonidentity element of `V` on this family of valuations has both a strictly positive and a strictly negative coordinate. Indeed away from `H` the evaluations of units in `A[1/H]` are zero. Nonnegative values everywhere would put the element in `⋂_M A_M=A`, hence in `A∩O=F`; residue one then makes it 1. Apply the same argument to its inverse. Thus projecting away **any one chosen maximal over `H`** is injective on `V` and its image still has infinite rational rank. Here units of `V` are arbitrary products, not necessarily all normalized divisors themselves.

More precisely, discard any collection `E` of places on which the **joint image** of `V` has finite rational rank `r`. For every specified order value `δ>0` there is a nonidentity `g∈V` with `0<v_order(g−1)<δ`, *zero at all places in `E`*, and opposite signs at two places outside `E`. Take `r+1` normalized divisors from z82 §8 with distinct strictly decreasing positive difference-values below `δ`; their valuation vectors on `E` satisfy a nonzero ordinary integer relation after clearing denominators (the groups are torsion-free). In that product the term of smallest difference-value with nonzero exponent survives by the characteristic-zero binomial argument; apply the two-sign criterion to the resulting `g`. In particular, for **finite actual maximal support over `HA`** at least two of the local value images of `V` must have infinite rational rank, not just one infinite-rank axis plus finitely many finite-rank auxiliaries. For these local rank tests only the convex subgroup generated by `w_M(H)` can contribute (both `g` and its inverse have an `H`-power denominator). With infinitely many places we do not sum abstract local ranks to bound a product image, and a finite list of defining valuations is *not* a theorem that the actual maximal support is finite. This refines the total-rank obstruction for plain successors, without using a prime-primary least filter or (Sep).

**PROVED (single-valuation no-go).** More generally suppose there is an upper ring `A₀⊇A` with `A₀∩O=F`, a valuation `w` of the ambient field (arbitrary ordered value group), and that for **units `z` of `U`** of nonnegative `w`-value we have `z∈A₀`. For example `A₀=A₀[1/H]∩{w≥0}` with `A⊆A₀`. Then there is no least divisor of `H²` above `H`, even without Bézout: `g=t/H∈U*` for any such alleged successor and one of `g,g⁻¹` has nonnegative `w` and is in `O`; it must be scalar, contrary to the midpoint argument. This kills single-landing infinite-rank twists with that intersection and hull, **even if one has not calculated a single punctured unit or the relation between the order and `w`**. Adjoining a principal unit of the required size while preserving the single-valuation and hull axioms is impossible already before checking Lag, TS, QR or specialization. An end extension after a completed failed successor cannot help: a smaller positive divisor and cofactor of `H²` would both be old. If a proposed pre-completion repair uses incomparable valuation vectors, the sign calculation is necessary but not sufficient; we do not exclude it. The true BCM axiom here is `∀x ∃H>0 ∀d (0<d≤x → d|H)`; a bound at any `x>D` implies the all-old hypothesis, but mere divisibility by all ordinary integers at one stage is not global BCM.

**PROVED (one Hahn test, including BCM).** In the canonical full truncation `R=ℤ+k((t^Γ))_{<0}` take `k=ℝ`, nonzero divisible ordered group `Γ`, ordered at positive infinitesimal monomials `t^γ` for `γ>0`. Here `<0` means *all support exponents strictly negative*, not merely negative leading valuation. It is an integer part of the real closed Hahn field: truncate the negative exponents, then take the integer floor of the constant coefficient, lowering it by one if it is exact and the omitted tail is negative. The convex hull of constants intersects the truncation in `ℤ`. Nevertheless `Frac R` contains `√2` as `(√2 t^{-γ})/t^{-γ}`. A Bézout domain is integrally closed, so it would have to contain `√2`, impossible in a discretely ordered ring. This also kills any twisted-section version allowing both coefficient multiples of the same scale with that ratio; it is not an assertion about every integer part of a Hahn field.

When `Γ` has no largest archimedean class, the canonical truncation actually has **global BCM**. For a cutoff whose least support exponent is `−γ≤0`, every positive divisor-candidate `d` up to it has all exponents in the convex subgroup generated by `γ` (for a finite cutoff all are ordinary). Thus `t^{-δ}/d∈R` for every such `d` simultaneously. No such BCM conclusion follows from infinite rational rank alone. In this example the missing Bézout axiom is decisive regardless of TS or a choice of `P`; no ES-countermodel package is claimed.

**PROVED / CONDITIONAL on host ES (ultrapower and actual PA subrings).** A convex cut containing `P` in an elementary extension of the pointed z78 model has the bounded gap from z82 §2 below any alleged BCM bound at `P`.
