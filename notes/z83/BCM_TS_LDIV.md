### 2.1 Why a bounded algebraic repair at the large primes is still insufficient

**PROVED (relative to §1's ordered ring, power cut and scalar divisor/gcd hypotheses; elementary Kummer theory and finite fields).** In fact replace §1's square-tested seed by **any** cut parameter `Y` with scalar residues at the small listed primes and with `s=Y mod p` transcendental over `k_p=B/pB` at every large listed prime. Here is the polynomial for an ordinary `N≥2` with `2^(N−1)>d`. At an indeterminate `S` write `u_i²=S+i` for `1≤i≤N`, `G={±1}^N` acting by sign changes.
```
    F₀(X,S) = ∏_{σ≠1} ∏_{g∈G/⟨σ⟩} (X−gθ_σ),
    F(X,S)  = F₀(X,S) F₀(X−1,S).
```

The orbit products have coefficients in `ℤ[S]`: expand in the free module with monic relations `u_i²=S+i` and use sign invariance over the torsion-free coefficient ring. ## 4. The BCM successor: a test without prescribed monomials

**PROVED (relative to discrete order and Bézout).** Let `D` be a proper end-initial signed subring of `R`, `H>0` divisible in `R` by every `d∈D⁺`. Put `F=Frac D`, `A=(D∖0)⁻¹R`, `U=A[1/H]`. Use the convex hull `O` of `F` in the ordered real closure. Then `A∩O=F` (clear the one old denominator; initiality). Suppose merely that `H²` has a least positive divisor `t>H`. Then there are **two coprime positive divisors `a,b` of `H`, both outside `D`**, with `H=lab`, `t=la²` for `l∈R⁺` and `a/b>1` of residue one for this order valuation. In `A` there are distinct maximal ideals over `H`, one containing `a`, the other `b`. In the local valuation rings of the Bézout domain `A` there, `a/b` has opposite strictly signed values. In particular the obstruction is on the puncture at **this H after inverting every old scalar**, not merely the countless ordinary prime ideals of `R` below a BCM bound.

Indeed `H>D` on positives, and for every `f=c/d>0` in `F` the pair `Hf,H/f` is integral with product `H²`. Reduce `t/H=a/b` to coprime positive terms by the row gcd: `t=ga,H=gb`; from `t|H²` obtain `a|gb²`, hence `a|g`. This gives the displayed expressions. If either reduced coordinate belonged to `D`, the other would be bounded by an old integer, hence also old. Neither is an `A`-unit: an inverse would make it divide an old nonzero integer after clearing denominators, hence be in `D`. Replacing the threshold by the floors in z82 `BCM_LDIV` §8 forces normalized units at coinitial `v_order(g−1)>0`; for the units in that proof the **same coprime opposite-sign calculation** applies, since they too are nonscalar, relatively bounded with bounded inverses, and come from divisors of this `H²`. **PROVED (rank must survive deletion of any one place).** Suppose now plain successors hold at every threshold for `H²`, in an integer part as in z82 §8. ## 8. Why a dilatation does not put a least divisor on the old axis

**PROVED (punctured constant contraction).** Fix the first all-old multiple `H` over end-initial `D`, `F=Frac D`, and `U=(D∖0)⁻¹R[1/H]`. (The strict inequality is required.) This hypothesis holds on the original twisted monomial axis (z78 `IE1_UNITS` §§1–3; z82 `BCM_LDIV` §2): a positive unit above 1 is either an old scalar `>1`, in which case its scalar midpoint with 1 gives such a normalized divisor, or exceeds all positive field scalars by the separation of distinct value monomials. This needs neither endness over the completed successor nor preservation of its old arithmetic divisor list.

It holds even on the infinite-rank example §4.1 for **all punctured units**, not only those originally normalizing divisors. Indeed a relatively bounded unit of residue one in its Laurent unit description has exponent zero at `H` (each angular variable is relatively a unit). In a product involving later angles compare at the **latest** such angle: after placing all earlier factors and its chosen lift in the old real closure, it has shape `a(1+ε)^m`, `m≠0` an ordinary integer. Its positive increment is larger than every initial infinitesimal. In either case some old `Hz_i` is strictly intermediate. Units of residue greater than one pose no difficulty. This argument uses the *first completed successor*, not the localization of the later `ω`-union at different common multiples.

This is not a prohibition of actual splitting edges that change the punctured unit group, nor a deduction of ES at the bad prime.

## 11. Depth-one tests, local shadows, and a positive closure theorem

**PROVED (all fixed-arity linear witness systems over the reserve).** In each host-sorted variant of `T_F,T_Π,T_pf,T_mix` the following large class of projected tests, **and their Boolean combinations**, admit unary least search on the entire cone with arbitrary ambient parameters. A basic test is

```
               ∃y∈T^k_{≥0}     A y = B(t),
```

where `A` is any fixed ordinary-size matrix with entries in the scalar reserve `C` (arbitrary *nonstandard* reserve coefficients allowed), `B(t)` a vector of polynomials with arbitrary arithmetic coefficients. Inequalities and strict inequalities linear in `y` with such coefficients go in by slack variables/discreteness; include polynomial witness bounds if an `E₁` test is wanted. Signed variables can be split. Finite disjunctions give general open matrices that are Boolean combinations of these linear conditions, under a positive existential block. This permits many coupled, unbounded-size witnesses, not just an affine divisibility with its unique cofactor. It does **not** allow coefficients of `y` to vary polynomially with `t`, nor products of two witnesses. This first assertion is an **instance-level** theorem, equivalently in the language with constants for `C`; it is not the unrestricted first-order scheme for a matrix parameter `A`. For standard numeral matrices alone, Presburger quantifier elimination in the additive reduct of any `IOpen` model reduces tuple solvability to fixed-modulus congruences and linear inequalities in the right sides. Substitution followed by the ordinary finite residue split and open least search suffices; that standard fragment is not a new tower result. Below we separately internalize a nonstandard range by an open Pell guard. No general depth-one closure is claimed.

1. **Short dependent columns.** For any witness, let `J` be its coordinates `y_j≥K` where `K∈C`, `K>1` exceeds the absolute values of all minors of `A` and 1. If the columns on `J` are dependent, select a minimally dependent subset. Cofactors of a rank minor give a nonzero integral relation supported there with entries bounded by `K` (zero column: coefficient 1). Take a sign with at least one entry positive. Subtract the relation times the least `⌊y_j/u_j⌋` on its positive coordinates. All coordinates remain nonnegative and at least one becomes `<K`; negative entries of the relation only increase coordinates already in `J`. The small coordinates did not change. Recompute `J` and repeat at most the **ordinary** column count. Obtain a witness whose large columns are independent. This is finite linear algebra over `Frac C`, with actual integer division in the arithmetic ring, not a nonstandard support-reduction algorithm.
2. **Reduction to bounded scalar corrections.** For each of finitely many possible independent subsets `I`, set all other witness entries to numbers `z_j<K`, now in `C`. Solve for the columns `I` by a nonzero minor, Cramer's rule; retain only integral nonnegative solutions satisfying the unused rows. It is unnecessary to require the solved entries ≥`K`. Each test is an equality, sign or divisibility by a fixed nonzero minor in `C` on an expression `P(t)+γ(z)` (clear signs/denominators), where `P` is arithmetic polynomial and the corrections are bounded uniformly by a `c∈C`. This includes `I=∅`. The box for `z` is absolute in the PA host. At congruences take a common period `D_per∈C⁺` for all denominators; residues of every fixed arithmetic coefficient mod `D_per` are scalar by initiality and division. Thus the congruences are PA tests of *arithmetic* `t mod D_per` and `z`; we do not compute mod `D_per` at an algebraic real in the order host.
3. **Constants, intervals and bounded strips.** There are finitely many nonconstant `P`. Over the arithmetic real closure take the real parts of their finitely many complex roots. Off the closed strips of width `c+2` to either side of these points, `|P(t)|>c` by its product factorization and arithmetic leading coefficient of absolute value ≥1. On each of the remaining finitely many intervals signs are fixed and the equalities cannot hold; all the correction dependence there is in congruences and comparisons involving only constant `P`. The latter are PA tests on the correction box (via the order host, as in §3). In each such interval nonemptiness of the Boolean test depends only on `t mod D_per` by a PA definition with a bounded index search. Start at the first nonnegative arithmetic point of the interval, if any, using its floor/endpoint. Inspect the first `D_per` points or stop at the interval end: use the starting arithmetic remainder mod `D_per`, the PA-coefficient congruences and order-host endpoint comparison on these old indices. If no feasible index and the entire period was included, no hit exists in the interval.
4. Each bounded strip has arithmetic integer start and length at most `2c+O(1)`; with overlaps one may process each strip separately. Floors here are taken at the finitely many **fixed** endpoints in the arithmetic ring after intersecting with the cone and any bounded search cutoff (one can always bound by a supplied initial hit). Test all indices from its start in the PA host: the polynomial comparisons including the corrections are semialgebraic on the scalar index box, with arithmetic parameters in the ordered real closure of the PA host; translate to PA by ordered real-closed elimination as in z81 §13. Congruences use the true starting arithmetic remainder. The host finds the least included index; initiality ensures it is old. Minimize across the finite pieces. This avoids assuming a PA function computing arithmetic floors at a *varying* list of endpoints. Finite Boolean combinations use the common refinement/period and quantification over finitely many absolute boxes. For instance, witnesses for a simultaneous system of knapsack equations with **nonstandard small coefficients** and quadratic right sides at a large input can be sorted, including complements; this is substantially larger than a single invertible affine filter. Since the argument applies to the whole ring, it gives the stated rule verdict even with outer parameters unrelated to `C` on the right sides.

* **Euclid/Hurwitz/binary Gauss and Pell.** Compressed gcd rows, the terminal reduction `[A,B,C']` with the column bounds audited above, Hurwitz from a supplied four-square multiplier `0<m<p`, and positive→negative Pell at a prime `1 mod 4` from a supplied nontrivial positive unit are `A` theorems (z79 §§4,9,12, z81 §1).
