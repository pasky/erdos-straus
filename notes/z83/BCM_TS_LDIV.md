## 1. A uniform no-minimum test, without prescribing any digits

**PROVED (rel. the following class hypotheses).** Let `B` be a signed model of true arithmetic, `H=2^L` for an infinite `L∈B`, `C={a∈B : |a|^j<H for every ordinary j≥1}`, and `R` an uncut discretely ordered domain extending `B`. Use the signed power cut `T` of `R` about `H`. Make the following hypotheses:

1. Every positive divisor in `R` of a positive cut scalar is a `B`-divisor. Gcds with a positive cut scalar exist in `R` in the Bézout (row) sense. In particular `R/pR` is a field at each such scalar prime: `p` is irreducible by divisor contraction, and Euclid cancellation and coprimary rows apply. Equivalently for this argument it suffices to have the specific gcds and prime ideals used below. Divisibility at a nonzero scalar is absolute on cut inputs by the order bound on the quotient.
2. There is a positive cut parameter `P` with a **nonzero scalar** root of `X²−P` mod each odd scalar prime `q∈C` (root in `B` with the congruence in `R`). At odd scalar primes `p>C` in the cut, `X²−P` has **no root in `R/pR`**. We only need these assertions on the sparse PA list below. `p>C` means larger than all of `C` on the positive cone; it is not `p>H`.

Then for a single positive squarefree scalar `h≤H^4`, the set of nonnegative `u` satisfying the one atom

```
            h² | (1+u) ((1+u)² − P)²                               (1)
```

is nonempty and has no least element. Thus even unary least search at **constant divisor and polynomial dividend**, with neither a negation nor an existential projection, fails. The same counterexample refutes a several-variable scheme including the unary one. Here `0|b` conventions are immaterial.

**PROVED (sparse modulus).** In `B` put `m=⌊log₂ L⌋`, `x_i=2^(2^i)`, `0≤i≤m`, and choose the first prime `p_i` in `(x_i,2x_i)` by Bertrand. They are distinct increasing odd primes. Form `h=∏p_i` **in PA**. We need three estimates, using only internally finite products:

* `∏_{i≤j} p_i ≤ 2^(j+1+2^(j+1)−1) ≤ x_j^3` for `0≤j≤m` (since `j≤2^j`). Thus `h≤H³` and the prefix up to **any** `q=p_j∈C` has product in `C`.
* `x_m²>H`, so the list goes outside `C` (still inside the power cut).
* The listed primes in `C` are cofinal there. For `c∈C`, `c≥2`, choose the first internal `i` with `x_i≥c`; its existence follows from the preceding size bound at `m`. Apart from `i=0`, `x_i=x_{i−1}²<c²`. Thus `c<p_i<2 max(2,c²)∈C`.

**PROVED (lower bound for *every* hit).** Write `t=1+u`. At each listed `p>C` the second factor is a unit modulo `p`, so `p²|t` from (1) (cancel twice at a prime). Let `b=gcd_R(t,h²)>0`; by contraction it is a single scalar divisor of `h²`. The set of indices with `p_i²∤b` is now a **PA-coded** set in `B`: both its inputs are scalars, and `p_i²|b` in `R` has the same meaning in `B` by divisor contraction. This set has no prime outside `C`. If nonempty, it has a *last* prime `q∈C` by PA applied to that internal set, not to the external cut. Therefore `c=h²/b` divides the square of the prefix ending at `q`, so `c∈C` (in the empty case `c=1`). Since `b|t`, each hit satisfies `t≥h²/c` for **some** positive `c∈C`, possibly depending on the hit.

**PROVED (a smaller hit).** Given this `c` take a listed `q∈C`, `q>c`. Write `M=(h/q)²`. Since `M` is invertible mod `q` in `B`, choose `1≤z<q` in `B` so `Mz` agrees mod `q` with the supplied nonzero scalar root of `P`. For `t'=Mz` we have `M|t'`, `q|(t'²−P)` in `R`; multiplying gives (1) at `u'=t'−1`. Moreover `0<t'<Mq=h²/q<h²/c≤t`. It is in the cut, as is the signed quotient in (1). There is a starting hit `t=h²`. This proves the theorem. The repeated factor matters: it costs at most `q` in the archimedean choice of `z` while recovering `q²` of required modulus. No need to code the square roots as a word or to choose residues at different primes independently.

## 2. Which valuation schedules does (1) kill?

**PROVED (rel. z81 §§3–5,7,12; z82 BCM_LDIV §§6–7; z82 FAREY_IE1 §§7–11).** Apply §1 in every z81 square-tested tower using its full-precision reserve `C`, scalar contents and its **unchanged generic residue fibres at never-entered primes**. In particular this includes the schedules `T_g` (plain and first-digit coordinated), `T_F`, and the joint variant below. Here are the verifications; nothing is inferred from the absence of a square root of `−1` modulo the horizontal prime `P`.

* In the upper algebra the only units are the scalar field `K*=Frac(B)*` (on a paired edge use the polynomial algebra in two variables). Thus any two factors of a scalar in the uncut ring are upper units, hence scalars, integral precisely in `B`. Rows in the union give the gcd; one can equally use its old scalar profile. The positive `P` is primitive and in the cut by the seed order. The seed uses `K[P]`, with its valuations restricted along `P=(1+LZ)²` on an **auxiliary field**; it does not contain `Z`. At `q∈C` the auxiliary primitive `1+LZ` is integral of value zero, and invariant 2 on the auxiliary line at modulus `q` represents it by a nonzero scalar residue. This proves the asserted square congruence in the seed, since testing `(P−r²)/q` uses only that valuation at `q` (no horizontal denominator).
* At `p>C` no member of `C` enters that place. Its auxiliary line is baseline Gauss. On the **restricted** seed the residue field is `k_p(S)`, `k_p=B/pB` and `S=(1+L Z̄)²` transcendental, because `p∤L`. In detail, normalize any polynomial in `P` by its scalar coefficient minimum; its reduction is nonzero on the transcendental `S`. Taking fractions gives exactly the asserted residue field. `S` is *not* a square there (order one at the formal variable's zero). Testing in `k_p(Z̄)` instead would give the opposite answer.
* At such a place each free, Dirichlet or unit-pivot row chart adjoins a residual indeterminate. Another square-tested free edge restricts to a **new transcendental** in its own auxiliary line; it does not adjoin an algebraic root of an old residue. A rank-four sphere adjoins the function field of a geometrically integral quadric over the old residue field: the baseline is multiplicative in monic forms, and the quadratic polynomial has rank ≥4 even after scalar closure and even at zero target, so cannot factor into two affine linears. After making one coefficient a unit, monic normal forms and their fractions give this function-field description. At `p>C` the characteristic of the PA residue field is zero externally (in particular not two). These extensions are regular, hence cannot make the old nonsquare a square. A proposed root in a directed union occurs at a finite stage. The arithmetic prime quotient maps **into** the final valuation residue field: if an integral element has positive value at `p`, division by `p` preserves all integrality conditions (`p` has least strictly positive value there by the scalar-profile invariant); `p` is an upper unit. Thus a root in the quotient is impossible. Conversely it would have sufficed here just to map the prime quotient unitally to that field.
* This reasoning uses the generic fibre, not the identities of the particular good digits. The z82 squarefree word construction represents the **same generic residue fields** injectively in the new auxiliary host at `p>C`; nonvanishing of every normalized test there is exactly its genericity guarantee. Such an embedding is not existential reflection on the image. Even codes at **all** prime powers, if added while keeping the above arithmetic fibres, cannot make (1) have a minimum. In fact no isomorphism of the large-prime residue field here onto an internal PA prime field can be justified by coding: the independent squareclasses of `S` and `S+1` survive every regular extension (use the two linear valuations in `k_p(S)`); both occur already in the arithmetic quotient as `P,P+1`. An internal odd PA prime field has only two nonzero squareclasses. This is not a contradiction to a nonsurjective embedding.

**Assessment (invariant and limit).** (1) detects a change in *existence of arithmetic roots* across an external subpower boundary while the missing part of a scalar gcd always has internal support. It is not a matching whose unprotected next digit the constructor could choose differently. Changing the actual large-prime residue extensions (for example adjoining quadratic points there), or abandoning scalar divisor contraction, is outside the no-go. A full system of host codes **for arithmetic residues** is not by itself a system of lifts *into the arithmetic ring*. We do not refute every imaginable PA-host architecture; nor does the invariant force a divisor class `−1` mod a moving level at `P`. The positive/negative square test concerns scalar primes dividing `h`, not divisors of `4kP+1`.

## 4. The BCM successor: a test without prescribed monomials

**PROVED (relative to discrete order and Bézout).** Let `D` be a proper end-initial signed subring of `R`, `H>0` divisible in `R` by every `d∈D⁺`. Put `F=Frac D`, `A=(D∖0)⁻¹R`, `U=A[1/H]`. Use the convex hull `O` of `F` in the ordered real closure. Then `A∩O=F` (clear the one old denominator; initiality). Suppose merely that `H²` has a least positive divisor `t>H`. Then there are **two coprime positive divisors `a,b` of `H`, both outside `D`**, with `H=lab`, `t=la²` for `l∈R⁺` and `a/b>1` of residue one for this order valuation. In `A` there are distinct maximal ideals over `H`, one containing `a`, the other `b`. In the local valuation rings of the Bézout domain `A` there, `a/b` has opposite strictly signed values. In particular the obstruction is on the puncture at **this H after inverting every old scalar**, not merely the countless ordinary prime ideals of `R` below a BCM bound.

Indeed `H>D` on positives, and for every `f=c/d>0` in `F` the pair `Hf,H/f` is integral with product `H²`. Thus `1<t/H≤1+1/d` for all `d∈D⁺`, by the leastness hypothesis. `t/H` cannot be in `F` by the midpoint between 1 and itself. Reduce `t/H=a/b` to coprime positive terms by the row gcd: `t=ga,H=gb`; from `t|H²` obtain `a|gb²`, hence `a|g`. This gives the displayed expressions. If either reduced coordinate belonged to `D`, the other would be bounded by an old integer, hence also old. Neither is an `A`-unit: an inverse would make it divide an old nonzero integer after clearing denominators, hence be in `D`. The coprimality row persists in `A`, proving the maximal-ideal and sign assertions. Replacing the threshold by the floors in z82 `BCM_LDIV` §8 forces normalized units at coinitial `v_order(g−1)>0`; for the units in that proof the **same coprime opposite-sign calculation** applies, since they too are nonscalar, relatively bounded with bounded inverses, and come from divisors of this `H²`. No assumptions on total rational rank.

**PROVED (single-valuation no-go).** More generally suppose there is an upper ring `A₀⊇A` with `A₀∩O=F`, a valuation `w` of the ambient field (arbitrary ordered value group), and that for **units `z` of `U`** of nonnegative `w`-value we have `z∈A₀`. For example `A₀=A₀[1/H]∩{w≥0}` with `A⊆A₀`. Then there is no least divisor of `H²` above `H`, even without Bézout: `g=t/H∈U*` for any such alleged successor and one of `g,g⁻¹` has nonnegative `w` and is in `O`; it must be scalar, contrary to the midpoint argument. This kills single-landing infinite-rank twists with that intersection and hull, **even if one has not calculated a single punctured unit or the relation between the order and `w`**. Adjoining a principal unit of the required size while preserving the single-valuation and hull axioms is impossible already before checking Lag, TS, QR or specialization. An end extension after a completed failed successor cannot help: a smaller positive divisor and cofactor of `H²` would both be old. If a proposed pre-completion repair uses incomparable valuation vectors, the sign calculation is necessary but not sufficient; we do not exclude it. The true BCM axiom here is `∀x ∃H>0 ∀d (0<d≤x → d|H)`; a bound at any `x>D` implies the all-old hypothesis, but mere divisibility by all ordinary integers at one stage is not global BCM.

**PROVED (one Hahn test, including BCM).** In the canonical full truncation `R=ℤ+k((t^Γ))_{<0}` take `k=ℝ`, nonzero divisible ordered group `Γ`, ordered at positive infinitesimal monomials `t^γ` for `γ>0`. Here `<0` means *all support exponents strictly negative*, not merely negative leading valuation. It is an integer part of the real closed Hahn field: truncate the negative exponents, then take the integer floor of the constant coefficient, lowering it by one if it is exact and the omitted tail is negative. The convex hull of constants intersects the truncation in `ℤ`. Nevertheless `Frac R` contains `√2` as `(√2 t^{-γ})/t^{-γ}`. A Bézout domain is integrally closed, so it would have to contain `√2`, impossible in a discretely ordered ring. This also kills any twisted-section version allowing both coefficient multiples of the same scale with that ratio; it is not an assertion about every integer part of a Hahn field.

When `Γ` has no largest archimedean class, the canonical truncation actually has **global BCM**. For a cutoff whose least support exponent is `−γ≤0`, every positive divisor-candidate `d` up to it has all exponents in the convex subgroup generated by `γ` (for a finite cutoff all are ordinary). Its reciprocal belongs to the Hahn subfield on that subgroup, so shifting by `−δ`, `δ>nγ` for every ordinary `n`, puts its *entire* support below 0. Thus `t^{-δ}/d∈R` for every such `d` simultaneously. No such BCM conclusion follows from infinite rational rank alone. In this example the missing Bézout axiom is decisive regardless of TS or a choice of `P`; no ES-countermodel package is claimed.

**PROVED / CONDITIONAL on host ES (ultrapower and actual PA subrings).** A convex cut containing `P` in an elementary extension of the pointed z78 model has the bounded gap from z82 §2 below any alleged BCM bound at `P`. In a PA integer host actually satisfying `ES(P)`, any convex subring containing `P` retains a sorted positive triple (`x≤3P/4, y≤2Px, z≤Pxy`). It is enough that the subring contain every host integer `1≤j≤Q=⌊√P⌋` for infinite `P`: fixed-length radix expansion in base `Q` covers the polynomial bounds. Hence a nonconvex subring omitting the witnesses must omit such a digit, and must miss an entry of every host triple. The hypothesis is automatic for a **true-arithmetic** host if ES is true in ℕ, not for an arbitrary PA completion merely from truth in ℕ. Conversely a nonconvex subring of an ultrapower of the z76 external PID cannot use ambient **full unary minimum Skolem functions** to inherit minimization and thereby repair BCM. In that pointed host every `b>0` has a `0<d≤P` with `d∤b` (PID finite support in the precursor). The least such `d` is a unary Boolean divisibility minimum; if retained in the subring it still does not divide `b`. This last argument does not concern simply satisfying the internal minimum axioms, or closure only under positive divisor-successor functions. No unrestricted impossibility for nonconvex subrings, or construction preserving TS, BCM and plain successors together at a bad prime, follows.
