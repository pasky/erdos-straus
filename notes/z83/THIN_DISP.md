The analogous inverse-φ main-term tails satisfy the same estimate.

Fouvry–Tenenbaum, *Trans. AMS* 375 (2022), Thm 1.8, Def 1.1, gives, for each A and `f∈F(840,2)`, `∑_{v≤√y/log^{B(A,2)}y} max_{(a,840v)=1}|Δ_f(y;v,840,a)|≪_A y/log^A y`. Use `f=Bψ`, ψ a character mod 210: prime values constant on classes mod 840 in **every** dyadic band, `|f|≤1≤τ₂`; at `v=dcn` coprime to 840 (1.16) is the ordinary discrepancy against the coprime mean. Any unit class at v lifts to a unit at 840. Project back to the fibre by the fixed characters. The level fits because `log(c)/L=o(1)`, `n≤X^{2ν}`, `d≤X^τ`. Divisor multiplicity costs are absorbed by Cauchy and the trivial `X/φ(v)` bound. Endpoints y are common; partial summation for `h(P)` and `h(P)log(P/m)` costs `O(L)` using total variation on I (split off `log m`). Thus the aggregate absolute error is `O_A(X/L^A)`. This is the AP replacement, not yet (6).

Write `a_ℓ=(ℓ−1)/(ℓ−2)` at `7<ℓ≤z`, and 1 elsewhere. `A_d=∏_{ℓ|d}a_ℓ`; `F_z(u)=B(u)∏_{ℓ|u}a_ℓ`. The resulting formula, with errors `E_d` having `∑_d|E_d|≪_A X/L^A` including d=1, is

    I_d=H_z A_d/φ(d) ∑_{u≡1(210),(u,d)=1} F_z(u)h(4u−3) + E_d,
    H_z=∏_{ℓ≤z}(1−1/ℓ)^{-1} ∏_{7<ℓ≤z} (1−1/(ℓ−1)).                          (7)

Indeed the reduced mean has divisor `φ(d)φ(n)φ(c)`, and unit condition `(u,dnc)=1`. Drop `(n,u)=1`, `(n,d)=1`, replace φ(n) by n: all n are z-rough and their total reciprocal prime mass is `≪L/z`. In absolute harmonic sums the bad u at `ℓ|n` cost `≪X/ℓ` before dividing by `φ(d)φ(n)`; for the d test sum `∑_{n≤U²:ℓ|n}1/n≪ L/ℓ` for `ℓ|d,ℓ>z`. All other absolute harmonic sums and Vaughan coefficients cost only log powers. The c-sum now gives `∏_{7<ℓ≤z,ℓ∤du}(1−1/(ℓ−1))`; `(u,d)=1` factors the corrections as in (7). The remaining scalar rough Möbius moments at `U=X^ν` are exactly JLL v2 (3.7) (rel. unrefereed JLL method): the 0-th is `O_A(L^{-A})`, log moment `−∏_{ℓ≤z}(1−1/ℓ)^{-1}+O_A(L^{-A})`. The proof there only uses `log U=ν L`: convolve μ with the z-smooth indicator; at smooth divisors `v≤√U` use the ordinary Möbius PNT moments (Montgomery–Vaughan I (2007), (6.18) and Exercise 16(a) as cited at JLL (3.7)), and above √U the Rankin tail is `≪exp(−νL/(2log z)+O(log L))`. T₂ is negligible since its scalar factor contains the 0-th moment. This proves (7); `H_z≍1` by its convergent ordinary-prime correction.

**One lower-order term must be retained.** Let `b_d(s)` be the squarefree Euler multiplier with local factors, only at split primes >7:

    ℓ∤d:  1+(a_ℓ−1)ℓ^{-s};            ℓ|d:  1−ℓ^{-s}.                         (8)

It converts the split-only series prime to 210 to that of the restricted `F_z` sum in (7), before projection. For a character ψ insert ψ(ℓ) at every degree-one coefficient. Fixed-modulus Selberg–Delange (Tenenbaum, *Introduction to Analytic and Probabilistic Number Theory*, 3rd ed., 2015, Thm II.5.2) for the *base* `1_{(u,210)=1}B(u)ψ(u)` gives a two-term cumulative expansion `y[c₀(log y)^{-1/2}+c₁(log y)^{-3/2}]+O(y(log y)^{-5/2})` for principal ψ and ψ=χ inflated to 210; the two agree identically. For all other ψ the two coefficients vanish. For the literal analytic hypotheses: factor the Dirichlet series as `[L(s,ψ)L(s,ψχ)]^{1/2}` times an Euler product absolutely convergent/bounded on `Re s≥3/4` (the linear terms match outside 210). At the two polar twists exactly one L is principal, exponent at ζ=1/2; otherwise the exponent is 0 and `1/Γ(−j)=0`. After extracting this ζ power, fixed-conductor L-functions have zero-free logarithmic region, taking its constant smaller to exclude any fixed exceptional zero, with at most log-power growth there (equivalently polynomial growth exponent <1, narrowing the region). Majorize coefficients by the principal series of the same type. These are the analytic-continuation, growth and majorant hypotheses of the theorem; only two fixed Taylor terms are used. No assertion of uniform Selberg–Delange with conductor d is needed.

Convolving with (8) gives, for the two common interval endpoints and with the bounded variation weight h, constants/functions `J₀=O(X/L^{1/2}), J₁=O(X/L^{3/2})` **independent of d**, such that

    I_d = H_z A_d/φ(d) [J₀ b_d(1)+J₁ b′_d(1)] + e_d,
    ∑_{d|P₇,d≤D}|e_d| = O(X/L²).                                             (9)

Here J₀ includes the ordinary second term; factors 2/φ(210) are included in the J's. To check the aggregate error, let `b_d^+(v)` be the absolute convolution coefficients. Uniformly `∑_{d|P₇,d≤D} A_d/φ(d)∑_v b_d^+(v)/v^{3/4}≪√L`: factor the nonnegative sum into local factors; omitting the cutoff D only enlarges it. The unrestricted unpinned correction has product `∏(1+O(ℓ^{-7/4}))`; a pin adds `a_ℓ/(ℓ−1)·(1+O(ℓ^{-3/4}))` at a 7-inert prime, whose product of `1+1/ℓ+O(ℓ^{-7/4})` is `≪√L` by fixed-AP Mertens. This also covers the nonpolar character twists. In the convolution at cumulative endpoint y discard `v>√y` at power cost using this bound and `B≤1`. Below √y expand `(log(y/v))^{-1/2}`, `(log(y/v))^{-3/2}` through linear and constant degree respectively, error `O((1+log² v)(log y)^{-5/2})` when evaluated in units `y/v`. The linear term sums to `−b′_d(1)` with its constant absorbed into J₁. Thus the accumulated remainder is `O(X/L²)`, also after integrating the bounded variation on y≍X. For d=1 alone its size is `O(X/L^{5/2})`, sufficient when multiplying by `∑g(d)`. This expansion is why a leading-term `1+o(1)` error at fixed d would not suffice.

Put `g_z(d)=A_d b_d(1)/(φ(d)b_1(1))`. Direct multiplication gives `g_z(ℓ)=g(ℓ)` at ℓ≤z; above z it differs by relative `O(1/ℓ)`, whence `g_z(d)/g(d)=1+O(L/z)` uniformly. The logarithmic derivative difference `b′_d/b_d−b′_1/b_1` at 1 is additive on split primes of d, with summands `k_z(ℓ)=O(log ℓ/ℓ)` by (8), zero otherwise. After subtracting the common J₀ and J₁ terms, the remaining contribution in (6), apart from `o(X/L)`, is bounded by `O(X/L^{3/2})` times

    |∑_{ℓ} k_z(ℓ) ∑_{ℓ|d} λ_d g(d)| .                                        (10)

In fact (10) is `o(√L)`; the absolute value encloses the signed double sum. For ℓ>H₀ the absolute tail is `≪√L ∑_{ℓ>H₀} log ℓ/ℓ²`, by discarding signs and using the product of `1+g(ℓ)` over sieve primes. For the other primes take independent Bernoulli digits `Z_ℓ∈{0,1}` with means g on the sieve primes, and `W=∑ λ_d ∏_{ℓ|d}Z_ℓ`. The pointwise lower-sieve inequality gives `W_+≤1_{no pins}`; the lower main-sum estimate at s=τ/ζ gives `EW≥0` eventually by FI 2010 Thm 11.13 (same product hypothesis as in §3). Thus `E|W|=2EW_+−EW≤2`.

The exact pin identity is `∑_{d:ℓ|d} λ_d g(d) = E[W (Z_ℓ−g(ℓ))]/(1−g(ℓ))`, **not** `E[W Z_ℓ]`: the subtraction cancels all monomials not containing that pin. Its right side is bounded uniformly since `g(ℓ)≤1/9`. This directly bounds each small-prime inner sum. Take X→∞ first, then H₀→∞, to get the little-oh. No probabilistic model of actual primes was assumed; this is an identity for the sieve polynomial. Combining (7)–(10) proves (6). ∎
