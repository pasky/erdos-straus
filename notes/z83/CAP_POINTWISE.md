# Capped packets: simultaneous sieve bands and simultaneous carrier obstructions

**Assessment (scope and notation).** The cap bounds the largest prime valuation of the reduced square root, not its number of primes. It also requires its radical to divide the integral coordinate `y`, and coprimality to `2n`. A new prime freely supplied by an AP theorem need not be in this radical or in the support of a legal correction. We distinguish (i) the chart for the particular input, (ii) the union of input classes used for a sieve, and (iii) the majorant of the *whole avoider* of those classes. A bound in the opposite direction on (iii) does not bound the true failures from below. Hereafter `U` in a pointwise bound is a scale, while “z82 U” denotes a theorem. A neutral window means the `δ_p=0` branch of CAP_DOWN Lemma 3.1 (as in z81 T): the correction to `K` uses no signed power of the input prime `p`. In a nonneutral window that exponent is `±1`.

## 1. A multiscale comparison with no factor for the number of bands

**Lemma W (PROVED, elementary; finite, effective).** Let independent finite coordinates have uniform laws and forbidden lists of probabilities `p_i<1`. Give coordinate `i` cost `s_i≥0`. A real function `ν≥0` is a sum of terms, each depending on coordinates of total cost at most `H`. Require `ν≥1` whenever all lists are avoided. For *any* `σ>0`,

    log(1/Eν) ≤ C σH + e Σ_i exp(−σ s_i) p_i/(1−p_i).               (1)

There is no separation, geometric spacing, low arity or sign condition on the terms. The forbidden lists in this lemma are on **independent coordinates**, not arbitrary composite tests.

*Proof.* Replace independently the law at `i` by its mixture with its law conditioned on avoiding its list, using mixing weight `z^{floor(σs_i)}`, `0≤z≤1` (`z^0=1` even at zero). The new mean `P(z)` of `ν` is a real polynomial of degree at most `floor(σH)`: each marginal is affine in its own mixing weight. At 1 it is ≥1. At `0≤z≤1/e` the density with respect to the original product law is at most `exp(e Σ_i exp(−σs_i)p_i/(1−p_i))`, by multiplication of pointwise density bounds. Nonnegativity of the **function**, not its coefficients, therefore bounds `|P(z)|` by this factor times `Eν`. For degree `d≥1` interpolate at `j/(ed)`, `0≤j≤d`: the sum of absolute Lagrange coefficients at 1 is at most `(ed)^d 2^d/d! ≤ (2e²)^d`. Degree zero needs no factor. This proves (1). Unlike paying a separate `d~H/log ℓ` in each band, the *same* monomial has degree at most `σH` across their union. ∎

**Theorem M (PROVED rel. W, z82 UB's Euler bounds and U for attainment; comparison effective up to absolute constants, effectivity of attainment not asserted).** Put `L=log N`, `λ=log L`, `B=E+3`, `1≤E≤λ²` an integer. Use any finite subfamilies of the full z82 unit dictionary `C_E(f)` (`f=4A−1`, inputs `−4D mod f`). Use a partition of the primes appearing in the family into cofactor primes and pivots, the latter at **any** scales. Every family modulus is `f=kℓ`, with `ℓ` a pivot prime and `k` composed only of cofactor primes; for a fixed `β≥1`, require `f≤ℓ^β`. Thus `f` has exactly one pivot. Cofactors can be shared, and can have any number of prime powers; alternatively every `f` can be coprime to every other. Allow selectors `0 mod q` on any nonpivot primes, including additional primes not dividing a cofactor. On every unit cofactor fibre require the active target union at each pivot to occupy at most a fixed fraction `1−δ` of its digits (`δ>0` fixed). Assume family primes ≤`N^a` for fixed `a`.     log(1/E_Haar ν) ≪_{a,a',β,W₀,δ} L exp[−λ/(E+4)] / log(2B).       (2)

With the additional label height `D≤ℓ^β` the RHS divides by `B`. For `λ≤E≤λ²` the untruncated bound is `O(L[1+log(E/λ)]/log(2E))`. In particular at `E=λ^{1+o(1)}` the saving is `o(L)`. *Proof.* Restrict all cofactor and selector digits to units at log cost `O_a(log(2L))` by Mertens. Pin them. Average away irrelevant digits; the resulting nonnegative function majorises the entire active-list void. Apply W with `s_ℓ=log ℓ` at charged pivots, zero at the others. ## 2. A simultaneous, signed, prescribed-carrier obstruction

**Lemma S (PROVED, elementary, effective).** In a neutral window of a hard prime `p` let `4K=p+m`, `(K,mp)=1`, and let `u/v` be the positive rational represented by the signed prime powers on `K` (`(u,v)=1`, `m|u+v`). At cap `E`, a prime of signed exponent of magnitude `j` pays at least `max(1,j−2E)` powers into `K`. This is z81 T's necessary condition, even if we interchange `u` and `v` for the following **height bound only**. Write `S=rad(uv)`, `W₁=S^{2E}`. Then

    uv ≤ (p+W₁+1) W₁².                                            (4)

Indeed the product `C` of all paid powers divides `K` and `uv/C≤W₁`. If `C≤p` this suffices. Otherwise `m=4K−p>3C`. Orient `u≥v`, put `b₁=(u+v)/m`; then `b₁v≤2uv/(3C)≤W₁`. The paid part `C_u` supported on `u` divides `p b₁+v` since `C|p+m`; also `u/C_u≤W₁`. Thus `uv≤W₁(p b₁v+v²)≤(p+W₁)W₁²`, as claimed. No GRH or coprimality of the unselected factors of `K` to the menu is used.

**Theorem S (PROVED rel. effective Linnik, classical upper divisor bound; effective asymptotic).** There is an absolute small `c>0` such that the following holds at all sufficiently large `Y`. Prescribe `k≥1` distinct primes in `[Y,2Y]` and integer `E≥1` with `max(E,k)≤c log log Y`. There is a prime `p≡1 (840)`, `p≤Y^{O(k)}`, with **every one** of the prescribed primes a nonresidue at `p`, but *no* neutral cap-`E` certificate at *any* window whose entire signed support is contained in this menu. The signs and the nonzero exponents and window factorization can all be chosen after seeing `p`. Other unused primes in `K` are unrestricted. This extends z81's prescribed-singleton obstruction in a different direction than its polylog support theorem T: all generators have the correct quadratic sign available, and one target is actually missed. It is not a lower bound on `e*(p)` when the menu varies with `p`, when additional integral generators may participate, or in a nonneutral window. Shapes excluded include root 1 and any composite root ≤E in local valuations **only on this support-restricted neutral branch**.

*Proof.* Choose independently a uniform nonresidue mod each prescribed `s`. Together with `1 mod 840` these are reduced classes, realised by effective Linnik with `p≤Y^{A k}` at absolute `A`; reciprocity gives the claimed negative signs. Uniformly for every such possible `p`, (4) implies `log(uv)≤C₁(k+Et) log Y` for support size `t≥1`, and all signed magnitudes are ≤`C₁(k+Et)`. Make a list of all these vectors *before* sampling the classes (truncate also by this product bound). Ignore the empty vector, since `m≡3 mod 4` cannot divide 2. For each vector at most `τ(u+v)` windows need consideration. By the standard divisor bound and monotonicity of its majorant for large arguments, `log τ(u+v)≤C₂(k+Et) log Y / log log Y`. Pick `c` small enough that this is ≤`t(log Y)/3` simultaneously for every `t≥1`. The number of vectors is at most `(1+2C₁(k+Ek))^k=Y^{o(1)}`. A hit needs `p≡−m mod s` at **every** prime in the nonzero support. Independence of the sampled CRT digits gives probability at most `(2/(Y−1))^t` for each window. The union probability is `O(Y^{-2/3+o(1)})`. Hence some choice avoids all, also after taking its Linnik representative. The construction may additionally impose `p≡1` at all primes below `log Y` at the same `Y^{O(k)}` height, by Chebyshev, if a growing least nonresidue is wanted. ∎

**PROVED (group-theoretic diagnostic).** In a cyclic quotient of order `M`, choose `t` copies of its generator. Ordinary generation holds, but with coefficients in `0,…,E` the reachable set is just exponents `0,…,Et` (mod `M`); even the order-two target misses if `M` is even and `Et<M/2`. Distinct primes can occupy the same class. Conversely even `Et≥D(G)` with generation does not suffice in general: take in `C_2×C_4` many copies of `(1,0)` and just one `(0,1)`, `E=1`. Then `(0,2)` is absent however long the sequence. A zero-sum theorem is not a covering theorem for a specified target. The arithmetic issue is stronger: all used primes must divide the **same** `K` with sufficient paid powers. A large sieve for residues of primes not satisfying this divisibility is not a source of legal digits.

## 5. Round 2: exact fixed-denominator classification and two sorts of support

**Proposition B (PROVED, elementary, effective; includes nonneutral and nonunit windows).** Fix any integral first coordinate `x>p/4`, and reduce `4/p−1/x=a/B` to lowest terms (`a=(4x−p)/g, B=px/g, g=gcd(4x−p,px)`). For a positive integral second coordinate and a positive rational third coordinate the exact cap test is

    ∏_{t|B} t^{d_t} ≡ −1 (mod a),       b_t=v_t(B).

The basic range is `−b_t≤d_t≤b_t` at every prime. Additionally, **only** for `t∤2p` allow `d_t=b_t+2i`, `1≤i≤E`. At modulus 1 use the vacuous congruence. This is a tied *enlarged box*; in particular the negative end is not enlarged. Put `D=B∏t^{d_t}`. The bijection is `D=ay−B>0`, `y=(B+D)/a`, `z=By/D`. If `t|D` but `t∤B`, it cannot divide `y`, so its denominator debt is forbidden. At `t|B`, the denominator exponent of `z` is `max(0,v_t(D)−2b_t)` (check separately below, at, and above `b_t`); an overflowing prime divides `y`, and, since `t∤2p`, also `x`. This proves both directions. It is the **radical**, not necessarily the whole root, that divides `x`. At `p∤x`, `g=1`, so this is the full CAP_DOWN §3 test including `d_p=0,±1`. When `p|x` the reduction by `g` matters. `S` containing the entire signed support and `S` containing only the reduced root are different hypotheses. A quadratic nonresidue in the former at neutral sign is necessary by reciprocity; in the latter it is **not**: z81 S at `p=2521` has root 3 and `(3/p)=+1`. Even `e=0` need not have a nonresidue in the empty root. These distinctions prevent a purported lower bound for all single-prime roots from just counting divisors of `q^h+1`.

**Assessment (what main term is being modelled?).** B is exact, not an equidistribution theorem. For example, in the coprime integral-size range `k=4x−p≤p`, the multiplicity-weighted independent-unit model gives a weight `1/φ(k)` per word, not `1/k` per distinct residue. The usual random-integer Euler mean for these word counts grows as `(log p)^{E+2}`: the local mean has first term `1+(E+2)/t+O_E(1/t²)`. Summing a harmonic window range would give `c_model(E)=E+3`, giving power 3 at `E=0`; compare the cubic genus mass of the full Type II family with the quadratic mass of the restricted `(★)` line (`Z57_BFII.md` §4.3). This is a *heuristic* on that truncation, ignoring correlations; not even an assertion that the distinct-residue hit count has that power. Among other problems, the typical and mean order of the multiplicative weight differ. Cap certificates may lie outside this cutoff. No true pointwise asymptotic or canonical exponent is inferred.

**Assessment (weakest signed threshold).** For any declared finite search of legal words let `C_E(p)` count its hits, `M>0` a comparison mass. Requiring `C_E(p)−M>−M` uniformly is the sharp signed sufficient threshold, exactly positivity in this search; by itself it has no independent analytic content. An analogue `H⁻_E(η): C_E(p)≥ηM` with fixed `η>0` is stronger, while an absolute discrepancy `O(M^{1−δ})` suffices when `M→∞` already at cap zero. The model's larger exponent is not a theorem relaxing the required relative lower tail. `H⁻_BM` (BM_CORRECTED_COUNT §3.2) implies a cap-zero hit at every prime in its scope and hence positivity in any larger search including its witnesses, not necessarily a positive proportion of a larger cap-E mass. The reverse and a standard AP conjecture implying the quantitative lower tail are not established.

## 7. A budgeted adaptive window with two auxiliary primes

**Theorem T (PROVED rel. effective Burgess, Lenstra–Pomerance Lemma 11.2, BT/PNT, effective Linnik and z81 P; pointwise).** Write `α=1/(4√e)` (Euler's number here). For a hard prime `p` of least nonresidue `q`, for every fixed `ε>0` one can achieve *simultaneously*

*The two-prime arithmetic.* At `q≡1 (4)` put `y=q^{α+η}` with a sufficiently small fixed `η>0`. Choose `j≥0` so `(K/b₁)b₂^{2j}` projects to `−1` there. At `s≡3 (4)` it already projects to `−1`. Raising to the odd part of `λ(qs)` therefore gives **exactly** `−1` in both full unit groups. ## 8. Least-q versus sparse adaptive neutral words

**Theorem N (PROVED rel. Lemma S, reciprocity, BT, fixed-modulus PNT, classical divisor bound; method-only).** Fix `A₀≥1`. There is a small constant `c=c(A₀)>0` such that, for every sufficiently large odd prime `q`, integers `t,E≥1` with `(E+1)t≤c log log q`, there exist arbitrarily large primes `p≡1 (840)` of **least nonresidue exactly `q`** at which no neutral word uses at most `t` distinct primes, all `≤q^{A₀}`, at cap `E`, at **any** coprime window. The entire signed support is charged, not just the (possibly empty) root. This permits querying the actual factors at all windows before choosing the menu. For bounded sparsity this gives an `Ω(log log q)` obstructed cap scale in that method. Unlike the single-horizontal-divisor count it also permits arbitrarily high planted valuations. It is not a lower bound on the atlas-cover minimum without sparsity restrictions, on `e*`, or on the true exceptions. Proof asymptotic; no numerical onset for this barrier asserted.

*Proof.* Put `Y=q^{A₀}`. In `(X,2X]` take the prime profile of units `1 mod 840`, positive symbols at all odd primes `≤Y` except the negative at `q`. Treat `q,Y,t,E` as fixed for the limit `X→∞` **before** bounding its relative upper density uniformly in large `q`. Its fixed modulus `M` is the lcm of 840 and the odd primes ≤Y. It has positive relative prime density by PNT in progressions, and the desired least nonresidue. The neutral Jacobi sign (CAP_BOUNDED 1.1–1.2) forces `q` into the signed support. For a vector on support `S`, let `G=uv` and `C=∏_{s∈S}s^{max(1,|j_s|−2E)}` be its paid modulus. A success has `C|K`, `m|u+v`, `G/C≤Y^{2Et}`. At fixed vector, for each possible `m` the hit density relative to the profile is at most `O(2^{|S|}/φ(C))`: at an odd prescribed sign coordinate the fraction surviving one reduced class on its tower is at most `2/φ(s^a)` (the further `1 mod 840` constraints at 2,3,5,7 cost only an absolute factor). Incompatible classes cost zero. This bound also holds up to an absolute multiplicative constant **uniformly at `C≤X^{1/3}`** for `X` sufficiently large depending on the fixed profile: count the reduced lifts with BT at `[M,C]≤X^{1/2}` and use the fixed-modulus PNT lower bound in the denominator. At 2 it suffices to use the weaker necessary condition `p≡−m mod C` rather than requiring `4C|p+m`.

For `C>X^{1/3}`, (4) implies `G≪_{Y,E,t}X`. There are only `O_{Y,E,t}((1+log X)^t)` signed vectors and `X^{o(1)}` divisors per vector of `u+v≤2G`. Counting all integers in each required class gives `O(X^{2/3}+1)` per window, hence negligible even relative to the fixed prime profile. This step is essential: upper density alone does not commute with a countable union.

For the smaller `C`, the effective divisor bound gives `τ(u+v)≪G^δ` with `δ=C₀/log log q` and absolute `C₀`, since `G≥q` and `u+v≥√G`. Sum the relative bound for the vectors, overcounting with ordered supports, at `1≤j=|S|≤t`; distinguish `q` in one position. Use `1/φ(C)≤2^j/C`. For a given prime `s`, the sum over nonzero signed exponents of `s^{−(1−δ)max(1,|j_s|−2E)}` is `≤C₃(E+1)s^{-1+δ}`, uniformly once `δ<1/2`, by the geometric tail. Since `G^δ≤C^δ Y^{2Etδ}`, we obtain

    ≪ Y^{2Etδ} Σ_{j≤t} j [C₄(E+1)]^j q^{-1+δ} (Y^δ Σ_{s≤Y prime} 1/s)^{j−1}.

All polynomial/log factors here other than the powers of `q,Y` cost `q^{o(1)}` uniformly: `t,E≪log log q`. The total is `≤q^{-1+O(A₀(E+1)t/log log q)+o(1)}`, so less than half for sufficiently small `c`. This proves N. ∎
