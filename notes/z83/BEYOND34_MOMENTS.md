# Bounded gaps: count the last digit, not the words ending there

## 1. The model result

**Theorem V (PROVED rel. §§2–5).** Write $L=\log X$, $l=\log L$. For $K\ge3$ take the following classes at every odd $q\le K$, $q=3\pmod4$. Use *any finite collection* of labels $a,b\ge1$, $(a,b)=(ab,q)=1$, with no size or prime-factor restrictions. A class asks for $n=-q\pmod{4ab}$ and one of

1. $a+b=0\pmod q$ (a condition on the label only);
2. $na+b=0\pmod q$ (interchanging $a,b$ is permitted).

Call their joint avoider set $V$. Fix $A_0>0$. On any common CRT period containing these moduli, 8, and all integers $Q\le X^{A_0}$ as divisors (arbitrary further prime powers are allowed), there is a probability on **units with $n=1\pmod4$** agreeing *exactly* with uniform units on that fibre on every such $Q$, and assigning mass at least $\exp(-C W)$ to $V$. Here are choices of $W$:

| $K$ | $W$ |
|---|---|
| $K=\exp B$, $B\to\infty$, $B\le L^{1/4}$ | $L^{2/3}B^{1/3}$ |
| arbitrary $K$ | $L^{3/4}$ |
| $L^\beta$, $b_0<\beta<\log 3$, $b_0=\log3/(1+\log3)$ | $L^{1-u}/l^{1/(2z)}$, where $z=3\mathrm e\,3^{-1/\beta}$, $u=\beta/(z\log3)$ |
| $L^{\log3}$ | $L^{2/3}l^{1/6}$ |

Constants and onsets can depend on $A_0$ and the **fixed** $\beta$ in the third row, not on the family/period. In the first row one can take absolute constants for the supply estimates for all sufficiently large $B$. In particular the first row gives $L^{2/3}l^{1/3}$ at fixed $K=L^\beta$, $\beta>\log3$. At $K=L$ one has $z=\mathrm e$, $u=1/(\mathrm e\log3)$. This is *one law* for all those marginals. It is not Haar in general, and is not supported on the actual dyad. The same floor holds for the Haar mean of every nonnegative polynomial-level majorant of the **whole** void, even one with signed coefficients.

**Interpretation (PROVED rel. V and z82 G/G′).** On this chart a whole-avoider bound using polynomial-level marginals alone cannot improve $(2+\alpha)/3$ at $B=L^\alpha$, $0<\alpha<1/4$, reach a fixed positive multiple of $L^{3/4}$ at $B=o(L^{1/4})$, or beat z82's saving even by an unbounded factor at $K=L,L^{\log3},L^\beta$ ($\beta>\log3$ fixed). These are sharp *method rates*, comparing V with the bounded-level certificates of z82: in G's independent-digit step the probability $1/r$ at a private prime is replaced on units by $1/(r-1)$, and in the independent-bit comparison its odds $1/(r-J)$ by $1/(r-1-J)$ for $J$ windows. The error in the reciprocal mass and the logarithm of the no-mark factor is $O(J/R)$, negligible there. There is no nonunit small digit to repair. G′'s small physical pin has log period $o(L)$ (only radicals are used). In both arguments the even binomial polynomial for $2^{-S}$ majorizes the void; use the relative block envelopes of z82 §6.3 with the appropriate sign before expanding, so this is a pointwise upper polynomial, of log conductors $O(L)$ and with exponentially small unit-Haar mean. An exact-list test alone would involve *all* the primes of its band. This comparison is up to constants in the exponent/level, not an equality of optimal constants. The probability measure of V may depend on the constant in the level. The matching **upper bound on actual exceptional primes** is from G/G′, not from V.

**Why both clauses? (PROVED rel. elementary arithmetic.)** On a prime row $p\equiv1\pmod4$, $p>K$, $x=(p+q)/4$, the positive two-fraction completion at $x$ is equivalent to coprime ratio labels $A,B$ with $AB\mid px$, $A+B=0\pmod q$ (factor $(qy-px)(qz-px)=(px)^2$, or see z80 `EXTREME.md` §7). Since $p\nmid x$ and $q<p$, remove the optional single $p$ from either label. What remains gives exactly clause 1 or 2. Conversely these labels give positive $y,z>x$ since $p>q$. In particular, for a fixed finite dyad one *can* include all labels needed there. Off that interval the cylinder interpretations, not archimedean size tests, define $V$. Both clauses avoid odd squares $n=s^2$ on units: every odd prime $r\mid ab$ has $(r/q)=(-q/r)=1$ by reciprocity since $s^2=-q\pmod r$; for $2\mid ab$ we have $q=7\pmod8$. Thus $(a/b\mid q)=1$ in Jacobi notation, whereas $(-1/q)=-1$, and a square multiplier cannot change this. This check includes nonsquarefree $q$. There is no ES nonsolubility implication for a composite square.

## 2. Finite comparison and its small-prime hypotheses

**PROVED (comparison setup).** We use the *internal* `paper/sieve-limits-note.tex`, Thm `wseq`, Lemmas `kavoid`,`kthin`, Thm `kcomp`, Cor. `kmean` (these finite inequalities are arithmetic-free, not distribution theorems for integers). Here are the hypotheses and all the required costs. Charge $\sum_{r\mid Q,r>w}\log r$ for a term of modulus $Q$, where $w$ is an absolute large fixed integer to be chosen. The reference $U$ is uniform units conditioned on $1\bmod4$. Pin unit squares at primes $r\le w$ to their full precision, uniformly on this product; its $U$-mass costs at most $\exp(O(w))$, independently of the exponents. Enlarge the period as needed, then project. Every class with top prime $\le w$ is avoided by the square check. Process the other prime-power coordinates in increasing order. At $r$ let $p_r$ be the conditional *fresh unit-digit* mass completing at least one class whose largest prime is $r$, for the current history. The capped law resamples off that union if $p_r\le r^{-1/2}$ (light); at a heavy prime do nothing. The conditional density w.r.t. fresh units is $\le(1-r^{-1/2})^{-1}$ at every history. Consequently for **any** residue mod $m$ its mass under this path law is $\le\Gamma(m)/m$, where $\Gamma(m)=\prod_{r\mid m}\gamma_r$, $\gamma_2=8$, $\gamma_r=2r/(r-1)$ at odd $r\le w$ and $\gamma_r=(1-r^{-1/2})^{-1}(1-1/r)^{-1}$ at $r>w$. These are bounds relative to integer, not unit, density. They hold for partial powers too; apply the chain rule backwards on the selected coordinates. In particular $\gamma_r=1+O(r^{-1/2})$ off the base.

For a majorant $f\ge1_V$, $f\ge0$ on the entire unit fibre, of level $\lambda=\max(1,A_0)L$, the comparison bounds $-\log\mathbb E_U f$ by the base cost plus $\log2$ plus twice the sum of the following costs, provided the capped process leaks to a hit with probability $\le1/2$:

* At singleton primes use expected cost $\mathbb E[-\log(1-p_r)1_{\rm light}]\le2\mathbb E p_r$ by pointwise density comparison.
* On a block $s<\log r\le2s$, $d=\lfloor\lambda/s\rfloor\ge1$, use $d\{C+\log(1+\mathbb E\sum p_r/d)\}+O(\log(2d))$. Indeed a level test sees at most $d$ coordinates per term. `kcomp` on the conditional product of the block gives `kmean` with the conditional expected replaced mass, then Jensen removes the past. The auxiliary paths have the very same capped marginal law. Beyond $\log r>\lambda$ the test is constant in those digits (zero cost). One may truncate a block at either end. `wseq` composes these comparisons by conditioning nonnegative tests, followed by Jensen on the surviving paths. No assertion of independence of the capped digits is needed.

The passage to the probability law is exactly the finite LP dual of minimizing $\mathbb E_U f$ with these majorant constraints, on the span of the level cylinders (z80 `BEYOND_34.md` §16 LM). Normalization is the constraint at the constant. Thus it suffices to bound these costs. This is a new *gap-specific supply estimate* in the old comparison theorem, not a new LP theorem.

**Lemma S (PROVED; inflation and smooth supply).** Uniformly in finite subfamilies, the sum of $\mathbb E p_r$ through $w<r\le y$ is $\ll_w (\log(2y))^3$. The probability of a heavy hit is $\le1/2$ for an absolute choice of $w$, uniformly even without the gap cutoff. These claims also hold if the fresh categorical law is integer rather than unit, with the same upper inflation bounds on pins.

*First moment.* A class of modulus $G$ contributes at most $\Gamma(G)/G$: charge its other digits by inflation and the fresh decision by ordinary unit density. For clause 2 there are at most two orders per triple $a,b,q$ with $G=4abq$; if $P(G)\le y$ all three labels are smooth. The Euler sum $\sum_{P(h)\le y}\Gamma(h)/h\ll_w\log(2y)$ gives the cube. Here $P(1)=1$. For clause 1 the number of labels per coprime pair is $\le\tau(a+b)$. We show the bound
$$\sum_{a\le b,\ (a,b)=1,\ P(ab)\le y}\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)\tau(a+b)}{ab}\ll_w (\log(2y))^3.\tag{1}$$
For $b\in[V,2V)$, $a\le2V$, put $T_a(h)=\sum_{b\in[V,2V),\,h\mid b,(a,b)=1}\tau(a+b)$. If $(a,h)>1$ it vanishes. Otherwise the divisor hyperbola through $2\sqrt V$ gives $T_a(h)\ll (V/h)\log(2V)+\sqrt V$ (the small divisor is coprime to $h$); for $h\le\sqrt V$ this is $\ll (V/h)\log(2V)$. At all $h$ use $T_a(h)\ll_\epsilon V^{1+\epsilon}/h$ since $h\le2V$ or the count vanishes.

For clarity, the smooth tail of (1) costs **no** $\log\log y$. Write $\Gamma=1*g$ with $g$ squarefree supported, $g(r)=\gamma_r-1\ge0$. For $D\le V^{1/16}$ with $\omega(D)\le4$, split $h$ at $V^{1/4}$ to get
$$\sum_h g(h)T_a([D,h])\ll_w (V/D)\,[\Gamma(D)\log(2V)+1].\tag{2}$$
Indeed $\sum_h g(h)(D,h)/h\ll_w\Gamma(D)$ by the Euler product. On $h>V^{1/4}$ Rankin at $1/4$ gives $\ll_w V^{-1/16}$ in the same sum restricted to the tail: at each of the at most four exceptional primes the new factor is $1+g(r)r^{1/4}=O_w(1)$, and $\sum_r g(r)/r^{3/4}<\infty$. Use the trivial $T_a$ bound with $\epsilon<1/16$. At $D=1$ this already gives the untrimmed dyad estimate.

For $V\ge y^{64}$ use the local weight $Z_y(b)=\sum_{r^j\mid b,\ r^j\le y}\log r/\log y$ of `sieve-limits-note`, Lemmas `localweight`,`Zmoments`. Either the product of the prime-power components $r^{v_r(b)}>y$ of a smooth $b$ exceeds $\sqrt V$ and is squarefull, or $Z_y(b)\ge (\log V)/(2\log y)$. The first case contributes $\ll V^{4/5}$ to the weighted dyad by $\Gamma(b)\tau(a+b)\ll_\epsilon V^\epsilon$ and $\sum_{h>\sqrt V,\ h\ \mathrm{squarefull}}1/h\ll V^{-1/4}$ (write $h=d^2t^3$, $t$ squarefree). In the second, expand $Z_y^4$; its four prime powers have lcm $D\le y^4\le V^{1/16}$. Estimate (2) applies. The sum of $\Gamma(D)/D$ with the four log weights divided by $(\log y)^4$ is $O_w(1)$: group equal prime bases and sum $1/r^{\max j}$ over their exponents, $O(1/r)$ for each group; then use Mertens' prime-log sum. This gives weighted dyad $\ll_w V\log(2V)\min(1,(\log y/\log V)^4)$ at large $V$ (absorbing $V^{4/5}$); below $y^{64}$ use (2) at $D=1$. The sum divided by $V$ over powers of two is $O_w((\log(2y))^2)$, uniformly in the smaller $a$ (or sum over $a$ first at each dyad). Its remaining harmonic smooth Euler sum supplies one more logarithm. This proves (1).

*Heavy leak.* Here a logarithm of $y$ to an unspecified absolute power suffices, but control in **$w$** matters. For $G=k r^v$, $P(k)<r$, write $m_v(k)$ for the number of classes counted with multiplicity. The cofactor calculation of `EXCEPTIONAL_KARY2.md` Lemma 4.1 uses only inflation: the number $N_v$ of active cofactors before the $r$ digit has
$$\mathbb E N_v^2\le C(\log(2w))^C\log(2r)\sum_{P(k)<r}m_v(k)^2\tau(k)\Gamma(k)/k.$$
In fact two cofactor pins cost at most $\Gamma([k,k'])/[k,k']$; symmetrize $m_v(k)m_v(k')$ by the squares and split $k'$ by $(k,k')\mid k$. The remaining smooth Euler sum costs the prefactor. More generally, for fixed $c,j$, $\sum_{P(k)\le r}\tau(k)^c\Gamma(k)^j k^{-1+1/\log r}\ll (\log(2w))^C(\log(2r))^C$ for $r>w\ge 16$: expand the Euler product, terms at powers $\ge2$ bounded in total at large primes, and $\sum_{p\le r}(p^{1/\log r}-1)/p=O(1)$ by the prime-log sum. Small-base Euler factors at $p\le w$ increase the log product by $O(\sum_{p\le w}1/p)+O(1)$, not $O(w)$.

For clause 2, $m_v(k)\ll\tau_3(kr^v)\le (v+1)^2\tau(k)^2$ up to an absolute factor (divide out 4 first). For clause 1 put $D=r^v$. Split off the factor $D$ from the one label containing $r$. Thus $k=4hk'$, and by Cauchy $m_v(k)^2\ll\tau(k)\sum_{4hk'=k}\tau(Dh+k')^2$. In a dyad $T\le k<2T$ with $T\ge 64D^4$, Cauchy once more bounds the cofactor sum on this dyad by
$$ C(\log(2w))^C(\log(2r))^C e^{-\log T/(2\log r)}\left(\frac1T\sum_{hk'\asymp T}\tau(Dh+k')^4\right)^{1/2}.$$
Here the first factor used the smooth tilted Euler sum just proved, absorbing the divisor number of splits; in the second factor retain $(k',r)=1$. The parenthesis is $\ll (\log(2DT))^C$. Indeed dyadically split $h,k'$ with larger variable of size $V\gg\sqrt T\gg D$. With the smaller fixed, the linear form in the larger is $\ll V^3$ and has at most one root modulo every divisor (for slope $D$ use $(k',D)=1$; incompatible divisors have no roots). For an integer $m\ll V^3$, strip the $O(1)$ prime factors $>V$ with multiplicities, then greedily group remaining prime factors into $O(1)$ products $b_i\le V$: two consecutive completed blocks have product $>V$. Consequently $\tau(m)^4\ll\sum_{d\mid m,d\le V}\tau(d)^C$ by taking a largest divisor-count block. Counting solutions costs $O(V/d)$ per $d$, so the average is polylogarithmic by the divisor Euler product. This is the elementary argument of z78 `WILD_ES.md` §7, applied to this linear form. For $T<64D^4$ the pointwise divisor bound and $\Gamma(k)\le8\cdot 4^{\omega(k)}$ give $m_v(k)^2\tau(k)\Gamma(k)\ll D^{1/8}$ (enlarging the absolute constant); summing $1/k$ costs $O(\log(2D))$. Summing the long dyads against the displayed exponential, then using the short range, bounds $\mathbb E N_v^2$ by $C(\log(2w))^C(v+1)^C(\log(2r))^C r^{v/4}$. No growth parameter is hidden in a divisor-moment constant.

Finally $p_r\le 2\sum_{v\ge1} r^{-v}N_v$, since a fresh unit $r^v$-digit has density $1/\varphi(r^v)$. Minkowski gives $\mathbb E p_r^2\ll (\log(2w))^C (\log(2r))^C r^{-7/4}$. A union bound for heavy hits costs at most $\sum_{r>w}r^{1/2}\mathbb E p_r^2=o_{w\to\infty}(1)$. Fix $w$ now. This proves S. Its cubic estimate alone gives the second row of V: singletons up to log size $\lambda^{1/4}$, then dyadic blocks at $s=2^i\lambda^{1/4}$ with cost $O((\lambda/s)(1+i)+\log(2\lambda))$ each. No application of a four-type theorem to an unverified fifth class type has been made.

## 3. The cost of a *distinct* completing digit

**Lemma P (PROVED rel. Brun–Titchmarsh, Mertens and §2).** Take $R=K^{20}>w$ (eventually), $s\ge\log R$, $s<\log r\le2s$. Write $H=\log(2s/\log R)$. On the path law defined below the expected sum of fresh completing probabilities in this block is at most
$$C_h\sum_{q\le K}\varphi(q)^{-h}\,(2s)^{3^h-1}\,D_h(H)+O(K e^{-s}),\qquad 0<h\le1,\tag{3}$$
where $D_h=1$ always works with a constant bounded for $1/2\le h\le1$; for each **fixed** $0<h<1$ one can take $D_h(H)=(1+H)^{-1/2}$. The same upper bound holds if a block is clipped but estimated using its full upper endpoint (including a first block crossing $\log R$ with $2s>\log R$ and $s\ge\frac12\log R$). Crucially the parameter exponent is $3^h-1$, **not** $2h$.

*Higher powers.* Add to the forbidden rules all square marks $r^2\mid n+q$ at the relevant primes $r>R$ and $q\le K$, $q=3\bmod4$. We may enlarge the finite period, including the $q$ digits and any required precision at earlier primes. A majorant of the original void majorizes the enlarged family's void too. At large $r$ even the enlarged union requires $n=-q\pmod r$ for some $q$, so $p_r\le K/(r-1)<r^{-1/2}$; the added clauses cost at most $\sum_{r>e^s}2K/r^2\ll K e^{-s}$ in expected mass. No large-prime square mark occurs along the resulting path. At $r\le R$ nothing changes and S still controls the leak. Primes irrelevant to the family can be adjoined or omitted throughout.

*Seed.* For a specified $q$, after processing all $t\le R$ let $T_q=\prod_{t\le R}(2v_t+1)$, where $v_t$ is the valuation of $(n+q)/4$ truncated at the maximum needed for the finite family; ignore primes dividing $q$. There are at most $T_q$ unit ratios from these digits. For clause 2 include the two possible translates using $n^{\pm1}\bmod q$: that digit is already known. This costs only a factor three in target count, even if the translates are correlated with the seed. For $0<h\le1$,
$$\mathbb E T_q^h\ll (\log R)^{3^h-1} .\tag{4}$$
Given earlier digits, $\Pr(v_t\ge i)\le\gamma_t/t^i$, also at 2 with its absolute base constant (use the class mod $2^{i+2}$). Sum the increments $(2i+1)^h-(2i-1)^h$ against these tails. The first increment is $3^h-1$; the remaining terms are $O(t^{-2})$ uniformly in $h$. Multiply conditional upper bounds for the factors and use $\sum_{t\le R}1/t=\log\log R+O(1)$ and $\gamma_t=1+O(t^{-1/2})$ above the fixed base. No independence of the actual shifted small factors is being imposed.

*One-window domination.* Conditional on the entire small history, in any **one** window the subset of rough primes $R<t<r$ with $t\mid n+q$ is stochastically dominated by independent Bernoullis of parameters $b_t=\gamma_t/t$ (use independent uniforms at successive steps and sample the remaining digit conditionally on its mark). This holds even though the transition sees other windows too. Extend these independent bits through $t\le e^{2s}$, obtaining one common random upper list for testing each possible $r$ in this block. This is legitimate for the increasing event that the old directions, with arbitrary zero exponents, can correct $r$ or $r^{-1}$. For fixed upper list, of length $J$, the specification at $r$ is $n=-q\pmod r$ **and** $r$ in at most $6T_q3^J$ unit classes mod $q$ (supply both signs of the top letter). Indeed in a completion with top $r$ to exponent one the other factors are all earlier; large square marks never survive. Current higher powers can be charged to the added square test. The enlarged list may include $r$ itself; the upper list is sampled before summing in the deterministic index $r$ and is the *same distribution for every index*. We do not condition the actual past on the fresh digit or require any law on all windows jointly to be independent.

For this fixed list the harmonic sum of candidate $r$ is $\ll\min(1,6T_q3^J/\varphi(q))$. Use Montgomery–Vaughan, *The large sieve*, Mathematika 20 (1973), Brun–Titchmarsh bound $\pi(x;k,a)\le 2x/(\varphi(k)\log(x/k))$ for $(a,k)=1$, $x>k$, followed by partial summation on $\max(R,e^s)<r\le e^{2s}$; $\log(r/q)\gg\log r$. The cap by 1 here means an absolute constant times 1, by Mertens. The $1/(r-1)$ fresh unit probability costs only a constant versus $1/r$. Bounding the number of **distinct** digit values, not the number of successful words, is what permits the minimum inside this calculation.

*Anti-concentration after tilting.* Put $z=3^h$ and $M(z)=\mathbb E z^J\ll \exp((z-1)H)$ by Mertens, uniformly for $1\le z\le3$. For $A>0$, $\min(1,A3^J)\le A^hz^J$. If $0<h<1$ multiply the latter by $\exp[-(\log3)\min(h,1-h)|J+\log A/\log3|]$ and the inequality still holds. Under the tilt $z^J/M(z)$ the bits are independent of parameters $z b_t/(1+(z-1)b_t)$, with variance sum $\asymp H$ when $H$ is large. Here include **all** primes $R<t\le e^{2s}$ in the dominating list, not just primes dividing a particular period. Fourier inversion for their sum gives maximal atom $O(1/\sqrt{1+H})$: the characteristic function has modulus at most $\exp(-2\sin^2(\xi/2)\sum b'_t(1-b'_t))$, $b'_t$ the tilted parameters. Sum the geometric damping over integer $J$, uniformly in the real shift $\log A/\log3$. Apply this conditionally at $A=6T_q/\varphi(q)$ and then use (4). This proves (3). This anti-concentration concerns a dominating one-window **comparison**, not the distribution of a conditional number of arithmetic hits.

## 4. Putting the costs together

**PROVED (completion of V).** Write $B=\log K$. For $0<h<1$, $\sum_{q\le K}1/\varphi(q)^h\ll K^{1-h}/(1-h)$, uniformly e.g. for $1/2\le h<1$, and $\ll\log(2K)$ at $h=1$. Indeed $q/\varphi(q)=\sum_{d\mid q}\mu^2(d)/\varphi(d)$ bounds its $h$-th power, and $\sum 1/(d\varphi(d))<\infty$. In the following choices use singletons up to log size $s_*$, and the §2 local comparisons on blocks $2^i s_*<\log r\le2^{i+1}s_*$. Total singleton cost is bounded by summing (3) on *backwards* dyadic log blocks down to $R$, plus $O_w(B^3)$ by S. Increasing $s_*$ to at least $20B$ just for the small/large transition is harmless in the first case below; alternatively if $s_*<20B$, use singletons through $20B$, then dyadic log blocks from there. Terms $O(K e^{-s})$ are negligible.

* For $B\le L^{1/4}$ take $s_*=(L/B)^{1/3}$. With $h=1$, (3) is $O(B s^2)$. At the split $B s_*^2=L/s_*=:W$, and $B^3\ll W$. In the case $s_*<20B$, $20B\le20s_*$, so the modified split is still comparable. For the subsequent blocks $d_i=\lfloor\lambda/(2^is_*)\rfloor\asymp \lambda/(2^is_*)$ if $d_i\ge1$. The mean mass to depth ratio is $O_{A_0}(2^{3i})$; hence total cost is $O_{A_0}(W\sum_i2^{-i}(1+i)+\log^2(2\lambda))=O_{A_0}(W)$. This argument and S are uniform right up to the crossover. It gives $o(L^{3/4})$ at every $B=o(L^{1/4})$, not only at a fixed power distance below the endpoint (bounded $B$ follows also by monotonicity).
* For $b_0<\beta<\log3$ fixed take $h=(\log z)/\log3\in(0,1)$ from V and $s_*=L^u l^{1/(2z)}$. Then $\beta(1-h)+uz=1$. At $s\ge\sqrt{s_*}/2$, (3) is $O_\beta(K^{1-h}s^{z-1}/\sqrt l)$, and at $s=s_*$ this is $O_\beta(L/s_*)$. Below $\sqrt{s_*}$ use the version without $1/\sqrt l$; its whole geometric sum saves a fixed power compared with $W$ since $z>1$. Above $s_*$ the mass/depth ratio grows at most as $C_\beta 2^{iz}$. Thus singleton and subsequent block costs are both $O_{\beta,A_0}(L/s_*)$. There is no assertion uniform as $\beta$ approaches the endpoints.
* At $\beta=\log3$ use $s_*=L^{1/3}l^{-1/6}$ and $h=1-1/\sqrt l$ in the **undamped** (3). The totient sum costs $\ll \sqrt l\,K^{1-h}$. At the split, $K^{1-h}s_*^{3^h-3}=O(1)$ because $3^h=3-3\log3/\sqrt l+O(1/l)$ and $\log s_*=(1/3)l+O(\log l)$. The mean is $O(s_*^2\sqrt l)=O(L/s_*)$. Use the same geometric sums at fixed $h=h(X)$ for **all** blocks of this $X$; $3^h-1$ stays bounded away from zero. The $\sqrt l$ is from summing logarithmic gap scales, not a Stirling loss at a single scale. ∎

## 5. The intermediate logarithmic phase on primes

**Corollary I (PROVED rel. z82 G′'s seed sieve, group estimate, BV/BT good moduli and relative block transfer).** For each fixed $b_0<\beta<\log3$, take $z,u$ as in V. Then $\#\{p\le X:g(p)>L^\beta\}\ll_\beta X\exp(-c_\beta L^{1-u}/l^{1/(2z)})$. This interpolates G′ and improves the log-log denominator of z81 `GAP_ES.md` Thm C away from G′'s stated cutoffs. No gain over G′ at $\beta=1$. The primary exponent $1-u$ is already z81's. Here is the change needed in the lower estimate (the upper majorant/marginal floor of V alone does not give I).

On each eligible small fibre of z82 §2 use $q\in[K/2,K]$, $3\bmod4$, $K=L^\beta$, seed radical $d_q$ of $(n+q)/4$ at odd primes $\le y=K^{1/10000}$. Require $d_q\le K^{1/10}$ and $\omega(d_q)\le30\log l$. For **each** squarefree odd $y$-smooth $d$ meeting these cuts, z82's fixed-dimension lower sieve gives $\gg K/(d\log y)$ such windows of exact radical $d$ uniformly in the unit pin (one excluded root for each $t\nmid d$, level $K^{1/3}$, per-squarefree-modulus remainder at most 1). That step does not contain the tilt. The Euler product $\prod_{3\le t\le y}(1+z/t)\asymp l^z$ over odd primes, restricted to these two cuts, retains a positive proportion by Markov at the expectations $\le(z+o(1))\log y$ and $(z+o(1))\log\log y$. Hence $\sum_q z^{\omega(d_q)}\gg K l^{z-1}$. Removing the same $O_A(Kl^{-A})$ bad moduli as z82 §2 is harmless since $z\le3$. Use the physical pin only on primes $\le y$, so its log cost is $O(y)=o(L)$.

With $a=\beta/\log3$, $T=c_0 L^u l^{1/(2z)}$, the rough prime band $(L^{20},\exp T]$ has mass $H=u l+(1/(2z)-1)\log l+\log c_0+O(1)$. Use length $j=\lceil al\rceil-\omega(d_q)$; the $3^{\omega(d_q)}$ seed ratios are distinct since $d_q^2<q$. On this layer $3^{j+\omega(d_q)}\ge K$. The z82 §§2,7 conditional second-moment estimate and product cutoff give a bounded-below chance of a match on each good modulus, even requiring the exact rough list product $\le\exp(C_2 T)$. Its hypotheses hold: $j\asymp l$, $(5/9)^j 2^{\omega(q)}=o(1)$, repeats cost $O(j^2/(L^{20}H))$, and conditional expected log product given all unit directions is $O(jT/H)$ by BT. This is in the intensified independent comparison, before conditioning to the exclusive prime digits. Its Poisson layer mass by Stirling is comparable to
$$ c_0^{z-1}L^{-u I(z)} l^{(1/(2z)-1)(z-1)-1/2}z^{\omega(d_q)},\quad I(v)=1-v+v\log v,$$
since $a/u=z$ and the quadratic Taylor errors are $O_{\beta,c_0}((\log l)^2/l)$. The comparison constants can be fixed before $c_0$ by allowing its fixed value to affect the onset. Thus the total mean exceeds $c c_0^{z-1}W$, using $\beta-u I(z)=1-u$. Thin on each pin to that mean with a sufficiently small $c$. Apply the exact-list block envelopes of z82 §6.3 as in §7 with even truncation $M\asymp c c_0^{z-1} W$ large relative to the thinned mean, $MT\ll c_0^z L$. Their summation cost $\exp(O_\beta(MT))$ is absorbed by choosing $c_0$ small; no $l$ multiplies $MT$. As in G′, coins average out in each list intersection, fibres have length $X^{1-o(1)}$, the independent law conditioned on no double marks is the categorical law (conditioning cost $\le2$), and the resulting void majorant has exponentially small expectation on the integer interval. Finally intersect with primes and sum dyads above $\sqrt X$ using the smaller local cutoff. These steps require no prime-in-many-shifts independence. ∎

## 6. Pair cells in the Lenstra rectangle

**Theorem D (PROVED rel. Lenstra and z80's fixed-order Haar estimates).** In the z82 §9.3 box, all three natural periods $4ad-1,4bd-1,4ab$ exceed $p^{.58}$ for sufficiently large $p$ (strict inequalities as given there); the trial periods even exceed $p^{.63}$. Keeping periods $\le \exp(C L^\gamma)$, fixed $0<\gamma<1$, $C>0$, selects **nothing** from this box. This is not the small-trial count with mean $\asymp L^{2\gamma}$.

More quantitatively, for $X<n\le2X$ take actual trial labels $i=(a,d)$ in the fixed rectangle
$$J:\quad X^{.316}\le a<X^{.316+w},\qquad X^{.36}\le d<X^{.36+w},\qquad w=.0001.$$
Use all those labels and no parity restriction, $q_i=4ad-1$, hit at $n=-a\pmod{q_i}$, $Z(n)$ their count. A hit recovers $b=(n+a)/q_i$ with exponent $.324+O(2w+1/L)$, so lies strictly inside the Lenstra box, off the outer tie. Let $H_{ij}$ be the **unit-Haar** joint hit probability, $i\ne j$, and $S_X$ the set of ordered distinct pairs realized by at least one integer of the dyad. Then for some absolute $c>0$,
$$\sum_{(i,j)\in S_X}H_{ij}\ll X^{-c},\qquad \sum_{i\ne j}H_{ij}=m_J^2+O(X^{-c}),\qquad m_J\asymp L^2.\tag{5}$$
Here $m_J$ can be the exact Haar mean. Thus the normalized Haar pair-intensity (probability on pairs proportional to $H_{ij}$) gives probability $O(X^{-c})$ to the entire support of any empirical dyad pair-intensity. If the latter is normalized and nonempty the total variation distance tends to one, not zero. The *sum* of a signed difference can nevertheless be small.

*Proof.* The second assertion is the **Haar** lemma and empty-base mean in z80 `R_BELOW_619.md` §4.1, with fixed order two and $w<.316/100$, not its prime transfer. The large-period Haar application is explicitly described in its §5.3. For small period gcd $g=(q_i,q_j)\le X^{.01}$ one has $H_{ij}\le1/\varphi([q_i,q_j])\le X^{-1.352+.01+o(1)}$. There are at most $\sum_n Z(n)^2\ll X^{4/3+o(1)}$ realized pairs: the first moment over integers is $\sum_n Z(n)\ll X L^2+X^{.676+2w}\ll XL^2$, by summing $X/q_i+1$. For each $n$ Lenstra, *Divisors in residue classes*, Math. Comp. 42 (1984), Prop. (3.1), bounds the number of divisors of $m>0$ in $r\bmod s$ by $C_\eta$ when $(r,s)=1$, $s>m^{1/4+\eta}$. Here $m=n+a$, $s=4a$, $r=-1$, e.g. $\eta=.01$. So $Z(n)\ll X^{.316+w}\ll X^{1/3}$. All bounds concern positive $n$ in the interval, not whole-period rows. This gives a power saving since $4/3+.01<1.352$.

For the large gcds fix $i$ and sum the conditional probabilities of all compatible $j\ne i$ at $g>X^{.01}$. On dyads $a'\asymp A,d'\asymp D$ they are each at most $X^{o(1)}g/(AD)$. Given $g\mid q_i$, $a'=a\pmod g$, the condition $g\mid4a'd'-1$ fixes $d'=d\pmod g$. The product of the two progression counts therefore gives conditional mass $\ll X^{o(1)}(1/g+1/A+1/D+g/(AD))$. If $a'\ne a$, $g\mid a'-a\ne0$ so $g\le2X^{.316+w}$. Otherwise $d'\ne d$ and $g\le2X^{.36+w}$. Every term saves a fixed power, even after summing over the divisor number of $g$, the dyads and then the harmonic mass of the first pin. This is the one-pin determinant cut, not a growing-order bound. Together these estimates give (5). For a concrete fully coprime pair in z82's larger patch take $X=t^{25}$, $a=t^8$, $m=t$, $d=(a+1)m+1$, $(a',d')=(a+1,d-m)$. Then $q'=q+4$, $(q,q')=1$. A joint hit requires $(b-b')q=4b'-1$ with $0<4b'-1<q$ for large $t$. Thus the intersection is empty on integers in the dyad, although it has strictly positive Haar probability. ∎

**A separating statistic (PROVED on integers, CONDITIONAL on Elliott–Halberstam on primes).** Set $T_X=\sum_{(i,j)\in S_X}1_i1_j$, a nonnegative polynomial with each modulus $\le X^{1.36}$. By (5), $\mathbb E_{\rm unit\ Haar} T_X=O(X^{-c})$, and the integer-Haar mean is no larger termwise. On the actual dyad $T_X=Z(Z-1)$ identically and its mean is $\gg L^4$ on integers: $\mathbb E Z=\sum 1/q_i+O(X^{.676+2w-1})\asymp L^2$ and Jensen suffices. On primes **EH alone** gives this last lower bound on $\mathbb E_p T_X$. Indeed let $D(q)$ denote the maximum absolute dyadic prime discrepancy from the dyadic prime count divided by $\varphi(q)$ over reduced residues. These moduli are below $X^{.68}$, with multiplicity $\le\tau(q+1)$. EH gives $\sum D(q)\ll_A X L^{-A}$ there for each fixed $A$, while trivially $D(q)\ll X/\varphi(q)$. Cauchy with the two sums $\sum D(q)$ and $\sum \tau(q+1)^2 D(q)$ yields arbitrary log savings: the latter is $\ll X L^{O(1)}$ by $1/\varphi(q)\ll (\log\log(3q))/q$ and $\sum_{k\le x}\tau(k)^2\ll x\log^3(2x)$. Thus $\mathbb E_p Z\sim m_J$; Jensen again. Any law matching the corresponding moduli has the *Haar* value of $T_X$. This names an interval input actually false in the exact-marginal model, but the statistic vanishes at $Z=0$: its large mean need not reduce the void at all. $S_X$ depends on the interval and is not being charged as a cheaply enumerable coefficient list.

**Assessment (moment meaning).** Lenstra also bounds the other two vertex degrees on this patch by the z82 §9.3 argument. At a fixed $n$ this gives a matching of size $\gg Z(n)$, but it is not a dependency graph for the random events. If all three coordinates are indexed in advance the event $p=4abd-a-b$ is an equality, not a small congruence. If only two are indexed it is a congruence plus any remaining height cuts. (5) shows that even a relative $o(1)$ **aggregate unsigned** cellwise approximation to the Haar factorial of order two is false; arithmetic has to concentrate its pair mass on the tiny surviving label set, if it is to approximate the total. This is neither a lower bound for the variance nor a rejection of an upper bound for the scalar Laplace transform. The signed two-cell approach exploits precisely the difference. LM reproduces Haar on a fixed tuple when its lcm is below its ceiling, even for this rectangle (one could take a sufficiently high fixed power for any fixed order). It does not reproduce (5)'s interval support. Conversely LM on integers is not a prime model; its prime-unit version has the stated `PLcap` qualifications. It is not an assertion of full independence on arbitrary coprime lists with no bound on total level. Nothing proves concentration on primes from bounded vertex degrees alone; the hypotheses of Janson, Suen, Talagrand and independent-coordinate polynomial concentration cannot be imported that way.

**CONDITIONAL on BCR for this paragraph only.** We use Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłł, *The mean square of the product of the Riemann zeta-function with Dirichlet polynomials*, arXiv:1411.7764v1, Conjecture 1, (1.4): let $A\ll(NM)^{1/2+\epsilon}$. For their $S_{A,M,N}=\sum_{a,m,n:(m,n)=1}\nu_a\alpha_m\beta_n e(a\bar m/n)$, $A\le a<2A$, $M\le m<2M$, $N\le n<2N$, the bound is
$$\ll_\epsilon \|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_2\|\nu\|_2(M+N)^{1/2+\epsilon}+\|\nu\|_2 A^{1/2}\big(\|\alpha\|_\infty\|\beta\|_2 N^{1/2+\epsilon}+\|\alpha\|_2\|\beta\|_\infty M^{1/2+\epsilon}\big).$$
There is no arbitrary coefficient array on tuples here. z80 §5.6 gives $\mathbb E_p(Z-m_J)^2\ll_J L^2$ on sufficiently narrow fixed interior boxes about this point ($x+y=.992<1$, $x=.316$, $y=.676$); one may narrow $J$ for the dispersion step. Thus on that subpatch there is a matching of $\gg_J L^2$ edges except on $O_J(\pi(X)/L^2)$ primes by Chebyshev. This is a corollary, not a better zero bound for $R$; even a variable with mass $1/m$ at 0 and $1-1/m$ at integer $m\asymp L^2$ has mean $m-1$ and variance $m-1$. z80's variation of all tiles gives its stated normal order in prime probability, not a growing-moment hierarchy for the marked periods. In particular (5) is compatible with the conjectural second moment.

## 7. What subpower moments do give

**Theorem M (PROVED rel. the two z75 quadratic supplies (2), based on BV/BT, and elementary interval counting).** Let $0<\gamma<1$ be fixed, $t=L^\gamma$, $L=\log X$. Among primes of **any** interval of $X$ consecutive positive integers all but $O_\gamma(X\exp(-c_\gamma F))$ have at least $ct^2$ (★) words of **each** $d$-parity, with distinct pivot primes in each collection and periods $\le\exp(Ct)$. One may take
$$ F=\begin{cases}L^{2\gamma},&\gamma\le1/3,\\L^{1-\gamma}\log L,&\gamma>1/3.\end{cases}$$
Constants $c,C>0$ in the threshold/height are absolute for the two fixed parities; the onset and exception constants may depend on $\gamma$. These are *unbalanced* words. This trades the size of the guaranteed count against the exceptional rate; at $\gamma=1/3$ this is the previous z75 bound. It is not a positive fraction of the full quadratic-log center.

*Center on the fibre, but do not transfer the fibre.* In z75 `ANALYTIC_PUSH2.md` §§1–2 use their auxiliary search scale $Y=e^t$, all odd multipliers $k\le Y^{1/480}$, their atoms (1) at $(a_2,b_2)=(1,2)$ or $(0,3)$ for the valuations of the word coordinates. The other indices are primes $\sqrt Y<r\le Y$ and odd $w\le Y^{1/6}$ with $u=2^{a_2}w$, $kr=2^{a_2+b_2}w-1\pmod{2^{a_2+b_2+1}w}$ and z75's dyad cuts. A hit requires $n=-u\pmod{kr}$ and has $d=(kr+1)/(4u)$ of the indicated parity. We use separately the two finite families, of size $\exp(O(t))$. In the integer Haar model repair **all** nonunit multiplier prime digits to 1 at full precision, leaving unit digits alone. Write $A_i(n)$ for activation of atom $i$ at its multiplier, i.e. the repaired $n=-u\pmod k$. This depends on $n\bmod k$ only. On primes larger than $Y$ the tests are the genuine tests. Given the small digits, hits at each $r$ are disjoint (the different $u<r$ and the modulus/size cut on $w$ ensure the distinctness in z75 §1) and counts at different $r$ are independent Bernoullis on integer Haar. Consequently
$$ Z=\sum_i A_i(n)1_{n=-u_i\ (r_i)},\qquad m(n)=\sum_i A_i(n)/r_i$$
have the *exact* conditional-mean relation and $c_1t^2\le m(n)\le C_1t^2$ uniformly by z75 (2) on the repaired all-unit pin. The range of the sum of reciprocals of active multipliers is then the **whole** odd harmonic sum. One must not center a growing moment at an unspecified asymptotic $\asymp t^2$.

For $j\le c_2t^2$, both signs of the centered exponential have conditional mean at most $\exp(Ct^2 h^2)$ for $0\le h\le1$ by the Bernoulli product. At $h=\sqrt j/t$ this yields $\mathbb E (Z-m(n))^{2j}\le(Ct^2j)^j$, uniformly in the small digits and $j$ (use $(2j)! h^{-2j}$ and both signs). Normalize by $(c_1 t^2/2)^{2j}$ to majorize $1_{Z<c_1t^2/2}$. In addition to its small Haar mean $(C'j/t^2)^j$, this polynomial has absolute cylinder coefficient sum $\exp(O(jt))$: each $A_i$ expands in at most $k$ ordinary classes and $m$ has this same expansion with coefficients $1/r_i$. Thus the normalized interval mean exceeds the Haar mean by at most $\exp(O(jt))/X$ as an upper error, one rounding unit per compatible intersection. With $j=\lfloor c_3\min(t^2,L/t)\rfloor$, sufficiently small absolute $c_3$, this is $\ll X^{-1/2}$. The log saving in the first term is $j\log(t^2/(C'j))\gg_\gamma F$, including the equality case $\gamma=1/3$. No interval majorant claims independence. Restrict to primes and discard at most $Y$ small primes, negligible at this scale. ∎

**Assessment (distribution level and sharpness).** For individual cost $O(t)$, nominal tuple order is $O(\theta_0L/t)$ when one insists on a worst-product log level $\theta_0 L$; overlaps actually cost the lcm. A fixed change to $\theta_0$ affects only constants in this calculation. In z79 `ANALYTIC_PUSH.md` §1, even an optimal Selberg square in a single independent band, with pivot probabilities $\le p_*<1$ and mean $O(t^2)$, has inverse mean bounded by $\sum_{j\le C L/t}(Ct^2)^j/j!$ when normalized at the void. Its log is $O_\gamma(F)$ by the exponential bound when $t^3\le L$, and by $O((L/t)\log(2+t^3/L))$ otherwise. No claim that every polynomial method requires mean-order moments. In particular the best **power** here is $2/3$, the binding constraints being quadratic supply and transferable order even with perfect conditional independence. At cubic supply the same calculation gives $3/4$. Kandukoori–Ramesh–Kumar and Trybushnyi have earlier claims at that ambient exponent (priority details z81 `LIT.md` §0.4).

For clarity, the unconditional analytic input to M is *only* in the pivot prime, via z75 (2), using the maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem: for each fixed $A>0$ some fixed $B(A)$ gives $\sum_{k\le x^{1/2}/(\log x)^B}\max_{(a,k)=1}\max_{y\le x}|\psi(y;k,a)-y/\varphi(k)|\ll_A x/(\log x)^A$, and its dyadic $\pi$ consequence; the relevant moduli are $\ll h_0^{1/6}$ on pivot dyads of size $h_0$, as in that proof. The same fixed-$A$ version is used for the good moduli of I. EH extends this input-prime distribution statement to each fixed $1-\epsilon$ level (in its usual prime-count form). Neither the BV nor EH error is exponentially small, nor is the implied constant in either theorem given uniformly in a growing log-saving power. There is no general assertion that even the expansion coefficients for all $j\asymp L^{1-\gamma}$ can be paid by BV or EH. Under Dirichlet GRH the usual explicit-formula dyadic error per reduced class in log-weighted prime counts is $O(\sqrt X\log^2(2Xq))$ for $q\le X$; even GRH does **not** evaluate the cells exactly or give relative accuracy near level one. For M one could pay this error times $\exp(O(jt))$ on a dyad with smaller $c_3$ and unit Haar instead. M already holds under BV alone, hence under GRH, EH or BCR without an improvement here. No deduction of z81's bespoke (M) or (DR) from these hypotheses or from a Montgomery AP hypothesis is known here. This limitation is about the estimates used, not a logical nonimplication theorem for those conjectures.
