# Bounded gaps: count the last digit, not the words ending there

## 1. The model result

**Theorem V (PROVED rel. §§2–4).** Write $L=\log X$, $l=\log L$. For $K\ge3$ take the following classes at every odd $q\le K$, $q=3\pmod4$. Use *any finite collection* of labels $a,b\ge1$, $(a,b)=(ab,q)=1$, with no size or prime-factor restrictions. A class asks for $n=-q\pmod{4ab}$ and one of

1. $a+b=0\pmod q$ (a condition on the label only);
2. $na+b=0\pmod q$ (interchanging $a,b$ is permitted).

Call their joint avoider set $\mathcal A$. Fix $A_0>0$. On any common CRT period containing these moduli, 8, and all integers $Q\le X^{A_0}$ as divisors (arbitrary further prime powers are allowed), there is a probability on **units with $n=1\pmod4$** agreeing *exactly* with uniform units on that fibre on every such $Q$, and assigning mass at least $\exp(-C W)$ to $\mathcal A$. Here are choices of $W$:

| $K$ | $W$ |
|---|---|
| $K=\exp B$, $B\to\infty$, $B\le L^{1/4}$ | $L^{2/3}B^{1/3}$ |
| arbitrary $K$ | $L^{3/4}$ |
| $L^\beta$, $b_0<\beta<\log 3$, $b_0=\log3/(1+\log3)$ | $L^{1-u}/l^{1/(2z)}$, where $z=3\mathrm e\,3^{-1/\beta}$, $u=\beta/(z\log3)$ |
| $L^{\log3}$ | $L^{2/3}l^{1/6}$ |

Constants and onsets can depend on $A_0$ and the **fixed** $\beta$ in the third row, not on the family/period. In the first row one can take absolute constants for the supply estimates for all sufficiently large $B$. In particular the first row gives $L^{2/3}l^{1/3}$ at fixed $K=L^\beta$, $\beta>\log3$. At $K=L$ one has $z=\mathrm e$, $u=1/(\mathrm e\log3)$. This is *one law* for all those marginals. It is not Haar in general, and is not supported on the actual dyad. The same floor holds for the Haar mean of every nonnegative polynomial-level majorant of the **whole** void, even one with signed coefficients.

**Consequence of V alone (PROVED).** On this chart a bound on *whole* avoiders from the given marginals cannot improve $(2+\alpha)/3$ at $B=L^\alpha$, $0<\alpha<1/4$, reach a fixed positive multiple of $L^{3/4}$ at $B=o(L^{1/4})$, or beat z82's saving by an unbounded factor at $K=L,L^{\log3},L^\beta$ ($\beta>\log3$ fixed).

**Attainment lemma (PROVED rel. z82 G/G′ and, for intermediate powers, §5).** At each of the fixed cutoff regimes of those upper bounds, there exists a **choice** of finite family and a nonnegative test of log modulus $O(L)$ majorizing its whole void on units $1\bmod4$, of unit-Haar mean $\ll \exp(-cW)$. Thus the comparison is up to constants in the level and rate. The law in V may depend on the fixed level constant. This upper side is not asserted for *every* subfamily.

*Proof.* Take all labels which can be selected by the corresponding G or G′ tests, including the cap on list product. A success implies an actual cylinder of clause 1, also on CRT: its small and rough labels divide $(n+q)/4$ on the corresponding prime-power digits and the matched ratio is $-1\bmod q$. Small repaired digits are unchanged on units. G′'s full small pin has log period $o(L)$; G encodes only one small congruence per selected list. Let $S$ be the number of (possibly thinned) successes. Given the pin, at a private prime there are $J$ distinct nonzero possible marks, each of unit probability $1/(r-1)$. Generate these by conditioning $J$ independent bits of odds $1/(r-1-J)$ on at most one success per prime. Joint conditioning costs $1+O(J^2/R)$ since $\sum_{r>R} J^2/r^2\ll J^2/R=o(1)$. In the independent-bit model changing from integer to unit digits changes both the reciprocal mass and the log no-mark probability per window by $O(J/R)$; all iid residue odds are perturbed by relative $1+O(1/R)$ per letter versus the integer intensified model. Hence z82's $j=O(l)$ list estimates give mean $\asymp W$ uniformly in the pin after the same thinning. For nonnegative tests conditioning costs at most 2, giving $\mathbb E 2^{-S}\ll e^{-cW}$ and $b_m:=\mathbb E\binom Sm\le2(CW)^m/m!$. In G the thinning coins, if any, can be averaged out; no full small-pin modulus enters the expansion.

As in z82 §6.3, take even $m_0$ a sufficiently large constant times $W$ and apply the even Taylor upper polynomial for $2^{-S}$ through degree $m_0$ (its excess in mean is $\le2^{-m_0-1}b_{m_0+1}$). Replace binomial terms by the **upper** exact-list envelopes for positive coefficients, **lower** ones for negative coefficients. This gives a pointwise upper polynomial nonnegative on the fibre, at least 1 at the whole void. Average the coins. For each set of $m$ pinned lists, each remaining private prime has $m$ excluded digits of density $1/(r-1)$ rather than $1/r$. Conditional independence across the remaining primes is exact on units. On each log block their harmonic mass is bounded; the no-mark mass is $\ge e^{-Cm}$ and the order-$k$ binomial moment is $\le(Cm)^k/k!$. The adjacent even/odd Bonferroni block envelopes of §6.3 with $k\asymp m_0$ thus keep the same relative Haar error $\ll e^{-A m_0}$ for any fixed $A$ on choosing the constant in $k$ sufficiently large. After summing binomials this costs $O(e^{-A m_0}\sum b_m)$, negligible by the factorial bound. Terms have log modulus $O(m_0 T)+o(L)=O(L)$ by the geometric sum of the log endpoints, the accepted product bound $\exp(C_2T)$, and (in G′) the small pin. An exact-list indicator *without* these envelopes need not have small modulus. This proves the lemma; the matching **upper count of actual primes** uses the additional interval step of G/G′ or §5. ∎

**Why both clauses? (PROVED rel. elementary arithmetic.)** On a prime row $p\equiv1\pmod4$, $p>K$, $x=(p+q)/4$, the positive two-fraction completion at $x$ is equivalent to coprime ratio labels $A,B$ with $AB\mid px$, $A+B=0\pmod q$ (factor $(qy-px)(qz-px)=(px)^2$, or see z80 `EXTREME.md` §7). Since $p\nmid x$ and $q<p$, remove the optional single $p$ from either label. What remains gives exactly clause 1 or 2. Conversely these labels give positive $y,z>x$ since $p>q$. In particular, for a fixed finite dyad one *can* include all labels needed there. Off that interval the cylinder interpretations, not archimedean size tests, define $\mathcal A$. Both clauses avoid odd squares $n=s^2$ on units: every odd prime $r\mid ab$ has $(r/q)=(-q/r)=1$ by reciprocity since $s^2=-q\pmod r$; for $2\mid ab$ we have $q=7\pmod8$. Thus $(a/b\mid q)=1$ in Jacobi notation, whereas $(-1/q)=-1$, and a square multiplier cannot change this. This check includes nonsquarefree $q$. There is no ES nonsolubility implication for a composite square.

## 2. Finite comparison and its small-prime hypotheses

**PROVED (comparison setup).** We use the *internal* `paper/sieve-limits-note.tex`, Thm `wseq`, Lemmas `kavoid`,`kthin`, Thm `kcomp`, Cor. `kmean` (these finite inequalities are arithmetic-free, not distribution theorems for integers). Here are the hypotheses and all the required costs. Charge $\sum_{r\mid Q,r>w}\log r$ for a term of modulus $Q$, where $w$ is an absolute large fixed integer to be chosen. The reference $U$ is uniform units conditioned on $1\bmod4$. Pin unit squares at primes $r\le w$ to their full precision, uniformly on this product; its $U$-mass costs at most $\exp(O(w))$, independently of the exponents. Enlarge the period as needed, then project. Every class with top prime $\le w$ is avoided by the square check. Process the other prime-power coordinates in increasing order. At $r$ let $p_r$ be the conditional *fresh unit-digit* mass completing at least one class whose largest prime is $r$, for the current history. The capped law resamples off that union if $p_r\le r^{-1/2}$ (light); at a heavy prime do nothing. The conditional density w.r.t. fresh units is $\le(1-r^{-1/2})^{-1}$ at every history. Consequently for **any** residue mod $m$ its mass under this path law is $\le\Gamma(m)/m$, where $\Gamma(m)=\prod_{r\mid m}\gamma_r$, $\gamma_2=8$, $\gamma_r=2r/(r-1)$ at odd $r\le w$ and $\gamma_r=(1-r^{-1/2})^{-1}(1-1/r)^{-1}$ at $r>w$. These are bounds relative to integer, not unit, density. They hold for partial powers too; apply the chain rule backwards on the selected coordinates. In particular $\gamma_r=1+O(r^{-1/2})$ off the base.

For a majorant $f\ge1_{\mathcal A}$, $f\ge0$ on the entire unit fibre, of level $\lambda=\max(1,A_0)L$, the comparison bounds $-\log\mathbb E_U f$ by the base cost plus $\log2$ plus twice the sum of the following costs, provided the capped process leaks to a hit with probability $\le1/2$:

* At singleton primes use expected cost $\mathbb E[-\log(1-p_r)1_{\rm light}]\le2\mathbb E p_r$ by pointwise density comparison.
* On a block $s<\log r\le2s$, $d=\lfloor\lambda/s\rfloor\ge1$, use $d\{C+\log(1+\mathbb E\sum p_r/d)\}+O(\log(2d))$. Indeed a level test sees at most $d$ coordinates per term. `kcomp` on the conditional product of the block gives `kmean` with the conditional expected replaced mass, then Jensen removes the past. The auxiliary paths have the very same capped marginal law. Beyond $\log r>\lambda$ the test is constant in those digits (zero cost). One may truncate a block at either end. `wseq` composes these comparisons by conditioning nonnegative tests, followed by Jensen on the surviving paths. No assertion of independence of the capped digits is needed.

The passage to the probability law is exactly the finite LP dual of minimizing $\mathbb E_U f$ with these majorant constraints, on the span of the level cylinders (z80 `BEYOND_34.md` §16 LM). Normalization is the constraint at the constant. Thus it suffices to bound these costs. This is a new *gap-specific supply estimate* in the old comparison theorem, not a new LP theorem.

**Lemma S (PROVED; inflation and smooth supply).** Uniformly in finite subfamilies, the sum of $\mathbb E p_r$ through $w<r\le y$ is $\ll_w (\log(2y))^3$. The probability of a heavy hit is $\le1/2$ for an absolute choice of $w$, uniformly even without the gap cutoff. These claims also hold if the fresh categorical law is integer rather than unit, with the same upper inflation bounds on pins.

*First moment.* A class of modulus $G$ contributes at most $\Gamma(G)/G$: charge its other digits by inflation and the fresh decision by ordinary unit density. For clause 2 there are at most two orders per triple $a,b,q$ with $G=4abq$; if $P(G)\le y$ all three labels are smooth. The Euler sum $\sum_{P(h)\le y}\Gamma(h)/h\ll_w\log(2y)$ gives the cube. Here $P(1)=1$. For clause 1 the number of labels per coprime pair is $\le\tau(a+b)$. We show the bound
$$\sum_{a\le b,\ (a,b)=1,\ P(ab)\le y}\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)\tau(a+b)}{ab}\ll_w (\log(2y))^3.\tag{1}$$
For $b\in[V,2V)$, $a\le2V$, put $T_a(h)=\sum_{b\in[V,2V),\,h\mid b,(a,b)=1}\tau(a+b)$. If $(a,h)>1$ it vanishes. Otherwise the divisor hyperbola through $2\sqrt V$ gives $T_a(h)\ll (V/h)\log(2V)+\sqrt V$ (the small divisor is coprime to $h$); for $h\le\sqrt V$ this is $\ll (V/h)\log(2V)$. At all $h$ use $T_a(h)\ll_\epsilon V^{1+\epsilon}/h$ since $h\le2V$ or the count vanishes.

For clarity, the smooth tail of (1) costs **no** $\log\log y$. Write $\Gamma=1*g$ with $g$ squarefree supported, $g(r)=\gamma_r-1\ge0$. For $D\le V^{1/16}$ with $\omega(D)\le4$, split $h$ at $V^{1/4}$ to get
$$\sum_h g(h)T_a([D,h])\ll_w (V/D)\,[\Gamma(D)\log(2V)+1].\tag{2}$$
On the short range $[D,h]\le V^{5/16}<\sqrt V$, so the $\sqrt V$ error is absorbed term by term into $V/[D,h]$, before weighting. Now $\sum_h g(h)(D,h)/h\ll_w\Gamma(D)$ by the Euler product. On $h>V^{1/4}$ Rankin at $1/4$ gives $\ll_w V^{-1/16}$ in the same sum restricted to the tail: at each of the at most four exceptional primes the new factor is $1+g(r)r^{1/4}=O_w(1)$, and $\sum_r g(r)/r^{3/4}<\infty$. Use the trivial $T_a$ bound with $\epsilon<1/16$. At $D=1$ this already gives the untrimmed dyad estimate.

For $V\ge y^{64}$ use the local weight $Z_y(b)=\sum_{r^j\mid b,\ r^j\le y}\log r/\log y$ of `sieve-limits-note`, Lemmas `localweight`,`Zmoments`. Either the product of the prime-power components $r^{v_r(b)}>y$ of a smooth $b$ exceeds $\sqrt V$ and is squarefull, or $Z_y(b)\ge (\log V)/(2\log y)$. The first case contributes $\ll V^{4/5}$ to the weighted dyad by $\Gamma(b)\tau(a+b)\ll_\epsilon V^\epsilon$ and $\sum_{h>\sqrt V,\ h\ \mathrm{squarefull}}1/h\ll V^{-1/4}$ (write $h=d^2t^3$, $t$ squarefree). In the second, expand $Z_y^4$; its four prime powers have lcm $D\le y^4\le V^{1/16}$. Estimate (2) applies. The sum of $\Gamma(D)/D$ with the four log weights divided by $(\log y)^4$ is $O_w(1)$: group equal prime bases and sum $1/r^{\max j}$ over their exponents, $O(1/r)$ for each group; then use Mertens' prime-log sum. This gives weighted dyad $\ll_w V\log(2V)\min(1,(\log y/\log V)^4)$ at large $V$ (absorbing $V^{4/5}$); below $y^{64}$ use (2) at $D=1$. The sum divided by $V$ over powers of two is $O_w((\log(2y))^2)$, uniformly in the smaller $a$ (or sum over $a$ first at each dyad). Its remaining harmonic smooth Euler sum supplies one more logarithm. This proves (1).

*Heavy leak.* Here a logarithm of $y$ to an unspecified absolute power suffices, but control in **$w$** matters. For $G=k r^v$, $P(k)<r$, write $m_v(k)$ for the number of classes counted with multiplicity. The cofactor calculation of `EXCEPTIONAL_KARY2.md` Lemma 4.1 uses only inflation: the number $N_v$ of active cofactors before the $r$ digit has
$$\mathbb E N_v^2\le C(\log(2w))^C\log(2r)\sum_{P(k)<r}m_v(k)^2\tau(k)\Gamma(k)/k.$$
In fact two cofactor pins cost at most $\Gamma([k,k'])/[k,k']$; symmetrize $m_v(k)m_v(k')$ by the squares and split $k'$ by $(k,k')\mid k$. The remaining smooth Euler sum costs the prefactor. More generally, for fixed $c,j$, $\sum_{P(k)\le r}\tau(k)^c\Gamma(k)^j k^{-1+1/\log r}\ll (\log(2w))^C(\log(2r))^C$ for $r>w\ge 16$: expand the Euler product, terms at powers $\ge2$ bounded in total at large primes, and $\sum_{p\le r}(p^{1/\log r}-1)/p=O(1)$ by the prime-log sum. Small-base Euler factors at $p\le w$ increase the log product by $O(\sum_{p\le w}1/p)+O(1)$, not $O(w)$.

For clause 2, $m_v(k)\ll\tau_3(kr^v)\le (v+1)^2\tau(k)^2$ up to an absolute factor (divide out 4 first). For clause 1 abbreviate the prime power by $P_v=r^v$. Split it off from the one label containing $r$. Thus $k=4hk'$, and by Cauchy $m_v(k)^2\ll\tau(k)\sum_{4hk'=k}\tau(P_v h+k')^2$. In a dyad $T\le k<2T$ with $T\ge 64P_v^4$, Cauchy once more bounds the cofactor sum on this dyad by
$$ C(\log(2w))^C(\log(2r))^C e^{-\log T/(2\log r)}\left(\frac1T\sum_{hk'\asymp T,\ (k',r)=1}\tau(P_v h+k')^4\right)^{1/2}.$$
Here the first factor used the smooth tilted Euler sum just proved, absorbing the divisor number of splits; in the second factor retain $(k',r)=1$. The parenthesis is $\ll (\log(2P_v T))^C$. Indeed dyadically split $h,k'$ with larger variable of size $V\gg\sqrt T\gg P_v$. With the smaller fixed, the linear form in the larger is $\ll V^3$ and has at most one root modulo every divisor (for slope $P_v$ use $(k',P_v)=1$; incompatible divisors have no roots). For an integer $m\ll V^3$, strip the $O(1)$ prime factors $>V$ with multiplicities, then greedily group remaining prime factors into $O(1)$ products $b_i\le V$: two consecutive completed blocks have product $>V$. Consequently $\tau(m)^4\ll\sum_{d\mid m,d\le V}\tau(d)^C$ by taking a largest divisor-count block. Counting solutions costs $O(V/d)$ per $d$, so the average is polylogarithmic by the divisor Euler product. This is the elementary argument of z78 `WILD_ES.md` §7, applied to this linear form. For $T<64P_v^4$ the pointwise divisor bound and $\Gamma(k)\le8\cdot 4^{\omega(k)}$ give $m_v(k)^2\tau(k)\Gamma(k)\ll P_v^{1/8}$ (enlarging the absolute constant); summing $1/k$ costs $O(\log(2P_v))$. Summing the long dyads against the displayed exponential, then using the short range, bounds $\mathbb E N_v^2$ by $C(\log(2w))^C(v+1)^C(\log(2r))^C r^{v/4}$. No growth parameter is hidden in a divisor-moment constant.

Finally $p_r\le 2\sum_{v\ge1} r^{-v}N_v$, since a fresh unit $r^v$-digit has density $1/\varphi(r^v)$. Minkowski gives $\mathbb E p_r^2\ll (\log(2w))^C (\log(2r))^C r^{-7/4}$. A union bound for heavy hits costs at most $\sum_{r>w}r^{1/2}\mathbb E p_r^2=o_{w\to\infty}(1)$. Fix $w$ now. This proves S. Its cubic estimate alone gives the second row of V: singletons up to log size $\lambda^{1/4}$, then dyadic blocks at $s=2^i\lambda^{1/4}$ with cost $O((\lambda/s)(1+i)+\log(2\lambda))$ each. ## 3. The cost of a *distinct* completing digit

**Lemma P (PROVED rel. Brun–Titchmarsh, Mertens and §2).** Take $R=K^{20}>w$ (eventually), $s\ge\log R$, $s<\log r\le2s$. Write $H=\log(2s/\log R)$. On the path law defined below the expected sum of fresh completing probabilities in this block is at most
$$C_h\sum_{q\le K}\varphi(q)^{-h}\,(2s)^{3^h-1}\,\mathcal B_h(H)+O(K e^{-s}),\qquad 0<h\le1,\tag{3}$$
where the damping factor $\mathcal B_h=1$ always works with a constant bounded for $1/2\le h\le1$; for each **fixed** $0<h<1$ one can take $\mathcal B_h(H)=(1+H)^{-1/2}$. The same upper bound holds if a block is clipped but estimated using its full upper endpoint (including a first block crossing $\log R$ with $2s>\log R$ and $s\ge\frac12\log R$). Crucially the parameter exponent is $3^h-1$, **not** $2h$.

*Higher powers.* Add to the forbidden rules all square marks $r^2\mid n+q$ at the relevant primes $r>R$ and $q\le K$, $q=3\bmod4$. We may enlarge the finite period, including the $q$ digits and any required precision at earlier primes. A majorant of the original void majorizes the enlarged family's void too. At large $r$ even the enlarged union requires $n=-q\pmod r$ for some $q$, so $p_r\le K/(r-1)<r^{-1/2}$; the added clauses cost at most $\sum_{r>e^s}2K/r^2\ll K e^{-s}$ in expected mass. No large-prime square mark occurs along the resulting path. At $r\le R$ nothing changes and S still controls the leak. Primes irrelevant to the family can be adjoined or omitted throughout.

*Seed.* For a specified $q$, after processing all $t\le R$ let $T_q=\prod_{t\le R}(2v_t+1)$, where $v_t$ is the valuation of $(n+q)/4$ truncated at the maximum needed for the finite family; ignore primes dividing $q$. There are at most $T_q$ unit ratios from these digits. For clause 2 include the two possible translates using $n^{\pm1}\bmod q$: that digit is already known. This costs only a factor three in target count, even if the translates are correlated with the seed. For $0<h\le1$,
$$\mathbb E T_q^h\ll (\log R)^{3^h-1} .\tag{4}$$
Given earlier digits, $\Pr(v_t\ge i)\le\gamma_t/t^i$, also at 2 with its absolute base constant (use the class mod $2^{i+2}$). Sum the increments $(2i+1)^h-(2i-1)^h$ against these tails. The first increment is $3^h-1$; the remaining terms are $O(t^{-2})$ uniformly in $h$. Multiply conditional upper bounds for the factors and use $\sum_{t\le R}1/t=\log\log R+O(1)$ and $\gamma_t=1+O(t^{-1/2})$ above the fixed base. No independence of the actual shifted small factors is being imposed.

*One-window domination.* Conditional on the entire small history, in any **one** window the subset of rough primes $R<t<r$ with $t\mid n+q$ is stochastically dominated by independent Bernoullis of parameters $b_t=\gamma_t/t$ (use independent uniforms at successive steps and sample the remaining digit conditionally on its mark). This holds even though the transition sees other windows too. Extend these independent bits through $t\le e^{2s}$, obtaining one common random upper list for testing each possible $r$ in this block. This is legitimate for the increasing event that the old directions, with arbitrary zero exponents, can correct $r$ or $r^{-1}$. For fixed upper list, of length $J$, the specification at $r$ is $n=-q\pmod r$ **and** $r$ in at most $6T_q3^J$ unit classes mod $q$ (supply both signs of the top letter; the factor two from inversion may overcount, since the small/old ratios and the three translations are already inverse-stable). Indeed in a completion with top $r$ to exponent one the other factors are all earlier; large square marks never survive. Current higher powers can be charged to the added square test. The enlarged list may include $r$ itself; the upper list is sampled before summing in the deterministic index $r$ and is the *same distribution for every index*. We do not condition the actual past on the fresh digit or require any law on all windows jointly to be independent.

For this fixed list the harmonic sum of candidate $r$ is $\ll\min(1,6T_q3^J/\varphi(q))$. Use Montgomery–Vaughan, *The large sieve*, Mathematika 20 (1973), Brun–Titchmarsh bound $\pi(x;k,a)\le 2x/(\varphi(k)\log(x/k))$ for $(a,k)=1$, $x>k$, followed by partial summation on $\max(R,e^s)<r\le e^{2s}$; $\log(r/q)\gg\log r$. The cap by 1 here means an absolute constant times 1, by Mertens. The $1/(r-1)$ fresh unit probability costs only a constant versus $1/r$. Bounding the number of **distinct** digit values, not the number of successful words, is what permits the minimum inside this calculation.

*Anti-concentration after tilting.* Put $z=3^h$ and $M(z)=\mathbb E z^J\ll \exp((z-1)H)$ by Mertens, uniformly for $1\le z\le3$. For $A>0$, $\min(1,A3^J)\le A^hz^J$. If $0<h<1$ multiply the latter by $\exp[-(\log3)\min(h,1-h)|J+\log A/\log3|]$ and the inequality still holds. Under the tilt $z^J/M(z)$ the bits are independent of parameters $z b_t/(1+(z-1)b_t)$, with variance sum $\asymp H$ when $H$ is large. Here include **all** primes $R<t\le e^{2s}$ in the dominating list, not just primes dividing a particular period. Fourier inversion for their sum gives maximal atom $O(1/\sqrt{1+H})$: the characteristic function has modulus at most $\exp(-2\sin^2(\xi/2)\sum b'_t(1-b'_t))$, $b'_t$ the tilted parameters. Sum the geometric damping over integer $J$, uniformly in the real shift $\log A/\log3$. Apply this conditionally at $A=6T_q/\varphi(q)$ and then use (4). This proves (3). This anti-concentration concerns a dominating one-window **comparison**, not the distribution of a conditional number of arithmetic hits.

## 4. Putting the costs together

**PROVED (completion of V).** Write $B=\log K$. For $0<h<1$, $\sum_{q\le K}1/\varphi(q)^h\ll K^{1-h}/(1-h)$, uniformly e.g. for $1/2\le h<1$, and $\ll\log(2K)$ at $h=1$. Indeed $q/\varphi(q)=\sum_{d\mid q}\mu^2(d)/\varphi(d)$ bounds its $h$-th power, and $\sum 1/(d\varphi(d))<\infty$. In the following choices use singletons up to log size $s_*$, and the §2 local comparisons on blocks $2^i s_*<\log r\le2^{i+1}s_*$. ## 6. Pair cells in the Lenstra rectangle

**Theorem D (PROVED rel. Lenstra and z80's fixed-order Haar estimates).** In the z82 §9.3 box, all three natural periods $4ad-1,4bd-1,4ab$ exceed $p^{.58}$ for sufficiently large $p$ (strict inequalities as given there); the trial periods even exceed $p^{.63}$. Keeping periods $\le \exp(C L^\gamma)$, fixed $0<\gamma<1$, $C>0$, selects **nothing** from this box. This is not the small-trial count with mean $\asymp L^{2\gamma}$.

More quantitatively, for $X<n\le2X$ take actual trial labels $i=(a,d)$ in the fixed rectangle
$$J:\quad X^{.316}\le a<X^{.316+\eta_J},\qquad X^{.36}\le d<X^{.36+\eta_J},\qquad \eta_J=.0001.$$
Use all those labels and no parity restriction, $q_i=4ad-1$, hit at $n=-a\pmod{q_i}$, $Z(n)$ their count. A hit (write $1_i$ for its indicator) recovers $b=(n+a)/q_i$ with exponent $.324+O(2\eta_J+1/L)$, so lies strictly inside the Lenstra box, off the outer tie. Let $H_{ij}$ be the **unit-Haar** joint hit probability, $i\ne j$, and $S_X$ the set of ordered distinct pairs realized by at least one integer of the dyad. Then for some absolute $c>0$,
$$\sum_{(i,j)\in S_X}H_{ij}\ll X^{-c},\qquad \sum_{i\ne j}H_{ij}=m_J^2+O(X^{-c}),\qquad m_J\asymp L^2.\tag{5}$$
Here $m_J$ can be the exact Haar mean. Thus the normalized Haar pair-intensity (probability on pairs proportional to $H_{ij}$) gives probability $O(X^{-c})$ to the entire support of any empirical dyad pair-intensity. If the latter is normalized and nonempty the total variation distance tends to one, not zero. The *sum* of a signed difference can nevertheless be small.

*Proof.* The second assertion is the **Haar** lemma and empty-base mean in z80 `R_BELOW_619.md` §4.1, with fixed order two and $\eta_J<.316/100$, not its prime transfer. The large-period Haar application is explicitly described in its §5.3. For small period gcd $g=(q_i,q_j)\le X^{.01}$ one has $H_{ij}\le1/\varphi([q_i,q_j])\le X^{-1.352+.01+o(1)}$. There are at most $\sum_n Z(n)^2\ll X^{4/3+o(1)}$ realized pairs: the first moment over integers is $\sum_n Z(n)\ll X L^2+X^{.676+2\eta_J}\ll XL^2$, by summing $X/q_i+1$. For each $n$ Lenstra, *Divisors in residue classes*, Math. Comp. 42 (1984), Prop. (3.1), bounds the number of divisors of $m>0$ in $r\bmod s$ by $C_\eta$ when $(r,s)=1$, $s>m^{1/4+\eta}$. Here $m=n+a$, $s=4a$, $r=-1$, e.g. $\eta=.01$. So $Z(n)\ll X^{.316+\eta_J}\ll X^{1/3}$. All bounds concern positive $n$ in the interval, not whole-period rows. This gives a power saving since $4/3+.01<1.352$.

For the large gcds fix $i$ and sum the conditional probabilities of all compatible $j\ne i$ at $g>X^{.01}$. On dyads $a'\asymp A,d'\asymp D$ they are each at most $X^{o(1)}g/(AD)$. Given $g\mid q_i$, $a'=a\pmod g$, the condition $g\mid4a'd'-1$ fixes $d'=d\pmod g$. The product of the two progression counts therefore gives conditional mass $\ll X^{o(1)}(1/g+1/A+1/D+g/(AD))$. If $a'\ne a$, $g\mid a'-a\ne0$ so $g\le2X^{.316+\eta_J}$. Otherwise $d'\ne d$ and $g\le2X^{.36+\eta_J}$. Every term saves a fixed power, even after summing over the divisor number of $g$, the dyads and then the harmonic mass of the first pin. This is the one-pin determinant cut, not a growing-order bound. Together these estimates give (5). For a concrete fully coprime pair in z82's larger patch take $X=t^{25}$, $a=t^8$, $m=t$, $d=(a+1)m+1$, $(a',d')=(a+1,d-m)$. Then $q'=q+4$, $(q,q')=1$. A joint hit requires $(b-b')q=4b'-1$ with $0<4b'-1<q$ for large $t$. Thus the intersection is empty on integers in the dyad, although it has strictly positive Haar probability. ∎

**A separating statistic (PROVED on integers, CONDITIONAL on Elliott–Halberstam on primes).** Set $T_X=\sum_{(i,j)\in S_X}1_i1_j$, a nonnegative polynomial with each modulus $\le X^{1.36}$. ## 7. What subpower moments do give

**Theorem M (PROVED rel. the two z75 quadratic supplies (2), based on BV/BT, and elementary interval counting).** Let $0<\gamma<1$ be fixed, $t=L^\gamma$, $L=\log X$. Among primes of **any** interval of $X$ consecutive positive integers all but $O_\gamma(X\exp(-c_\gamma F))$ have at least $ct^2$ (★) words of **each** $d$-parity, with distinct pivot primes in each collection and periods $\le\exp(Ct)$. $$ F=\begin{cases}L^{2\gamma},&\gamma\le1/3,\\L^{1-\gamma}\log L,&\gamma>1/3.\end{cases}$$
Constants $c,C>0$ in the threshold/height are absolute for the two fixed parities; the onset and exception constants may depend on $\gamma$. These are *unbalanced* words. This trades the size of the guaranteed count against the exceptional rate; at $\gamma=1/3$ this is the previous z75 bound. It is not a positive fraction of the full quadratic-log center.

*Center on the fibre, but do not transfer the fibre.* Here $u$ denotes the first word coordinate, unrelated to the real phase exponent of V. In z75 `ANALYTIC_PUSH2.md` §§1–2 use their auxiliary search scale $Y=e^t$, all odd multipliers $k\le Y^{1/480}$, their atoms (1) at $(a_2,b_2)=(1,2)$ or $(0,3)$ for the valuations of the word coordinates. A hit requires $n=-u\pmod{kr}$ and has $d=(kr+1)/(4u)$ of the indicated parity. We use separately the two finite families, of size $\exp(O(t))$. In the integer Haar model repair **all** nonunit multiplier prime digits to 1 at full precision, leaving unit digits alone. Write $A_i(n)$ for activation of atom $i$ at its multiplier, i.e. the repaired $n=-u\pmod k$. This depends on $n\bmod k$ only. On primes larger than $Y$ the tests are the genuine tests. Given the small digits, hits at each $r$ are disjoint (the different $u<r$ and the modulus/size cut on $v$ ensure the distinctness in z75 §1) and counts at different $r$ are independent Bernoullis on integer Haar. $$ Z=\sum_i A_i(n)1_{n=-u_i\ (r_i)},\qquad m(n)=\sum_i A_i(n)/r_i$$
have the *exact* conditional-mean relation and $c_1t^2\le m(n)\le C_1t^2$ uniformly by z75 (2) on the repaired all-unit pin. ## 8. What uniform prime-tuples hypotheses actually test

**Assessment (source audit, not equivalences between conjectures).** Here $\mathfrak S(\mathcal H)=\prod_p (1-\nu_p(\mathcal H)/p)(1-1/p)^{-k}$, $\nu_p$ the number of occupied residues, $k=|\mathcal H|$, and $I_k(x)=\int_2^x(\log v)^{-k}\,dv$. All sets in these statements have distinct offsets. Admissible means $\nu_p<p$ for every prime. Distinguish (a) true primality of the **entire** translate, (b) the large prime factors of a *composite* translate, and (c) a probabilistic model's prime indicator. The following ranges are for (a) except where expressly indicated:

* Montgomery–Soundararajan, *Primes in short intervals*, [Thm 3, (20)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409258), assumes $|\sum_{n\le x}\prod_{d\in\mathcal H}\Lambda(n+d)-x\mathfrak S(\mathcal H)|\ll N^{1/2+\epsilon}$ uniformly for $k\le K$, $0\le x\le N$, offsets in $[1,H]$. The moment conclusion is uniform in $\log N\le H\le N^{1/K}$ **at fixed $K$**; Cor. 1 assumes (20) for arbitrarily large fixed orders. Gallagher, *On the distribution of primes in short intervals*, Mathematika 23 (1976), Thm 1, uses the tuples asymptotic for fixed orders with shifts in $[0,\lambda\log x]$ for fixed $\lambda$ to obtain Poisson statistics (see also Banks–Ford–Tao §1.6); neither result grants a uniform implied constant through $L^\eta$.
* Kuperberg, *Sums of singular series with large sets and the tail of the distribution of primes*, [Conjecture 1.3 (7)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.09775): some absolute $C,\epsilon>0$, every admissible $\mathcal H\subset[0,(\log x)^2]$, $k\le(\log\log x)^3$, prime-indicator count up to $x$ within $Cx^{1-\epsilon}$ of $\mathfrak S(\mathcal H) I_k(x)$. Tao, *The convergence of an alternating series of Erdős*, [Conjecture 1.3 (1.1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.07205), instead takes $x\ge10$, $k\le(\log\log x)^5$, same offset bound, including inadmissible sets. * A much stronger actual *literature hypothesis* is Banks–Ford–Tao, *Large prime gaps and probabilistic models*, Invent. ## 9. The lossless two-thirds word ceiling, including the colours

**Theorem W (PROVED rel. §§2 and 9, quadratic reciprocity and the z75 supply).** On uniform units $n=1\pmod{24}$, consider any finite mixture of the following cylinder charts of the word $n=txv-x-v$. For the endpoint $tx-1>0$ and for the pair $xv>0$. ## 11. Restricted normal order; limits of the uniform period budgets

**PROVED (rel. z80 `R_BELOW_619.md` §§3–5.5; no new dispersion estimate).** For $(u,v,w)=(\log a,\log b,\log d)/L$ normalized onto $u+v+w=1$ (an $O(1/L)$ displacement on the dyad), a trial or pair chart omits one letter.
