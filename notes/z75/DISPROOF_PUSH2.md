# Round 2: a sharp sterile pencil, bounded-height descent and targeted searches

## 1. Non-BM failures at interior heights of the Type II pencil

Let Y_{n,c} be the **fixed-c fibre as an integral model**: variables a,b,d, equation 4abcd=a+b+nc with n,c fixed and positive, and positive real component. Its rational fibre is a smooth (★) surface, *not* a torus curve. For n odd, Z58_STRUCT2 Prop F computes the whole positive-integral BM test: it passes iff the squarefree kernel of n does not divide c. Indeed at 4545, Prop F already gives infinitely many failures, for every 505∤c. Mere infinitude on fibres is therefore not the novelty here, nor is an external priority claim being made for a Type II size estimate. The following cutoff instead applies at **growing n and c of order n^{2/3}**, where the usual bound c≤n does not exclude a point.

**Theorem 1 (sharp cutoff on quotient strips).** Fix j≥16. Among all n,c>0 with c=⌊n/j⌋ for which Y_{n,c} has a positive integral point, the **maximum** c is

    M(j)=floor((4a₀²+j)/δ),  δ=4−(j mod 4) ∈ {1,2,3,4},  a₀=(j+δ)/4.

*Proof.* Write n=jc+r with 0≤r<j. Sort a≤b and let e=4abd−n=(a+b)/c≥1. Substituting b=ce−a gives

    (T−j)c=B+r,       T=4ade,     B=4a²d+e.

Thus h=T−j≥δ. Also 2a≤ce gives 4a²d≤Tc/2 and e≤T/4, whence T≤(4jc+4r)/(2c−1). Put w=T/4=ade. Then B=4a(w/e)+e≤4(w/e)²+e≤4w²+1: for e=1 this is immediate; for e≥2 use e≤w, w≥2. Equality in the maximum is realized with d=e=1, a=w.

If c≤j+1 we are done: M(j)≥j+1 since (j+δ)²/4+j≥δ(j+1) for j≥16. Otherwise c≥j+2 gives T≤2j+3, hence h≤j+3. We get c≤F(h)=j/2+h/4+(j²+4j)/(4h) using r≤j−1. For δ≤h≤j+3,

    F(δ)−F(h)=(h−δ)/4 · ((j²+4j)/(hδ)−1) ≥ 0.

This proves the bound. Conversely take e=d=1, a=a₀, c=M(j), b=c−a, r=δc−4a²−1. The floor gives j−δ≤r≤j−1 and c≥2a, so the tuple is positive, solves the equation and lies on the strip. ∎

**Corollary (explicit cubic family).** For every sufficiently large positive t, put n=4t³−3, c=t²+t+1. Then Y_{n,c} has **no positive integral point**. If n is nonsquare, it nevertheless has a **nonempty positive-integral Brauer–Manin set**. Indeed j=4(t−1)≥16, n=jc+1, and M(j)=(t−1)²+3(t−1)+1=c−2. Coprimality (n,c)=1 gives the BM assertion. There are infinitely many *distinct in-𝔐 composite n*: take t≡1 mod 840 and t≡4 mod 121, so n≡1 mod 840 and n≡11 mod 121. Or simply take t≡0 mod 6 for nonsquare composites n≡5 mod 8 divisible by 3. The integral fibres vary, even though the generic rational-surface type is fixed; unlike Markoff orbit failures, this mechanism is a sign-and-height inequality. It does **not** imply R(n)=0 or N_II(n)=0.

**Prime inputs too.** For any sufficiently large prime p and j=⌊p^{1/3}⌋, set c=⌊p/j⌋. We have c≥j² while M(j)≤(j+4)²/4+j<j², and 1≤c<p. Hence Y_{p,c} fails modulo BM in the same sense (BM set nonempty but no global positive integer point). No prime-values conjecture about 4t³−3 is being invoked. Ordinary étale-BM on these smooth fibres adds nothing by Z58_STRUCT Thm A (the rational S-surface is geometrically simply connected). This does not concern the coordinate-deletion open.

## 2. Exact support and height cost of the moving-denominator repair

Use D, the adelic domain of DISPROOF_OBSTRUCT §1, not integral points of the coordinate-deletion model. Write Q_x=∏ℓ ℓ^{vℓ(xℓ)} for each original coordinate and H_D=max_x Q_x. This is a **finite-valuation height**, invariant under moving the real coordinates in the positive component, not the height of an arbitrary rational approximation. Consider first precisely the patch family of Round 1: k=4 or 8; q≡−1 mod k prime with q∤n(n+1); m≥2 even. Take (u,B,C)=(1,(n+1)/q,(q+1)/k) except at q, and at q take B′=q^m B, C′=C, u′=(n+B′)/(kB′C−1). “Support” in this paragraph means the allowed *repair places*, not the primes of the Q_x.

**Theorem 2.** This patch preserves the three coordinate classes modulo m-th powers iff

    (−n)^((q−1)/gcd(m,q−1)) ≡ 1 mod q   and
    v_q(n^{q−1}−1) ≥ 1+v_q(m).

In particular, for n>1 and a preassigned finite set of repair places there exists a single m killing every patch from that set, whereas for the entire 2-power tower the eligible q's are exactly those with (−n/q)=+1, at **every individual positive level**. The minimum H_D in this family is obtained at the least eligible q (restricted to the permitted set, if specified). Its value on S and embedded X is (n+1)q^{m−1}; on the image in U it is n(n+1)q^{m−1}(q+1)/k.

*Proof.* At q, u′/(−n)≡1 mod q^{m−1}, and m−1≥1+v_q(m) for odd q and m even ≥2. Thus the ratio is an m-th power by the principal unit logarithm. The other two ratios are already powers. The claimed test for −n uses ℤ_q^×=μ_{q−1}×(1+qℤ_q); raising a principal unit to the (q−1)-st power preserves its depth. For finite candidate q choose m divisible by q^{v_q(n^{q−1}−1)} for each; on the other hand v₂(q−1)=1, giving the assertion for powers of 2. At all points in this family Q_u=1, Q_B=(n+1)q^{m−1}, Q_C=(q+1)/k; for X the extra c=1, for U take coordinates uBC,nuC,nBC as in Round 1. These coordinates' heights are increasing functions of q. ∎

**General height truncation, not limited to this repair.** For any of the three models V and any H, testing the coordinate characters of conductor dividing 2^j suffices to force a genuine positive integer point of height ≤H as soon as 2^j exceeds an upper bound for |F(Q)| on [1,H]^r (F is the displayed integral equation). In fact these characters give x₂/Q_x≡1 mod 2^j for every coordinate as in Round 1. Thus F(Q)≡0 mod 2^j, forcing F(Q)=0. This is a sufficient j, **not** claimed minimal. At a genuine integral point the diagonal passes every exponent with fixed H_D. In contrast, for each failed global equation the minimum passing height for m=j! diverges by the same argument (all characters of conductor 2^k have order dividing j! for sufficiently large j).

**Exact minimum over *all points of D* passing the exponent-2 test.** This is computable much more sharply than Theorem 2's cost. Fix the vector of candidate positive norms Q_i≤H. The order-2 coordinate characters all vanish on pairing iff x_ℓ/Q_i is a square unit at each finite ℓ: the Artin image of the normalized idèle is in ∏ℓ ℤ_ℓ^×, whose quadratic characters separate the quotient by squares (the class-field-theory argument of Z58_STRUCT2 Prop T; characters supported in the conductor at one prime suffice). If so, all quaternion pair classes of the open also pass by the global product formula, since the coordinate classes come from the same rational Q_i and the positive real component. Conversely, the actual coordinate units generate the open unit lattice modulo constants (use the maps of Round 1 §2). By the explicit character and pair-symbol generators of **DISPROOF_OBSTRUCT §2, Proposition** for W and W×𝔾ₘ, there are no further nonconstant classes to test. Hence this tests **all Br(V)[2]**, and equivalently rational lifting to the simultaneous square-root torsor. We require x_ℓ=Q_i y_i² with y_i a local unit; this is essential, not just membership in Q_i ℚ_ℓ^{×2} without fixing valuations.

After unit-monomial changes the local equation is A x²+B y²+C z²=D with x,y,z units. For S with original norms a,b,c the coefficients are (−a,−b,kabc;n). For X with norms a,b,c,d divide the equation by the square unit of the c coordinate: coefficients (−a,−b,kabcd;nc). The three unit variables can be chosen freely (on S choose y_u,y_B,y_u y_B y_C; on X choose y_a/y_c,y_b/y_c,y_a y_b y_d). For U with denominator norms g,a,b, use reciprocals of unit variables and clear gab, giving (nab,nbg,nag;kgab). At infinity original positive local points always exist and positive ratios are squares. The resulting conditions do give a restricted-product adelic point: at primes not dividing any Q_i all coordinates are units.

Divide the four coefficients by their gcd. At 2 it is necessary and sufficient that A+B+C≡D mod 8 and at least one coefficient on the left be odd: set other variables to 1 and use the odd-unit square criterion. At odd p check nonzero squares mod p for the three variables; lift by Hensel at any nonzero coefficient (if all vanish, primitivity precludes a solution). For p≥7 dividing none of ABC there is nothing to check: the sum of three scalar multiples of the nonzero squares fills 𝔽_p by Cauchy–Davenport, 3(p−1)/2−2≥p. Thus only the primes of 30ABC are needed. This is also an exact finite test for specified prime support of the Q_i at bounded H. `scripts/z75_push2_height.py` optimizes H by exhaustive search over norm tuples. For U_n with n odd prime not dividing k, the equation mod n forces at least one denominator divisible by n, so H≥n; reducing the tuple search by this bound is exact. The Python enumeration additionally uses, at q∤n, that the maximal valuation of the ES denominators cannot occur only once unless all are units: otherwise the reciprocal sum has negative valuation. This restricts possible third norms for each pair, without imposing global ES solvability. `scripts/z75_push2_checks.c` independently enumerates every tuple through the claimed optimum without this restriction, using the binary-conic count for two active coefficients at p≥7. No general optimization over twists for arbitrary m>2 is asserted.

## 3. Certified computations: unrestricted targets

**(MS⁺₁₀₅) to 10¹¹**, combining the prior z58 LOGIC census through 10¹⁰ and the following independent two-engine check on the next decade. Every nonsquare n≡1 mod 4, (n,105)=1 in (10¹⁰,10¹¹] has R(n)>0. The first C engine covers 9,642,857,144 non-𝔐 inputs and 642,807,720 in 𝔐, using arithmetic-progressions of known positive witnesses (not density extrapolation). The first modulus d≡3 mod 4 with r=d−n mod d (meaning d−(n mod d)), r|(d+1)/4 gives u=r, B=(n+u)/d, C=(d+1)/(4u). The engine crosses such classes after solving the linear index congruence and also crosses pair hits 4uB|n+u+B. Unresolved lines would **not** be zeros; there were none. Independent C engine uses the literal one-modulus predicate on every residue in a period, no pair sieve/CRT. n≡5 mod 12 has u=C=1; other admissible n are ≡1 mod 12. Its 1,981,452 unsquared residual candidates all had exact positive witnesses, largest f=37,105,343. Thus this result does not rely on the previously much larger 𝔐-only domain. It also implies no 15th prime R-zero in this added decade; independent prime-only data are in `data/z75/push2prime/`.

**Dead factors.** On 11·13·17|n, the full (MS⁺₁₀₅) domain was tested to 10¹² starting above 10¹⁰ (43,632,838 outside 𝔐, 2,908,771 inside). All had R hits. These sparse runs use the first engine only.

**U₇ on all odd nonsquares, both 1 and 3 mod 4.** Positive throughout (10¹⁰,2·10¹⁰]: 4,928,560,185 non-𝔐 and 71,419,104 in 𝔐. Word modulus f≡7 mod 8 and u|f+1, n≡−u mod f suffice; u may equal f+1. On **all odd nonsquare multiples of 105** we additionally tested (10¹⁰,5·10¹⁰], 190,475,601 inputs, plus the 4545-multiple and other panels in `composite.txt`. The deepest 105-multiple survivor n=43,749,882,435 had f=45,430,823 and u=114, hence (t,u,v)=((f+1)/114,114,(n+114)/f); the dead moduli do not kill this rescue. We did not need to assume every deep U_II survivor was tested by a full Type II count.
