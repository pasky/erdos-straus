## 1. The dyadic obstruction: changing the upper prime to a composite does not help

Let `D` be the signed ring of any IOpen model and `J=⋂_{n≥1}2ⁿD` (external intersection, **standard** n). Division by standard numerals gives `D/2ⁿD=ℤ/2ⁿ`; hence `J` is the prime kernel of the canonical `D→ℤ₂`. If `±h=Σ₁⁴u_i²` for `h∈J`, all `u_i∈J`: map the equation into ℚ₂, where four squares are anisotropic. Indeed a primitive zero mod 8 with an odd coordinate is impossible: mod 4 it must have four odds, giving 4 mod 8. Equivalently divide all the coordinates by 2 and repeat at each standard depth. With Lag this proves **`J²=J`** (change the sign of each target, not of the quadratic form, if needed). BCM with an infinite cutoff gives `J≠0`. Finite generation is not needed for the equality; indeed a nonzero proper finitely generated idempotent ideal in a domain is impossible, by localizing at a containing maximal ideal and Nakayama. More generally at **any** multiplicative set disjoint from `J`, its extension is still proper prime idempotent and, if nonzero, not finitely generated. This is a restriction on all such models, not an implication to ES.

In particular if `D` end-extends the external IOpen PID `D₀` of the five-square recipe and contains a nonzero multiple of all old positives, take `U=D₀∖{0}`. Since old elements have finite 2-valuation in the PID (and old quotients mod numerals do not change), `U∩J=∅`. Thus **`U⁻¹D` cannot be Noetherian**, PID or not. This includes a first successor with Lag but not yet full BCM. In the old CRT-translation recipe `B=D+Frac D₀` implies `B=U⁻¹D` when `B` is an algebra over `Frac D₀`: no choice of an upper prime, composite kernel or faithful rank-two mark can keep both Noetherianity and that translation lemma. The relation `h_n=4h_{n+1}` produces only associates upstairs, since 4 was inverted. What is needed is a *proper idempotent* dyadic ideal upstairs, not just an infinite 2-division chain downstairs.

The proposed coefficient field `K=ℚ₂(√−3)` and `W=ℤ₂+𝔪` (`𝔪` the positive Hahn-value ideal in `K((t^Γ))`) do pass the stated quotient and zero-vector tests: `2ⁿ𝔪=𝔪`, and `(t√−3,t,t,t)` works. But `W` is not a valuation ring of that field: neither `√−3` nor its inverse has constant term in ℤ₂. More importantly, if a residue **field** is embedded there over old `F=Frac D₀ ⊂ ℚ₂`, meeting `𝔪` nontrivially, it also contains the inverse of that element, of strictly negative Hahn value. No translation by an `F`-constant makes this inverse integral. In a pullback from a surjective `B→k'` to marks `S'⊂k'` integral at this place, `k'≠S'+F`, hence `B≠D'+F`. Even Bézout content scaling by old upstairs units `F^×` fails on a singleton of nonzero Hahn value. These are failures *before* Lang–Weil or any tail lifting. No conclusion that all non-Noetherian upper Bézout rings fail. In a Bézout `D`, a four-square representation of `0<h∈J` yields some `g∈J` (the gcd of the four coordinates is their linear combination) with `g²|h`; the divided coordinates generate 1. This gives a more appropriate repeatedly branching denominator test than `h_n/4`. Section 1 does not settle Target A.

## 2. Keeping all prime characters consistent: a residue identity on Brauer classes

Hereafter there is **no common-multiple axiom**. Use the rational-stage PID method, starting at `ℚ[P]`, with the order `P>ℚ` and with `P` a square unit at **all** ordinary marks, `P≡1 mod 840`. We do not start with the *finished* joint PID. Write `A` for a countable finite-type stage UFD over ℚ, `F=Frac A`, `κ_q=Frac(A/qA)` for one representative of each prime element up to associates. Each mark is an embedding `F→ℚ_l`; for every fixed nonzero field element all but finitely many marks give units. There is also the evaluation in the ordered real closure. The assignments of marks on a finite-branching tree, made before any actual character selection, are specified in §4.

Use additive bits in `𝔽₂`. For `α∈Br(F)[2]`, put `L_F(α)=∑_l inv₂(α_l)+inv₂(α_real)` where `inv₂` takes values 0,1 on the trivial/nontrivial local 2-torsion class. The sum is finite: by the degree-two norm residue theorem each such class is a finite sum of quaternion symbols `(a,b)`; at odd places where `a,b` are units its symbol is trivial. Alternatively spread an Azumaya representative to almost all marks. This functional is additive and compatible on extension of marked ordered fields. The real closure need not embed in ℝ: the bit of a quaternion is 1 precisely when both entries are negative. **`L_ℚ=0`** by Hilbert reciprocity for the ordinary rational field, extending from symbols by additivity.

At every node on the actual path we shall have characters `χ_q:κ_q^×/κ_q^{×2}→𝔽₂` for **every** stage prime, not just already scheduled primes. Require, simultaneously for all `α`,

```
             L_F(α) = ∑_{q ∈ Spec¹ A} χ_q(∂_q α).                       (R)
```

`∂` is the tame Brauer residue: for `(a,b)` at a DVR with valuations `m,n` it is the squareclass of `(-1)^{mn} a^n/b^m` reduced after cancellation. Thus sums on the right are finite. For old primes the new characters must extend old ones. No freeness of reductions of distinct prime generators in `κ_q` is hypothesized. It would not generally hold: even a prime polynomial `X²−m` kills a squareclass on its own divisor.

**Extension lemma.** Suppose `A→B` are the stages described below, `E=Frac B`. The vertical height-one primes of `B` (not disjoint from `A∖0`) are exactly the old primes, each of ramification index one: every old prime is a nonunit prime and `A` is factorial. The others correspond to **all** the prime divisors of `X=Spec(B⊗_A F)` by localization; height is unchanged for primes disjoint from the multiplicative set. Suppose

1. each `χ_q` extends to `κ'_q=Frac(B/qB)`;
2. every class in `Br(E)[2]` unramified at every divisor of `X` is the restriction of a class in **`Br(F)[2]`**.

Choose *any* extensions on the vertical primes as in 1. Subtract from `L_E` the sum of their values on the vertical residues. This difference vanishes on the kernel of the horizontal residue map: by 2 a class there is old, and on old classes vertical residues are restrictions of old residues (index one), so use (R). Consequently the difference defines a linear functional on the **image** of the horizontal residue map in `⊕_{r horizontal} κ_r^×/κ_r^{×2}`. Extend it to the entire direct sum by vector-space basis extension; the restrictions to the summands give all new `χ_r`. This proves (R) upstairs, with no exclusion of composite cofactors. In particular a relation of the form `u²−m v²=q w` never requires declaring the divisors of `w` harmless: the *same* identity on the full residue vector accounts for all of them. No surjectivity of the residue map or smoothness of the total space over ℚ is needed.

**Geometric input to 2.** Our generic fibres `X` are just affine lines or the affine smooth quadric `Q₄=s` with `s∈F^×`. For a smooth variety in characteristic zero, purity for the Brauer group identifies the unramified subgroup at its codimension-one points with the image of `Br(X)` in `Br(E)`, injectively (cohomological Brauer group). We need surjectivity from constants on **2-torsion**, not just modulo constants. Over an algebraic closure the quadric is `det M=s`, hence `SL₂` by scaling a column. For both this variety and the affine line, `H¹_et(Xbar,μ₂)=H²_et(Xbar,μ₂)=0`: embed the countable algebraic closure into ℂ, apply invariance of finite-coefficient étale cohomology under algebraically closed extension and comparison with singular cohomology; `SL₂(ℂ)` deformation retracts onto `SU(2)=S³` by polar decomposition. Geometric connectedness gives `H⁰=μ₂`. The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence now gives `H²(F,μ₂) ≅ H²_et(X,μ₂)` (no incoming differential from degree 1 geometric cohomology). The Kummer sequence maps the latter onto `Br(X)[2]`, proving 2. This proof also starts the induction by the line `ℚ→ℚ[P]` with no vertical primes.

## 3. The edges and their tests

                B=A[x,y]/(x²−q y−a) ⊂ F[x],     y=(x²−a)/q .

## 5. Computing the law, supplements, and failure of ES

Put `ε(u)=(u−1)/2 mod 2` and `ω(u)=(u²−1)/8 mod 2` for odd `u`, equivalently the ordinary tables on the residues modulo 4 and 8. For two distinct positive **nonstandard** primes `p,q`, apply (R) at a common stage to `(p,q)`. Both are normalized, so at odd `l` they are units and the local Hilbert bit vanishes. The real bit is zero. The 2-adic bit is `ε(p)ε(q)` by the unit-unit formula. The only rational-stage residues are `q` at `p` and `p` at `q` (inversion is trivial modulo squares). We obtain

```
                 χ_p(q) + χ_q(p) = ε(p) ε(q).
```

If `l` is a positive standard odd prime and `p` nonstandard, `(p,l)` has only the vertical-stage residue `l` at `p` (ordinary integers are scalars of `A`); on the mark side the odd place `l` contributes the ordinary Legendre bit of `p mod l` and 2 contributes `ε(p)ε(l)`. This gives both directions of the same law. Two standard primes have ordinary QR. Similarly `(-1,p)` and `(2,p)` have respectively the sole stage residues `−1` and `2` at nonstandard `p`, zero real bit, zero odd-place bits and dyadic bits `ε(p)` and `ω(p)`. Standard supplements are classical. This proves the prime QR axiom with **both primes universally quantified inside one model**, not just the scheme `QR_std`. At `P` specifically the ordinary prime residues have positive character sign by its square-unit marks and `P≡1 mod 8`. The distinction between prime QR and internally coded Jacobi algorithms or higher induction is essential.

Finally any positive ES triple at `P` descends through the finite edges to three polynomials in `ℚ[P]` by reciprocal descent (§3). If they were all in `R`, their coefficient denominator `M` would give a primitive square class modulo `M` on which all three take integral values: compare to the integral values at the marks of `P` at each prime power dividing `M`, and combine local unit squares by CRT. Their leading signs are positive. The *polynomial* obstruction `LOGIC_SPLIT_SQUARE_TEST.md` (proof map L3 in `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md`) excludes precisely this case. Neither marks nor the QR witnesses are specialized to positive integers; doing that with the Lag gap certificates would be invalid. We obtain the announced nonstandard countermodel. BCM fails here since a nonzero common multiple of all ordinary primes cannot occur in an external PID. This does not settle ES in ℕ or in stronger induction theories.

**Scope of the choice of edges.** In particular the generic norm **circle** has geometric fibre `𝔾_m`, not `SL₂`, and `H¹_et(𝔾_m,μ₂)≠0`. One cannot insert all the old TS steps by citing §2. The Gaussian UFD invariant also cannot simply be imported for the new root edge. Obtaining the same theorem with *universal* TS imposed separately would require an additional argument; universal TS is not part of the full prime QR axiom used here.
