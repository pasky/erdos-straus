# Lane 4 — one bad finite place separates bounded from unbounded descent

## 1. Two different integral questions

Write S: k uBC = n+u+B, X: k abcd = a+b+nc, U: k xyz=n(xy+xz+yz), k=4 or 8, n>0. Superscript ° deletes the coordinate zeros. On, for example, S° we test points positive at ∞, integral for **S** at every finite place, lying in S°(ℚ_v) everywhere and having u,B,C units at almost all primes. (This last restricted-product condition matters.) Call this adelic domain D. Likewise for X°, U°. This is *not* the test with units at **every** prime (sections of the open ℤ-model). That would throw away genuine ES solutions on reduction. Z58_STRUCT2 Prop T already shows that the subgroup of all coordinate-character algebras on X° detects exactly the global positive integral points. The sharpenings here distinguish **exponent**, **conductor** and **geometric** classes rather than doing that test again.

## 2. The actual open groups and covers

**Proposition.** For V=S° or U°, let r=3; for V=X°, r=4. With A=H¹(ℚ,ℚ/ℤ),

    Pic(V) = Pic(V_Qbar) = 0,   Br(V)/Br(ℚ) ≅ A^r ⊕ (ℤ/2)^{binom(r,2)}.

A basis of geometric units modulo constants is given below by r forms l_i. The first summands are (χ,l_i); the second are quaternion symbols (l_i,l_j), i<j. Geometric π₁ is Ẑ(1)^r (Galois action included). In particular every connected geometric finite étale cover is dominated by adjoining m-th roots of all the l_i, for some m; all are abelian over ℚbar. This does **not** say the arithmetic fundamental group is abelian.

*Proof.* Put W={h₁+h₂+h₃=1, h₁h₂h₃≠0}. For S take h₁=−u/n,h₂=−B/n,h₃=(n+u+B)/n=k uBC/n. For U take (h₁,h₂,h₃)=(n/(kx),n/(ky),n/(kz)). Both are isomorphisms to W (the inverses are regular on the specified opens). For X take h₁=−a/(nc),h₂=−b/(nc),h₃=1−h₁−h₂: X°≅W×𝔾ₘ with fourth coordinate c and d=h₃/(knc²h₁h₂). Use the h_i and, if present, c as the l_i. These are localizations of polynomial rings at distinct irreducible linear factors: the unit and Pic assertions follow, also geometrically.

Compactify W as ℙ² minus four lines in general position (the fourth is infinity). The complement of a nodal plane curve has abelian topological π₁ (Fulton/Deligne, same input as Z58_STRUCT Thm A(iv)); abelianization is freely generated by three affine meridians, the degree relation eliminating the fourth. The winding characters of the h_i are their integral dual basis. The extra torus contributes one independent meridian. Riemann existence and Kummer then give the stated π₁ with cyclotomic action, and the cofinal geometric covers. Over ℚ the full m-th-root tower consists of μ_m^r-torsors; after constant extension every geometric cover is a lattice quotient of one of them. No nonabelian *geometric* monodromy is hidden by taking a higher derived quotient of π₁. This observation alone says nothing about secondary cohomology operations on the arithmetic group or the Brauer groups of the covers.

For the Brauer group, successively delete the three affine lines. At the i-th step purity injects the new quotient into H¹(D_i,ℚ/ℤ), where D_i is the line minus its i−1 distinct rational intersections with earlier lines. The latter group is A ⊕ (ℤ/2)^{i−1}: split off A by a rational point; the geometric H¹ is (ℚ/ℤ(−1))^{i−1}, with rational punctures, whose invariants have order 2 in each slot. The quadratic Kummer characters of the earlier forms restricted to D_i realize these slots separately (distinct simple zeros). The corresponding residues are all realized by (χ,h_i) and (h_j,h_i), j<i, unramified off the deleted lines. They give a splitting onto the quotient (up to changing the constant-character basis); in particular this is a group computation, not just a filtration by possible residues. Start with Br(𝔸²)=Br(ℚ). For W×𝔾ₘ apply the same sequence to c=0 on W×𝔸¹, using Brauer homotopy invariance. H¹(W,ℚ/ℤ)=A⊕(ℤ/2)^3 by the meridians and rational point, with residues of (χ,c),(h_i,c) as before. ∎

## 3. A single moving denominator defeats *every* bounded exponent

**Theorem.** Write s for the squarefree kernel of n≥1. Suppose s≠1 for k=4, or s∉{1,2} for k=8 (precisely the regular-model nonobstruction ranges on S and X). For every positive integer m and for each of S°, X°, U°, the set D orthogonal to Br(V)[m] is nonempty. In fact the *whole* bounded-exponent part modulo constants of exponent dividing m is simultaneously harmless, with **no bound on conductors or number of characters**. There is a positive rational point integral away from a single prime q and a repair at q preserving every unit coordinate modulo m-th powers. The same D-point passes coordinate Kummer descent of degree parameter m: it lifts everywhere to a single rational twist of the simultaneous m-th-root torsor. No assertion that those lifts pass unrestricted BM upstairs.

*Elementary prime selection in the main case.* Enlarge m to be even. If n is odd and some L≥5 divides s, pick a prime q∤mn(n+1) with

    q ≡ −1 mod 8,   gcd(q−1,m)=2,   (−n/q)=+1.

Here is an explicit Dirichlet construction even when m is a multiple of the discriminant of ℚ(√n). Prescribe −1 at each odd prime dividing mn except L. At L choose a nonzero residue other than 1 of Legendre sign **opposite** to (−1/L): such a residue exists since (L−1)/2≥2. At the class with every sign equal to that of −1, reciprocity gives (s/q)=+1 (the reciprocity sign at q≡3 mod 4 cancels (−1/s)). Flipping just the L sign gives (s/q)=−1 and hence (−n/q)=+1. CRT and Dirichlet finish. This flexibility at L is important: demanding q≡−1 mod m outright would generally give the wrong sign.

*General prime selection (including kernels 2,3 when admissible).* It suffices that q≡−1 mod k, q∤mn(n+1), and −n be an m-th power modulo q. Write m=2^a h with h odd, a≥1. Set K=ℚ(ζ_k,√(−n)), F=ℚ(α), α the real root of α^h=−n. Its minimal polynomial has exactly one real root (it divides T^h+n), hence odd degree. K/ℚ is Galois of 2-power degree; it is linearly disjoint from F by coprime degrees. Under the stated condition on s the imaginary quadratic field ℚ(√(−n)) is **not** contained in ℚ(ζ_k) (for k=8 the only imaginary quadratic subfields are ℚ(i), ℚ(√−2)). Thus the automorphism of K given by complex conjugation on ζ_k and identity on √(−n) exists. Extend it by the identity on F, then extend to an automorphism of the normal closure of KF. Chebotarev supplies infinitely many unramified primes with this Frobenius class; choose a prime upstairs realizing that particular representative. Outside the finitely many primes dividing kmn(n+1), reduction gives both an h-th root and a square root of −n in 𝔽_q, since the two algebraic integers are fixed by Frobenius. As q≡−1 mod k, one has v₂(q−1)=1: squares are already 2^a-th powers in 𝔽_q^×. Coprimality of the exponents h and 2^a gives the required m-th power. This needs **no simultaneous splitting** of an entire radical field.

*Local repair.* Use the positive rational (★) point P given by u=1, C=(q+1)/k, B=(n+1)/q. Keep it at every place except q. At q instead take

    C′=C,    B′=q^m B,    u′=(n+B′)/(k B′ C−1).

All three coordinates are nonzero and q-integral; the denominator is −1 mod q, and u′≡−n mod q. By the choice of q, u′ is an m-th power on reduction; since q∤m it is an m-th power in ℚ_q by Hensel. Thus all three ratios of new to old coordinates are m-th powers, including the ratio q^m at the coordinate which used to be nonintegral. The patch is adelic for the open because it modifies a rational point at only one place. The arrangement units are constant multiples of monomials in the coordinates. Every (χ,l_i) of order dividing m has unchanged local invariant, by local reciprocity, and every pair symbol is unchanged since m is even. The explicit direct sum in §2 proves the assertion for the whole m-torsion modulo constants (constant classes do not affect the pairing). The m-th-root torsor twisted by the l_i(P) has local lifts by construction. More generally, this *same adelic point* passes ordinary descent by any finite ℚ-group torsor on V whose geometric monodromy has exponent dividing m. Indeed pull the torsor back to the simultaneous Kummer cover twisted by the l_i(P). That connected cover corresponds geometrically to mπ₁, so the pullback torsor has trivial geometric monodromy and descends from Spec ℚ (use the fundamental group exact sequence, or the rational lift of P as base point). The local fibres are therefore all the same global twist. This statement is about rational local lifting, not orthogonality to every class upstairs. Notice that **q and P may depend on m**; there is no compactness passage to all m.

For X use (a,b,c,d)=(u,B,1,C), and for U use (x,y,z)=(uBC,nuC,nBC). These maps preserve positivity, outer integrality and unit ratios modulo m-th powers. For U the sums of the three reciprocal terms give (n+u+B)/(nuBC)=k/n. Thus the same patch works, using the independently computed list of open classes for each target. ∎

**Sharper opposite endpoint.** Use only the (χ,l_i) where χ runs over Dirichlet characters of **2-power conductor**, unbounded. For any V above, D orthogonal to this subgroup is nonempty **iff the original positive integral point exists**. This is stronger than requiring all conductors in Z58_STRUCT2 Prop T, but is still a tautological existence test, not an effective fixed-depth obstruction.

Indeed use the actual coordinate units x (u,B,C; or a,b,c,d; or the three ES denominators); all are constant monomials in the l_i, so their character classes are included modulo constants. For each associated idèle x_v take the positive integer Q_x=∏_ℓ ℓ^{v_ℓ(x_ℓ)} (integral everywhere, unit almost everywhere). After division by Q_x, the idèle has all finite components units and positive real component. In the cyclotomic quotients Gal(ℚ(μ_{2^j})/ℚ), its Artin symbol is the unit x₂/Q_x mod 2^j (up to inversion). All odd-place units act trivially. Orthogonality at all j gives **x₂=Q_x** for every coordinate, not just their signs. The polynomial equation at the *single* place 2 therefore holds on these positive integers. Conversely a rational positive integral solution supplies an orthogonal diagonal point. This character subgroup need not make the other finite components diagonal.

There is an elementary descent version of this opposite endpoint, without CFT. Use just the tower of simultaneous coordinate-root torsors of degrees parametrized by m=2^j. If a *single* adelic point lifts everywhere to a global twist at each j, then x_v∈a_j ℚ_v^{×m} at every v, with a_j∈ℚ^× (for each coordinate separately). Positivity at ∞ forces a_j>0, and v_ℓ(a_j)≡v_ℓ(Q_x) mod m at *all* finite places. Thus a_j/Q_x is a positive rational m-th power by unique factorization. At 2 we have x₂/Q_x∈⋂_{j≥1}(ℚ₂^×)^{2^j}={1} (valuation, sign and principal units), again giving an integral solution. This proves the unrestricted ordinary finite-descent existence equivalence on the coordinate open, while **no single level** detects any of our nonsquare failures. As for the character test, it does not give a search for the denominator integers.

**Interpretation.** At e.g. 4545 on X or 179530327201 on S for k=8, no bounded exponent, even with arbitrary ramification, suffices. But unlimited depth at **one** prime suffices for emptiness on the coordinate open. This distinguishes the failure of the regular model from the open BM answer without reclassifying it as a finite reciprocity obstruction. The argument is numerator-blind on purpose: it works at both full 8/131 and 8/241 failures. It is not a “prime T1”. On S° and X° this proves a dichotomy: outside the ranges above the order-2 class from the regular model already obstructs (Z58_STRUCT Thm A, Z58_STRUCT2 Thm B); inside them no bounded exponent does. The proof does not establish non-tautological obstructions on the twists.
