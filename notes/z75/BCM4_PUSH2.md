## 1. Why twist the roots

We need to halve values at the distinguished place **and** have only finitely many poles of each field element among discrete off-axis places at the next successor. In the untwisted base `⋃ F[h^(1/2^j)]` even `h−1` keeps splitting: `X^(2^j)−1` has at least `j+1` pairwise coprime nonconstant factors in characteristic zero, and these give arbitrarily many distinct places above `h−1`. Divisible exponents alone do not give a PID after inverting `h`. Instead work over a countable ordered field `F` with infinitely many distinct positive squareclasses. We will always have the distinct classes of the ordinary positive primes. The successor base is

```
        C = ⋃ F[x_j],    x₀=h,    x_j=c_j x_{j+1}²   (c_j ∈ F positive).
```

Choose the `c_j` **before** any floor task. Order every `x_j` positive infinite over `F`, compatibly (they may be taken in `rc(F(h))`). Thus the only relatively bounded elements of `C` are `F`, even for the full root tower. No root is taken later of an old floor approximand. Let `G=ℤ[1/2]`. At the branch over zero the normalized valuation is `w(x_j)=2^(−j)`; take `t(λ)=x_j^m` for a chosen expression `λ=m/2^j` (`m` signed). These are positive elements of `C[1/h]^×`, in `C` for `λ≥0`. Only the values, not multiplicativity of the section, matter.

**Twist lemma.** The `c_j` can be chosen so `C°=C[1/h]` is an external PID. Its units are `F^×` times the `t(λ)`. Enumerate all nonzero Laurent polynomials from all levels by dovetailing their birth levels and coefficient indices. At transition `j`, protect the union of the finite lists admitted so far (exhaustive in the limit). Factor them at that level in `F[x_j,x_j⁻¹]`. For each irreducible factor `r`, `E=F[x_j]/r` is a finite extension with `x_j` nonzero there (use a polynomial representative prime to `x_j`). Demand `x_j/c_j` nonsquare in `E`. The kernel of `F^×/2→E^×/2` is finite: each nontrivial kernel class gives a distinct quadratic subfield of the finite Galois closure. Thus this requirement excludes at most one coset of a finite subgroup, and finitely many such requirements do not exhaust the available positive classes. At the chosen transition every protected factor remains a prime, by the irreducible monic quadratic over its residue field. Protect the original lists at *every* subsequent transition. Hence every element has a factorization at some level which is permanent thereafter. Each pair has a Bézout gcd at a common Laurent PID level (the equality of the generated ideals persists). Atomic Bézout implies external PID by decreasing finite factor length. Any unit and its inverse occur together at a finite level, proving the unit assertion. This is an adaptive nonsplitting twist, not a claim about untwisted Kummer towers. Only a **2-divisible** group is required.

## 4. CRT without a least axis value; Bézout content; four squares

Define `T'=⋂ φ_v⁻¹(V_v) ⊂ k'`, and `S'=T'` initially, otherwise restrict at `H` also by `red_H φ_H(z)∈S`. Set `D'={b∈B:b mod M∈S'}`. Here `T'` is only an auxiliary integral ring in the *residue field*; the next upper ring is **`B`** with axis `w` and distinguished element `h`, not `T'` and not `D'`. Since `S'∩F=D`, the relatively bounded elements of `D'` are exactly old `D`. Also `h/d∈D'` for all nonzero `d∈D`. In particular the end extension is proper. The fraction field of `D'` is `Frac B` since `hB⊂D'` and `h≠0`.

Here are the approximation details for the old upper ring `T`. Its isolated generators are the normalized discrete primes and, at `H`, the monomials `t_H(λ)` for all positive `λ∈G`. The principal ideals for positive thresholds at different places are comaximal: any common divisor has value zero everywhere, hence is a unit, and `T` is Bézout. An isolated element `e` of value `λ` cuts out the ideal of integral elements of value `≥λ` at that place, by division and the valuation-intersection formula. Moreover `T` maps onto `V_v/eV_v`: approximate an integral element by one in `F` within value `>λ` by density. Clear its finitely many poles at *other* places by isolated factors, so its denominator in `T` is a unit at `v`; this denominator is invertible modulo `(e)` because coprime there by Bézout. Thus CRT in `T` approximates finite tuples modulo their specified positive thresholds, **including arbitrarily large finite axis thresholds**. This only needs principal thresholds in `G`, never a largest infinitesimal or a uniformizer of `H`. At the first stage it is ordinary PID CRT.

For `z=b mod M∈k'`, take `d∈T` supported at the finite pole set of `z` under the marks, and also at `H` when present, with sufficient values to clear the poles and positive `v_H(d)`. Use CRT to match `dφ_v(z)` by `a∈T` modulo each pole threshold `v(d)` and, at `H`, to threshold **strictly greater than `v_H(d)`**. Then `c=a/d∈F` has `z−c∈S'` (zero residual error at `H`). Hence **`B=D'+F`**. Clearing by a single integral `d` with sufficiently large `H`-value to make both `d` and `dz` strictly axis-positive if necessary gives `Frac S'=k'`. For floors: the tasks approximate every real-closure target within the relative hull by an element of `B`. CRT translation retains that bound. The relative residue of the remaining error lies in the fixed ordered host; approximate it by an old `rc(F)` constant within `1/4` as in `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §§1,3. For Bézout first normalize any nonzero fractional pair from `k'` by a scalar `c∈F^×` of the **minimum value at every mark** (ignore a zero entry in the minima). Such `c` is a finite product of powers of the old isolated prime generators and an old axis monomial of the exact `G`-value. The resulting `f,g∈T'` are locally primitive at every mark and have a `T'`-row: if `g≠0`, match an inverse of `f` by `u∈T` modulo thresholds `v(g)` wherever these are positive, using the preceding CRT. Initially this step is absent. Correct `c` by such a unit, without altering any values. Now the divided pair lies in `S'`, is primitive over `T'`, and the two `H`-residues have an `S`-row. No change to Lag, ES descent or prime QR. Lane 2's `notes/z75/IE1_ES.md` Theorem 1 (branch `side-agent/z75-ie1-es`) applies directly: every such external IOpen PID satisfies the unary least-number scheme `L(D)` for Boolean combinations of polynomial comparisons and polynomial divisibility tests with parameters. This is one QR + Dirichlet + Lag + `L(D)` PID, not a PID with BCM in it.

**Why the same proof does not immediately admit a TS circle.** There is an explicit horizontal-unramified nonconstant 2-torsion obstruction even with `s>0` a prime target. For instance, take `F=ℚ(s)` ordered with `s` positive infinite and `X:x²+y²=s`. In `F(X)` consider the symbol `(d,x)` for a choice of `d` which becomes square over `F(√s)` so it is unramified at the zeros of `x`. Taking `d=s` gives a concrete class: the affine divisor `x=0` has residue `F(√s)`, so its only possible affine residue there vanishes; at every other affine divisor it is unramified. At the single closed point at infinity of the smooth projective conic the order of `x` is −1 (transverse line at infinity) and the residue field is `F(i)`; the residue of `(s,x)` there is the nonsquare `s∈F(i)`. Thus **this unramified-on-`X` class is not constant**, even on a one-prime generic target, disproving condition 2 of the QR extension lemma for actual circle shapes. This is a real obstruction to that sufficient lemma, not a proof of incompatibility with universal TS. For example at real marks `s>0` its real bit is 0, but an arbitrary unramified class cannot simply be removed from the defect without controlling its vertical residues and other places. No theorem asserting QR + universal TS (or full QR of new primes in the BCM union) is claimed here.
