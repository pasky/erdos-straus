# Pell windows in rank one; a prime without a least quadratic nonresidue

## 1. The universal axiom, with no standardness proviso

Write `W(n;d,a)` for the two-witness quadratic test

`∃u,v≤(2a+1)(n+1) [n≤u ∧ u²=1+dv²]`.

All variables range over the nonnegative cone. The axiom **PW** is `∀d≥2 ∀a>1 ∀b [a²=1+db² → ∀n W(n;d,a)]`. Over IOpen it is exactly ordinary induction for these windows under the supplied-unit hypothesis: base `(1,0)`, and if a witness does not already cover `n+1` then `u=n`; multiply by `a+b√d` as in `IE1_ES.md` §2. In particular the upper bound uses the actual input a; there is no standard uniform factor for arbitrary radicands. ES uses the full positive reciprocal equation, not a two-squares or (★) restriction.

**Theorem A.** There exists a countable discretely ordered normal ring R with `IOpen + Lag + PW`. It contains a positive infinite prime `P≡1 mod 840` with `¬ES(P)` and **positive** `X,Y` satisfying `X²+1=PY²`. In the same model, for arbitrary parameters d≥2 and c, for each open polynomial filter φ the set

`{u≥0 : ∃v≤u [u²=1+dv² ∧ φ(u,v,c)]}`

has a least element if nonempty. This is a variable-radicand one-witness least-number schema; it does not require being given a fundamental unit. For an **infinite** d, the internally bounded part of the solution set is even externally finite. No assertion that R is Bézout, factorial or a model of universal positive Pell. PW at P is nonvacuous: square the negative-norm unit.

**Growth lemma.** For a nonzero field element write `[h]` for the natural archimedean size class of `|h|` modulo factors bounded above and below by positive standard constants. We use *larger class = larger magnitude*. A countable ordered field with classes contained in `ℚ·[P]`, `[P]>0`, suffices. Every positive infinite element then has standard powers cofinal in the field. In a discrete ordered subring consider norm-one solutions, with group law on `s=u+√d v>0` (signed u,v for inverses). For d infinite, any element s>1 of this group has `u,v>0`, since `u=(s+s⁻¹)/2`, `v=(s−s⁻¹)/(2√d)`. In particular `s≥√d` by discreteness. Distinct ordered group elements have ratios either ≥√d or ≤1/√d, as appropriate. Between 1 and a field bound B there are therefore at most `k+1` elements, for **some standard k**, by `(√d)^k>B`. This is an external packing bound, not an assumption that internal finite sets have codes. Hence even arbitrary subsets of bounded pieces have extrema. Any nonempty subset of the positive branch has a least coordinate u by truncation at one member. If a nonidentity element exists the positive group is generated by a least element above 1, with **standard** integer exponents: its powers are cofinal and consecutive powers have no intervening group element by division. The finite-d case does not have that last description in our construction.

For an infinite supplied unit `e=a+√d b`, for each `n≥1` take the first **standard** j≥0 with `e^j≥2n`, by cofinality. Then `2n≤e^j<2en`, and `u=(e^j+e^(−j))/2` satisfies `n≤u≤en+1≤(2a+1)(n+1)` since `e<2a`. The ordinate is nonnegative and ≤u. Both coordinates lie in the ring by *finite* multiplication of the given integral pair. No logarithm or internal power function is required. All d infinite with supplied unit are covered. Standard d with **finite** a still require an additional construction.

### Construction: no new growth classes beyond rational powers of P

Start at `A₀=ℚ[P]`, order P at +∞, `F=Frac A`. At each step keep the ordered ambient the countable real closure of the field constructed so far. Adjoining independent bounded parameters of algebraically independent residues is done by the ordered Gauss valuation with those fresh positive real residues (old natural residue field is countable). It does **not** change the size group: the magnitude of a polynomial is that of a largest coefficient, up to a standard bounded factor in both directions, by normalizing and reducing; use ratios. Real algebraic extension takes the group into the divisible hull: in a polynomial relation at a nonzero root at least two terms have equal maximal size class, so a nonzero integer multiple of the root's class belongs to the old group. Thus all subsequent real closures have exactly `ℚ·[P]` if we use only these two devices. The ring stages are much smaller than these ordered ambient fields.

1. **Negative Pell at the target.** Insert `A₁=ℚ[P,Y,X]/(X²+1−PY²)`. Set `Y=Pθ`, with a new residue in `(1,2)` transcendental over the base residue, and `X=+√(PY²−1)`. The algebra embeds: over `ℚ(P)(Y)` the quadratic radicand has simple zeros. Every element has monic normal form `f(P,Y)+X g(P,Y)`. A nonzero f-block with top total degree m grows as `P^m` with nonzero residue polynomial in θ (distinct powers at fixed total degree); the Xg-block with top total degree k grows as `P^(k+3/2)` since `X/P^(3/2)` has residue θ. These exponents never coincide, so only rational constants are bounded. The algebra is geometrically integral (monic and generically integral after constant base change), smooth over ℚ by the partial derivatives, hence normal. Modulo P it gives `ℚ(i)[Y]`, a domain. The generic degree filtration over `ℚ(P)` has domain top form `X²−PY²`, and monic contraction to `ℚ[P]`, giving positive reciprocal descent (z74 L2). After any real quadratic **constant** extension this top form stays irreducible since P is a field indeterminate; additivity of degrees gives constant units on the generic fibre. Monic contraction for a unit and its inverse thus gives `A₁[√d₀]^×=ℚ(√d₀)^×` for all squarefree standard `d₀>1`. This last unit computation is for constant extensions only, not for adjoining `√P`.
2. **Remaining steps.** Use exactly the constant-torus, polynomial-floor and four-square tasks and the invariant of `IE1_ES.md` §3 starting at this new A₁. The required scalar unit lists start empty by step 1. To check the rank constraint explicitly: for a torus at an old height H the new real parameter is `T=Hθ` with bounded fresh residue, and all rational powers are in the new real closure; for a floor the parameter is `α+θ`, with α in the old real closure; at a sphere the coordinates are `√s θ_j` and `√s √(1−Σθ_j²)` with the first three θ's of independent fresh residues in a ball. All preserve the size-group bound just proved. Dovetail over countable stages. In fact one independent class `[P]` suffices for the rational scale span for each constant squarefree radical, but the full skip rule also works. For clarity, on a constant norm torus a bounded fixed Laurent sum at a fresh scale forces each pair of opposite coefficients times its height power to be bounded. Their product is a bounded old nonzero rational norm. Hence the coefficients are old quadratic units and force the supposedly fresh class into the divisible old unit span. This is the discreteness check of `IE1_ES.md`, not a parallel argument for tori at infinite radicands. Different constant radicals keep their old unit lists by conjugation negating the new exponent. Floors and spheres have constant generic units over every ordered real quadratic constant extension. Normality through directed finite-level tori follows using the finite-type subsystem proof there. For P, each constant torus has a geometrically integral fibre over its residue fraction field (also if that torus splits); the rank-four sphere modulo P stays integral even at zero, and free lines preserve primality. Each generic degree filtration over the **old** field in the ES descent has domain graded ring, including `X²−d₀Y²` at each finite constant-torus level (old field geometrically integral over ℚ). Contraction is monic or polynomial. Thus no new positive reciprocal tuples at P are introduced.
3. **Actual arithmetic ring.** One can mark `P=1,Y=1,X=0` at every ordinary ℓ. These are integral homomorphisms on the algebra and do **not** preserve order or embed its fraction field. At all later tori mark every root level by the identity, at floor lines by zero, and at each sphere choose a four-square representation in `ℤ_ℓ` for integral target (including zero) and `ℚ_ℓ` otherwise. Existence follows from compactness and ordinary Lag, then square-denominator scaling. Finite support of *poles* follows stagewise. For the union A take `R={x∈A: ∀ℓ φ_ℓ(x)∈ℤ_ℓ}`. Clearing standard denominators gives `Frac R=Frac A`. CRT translation makes the bounded-error floor approximants integral at all marks; a standard shift then floors exactly. Bounded elements of R are ℤ. If an element of the fraction field is integral over R, it belongs first to A by normality, then its marks lie in the integrally closed `ℤ_ℓ`. Thus R is normal. Lag witnesses for a positive infinite member are all-place integral by the scheduling rule; finite values are ordinary. P is prime in A and a unit at every mark, hence prime in R. It has residue 1 at every standard modulus, a stronger congruence than required. The negative-Pell pair really is in R, positive in the chosen order despite its marks. Any positive ES triple would descend to polynomials in P. If coefficient denominators divide M, those polynomials are integral throughout `1 mod M` by integrality of their values at P=1. Positive leading signs and the primitive square-class obstruction (`LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md` L3) rule this out. No specialization of every Lag witness in ℤ has been used.
4. **Verify all quantifiers.** The constant-torus argument of `IE1_ES.md` §3 gives a norm-one unit of comparable size at every infinite height for every standard nonsquare d, including nonsquare multiples by standard squares (identity marks allow division of the ordinate by the square factor's root). Multiply by standard positive or negative powers of the ordinary least norm-one integral unit to enter the actual window. Its least unit has a no larger than that of any supplied unit: any smaller positive real unit would have bounded coordinates and be ordinary. Thus the window for the least unit implies the one for each supplied a, even infinite. That note proves least search on the constant-radicand Pell branch with any open filter by decomposing into finitely many intervals and bracketing endpoints using ordinary least-unit translates; ratio between successive translates cannot be an integral unit. For d infinite apply the growth lemma; if there is no supplied unit the positive branch is trivial. For square d in R the product `(u−√d v)(u+√d v)=1` forces `u=1,v=0` by discreteness (take the ring square root). Finite squares are standard. This exhausts d≥2 and proves Theorem A. The rank-one restriction itself excludes IE₁ by Wilmers' PA-initial-segment theorem: in any nonstandard PA initial segment a nonstandard t has a power `2^t` larger than every standard power of t, impossible in this order. This is an external structural test, not a claimed independence from IE₁.

## 2. What the normalized branch does and does not fix

At a positive old d nonsquare in F, `A[U,Y]/(Y²−2U−dU²)` gives `X=1+dU`. Over a characteristic-zero coefficient field the zero-d fibre really is the affine line `Y²=2U`, and at nonzero d it is a smooth geometrically integral conic (the polynomial `U(2+dU)` has distinct simple roots). Smoothness over A follows from the relative partials and the norm identity. Monic normal form gives contraction and preserves an old prime via its integral generic residue fibre. This also gives normality over a normal finite-type base. Over F the domain top form `Y²−dU²` gives z74 L2. At level N use this **whole algebra** on the Nth pair with transition by powers, not an independent root extension over a point with prescribed coordinates. The Chebyshev identity gives `U₁=(T_N(1+dU_N)−1)/d=N² U_N+d·(...)` in the presentation; `Y₁=Y_N·S_{N−1}(1+dU_N)` with `S_{N−1}(1)=N` (second-kind polynomial). At the zero fibre this is `Y₁=N Y_N`, an isomorphism, including even N. The finite maps on the generic tori on coordinate rings are injective (over a splitting field they send the Laurent parameter to its Nth power); this statement concerns the maps on **functions**, not surjectivity on integral points. The marks `(0,0)` are compatible. Filtered unions over levels are normal by finite-stage descent as usual. **So the proposed repair of primality and reciprocal descent works.**

There is a separate, exact **ordered obstruction**. A nontrivial positive norm-one unit w over infinite d with integral coordinates satisfies `w≥√d`, by the growth lemma. If it admits positive roots of every standard degree with integral coordinates, then `w≥(√d)^N` for every standard N. In the normalized positive branch the stronger `X_N≥1+d` holds, since `0<U_N∈R`. Consequently when `w≤d^C` for standard C the full root task is impossible, independently of the marks and of smoothness. For instance this kills any all-root strategy for a unit of size a fixed polynomial in P at d=P. Algebraic injectivity cannot turn `0<Y_N<1` into an integer. Theorem A keeps the good fibre at the *initial* negative-Pell equation and obtains the missing windows by changing the value group; it does not assert the order invariant for arbitrary normalized blowups. In fact neither the conic pair at norm −1 nor its integral square needs to satisfy the normalized `X=1+dU` condition.

## 3. Full QR with no least quadratic nonresidue at the ES-failing prime

**Theorem B.** There is a version of the lane-1 PID (`notes/z75/BCM4_QR.md`, proof at commit `95721d096`) satisfying `IOpen + Bézout + Lag + full prime QR (both supplements, exactly two squareclasses) + L(D)` with an infinite prime `P≡1 mod 840` failing ES and some `0<c<P` such that

`S(t) := 0<t<P ∧ ∃v<P ∃k≤P² [v²=ct+kP ∨ ct=v²+kP]`

is nonempty and has no least member. Both the prime modulus and coefficient c are **fixed parameters** in the bad search; g(t)=ct is only linear. It is a search with a bounded existential block (two ring witnesses in this expansion of congruence), not a failure of plain polynomial divisibility minimization or a proof about single-witness ordinary induction. The variable-modulus family therefore fails least search a fortiori. At a standard fixed modulus the truth set is periodic in t with finitely many residue classes for any fixed polynomial, so that case gives no such failure.

**Proof.** Every full-QR PID in lane 1 satisfies `L(D)` by `IE1_ES.md` Thm 1. We refine the **order choices of some free lines**, not the Brauer extension lemma, the root predicate, or the reciprocity computation. Recall three specific interfaces of the lane-1 construction. (i) `L_F` is computed with the actual real ordering and all finite-place marks, hence extends `L` on the old field for *every order extension*. (ii) On a polynomial line, the vertical residue fields extend purely transcendently. For each old prime the new free coordinate Z is independent modulo squares of the old field, by its Z-valuation. The proof of (R) permits **arbitrary compatible extensions of the old vertical characters**, because the remaining horizontal functional accounts for the difference. In particular we may set `χ_P(Z)=1` (additive nonsquare bit) at every such free line. (iii) Every actual normalized prime eventually has exactly two nonzero squareclasses in its residue field and bit one stays nonsquare. All algebraic stage results and character conclusions for these interfaces are in `BCM4_QR.md` §§2–5.

To the repeated free-line tasks add, for each positive infinite stage element h, a task introducing `A[Z]` with `Z=√h θ`, θ of fresh residue in `(1,2)` over the entire old ordered real closure. Then `0<Z<h`. A nonconstant polynomial in Z over A cannot be bounded: in θ normalize by a largest scaled coefficient and use its transcendental residue; the top nonzero old coefficient is not infinitesimal, and is multiplied by an infinite positive power of `√h`. Thus all bounded ring elements remain rational, and the ordinary polynomial-algebra reciprocal descent does not use a largest-scale order. Preserving PID, floors, Lag, primality, the Brauer input, and Lang–Weil prefixes requires no new algebraic fibre type. Prepare the same finite-branching tree with these additional deterministic lines **before** the marks as in §4 there, and mark each such Z integral in all tails (choose a generic ℓ-adic integer). In prefixes the coordinates are integral by presentation. The exhausting lists impose units almost everywhere on each fixed nonzero field element as before. The new lines impose no simultaneous finite-field character prescriptions. The old real orders extend down the path, so the use of `L_E` on an actual child still works for each compatible new ordering; no assumption is made that `L_E` is unchanged at new symbols. P is permanently prime with its positive unit normalization, so its prescribed `χ_P(Z)=1` pertains to the final `R/P`. Retain the original nontriviality tasks for all other primes. Every positive infinite t in R was available at a node and eventually has a new **integral** positive nonsquare Z<t. For t<P this produces a nonsquare strictly inside `(0,t)` with no need to reduce Z mod P.

At P all positive standard integers are squares by the unit-square marks/QR (`BCM4_QR.md` §5, including the supplement at 2), multiplicativity and ordinary finite factorization. There exists a nonsquare; choose a representative `0<c<P` by division. In particular there is **no least quadratic nonresidue modulo P**. Indeed the set S picks out exactly the nonsquare representatives in `(0,P)` because the squareclass group has order two. The witness bound follows by taking v reduced mod P and the nonnegative quotient in the signed difference. Every member is infinite and therefore has a smaller member by the added task. This proves the result. No claim that the particular unspecified free-line choices *as originally written* already make the nonsquares coinitial. The new failure is location-specific: least search fails for the linear polynomial at this P. Failure of LE₁ in general is not new; a Pell test already exhibits that in the original lane-1 model (§4).

## 4. Why the untwisted divisible torus does not give an external PID

In any nonstandard discretely ordered IOpen external PID the map to `ℤ_ℓ` from standard remainders is injective for every standard prime ℓ: a nonzero kernel element would have unbounded ℓ-adic valuation in the PID. For a standard nonsquare `d₀` the group of norm-one pairs over `ℤ_ℓ` injects into the inverse system of finite norm-one groups mod `ℓ^k` (use the **free quadratic algebra** even if d₀ splits locally). If a pair admits norm-one roots of every standard order among integral pairs, its image in each such finite group is the identity, by choosing the order of the entire group. Its image over `ℤ_ℓ` must be `(1,0)`. Thus **no nontrivial integral untwisted divisible torus in an external PID**, and no replacement of the torus identity marks in `IE1_ES.md` by injective all-root integral marks. This is unconditional and does not assume QR. In Theorem A the nontrivial *standard-radicand* torus ordinates have zero at all marks, so are divisible by every standard prime in R (divide by that prime in A); this explicitly prevents external PID.

**Caution: this is not `PID ⇒ ¬IW₂`.** The difference can be measured exactly on the Pell group. For d₀=2 and `e=3+2√2`, in any IOpen ring satisfying `IW₂` the group G of positive norm-one integral units maps by size onto the natural size group of `Frac R` with kernel `e^ℤ` (ℤ standard). To see surjectivity at infinite positive magnitude, floor the magnitude in R and use the window to find a unit at that class; use reciprocals for small magnitude. The kernel consists of ordinary integral units by discreteness and the coordinate formulas (take the inverse at small real value). The target is divisible: real positive nth roots of infinite sizes admit comparable R-floors, and invert for negatives. Thus each `w∈G` satisfies `w=e^r v^N` with standard r and `v∈G`, for each standard N. The exponent r is determined modulo N (if a power has trivial class its root also does). The residues are coherent, giving a homomorphism `ρ:G→Ẑ` taking e to 1. In an external PID or with one injective ordinary mark this would be injective: a unit in the kernel is an actual Nth power for all standard N, so the finite-group argument applies. In particular it induces a size-group injection `G/e^ℤ→Ẑ/ℤ`. The constants `e^r` in the root tests **cannot be suppressed**. A twisted inverse system realizing nonstandard exponent residues is not ruled out by this argument. For clarity, the lane-1 tower as written fails `IW₂`. Let `k=ℚ(√2)`. Its initial `k[P]` has only constant units. At each free/row/sphere edge units descend after scalar extension by k by `IE1_ES.md` Prop. 2 (geometrically integral stages; ordered extended old field). At a pivot edge `x²=qy+a`, over the extended old fraction field the generic ring is a polynomial line in x; its units are constants. In the monic normal form `f(y)+x g(y)` over `A_k`, substituting `y=(x²−a)/q` makes the two blocks' leading x-degrees even and odd respectively. Only an old constant block can lie in the old extended fraction field. This argument applies to a unit and its inverse and requires no permanence of q over k. Geometric integrality persists here too, by that embedding into a polynomial line after extension of constants. Thus the final extended rational algebra has only `k^×` units; an integral norm-one pair yields rational, hence standard integral, coordinates. The bounded formula `W₂` fails at P as in `IE1_ES.md` Prop. 2. Neither that calculation nor Theorem B excludes another external PID with `IW₂`.

**Boundary / next push.** A model with full QR, L(D) and PW simultaneously remains open. In Theorem A the negative-Pell point is genuine, its witnesses need not specialize positively to ordinary squares, and full ES descends before the square-class test. In Theorem B failure of least nonresidue is for one nonstandard prime parameter, not an ordinary least-nonresidue estimate. No new numerator-eight, ordinary exceptional prime, or ordinary composite conclusion follows. The next PID test is twisted (not untwisted) power transitions compatible with the profinite exponent `ρ`; the next induction test on the rank-one side is a bounded modular filter on Pell coordinates, beyond open polynomial inequalities/equalities on a single branch.
