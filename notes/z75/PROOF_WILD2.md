## 1. Both endpoints move: signed product switches

For the determinant of a three-letter path write K(U,t,V)=tUV−U−V. A signed seed for any divisor t+1 of p−1, t≥2, is K(−1,t,−b)=p with b=(p−1)/(t+1). Its two *unsigned* end minors are A=t+1, B=tb+1, AB=tp+1. The following surgery uses factors on both sides of the pivot; there is no invariant common endpoint. Write A=gi, B=jh, with g,i,j,h positive, and transfer the factors i,j to opposite ends. If i+j≡0 mod t, then C=gj,D=ih are both −1 mod t (i,j are units mod t and ij⁻¹≡−1). This gives a new word K((C+1)/t,t,(D+1)/t)=p. Require the two endpoints ≥2. Its character relative to the signed input **at p≡1 mod 4** is χ_p(C)/χ_p(−A)=χ_p(ij). In fact the input character is always +1 on p≡1 mod 8: reciprocity gives (ℓ/p)=1 for each odd ℓ|t+1 dividing p−1, and (2/p)=1. Thus (ij/p)=−1 gives the (S) bridge for 4, not for 8. The signed source is a determinant with negative endpoints, not a positive HJ expansion. These facts use (tu−1)(tv−1)=tp+1; no new criterion for (S) is being asserted.

**Instant test.** At 2521 choose t=44: (A,B)=(45,2465)=(3·15,29·85). Since 15+29=44 the switch gives (87,1275). The words are (−1,44,−56) and (2,44,29), with opposite characters. This cannot be an exchange at a common endpoint. It also works at 193,409,3361,5569,9601,21841 and 8813281 (examples in the archive). When a positive word is already given, it is reachable by this surgery whenever t+1|p−1: let g=gcd(A,C), i=A/g, j=C/g, then j|B and i+j≡0 mod t since A+C≡0 and g is a unit. This converse is important: the switch explains a class change but does not make finding the positive factorization free.

**Kill of the universal rule.** All 87 primes 1 mod 840 up to 200000 pass. Nevertheless 1269241≡1 mod 840 has **no** qualifying switch, even with no cap on factors or t. 18169 is a smaller miss in another hard class. Two checks: enumerate every positive triple and filter on t+1|p−1; independently enumerate all t+1|p−1 and divisors of tp+1. Trial division verifies primality. Either engine suffices for the zero. This kills this seed, not unrestricted signed determinant surgery.

## 2. An adaptive-cyclotomic barrier that allows arbitrary multipliers

The thin-family construction reported by lane 6 suggests choosing p+u=cN, with N=a^m±b^m supplying one or more cyclotomic factors, **without** requiring c to be small. The following counts all such choices at once. The result depends on counting *distinct integers N*, not parametrizations, congruence covers, or bounds on c.

**Thin-core theorem.** Let a fixed set T of positive integers satisfy #T∩[1,Z] ≪ε Z^(α+ε) for every ε>0, with 0≤α<1. A core witness at a prime p consists of t,u,v≥2 with tuv−u−v=p, d=tu−1, and **d|N|p+u** for some N∈T. The character of d is unrestricted (so this is an upper bound also for negative hits). For 2≤Y≤√X, the number of primes p≤X admitting any such witness with N≥Y is

    ≪ε (X/log X) Y^(α−1+ε) + X^((1+α)/2+ε).

Here and below ε can be taken arbitrarily small; constants depend on the catalogue. In particular for *any* prescribed function f(p)→∞, the primes having even one witness of this shape with N≥f(p) have relative density zero. This holds within every fixed reduced prime progression too (PNT in progressions). It does not require f to grow as a power or even as log log p.

*Proof.* Since 2u−1≤d≤N and N|p+u, we have u≤(N+1)/2 and N≤2X+1. For fixed N the number of candidate shifts u is at most ∑_{d|N}τ(d+1)≪ε N^ε: one must have u|d+1. So each core permits only N^ε residue classes p≡−u mod N, even though c=(p+u)/N is free. They are reduced for any actual prime witness: u<p since t,v≥2, hence gcd(p,p+u)=1. For Y≤N≤√X Brun–Titchmarsh gives ≪X/(φ(N)log X) for each reduced class. Use φ(N)≫ε N^(1−ε) and sum the dyadic intervals starting at Y, rechoosing ε. For √X<N≤2X+1 use the integer count X/N+1; the dyadic sum of the first term is Oε(X^((1+α)/2+ε)), the sum of the second Oε(X^(α+ε)). No independence or prime average was used to infer anything *at one p*: this theorem is intentionally a density upper bound on the domain of a strategy. For the corollary first hold Y fixed, discard finitely many primes with f(p)<Y, then let Y→∞. ∎

**Concrete catalogue.** All positive numbers a^m±b^m with integers a>b≥1, m≥3, in one catalogue, have α≤2/3, allowing both bases and the exponent to depend on p. Indeed a³−b³≥(a−b)a²; put j=a−b. Then j≤Z^(1/3), and for fixed j there are ≤(Z/j)^(1/2) choices for a. Sum over j; higher powers in the difference only increase it, and a^m−b^m≥a^(m−1) bounds m by 1+log₂ Z. Plus signs cost no more. One can include b=0 and pure squares (O(√Z log Z) additional values), equal-base sums and fixed-base exponentials as well. No coprimality condition, fixed-rank factor limit or restriction that d be a primitive prime was used. A composite d consisting of any product of available factors is allowed. For Y=X^δ (0<δ≤1/2) this yields Oε(X^(1−δ/3+ε)+X^(5/6+ε)) even without the logarithmic gain. Arbitrary multipliers c never enter this exponent.

**Polynomial borrowing corollary.** One can even drop d|N: suppose N|p+u, and the part borrowed from the multiplier is e=d/gcd(d,N)≤N^B for *any fixed* real B≥0. The density-zero conclusion for N≥f(p)→∞ survives, with α replaced in the tail bound by (α+B)/(1+B)<1. Indeed M=lcm(d,N)=Ne divides p+u and contains all of d. Its expanded catalogue {Nh : N∈T, 1≤h≤N^B} is still sparse: to count M≤Z, split at N=Z^(1/(1+B)). Below that point sum N^B over the catalogue; above it sum Z/N dyadically. Both bounds are Oε(Z^((α+B)/(1+B)+ε)). Apply the thin-core theorem to M≥N. This covers **all** borrowing, however factored, if the tested core N≥p^δ for fixed δ>0: then M|p+u<2p and e≤2p/N≤N^(1/δ) for large p. Thus merely exhibiting a polynomially large divisor a^m±b^m (a>b≥1, m≥3) anywhere in the *shift of a successful word* is already a density-zero property, with no required intersection between the word divisor and that core.

**What it excludes.** Neither (i) a genuinely escaping core at *every* large prime, nor (ii) any fixed finite menu of (S) slopes **or else** an escaping core, can prove ES by this strategy. For (ii) take p≡1 mod 8 and mod every odd prime dividing the finitely many slopes. This is a positive-density progression of primes; reciprocity kills every fixed slope's negative character, and the escaping cores give only relative density zero there. This is unconditional relative to the classical theorems above, not Hypothesis H. It does **not** exclude an infinite menu with a tight distribution of necessary cores (like least nonresidues), or thin input sequences such as 15·2^s+1, or success at a specified p. In particular the catalogue theorem is **not** that all primes without any core have positive density. It also does not bound general superpolynomial borrowing e>N^B for every fixed B along a subsequence, cases when N is itself >p+u (e.g. common divisors with a huge group order expression), or to dense catalogues of binary quadratic norms (α=1). These distinctions matter more than the name 'cyclotomic'.

**Exact test.** Enumerate all N=a^m±b^m≤50000, m≥3, a>b≥1. At 2521 one oriented negative hit has N=1304; at 3361 and 21841 none. These latter checks are complete for *this catalogue* since 2p+1≤50000. They refute universality even without a growth condition for this specific catalogue (not its square/b=0 enlargement). Engine: enumerate all positive triples, and test all N=d·f, f|v, against a generated set. Independent forward enumeration for n≤2521 takes u=−n mod N, enforcing u≤(N+1)/2, then checks the divisors of N. This is for (S), not the arbitrary partial-fraction identity of lane 6.

## 3. Stepanov on the reciprocal hyperbola: how much degree do the character jets really cost?

The mod-p image of a length-three slope and its reverse lies on XY=1. Let L₃(p) here mean precisely the set of canonical integers A=tu−1 or tv−1 with t,u,v≥2, tuv−u−v=p. The height condition t=(AB−1)/p, t|A+1,B+1 is **not** the equation XY=1. We ask two different questions: can a polynomial on the hyperbola carry the *character with jets*, and can the raw character-evaluation matrix on an unknown subset force a sign? Stepanov was mentioned in `BRAINSTORM/polynomial_method.md`; we claim only the explicit restricted calculation below.

**Flat mask degree theorem (elementary).** For odd prime p, h=(p−1)/2, 1≤m<p, let a mask F(X,Y) have, on XY=1, the constant m-jet χ_p(a) at every a∈F_p^×. That is, F(a+Z,(a+Z)⁻¹)≡χ_p(a) mod Z^m. The least possible *total degree* is exactly

    D_m = h (2 ceil(m/2) − 1).

This measures cost on the curve, not separate partial derivatives normal to it. Quotienting by XY−1, total-degree ≤D polynomials give exactly Laurent polynomials P with support [−D,D]. For 0≤j<m, (X∂/∂X)^j acting at each a must give χ_p(a) for j=0 and zero otherwise. These constraints are equivalent to the jets, since j! and a are units. Group exponents modulo p−1; powers a^r on all nonzero a are a basis. For the frequency h the required equations are therefore ∑_{e≡h} c_e e^j=δ_{j0}, 0≤j<m. Other frequencies can be set to zero. The relevant exponents are odd multiples of h. If D<D_m there are <m of them and none is 0 mod p (their odd multipliers have absolute value <p). Taking the moments of the polynomial ∏(T−e) over these exponents gives 0 on the left but its nonzero constant term on the right. At D=D_m there are ≥m consecutive exponents in this class; any m consecutive ones are distinct mod p as their spacing p−1 is a unit. Interpolate evaluation at T=0 on these m nodes (Vandermonde), producing the coefficients c_e. Lift each X^{−s} to Y^s. This is the advertised explicit construction, and proves sharpness.

Two one-sided variants clarify the scope. If one only demands m-fold zeros on the QR coset, with **no** normalization elsewhere except nonzero restriction, the exact cost is ceil(mh/2), since (X^h−1)^m must divide X^D P and shifting this divisor attains the bound. If one normalizes the values on *every* nonresidue to 1, without requiring their higher jets, the exact cost is h ceil(m/2): average P(aX) over a in the subgroup of squares. It keeps the constraints, leaves a nonzero Laurent polynomial in z=X^h, and requires a root at z=1 of multiplicity m (h≠0 mod p). Its width in z is at most 2 floor(D/h). Conversely use a normalized z^(−ceil(m/2))(z−1)^m. These are degree costs for **uniform** masks, not for cleverly shaped small sets.

**Local jet test.** On q distinct *known square* slopes in L₃ take columns X^i (X^h−1)^j for 0≤i≤d, 0≤j<m. The jet matrix has rank exactly m·min(q,d+1). Filter by order of vanishing, using the simple zero of X^h−1 at each square. At order j the diagonal block is the q-point Vandermonde times nonzero row weights (h a^(h−1))^j. For d+1≤q this gives injectivity block by block; for d+1≥q solve successively for any desired jets by interpolation. In particular there is no additional rank defect caused by the arithmetic origin of the points for this ansatz. More generally for *arbitrary* selected q slopes with no prescribed values elsewhere, the least degree of a nonzero hyperbola restriction vanishing to multiplicity m there is ceil(mq/2), depending on their number alone. Proof: multiply by X^D and count zeros; shift ∏(X−a)^m to attain the bound. Hence a zeros-versus-degree count from unstructured jets alone cannot distinguish L₃ from a set of the same size lying entirely among the squares. This is not a no-go for an auxiliary polynomial exploiting integer lifts, or identities constraining the particular slope positions.

At 193,409,2521 the size/QR counts are 18/8, 16/8, 14/10. [1,χ] has rank 2 in all three cases: this already uses a nonresidue in the input data; it cannot be used to deduce one without independent control of the entries. For m=3,d=7 all three QR jet ranks are 24 as predicted (direct Hasse-coefficient matrices). `wild2_screen.txt` also checks the upper degree and one degree below by independent modular elimination, m=1,…,7 at five primes. For 2521 the optimum degrees are 1260,1260,3780,3780,6300,6300,8820. Raising multiplicity did not buy cheap access to the Legendre symbol. T1 is not being assumed away: over a composite odd square there is no corresponding field Euler/jet argument for Jacobi −1.

## 4. Eliminating all x sharing a divisor ratio

Fix k (numerator), coprime a,b>0 and p prime, and use the completion test from `PROOF_WILD.md` §2. In the first-denominator interval x>p/k, x≤3p/k with gcd(kx−p,px)=1, look at **all multiples of ab simultaneously**; write x=abj. No ES completion implies that neither of the integers a+b and a+p b has any divisor r≤2p with

    r≡−p mod kab,   gcd(r,p(r+p)/k)=1.

Indeed these divisors correspond bijectively, for each target separately, to the offending x=(r+p)/k: a,b|x and a/b≡−1 or −p mod r. Thus an interval of potential incidents indexed by j is squeezed into ≤τ(a+b)+τ(a+pb) actual edges at that ratio. Common factors of a pair can be removed in unit slices, so gcd(a,b)=1 is no loss. For m distinct x at a fixed ratio and target the lcm of their r's divides the same integer; pairwise gcd(r_j,r_l) divides ka b(j−l), and when gcd(p,kab)=1 it divides j−l. This is a coupling, not m independent trials. It also explains why that particular coupling by itself does not force a hit: it is exactly one divisor-in-a-moving-class event after projection, not p/2 independent constraints. Fixing a,b and discarding the upper bound on r does not remove the class condition.

At p=2521 and 1≤a,b≤8 the union visits 1260 admissible x's (1,1 alone visits them all), with hits at only 3 distinct x's. For 8/131 and 8/241 the union has zero hits through this box, and the separate exhaustive reduced two-denominator search confirms actual failure. The printed composite and square rows for the incidence test omit nonunit x on purpose. At 4545 a negative Jacobi triple/seed does occur, as does a ratio hit; neither observation claims a Type II solution (the prime (S) bridge and the Type-I-or-II dichotomy must not be transferred to arbitrary composites). At the five odd squares all negative tests are empty. At 131 positive-path character columns are exploratory only (131 is 3 mod 4); no 8-bridge is implied anywhere. The full results do not assume a (★) witness at any prime.

## 5. Conductor handback

The strongest obstruction is §2: arbitrary multipliers and adaptive exponents do *not* turn thin cores into a general growing-prime mechanism. A sharp escape to try is a **dense** catalogue, e.g. binary quadratic norms with both coordinates free, where the dyadic large-core tail ceases to converge by this argument; test norm cores that co-occur with successful words without presupposing d|N. Alternatively, at thin subpolynomial N the multiplier must be allowed to contribute superpolynomially in N for borrowing to escape the enlarged-catalogue argument. For a polynomial push, add the lift carry (AB−1)/p as independent data before interpolation; the reciprocal curve by itself has no slot for it. For surgery, relax the signed seed −1 to −a only with an independent method of choosing a at the certified miss 1269241. Otherwise it is just another factor search on tp+1. No external priority claim for these elementary calculations.
