# Bounded existential search vs positive reciprocal descent

Work on the nonnegative cone of a discretely ordered ring; signed polynomial notation abbreviates moving negative terms to the other side, or a finite sign split. All quantifier bounds below are polynomial unless explicitly called unbounded. `Iφ` means ordinary successor induction (not strong induction); `Lφ` is the least-number axiom. `IE₁` permits parameters and a finite block of bounded existential **number** quantifiers. `IE₁⁻` omits parameters, not witnesses. Wilmers' equivalence `IE₁ = LE₁` is for the full classes; we do **not** invoke it to maintain a one-witness count in a restricted schema.

## 1. An induction schema actually available in the joint PID

Let **D** be the Boolean closure of polynomial comparisons and atoms `f(t,a) | g(t,a)`; `a` is an arbitrary parameter tuple, **not** a tuple of numerals. Divisibility has its ordinary signed meaning (`0 | b` iff `b=0`). Over IOpen both signs of the predicate have bounded existential definitions by division with remainder (Smith, NDJFL 1993 §2; use absolute values). Hence `L(D)` is a genuine subfamily of consequences of IE₁. It includes minimization for `∃z≤B(t,a) [f(t,a) z=g(t,a) ∧ ψ(t,z,a)]` with open ψ: off `f=0` substitute the unique fraction for z and clear denominators, with sign and bound checks. On `f=g=0` this last reduction **requires that the guard determine z or be independent of z**; without that hypothesis omit the degenerate branch or require `f≠0` explicitly.

**Theorem 1.** In every discretely ordered **external PID** with IOpen, every nonempty unary D-set has a least member. In particular

`IOpen + Bézout + internal Dirichlet + TS + NR + Lag + L(D) ⊬ ES_prime[1 mod 840]`

at arbitrarily large standard cutoffs, using the one joint PID of `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md` L1–L5/A1–A6. These are genuine interval least-number principles, not just taking a minimum among the divisors of a preassigned nonzero integer. `L(D)` implies ordinary induction for D by taking a least counterexample to its (Boolean) complement.

**Proof.** Fix the coefficients in the ring R. Units are ±1 by discreteness, so any nonzero constant of R has externally finitely many signed divisors. We classify the unary tests in the ordered ring, not just over ordinary prime fields.

1. In `Frac(R)[T]` cancel the gcd of `f,g`, taking a common scalar multiple of the two reduced polynomials `F,G` to put them in `R[T]`. Away from finitely many zeros of the cancelled polynomial the ratio, hence the divisibility test, is unchanged. Identically zero inputs are handled separately. For coprime `F,G` there are `U,V∈R[T]` and `0≠c∈R` with `UF+VG=c` (clear denominators in a polynomial Bézout identity). If F has positive degree, then `F(t)|G(t)` forces `F(t)|c`; there are finitely many possible values of `F(t)` and finitely many inverse images of each. Zeros of the denominator are handled by the convention above. **Do not** apply this finiteness assertion when F is constant: e.g. `m|t`.
2. For constant nonzero modulus m, `m|G(t)` is a finite union of cosets of principal nonzero ideals (empty and all allowed). Factor m externally. At `π^e` the exponent is standard. Remove a common power of π from the coefficients and lower e, or return all if they vanish to order e. A nonzero reduced polynomial has finitely many residue roots in the field `R/π`; no finiteness of that field is needed. In each lifted class `t=r+πs`, substitute and divide the polynomial by π, then recurse at `e−1`. This gives a finite tree of cosets; impose all prime-power conditions by CRT.
3. Polynomial comparisons cut the real closure line into finitely many cells. Each has endpoints floored in R by IOpen; in particular a nonempty interval intersected with a **positive progression** `b+mR` has a first nonnegative member (round in the progression coordinate). First split the cells at all finitely many exceptional points and handle each singleton separately. A Boolean combination of the tests then reduces on each remaining interval to disjunctions of one progression minus finitely many progressions: intersections of positive cosets are a coset or empty by PID arithmetic. In its progression coordinate k, each prohibition is empty, all, or a coset modulo `d>0` (solve one linear congruence by gcd). Combine the standard moduli, split into their finitely many allowed progressions. In any such subprogression each remaining prohibition, after change of coordinate, still has nonstandard period (division by a standard common divisor cannot make it finite). Starting at the first interval point there, each of J such prohibitions can kill at most one of the first `J+1` consecutive indices. If the interval ends earlier just inspect the finite list. This gives a first point whenever the piece is nonempty. Finite union proves the claim. ∎

For example this covers minimizing `u` in `(4Cu−1)|(p+u)` with C **fixed as a parameter**, but does not let one quantify over C and then minimize. It covers tests `G(t)≡0 mod m` and their negations for arbitrary m as a parameter; it does not test whether a varying polynomial value is in the image of the squaring map modulo a prime.

**A real separation, not just syntax.** In the first successor over ℤ of `LOGIC_BEZOUT_NORM_COLLECTION.md`, take its new `h>0` with landing zero. The one-witness quadratic predicate in t

`J(t;h) := 0<t≤h ∧ ∃j≤h [h=t(2j+1)]`

has no least solution: it contains h, but each solution has landing zero since the odd cofactor has nonzero (odd) integer landing. Thus `3|t` and `t/3` has zero landing and is a smaller solution with odd cofactor `3(2j+1)`. The predicate is in D over IOpen: `t|h ∧ 2t∤h` for `0<t≤h`. No smaller later witness can repair this bounded failure in an end extension. Consequently even `IOpen + Bézout + PD + TS + Coll + BCM` does **not** imply `L(D)` (use the final chain of that note, starting at ℤ; the all-old-divisibility of each successor gives BCM). No claim that its collection model has Lag. In particular this is strict over `IOpen+Bézout`. Strictness over the **entire** first-order z74 PID axiom list, including Dirichlet, NR and Lag, is not deduced from that example.

## 2. A parameter-free induction test that the PID really fails

For each standard nonsquare `d≥2`, fix an ordinary positive integral Pell unit `e=a+b√d>1`, `a²−db²=1`. Write `K=2a+1`. Define a **parameter-free** E₁ formula

`W_d(n) := ∃u,v≤K(n+1) [n≤u ∧ u²=1+dv²]`.

For `d=2` one can use `K=7`. **IOpen already proves the base and successor implications**: `(1,0)` works at 0. If `u>n` keep the pair; otherwise `u=n≥1` and multiply by e, obtaining `u'=au+dbv`, `v'=bu+av`. Since `√d v<u`, `n<u'≤(a+b√d)n<2an`, and `v'≤u'`; these estimates are ordered-field algebra (or square to remove radicals). Thus `IW_d` is equivalent over IOpen to `∀n W_d(n)`. In particular even **parameter-free** two-witness induction forces Pell solutions in a constant-factor interval at every scale. More generally for variable `d≥2` and any given positive Pell unit, the same proof with parameters works; the upper factor can be taken to be `2a+1`. This applies at any prime P failing ES in a hypothetical model of IE₁ **if a unit at P is given**, including one obtained by squaring a negative-Pell unit. It neither posits a unit for all P nor takes its nonstandard powers by fiat.

**Proposition 2.** In the unmodified joint PID of `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md` (not the negative-Pell-root variant M3), the solutions of `u²−2v²=1` are all standard. In particular `W₂(n)` defines the standard cut in the cone. Exactly this induction axiom, with **no parameters** and a degree-two equation, fails there, in the z74 five-square BCM end extension of this PID, and in the norm/collection chain started over ℤ as below (failure of the axiom, not necessarily the same cut at later stages). None of these three models even satisfies IE₁⁻.

*PID proof.* Base-change every rational stage to `k=ℚ(√2)`. The base norm plane is just a polynomial ring over k, with constant units. At every edge units descend in this real constant extension: over the previous fraction field extended by k, each graded domain of L2 is still a domain (linear, `DH−V²`, `X²+Y²`, or rank-four form). Nonnegative degrees add, so units have degree zero. The rational-stage contraction after base change is by monic forms or the two row charts, equivalently by flat field base change of `B∩Frac(A)=A` and geometric integrality. More explicitly write in the basis `1,√2`, which remains independent over the new fraction field since the stages are geometrically integral. Then an element in the old extended field has both coefficients in the old field, and hence in A. Apply this also to its inverse. This is the constant-quadratic version of `LOGIC_PELL_UNIT_GROUP.md` §2. Hence a Pell unit `u+√2 v` in the final ring belongs to k and `u,v∈R∩ℚ=ℤ`. Standard powers of `3+2√2` cover standard heights by the successor argument in ℕ. End extensions preserve the failure `¬W₂(n)` at each old nonstandard n since **both** quantifiers are bounded by an old term. The base/successor implications continue to hold. Elementary extensions preserve the failed sentence as well; recursive saturation of an elementary extension cannot help.

*Norm/collection proof.* In the first successor over ℤ, the same quadratic base change has constant units. For polynomial and row steps use the preceding proof (the coprime row stays coprime after this finite étale constant extension, or use coefficientwise contraction and a generic line). At a controlled split every unit on the localization chart over `A_k` is an old unit times `T^j`; T is a nonunit even after base change (its tangent fibre is nonempty), so j=0. At a norm stage the localized chart is `A_k[X,Y,1/(X²+Y²)]`. Here `X²+Y²` is prime (monic, `−1` nonsquare in the ordered extended old field); its units are old units times powers of `X²+Y²`, by primality, without needing a UFD. The latter is a nonunit before localization, since the quotient by it is `(A/(q))[X,Y,W]/(X²+Y²)` base-changed to k, nonzero by faithful flatness. This proves unit descent by induction on stages. The first `h` therefore fails `W₂`; bounded absoluteness again suffices through the chain. Independent faster witness for the collection case: the failed least-number axiom for `J(t;h)` in §1. A failure of **least** for one witness does not by itself give failure of **ordinary induction** for a one-witness formula.

## 3. Repair, rather than just another obstruction: the torus construction

**Theorem 3.** There is a countable discretely ordered normal ring with IOpen, Lag, and a prime `P≡1 (840)` above all standard integers with `¬ES(P)`, satisfying **simultaneously for all standard nonsquares d** the exact induction axioms `IW_d`. For each standard nonsquare d≥2 it even has the least-number scheme minimizing u for `∃v≤u [u²−dv²=1 ∧ φ(u,v,c)]` with arbitrary open filters φ and arbitrary parameters c. These are assertions of the model on its whole cone, not just below a chosen cutoff. This theory including open induction and Lag is strictly strengthened by the displayed window axioms (Prop. 2). The construction does not require an unproved IE₁ tower lemma.

Here are the construction details; the key issues are keeping discreteness when adjoining a point at a *prescribed height*, and ensuring that taking roots does not destroy the local arithmetic.

**Scale group.** In an ordered field write `[u]=[v]` for positive u,v if their ratio is bounded above and below by positive standard rational constants. Work in a compatible real closure; the quotient of its positive multiplicative group by this convex subgroup is ordered and divisible torsion-free, hence a ℚ-vector space in additive notation. This quotient is only an external construction tool. At all stages of a countable tower keep `A` an ordered geometrically integral ℚ-algebra, `A`'s bounded elements exactly ℚ. Start with `ℚ[P]`, P positive infinite. In addition to the old polynomial floor and infinite-input rank-four sphere edges of `LOGIC_POSITIVE_PELL_AUDIT.md` §2, use the following edge. We do not include that note's variable-radicand Pell stages.

For every positive **squarefree standard** d>1 track the group of units of `A_k=A⊗k` for `k=ℚ(√d)` ordered by `√d>0`. Our invariant: these units are `k^×` times products of the recorded `d`-torus generators to **rational** powers with finite support. The pure products have norm 1 under `√d↦−√d`, have positive real value, and their real coordinates specialize to `(1,0)` at the local marks described below. Initially all these lists are empty. Floor lines and spheres preserve the invariant: each generic graded algebra is still a domain over the extended old field; contraction of constants is coefficientwise as in §2. These edges also preserve `A∩{bounded}=ℚ`, by the floor and fresh angular residue proofs cited above. They work for countable, not necessarily finite-type, coefficients: each actual polynomial and its coefficients are finite, and fresh residues are chosen over a countable field.

At a `d,H` task (`H∈A` positive infinite), let V be the ℚ-span of scales of recorded d-generators. If `[H]∈V`, skip. Otherwise take a new positive symbol `T=Hθ` with θ bounded and with positive residue in `(1,2)` transcendental over the natural residue field of the **entire old real closure**. Take its consistent positive rational powers. Over k use the group algebra `A_k[T^ℚ]` (finite sums; Laurent polynomial algebras in `T^(1/N)` in a directed union), and take for B its fixed ring under the simultaneous involution on k and `T↦T⁻¹`. This is an *algebraic* involution, not an ordered-field automorphism. Each finite level is the constant norm-one torus

`A[X,Y]/(X²−dY²−1),   X=(T^(1/N)+T^(−1/N))/2,   Y=(T^(1/N)−T^(−1/N))/(2√d)`.

Indeed after extension to k these are inverse Laurent presentations, and in the fixed algebra coefficients pair under conjugation. In particular B is obtained from A by scalar extension from a directed union of geometrically integral ℚ-tori (transition maps are power maps). It stays geometrically integral; modulo any old prime element it stays a nonzero domain by geometric integrality over the residue fraction field and flatness over ℚ. This applies at P as well. Each finite level is smooth over A and normal if A is normal; the directed union is normal in its union fraction field. At polynomial and sphere stages use the normality/primality lemmas of the cited z74 note (sphere at zero is a normal quadric cone). For the Noetherian hypotheses in these normality citations, maintain a presentation of each A as a directed union of geometrically integral normal **finite-type** ℚ-algebras. For a sphere restrict to a cofinal system containing its target s; take the new sphere over each finite piece. The monic presentations embed compatibly, and the old finite-type normality lemma applies there. A polynomial line is treated identically. For the root tower use finite pieces tensored with the individual smooth tori at levels N (directed jointly by inclusion and divisibility). The maps are injective by the Laurent presentations after scalar extension. Directed-union normality follows by placing a monic equation and the numerator and denominator of its root together in a finite piece. Start with the finite-type normal norm-free base `ℚ[P]`.

**Why bounded pollution is impossible.** Write a fixed element as `Σ c_q T^q` with `c_−q=conj(c_q)∈A_k`. If the sum is bounded, every `c_q H^q` is bounded: divide by a largest absolute coefficient after substituting `T=Hθ`, and reduce the resulting Laurent polynomial in the transcendental residue of `θ^(1/N)`. It cannot vanish. For a surviving `q≠0` the two opposite blocks imply `c_q conj(c_q)` is nonzero and bounded in `A`, hence in `ℚ^×`. Thus `c_q` is a unit of `A_k`; moreover `|c_q|H^q` is bounded **away from zero** as well, by the nonzero rational norm and the bound on its opposite block. The unit invariant then gives `−q[H]=[|c_q|]∈V`, impossible. Only the old constant block remains. This is why the skip test uses the *divisible* span, not just previously serviced heights. A naive new angular parameter at every height would produce bounded nonconstant elements, e.g. the trace of the ratio of comparable Pell units.

**Updating all unit lists.** Over `k` the new algebra is the full Laurent union with units precisely `A_k^× T^ℚ` (a unit and its inverse occur at a common finite level). For any other real quadratic `l=ℚ(√d')`, extend from l to `lk`; at each finite level the units are old units over `lk` times a Laurent monomial. The `lk/l` conjugation negates its exponent, so a fixed unit has exponent zero and already belongs to `A_l^×` by scalar descent. Geometric integrality gives all the field embeddings used here, also at finite levels. Hence the invariant is simultaneous for every standard d. Record T for this d. The scale vector of T is `[H]`.

**Descent and marks.** A tuple in B uses a common finite level (take a common multiple of denominators of exponents). Its generic algebra over `F=Frac(A)` has top form `X²−dY²` irreducible since `√d∉F` by geometric integrality. L2 thus descends every positive reciprocal tuple at P to A, using the monic normal form for contraction. The same descent holds on floor and sphere edges. No claim that infinite divisible tori have finite type is needed.

Start with `P` marked at each ℓ by a square unit in `ℤ_ℓ`, in `1 mod 840` locally (a transcendental square unit may be used). Marks here are **homomorphisms on the algebras** to `ℚ_ℓ`, not necessarily field embeddings. Extend every torus mark by evaluation at identity `(1,0)` at *every root level*, compatible with all power maps. Mark a floor variable by zero. For a sphere of value s at a mark, choose a four-square representation in `ℤ_ℓ` when the marked value is integral (including zero), in `ℚ_ℓ` otherwise: compactness/density and ordinary Lagrange, then denominator scaling. These evaluations are homomorphisms, so no generic lifting is needed. Each fixed element is integral at almost all places since old elements have this property and each expression is finite. There is **no** claim of finite valuation support of nonzero values or external PID. In fact a torus ordinate specializes to zero everywhere, so imposing injective marks as in the PID would be wrong. All polynomials used in L2 are identities in the ordered tower, *before* applying any mark.

Dovetail all tasks on each countable stage (including floors in the ordered real closure of its fraction field). The stages and countably generated unions above are countable. For the union put `R=A∞∩⋂ φ_ℓ⁻¹(ℤ_ℓ)`, i.e. the set of all-place integral elements of the algebra. Standard denominator clearing gives `R⊗ℚ=A∞`, and `R∩ℚ=ℤ`, so it is discrete. For floors, subtract one rational matching an approximant's finitely many fractional parts (CRT with denominators); this disturbs its finite archimedean error by a standard amount. Shift by a standard integer for an exact floor. Shepherdson gives IOpen. If an element of `Frac(R)` is integral over R it first lies in normal `A∞` by its monic equation, then each well-defined mark of this element is integral over `ℤ_ℓ`, hence integral. Thus R is normal even though the marks may have kernels. Lag holds: every positive infinite s belonging to R has been serviced, its witnesses are integral at **all** marks, and standard s use standard witnesses. P is prime in R: it is prime in the union and a unit at every mark, so division by it in the union preserves integrality of the quotient. By L2 any ES triple there would consist of polynomials in P with positive leading coefficients; their integrality on the unit-square progression modulo a common coefficient denominator contradicts L3 of `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md`. No specialization of all Lag witnesses to an ordinary square is involved.

**Coverage at every height.** At the end `[H]` for each positive infinite H in R belongs to the recorded scale span for each squarefree d. There is consequently a pure torus monomial w of precisely this scale. It is an actual positive norm-one unit in `A∞[√d]`, with real coordinates integral at all marks (identity specialization). For `d'=m²d` we may use the same w, with its ordinate divided by the standard m: identity specialization still gives an integral zero. For either radicand multiply w by a *standard integral* Pell unit `e^j` of that radicand, `j∈ℤ` standard, so that `2H≤w e^j<2eH`: this is possible because the ratio of w and H lies between positive standard constants. The resulting integral coordinates `u=(w e^j+(w e^j)^−1)/2` and `v=(w e^j−(w e^j)^−1)/(2√d')` are integral by multiplication of the two pairs, not by an assumption that 2 is invertible in R. They satisfy `H≤u≤eH+1≤(2a+1)(H+1)` and `0≤v≤u`. Finite heights are standard. This proves all window axioms.

**Least-number assertion.** In the positive norm-one branch `s=u+√d v≥1`, the coordinates are strictly increasing in s (u strictly increases for s≥1, v likewise). An open polynomial filter cuts it into finitely many intervals and points with endpoints in the real closure, by substitution of the Laurent expressions. At every finite positive left endpoint of an interval on this half-line (meaning an endpoint in the field, possibly infinite over ℚ), there is a first integral unit above it, strictly above if required. For a bounded endpoint this is ordinary Pell discreteness; for an infinite endpoint take its R-floor and use the window theorem to find a unit on a comparable scale, then multiply by standard powers of the ordinary **least** norm-one unit e>1 to bracket the endpoint (`s` is between u and `2u`). No integral unit lies between consecutive translates: their ratio would have integral coordinates by norm-one inversion, and both coordinates of any ratio in `(1,e)` are nonnegative and standard bounded, contrary to the ordinary leastness of e. For an inclusive endpoint allow equality. Apply the right endpoint test and take the least across finitely many nonempty pieces. This proves the stated filtered one-witness least-number scheme. It is not unrestricted quadratic induction with parameters in the radicand or with moving modular constraints. ∎

## 4. Logical boundaries and next test

* **PROVED (rel. Parikh; context).** Neither IE₁ nor even IΔ₀ proves global BCM (positive common multiples on each bounded interval). This is a **growth** question, not a Π₁ independence claim. By Parikh boundedness (`data/z66/ie1/sources/hajek_pudlak_chV_jina.md`, Ch. V), a proof in IΔ₀ of `∀b∃L C(b,L)` for the bounded matrix of `LOGIC_BCM_HULL_AND_ODD.md` would give a polynomial bound for some such L. Already in ℕ any multiple of `1,…,b+1` is ≥`2^b/(b+1)`: every binomial coefficient `binom(b,k)` divides `lcm(1,…,b+1)` since its prime exponent is the sum of carry terms `⌊b/p^i⌋−⌊k/p^i⌋−⌊(b−k)/p^i⌋∈{0,1}`, zero for `p^i>b`. Now take the largest binomial coefficient. Model-theoretically one can instead take the polynomial cut about an infinite element in a model of true arithmetic; bounded induction is absolute there but no common multiple for that interval lies in the cut. BCM is not a necessary checkpoint on a route to IE₁.
* `Lag⇒P₄` over the base; no proof of either from IE₁ was found (`Z66_IE1.md` §2, §4). Smith 1993 §3's reciprocity is one-directional with a **standard** prime; the reverse direction absent a root is not one of his theorems. In particular lack of a square root of −3 at `P≡1 mod 3` is **not by itself** a counterexample to Smith's implication. Smith §4's two-square equivalences are conditional on residuosity, not proofs that every prime `1 mod 4` represents −1 residually. `¬ES` and “a is a quadratic nonresidue” are bounded **universal** tests: Wilmers' LE₁ does not automatically pick a least failure of ES or a least nonresidue. Conversely it *does* minimize, e.g., a divisor >1 or the first denominator among existing triples using the polynomial ES bounds (`LOGIC_BEZOUT_NORM_COLLECTION.md` §4). Existence is essential.
* Smith 1993 (NDJFL) works **inside** models of induction. Smith 1993 (J. Algebra) constructs models of **IOpen**, not IE₁. Wilmers proves necessary properties including the PA initial segment (quoted by D'Aquino 1997). D'Aquino Thm 1.2 reports Kaye's results about true universal bounded-existential sentences and parameter-free induction; these do not give existential closures of arbitrary discretely ordered rings preserving `¬ES(P)`. These source limitations and the primary/secondary coverage for Kaye and Wilmers are documented in `data/z66/ie1/lit_ie1.md`; the two archived NDJFL texts are primary. IE₁+full collection entails PA by Enayat–Łełyk–Visser Thm B.2; adding both is **not** a harmless union of the z73 axioms. An end extension of a *bad* PID cannot cure the parameter-free test of §2. Conversely having an old good PA initial segment says nothing about a new bad height.
* Next test: relative Pell windows for **nonstandard radicands** with a supplied norm-one unit, particularly at a negative-Pell ES-failing P (the variable-radicand IW axiom in §2). Over nonconstant d, coordinates contain `1/√d`, and a naive tower of power maps on `X²−dY²=1` has split fibre `X²=1` at d=0, imperiling primality at P. The normalized z74 Pell chart fixes one zero fibre but does not yet give the unit bookkeeping of §3. Another concrete unsolved boundary is a moving residue-root search `∃v<m(t): v²≡g(t) mod m(t)` rather than the fixed-polynomial divisibility atoms of §1.
* Falsifier checks: all ES failures above are nonstandard prime failures with the same unit-square obstruction **only after reciprocal descent**. They imply no new ordinary composite or prime exception, no change to the known (★)-zero list, no assertion about a separate numerator-eight/C-even counting mechanism, and no independence from PA or ZFC.
