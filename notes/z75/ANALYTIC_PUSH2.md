# A two-thirds lower-tail estimate on both parities of (★)

**Theorem.** Write V_{a,b}(n) for the number of ordered positive words xyz−x−z=n with v₂(x)=a and v₂(y)=b, for fixed nonnegative integers a,b with a+b≥3 and b≥2. There are constants c,δ>0 (depending only on a,b) such that in every interval I=(M,M+H] of positive integers (M≥0, H large), all but

    O(H exp(−c(log H)^{2/3})) primes p in I

have V_{a,b}(p) ≥ δ(log H)^{2/3}. In fact the witnesses counted by this lower bound can be taken with mutually distinct primes ℓ, each the largest prime factor of f=xy−1=kℓ, where f ≤ exp(O((log H)^{1/3})). The exceptional bound holds more generally for inputs coprime to all primes up to B(log H)^{2/3} for a suitable constant B. For **all** integers in I put L=(log H)^{2/3}/(log log H)^{1/3}. All but O(H exp(−cL)) have V_{a,b}(n)≥δL with distinct witness primes and f ≤ exp(O((log H log log H)^{1/3})). The restriction a+b≥3 ensures the U₇ and square screens; no analytic step requires that particular threshold for fixed a,b with b≥2.

**Applications.** Put (u,B,C)=(x,z,y/4); then 4uBC−u−B=n, so every word is counted by R(n). Since 8|xy and x<f, U₇ holds at the *same* f, r=x. For (a,b)=(1,2), C is odd, v₂(f+1)=3, so r neither divides (f+1)/8 nor is a multiple of 8. This certificate is genuinely among the extra U₇ targets of `Z60_LOPEZ.md` Lemma 7(iv), not a member of U⁽⁸⁾ at this f. For (a,b)=(0,3), C is even and one obtains a witness on the R₈ line. A union bound gives the two conclusions **simultaneously**, with common constants. In particular the size bound for {p:R(p)=0} improves on the 1/2-exponent prime-modulus sieve of `Z56_FAMTRY.md` Thm A; it also improves the prime bound of `Z68_U7O.md` Thm A. The published Pomerance–Weingartner Thm 1.3 concerns arbitrary m/n solutions using Vaughan's ratio classes (their §4); that theorem as stated does not enforce these word shapes. This is a lower-tail distinction between the actual witness sets, **not** an argument toward U₇ at every prime: on a density statement finite failures of either family are allowed. No external priority asserted.

## 1. Atoms with exact 2-adic valuations

We adapt the **existing** one-divisor multiplier sieve (`I3_DEEP_ASTRA.md` §§8.7–8.8 and `EXSET_BEYOND_CRT.md` §§3.1–3.7). Neither the multiplier nor the quadratic supply argument itself is new. The changes are the exact residue condition on the middle letter, its use on both (★) sublines, and an exponential majorant for the positive lower tail. In particular there is no inference U⇒(★) or U⇒U₇ here.

Set t=log X large, K=⌊X^{1/480}⌋, D=K^{10}. Use disjoint full dyadic blocks (h,2h] starting at h=√X and ending with 2h≤X (there are ≍t). Write z_h=h^{1/6}. Let 1∈J₀⊆{odd integers k≤K} be a multiplier set specified below. Choose k∈J₀, a prime ℓ∈(h,2h], and an odd w∈(D,z_h]. Put u=2^a w and impose

    kℓ ≡ 2^{a+b}w − 1  (mod 2^{a+b+1}w),     n ≡ −u  (mod kℓ).       (1)

If (1) holds, f=kℓ, x=u, y=(f+1)/u=2^b times an odd integer, z=(n+u)/f∈ℕ. Hence xyz−x−z=n, v₂(x)=a and v₂(y)=b. For large X, x<ℓ≤f and f≤KX. At fixed ℓ distinct atoms have distinct residues modulo ℓ: equality forces w=w' by size, then w|k−k' with w>K. In particular a hit at each ℓ supplies a distinct word (f determines ℓ since ℓ>k). For the analytic count, (1) is **one reduced class** for ℓ modulo q_w=2^{a+b+1}w whenever (k,w)=1: the numerator 2^{a+b}w−1 is odd and −1 mod w. Fixed a,b are essential to the constants.

## 2. Prime supply in each fibre

Work in the uniform residue distribution modulo the lcm of all moduli under consideration; this is only for the CRT calculation. Fix c=n mod lcm(J₀). Put J={k∈J₀:(k,c)=1}, h(J)=∑_{k∈J}1/k. At active k, w ≡ −c 2^{-a} (mod k); thus (w,k)=1, and no inactive k can contribute. With F_c(ℓ) the number of available hits at ℓ, the conditional count Z of hits is a sum of independent Bernoullis of mean μ_c=∑ F_c(ℓ)/ℓ. Conditioning also on a selector involving primes ≤K does not affect independence at ℓ>K. Uniformly in c and J₀ we have

    c₁ t h(J) ≤ μ_c ≤ C₁ t h(J).                                      (2)

Details to check the changed progression. For each odd d≤K² and invertible target A mod d, the odd w≡A(d) are a unit class mod 2d. With Λ=log(z_h/D)≍t, ∑1/w=Λ/(2d)+O(1/D), and ∑1/φ(q_w)≪_{a,b}Λ/d over these w. The latter follows from the reciprocal-totient sum of `I3_DEEP_ASTRA.md` §8.7 (or dropping oddness and using φ(q_w)≥φ(w)). Directly: expand w/φ(w)=∑_{e|w} μ²(e)/φ(e); for e≤√D the inner harmonic sum on w/e gives Λ/d+O(e/D) for (e,d)=1, while for e>√D use O(1+log z_h) without the progression. The error after the prefactor 1/e is ≪ D^{-1}∑_{e≤√D}1/φ(e)+(1+log z_h)∑_{e>√D}1/(eφ(e))≪(1+t)D^{-1/4}, using φ(e)≫√e; this is o(Λ/K²). The main sum ∑1/(eφ(e)) converges.

In a block the sum over (k,w) of the principal prime count (li(2h)−li(h))/φ(q_w) is ≍h h(J), using φ(q_w)≤2^{a+b+1}w for the lower bound and K/D negligible. The multiplicity for q_w is r(w)=#{k∈J : k|c+2^a w}. By expanding its square and using the upper sum at d=[k,k']≤K², we have ∑ r(w)²/φ(q_w)≪t∑_{k,k'≤K}1/[k,k']≪t⁴ (write k=gi,k'=gj with g their gcd). For the maximum reduced-class discrepancy E_h(q) of the dyadic π count, BT gives E_h(q_w)≪h/(φ(q_w)log h), and BV gives ∑_w E_h(q_w)≪h t^{−10} (these moduli are distinct and ≤const·h^{1/6}). Thus the total absolute error over the pairs is at most (∑ r(w)² E_h(q_w) · ∑ E_h(q_w))^{1/2} ≪ h t^{-7/2}, negligible as h(J)≥1. Division by ℓ≍h and summation over the blocks give (2).

## 3. From fibre means to a positive lower tail on integers

**Prime/rough case.** Choose J₀ all odd k≤K, with h(J₀)≍t. Set m=t², y=m, P_y=∏_{p≤y}p and S=1_{(n,P_y)=1}. Conditional on S=1, define W=∑_{y<p≤K,p|n}1/p. CRT gives E exp(yW) ≤ exp(∑_{p>y}(exp(y/p)−1)/p) ≪ 1, using e^v−1≤2v for 0≤v≤1. Therefore P(W>1/2 | S=1)≪exp(−y/2). Removing k not coprime to c loses harmonic weight at most W h(J₀), since the multiples of p in J₀ contribute at most h(J₀)/p. Thus, on good fibres μ_c≥c₂m, uniformly μ_c≤C₂m.

**All integers.** Use instead m=t²/log t, y=m, S≡1 and J₀={k≤K:(k,P_y)=1}. Here h(J₀)≍log K/log y≍t/log t (`I3_DEEP_ASTRA.md` (8.12)): the upper bound is Mertens' product over y<p≤K; for the lower bound take the product up to K^η with small fixed η. The tail k>K divided by that product is ≤exp(−1/η+O(1)) by Rankin at 1/log(K^η) and ∑_{p≤z}log p/p=log z+O(1). Again removed multiples of p cost at most h(J₀)/p, and the same W tail applies. Thus both cases give μ_c≥c₂m outside O(e^{−c₃m}) fibres and μ_c≤C₂m everywhere in the selected population.

Given c, (1) and distinctness imply E[2^{−Z}|c]≤exp(−μ_c/2) and E[binom(Z,j)|c]≤(C₂m)^j/j! (also after the rough selector). Consequently E[S·2^{−Z}]≪exp(−c₄m). For even d equal to a sufficiently large constant times m rounded up to even, put Q_d(Z)=∑_{j≤d}(−1/2)^j binom(Z,j), with j starting at 0. Taylor's formula for (1−v)^Z on [0,1/2] gives 0≤Q_d(Z)−2^{−Z}≤2^{−d−1}binom(Z,d+1) for integer Z≥0 (zero error if Z≤d). For sufficiently small δ₀>0,

    ν = 2^{δ₀m} S Q_d(Z) ≥ S 1_{Z≤δ₀m},       Eν ≪ exp(−c₅m).

Here d is chosen so the factorial remainder decays exponentially even after the factor 2^{δ₀m}. Integer transfer: there are at most K X·X^{1/6} atoms. Binomial polynomials expand into intersections of distinct classes, each empty or one class modulo an lcm. The optional selector costs ≤2^{π(y)} terms. Total absolute coefficient sum is ≤exp(O(mt)), including the real scalar 2^{δ₀m}. Counting each class on I costs at most one beyond H times its mean; the period itself is **not** a boundary error. Therefore the number of selected inputs with Z≤δ₀m is ≪ H exp(−c₅m)+exp(O(mt)), uniformly in M. For primes, add at most y primes removed by S. Choose t=α(log H)^{1/3} in the first case, t=α(log H log log H)^{1/3} in the second with α small so exp(O(mt))≤H^{1/2}. Every counted hit of Z is a genuine word on every positive input in its progression, even near the start of the interval. This proves the theorem.

## 4. Screens, scope and next push

* F1: f≡7 mod 8, every r|f+1 is a Jacobi residue mod f: for odd prime s|r, reciprocity gives (s/f)=(−1/s)(f/s)=1, and (2/f)=1. Thus (n/f)=−1 at a hit; no odd square hits. F2: the (1,2) words cannot be numerator-eight target words at this f; (0,3) is deliberately the C-even subline, and its known zeros do not violate a density bound. F3/F4: neither 285 composite nor 14 prime (★)-zeros are asserted to have a hit; both slices there must be empty. No contradiction with the eight odd nonsquare U₇-zeros. B6: this argument **is** an average, evaluated on an actual interval; never used pointwise.
* Why not 3/4 for ES? The exponent here comes from t² supply and t cost per atom, within the existing word CRT ceiling. Increasing the polynomial level by EH just rescales the constant. `EXSET_CHAR_FORCING.md` §§5–7 and `EXSET_LINNIK.md` Thm 3.1 require an extra implication from exceptional primes to a common character bias. A GRH upper bound on the least q with (q/p)=−1 does not show that an ES failure forces |∑_{q∈S}(q/p)|≥η|S| on a fixed block S. That latter implication is the missing input to the quadratic large sieve here. Round 1's subgroup correction has no reason to fit the zero-overflow box. We did not obtain that implication.
* Why not below L[1/2] in the cap? In the simplest adaptive variant choose any k primes ≤Y dividing K, without fixing the alphabet. For prime p fixed, q≤X and a product D of k distinct odd primes coprime to p, K≡0 mod D still imposes q≡−p mod D. At D≤√X, BT and a union bound give ≪(X/log X)(∑_{3≤v≤Y, v prime}1/(v−1))^k/k! candidate q, even dropping the exact valuation conditions. For Y≤X=p^{O(1)} the sum is O(log log p); at k≫√(log p/log log p) this costs exp(−Ω(k log k)), not merely a fixed-prime artefact. **Restricted assessment:** this is an upper bound on candidates with such a product ≤√X, not a lower bound on the true cap and not an obstruction for alphabets with large product or a different conditioning/deletion proof. Replacing v³ by v in the modulus only changes a constant in log ∏v. Thus no sub-L[1/2] claim here.
* Sharpness within the named architectures: `EXSET_BEYOND_CRT.md` Cor 2.5 (bounded-B word classes) caps the prime-law saving at exponent 2/3; our atoms have kℓ≤ℓ^{1+2/480}. Restricted to n≡1 (4), each of our classes is a union of native ES₅ refinements (y is 4-divisible). `EXSET_I3.md` Cor 7.1 caps the **whole-avoider coefficient-sum method** there at (log H)^{2/3}/(log log H)^{1/3}, including our zero-hit specialization. Neither caps arbitrary interval methods.
