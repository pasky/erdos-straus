### 1. Isolating one cubic sign, without factoring a Mersenne number

**Theorem 1 (PROVED, elementary).** Write h=qd, q=3^j, j≥1, (d,6)=1, H=2^h−1. Put B_a=1+2^a mod H (subscripts modulo h), and let R₃ be generated by B_a/B_b for equal gcd(a,h)=gcd(b,h) a power of 3, including 1. Write g=[3] in the quotient of the unit group by R₃. Then

    ord(g) | 2·3^{j−1}.

More precisely g^{3^{j−1}} is the class of the unique CRT sign which is −1 at N=1+2^d+2^{2d} and +1 at H/N. This holds even with d composite. The sign might have smaller order *in the quotient*. The bound does not assume anything about ord_h(2). In particular g²=1 for every 3∥h and g⁶=1 for every 9∥h, even when d is arbitrarily large. Neither conclusion here is in the unit-only quotient.

**Proof.** All B_a are units (2 has odd order at every prime of H). Put w=2^d. The factors w−1 and N_i=1+w^{3^{i−1}}+w^{2·3^{i−1}}, i=1,…,j, multiply to H and are pairwise coprime: a prime of H is not 3, and its order of w distinguishes the factors. On N_i, w has exact order 3^i even after reduction at each prime. Define

    F(x,w,q) = ∏_{0≤t<q, t≡1 mod 3} (1+x w^t)/(1+x w^{−t}),
    Z = F(2,w,q) / F(8,w³,q/3),             F(x,w,1)=1.

On w−1 both products are 1. On N_i put z=w^{3^{i−1}}, and L(y,z)=(1+yz)/(1+yz^{-1}). The numerator F is L(2^{3^{i−1}},z)^{3^{j−i}}: use ∏_{v=0}^{r−1}(1+Tη^v)=1+T^r for η=w³ of odd order r=3^{i−1}, an identity valid modulo N_i since the r roots are distinct at each prime (equivalently the differences are units, so the polynomial identity holds over the ring). For i≥2 the denominator gives **the same expression**, as 8^{3^{i−2}}=2^{3^{i−1}}. For i=1 it gives 1, whereas L(2,z)=−1 by 1+z+z²=0. Thus Z is the claimed sign at all prime powers, not a Jacobi symbol.

To count its class, let G_i denote [B_{3^i}], so G₀=g and G₁=g² by 1+2³=9. Each relevant exponent in the first F is 1±dt and coprime to d; in the second it is 3(1±dt) and also coprime to d. For t≡1 mod 3 exactly one choice of ± gives multiples of 3. Orient the ratio so these are in the numerator, inverting **both** F's if necessary (Z^{-1}=Z). In the first numerator the numbers 1±dt range over *all* multiples of 3 mod q once; the denominator contributes G₀^{q/3}. If j≥2 the second numerator ranges over all multiples of 9 mod q once, with the same gcd classes as their occurrences in the first; its denominator contributes G₁^{q/9}. Cancelling leaves 2q/9 copies of G₁ upstairs, plus q/9 from dividing by the second denominator. Hence [Z]=(G₁/G₀)^{q/3}=g^{q/3}. For j=1 the first ratio already has class G₁/G₀ and the second is empty. Squaring proves the bound. ∎

**Assessment.** For h=3^j the exponent bound itself also follows from the single doubling orbit. It is the identification with a sign at the *bottom* cubic layer and the bound independent of the entire 3-free cofactor that are new here. This does not prove g⁶=1 up the tower.

### 2. Core theorems and the density distinction

**Theorem 2 (PROVED rel. z78 `PROTH_ALLM.md` §§1,4,6 and Dirichlet).** Retain h=3^j d as above, m=(h+1)/2, k=2H+1. For h≥9 put L=2·3^{j−1}. Either of the following tests suffices for an effective progression Pℕ of positive exponents s with interior good words and ES at n=k2^s+1. Its closure meets the profinite unit squares (hence no finite eventual ET/divisor cover, rel. `EXPONENT_COVERING.md` Thm 1.4).

1. 2^b≡−1 mod h, b≥1, and b mod L is 0,1,2 or 3.
2. For a divisor a≥4 of h+1, 2^b≡−a mod h and L|b (b≥0).

In (1) the quotient class of −1 is g^b: multiply the orbit product ∏_{0≤i<b} B_{2^i}=2^{2^b}−1 by 2, a ratio B₁/B₋₁. An exponent t=b mod L up to 3 can be paid by selecting t of the three slots Φ₂(2)=3, Φ₆(2)=3 and c=B_m=2^m+1 (each of class g; take c=1 if not selected). These are legitimate single uses; c|k and c≡1 mod 16. In (2) take c=k/(2^a−1)≡1 mod 16. Modulo H, −1/c=1−2^a=2^a∏_{i<b} B_{2^i}, of class g^b=1. Apply the distinct-factors criterion of z78 §4. For closure include the order of 2 at any fixed odd modulus in s, obtaining k+1=4^m there, and 1 at the fixed dyadic part for large s.

**Density (PROVED rel. Mertens/Dirichlet and `notes/z75/CONJS_GEN.md` Thm 2, itself rel. computer-assisted Z64_SDEEPF Thm 3.6).** On q|s, either test gives density one relative to each fixed progression contained there. Indeed all ratios needed have base gcd dividing q. Reserve q in the degree D and choose distinct fresh odd prime factors ℓ of s in the prescribed unit classes mod h for every copy of (1+X^{g'ℓ})/(1+X^{g'}), g'|q. These use disjoint factors indexed by ℓe, e|g'; they avoid the exceptional slots. Take D=q times the product of the selected primes. Strip the h-part of s/D, then remove a spare prime in the resulting residue class mod h to obtain source r≡1 mod h; a further spare prime ≥5 ensures r≥4. Then s/(rD) is odd. The required finite multiplicities including spare primes occur with relative density one by the fixed-progression CRT variance argument of z78 §6. On 3∤s, k≡1 mod 7 gives n≡3,5 mod 7, hence a good word. Thus for j=1 we have density one relative to **every** fixed progression; for j>1 we make no conclusion this way when 0<v₃(s)<j. A positive general §4 core decision alone does not imply full density.

**Infinite classes (PROVED rel. Theorems 1–2).** Test 1 includes *every* h≥9 with 3∥h or 9∥h and −1∈⟨2 mod h⟩: in the second case b≡3 mod 6 by reduction at 9. Indeed Test 1 cannot reach j≥3: reduction modulo 3^j forces b≡3^{j−1} mod L, since 2 has order L there (LTE). Only Test 2 reaches those levels here. Examples for Test 1 are h=3·11^r or 9·11^r (r≥1), since 2⁵≡−1 mod 11 lifts to 2^{5·11^{r−1}}≡−1 mod 11^r; use an odd multiple of that exponent also divisible by 3 for the 9 family. The z78 hand mechanisms I/II both exclude these h.

Test 2 supplies infinitely many h for **each prescribed j≥1**, not just bounded 3-valuation. By Dirichlet and CRT choose a prime a≡5 mod 8, a≡3^j−1 mod 3^{j+1}. Its order o of 2 has v₂(o)=2 by the supplementary law for (2/a) and 3∤o since a≡2 mod 3. Take infinitely many positive b≡o/2 mod o, b≡0 mod 2·3^j (compatible since gcd(o,2·3^j)=2). Then h=2^b+a is odd of exact valuation j, since 2^b≡1 mod 3^{j+1}; a divides h+1 and 2^b≡−a mod h. No assertion of positive density of such h. These are not pure powers of 3 in general.

### 3. Where the factor of three in the index comes from

**Proposition (PROVED, elementary).** For 9|h, the index of the full gcd-ratio group R is divisible by 6. At H₉=511 the primitive bases 3 mod 7 and 5 mod 73 give the following logs of B_a at a=0,…,8:

    mod 7  (logs mod 6):   2 1 5 2 1 5 2 1 5
    mod 73 (logs mod 72):  8 6 1 12 21 61 60 57 70

For unit indices the first log is odd and their **difference** is fixed: log₇−log₇₃ equals 1 mod 3 on both unit residues (1−0, 2−1). For gcd 3 both logs are constant mod 3 in their components; gcd 9 is a singleton. Thus ratios in R kill both the parity of log₇ and log₇−log₇₃ mod 3. These maps jointly surject onto C₂×C₃ on the ambient group, by CRT and reduction from H onto 511 on units. For g=[3], parity at 7 gives order divisible by 2 even at 3∥h (equal-gcd indices both give even logs if 3 divides them, odd logs otherwise), and when 9|h the difference of the two logs is 1 mod 3. Combined with Theorem 1 this makes the order in its stated quotient exactly 2 at j=1 and 6 at j=2. Both projections separately allow full 3-primary images at h=9: B₂/B₁ has logs (4,−5); the latter generates C₇₂, the former generates the squares C₃. So the obstruction is not a missing cubic residue at 73. In fact this ratio has order 72, giving index 6 exactly at 511.

### 5. Round 2: the exact layerwise sign subgroup on the cubic tower

**Theorem 4 (PROVED, elementary).** Write H_j=2^{3^j}−1, N_i=H_i/H_{i−1} (H₀=1). In the CRT decomposition by N₁,…,N_j define V_j to consist of the 2^j elements *constant* ±1 on each entire layer. Let U_j use only gcd-1 and gcd-3 ratios on h=3^j. The intersection U_j∩V_j consists of **exactly all choices with sign + at N₁=7**. In fact the upper-layer sign generators belong to the smaller group C_j generated by X_a/X_b, for 3∤ab, where X_a=(1+2^a)^3/(1+2^{3a}). No determination of signs varying across primes within a composite N_i.

Proof. For j≥2 these X ratios use exactly the allowed two orbits. If a runs over the three lifts of a unit index from 3^l to 3^{l+1}, the product of the three X's has coordinates (X_a³,1) modulo (H_l,N_{l+1}): on the latter the three numerators before cubing multiply to 1+2^{3a}, and the three denominators are identical. This is the transfer C_l→C_{l+1} on ratio words, z↦(z³,1); taking a cube preserves every sign. At level i≥2 take d=3^{i−1} and start with X_{1+d}/X_{1+2d}. On H_{i−1} it is 1. On N_i use w=2^d, 1+2w=−(1+2w²); the denominators are equal, so this ratio is −1. Iterate the transfer to j. The resulting explicit product runs once through all lifts of each of those two indices mod 3^i. Thus every upper-layer sign is available independently. Conversely modulo 7 the gcd-1 ratios are squares and the gcd-3 ratios equal 1, but −1 is nonsquare. ∎

**Corollary (PROVED, rel. the certified z78 level-5 X-product).** For j≥6 the order of [3] modulo U_j divides 6·3^{j−6}, a factor 81 improvement over the elementary bound 2·3^{j−1} on this range. It is still an increasing bound. Indeed 3^6∈C₅ by `data/z78/proth/tower-6.json` (level **5** for the X-product, not 6). If 3^e∈C_l with l≥2, transfer gives (3^{3e},1)∈C_{l+1}. With d=3^l, X₁²/(X_{1+d}X_{1+2d})=(1,27), by the three-lift norm and X₁=3. Hence 3^{3e}∈C_{l+1}. At the last step there is no further loss: ∏_{t=0}² B_{a+td}/B_{3a} has coordinates (X_a,1), so ratios of such expressions lift C_l into U_{l+1} on the old component. Also B₁²/(B_{1+d}B_{1+2d})=(1,3) there by z78 §10. This proves the bound. Splitting the signs does **not** imply splitting a 3-primary quotient. The remaining obstruction is not an unidentified ± sign on some new layer.

### 6. Core and density in a multi-parameter odd-order family

**Theorem 5 (PROVED rel. LTE, CRT and z78 §7).** Each h=7^{4t+2}, t≥0, has *odd* ord_h(2) but admits a core with square-unit closure and good-word density one on every fixed exponent progression. More generally choose any nonempty subset of the following rows and put h=∏p^{e_p} with e_p≥1. Every such h≡−1 mod 5 has the same conclusions and odd order. This is a core via a multiplier prefactor, not a claim that bare −1 belongs to R. The condition h≡−1 mod 5 is just one affine congruence modulo 4 on the vector of powers, since every listed p≡2 or 3 mod 5.

|p|7|23|167|263|383|863|887|983|
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|ord_p(2)=q_p|3|11|83|131|191|431|443|491|
|log₂(−5) mod q_p|1|6|27|20|67|257|212|381|

Proof. Each entry satisfies v_p(2^{q_p}−1)=1 (small modular power checks). The order at p^{e_p} is q_p p^{e_p−1} by LTE. Therefore the entire preimage of ⟨2 mod p⟩ on units belongs to ⟨2 mod p^{e_p}⟩, by the kernel size. In particular −5 lifts, without requiring the same log as at p. These new orders are odd and **pairwise coprime** because the two prime lists are disjoint and have no repetitions within each list. CRT gives a simultaneous power 2^b≡−5 mod h. Now use a=5 in z78 §7 with c=k/31≡1 mod 16: the telescope yields −1/c=1−2⁵ in the unit-index ratio group. This seed-lifting argument, not a claim to a new telescope, supplies the odd-order family. With all eight primes, imposing the congruence leaves a positive fraction of the eight-dimensional exponent box. No assertion of positive integer density of these h. The earlier z78 §7 already had infinitely many h with −1 outside the doubling orbit, which by itself does not require the order to be odd.

**Boundary (PROVED rel. quadratic reciprocity).** For p≡7 mod 8 prime and h=p^r with r odd, **no** divisor a of h+1 satisfies −a∈⟨2 mod h⟩. For each odd prime l|h+1, the order of p mod l has 2-part exactly 2. Thus (p/l)=+1 when l≡1 mod 4 and −1 when l≡3 mod 4. In both cases (l/p)=+1 by reciprocity. Also (2/p)=+1. Thus all a|h+1 are squares mod p while −1 is not. This excludes only the single-divisor sign test; the integer H is not assumed prime and the core itself is not refuted. In particular lifting at 7 does not dispose of odd powers of 7.

### 7. General reflected products: a gluing obstruction

**Proposition (PROVED, local algebra).** For p an odd prime of odd ord_p(2), no rational product of the terms 1+2^a W^t (a,t integers, arbitrary signed multiplicities), optionally times a nonzero rational constant, can take the values 1 at W=1 and −1 at W=ζ_p in characteristic zero. More generally any such product has equal reductions at those two arguments modulo the prime (1−ζ_p), if it has value 1 at the first. Indeed all 1+2^a are p-units by odd order, and W→1 under this reduction. The rational constant must be a p-unit too. Hence 1≡−1 mod p would be necessary. The cubic exception is real: 1+2 is not a 3-unit. This rules out copying the *universal rational product identity* even with several base exponents, not sign generation after integer specialization and reduction mod H. One can still isolate unsigned values at a primitive layer by cancelling equal higher norms just as in §1; doing so does not change this gluing constraint.

### 8. The periodic transfer and a moving core can coexist

Thus the residual set for this combined certificate has relative density zero in every one of the 28 classes. The 28 are exactly the misses of the transferred fixed rows at s≥64 (not intrinsic ES failures); the small exponents already have ES. There is no claim that the residual set is empty.

Proof. Take the explicit T,O in `data/z79/proth/intersection.json`, M the odd part of T. These are positive certificate checks; neither T nor O needs to be minimal. Choose a prime a≡21 modulo the dyadic part of 2T, a≡z mod M, and a≡2 mod every odd prime l|O with l∤M. CRT and Dirichlet apply; choose a>3. Then a≡5 mod 8, so o=ord_a(2) has 2-part exactly 4; gcd(o,O)=4 because no odd prime of O divides a−1. Also a|h+1, 2^b≡−a mod h, and b is even. This is Test 2 of §2 with L=2, including its full density claim, simultaneous with the fixed rules of §4. ∎
