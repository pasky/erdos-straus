### 1. Isolating one cubic sign, without factoring a Mersenne number

**Theorem 1 (PROVED, elementary).** Write h=qd, q=3^j, j≥1, (d,6)=1, H=2^h−1. Put B_a=1+2^a mod H (subscripts modulo h), and let R₃ be generated by B_a/B_b for equal gcd(a,h)=gcd(b,h) a power of 3, including 1. Write g=[3] in the quotient of the unit group by R₃. Then

    ord(g) | 2·3^{j−1}.

More precisely g^{3^{j−1}} is the class of the unique CRT sign which is −1 at N=1+2^d+2^{2d} and +1 at H/N. This holds even with d composite. The sign might have smaller order *in the quotient*. The bound does not assume anything about ord_h(2). In particular g²=1 for every 3∥h and g⁶=1 for every 9∥h, even when d is arbitrarily large. Neither conclusion here is in the unit-only quotient.

**Proof.** All B_a are units (2 has odd order at every prime of H). Put w=2^d. The factors w−1 and N_i=1+w^{3^{i−1}}+w^{2·3^{i−1}}, i=1,…,j, multiply to H and are pairwise coprime: a prime of H is not 3, and its order of w distinguishes the factors. On N_i, w has exact order 3^i even after reduction at each prime. Define

    F(x,w,q) = ∏_{0≤t<q, t≡1 mod 3} (1+x w^t)/(1+x w^{−t}),
    Z = F(2,w,q) / F(8,w³,q/3),             F(x,w,1)=1.

On w−1 both products are 1. On N_i put z=w^{3^{i−1}}, and L(y,z)=(1+yz)/(1+yz^{-1}). The numerator F is L(2^{3^{i−1}},z)^{3^{j−i}}: use ∏_{v=0}^{r−1}(1+Tη^v)=1+T^r for η=w³ of odd order r=3^{i−1}, an identity valid modulo N_i since the r roots are distinct at each prime (equivalently the differences are units, so the polynomial identity holds over the ring). For i≥2 the denominator gives **the same expression**, as 8^{3^{i−2}}=2^{3^{i−1}}. For i=1 it gives 1, whereas L(2,z)=−1 by 1+z+z²=0. Thus Z is the claimed sign at all prime powers, not a Jacobi symbol.

To count its class, let G_i denote [B_{3^i}], so G₀=g and G₁=g² by 1+2³=9. Each relevant exponent in the first F is 1±dt and coprime to d; in the second it is 3(1±dt) and also coprime to d. For t≡1 mod 3 exactly one choice of ± gives multiples of 3. Orient the ratio so these are in the numerator, inverting **both** F's if necessary (Z^{-1}=Z). In the first numerator the numbers 1±dt range over *all* multiples of 3 mod q once; the denominator contributes G₀^{q/3}. If j≥2 the second numerator ranges over all multiples of 9 mod q once, with the same gcd classes as their occurrences in the first; its denominator contributes G₁^{q/9}. Cancelling leaves 2q/9 copies of G₁ upstairs, plus q/9 from dividing by the second denominator. Hence [Z]=(G₁/G₀)^{q/3}=g^{q/3}. For j=1 the first ratio already has class G₁/G₀ and the second is empty. Squaring proves the bound. ∎

**Assessment.** For h=3^j the exponent bound itself also follows from the single doubling orbit. It is the identification with a sign at the *bottom* cubic layer and the bound independent of the entire 3-free cofactor that are new here. This does not prove g⁶=1 up the tower.

### 2. Core theorems and the density distinction

**Theorem 2 (PROVED rel. z78 `PROTH_ALLM.md` §§1,4,6 and Dirichlet).** Retain h=3^j d as above, m=(h+1)/2, k=2H+1. For h≥9 put L=2·3^{j−1}. Either of the following tests suffices for an effective progression Pℕ of positive exponents s with interior good words and ES at n=k2^s+1. Its closure meets the profinite unit squares (hence no finite eventual ET/divisor cover, rel. `EXPONENT_COVERING.md` Thm 1.4).

1. 2^b≡−1 mod h, b≥1, and b mod L is 0,1,2 or 3.
2. For a divisor a≥4 of h+1, 2^b≡−a mod h and L|b (b≥0).

In (1) the quotient class of −1 is g^b: multiply the orbit product ∏_{0≤i<b} B_{2^i}=2^{2^b}−1 by 2, a ratio B₁/B₋₁. An exponent t=b mod L up to 3 can be paid by selecting t of the three slots Φ₂(2)=3, Φ₆(2)=3 and c=B_m=2^m+1 (each of class g; take c=1 if not selected). These are legitimate single uses; c|k and c≡1 mod 16. In (2) take c=k/(2^a−1)≡1 mod 16. Modulo H, −1/c=1−2^a=2^a∏_{i<b} B_{2^i}, of class g^b=1. Apply the distinct-factors criterion of z78 §4. For closure include the order of 2 at any fixed odd modulus in s, obtaining k+1=4^m there, and 1 at the fixed dyadic part for large s.

**Density (PROVED rel. Mertens/Dirichlet and `notes/z75/CONJS_GEN.md` Thm 2, itself rel. computer-assisted Z64_SDEEPF Thm 3.6).** On q|s, either test gives density one relative to each fixed progression contained there. Indeed all ratios needed have base gcd dividing q. Reserve q in the degree D and choose distinct fresh odd prime factors ℓ of s in the prescribed unit classes mod h for every copy of (1+X^{g'ℓ})/(1+X^{g'}), g'|q. These use disjoint factors indexed by ℓe, e|g'; they avoid the exceptional slots. Take D=q times the product of the selected primes. Strip the h-part of s/D, then remove a spare prime in the resulting residue class mod h to obtain source r≡1 mod h; a further spare prime ≥5 ensures r≥4. Then s/(rD) is odd. The required finite multiplicities including spare primes occur with relative density one by the fixed-progression CRT variance argument of z78 §6. On 3∤s, k≡1 mod 7 gives n≡3,5 mod 7, hence a good word. Thus for j=1 we have density one relative to **every** fixed progression; for j>1 we make no conclusion this way when 0<v₃(s)<j. A positive general §4 core decision alone does not imply full density.

**Infinite classes (PROVED rel. Theorems 1–2).** Test 1 includes *every* h≥9 with 3∥h or 9∥h and −1∈⟨2 mod h⟩: in the second case b≡3 mod 6 by reduction at 9. Examples h=3·11^r or 9·11^r (r≥1), since 2⁵≡−1 mod 11 lifts to 2^{5·11^{r−1}}≡−1 mod 11^r; use an odd multiple of that exponent also divisible by 3 for the 9 family. The z78 hand mechanisms I/II both exclude these h.

Test 2 supplies infinitely many h for **each prescribed j≥1**, not just bounded 3-valuation. By Dirichlet and CRT choose a prime a≡5 mod 8, a≡3^j−1 mod 3^{j+1}. Its order o of 2 has v₂(o)=2 by the supplementary law for (2/a) and 3∤o since a≡2 mod 3. Take infinitely many positive b≡o/2 mod o, b≡0 mod 2·3^j (compatible since gcd(o,2·3^j)=2). Then h=2^b+a is odd of exact valuation j, since 2^b≡1 mod 3^{j+1}; a divides h+1 and 2^b≡−a mod h. No assertion of positive density of such h. These are not pure powers of 3 in general.

### 3. Where the factor of three in the index comes from

**Proposition (PROVED, elementary).** For 9|h, the index of the full gcd-ratio group R is divisible by 6. At H₉=511 the primitive bases 3 mod 7 and 5 mod 73 give the following logs of B_a at a=0,…,8:

    mod 7  (logs mod 6):   2 1 5 2 1 5 2 1 5
    mod 73 (logs mod 72):  8 6 1 12 21 61 60 57 70

For unit indices the first log is odd and their **difference** is fixed: log₇−log₇₃ equals 1 mod 3 on both unit residues (1−0, 2−1). For gcd 3 both logs are constant mod 3 in their components; gcd 9 is a singleton. Thus ratios in R kill both the parity of log₇ and log₇−log₇₃ mod 3. These maps jointly surject onto C₂×C₃ on the ambient group, by CRT and reduction from H onto 511 on units. Both projections separately allow full 3-primary images at h=9: B₂/B₁ has logs (4,−5); the latter generates C₇₂, the former generates the squares C₃. So the obstruction is not a missing cubic residue at 73. In fact this ratio has order 72, giving index 6 exactly at 511.

**EVIDENCE (one PARI implementation).** `data/z79/proth/indices.txt` gives the full indices at h=9,27,35,77,105,115,119,127,133,143,165,255. No exception to 1,2,6. All prime-power projections are full away from 7 in this panel even using only unit-index ratios. The generator matrix uses one column per equal-gcd ratio to its orbit base. At a primary prime shared by several CRT components the script takes the HNF of the projected log matrix augmented by the diagonal component orders. Unshared primary primes need only local order tests. Independent certification of all the large group structures is not claimed.

### 4. All s for m=11,12; transfer off a sparse exceptional progression

**Theorem 3 (PROVED, computer-assisted rel. Mordell and z77 `PROOF_S_WILD.md` §2, as checked by z78 §3).** ES holds at (4^m−1)2^s+1 for m=11,12 and every integer s≥0, no primality hypothesis. The final certificates are `data/z79/proth/period-{11-3825360,12-55440}.json`. For m=11 the all-unit X row has degree 10395 (3³·5·7·11); at the intermediate period 166320 the gaps after the K rules were 24,83184. Refining by 23 exposes the prime divisor 47 on s≡1 mod 23 and enough further fixed rows. For m=12 degree 3465 works at period 55440. The standalone z78 reader checks every unit source for X, every fixed row including its periodicity after divisions, complete coverage at s≥64 and the 64 direct triples for each m. The z79 wrapper also asserts the miss lists are empty. No reliance on probable prime values of n or on density for this conclusion.

**Corollary (PROVED rel. Theorem 3).** There is an effective progression of m containing infinitely many m≥11 for which ES holds at **all** s not divisible by 41580, simultaneously. In particular it covers every s=B·2^v, v≥0, for every positive odd B not divisible by 10395, even with 3|B. This is not an all-s theorem for infinitely many m.

Indeed omit just the X row from the m=11 cover. Take T an even common multiple of the orders of 4 modulo the odd parts (>1) of all fixed-row test moduli in the z78 reader (so that 4^m≡4^{11} there when m≡11 mod T). At s≥64 the dyadic part of n is also unchanged since the dyadic parts of these fixed-row moduli divide 2^64 (checked in the replay). The word and II lower size bounds only improve. For the finitely many 1≤s<64 enlarge T by ord_{n_{11,s}}(4) (odd n_{11,s}); then n_{11,s}|n_{m,s}, so scale its checked triple. At s=0 use scaling from 4. Thus the same fixed rules cover every claimed sparse progression. This transfer is an off-zero-core result: z78 §5's unshifted failure and the bounded-menu rigidity for full cores remain intact.
