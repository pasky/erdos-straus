# Pulling the sieve back through a quadratic without losing its exponent

**Theorem A (PROVED rel. the §0 inputs).** There are absolute positive constants $b,c$ such that, on **every** interval of $H$ consecutive integers, for $F(j)=Aj^2+Bj+C$, $A>0$, $\Delta=B^2-4AC\ne0$, $\max(|A|,|B|,|C|)\le \exp(b(\log H)^{1/4})$, at most $O(H\exp(-c(\log H)^{3/4}))$ parameters have $F(j)>0$ and no ES solution. In fact, with this same exceptional-set bound, **simultaneously every positive integer** $j^2+d$, $0<|d|\le\exp(b(\log H)^{1/4})$, has an ES solution. (The constants may shrink.) Does not need a conjecture on primes of the form $j^2-3$. No claim per prescribed centre. The new point is not the 3/4 of the unrestricted interval (`paper/es-threequarter-note.tex`, hereafter TQ; its priority qualifications apply): even the unrestricted bound up to $H^2$ says nothing on just $H$ quadratic values.

**Theorem B (PROVED rel. the same sieve inputs without Vaughan).** Write $L=(\log H)^{2/3}/(\log\log H)^{1/3}$. Under A's hypotheses, outside $O(H e^{-cL})$ parameters with $F(j)>0$, each of the following two shapes occurs at least $cL$ times with distinct moduli $f$ within the shape:

* $n=xyz-x-z$, $f=xy-1$, $v_2(x)=1,v_2(y)=2$;
* the same with $v_2(x)=0,v_2(y)=3$.

Here $n=F(j)$ and $x,y,z$ are positive integers. Thus both parities of $(★)$ occur, $C_{★}=y/4$. The first shape gives $U_7$ with $r=x$ in the extra, non-numerator-eight targets at this $f$ (`notes/z75/ANALYTIC_PUSH2.md`, hereafter AP2). If only parameters giving prime values are counted, replace $L$ in both occurrences by $(\log H)^{2/3}$ (still an absolute count of parameters). An entire punctured band as in A works for B by union bound. In particular there are good interior words for $n\equiv1\bmod4$ in this almost-all statement: $v_2(xy)=3$ gives $8\mid(n+y)xyz$, the sign criterion of `Z64_SDEEPF.md` Thm 2.1. Here $x>h^e$, $y\ge4$, and we discard small $n$ to ensure $z\ge2$ as detailed below. The quantitative conclusion improves the density-zero statement of `Z60_MS105.md` Thm 3 for nondegenerate ($\Delta\ne0$) quadratics within our coefficient range only; that older qualitative theorem also covers higher degrees and nonsquare multiples of squares. Not a new all-polynomial statement.

## 1. What's different in the pullback

Take a TQ atom: $k$ odd, $\ell$ prime, $f=k\ell$, $4uv\mid f+1$, $(u,v)=1$, $nv\equiv-u\pmod f$. With $w=(f+1)/(4uv),\ s=(nv+u)/f$ the triple is $suw,nsvw,nuvw$. For the one-sided word version take $v=1$, $x=u=2^a w'$ with $w'$ odd and prescribe $f\equiv 2^{a+b'} w'-1\pmod {2^{a+b'+1}w'}$, $(a,b')=(1,2),(0,3)$. Then $y=(f+1)/x,z=(n+x)/f$. Use $q=4uv$ in the ratio version and $q=16w'$ in the word versions. In each case $\ell$ is in one reduced class $a_k\bmod q$ when $(k,q)=1$. The factor 4 is in the construction of the triple, not in the prime-counting estimate.

Pulling an atom back by $n=F(j)$ replaces its one large-prime cell by

$$1+\left(\frac{D}{\ell}\right)\quad\text{cells},\qquad D=v(\Delta v-4Au). \tag{1}$$

Indeed $(2Avj+Bv)^2\equiv D\pmod\ell$ there. In the ranges below $\ell\nmid 2Av$. A direct application of Bombieri–Vinogradov (BV) over reduced residue classes of the conductor of (1) **loses**: the splitting condition is a union of many classes. We keep the quadratic character and throw away the pairs for which any twist by a character mod $q$ has problematic zeros. The key is that a prime in the odd squarefree part of $\Delta v-4Au$ outside $2A\Delta$ cannot be cancelled by the twist: it is coprime to $uv$ by $(u,v)=1$. At $\Delta=0$ this assertion of fresh support breaks down completely.

## 2. Split-prime supply in a single small-prime fibre

Put $t=\log X$. Use the full disjoint dyadic blocks $(h,2h]$ from $\sqrt X$ to $X$ (omit a partial last block). Fix $e=10^{-5}$, $K=\lfloor X^{e/1000}\rfloor$, and in each block restrict to

$$h^e<u\le h^{1.1e},\qquad h^{.4e}<v\le h^{.5e} \quad\text{(ratio)},\qquad v=1\quad\text{(word)}.$$

Suppose throughout $\max(|A|,|B|,|C|)\le X^{e/1000}$, which can be arranged by the constant $b$ in A. Each $u$ window in a word row also enforces the stated parity of $w'$. We allow any subset $J$ of the odd $k\le K$, and any *one* $c_0$ coprime to its lcm. Call a triple active when $k\mid c_0v+u$. Define $h_J=\sum_{k\in J}\varphi(k)/k^2$ (ratio), or $h_J=\sum_{k\in J}1/k$ (word); in use $1\in J$. Write $r=2$ or $1$, respectively. The following uniform inequality is the arithmetic input:

$$\sum_{\rm blocks}\ \sum_{\substack{\text{active atoms}\\h<\ell\le2h}} (1+(D/\ell))/\ell \asymp t^r h_J. \tag{2}$$

No averaging in $c_0$ is used for (2). Constants can depend on the fixed $e$. Here and below all estimates are for sufficiently large $H$. We give the details of the two terms separately.

**Untwisted supply.** With weight 1 instead of $1+(D/\ell)$ this is TQ's cubic-supply proof and AP2's one-divisor proof, with smaller rectangular boxes. For clarity: on a block, the sum of $1/(uv)$ over coprime pairs with $(v,k)=1$, $u\equiv-c_0v\ (k)$ is $\gg_e t^2\varphi(k)/k^2$. In the proof of TQ's lattice lemma replace the two harmonic log lengths by the two corresponding log ratios: imposing a common divisor $d$ and summing with $\mu(d)/d^2$ gives leading factor $\prod_{p\nmid k}(1-p^{-2})$. With $Y=h^{.4e}$ the endpoint errors are $O(t\tau(k)\log h/Y+t^2/Y)$, by the $O(d/Y)$ harmonic progression errors for $d\le Y$ and the trivial harmonic bounds for $d>Y$. Explicitly, denote the lower endpoints by $U_0,V_0$, the log ratios by $\lambda_u,\lambda_v$. After dividing both variables by $d$ coprime to $k$, the unrestricted inner harmonic sum over $u/d$ in one class is $\lambda_u/k+O(d/U_0)$ by summation by parts. The sum over unit $v/d$ is $\lambda_v\varphi(k)/k+O(d\tau(k)/V_0)$ by inclusion-exclusion. For $d\le Y$ that outer sum is also $O(t)$ without coprimality. Multiplying, then summing with $\mu(d)/d^2$, gives the displayed total endpoint error (the $d>Y$ tail is $O(t^2/Y)$). Consequently the main term is $\lambda_u\lambda_v\varphi(k)k^{-2}\prod_{p\nmid k}(1-p^{-2})$; the error is relatively $o(1)$ since $k/\varphi(k)\le\tau(k)\le2\sqrt{k}$ and $K^2\ll Y$. This rectangular proof has no congruence restriction on odd $k$. For the word version, the odd $w'$ occupy a single class modulo $2k$, giving $\gg_e t/k$. The upper sums with reciprocal totients are $\ll_e t^r h_J$ by enlarging to the windows $K^{10}<u,v\le h^{1/6}$ of the TQ upper lattice bound, or AP2's reciprocal-totient bound on $w'$ (its harmonic-sum proof applies with this smaller $K$). Extra factors at 2 cost only a constant. BT therefore gives an upper count $\ll h t^{r-1}h_J$ per block, even after doubling for roots.

For the lower count, $1/\varphi(q)\ge1/q$ and BV controls the errors. In detail let $W(q)$ count indexed active triples with this modulus. TQ's weighted second incidence lemma gives $\sum W(q)^2/\varphi(q)\ll t^7$ by enlarging to $K^{10}<u,v\le h^{1/6}$, grouping coprime products and using Cauchy; its *stated* hypothesis restricts $k\equiv1\ (4)$, but the proof groups pairs by $[k,k']$ and then bounds the sum by that over **all** $k,k'\le K$, which proves the extension used here. No use of TQ's separate asymptotic for the harmonic sum on $1\bmod4$. In the word case AP2 §2 gives $\ll t^4$ by the same enlargement and the lcm sum over $k,k'$. BT bounds the maximum discrepancy $E(q)$ by $O(h/(\varphi(q)\log h))$, BV bounds $\sum E(q)\ll h/t^{30}$ for these moduli. Thus $\sum W(q) E(q)=o(h/t)$ by Cauchy. This works on any fixed positive-length subinterval after scaling by $h$ inside $(h,2h]$, by taking differences of the BV endpoint estimates. In particular the untwisted count is $\gg h t^{r-1}h_J$ also on the middle third of the block.

**Twist pruning (PROVED rel. Montgomery's density theorem and the smoothed explicit formula).** Fix a smooth nonnegative bump supported in $(1,2)$, bounded by 1 and equal to 1 on its middle third. Let $Q=h^{5e}, T=h^{10e}, \eta=6e$. List every primitive nonprincipal character of conductor $\le Q$ with a nontrivial zero $\beta+i\gamma$, $\beta\ge1-\eta$, $|\gamma|\le T$, and add the trivial character to the list unconditionally. There are at most $h^{.02e}$ entries for large $h$, by the classical bound $(Q^2T)^{3\eta/(1+\eta)}(\log(2QT))^6$ (Montgomery; `sources/omega9/montgomery-mnt3.pdf`, Thm 28.6, (28.30)). This is an upper bound on the **whole list**, not a zero-free strip for each progression.

Discard $D=0$ and every pair where for some character $\chi\bmod q$ the primitive inducer of $\chi(\cdot)(D/\cdot)$ is on the list (use the Kronecker character at odd primes). Its conductor is always $\le4q|D|<Q$ for $D\ne0$ in these boxes: $|\Delta|\ll h^{.004e}$, $|\Delta v-4Au|\ll h^{1.103e}$, and $q\ll h^{1.6e}$. For each listed conductor $d$ and each $v$, every discarded $u$ it accounts for must satisfy

$$\Delta v-4Au=\pm s z^2,\quad s\text{ squarefree},\quad s\mid 2A\Delta d.$$

Indeed an odd prime dividing the left to odd order outside $2A\Delta$ divides neither $u$ nor $v$, hence neither $q$ nor $v$, and ramifies in the primitive twist. **This is why the nonzero discriminant matters.** The integer $2A\Delta d$ has at most $h^{o(1)}$ divisors, uniformly. Thus at most $h^{.58e}$ values of $u$ per $v$ are discarded including the entire list and the possible zero. Bounding all $k$ by their number $K$, the lost sum of $1/\varphi(q)$ is $\ll K h^{.58e-e+o(1)}\sum_v 1/v=o(1)$, also when $v=1$. By BT this deletes $o(h/\log h)$ from the untwisted block count. The unequal scales in the two boxes avoid a multiplicity problem with quadratic conductors; no distribution of squarefree polynomial values is assumed.

For every remaining character twist, the smoothed sum of $\Lambda(n)$ on the block against the twist is $O(h^{1-5e})$. In detail, with bump $g$ and nonprincipal primitive inducer $\psi$, the smooth explicit formula is $\sum_n\Lambda(n)\psi(n)g(n/h)=-\sum_\rho h^\rho\widehat g(\rho)+O_g(\log(2Qh))$, summing nontrivial zeros with multiplicity. This follows by the Mellin contour shift to $\Re s=-1/2$; a possible trivial zero at 0 contributes $O_g(1)$, the shifted integral is $O_g(h^{-1/2}\log(2Q))$ by the functional equation. To see the uniformity, the Mellin transform of the bump is $\ll_N(1+|\gamma|)^{-N}$ on $0\le\beta\le1$. In the explicit formula the zeros through $T$ give $O(h^{1-\eta}\log(2QT))$ by unit-interval zero count $O(\log(2Q(|\gamma|+2)))$. The tail is $O(h T^{1-N}\log(2QT))$ for fixed $N\ge4$; gamma/trivial terms are logarithmic. The principal pole was excluded; passage from primitive to imprimitive removes only prime powers at prime divisors of $4qD$, costing $O(\log^2 h)$. Removing all higher powers costs $O(\sqrt h\log^2 h)$. Expand the single AP condition in characters modulo $q$; division by $\varphi(q)$ pays for their number. Even after summing the $O(K h^{1.6e})$ indexed triples, the absolute twisted weighted prime-log sum is $o(h)$. Meanwhile the untwisted smoothed prime-log sum on survivors is $\gg h t^r h_J$ by the middle-third lower bound. Removing weights by positivity of $1+(D/\ell)$ and dividing by $2h\log(2h)$ gives the block lower bound implicit in (2). Summing $\asymp t$ blocks proves (2).

## 3. Why parameter transfer works (including imprimitive values)

Use uniform CRT on the **parameter** $j$, with small moduli dividing $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K)$ and fresh independent primes $\ell>\sqrt X$. For a fixed fibre of all small digits, active cells at a single $\ell$ have disjoint root sets: $uv'-u'v$ is too small to be a nonzero multiple of $\ell$; reduced fractions give the same $u,v$, and $q>K$ then forces the same $k$ since $k\ell,k'\ell$ have the same target mod $q$. Two roots of the same quadratic count as distinct cells but not simultaneous hits. Thus the hit count $Z$ is a sum of independent Bernoullis conditionally, with mean given by (2) on the relevant active set.

**Unit repair for full ES.** Let $G$ be the odd primes having an ES solution. We need no list of them: their complement among odd primes has convergent reciprocal sum by Vaughan's ordinary exceptional-set theorem and partial summation. Take $J$ all odd $k\le K$ supported on $G$. Then $h_J\asymp t$: integers avoiding the complementary primes and 2 have positive lower density (finite Euler product, then union bound for the tail on $[1,Y]$); removing those with $\sum_{p\mid k}1/p$ larger than a sufficiently large fixed constant costs arbitrarily small upper density since the mean is bounded by $\sum_p1/p^2$. On what remains $\varphi(k)/k\gg1$, and partial summation gives the lower bound. For each prime power in the small lcm, use $F(j)$ as small digit **unless its prime divides $F(j)$, in which case use the unit 1 throughout this prime tower**. Call the glued residue $c_0$. At large $\ell$ still use the true $F(j)$. All small fibres now have $\mu\asymp t^3$ by (2). A positive ES failure cannot have a prime divisor in $G$, by scaling; thus **every repaired hit at a positive failing value would be a true hit**, impossible. This use of scaling is for full ES only, not for (★) or (S).

For **prime values** use the same repair in the word sieve, now with all odd $k\le K$. Then $\mu\asymp t^2$ everywhere; at a prime value $>K$ no small digit changes. For the word counts discard $0<F(j)\le 2KX$, so $z\ge2$. There are $O(KX)$ such parameters since each integer has at most two preimages. This cost is negligible below. There is no primality assumption on $j$.

For the **all-values word bound** use no repair. Put $m=t^2/\log t$, $y=m$, $J_0=\{k\le K:(k,\prod_{p\le y}p)=1\}$. AP2 §3 (Mertens product plus Rankin) gives $h_{J_0}\asymp t/\log t$. On a small fibre take $J=\{k\in J_0:(k,F(j))=1\}$, $c_0=F(j)\bmod\operatorname{lcm}(J)$. Other $k$ cannot hit. Losing $k$ divisible by $p$ costs $\le h_{J_0}/p$. Outside probability $O(e^{-c m})$ the sum of $1/p$ for $y<p\le K$ dividing $F(j)$ is less than $1/2$: primes dividing the content contribute $O(\log(2\max(|A|,|B|,|C|))/(y\log y))=o(1)$ deterministically, and the rest have independent indicators of means $\le2/p$ in CRT. Their exponential moment at $y$ is $\le\exp(\sum_{p>y} (2/p)(e^{y/p}-1))=O(1)$. Now (2) gives $\mu\gg m$ off these bad fibres, and always $\mu\ll m$. In the other two cases write $m=t^3$ or $t^2$, respectively, with no bad fibres.

**Finite transfer.** In each case $\mathbb E[2^{-Z}]\ll e^{-c m}$ and $\mathbb E{Z\choose i}\le(Cm)^i/i!$ by the independent Bernoulli description. The even Taylor truncation $P_d(Z)=\sum_{i=0}^d(-1/2)^i{Z\choose i}$, $d$ a sufficiently large even multiple of order $m$, lies between $2^{-Z}$ and $2^{-Z}+2^{-d-1}{Z\choose d+1}$. Hence $2^{\delta m}P_d$ majorizes $1_{Z\le\delta m}$ and has CRT mean $O(e^{-c' m})$ for small fixed $\delta>0$. There are $\exp(O(t))$ atoms. For each one, even with repair, the small-digit rule depends **only on $j\bmod k$**, not on the whole lcm; the rule $p\mid F(j)\Rightarrow 1$ is compatible down the tower. An atom is a union of at most $k\ell$ parameter classes mod $k\ell$, hence still $\exp(O(t))$ elementary cells. Each intersection is empty or one class; counting on a parameter interval costs at most one beyond $H$ times its CRT probability. Absolute coefficient norm $\exp(O(mt))$ therefore suffices for the boundary, independently of the position of the interval. The choices, with sufficiently small fixed $\alpha>0$, are:

|case|multiplier set|$m$|$t/\alpha$|
|---|---|---|---|
|full ES|supported on $G$, repaired|$t^3$|$(\log H)^{1/4}$|
|word / prime value|all odd, repaired|$t^2$|$(\log H)^{1/3}$|
|word / all values|$y$-rough, coprime to $F(j)$|$t^2/\log t$|$(\log H\log\log H)^{1/3}$|

The smaller coefficient range shared with A is intentional. Then the boundary is $O(H^{1/2})$. This proves A and B, including distinctness of the moduli since $\ell>k$ is their largest prime factor. The band conclusions cost a union of only $2\exp(b(\log H)^{1/4})$ sets, absorbed in the stated saving. No effective numerical onset is asserted.

## 5. Round 2: the ramified pencil has an exact sign switch

Use $r=1$ for each word row, $r=2$ for ratio atoms. Write $L_r=(\log H)^{(r+1)/(r+2)}/(\log\log H)^{1/(r+2)}$, $P_r=(\log H)^{(r+1)/(r+2)}$. A **tail at scale $V$** means at most $O(H\exp(-cV))$ parameters in any interval of $H$ consecutive integers have fewer than $cV$ genuine hits with distinct $f$ in that row, restricted to the stated population and positive values. Constants may depend on the fixed degree or kernel. For $n\equiv1\ (4)$ the two rows of B imply good interior words; $f\equiv7\ (8)$ gives the stated $U_7$ consequence on its odd domain. Ratio hits are counted by distinct $f$ in the Type II identity of §1; no assertion of a unique primitive ET parametrisation at composites.

**Theorem C (PROVED rel. BV, BT, Mertens and the internal TQ/AP2 incidence bounds).** Let $F(j)=sj^2$ with $s>1$ fixed squarefree. The ratio row has a tail at $L_2$. If $s$ has an odd prime factor, **both** word rows of B have tails at $L_1$. For $s=2$, instead there is a word tail at $L_1$ with $x$ odd, $v_2(y)=2$, $f\equiv3\ (8)$ (a genuine odd-$C_{★}$ row). The equation $n=xyz-x-z$ with $8\mid xy$ has **no** solution at any $2j^2$, not merely fewer solutions. For prime **parameters** $j$ the same tails hold at the log-free $P_r$. None of these hits uses scaling by a factor of $s$, not even in the proof of the exceptional bound. Exponents for unrestricted $j$ here have the displayed log-log loss; we do not drop it.

**Proof.** Keep the windows and multiplier modulus $k$ of §2. For any true active $k$ coprime to $sj$, $(-suv/k)=1$, since $-u\equiv sj^2v\ (k)$. In the ratio row every divisor of $(f+1)/4$ is a Jacobi residue mod $f$ (reciprocity in F1); hence $(-uv/f)=-1$, also in each word row with $v=1$. At $\ell\nmid s$ the number of roots is **exactly**

$$1+(-suv/\ell)=1-(s/f). \tag{3}$$

This uses multiplicativity in the *denominator* $f=k\ell$. The switch is at the whole witness $f$, not necessarily at $\ell$ alone. If $k$ shares a prime with $sj$, there is no active reduced pair. In particular $s=1$ always gives zero. When $s=2$, $f=7\bmod8$ gives zero, also without the prime-times-multiplier restriction: every divisor of $f+1$ has symbol +1 mod $f$ at $f\equiv7\ (8)$, so $f\mid n+x$, $x\mid f+1$ is impossible there. Instead impose $f=3\bmod8$; (3) gives two roots on every active atom. In the ratio row here use odd $u,v$, so fixing $f=3\bmod8$ is compatible with $f=-1\bmod 4uv$; in the word row take $u=w'$ odd and $f=4w'-1\bmod8w'$.

For $s$ with an odd prime divisor $p$, avoid the primes of $2s$ on the odd parts of $u,v$ (in the ratio row simply make $uv$ odd). Take $k$ coprime to $2s$. Refine the original AP for $\ell$ to one reduced class modulo $[q,8s]$ with $(s/k\ell)=-1$. To do so, choose compatible digits for $f$ at 2, unit digits at the odd primes of $s$ other than $p$, and choose the free digit at $p$ by quadratic reciprocity; divide by $k$. The choice depends on the indexed pair but costs at most a fixed modulus factor. This makes both roots available, not merely a lower bound by a principal character term. In particular $(x,s_{\rm odd})=1$ on the word rows.

**Fixed congruence refinements throughout Round 2.** For clarity, restricting odd parts to units at fixed primes and prescribing possible fixed residues on $u,v$ costs a constant in the lower lattice estimate of §2: impose unit classes on the odd variables modulo a fixed $M$ coprime to $k$, then impose $u=-c_0v\ (k)$ by CRT.
