## 1. A cube-divisor certificate with a split successor

**Theorem A (PROVED rel. QR).** Take any positive `x ≡ 1 (8)`, and put

    y=x²+2,    A=Φ₆(y)=y²−y+1,    u=y−x,    t=y+x.

For every `n≡1 (4)` with `n−1=k y³`, `k≥1`, and `k≡u+1 (mod A)`, there is an explicit (★) triple with `C=t/4` **odd**, and `(A/n)=−1`. The whole input test uses factors of `n−1` only and one remainder of k. In particular the test can be run by enumerating cube divisors of `n−1` and testing whether `y−2` is a square whose root x is 1 mod 8. No primality requirement; since y is odd, the n≡1 (4) hypothesis just says 4|k.

*Proof.* The two identities needed are

    y³+1=(y+1)A,                 A+1=y²−y+2=(y−x)(y+x).

The second holds because `x²=y−2`. Write `h=(k−u−1)/A`. Then `n+u=A v` with `v=k(y+1)−h`. Thus `n=tuv−u−v`, `t≡4 (8)`, and `v≥2` since `n+u>2A` for `y≥3`. All slots are positive. With `C=t/4` this is exactly `4Cuv−u−v=n` and the denominators are `Cuv,nCv,nCu`. Since `(A,u)=1`, `(A,n)=1`. By reciprocity for the odd `A≡3 (4)` and `n≡1 (4)`, `(A/n)=(−u/A)=−1`: indeed `(u/A)=1` here, as `u` has exactly one factor 2 (`u≡2 (8)`), `A≡7 (8)` (`t≡4 (8)` times even u), and `d=u/2≡1 (4)` gives `(d/A)=(A/d)=(−1/d)=1` using `A≡−1 (d)`. This works also for nonprime A and n. In particular the construction fails at an odd square at this exact sign step. ∎

**Scope and attribution.** The Φ₆ divisor of `ky³+1+(k−1)` is from `notes/z78/PROTH_ALLM.md` Thm 2 (ratio method: `notes/z78/WILD_ES.md` §1). Here `y=x²+2` factors its successor identically, so both t and u grow, but the condition on k is *equivalent* to the classical Type II class `n≡−u mod A` once `y³|n−1`. Cube divisibility is optional for the ES certificate itself and restricting to it makes the progression thinner. Unlike z78, we are **not** solving a preassigned k on a square-limit core by this congruence.

**Theorem B (PROVED rel. Siegel–Walfisz, Brun–Titchmarsh and the square-unit obstruction; ineffective onset).** The set in Theorem A restricted to `1 mod 840` has positive natural density among the integers and positive relative natural density among primes. More strongly, an explicit subfamily in dyadic intervals `(X,2X]`, `X=2^j`, has **all** its members tending to `1` profinitely and still contains at least `X exp(−O(√log log X))/log X` primes per block. There is a single formula for the parameters for the entire block, not an ES search or an invocation of an ES exceptional-set estimate. The positive-density family admits no finite ET, Mordell polynomial-class or fixed-prime-divisor covering, even eventually. On the thinner dyadic subfamily any such fixed menu hits only finitely many members.

*Proof.* For any multiple M of 840 let m be M stripped of all factors 2 and 3. Set `x=M + m·(m⁻¹ mod 24)` (least positive inverse); thus `M<x≤24M`, `x≡1 (24)` and `x≡0` at each prime factor of M other than 2,3. The resulting A is prime to M: it is odd, is 1 mod 3 (since `3|y`), and at the remaining primes equals 3 mod the prime (since `y≡2`). Fix `k≡0 mod M`, `k≡u+1 mod A`. Then `n=1+k y³` runs, up to an initial segment, through one progression of modulus `Q=M A y³ ≪ M^{11}`. It is reduced: n is 1 at primes of My, and `−u` at primes of A. Every member is `1 mod M`. For fixed M the PNT in progressions suffices for positive lower density; M=840 suffices for positivity. For natural densities truncate at `x≤T`; this is a finite union of progressions up to finitely many points. For integers the tail proportion is `≪ Σ_{x>T}1/y³+O(X^{1/6}/X)` up to X. For primes use Brun–Titchmarsh on `p≡1 mod y³` when `x≤X^{1/12}/2`, giving relative tail `≪Σ_{x>T}1/φ(y³)=o_T(1)` (e.g. `φ(y³)≥y²`). For the dyadic assertion use `B=max(7,⌊√log log X⌋)`, `M=lcm(840,1,2,…,B)`. By Chebyshev `log M=O(B)`; hence the modulus Q is eventually at most log X. Hence all finite menus of the stated kind eventually miss. This is stronger than adjoining a thin escaping subsequence to a dense congruence class. No external prime-value conjecture. ∎

## 3. A power-scale shifted square factor at primes

**Theorem D (PROVED rel. BV, Baier–Zhao's square-moduli BV and Mertens).** Fix `0<α<2/9`. For large dyadic X let `Q=X^α`, `M=lcm(840,1,…,max(7,⌊√log log X⌋))`. There are `≫ X/(M² Q log² X)` distinct primes `X<p≤2X`, `p≡1 mod M`, with an interior (★) word having

    A=tu−1=q prime in (Q,2Q],   q² | p+u,
    C=t/4 a prime in (Q^{1/5},Q^{1/4}],     u=(q+1)/(4C).

Thus all three word letters grow as powers of p, the genuine negative divisor is *repeated* in the shifted number, C is odd, and no fixed menu hits the tail. This is a prime distribution statement with an extra square layer, distinct from the cube-divisor algorithm of §1. It does not assert that the minimum over other words grows at a power rate.

*Proof.* Retain primes `q≡3 (8)` in `(Q,2Q]` having a prime `ℓ∈(Q^{1/5},Q^{1/4}]` dividing `q+1`. There are `≫Q/log Q` such q. Indeed BV in the variable q at moduli `8ℓ` counts the pairs with main term `(Li(2Q)−Li(Q)) Σ_ℓ 1/(4(ℓ−1)) ≫ Q/log Q` by Mertens, error `O(Q/log³ Q)` (partial summation or prime-counting BV). Multiplicity per q is bounded by 5 since the product of six such primes exceeds `2Q+1`. For each retained q choose the smallest qualifying ℓ, fix `C=ℓ,t=4ℓ,u=(q+1)/t`; in particular `(q,u)=1`. This gives one class `p≡−u (q²), p≡1 (M²)` modulo `(Mq)²`, reduced since q exceeds every prime in M. Then `p=tuv−u−v` with `v=(p+u)/q` an actual multiple of q, and its denominators are as in A. The negative Jacobi sign follows either from the 4|t criterion or `(q/p)=(−u/q)=−1` (`u≡(4ℓ)^{-1} mod q`, `(ℓ/q)=(q/ℓ)(−1/ℓ)=1`).

Use S. Baier and L. Zhao, *Bombieri–Vinogradov type theorems for sparse sets of moduli*, Acta Arith. 125 (2006), Theorem 4 (arXiv:math/0602116; the resulting prime lower bound has ineffective onset): for fixed ε,H>0,

    Σ_{b≤X^{2/9−ε}} b max_{(a,b)=1} |ψ(X;b²,a)−X/φ(b²)| ≪_{ε,H} X/log^H X.

Apply at X and 2X, taking `b=Mq` which are distinct, `b≥MQ`, and `2MQ<X^{2/9−ε}` for some fixed ε. This bounds the **unweighted** total error by `≪ X/(MQ log^H X)`. The dyadic von Mangoldt main term summed over q is `≫ X/(M² Q log Q)`. Since `M=(log X)^{o(1)}`, the error is negligible. Prime powers cost at most `O(Q√X log² X)` in total, negligible since `α<1/4`. Dividing by log(2X) proves the incidence lower bound. To pass to distinct primes, count even *integers* in pairs of different classes: by CRT each pair costs `O(X/Q⁴+1)` ignoring M, so all double intersections total `O(X/Q²+Q²)=o(X/(M²Q log² X))`. Bonferroni proves the claim. No individual prime was retrieved from an average. For fixed q the recipe is just one AP; what the sparse-moduli input buys is q at a fixed power of p **and** the second factor q in v, while keeping all word letters large. ∎

## 6. Two factors of n−1 and exact subproduct depth below X^γ

Here **depth** means the number of distinct primes needed *in a positive divisor ≤X^γ of n+u congruent to −1 mod this u*. We take the minimum over that window. A larger prime divisor of the rough cofactor may be −1 mod u: no global minimum is asserted even at this shift. The exclusion of shallower words, not the existence of a word considered in isolation, is what a one-row congruence cannot copy.

The algebraic input is the following identity in **independent** r,s,h. Write `u=r−1, v=s−1`. Then

    n=1+rs[uv·rs h−1],          n+u=rv[K h−1],        K=rs²u,
    (rv[K h−1])/v=r[K h−1]=tu−1,                       t=(rs)²h−1.

Thus a unitary divisor `rs` of `n−1` supplies a factor r and v=s−1 of `n+u` **without asking that a free parameter be −u mod the final A**. More precisely the third factor of `n−1` is coprime to rs, so r and s are disjoint first-power blocks when `(r,s)=1`; this does not assert s is squarefree. The final bracket of `n+u` is already −1 mod u. This identity alone with frozen endpoints would again be a progression. Instead we prescribe parts of both v and the bracket and show that certain growing subproducts are **all possible** hits in a power window, not just selectable hits. Only the following growing regime is asserted as a new family.

**Theorem G (PROVED rel. Dirichlet, QR, Siegel–Walfisz, Bombieri–Vinogradov and the dimension-one lower-bound linear sieve; ineffective onset).** Fix `0<α<β<γ`, `β+2γ<1/2`. On every sufficiently large dyadic block `(X,2X]` there is an explicit family of the displayed polynomial values, `≡1 (840)`, containing at least `X/(log X)^{2+o(1)}` **distinct primes** and at least `X/(log X)^{1+o(1)}` distinct integers. The entire family tends to 1 profinitely. There is a common block shift `u→∞` and an odd `d=d(X)→∞`. On every input, the positive divisors of `n+u` up to `Y=X^γ` congruent to −1 mod u are **exactly**

    A = P times any subproduct of d distinct neutral primes,

where P is squarefree with exactly d distinct primes, disjoint from the neutral ones. All d primes in P are necessary at this shift below Y. P includes a prime `X^α<q≤X^β`, and every A gives (S), U₇ and ES. Thus all the `2^d` witnesses have `A>X^α`, `t=(A+1)/u>X^{α−o(1)}`, and `v_A=(n+u)/A>X^{1−γ}`. These are **not** d distinct primes reused as powers. No lower bound at other shifts. No fixed ET/Mordell/fixed-divisor menu meets even a tail subsequence.

*Packet design.* The following precomputation depends only on the stage, never on which primes p pass. At stage j≥1 put `d=2j+1`, `M=lcm(840,1,…,j)`. Choose a prime λ≡1 mod 2d coprime to M, then a prime `r≡1 mod M`, `r≡1+λ mod λ²`, and set `u=r−1`. At λ take an element of order 2d; at `u/λ` take −1. CRT gives a unit g mod u with order exactly 2d and `g^d=−1` (d odd). Consequently `g^i=−1` for `0≤i≤d` **only at i=d**. Take one prime c≡g and d−1 distinct neutral primes ≡1 mod u, all >2u and ≡1 mod r. Put v equal to their product and `s=v+1`. Thus `s≡0 mod M`, `(s,r)=1` (s≡2 mod the odd prime r). Next take d−2 fresh primes ≡g mod u not dividing `rvK`, and let B₀ be their product. Use least positive choices in successive prime searches and least admissible g (e.g. least at λ); existence is Dirichlet and CRT. All packet quantities here are fixed finite integers for each j. Choose the last stage of the longest initial segment with every `u M r v B₀ K ≤ exp(√log log X)`; stages tend to infinity, since every fixed initial segment eventually fits and M grows without bound. All fixed packet costs below are therefore `(log X)^{o(1)}`. This slow schedule is explicit, not a diagonalization on unknown prime counts.

*Insert a power-sized factor.* Let q range over primes in `(X^α,X^β]` congruent to g mod u (so eventually distinct from all packet primes). Set `B=B₀q`, `T=rvB`, and put

    n=T L−u,          L≡−B⁻¹ (mod K),       P=cB.

Take only `X<n≤2X` with **no prime ≤Y dividing L**. Here `(B,K)=1`, and `h=(BL+1)/K` shows that this is precisely a subfamily of the rectangle above. The underlying unsifted row has modulus `T K` and is reduced: n equals −u mod T, a unit there, and equals `−rv−u=1−rs mod K`, a unit at r,s and at primes of u since `(v,u)=1`. As `M|s`, `n≡1 mod M` so every fixed ET/Mordell polynomial class or fixed-prime-divisor menu eventually misses, by the unit-square obstruction of §1.

Now `T<Y` and all d+D prime entries of the packet product T are distinct (D=d neutral entries, including r≡1 mod u). Every divisor of `n+u=TL` up to Y divides T, because L is Y-rough. Reading its factors mod u, the residue is `g^i`, where i is exactly the number chosen among c, the primes of B₀, and q. There are d of these, forcing all of P and allowing any subset of the others. The restriction in size is essential: roughness alone puts no useful restriction on the residue of a prime *above* Y. Conversely every such divisor divides T≤Y. Since `8|u`, each gives a positive U₇ word and negative sign (`Z68_U7F.md` Lemma 1(a), `DEDEKIND_ES.md` Thm 4.5); explicitly with `w=u/4, f=t v_A−1`, denominators are `wf,tnw,tnwf`. This argument uses the whole group element, not just its quadratic character. The Jacobi sign is impossible at an odd square, even before imposing primality. The constructed r,s are coprime divisors found in the first power of rs in n−1, while the selected A draws essential factors from **both** s−1 and `K h−1`. The latter is *not* itself the divisor asserted to be least.

*Prime count.* Write `D₀=rvB₀ K`, so the progression modulus is `D₀ q`. For a squarefree e coprime to K, `e|L` imposes exactly one reduced class modulo `D₀qe`: in the row L advances by K, and modulo primes of e one has `n≡−u`. For primes dividing K, L is already a unit. For prime inputs the multiplicative sieve density `g_q(e)=φ(D₀q)/φ(D₀qe)` on `(e,K)=1` therefore has prime values `1/(l−1)` off the modulus, `1/l` at its primes, and we set it zero at primes dividing K. In particular prime 2 is omitted. The dimension-one product condition holds **uniformly**: on `2≤w<z`, the inverse product over `[w,z)` is at most `(log z/log w)(1+O(1/log w))` by comparison with all odd primes at `1/(l−1)` and Mertens (enlarge the constant for small w). The full sifting product down to Y is `≫1/log Y`. The lower linear sieve (Rosser–Iwaniec weights of absolute value ≤1, dimension one, level `X^δ`, fixed `2γ<δ<1/2−β`) thus gives per q a positive constant times

    (Li(2X)−Li(X))/(φ(D₀ q) log X)

minus the sum of absolute progression errors up to that level. This uses `δ/γ>2`, not a parity-breaking assertion that L is prime. On summing in q all error moduli are `D₀qe≤X^{1/2−η}` for fixed η>0. A modulus is repeated only `O(1/α)` times, since q divides its quotient by D₀ and `q>X^α`; e is then determined. Dyadic π-form BV gives total error `≪_H X/log^H X` for every fixed H. By Siegel–Walfisz uniformly at the small u and partial summation, `Σ_q 1/q=(log(β/α)+o(1))/φ(u)` over the given range. So the total main lower bound is `≫ X/(D₀ φ(u) log² X)`. In fact each sifted n is counted only once: a different q′ from this range would divide L (it exceeds every precomputed packet prime), impossible since q′<Y. This proves the prime bound. For integers the base count is `X/(D₀q)`, the densities are `1/l` for primes not dividing K, the same dimension-one bound holds, and all floor errors combined are `O(X^{β+δ})`. This saves one more log, as claimed. ∎

*Mechanism comparison (Assessment).* The rectangle enforces the **additional first-power shape of n−1**. It is not needed for the depth phenomenon: drop K and just impose `n≡1 (M), n≡−u (T)` with the same residue packet. Exactly the same rough-cofactor sieve gives the window-depth result at smaller modulus (then omit primes of M and primes dividing u from the sieve, since at p large they cannot divide L). Thus the forcing in G is a planted-residue sieve, not a new algebraic way around the prime lower tail.

*Why a moving u is necessary (PROVED).* The prime factor sequence, with multiplicities, of the smallest divisor ≤Y hitting −1 has no nonempty subproduct 1 modulo u: removing one would give a smaller divisor still hitting −1. Its length is at most `D((ℤ/u)×)−1`, where D is the Davenport constant (least length forcing a nonempty product 1). In particular it is less than φ(u), or at most φ(u)−1, by the prefix-product pigeonhole bound. Thus no fixed u can give G's unbounded minimum depth, even using arbitrary input-dependent divisors at that shift. The growth need not be fast: `log u≫j` from `M|u` and the PNT for lcm(1,…,j); `log v≥d log(2u)` and the stage budget give `d≪(log log X)^{1/4}`. This is an upper bound for this schedule, not a lower rate.

*Why the stronger assertion is not an AP certificate (PROVED, scoped).* A single full unsifted q-row contributes at most `O(X^{1−α}+1)` integers, power-negligible in either count. More importantly the congruences of **that very row** do not force the claimed first hit. Take a further prime b≡−1 mod u, `b>2u`, not dividing `T K`, impose `b|L` by CRT. This remains a reduced prime progression. The resulting inputs have a depth-one word at the same shift instead of least depth d (for large inputs with v_b≥2). b can be preselected at each stage as part of the small budget, so b<Y and b<q. Dirichlet here concerns the infinite ambient row, not an individual prime in the dyadic block; alternatively the same BV sum with the fixed extra b and no sieve supplies such prime counter-rows in the blocks. Dropping the roughness condition would thus change a mathematical conclusion, unlike dropping the cube condition in Round 1. In fact for frozen r,s the outer letters r−1,s−1 already give an ES class for the *unsifted* rectangle via 8|u. We do **not** claim that APs cannot give ES, arbitrarily many words, or prescribed subproducts as *upper* certificates; the sieve proves their *exclusivity in the full shifted integer*. This is also different from a lower bound for the global minimum over all shifts (`notes/z78/WILD_ES.md` §6) or a disproof of the bounded-ω U₇ conjectures in `Z68_U7F.md`. Relative to `notes/z76/PROOF_LIFT.md` §5 this is many forced prime inputs to **one** word divisor at one moving shift, not many unrelated norm-prime hits at fixed u.
