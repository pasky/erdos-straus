# Programming the two sides of a divisor

**Assessment — preflight (FRONTIER §4).** Use the negative Jacobi word and a 4-divisible letter, not character positivity alone (1–3,9). Prescribe two consecutive factors `A, A+1` *before* looking at the putative divisor list at a given n (4,8,16,17). Use distribution of primes only to count inputs, never to guarantee any particular p is in the family (5–7). No collision across shifts, signed reflection, relaxation, BM transfer or nonstandard model (10–13,18–19). Run the named screens below (14). For near squares (15) work **below** the square in the quadratic example; no deformation through the square is claimed. The top homogeneous form obstruction in `Z68_U7O.md` Thm B is a reason NOT to assert that a Gaussian representation plus a bounded-degree word identity suffices for general p. The underlying distinction in this note is between a set *containing* points arbitrarily near 1 and a sequence *converging* to 1. ## 1. A cube-divisor certificate with a split successor

**Theorem A (PROVED rel. QR).** Take any positive `x ≡ 1 (8)`, and put

    y=x²+2,    A=Φ₆(y)=y²−y+1,    u=y−x,    t=y+x.

For every `n≡1 (4)` with `n−1=k y³`, `k≥1`, and `k≡u+1 (mod A)`, there is an explicit (★) triple with `C=t/4` **odd**, and `(A/n)=−1`. The whole input test uses factors of `n−1` only and one remainder of k. In particular the test can be run by enumerating cube divisors of `n−1` and testing whether `y−2` is a square of the required parity. No primality requirement.

*Proof.* The two identities needed are

    y³+1=(y+1)A,                 A+1=y²−y+2=(y−x)(y+x).

The second holds because `x²=y−2`. Write `h=(k−u−1)/A`. Then `n+u=A v` with `v=k(y+1)−h`. Thus `n=tuv−u−v`, `t≡4 (8)`, and `v≥2` since `n+u>2A` for `y≥3`. All slots are positive. With `C=t/4` this is exactly `4Cuv−u−v=n` and the denominators are `Cuv,nCv,nCu`. Since `(A,u)=1`, `(A,n)=1`. By reciprocity for the odd `A≡3 (4)` and `n≡1 (4)`, `(A/n)=(−u/A)=−1`: indeed `(u/A)=1` here, as `u` has exactly one factor 2 (`u≡2 (8)`), `A≡7 (8)` (`t≡4 (8)` times even u), and for its odd part d we have `(d/A)=(−1/d)(A/d)=1` since `A≡−1 (d)`. This works also for nonprime A and n. In particular the construction fails at an odd square at this exact sign step. ∎

**Theorem B (PROVED rel. Siegel–Walfisz and the square-unit obstruction).** The set in Theorem A restricted to `1 mod 840` has positive natural density among the integers and positive relative natural density among primes. More strongly, an explicit subfamily in dyadic intervals `(X,2X]`, `X=2^j`, has **all** its members tending to `1` profinitely and still contains at least `X exp(−O(√log log X))/log X` primes per block. There is a single formula for the parameters for the entire block, not an ES search or an invocation of an ES exceptional-set estimate. The positive-density family admits no finite ET, Mordell polynomial-class or fixed-prime-divisor covering, even eventually. On the thinner dyadic subfamily any such fixed menu hits only finitely many members.

*Proof.* For any multiple M of 840 let m be M stripped of all factors 2 and 3. Set `x=M + m·(m⁻¹ mod 24)` (least positive inverse); thus `M<x≤24M`, `x≡1 (24)` and `x≡0` at each prime factor of M other than 2,3. The resulting A is prime to M: it is odd, is 1 mod 3 (since `3|y`), and at the remaining primes equals 3 mod the prime (since `y≡2`). Fix `k≡0 mod M`, `k≡u+1 mod A`. Then `n=1+k y³` runs, up to an initial segment, through one progression of modulus `Q=M A y³ ≪ M^{11}`. It is reduced: n is 1 at primes of My, and `−u` at primes of A. Every member is `1 mod M`. For fixed M the PNT in progressions suffices for positive lower density; M=840 suffices for positivity. For natural densities truncate at `x≤T`; this is a finite union of progressions up to finitely many points. For integers the tail proportion is `≪ Σ_{x>T}1/y³+O(X^{1/6}/X)` up to X. For primes use Brun–Titchmarsh on `p≡1 mod y³` when `x≤X^{1/12}/2`, giving relative tail `≪Σ_{x>T}1/φ(y³)=o_T(1)` (e.g. `φ(y³)≥y²`). Above this split count all integers: `≪ X Σ_{x≫X^{1/12}}x^{-6}+X^{1/6}=o(X/log X)`. This also shows why there is no positive prime density with the cube parameter required to tend to infinity.

For the dyadic assertion use `B=max(7,⌊√log log X⌋)`, `M=lcm(840,1,2,…,B)`. By Chebyshev `log M=O(B)`; hence the modulus Q is eventually at most log X. Siegel–Walfisz in `(X,2X]` now gives `(1+o(1))(Li(2X)−Li(X))/φ(Q)`, yielding the bound and the stated log-exponent asymptotic using the trivial prime upper bound. Every modulus eventually divides M. A primitive square residue class cannot be solvable by polynomials (Mordell/Schinzel, `EXPONENT_COVERING.md` §1), and 1 is not in a prime-divisor class. Hence all finite menus of the stated kind eventually miss. This is stronger than adjoining a thin escaping subsequence to a dense congruence class. No external prime-value conjecture. ∎

**Uniform smooth multipliers (PROVED, scoped).** For *each* positive odd k one can choose an explicit fixed H and period D with `ES(kH·2^{jD}+1)` for every `j≥0`, in `1 mod 840`. Use the preceding x recipe with `M=840k`, which ensures `(840k,A)=1`. Take `H=840 y³ h`, `1≤h≤A` solving `840kh≡u+1 mod A`, and `D=φ(A)`. Apply A. This gives smooth part supported on finitely many primes depending effectively on k, not H=1 nor an absolute smoothness bound. The nested test itself is powerless for the putative all-large-s law on `k·2^s+1` when k is cubefree: y is odd ≥3, so y³ cannot divide `k·2^s`. For every fixed k the test has only finitely many possible odd y, regardless of s. The z78 off-core obstruction (§5 Prop. 5) additionally applies to a different, unshifted 2-power ladder. Neither is an obstruction to ES itself.

## 2. Two polynomial families, without distribution of primes

**Proposition C (PROVED, elementary with the square-unit obstruction).** (i) For positive `w≡0 mod 840`, `n=4w²−6w+1` has `R(n)>0`, has order of growth Θ(√N) in counting distinct n≤N, and tends to 1 along factorial multiples of w's modulus. Use `u=w,v=w−1,C=1`. This gives a benchmark for A: the √N exponent by itself is *not* the hard part. It is a fixed level, not a fixed ET progression. Cf. the below-square examples in `NEAR_SQUARE.md` §3; no quadratic prime-value assertion.

(ii) For `a≡1,b≡0 mod 35`, `a≡5,b≡17 mod 23`, `a,b>0`, the binary cubic `n=24(a³+b³)−23` lies in `1 mod 840`, satisfies U₇, (S) and ES, and its closure contains **1** even after deleting any finite prefix. Its distinct values up to N number `N^{2/3+o(1)}`. This uses a homogeneous cyclotomic *value*, not a list of its prime factors. Put `A=a²−ab+b²`, `u=23`, `v=24(a+b)`. Then `n+u=Av`, `A≡−1 mod 23`, and `t=(A+1)/23≥2` (the prescribed positive a,b give A>23). Thus we have a word and `8|v`. The symmetric criterion (`Z68_U7F.md` Lemma 1(a)) gives U₇; the divisibility of v by 4 gives the negative Jacobi sign and the triple `vA/4, tnv/4, tnvA/4` (`DEDEKIND_ES.md` Thm 4.5). At primes other than 23 approximate `(a,b)=(1,0)` locally. At 23, `5³+17³≡1 (23)` with nonzero derivative `3·5²`; keep b=17 and lift a by Hensel to obtain `a³+b³=1` in ℤ₂₃. CRT gives arbitrarily large positive lattice points in every resulting neighbourhood. For the count take a box `[T,2T]²` with `T≍N^{1/3}` small enough. Each level `s=a³+b³` has at most `2τ(s)` representations: `a+b|s` and fixing this sum fixes ab, hence the unordered pair. Apply the elementary divisor bound; the opposite exponent follows by positivity. No count of prime values of this particular binary form.

## 3. A power-scale shifted square factor at primes

**Theorem D (PROVED rel. BV, Baier–Zhao's square-moduli BV and Mertens).** Fix `0<α<2/9`. For large dyadic X let `Q=X^α`, `M=lcm(840,1,…,max(7,⌊√log log X⌋))`. There are `≫ X/(M² Q log² X)` distinct primes `X<p≤2X`, `p≡1 mod M`, with an interior (★) word having

    A=tu−1=q prime in (Q,2Q],   q² | p+u,
    C=t/4 a prime in (Q^{1/5},Q^{1/4}],     u=(q+1)/(4C).

Thus all three word letters grow as powers of p, the genuine negative divisor is *repeated* in the shifted number, C is odd, and no fixed menu hits the tail. This is a prime distribution statement with an extra square layer, distinct from the cube-divisor algorithm of §1. It does not assert that the minimum over other words grows at a power rate.

*Proof.* Retain primes `q≡3 (8)` in `(Q,2Q]` having a prime `ℓ∈(Q^{1/5},Q^{1/4}]` dividing `q+1`. There are `≫Q/log Q` such q. Indeed BV in the variable q at moduli `8ℓ` counts the pairs with main term `(Li(2Q)−Li(Q)) Σ_ℓ 1/(4(ℓ−1)) ≫ Q/log Q` by Mertens, error `O(Q/log³ Q)` (partial summation or prime-counting BV). Multiplicity per q is bounded by 5 since the product of six such primes exceeds `2Q+1`. For each retained q choose the smallest qualifying ℓ, fix `C=ℓ,t=4ℓ,u=(q+1)/t`; in particular `(q,u)=1`. This gives one class `p≡−u (q²), p≡1 (M²)` modulo `(Mq)²`, reduced since q exceeds every prime in M. Then `p=tuv−u−v` with `v=(p+u)/q` an actual multiple of q, and its denominators are as in A. The negative Jacobi sign follows either from the 4|t criterion or `(q/p)=(−u/q)=−1` (`u≡(4ℓ)^{-1} mod q`, `(ℓ/q)=(q/ℓ)(−1/ℓ)=1`).

Use S. Baier and L. Zhao, *Bombieri–Vinogradov type theorems for sparse sets of moduli*, Acta Arith. 125 (2006), square-moduli theorem: for fixed ε,H>0,

    Σ_{b≤X^{2/9−ε}} b max_{(a,b)=1} |ψ(X;b²,a)−X/φ(b²)| ≪_{ε,H} X/log^H X.

Apply at X and 2X, taking `b=Mq` which are distinct, `b≥MQ`, and `2MQ<X^{2/9−ε}` for some fixed ε. This bounds the **unweighted** total error by `≪ X/(MQ log^H X)`. The dyadic von Mangoldt main term summed over q is `≫ X/(M² Q log Q)`. Since `M=(log X)^{o(1)}`, the error is negligible. Prime powers cost at most `O(Q√X log² X)` in total, negligible since `α<1/4`. Dividing by log(2X) proves the incidence lower bound. To pass to distinct primes, count even *integers* in pairs of different classes: by CRT each pair costs `O(X/Q⁴+1)` ignoring M, so all double intersections total `O(X/Q²+Q²)=o(X/(M²Q log² X))`. Bonferroni proves the claim. No individual prime was retrieved from an average. For fixed q the recipe is just one AP; what the sparse-moduli input buys is q at a fixed power of p **and** the second factor q in v, while keeping all word letters large. ∎

The stronger outputs here are a deterministic split of both A and A+1, prime values in abundance **on a whole sequence converging to 1**, and a power-sized repeated factor in D.
