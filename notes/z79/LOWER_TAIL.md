# The conditional second moment by deleting the pinned atom

**Assessment — preflight (FRONTIER §4).** Targets A and B require different tests. For A vary *p* only on the stipulated fibre, count actual residues, do not infer anything at a specified p. Neither a pointwise two-sided asymptotic nor a lower bound on every near-square is at issue (#4–7,15–16). An atom is square-unsafe to impose on an arbitrary input: its target is a nonsquare at some factor of its period, necessarily >B on the 1-fibre (#1,8–10,17); conditioning on an actual hit is legitimate only in the second moment. The test for B is the sign of the overlap of actual divisibility events, not a fixed menu, a pooling of large factors, or forcing by parity. Neither argument is a signed, relaxed, BM, logic or étale descent (#11–13,18–19). Numerator and concrete screens (#2,3,14) below. A depth condition on the *period* need not be a condition on every coordinate of a Type II solution.

## 1. Statement

**Theorem A (PROVED rel. PNT; Haar).** Use the hypotheses of `notes/z78/WILD_ES.md` §6.3 Thm H: fixed θ, σ, γ with 0<12σ<θ<1/2, 4σ<γ<θ/s, σ+θ<1/2, s=2 or 3. Write L=log X, B=σL, Q=8∏_{3≤q≤B}q (prime products). Each free coordinate runs from X^γ to X^{θ/s}/4. For s=2 use (a,d), c=1; for s=3 use (a,c,d), (a,c)=1. Give each atom i its **unit weight** 1, period f_i=4acd−1, target x_i=−a/c mod f_i. Write N=Σ_i 1[n≡x_i mod f_i]. The Haar law is uniform on units modulo lcm(Q, all f_i), conditioned on n≡r mod Q, (r,Q)=1. With μ₀=Σ_i 1/φ(f_i) and μ_Q=E_r N one has **uniformly in r**

    E_r (N−μ₀)² = o(μ₀²),       E_r N² = (1+o(1)) μ_Q²,       μ₀ ≍ L^s.

No prime-f restriction, loss of logarithmic order, fixed-r averaging, truncation *within* H's bulk, or Selberg weights. In particular this addresses H's proposed next step for normalized II2, not just its easier c=1 chart. Constants may depend on the fixed exponents. A finite union bound allows both charts at once with admissible parameters for each.

**Theorem B (CONDITIONAL on Dirichlet GRH; PROVED rel. Gallagher (G) in the small-level range).** The same centered estimate, with the same μ₀, holds for the uniform law on primes p≡r (Q) in (X,2X], under GRH if σ+2θ<1/2. Unconditionally relative to (G) it holds if σ+2θ<δ_G for a sufficiently small **absolute** δ_G>0. In particular for every sufficiently small fixed σ>0 one can choose θ>12σ and γ as above for both charts. On relative density 1 in this single progression each count is (1+o(1))μ₀ (convergence in relative probability). At r=1 every hit has a prime factor >B in its **period** by the II2 Jacobi obstruction (H). Even the cheap (★) count then has order at least L² and the cheap normalized II2 count at least L³. The same small-level conclusion holds on any subprimorial Q'|Q and any unit class, e.g. cut at A log log X or c√log X for fixed A,c>0. For polylogarithmic Q' the full range 2θ<1/2 works unconditionally by ordinary BV (§4.3). The σ in the box conditions can be an auxiliary enlargement depth in the subprimorial result.

**PROVED (interpretation).** For a prime hit let e=(p+4a²d)/f and b=ce−a=(p+e)/(4ad)>0. These integers give the ordered denominators (abd,pacd,pbcd). In the bulk a,d<p, e≡−p mod a and (a,c)=1, so (a,b)=1. The ratio of the last two denominators determines the reduced a:b, then the first gives d and the others c. Thus these are distinct ordered solutions, not multiples from rescaling a/c. For c=1 this is exactly (★). No converse or inference of a new pointwise ES law.

## 2. Conditional-row estimate

**Proof of A (PROVED, including the lemmas below).** Split the logarithms to base X of the free coordinates into a *bounded* number of intervals of length ≤ε, with ε<γ/20; include the factor 1/4 at the upper endpoints. One can use equally spaced log intervals (up to the true upper endpoint), so each length stays bounded below by a positive constant. Write J for one resulting rectangle and H_J=Σ_{J, without gcd}1/(4acd) ≍ L^s (omit c as a variable at s=2). Partition J further into ordinary dyadic boxes clipped at the ends. In estimates per such box write A,C,D for the dyadic scales, C=1 for s=2, V=ACD. They are within absolute constant factors of the lower endpoints in physical units, and all nonfixed coordinates are ≫X^γ. O(L^s) such boxes suffice. Distinct dyadic boxes *inside the same J* differ by at most X^ε times absolute constants in each coordinate.

Consider the mean in J conditional on n≡w modulo m. Use either m=Q alone, or m=[Q,f_i] and the combined unit residue w, where i is a compatible candidate **in J**. In the latter case remove this one i before computing the mean, then add its (deterministic) contribution 1. We prove, for either row, that the mean after deletion is

    (1+o(1)) H_J · K_s,       K_s = ∏_q (1−1_{s=3}/q² + t_q/(q−1)),                (1)
    t_q = (q−1)^{s−1}/q^s (odd q),   t_2=0.

Uniformity in i,w,J matters. In particular conditional means do **not** grow with τ(f_i) at an adversarial period. K_s is finite and positive. A conditional atom j with parameters a,c,d and f=4acd−1 has probability 1_{x≡w mod g}φ(g)/φ(f), g=(f,m). Since all q≤B divide m, this is

    (1+o(1)) (4acd)^{-1} · W_m,       W_m = g 1_{a+wc≡0 (g)}.                     (2)

Indeed f/(4acd)=1+O(X^{-γ}), and the omitted product ∏_{q|f, q∤m}(1−1/q)^{-1} is 1+O(L/(B log B)). Formula (2) uses **nonnegativity before expansion**; zero-probability atoms stay zero.

**Signed divisor expansion.** For each odd q^v∥m expand the local factor of W_m as

    1 + Σ_{j=1}^v 1_{q^j|f} [q^j 1_{q^j|a+wc} − q^{j−1} 1_{q^{j−1}|a+wc}].

This telescopes exactly even at higher powers of the small primes. The 2-factor is 1. Each resulting term is ±k·1_{h|f, k|a+wc}, with k|h|m (odd), and there are ∏_{q^v∥m}(1+2v) ≤ τ(m)² = X^{o(1)} terms; m≤X^{σ+θ+o(1)}. For large h it is important that the two moduli h,k can differ. A bound using only an unsigned inflation φ(gcd) misses the cancellation among the small h terms.

**Completion at small h.** Take λ=γ/20, η=γ/5. For h≤X^λ replace each signed term's harmonic sum by H_J times its complete-residue density **including primitivity**; total additive error o(1) (also per dyadic box with its own harmonic sum). Here and below the complete model is independent uniform prime-adic free coordinates, *not* independent hits. Details:

* For s=3 insert Σ_{b|a,c} μ(b) for (a,c)=1; truncate at b≤X^η. The absolute tail, per box, even multiplied by k, is O(k X^{−η}): for b≤2min(A,C) at most O(A/b) and O(C/b) multiples in the full dyadic intervals, none for larger b. In the completed model the same bound follows from Σ_{b>X^η}1/b². For s=2 just take b=1.
* Each retained function (indicators only) has period dividing hb in each variable. For any bounded function |F|≤1 of these residues, replacement of the weighted sum by its complete mean times the box harmonic weight costs O_s(hb/X^γ) per box. In one variable split into blocks of length hb; incomplete ends cost O(hb/A), and relative variation inside a full block is O(hb/A), then iterate in the free variables. No relative estimate on a tiny clipped interval is needed. Even summing the crude errors with factor k over b and over all terms costs O(X^{2λ+2η−γ+o(1)}+X^{λ−η+o(1)}) per box. Deleting the pin from the small terms costs O(X^{λ+o(1)}/V_i), V_i=a_i c_i d_i. The estimates allow all end boxes and their polylogarithmic number.

**Large h in the complete model.** For every fixed c invertible mod h (otherwise h|f impossible), k|a+wc allows at most 1/k of the a's, and for each allowable unit a, h|f fixes one d mod h. The complete density times k is ≤1/h, also at c=1; primitivity only decreases it. Thus every term h>X^λ can be dropped in the complete model with total O(X^{−λ+o(1)}) before multiplying by H_J.

**Large h on the actual box — the determinant cut.** In the clipped dyadic box the primitive pairs (a,c) with k|a+wc number O(1+AC/k). Indeed fractions a/c in an interval of length O(A/C) are distinct and separated by at least k/(4C²) (k divides the determinant of two such pairs). For s=2 this is the ordinary single progression bound. Given a,c there are O(1+D/h) choices for d with h|f. Hence the absolute harmonic mass times k, after restricting to nonpin candidates, is at most a constant times

    1/h + 1/D + k/(AC h) + k/V.                                                   (3)

The last term is the only dangerous one. With m=Q it is ≤Q/V. Otherwise let i=(a_i,c_i,d_i) be the pin. For a nonpin point actually contributing, gcd(k,f_i) divides ac_i−a_i c, since w agrees with x_i mod f_i. If this determinant is nonzero its magnitude is O(X^ε AC). Since k|[Q,f_i], k/gcd(k,f_i) divides Q, so **k/V ≪ QX^ε/D**. If the determinant vanishes, primitivity gives (a,c)=(a_i,c_i). Then d≠d_i; gcd(h,f_i) divides d−d_i by subtracting periods and using (4ac,f_i)=1. Thus **k/V ≤ h/V ≪ QX^ε/(AC)**. Both bounds are negative powers of X since γ>4σ. (Allowing X^{2ε} would also suffice.) When there are no contributors no k/V estimate is needed. All terms of (3) now save a power for h>X^λ; even τ(m)² times the number of boxes is harmless. This is why deleting the one pin, using reduced fractions, and localizing the logarithmic aspect ratios occur *together*. It does not assert independence for all distinct periods.

**Finishing (1).** In the completed expansion sum all h again. At q^j|f the tuple of active variables is a tuple of units, and −a/c is uniform on units mod q^j: for each c and each unit a there is exactly one d giving q^j|f. Thus for j≥2 the expected bracket vanishes (q^j/φ(q^j)=q^{j−1}/φ(q^{j−1})); for j=1 the gain is t_q/(q−1). When q|f primitivity at q is automatic. When q∤m there is no multiplier. Independence across primes and the summable primitive-density tail give the complete product in (1) except that t_q/(q−1) is omitted for q∤m. The omitted terms sum to O(1/B), uniformly even for the pinned row. The dyadic sums, the small factors in (2), and this product prove (1).

**From rows to a second moment.** Denote the count in J by N_J. Its mean M_J∼H_J K_s tends to infinity. In E_r N_J² sum the pin probabilities times their conditional row means. Every live row has mean 1+(1+o(1))H_J K_s, uniformly, so Var_r N_J=o(M_J²). For the original rectangle use the triangle inequality in L² on the **centered** counts of its bounded number of pieces. No far-apart-aspect-ratio overlap estimate is required. The same means hold without the r-fibre by averaging r; hence μ₀∼Σ_J H_J K_s uniformly relative to μ_Q. This proves A. In particular the GRH range restrictions were not used to bound the CRT overlap.

## 3. Cross-C sign and the numerator test

**Proposition C (PROVED, exact sign on Haar units).** For two (★) trials (a,d),(a',d'), let f=4ad−1, f'=4a'd'−1, g=(f,f'). On a unit fibre r mod any Q delete base-incompatible atoms. The joint probability divided by the product of the two marginal probabilities of live atoms is exactly

    1_{g|d−d'} · φ(g)/φ((g,Q)).                                                   (4)

Indeed modulo g, 4ad=4a'd'=1, so a=a' mod g iff d=d' mod g; both are equivalent to compatibility. Given that compatibility and base compatibility, CRT gives the quotient by the totient gcd/lcm identity prime by prime. Consequently two entire distinct finite fixed-d slice *counts*, with any nonnegative atom weights, have nonpositive covariance if |d−d'| is a power of two (including 1). At a **single** n, any two actual hits on such slices have coprime periods. More generally all common period primes with multiplicity must divide the slice separation. This last deterministic conclusion has no distributional hypothesis. It specifies where a putative cross-slice rescue cannot recycle a nonresidue prime. It is not a negative-association claim for arbitrary events, nor does negative covariance force any one n to be hit. At general d−d' both signs in (4) really occur: f=3 at (1,1) and f'=15 at (1,4) give positive covariance on unrestricted units, whereas (1,1),(2,2) have incompatible targets at the same gcd 3. No universal negative covariance for all trials.

**PROVED (4-specific test on a slice).** An unordered solution of 8/n corresponds bijectively by doubling *all* denominators to an all-even unordered solution of 4/n. On (★) for odd n the outer letters a,b have opposite parity; the triple (abd,nbd,nad) is all even iff d is even. When d is odd exactly one of the three is odd. This distinguishes a valid 4-witness from a transferable one without treating 8/n as interchangeable with its c=1 line. In fact at odd n any solution not all even has exactly one odd denominator: three odd denominators give an odd numerator over an odd common denominator, and exactly one even denominator has a unique term of negative 2-adic valuation. This elementary dichotomy does not say whether the all-even branch is empty.

## 4. Prime transfers (proof of B)

### 4.1 GRH

**CONDITIONAL on Dirichlet GRH.** Each compatible joint cell contributes one reduced progression of modulus [Q,f_i,f_j] (one atom or no atom for the lower moments). The GRH dyadic ϑ estimate is X/φ(modulus)+O(√X log² X). There are ≤X^{θ+o(1)} atoms by the product bound or the divisor function, hence after dividing by X/φ(Q) and expanding (N−μ₀)² the absolute error is ≤X^{σ+2θ−1/2+o(1)}. A transfers to log-weighted primes, including a positive denominator with the expected asymptotic. Removing log p changes averages of nonnegative functions relatively by 1+O(1/L). No use of GRH on an individual integer's divisor function.

### 4.2 Why Gallagher works on this *one* power-size modulus

**PROVED rel. (G) as formulated in `H4_NOT_POLYLOG.md` §5 (Gallagher, Invent. Math. 11 (1970); Montgomery–Vaughan III draft Thm 28.19 with the qualifications there).** We only need an upper bound by a fixed multiple of the small centered Haar second moment. Let F=(N−μ₀)², v=E_r F=o(μ₀²); also consider F=1, v=1. On the full finite unit group extend 1_{n≡r(Q)}F by zero off that fibre. Every Fourier coefficient is bounded in modulus by v/φ(Q) by **nonnegativity**, with equality and value v/φ(Q) at the principal character. All conductors are ≤U=X^δ where σ+2θ<δ, by the linear expansion into cells (a cell's indicator lifts from a modulus ≤Q X^{2θ}). Each primitive character induces only one character on the full group. There is no giant-lcm imprimitive-character penalty: in the *interval* all primes exceed every prime factor of the group modulus. This coefficient estimate does not assume small total variation of the signed cell expansion. Use exactly the same bound and support for F=1; in that case nonzero coefficients have conductors dividing Q.

For clarity, apply (G0),(G1) in the cited §5 at X and 2X with this **same U** and unique exceptional primitive real character (if any) of conductor ≤U and real zero β>1−1/(κ_G log U). The dyadic error after the principal and the possible exceptional main terms is bounded by (vX/φ(Q)) times

    O_G(exp(−1/(κ_G δ))+o(1))                             (no exception),
    O_G(u(exp(−1/δ)+o(1))) ,   u=(1−β)log X < 1/(κ_G δ)   (exception).

The little-oh in the second line is **inside the u factor** by (G1); at 2X its log and u change by 1+O(1/L). Choose fixed δ sufficiently small depending only on the constants in (G), with 6cδ<1. The first multiplier is <1/10; the second is ≤min(u,1)/10 (or any smaller prescribed constant times min(u,1)). One can fix such a δ_G and take δ=δ_G once σ+2θ<δ_G. The principal dyadic main term is vX/φ(Q); the exceptional term is −c_ψ∫_X^{2X}t^{β−1}dt with |c_ψ|≤v/φ(Q). If cond ψ divides Q then c_ψ=ψ(r)v/φ(Q) **for both choices of F**. For sign +1 the common bracket after dividing by X lies between 1−exp(−u) and 1−exp(−u(1+log 2/L)), hence is comparable to min(u,1), even for a very close zero. For sign −1 it lies between 1 and 2. Thus the actual base-prime weight is bounded below by a constant times its own main term and the F-weight is bounded above by a constant times v times that base weight. If cond ψ does not divide Q, the base has no exceptional term and main term X/φ(Q); the F main terms in total are at most 2vX/φ(Q). With no exception the comparison is immediate as well. All estimates are dyadic; in particular positivity of the base follows without silently asserting a PNT for Q=X^σ. Divide the weights, remove log p as in §4.1, and use Chebyshev. This proves the small-power assertion. It is not BV applied to a sparse subfamily with a power-sized normalization loss. No claim about a numerical effective δ_G or onset.

**PROVED (subprimorial consequence).** Conditional Haar laws and prime laws on Q'|Q are mixtures of the laws on its reduced lifts. Uniformity of the centered bound and the *common* center μ₀ make these mixtures harmless, without assuming the lift populations are equal. No deletion of an exceptional prime from the primorial is needed. In particular this covers exp((c+o(1))√log X) without invoking Siegel–Walfisz at that modulus.

### 4.3 Ordinary BV for the polylogarithmic base, full transfer range

**PROVED rel. Bombieri–Vinogradov and Siegel–Walfisz.** For Q' a subprimorial ≤L^A with A fixed, use the Haar law supplied by A via mixing, at 2θ<1/2. Let E(l) denote the maximum absolute discrepancy for the dyadic prime count from the ordinary prime count divided by φ(l), over reduced classes. Moduli of all cells are ≤L^A X^{2θ}. We spell out why repetitions, including those from [Q',f_i,f_j], cost only log powers. The candidate multiplicity at f is ≤τ₃(f+1) (ordered factorizations of (f+1)/4). For a given joint modulus l both periods divide l, so pair multiplicity is ≤ρ(l)² with ρ(l)=Σ_{f|l}τ₃(f+1). Cauchy, E(l)≪X/φ(l) (trivial progression bound here) and BV give total absolute discrepancy ≤(Σ_l E(l))^{1/2}(O(X)Σ_l ρ(l)^4/φ(l))^{1/2}. The last sum over l≤X is polylogarithmic: ρ(l)^4≤τ(l)^3Σ_{f|l}τ₃(f+1)^4; write l=fk, use τ(fk)≤τ(f)τ(k), φ(fk)≥φ(f)φ(k), and τ₃(t)≤τ(t)^2. Cauchy and the classical fixed-power τ moment bounds for f,f+1, partial summation and f/φ(f)≪log log(3f) finish, as in `Z53_PZ.md` §3. BV permits an arbitrarily large fixed log saving since 2θ<1/2, also for one or zero atoms. Pay the polylogarithmic coefficients in F and the full factor φ(Q')L dividing by the base prime count (Siegel–Walfisz). This proves the centered estimate without a small Gallagher level requirement in this range.

## 5. Next experiment (Assessment)

The determinant cut controls a **single** pinned row all the way up to its period. For a higher factorial moment two pins cost two different determinants, and a candidate combining prime factors of both periods can survive their separate cuts: the bound for k is no longer a *single* determinant. Test whether there are many such mixed-period triangles even after deleting all repeated vertices, on the same narrow log boxes. A uniform third centered/factorial estimate with a quantitative exceptional bound would strengthen the relative lower tail rather than posit positivity at a given p. Proposition C suggests testing adjacent d-slices as controls where compatible periods cannot share any prime at all. Neither density one nor the finite 4/8 dichotomy explains a last zero in ℕ.
