# A cofinal PA subring, a bad prime and the Farey test

**Assessment (strategy).** A cut and a subring serve different purposes. Keep the prescribed PA ring `D` initial so its bounded theory is undisturbed. Put a *second*, cofinal PA ring `B` inside the new model, but do **not** keep it initial. Put `P` at a size already occupied by an element of `B`. The coefficients of a descended ES triple will now be in a localization of `B`, not `Frac D`. This allows integral points of all the norm tori at once, without asking the character groups of different tori to be independent. The cost can be measured at the very same prime by a bounded induction test (§4).

## 1. The two rings and two valuations

**Theorem (PROVED rel. the cited stage lemmas).** For every countable signed PA model `D` there are countable signed ordered rings `D ⊂ B ⊂ R` with the following properties:

* `B` is a proper elementary end extension of `D` in arithmetic. `R` is normal, its cone satisfies IOpen and Lagrange's four-square theorem, and it end extends `D`.
* For a ring-theoretic prime `P>D` of `R`, `P≡1 (mod d)` for **all** `0<d∈D`, and `4/P` is not the sum of three positive integral unit fractions in `R`.
* In `rc(Frac R)` every positive size modulo ratios bounded above and below by positive `Frac D` constants is the size of a member of `Frac B`. More precisely, if `x>D` and `0<ε<1` belongs to `Frac D`, there is `m∈B` with `x<m<(1+ε)x`. The inequality is about **relative** precision, not about approximating `x` to within 1.
* One may simultaneously include all the pointed geometrically integral single-boundary windows of z78 §15 for the specified base (details in §2). Whether or not they are included, all the conclusions in §§3–4 hold, including the failure of Bézout.

Here `x>D` abbreviates `x>d` for all positive `d∈D`. Rings above a cut always mean the signed versions of the corresponding cones. We use `ES(P)` in the original, unrestricted three-denominator sense.

**Proof (PROVED rel. z78 §13).** Take a countable proper elementary end extension `B` by MacDowell–Specker and downward Löwenheim–Skolem. Choose `T∈B` above `D`. Write `F=Frac D`, `C=(D\{0})⁻¹B`, `K=Frac B`, `I=⋂_{d>0 in D}dB`, `S=B/I`. The factorial of `T` in `B` is a positive element of `I`. Thus the single landing of z78 §13 applies: `S` is `D`-torsion-free, relatively integrally closed in `S_F`, `S_F=S+F`, and every element of `S` is a sum of four squares there. For the localized reduction `ρ:C→S_F` we need the stronger contraction

```
                     ρ⁻¹(S) = B.
```

Indeed if `ρ(b/d)=ρ(v)` with `v∈B`, then `b−dv∈I⊆dB` by torsion-freeness. The converse is immediate. In particular landing in `S` can certify actual integrality **in the unordered specialization back to B**, even though the target `S` of reduction may have zero divisors and need not be ordered. `B` is integrally closed by PA's gcd arithmetic, as is `C` by localization. `C∩hull(F)=F` by endness: if `b/d` is relatively bounded, so is `b`, hence `b∈D`.

Start the upper algebra with `A₀=C[P]`. Its mark is polynomial evaluation at 1 followed by `ρ`. For the order put `P=Tθ`, with `1<θ<2` a fresh transcendental **relative residue** variable over `rc(K)` for the convex valuation given by the hull of `F`. This order makes every nonconstant polynomial from `C[P]` unbounded over `F`: its magnitude has the valuation of the largest `|c_j|T^j` (Gauss), and every nonzero `c_j∈C` is a scalar from `F` or is already outside its hull; explicitly `|b/d|≥1/|d|` for `b≠0`. This is not the order with `P>K`, nor evaluation at a positive integer `P=T`.

In `rc(K)` the relative residue field embeds over `rc(F)` in `L`. **Window transfer (PROVED).** Here is one criterion covering variable inequalities and congruences too. Let `φ(x,c)` be a bounded existential block with open matrix and ring parameters `c∈B`. ## 4. A parameter-free induction forcing arithmetic at the prime

**PROVED (ordinary language, no PA subring needed).** `IE₁⁻` proves the bounded Farey-bracket theorem, hence signed Bézout, the field quotient at each prime, and short congruence vectors at *variable* prime moduli. In particular, for every odd prime `p` at which `−1` has a square root, it proves `p=x²+y²` with positive integers. Smith, JPAA 79 (1992), proves `IE₁ ⊢ IOpen + Bez`; his NDJFL 1993 Thm 4.2 proves the two-square consequence using parameterized least search (sources in `data/z66/ie1/`). For parameter/parameter-free conservation compare Kaye, *Diophantine induction*, APAL 46 (1990), and Kaye–Paris–Dimitracopoulos, *On parameter free induction schemas* (1988); our proof below is direct and asserts no priority for parameter removal. The point here is a **specific parameter-free** axiom with independently valid step, not a priority claim for Thue approximation or a claim that the supplement `p≡1 mod 4 ⇒ (−1/p)=1` follows. No primitive recursion/continued fraction code, minimization with a prime parameter, or finite-field pigeonhole principle is needed.

For `0≤a≤b`, `b>0`, at level `k≥1` ask for either:

* an exact fraction `u/v=a/b`, `0≤u≤v≤k`, `v>0`, with a signed row `iu+jv=1`, `|i|,|j|≤k+1`; or
* `u/v < a/b < w/z` in `[0,1]`, `1≤v,z≤k`, `wv−uz=1`, `v+z>k`.

These are `E₁` after clearing positive denominators and sign splitting. At 1 use `0/1,1/1` or the appropriate exact endpoint. At `k+1` keep the exact row or a bracket of sufficient denominator sum. Otherwise `v+z=k+1`: compare the mediant to the target. Equality gives the exact row since `v(u+w)−u(v+z)=1`. Otherwise retain the appropriate adjacent pair; determinant is one, both denominator bounds hold, and the new sum is strictly larger than `k+1`. This is a uniform open-algebra proof. If `k=b`, no strict bracket is possible: the two positive integral errors `r=av−bu`, `s=bw−az` satisfy `rz+sv=b`, contradicting `v+z>b`.

**Packaging the parameters (PROVED).** Use one unary induction input `y=m²+j`, `0≤j≤2m`, decoding `m` by integer square root. Pack `(a,b,h)` into `m` by nesting `pair(c,d)=(c+d)²+c`; inverse by square root and subtraction when valid (if the remainder exceeds the root it is not a pair). Invalid code or invalid `a,b` asks for nothing. Valid code asks for the above test with `k=j+1`; `h` is padding and can exceed any desired `k`. The whole formula is a single parameter-free `E₁`: existentially specify the bounded roots and remainders with their defining open inequalities, assert the open invalid alternative or the bounded positive test. Root decoding is total even if one starts with just parameter-free induction: `∃r≤y+1 [r²≤y<(r+1)²]` has the usual base and step (increment at a square). All auxiliary bounds are fixed polynomials in `y+1`; no existential *failure* of an `E₁` calculation is tested. At `j=2m` the next input is `(m+1)²` and the new parameters need only the level-one initializer; every other successor uses the mediant proof for **unchanged** parameters. This proves base and universal step independently in IOpen (or using just the root axiom and discrete order). Use padding and instantiate the universal conclusion at `m²+k−1` for arbitrary tuple and `k`. The same block device applies to other uniformly initialized, uniformly progressive parameterized bounded-existential tests; it is not elimination of parameters from arbitrary *hypothetical* induction premises.

For Bézout use the exact fraction at `k=b`. The row gives `g=ia+jb` with `gv=b`, `gu=a` by the cleared identity. Thus `g>0` generates the pair ideal. Swap inputs, take absolute values, and handle two zeros directly. For `0<a<p` the generator at `(a,p)` must be 1 by irreducibility, giving an actual inverse mod `p`. Division with remainder itself needs no parameter induction here: use the same padded block for fixed `b>0` and the level test `k=bq+r`, `0≤r<b`, `q,r≤k`. At level 1 initialize directly and at each step increment `r` or reset it to zero and increment `q`; signed dividends reduce by reflection. For arbitrary residues one can therefore reduce by division (or use the row for the unsorted pair); for irreducible `p` this gives a field. For a short vector at an odd prime take `k=⌊√p⌋` and `0<a<p`. The exact branch is impossible since `p∤a` and `v<p` (primality by Bézout). In the bracket `min(r,s)≤p/(v+z)<√p` since `v+z≥k+1`. This yields `0<Y≤k`, `0<|X|<√p` and `X≡aY mod p` (use `X=r` or `−s`). If `a²≡−1 mod p`, `p` divides `X²+Y²` strictly between 0 and `2p`, hence equals it. This is an arithmetic consequence at the modulus itself; a sum of two squares still need not extract a positive ES witness.

**The lock (PROVED).** Return to §1. In `C`, `T` is not a unit: if `Tb=d∈D` for nonzero `b∈B`, then `T≤|d|` is impossible. Hence `(T,P)` is proper in `C[P]` by evaluation at 0 modulo `T`. Use the flat-edge proper-ideal obstruction of `LOGIC_LAGRANGE_END_TEST.md` §4, now at a pair containing the bad prime itself. It stays proper through *every* added edge: constant tensor, polynomial and monic sphere edges are free with basis containing 1, even at a zero sphere target. `P∤T` in the union by the injection modulo `P`. Thus the input `0<T<P` has **no inverse mod P** even in `A`, much less in `R`. The level-`P` exact Farey fraction would generate the pair and give that inverse, as `P` is prime. At the unary code with parameters `(T,P,h=P)` and `j=P−1` the formula fails. Its base and step hold in every IOpen continuation, its negative bounded instance is frozen in every end extension. Thus the familiar non-Bézout defect produces a particular parameter-free lock at the very prime where the displayed height tests succeed. The z78 §14 equivalence now rules out density for the full class just described, not for every smaller viable class. The obstruction is at a residue of size between `P/2` and `P`, not a missed fixed algebraic group at an inaccessible size.

## 5. Divisibility minimization: how the prime spectrum would have to change

**PROVED (prime-ideal criterion, no ES hypothesis).** In any discretely ordered Bézout IOpen ring satisfying just the least instances `t|h ∧ pt∤h` on `t>0`, for every positive prime `p` and `h>0` there is a factorization `h=t b` with `p∤b` and every positive divisor of `t` coprime to `p` equal to 1. Indeed take the least hit; dividing it by a nontrivial divisor prime to `p` leaves a hit since `p` is prime. Conversely such a `t` divides every hit `u`: write `h=u b'` with `p∤b'`, cancel the gcd of `t,u`; the remaining part of `t` divides `b'` by the Bézout row, hence is coprime to `p` and is 1. Thus the least hit is unique and is the entire `p`-primary part in this divisor sense, not necessarily a coded power.

Consequently **two distinct nonassociate positive prime elements can have no common nonzero prime ideal below their principal ideals**. If `0≠J⊆(p)∩(q)` is prime, choose `0<h∈J` and split off the `p`-part. Neither factor can lie in `J`: one is prime to `p`, the other not divisible by `q`. Contradiction. This strengthens the z78 §10 common-conductor obstruction: it needs no divisibility identity `J=qJ` and only one primary least instance per test. Even a nonprime ideal `0≠I⊆(p)∩(q)` must admit, for **every** positive `h∈I`, such a product outside `I` in both factors. Applied to BCM, take a common multiple for an interval containing high powers of both primes: for each standard `j` for which `p^j,q^j` are included, `p^j` divides `t` by coprimality to `b`, and `q^j` divides `b` by coprimality to `t`. This uses only finite Bézout cancellation; no internal power coding is required. So merging those two types of divisibility into a common nonzero prime axis cannot be a repair. In z78 §§1–3 the first positive-value conductor is prime (pullback of the axis valuation's maximal ideal), nonzero and inside `(2)∩(3)`: division there by either old scalar preserves positive value. Thus rotations, changing residue characters, or weakening divisibility of the entire conductor while leaving that containment cannot give `L(Div)`. No general inconsistency follows for BCM plus prime-ideal separation (PA itself illustrates the distinction); there is no construction here satisfying the z78 package *and* `L(Div)` with ES failure.
