# A cofinal PA subring, a bad prime and the Farey test

**Assessment (strategy).** A cut and a subring serve different purposes. Keep the prescribed PA ring `D` initial so its bounded theory is undisturbed. Put a *second*, cofinal PA ring `B` inside the new model, but do **not** keep it initial. Put `P` at a size already occupied by an element of `B`. The coefficients of a descended ES triple will now be in a localization of `B`, not `Frac D`. This allows integral points of all the norm tori at once, without asking the character groups of different tori to be independent. The cost can be measured at the very same prime by a bounded induction test (§4).

## 1. The two rings and two valuations

**Theorem (PROVED rel. the cited stage lemmas).** For every countable signed PA model `D` there are countable signed ordered rings `D ⊂ B ⊂ R` with the following properties:

* `B` is a proper elementary end extension of `D` in arithmetic. `R` is normal, its cone satisfies IOpen and Lagrange's four-square theorem, and it end extends `D`.
* For a ring-theoretic prime `P>D` of `R`, `P≡1 (mod d)` for **all** `0<d∈D`, and `4/P` is not the sum of three positive integral unit fractions in `R`.
* In `rc(Frac R)` every positive size modulo ratios bounded above and below by positive `Frac D` constants is the size of a member of `Frac B`. More precisely, if `x>D` and `0<ε<1` belongs to `Frac D`, there is `m∈B` with `x<m<(1+ε)x`. The inequality is about **relative** precision, not about approximating `x` to within 1.
* One may simultaneously include all the pointed geometrically integral single-boundary windows of z78 §15 for the specified base (details in §2). Whether or not they are included, all the conclusions in §§3–4 hold, including the failure of Bézout.

Here `x>D` abbreviates `x>d` for all positive `d∈D`. Rings above a cut always mean the signed versions of the corresponding cones. We use `ES(P)` in the original, unrestricted three-denominator sense.

**Proof (PROVED rel. z78 §13).** Take a countable proper elementary end extension `B` by MacDowell–Specker and downward Löwenheim–Skolem. Choose `T∈B` above `D`. Write `F=Frac D`, `C=(D\{0})⁻¹B`, `K=Frac B`, `I=⋂_{d>0 in D}dB`, `S=B/I`. The factorial of `T` in `B` is a positive element of `I`. Thus the single landing of z78 §13 applies: `S` is `D`-torsion-free, relatively integrally closed in `S_F`, `S_F=S+F`, and every element of `S` is a sum of four squares there. For the localized reduction `ρ:C→S_F` we need the stronger contraction

```
                     ρ⁻¹(S) = B.
```

Indeed if `ρ(b/d)=ρ(v)` with `v∈B`, then `b−dv∈I⊆dB` by torsion-freeness. The converse is immediate. In particular landing in `S` can certify actual integrality **in the unordered specialization back to B**, even though the target `S` of reduction may have zero divisors and need not be ordered. `B` is integrally closed by PA's gcd arithmetic, as is `C` by localization. `C∩hull(F)=F` by endness: if `b/d` is relatively bounded, so is `b`, hence `b∈D`.

Start the upper algebra with `A₀=C[P]`. Its mark is polynomial evaluation at 1 followed by `ρ`. For the order put `P=Tθ`, with `1<θ<2` a fresh transcendental **relative residue** variable over `rc(K)` for the convex valuation given by the hull of `F`. This order makes every nonconstant polynomial from `C[P]` unbounded over `F`: its magnitude has the valuation of the largest `|c_j|T^j` (Gauss), and every nonzero `c_j∈C` is a scalar from `F` or is already outside its hull; explicitly `|b/d|≥1/|d|` for `b≠0`. This is not the order with `P>K`, nor evaluation at a positive integer `P=T`.

Here is why the ordered-residue host needed for the later floors really exists with this start. Set `L=rc(Cauchy completion of rc(F))` as in `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §1. In `rc(K)` the relative residue field embeds over `rc(F)` in `L`. Indeed for any relatively bounded `u` and every `0<δ∈F`, choose `d∈D` with `1/d<δ`; the `B`-integer part of `du` lies in `D` by endness. Thus the residue is approximated within every `F`-precision by old fractions, defining its image by a Cauchy sequence along a countable coinitial sequence. This respects order, sums and products and kills exactly the relative infinitesimals (and fixes the canonical copy of `rc(F)` by the same approximations). Every interval of `L` has uncountably many choices. The fresh-residue Gauss construction with residue in `L` now applies; extend the embedding on real closure by uniqueness of ordered real closure on the algebraic residue extension. The Gauss variable, and then real closure, introduce no new classes: rational functions have old values and an algebraic extension has values in the divisible hull.

Optionally insert §2's constant affine factors now. Then run the fair relative floor and four-square tasks of `LOGIC_LAGRANGE_END_TEST.md` §§2–3 and z78 §13 over `F`, with the mark into `S_F`. **Window transfer (PROVED).** Here is one criterion covering variable inequalities and congruences too. Let `φ(x,c)` be a bounded existential block with open matrix and ring parameters `c∈B`. Congruence conditions independent of `x` can use actual integral quotient/remainder witnesses in the tuple. For an `x`-dependent divisibility condition it is the **expanded** open matrix and all its integer witnesses that must pass the test; a real divisibility certificate is useless. Also one can use witnesses varying *polynomially in x* with coefficients from the chosen tuple if the same interval test verifies them, with no division by nonunits. ## 4. A parameter-free induction forcing arithmetic at the prime

**PROVED (ordinary language, no PA subring needed).** `IE₁⁻` proves the bounded Farey-bracket theorem, hence signed Bézout, the field quotient at each prime, and short congruence vectors at *variable* prime moduli. In particular, for every odd prime `p` at which `−1` has a square root, it proves `p=x²+y²` with positive integers. Smith, JPAA 79 (1992), proves `IE₁ ⊢ IOpen + Bez`; his NDJFL 1993 Thm 4.2 proves the two-square consequence using parameterized least search (sources in `data/z66/ie1/`). For parameter/parameter-free conservation compare Kaye, *Diophantine induction*, APAL 46 (1990), and Kaye–Paris–Dimitracopoulos, *On parameter free induction schemas* (1988); our proof below is direct and asserts no priority for parameter removal. The point here is a **specific parameter-free** axiom with independently valid step, not a priority claim for Thue approximation or a claim that the supplement `p≡1 mod 4 ⇒ (−1/p)=1` follows. No primitive recursion/continued fraction code, minimization with a prime parameter, or finite-field pigeonhole principle is needed.

For `0≤a≤b`, `b>0`, at level `k≥1` ask for either:

* an exact fraction `u/v=a/b`, `0≤u≤v≤k`, `v>0`, with a signed row `iu+jv=1`, `|i|,|j|≤k+1`; or
* `u/v < a/b < w/z` in `[0,1]`, `1≤v,z≤k`, `wv−uz=1`, `v+z>k`.

These are `E₁` after clearing positive denominators and sign splitting. At 1 use `0/1,1/1` or the appropriate exact endpoint. At `k+1` keep the exact row or a bracket of sufficient denominator sum. Otherwise `v+z=k+1`: compare the mediant to the target. Equality gives the exact row since `v(u+w)−u(v+z)=1`. Otherwise retain the appropriate adjacent pair; determinant is one, both denominator bounds hold, and the new sum is strictly larger than `k+1`. This is a uniform open-algebra proof. If `k=b`, no strict bracket is possible: the two positive integral errors `r=av−bu`, `s=bw−az` satisfy `rz+sv=b`, contradicting `v+z>b`.

**Packaging the parameters (PROVED).** Use one unary induction input `y=m²+j`, `0≤j≤2m`, decoding `m` by integer square root. Pack `(a,b,h)` into `m` by nesting `pair(c,d)=(c+d)²+c`; inverse by square root and subtraction when valid (if the remainder exceeds the root it is not a pair). Invalid code or invalid `a,b` asks for nothing. Valid code asks for the above test with `k=j+1`; `h` is padding and can exceed any desired `k`. The whole formula is a single parameter-free `E₁`: existentially specify the bounded roots and remainders with their defining open inequalities, assert the open invalid alternative or the bounded positive test. Root decoding is total even if one starts with just parameter-free induction: `∃r≤y+1 [r²≤y<(r+1)²]` has the usual base and step (increment at a square). All auxiliary bounds are fixed polynomials in `y+1`; no existential *failure* of an `E₁` calculation is tested. At `j=2m` the next input is `(m+1)²` and the new parameters need only the level-one initializer; every other successor uses the mediant proof for **unchanged** parameters. This proves base and universal step independently in IOpen (or using just the root axiom and discrete order). Use padding and instantiate the universal conclusion at `m²+k−1` for arbitrary tuple and `k`. The same block device applies to other uniformly initialized, uniformly progressive parameterized bounded-existential tests; it is not elimination of parameters from arbitrary *hypothetical* induction premises.

For Bézout use the exact fraction at `k=b`. The row gives `g=ia+jb` with `gv=b`, `gu=a` by the cleared identity. Thus `g>0` generates the pair ideal. Swap inputs, take absolute values, and handle two zeros directly. For `0<a<p` the generator at `(a,p)` must be 1 by irreducibility, giving an actual inverse mod `p`. Division with remainder itself needs no parameter induction here: use the same padded block for fixed `b>0` and the level test `k=bq+r`, `0≤r<b`, `q,r≤k`. At level 1 initialize directly and at each step increment `r` or reset it to zero and increment `q`; signed dividends reduce by reflection. For arbitrary residues one can therefore reduce by division (or use the row for the unsorted pair); for irreducible `p` this gives a field. For a short vector at an odd prime take `k=⌊√p⌋` and `0<a<p`. The exact branch is impossible since `p∤a` and `v<p` (primality by Bézout). In the bracket `min(r,s)≤p/(v+z)<√p` since `v+z≥k+1`. This yields `0<Y≤k`, `0<|X|<√p` and `X≡aY mod p` (use `X=r` or `−s`). If `a²≡−1 mod p`, `p` divides `X²+Y²` strictly between 0 and `2p`, hence equals it. This is an arithmetic consequence at the modulus itself; a sum of two squares still need not extract a positive ES witness.

**The lock (PROVED).** Return to §1. In `C`, `T` is not a unit: if `Tb=d∈D` for nonzero `b∈B`, then `T≤|d|` is impossible. Hence `(T,P)` is proper in `C[P]` by evaluation at 0 modulo `T`. Use the flat-edge proper-ideal obstruction of `LOGIC_LAGRANGE_END_TEST.md` §4, now at a pair containing the bad prime itself. It stays proper through *every* added edge: constant tensor, polynomial and monic sphere edges are free with basis containing 1, even at a zero sphere target. `P∤T` in the union by the injection modulo `P`. Thus the input `0<T<P` has **no inverse mod P** even in `A`, much less in `R`. The level-`P` exact Farey fraction would generate the pair and give that inverse, as `P` is prime. At the unary code with parameters `(T,P,h=P)` and `j=P−1` the formula fails. Its base and step hold in every IOpen continuation, its negative bounded instance is frozen in every end extension. Thus the familiar non-Bézout defect produces a particular parameter-free lock at the very prime where the displayed height tests succeed. The z78 §14 equivalence now rules out density for the full class just described, not for every smaller viable class. The obstruction is at a residue of size between `P/2` and `P`, not a missed fixed algebraic group at an inaccessible size.

## 5. Divisibility minimization: how the prime spectrum would have to change

**PROVED (prime-ideal criterion, no ES hypothesis).** In any discretely ordered Bézout IOpen ring satisfying just the least instances `t|h ∧ pt∤h` on `t>0`, for every positive prime `p` and `h>0` there is a factorization `h=t b` with `p∤b` and every positive divisor of `t` coprime to `p` equal to 1. Indeed take the least hit; dividing it by a nontrivial divisor prime to `p` leaves a hit since `p` is prime. Conversely such a `t` divides every hit `u`: write `h=u b'` with `p∤b'`, cancel the gcd of `t,u`; the remaining part of `t` divides `b'` by the Bézout row, hence is coprime to `p` and is 1. Thus the least hit is unique and is the entire `p`-primary part in this divisor sense, not necessarily a coded power.

Consequently **two distinct nonassociate positive prime elements can have no common nonzero prime ideal below their principal ideals**. If `0≠J⊆(p)∩(q)` is prime, choose `0<h∈J` and split off the `p`-part. Neither factor can lie in `J`: one is prime to `p`, the other not divisible by `q`. Contradiction. This strengthens the z78 §10 common-conductor obstruction: it needs no divisibility identity `J=qJ` and only one primary least instance per test. Even a nonprime ideal `0≠I⊆(p)∩(q)` must admit, for **every** positive `h∈I`, such a product outside `I` in both factors. Applied to BCM, take a common multiple for an interval containing high powers of both primes: for each standard `j` for which `p^j,q^j` are included, `p^j` divides `t` by coprimality to `b`, and `q^j` divides `b` by coprimality to `t`. This uses only finite Bézout cancellation; no internal power coding is required. So merging those two types of divisibility into a common nonzero prime axis cannot be a repair. In z78 §§1–3 the first positive-value conductor is prime (pullback of the axis valuation's maximal ideal), nonzero and inside `(2)∩(3)`: division there by either old scalar preserves positive value. Thus rotations, changing residue characters, or weakening divisibility of the entire conductor while leaving that containment cannot give `L(Div)`. No general inconsistency follows for BCM plus prime-ideal separation (PA itself illustrates the distinction); there is no construction here satisfying the z78 package *and* `L(Div)` with ES failure.

## 8. A structural consequence in every IE₁⁻ model; the primary extraction test

**Coprime splitting (PROVED rel. §4).** In any signed `IE₁⁻` model and for `p,q,h>0` with `(p,q)=1` (not necessarily primes), one can factor

```
            h=tb,       (t,q)=(b,p)=(t,b)=1 .                 (Sep)
```

Here parentheses mean gcd; all coprimality assertions have bounded row certificates by §4. Simulate the following finite-description test, **not** a coded sequence of divisions. At level `k` exhibit `h=tb`, `t,b>0`, `(t,q)=1`, with either `t≥k` or `(b,pt)=1`. Keep a terminal witness and otherwise start at `t=1`. If `t=k` and the state is not terminal, let `g=gcd(b,pt)>1`. Replace `t` by `gt` and `b` by `b/g`; this preserves coprimality to `q` since `pt` is coprime to `q`. It increases `t` by at least 1. The terminal criterion implies both final assertions. When `k=h` even a nonterminal-branch witness with `t≥k` has `b=1`. The padded base/step argument applies *uniformly*: invalid parameters `h,p,q=0` are an open branch, and for noncoprime `p,q` one accepts a common divisor `>1` (bounded quotient certificates). For valid inputs every row for positive `u,v` can be shrunk by division to `|i|≤v+1, |j|≤u+1`; the largest entry `pt` is at most `ph`. Thus all test witnesses and all decode bounds are fixed polynomials of the unary input. The computation of `g` in the proof of progress is justified by §4, not an oracle inside the formula. No bounded-universal primality parameter, no `p^k` predicate, and no minimization assumption is hidden here.

**Consequences (PROVED).** Every nonzero prime ideal of the signed ring lies in **exactly one maximal ideal** (not just at most one principal prime ideal). Indeed if a nonzero prime ideal `J` lay in distinct maximal ideals `M,N`, choose `p∈M,q∈N` with `p+q=1`, changing signs for positivity. They are nonzero and coprime. For `0<h∈J`, (Sep) puts `t` outside `N` and `b` outside `M`, an impossibility. Existence of some maximal over the ideal is the ordinary external maximal ideal theorem. In particular every quotient by a nonzero principal ideal is a Gelfand ring (each prime has a unique maximal above it). More concretely its idempotents separate distinct maximals: for `h∈M∩N`, choose the same `p,q`, apply (Sep), and take the CRT idempotent `e≡0 mod t, e≡1 mod b`. Primality of the maximals forces `t∈M,b∈N`, so the two residues of `e` differ. This statement concerns the external ideal spectrum of *every* model, with a first-order witness theorem (Sep) underlying it. The zero prime must be excepted even for ℤ.

**Exact remaining primary test (PROVED rel. (Sep) and Bézout).** Fix a prime element `p|h`, `h>0`. Write `X_h` for the space of maximal ideals containing `h`, with the usual Zariski topology. Then the following are equivalent:

1. `t|h, pt∤h, t>0` has a least solution;
2. `h=tb` with `p∤b` and no positive nontrivial divisor of `t` prime to `p`;
3. the point `(p)` is **isolated in `X_h`**, i.e. some `q∉(p)` belongs to every other maximal containing `h`.

Indeed `(p)` is maximal by §4. The equivalence 1–2 is §5. Given 2, no maximal `M≠(p)` can contain `t`: choose `x∈M\(p)`, and `gcd(x,t)` would be a nonunit in `M` prime to `p`. Hence `b` certifies 3. Conversely use a positive `q` as in 3 in (Sep); the factor `t` can belong to no maximal other than `(p)` since every maximal containing it contains `h`. A positive nontrivial divisor of `t` prime to `p` would belong to a maximal ideal other than `(p)`, a contradiction. If `p∤h` the least hit is simply 1. Basic opens on `X_h` justify the single-element description of isolation. In particular this proves the primary least test for **finite maximal support** `X_h`: multiply one separator in `M\(p)` for each other `M` (empty product 1); the same works for infinite support when a single separator is supplied. Pairwise idempotent separation is not isolation of one point from *all* others. The bounded test for (Sep) cannot be used by just dropping `q` to conclude full prime-primary extraction or `L(Div)`. We prove neither that such isolation always holds nor that it ever fails in an `IE₁⁻` model. For comparison Smith, JSL 57 (1992), proves unique factorization into prime powers for **parameterized** `IE₁` (abstract verified, full result marked PARTLY checked in `data/z66/ie1/lit_ie1.md`). The primary least instance also follows in that theory from `IE₁=LE₁` and the bounded-existential test for hits. The unresolved isolation question here concerns **only parameter-free** induction. The `Pow` divisor characterization reproduced in Smith, NDJFL 1993 (around §3; `data/z66/ie1/`), has a universal divisor test. Being bounded existential for the *hits* is not a bounded existential certificate of their leastness.

**BCM and the number of axes (PROVED).** In a Bézout IOpen domain with the prime-primary least instances of §5, if a nonzero proper ideal `I` is contained in `s` distinct principal prime ideals, it has at least `s` distinct minimal prime ideals over it, for every standard finite `s`. Choose a minimal over `I` inside each selected prime (external prime-ideal theorem, or intersect descending chains of primes). A minimal prime is nonzero and cannot be selected twice by §5. For `IE₁⁻` the same holds for **arbitrary distinct maximals** by the stronger result above. In a nonstandard BCM model choose an infinite cutoff and a common multiple `h>0` of that interval: each standard prime divides `h` and remains prime by ordinary remainders. Thus even `hR` needs infinitely many minimal primes above it under the least hypotheses. A nonzero shared conductor contained in all those standard prime ideals cannot be prime, primary, or an ideal with just finitely many minimal primes. This rules out replacing one shared conductor by a finite intersection of prime axes with the same shared divisibility, independently of twisting, value groups, Lag, QR and TS. In fact for an axis shared by just 2 and 3, §7's two-prime block alone already excludes `IE₁⁻`: factor any positive `h` in the common nonzero prime ideal to get the same contradiction. These are necessary constraints on architectures, not an inconsistency of BCM + least search; there is no z78-model upgrade to the joint package in this note.

## 9. Another positive application: proper Gauss reduction without a coded algorithm

**PROVED (relative to §4 and the elementary identities below).** `IE₁⁻` proves that every positive definite integral binary quadratic form `Q=aX²+bXY+cY²` (`a>0`, `d=4ac−b²>0`) is properly equivalent to `[A,B₁,C₁]` with `|B₁|≤A≤C₁` and `3A²≤d`. Only existence, not uniqueness of a reduced representative or enumerability of a form class group, is asserted. In particular this is uniform in the discriminant. The device is worth spelling out since the shortest-vector or minimizing-multiplier property in itself is not a bounded existential certificate.

Certificates are the two signed columns `(x,y),(z,w)` of a determinant-one change of basis and the transformed coefficients. Normalize the second column by adding an integral multiple of the first so `|B₁|≤A`: center `B₁` modulo `2A` by division, since this shear changes the middle coefficient by `2A` times that multiple. Always maintain `1≤A≤a`. If `C₁<A`, change columns to `(z,w),−(x,y)` and normalize again, strictly decreasing the positive first coefficient. Initialize with the identity and normalize. Definite positivity for nonzero integral columns follows by `4aQ(x,y)=(2ax+by)²+d y²`; determinant gives nonzero columns. `4AC₁=d+B₁²` yields `C₁≤d+a²`. Importantly the **columns**, not just form coefficients, have bounded existential witnesses: if `Q(x,y)≤d+a²`, then `y²≤4a(d+a²)` and `|2ax+by|²≤4a(d+a²)`. These bound `|x|,|y|` by fixed polynomials in the signed input magnitudes, and likewise for `(z,w)`. This holds after *every* normalization, including the one after a swap. Determinant one and the coefficient formulas are polynomial equalities. At level `k≥1` exhibit such a normalized matrix with either `A≤C₁` or `A+k≤a+1`; on failure of the reduced alternative the swap gains the required unit in the second inequality. At `k=a+1` the latter is impossible. Use §4's padding with signs encoded, declaring invalid form inputs by open tests; at `k=1` the normalized identity works. Thus this is a parameter-free `E₁` induction instance with base/step from division and ring arithmetic, not an invocation of reduction over ℤ transferred to a nonstandard ring.

**Consequence at a prime (PROVED).** For a supplied root `u²≡−m mod p` with `p,m>0` one can reduce `[p,2u,(u²+m)/p]` to a properly equivalent `[A,2B,C]` with `AC=B²+m`, `3A²≤4m`, and `p` primitively represented by it (inverse unimodular matrix applied to `(1,0)`). The new middle coefficient stays even by substitution. For example for `m=7` and odd prime `p` this proves `p=x²+7y²` from the root: reduced forms have `A≤3`; `A=1` gives `[1,0,7]`, `A=2` gives `[2,±2,4]` all values even, and `A=3` permits only `B=0,±1`, none with `3|B²+7`. For `p=7` the zero root and `x=0` are allowed. The same conditional representation holds for `m=1,2,3,4`: for `m=1,2` reduction forces `A=1`; for `m=3,4` the only other possibility `A=2` gives `[2,±2,2]` or `[2,0,2]`, both even forms. When `A=1`, `2|B|≤1` forces `B=0`. Compare Smith, NDJFL 1993 Thms 4.2, 4.4 and Cor. 4.5 for `m=1,2,3,4` in the parameterized theory: the point here is an explicit parameter-free reduction and the list including 7, not a new classical representation criterion. This is not a QNR existence theorem. It does not produce a nonprincipal genus, an admissible sign carrier, a cap `e*(p)`, or a four-square witness without additional hypotheses; compare the actual genus input in z75 `ANALYTIC_BREAK.md` §1. No priority claim for binary form reduction or its bounded-arithmetic treatment (see literature pointers in `data/z66/ie1/lit_idelta.md` §5).

## 10. Splitting the landing fibre: why the two evaluations agree for an ES tuple

**PROVED (reciprocal rigidity at a product fibre).** Let `C` be any ordered domain with fraction field `K`, and put `p=1+x₁⋯x_l` in the polynomial algebra, `l≥2` standard. Suppose polynomials `g_i` give an identity `Σ 1/g_i = k/p` with `k>0` constant, all positive at an ordered generic point with the coordinates positive. Then there are positive `c_i∈C` with

```
                 g_i = c_i + (p−1) h_i,         h_i∈C[x₁,…,x_l],
                 Σ 1/c_i = k.
```

Work in a common ordered real closure. The nonnegative orthant is semialgebraically path connected and `p≥1` there. Positivity propagates throughout it: on the simultaneous positive locus all denominators are `≥p/k`, so a point in its relative closure stays positive (the identity is polynomial after clearing). On any coordinate face `x_j=0` the sum is now constant. A positive reciprocal identity with **constant** right hand side among polynomial denominators positive on the whole orthant has only constant denominators. To see this, fix all but one remaining coordinate at positive values and let the last tend to `+∞`. Nonconstant polynomials tend to `+∞`; the constant summands already supply the full sum, so positivity precludes any others. Equivalently compare degrees after clearing, an argument valid over nonarchimedean real closed fields. Vary the fixed coordinates on the Zariski dense positive open. The restrictions are thus constant on each face; all faces contain the origin, so their constants coincide. In the polynomial basis vanishing on every face is exactly divisibility by the **squarefree** product. This proves the formula coefficientwise, without comparison of orders on a product of residue rings. In particular a mark into *any* algebra annihilating `x₁⋯x_l` gives the same marks for these particular denominators as evaluation at the origin. This is not true for arbitrary polynomials.

## 11. Absorb the factorial into the slope: all prime-primary tests at the bad fibre

**Theorem (PROVED rel. §§1–3,7 and strict odd-square descent in PA).** There is a further simultaneous window model over the same prescribed `D` with `J=T!`, a positive ring-theoretic prime `X`, `P=1+JX` an ES-failing prime, both `R/XR` and `R/PR` fields, and `P≡1 mod d` at every old modulus. Its positive divisors at `H=P−1` are **exactly**

```
                    d, dX    for positive B-divisors d of J.
```

The divisor lattice at `H` is the product of the divisor lattice of `J` in `B` with a two-element chain. Here `(H,J)=(J)` even when the image of `J` is zero; at `H` every prime-primary least test of §5 holds, and (Sep) holds for coprime switches from `B`. Unlike the two-factor construction, no finite preassigned partition of factorial support occurs in this statement. Still not global (Sep) or global Bézout.

Use `C[X]`, with `X=Tθ` as in §1 and mark `e(X)=1`. The new point is the **slope** `J` which need not be invertible in `C`. `C[X]/(P)=C[1/J]` by `X↦−1/J`, so `P` is a prime nonunit. The identity `(P−1)/d=(J/d)X` gives the congruences, `e(P)=1` since `J∈I`. Service §7 rows at **each** of `X,P`: both marks are 1 and the pivots generate 1 together. Modulo the pivot being serviced the old quotient embeds by the localization test; modulo the *other* pivot the serviced pivot is already a unit (at `P`, `X` has inverse `−J`), giving a polynomial extension. Thus both primes persist with field quotients at arithmetic completion. The same countable scheduling, optional varieties, order and mark produce exactly the approximation and windows of §§1–3. What changes is the ES seed proof: the mark gives `g_i(1)∈B` for the descended polynomials in **`X`**, not in `P`. We cannot simply land at `P=1`.

**Square-specialization lemma (PROVED).** Suppose `g_i(X)∈C[X]` give a positive ES triple at `1+JX` in this order with `g_i(1)∈B`. Choose `d∈D⁺` clearing coefficients into `B`. The polynomials `G_i` are positive on the entire positive `p`-ray by the clopen argument of §1 (the affine change is over `K`). Choose an odd `s>1` in `B` such that

```
         s ≡ 1+J/2 mod Jd,         gcd(s, M·∏_{F_i(0)≠0} |F_i(0)|)=1.
```

Indeed `4d|J`, so `1+J/2` is odd and coprime to `Jd` (each prime factor of `Jd` divides `J/2`). CRT and a positive translate suffice. Then `s²≡1+J mod Jd`, and `z=(s²−1)/J≡1 mod d`. At `n=s²` the three values `G_i(n)=g_i(z)` are positive `B`-integers. Each zero constant term of `F_i` gives `n|G_i(n)` since `M` is prime to `s`; each nonzero one gives `gcd(n,G_i(n))=1`. There is at least one zero by evaluating the cleared polynomial identity at zero. All three cannot vanish since `4≤3` after cancellation at `n`. Thus this would be a **strict** Type I or II solution at an odd square inside `B`.

For precision about this last use of PA, the strict obstruction of `LOGIC_SPLIT_SQUARE_TEST.md` (cf. Elsholtz–Tao Prop. 1.6) is an elementary Jacobi-symbol proof, not an appeal to transferring true arithmetic to arbitrary `B`. For a strict triple use `(nw,Y,Z)` or `(w,nY,nZ)`, respectively, with the stipulated coprimality. Put `d'=(4w−1)/n` in the first case (integral by reduction mod `n`) and `d'=4w−n` in the second. ## 12. What parameter-free induction buys in four-square descent

**PROVED (ordinary `IE₁⁻`, conditional representation criterion, not unconditional Lag).** For an odd prime `p`, the following are equivalent: `p` is four integral squares; the four-square form has a nontrivial zero mod `p`. In the latter test bound coordinates by `p−1`. In fact it suffices to have a **single** `1≤m<p` with `mp` a sum of four squares. All implications use ordinary number variables, not nonstandard powers. Forward reduce the representing vector mod `p` (not all zero since `p²∤p`). From a nontrivial zero, center the coordinates mod `p`; the positive norm is a multiple `mp≤(p−1)²<p²`. This supplies the first witness.

Here is the descent packaged without a least-multiplier axiom with parameters. At level `k≥1`, exhibit four signed entries with norm `np`, `1≤n≤m`, and either `n=1` or `n+k≤m+1`. If `n` is even, pair entries of like parity (there are an even number of odd entries); replacing each pair `(u,v)` by `((u+v)/2,(u−v)/2)` halves the norm. If `n>1` is odd, let `v` be the vector of centered residues of the current vector `u` modulo `n`, viewing vectors as integral Hamilton quaternions. Then `N(v)=n n'`, where `n'<n` since all four coordinates have size `≤(n−1)/2`. Also `v≠0` or else `n|p`, impossible since `1<n≤m<p`. Now `u conjugate(v)` is zero coordinatewise mod `n` (it is `u conjugate(u)` there), so the divided vector has integral entries and norm `p n'` by the polynomial four-square identity. These are descent witnesses, not elements of a new noncommutative theory. All entries have absolute value `≤mp+1`. Pack `p,m` **and the initializing four-vector** into §4's parameter block; allow openly invalid data or a nontrivial factorization of `p` as a bounded existential alternative. At level one reuse the valid initial vector, at `k=m+1` only `n=1` works at a prime. Base and step use division and parity only. This makes the precise parameter-free gain visible. It neither produces a nontrivial zero at every prime nor proves that every composite is four squares. Known stronger sufficient counting principles for full Lagrange are recalled in `Z66_IE1.md` §§2,7. No QNR or Euler/QR consequence without new residue information is obtained here. In particular a field quotient alone does not certify the finite-field counting argument in `IE₁⁻`.

**PROVED (unit progressions from (Sep)).** In `IE₁⁻`, if `h>1` and `(a,b,h)=1`, there is `0≤s<h` such that `a+bs` is invertible modulo `h` (stable range one in each nontrivial principal quotient). To see this, remove `g=gcd(a,b)`, a unit modulo `h`, and use the coprime cofactors `a',b'` (zero entries are immediate). Apply (Sep) with positive absolute switches `a',b'`: `h=uv`, `(u,b')=(v,a')=(u,v)=1`. Solve `a'+b's≡1 mod u`, `s≡0 mod v` by the rows and CRT, and take the remainder mod `h`. Thus `a'+b's` is coprime to both factors. This is a moving-modulus avoidance lemma, not an assertion that a particular progression contains a prime or an admissible ES divisor.

## 13. How many axes? A cardinal barrier with PA support

**PROVED (external, with a PA subring).** Let a discretely ordered IOpen domain contain an ordered signed PA subring `B` with a nonstandard element. Suppose every prime of `B` in the following binary partition stays a nonunit upstairs (automatic by discreteness). There is a nonzero element `h∈B` with **at least continuum many distinct minimal primes over `hR`**. No assertion of continuum many *elements* in the countable ring. If there is BCM and one takes a nonzero positive common multiple `L` for an interval containing `h`, then there are continuum many distinct maximals containing `L`, and if (Sep) and Bézout hold there are also continuum many minimal primes over `LR`. This is an additional obstruction to architectures with merely countably many minimal axes *over each element*, under the PA-subring hypothesis, not an unconditional result of BCM alone.

Take nonstandard `b∈B` and the internally coded product `h` of the first `b` primes of `B`, indexed starting at zero. At each standard binary level `k`, partition the indices by their residues modulo `2^k`, and let `h_s` be the product in cell `s`. Every cell contains a prime and the products are pairwise comaximal by rows in `B`, which persist upstairs. The root is `h`, and the parent product is its two children multiplied. Along each external binary path the ascending ideals generated by the corresponding products have proper union (all products are nonunits). Choose a maximal ideal containing that union, then a minimal prime over `hR` inside it. At every level the minimal prime contains a cell product since it contains their finite product `h`; its containing maximal can contain **only** the path cell. Thus distinct paths yield distinct minimal primes as well as distinct maximals, entirely without (Sep). All those maximals contain `L` if `h|L`; choose minimal primes over `LR` inside them. In an `IE₁⁻` model §8 says a nonzero prime cannot serve twice. More generally if `(Sep)` holds the same uniqueness proof applies. Without this uniqueness the last step for `L` requires a different argument. This does not preclude PA or a Prüfer ring with a genuinely large minimal spectrum. For instance a nonstandard PA model itself meets the splitting requirements. Countably many *presented valuations* need not bound the number of minimal primes of an intersection, so one must test the actual spectrum, not count labels in a construction.
