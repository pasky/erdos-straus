# Full collection, prime divisors, two squares and cofinal near-square ES failures

Result: every countable model of IOpen + Bézout + PD + TS has a proper countable end extension of the same kind containing h above the old ring with 1+h² prime and ¬ES(1+h²); an ω₁-chain gives one model of IOpen + Bézout + PD + TS + full collection with ∀a ∃h>a [Prime(1+h²) ∧ ¬ES(1+h²) ∧ ∀d≤a d|h]. **Joint corollary.** Every countable model of `IOpen + Bézout + PD + TS` has a proper countable ordered end extension (on nonnegative cones) satisfying all four axioms and containing a positive `h` above the old ring such that `p=1+h²` is prime and `¬ES(p)`. Consequently there is a model of those four axioms **and full first-order collection** in which

    ∀a≥0 ∃h>a [Prime(1+h²) ∧ ¬ES(1+h²) ∧ ∀d (0<d≤a → d | h)].

Here `ES(p)` is existence of three positive denominators with `4xyz=p(xy+yz+zx)`; PD asks for a positive prime divisor of every element ≥2, TS for a sum of two squares for every positive prime ≡1 mod 4. Primes are positive irreducibles; in a Bézout domain these are ring primes. In particular the failures are cofinal even among primes ≡1 modulo any prescribed standard modulus. Collection means a common bound for witnesses on each bounded domain, with arbitrary formulas and parameters, **not a code for choices**. This is a weak-theory countermodel, not a standard integer counterexample.

This combines the relative end construction in `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` with the absolute norm stage of `LOGIC_NORM_SPLIT.md` (logic-lift commits `88bc21143`, `32a2b999f`; absolute norm audit in `reviews/logic-norm-split-audit.md`). The special successor construction below proves PD and TS **on new inputs**. Preservation of bounded facts alone would not establish these global assertions.

## 1. Relative norm stage

Write R₀ for the old signed ring, F=Frac(R₀), K=rc(F). Use the convex hull O of F in the ambient ordered field, and the compatible embedding of its ordered residue field into H=rc(Cauchy completion of K) constructed in `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §1. Inside a single countable successor construction, F and H are fixed. Start with A=F[h], h above F, e(h)=0, where e:A→F is a retraction. Maintain A geometrically integral of finite type, **both A and A[i] UFDs**, A∩O=F, and e a smooth F-rational point. Only the smoothness of the landing is required, not global smoothness of A. A unit and its inverse cannot both have magnitude exceeding F, so A×=F×. The norm of a Gaussian unit is in F× and bounds the squares of its two coordinates, so A[i]×=F(i)×.

Floor tasks, controlled splits at m=ker e, and row principalizations are as in the relative proof, with two additional algebra checks. On a row a coprime pair stays coprime over A[i]: finite étale extension preserves height of primes (incomparability and going down), so no height-one prime can contain both. The two chart and localization arguments apply over both constant fields. At a controlled split B=A[T,m/T], the associated graded at the rational smooth maximal ideal is polynomial over F, and after base change polynomial over F(i). Localizing at T gives the factorial Laurent algebra in either case; Nagata gives both UFDs. Smoothness at the new tangent landing follows from flatness over F[T] and geometric smoothness of its zero fiber. Geometric integrality follows by the inclusion in the geometrically integral Laurent algebra after extension of constants. Thus singularities elsewhere do not invalidate the later steps.

Given a nonconstant finite-stage prime q normalized to e(q)=1, there are two cases. If q is not Gaussian-prime, it is already a normalized norm. Indeed (A/q)[i] is free of rank two over A/q and injects into the generic quadratic algebra. In the split case its two generic conjugate kernels are distinct primes with zero intersection, hence exactly its minimal primes. The quotient is reduced. Unique factorization gives q=c z·conj(z) with c∈F× (units are constant and conjugation fixes c). Evaluating gives e(z)≠0 and q=N(z/e(z)); the coordinates land at 1 and 0. Otherwise A[i]/q is a domain and −1 is nonsquare in Frac(A/q). Use the norm stage

    B=A[X,Y,W]/(DW−q),        D=X²+Y²,        (e(X),e(Y),e(W))=(1,0,1).

It supplies the prime divisor D of q, not necessarily a norm representation of q. In any coefficient domain with q nonzero, q acts injectively modulo D by monicity in X; reducing modulo D shows the relation ideal is D-saturated. Hence B embeds after inverting D in A[X,Y,1/D]. The same check over extensions of constants gives geometric integrality, and over A/s preserves every old rational-stage prime s not associate to q. Further B/D is a domain by the nonsquare condition and monicity. Localizing at D gives a UFD, so B is factorial. Over F(i) the relation is `(X+iY)(X−iY)W=q`: both linear factors are prime by the domain A[i]/q, and their simultaneous localization is a Laurent polynomial UFD, proving B[i] factorial. The landing is in the smooth product chart D≠0.

**Order check relative to F.** Over the old ambient real closure first take ζ with residue in (1,2) in H transcendental over the *entire old residue field*. Next take T above F but so slow that every old positive element outside O, **including those of the enlarged field containing ζ**, exceeds every T^j, j a positive standard integer. Use X=T, Y=Tζ, W=q/(T²(1+ζ²)); T,ζ are algebraically independent over Frac(A). These are the Gauss and intermediate-scale extensions of the relative proof, so their residues, and those of their ordered real closures, still embed compatibly in H. Taking T merely larger than standard numerals does not suffice.

Write an element as P(X,Y)/D^k with P=Σ b_j(X,Y)q^j D^(k−j), 0≤j≤k, from an expression polynomial in W. In each homogeneous degree d the ordered term is `T^(d−2k) P_d(1,ζ)/(1+ζ²)^k`. If a coefficient of P is outside F, it lies outside O. In its block, divide by a coefficient of minimal relative valuation: reduction is a nonzero polynomial at the fresh residue (distinct monomials give distinct powers). Thus the block coefficient is outside O. Even negative powers of T cannot bring it into O, and different exponents cannot cancel by the scale comparison. Boundedness forces P∈F[X,Y]. Now P≡b₀D^k mod q, and F embeds into A/q. Monic division in X shows D^k divides P in F[X,Y]. A nonconstant remaining F-polynomial has a positive-degree top block of relative-unit coefficient and is unbounded. This proves B∩O=F, including for candidates of numerator and denominator of equal degree. The separate residue approximation for the floor tasks still applies: approximate bounded residues by K within 1/4 and then floor in R₀; do not round an arbitrary F-bound by a standard integer.

## 2. Global prime assertions in the successor

In addition to floors and principalizations, service each nonconstant stage element r with e(r)=±1. Choose a prime divisor at **service time** in the current UFD and normalize that divisor to evaluation 1; indeed all its factors have nonzero evaluation. In the split case record that divisor, in the inert case record the new D. In either case it is a positive normalized prime with a Gaussian norm representation whose two coordinates have R₀-landing, and it divides r with R₀-landing quotient. Such a prime is protected. Polynomial and row stages preserve it; at a controlled split the exceptional parameter T becomes invertible modulo it since its evaluation is 1, giving the nonzero domain `(A/s)[T,T⁻¹]`. At another norm stage it cannot be the inert target: its two nonconstant Gaussian factors remain nonunits since Gaussian units are constants and the embeddings fix F(i). The saturation check preserves it. The same protection applies from the beginning to **p=1+h²**: this is prime in F[h] since F is ordered, and has Gaussian factors h±i. It is normalized, positive and has integral-landing norm coordinates.

In the countable union A*, let R'=e⁻¹(R₀). The floor proof, fractional-ideal Bézout descent and endness proof of `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §3 apply unchanged; they use the finite-stage UFDs but not a UFD at the union. Recorded primes remain prime in R' because division by an evaluation-1 prime in A* preserves membership in R'. To check PD and TS one must also account for the **old nonstandard primes**:

* For each positive old irreducible ℓ, the surjection `R' → R₀/ℓR₀` has kernel ℓR': if e(b)∈ℓR₀ then b/ℓ is defined in A* (ℓ∈F×) and has R₀-landing. The quotient is a field by old Bézout and irreducibility. Thus every such ℓ remains prime. For b≥2 of nonunit nonzero landing use old PD on |e(b)|; for zero landing take ℓ=2, prime by discreteness. In both cases an old positive prime divides b.
* If e(b)=±1 and b≥2 it cannot be a constant. Its serviced task gives a protected positive prime divisor. This covers all b because the only units in the discrete ordered ring R₀ are ±1. It proves PD in R'. If b itself is a positive prime it must equal its positive prime divisor (the quotient is a positive unit). Thus any prime not in R₀ has evaluation **+1** and is a protected norm; both coordinates belong to R'. Such primes are also ≡1 mod 4 by evaluation. An old prime ≡1 mod 4 in R' was already so in R₀ (the quotient (b−1)/4 lies in F and R'∩F=R₀), and retains its old TS witnesses. This proves global TS, not merely preservation for a preselected list.

## 3. Landing positivity over a nonarchimedean base

The retraction need **not** preserve the ordering on A. To rule out ES at the *new* p, maintain that e and the ordered generic point are in the same **semialgebraically connected component of the smooth locus**. More precisely take the component of e over K; its interpretation in the ambient real closure contains the generic point. Semialgebraic components and their interpretations commute with extension of real closed fields (semialgebraic triangulation, or definable path components). All coordinates of the algebra and of e are allowed as parameters in K. There is no use of an embedding of K into ℝ. Initially the line is connected. Since 1+h² is strictly positive over every ordered field, no additional open-set restriction for p is needed.

Here are the local checks in that invariant. The topology is the order topology and connectedness always means *semialgebraic* connectedness, not unrestricted topological connectedness.

* Polynomial steps lift the old smooth component to a product. At a norm stage the D≠0 chart above the old component is its product with the punctured affine plane on real points. This is semialgebraically path connected over any real closed field and contains both lifts after scalar extension; W has no sign requirement.
* At a row, the common zero locus of a coprime pair has algebraic codimension ≥2; the dimension bound persists on scalar extension and bounds semialgebraic dimension in the old smooth manifold. Deleting this closed subset does not disconnect the component over K. Locally, in a chart ball of dimension n, any two points off a set of dimension ≤n−2 can be joined by two segments avoiding it: the cones of bad intermediate points from the two endpoints have dimension ≤n−1. Such ball complements are dense and connected. The finitely many semialgebraic components of the global complement consequently have disjoint relatively open closures covering the old connected manifold, so there is just one. Above the complement, the real row is a trivial line bundle by the section (u,v)/(u²+v²). In dimensions <2 the common zero locus is empty there.
* At the deformation the fiber T=0 is the smooth tangent space. The projection to the T-line is smooth **at the chosen tangent landing**, by flatness and geometric smoothness of this fiber. Locally there is a smooth semialgebraic chart with T one coordinate: choose étale coordinates relative to T and use the semialgebraic inverse function theorem for a nonsingular Jacobian over a real closed field. Equivalently its local openness is the algebraic implicit-function property, valid with parameters by real closed transfer. The old projection sends the tangent landing to e. Shrink the chart so the old projection lies in the old smooth component, which is relatively open in the real locus near e. Every neighborhood of the tangent landing in this chart meets T>0. Thus the tangent landing belongs to the closure of the semialgebraically connected positive product slice above **that** component. All these points are smooth. Adjoining the landing to the slice preserves semialgebraic connectedness, so it lies in the component containing the lifted ordered point. No sequential compactness, analytic limit over ℝ, or ordinary intermediate-value principle for arbitrary functions on K is being invoked.

Now a putative positive ES triple in R' and h occur in a common finite stage. The equation is a polynomial identity there. In its smooth component, the sector where all three coordinates are positive is semialgebraically clopen: there `1/x+1/y+1/z=4/p` forces each coordinate ≥p/4, and p stays strictly positive at every point of its closure. The sector contains the generic point in the extended real closed field, hence the landing there. At the landing the three values are in R₀; they are strictly positive in K and so ≥1 by **discreteness of R₀**, even when nonstandard. Their reciprocal sum cannot be 4=4/e(p). This proves ¬ES(p) in the successor. Also for each positive d∈R₀, h/d has zero landing and belongs to R'. Thus this single h exceeds the old ring and is divisible by all its positive elements. Neither divisibility nor positivity here requires elementarity of the extension.

## 4. Iteration

Start, for example, with ℤ, and use this special successor through ω₁, resetting F and its residue host at each successor. At a countable limit, endness and the directed-union floor and Bézout arguments apply. PD witnesses from each stage remain prime by endness (a positive factor of an old prime is bounded by it). A prime in the union was prime at its stage, and its congruence mod 4 is absolute by endness; old TS witnesses suffice. The same arguments apply at the final union. For a sorted ES triple at p the ordered-ring bounds `x≤3p/4`, `y≤2px`, `z≤pxy≤2p⁴` hold (see `LOGIC_BEZOUT_COLLECTION.md` §4). Therefore primality and **absence of any ES triple** at every newly produced p persist forever. Given a bound a in the final union, its next successor supplies a new h>a and p=1+h² as above. All 0<d≤a are already in a's stage, so the extra divisibility clause holds in the union. Only one such h per successor is needed; no global retraction to the very first ring is assumed.

Finally every bounded input interval of the union is countable, and every countable set of witness stages is bounded below ω₁. Selecting witnesses in the final union and using a later proper end extension bounds them all, for each full-collection instance with final-model parameters. This completes the joint corollary. No coded factorization, uniform choice sequence, or stronger induction scheme was obtained.
