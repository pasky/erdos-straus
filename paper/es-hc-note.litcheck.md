# Literature/priority check: Erdős–Straus HC statistic

**Search date:** 2026-09-24. This is a targeted priority check, not an exhaustive MathSciNet/zbMATH review. I searched arXiv records/listings and web-indexed Scholar-like results for the query families in the assignment (Erdős–Straus, unit fractions, least solutions/denominators, shifted sums of two squares, shifted primes, congruence covers, Elliott–Halberstam, and 2023–26 work). For the arXiv records below, I visited the linked abstract pages; the links are direct abstract records. Search engines returned some dubious or very recent purported ES manuscripts; I do not treat snippets or unpublished claims as established results.

## Most relevant findings

1. **Christian Elsholtz and Terence Tao, “Counting the number of solutions to the Erdős–Straus equation on unit fractions,” J. Aust. Math. Soc. 94 (2013), 50–105.** [arXiv:1107.1010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1010). The paper estimates the total number of ordered positive-integer solutions, summed over prime denominators: its abstract gives \(N(\log N)^2 \ll \sum_{p\le N} f(p) \ll N(\log N)^2\log\log N\), with typical-prime and pointwise bounds as consequences. This counts solutions rather than minimizing a denominator or the HC parameter. **Overlap: partial with (a), none with (i)–(vi)** (context for solution abundance, not least-parameter statistics).

2. **Serge E. Salez, “The Erdős-Straus conjecture: New modular equations and checking up to \(N=10^{17}\),” arXiv:1406.6307 (2014).** [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.6307). Constructs a complete system of seven modular equations and describes a sieve computation verifying ES through \(10^{17}\). This is a direct precedent for finite computation and congruence-based ES coverage, but not the proposed HC census or its explicit maximum. **Overlap: partial with (v), (vi); none with the other items.**

3. **Miguel Angel Lopez, “A Complete Congruence System for the Erdos-Straus Conjecture,” arXiv:2404.01508 (2024).** [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01508). Proposes a solution-type-based system of congruences, discusses Mordell’s restriction that congruences admitting polynomial identities are non-quadratic-residue classes, and conjectures that every prime has a Type A or B solution. This is the closest recent ES/congruence reference found, but the abstract asserts a conjectural coverage program, not a theorem ruling out every finite one-coordinate certificate family on specified non-square classes. **Overlap: partial with (v); none with (i)–(iv), (vi).**

4. **Martin Bright and Daniel Loughran, “Brauer-Manin obstruction for Erdős-Straus surfaces,” Bull. Lond. Math. Soc. 52 (2020), 746–761.** [arXiv:1908.02526](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02526). Studies the integral Hasse principle and strong approximation for the ES surface using Brauer–Manin obstructions. This is arithmetic-geometric local/global obstruction theory, not a finite-covering impossibility theorem for the one-coordinate certificates in the note; do not conflate the two meanings of obstruction. **Overlap: partial thematic context for (v), no direct overlap with (i)–(iv), (vi).**

5. **Spiridon Mihnea and Dumitru C. Bogdan, “Further verification and empirical evidence for the Erdős-Straus conjecture,” arXiv:2509.00128 (2025).** [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00128). Reports computational verification up to \(10^{18}\) and evaluates the solution-counting function. It supersedes the scale of the Salez computation as a general ES verification, but says nothing in its abstract about the HC statistic or the restricted \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) census through \(10^{12}\). **Overlap: partial with (vi); none with (i)–(v).**

## Analytic number theory and shifted two-square inputs

6. **Kunjakanan Nath and Likun Xie, “Almost primes and primes that are sums of two squares plus 1,” arXiv:2501.16723 (2025; revised 2026; published in Acta Arithmetica).** [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16723). Gives a lower bound for primes \(p\le x\) with \(p-1\) a sum of two squares and \(p+2\) having boundedly many prime factors, using a vector sieve (semi-linear and linear sieves). This is an adjacent sieve precedent, but it does not establish the note’s asymmetric conditions on \(p+4\) and \(4p+1\), nor the stated small-prime-factor exclusion for the latter. **Overlap: partial methodological precedent for (iv); none with the other items.**

7. **John B. Friedlander and Henryk Iwaniec, “Hyperbolic prime number theorem,” Acta Mathematica 202 (2009), 1–19.** [Publisher/article record](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/6709-11511_2009_Article_33.pdf). This is a prime-distribution theorem in a hyperbolic setting; the cited analogue concerning simultaneous representation/weights at two shifts is relevant as an analytic model, not an ES theorem. I did not find evidence that it treats the particular pair \(p+4,4p+1\), or proves the note’s EH-conditional lower bound. **Overlap: partial with (iii) as an analogue/methodological precedent; none with (i), (ii), (iv)–(vi).**

8. **Serge E. Salez, “The Erdős-Straus conjecture: New modular equations and checking up to \(N=10^{17}\),”** is also listed above; no separate shifted-two-square result was found attributable to Salez. Searches for “Elliott–Halberstam sums of two squares two shifts,” “\(p-2,p+2\),” and Teräväinen/Matomäki/Sedunova did not locate an arXiv result proving simultaneous all-prime-factors-\(1\bmod4\) for the target shifts. In particular, the Nath–Xie theorem is for \(p-1,p+2\), with the second shift only almost-prime, and should not be presented as that result.

## Other ES work and scope limits

- ArXiv search surfaced **Mihnea–Dumitru, arXiv:2509.00128** as above. Its abstract claims empirical verification, not a proof of the conjecture.
- ArXiv search also found **Philemon Urbain Mballa, “Partial Resolution of the Erdős–Straus, Sierpiński, and Generalized Erdős–Straus Conjectures Using New Analytical Formulas,” arXiv:2502.20935** ([record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20935)). It advertises a proposed partial analytical approach, but I found no verified theorem relevant to HC, shifted two-square primes, or certificate sterility. **Overlap: none established with (i)–(vi).**
- “Constructive Proofs …” and other 2025–26 ES titles appeared in search results, but abstracts/snippets were insufficient to validate their claims; some results were dated after the search date or appear to be speculative/preprint claims. They are intentionally not cited as established priority. No relevant Lean formalization was verified in the targeted arXiv search.
- Searches did not identify a paper specifically defining the least-denominator/smallest-parameter statistic HC, proving extreme-value results for it, or treating this exact least divisor-certificate parameter. The best neighboring literature is Elsholtz–Tao’s count of solutions and ES computational verification papers. Therefore the novelty claim should be stated narrowly: “we did not locate prior work on this particular HC statistic/certificate minimum,” not as a universal claim that no related least-solution work exists.
- For the non-square-class result, the closest sources are Mordell-type polynomial-congruence limitations as discussed in Lopez and the general Brauer–Manin literature. No paper was found proving the precise impossibility assertion for finite families of the note’s one-coordinate certificates on the class \(p\equiv1\pmod{840}, p\equiv R\pmod\ell\) with \(R\) a non-residue. Avoid attributing the note’s precise “sterile profinite point” statement to those sources.

## Item-by-item priority judgment

| Note item | Closest literature found | Priority/overlap judgment |
|---|---|---|
| (i) HC(p)>4 iff both specified shifts have only prime factors 1 mod 4 | No direct match located | No overlap found; a note-internal characterization. |
| (ii) \(p=5m^2+1\), \(4\mid m\), implies HC(p)≥7 | No direct match located | No overlap found. |
| (iii) EH lower bound for both \(p+4\), \(4p+1\) having only 1 mod 4 prime factors | FI 2009 is an analogue; no exact-shift theorem located | Partial precedent only, not same result. |
| (iv) unconditional vector-sieve lower bound with \(p+4\) restricted and no small 3-mod-4 factor in \(4p+1\) | Nath–Xie 2025 | Partial technique/shape precedent; different shifts and conclusion. |
| (v) finite certificate families fail on specified non-square class | Mordell/Lopez; Bright–Loughran BM | Partial neighboring context only; precise theorem not found. |
| (vi) HC≤64 census for primes \(1\bmod4\) below \(10^{12}\) | Salez and Mihnea–Dumitru general ES checks | Partial computational precedent; distinct statistic and range/subset. |

**Bottom line:** the clearest overlap risk is that ES solution-counting and modular congruence systems are already studied, and recent general ES verification is substantially beyond the proposed census range. None of the verified abstract records found appears to contain the particular HC minimum, the exact shifted-prime assertions in (iii)–(iv), or the stated finite-certificate obstruction on non-square classes. The latter conclusions are search-negative, not proof of absence; check full texts and references before making a strong novelty claim.
