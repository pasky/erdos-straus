## Verbatim evidence and page/number details

### 1. Nath–Xie (version comparison)
- v1, Theorem 1.1, printed p. 3: “Ω(p + 2) ≤ 11” and “≫ x/(log x)^(5/2).” **Matches the v1 claim.**
- Latest retrieved v3, Theorem 1.1, printed p. 3: “Ω(p + 2) ≤ 9” and “≫ x/(log x)^(5/2).” **The user’s ≤11 claim is stale for latest version.**
- (3.4), printed p. 8: “V_i(w)/V_i(z) ≤ (log z/log w)^{κ_i}(1+L_i/log w).” Thus it is the sieve product-ratio hypothesis.
- Lemma 3.6 (printed pp. 8–9): “Then for i∈{1,2}” the upper/lower estimates use F_i(s_i), f_i(s_i), with “β_i=β_i(κ_i)”; Remark 3.7 gives “β_i(1/2)=1” and “β_i(1)=2.”
- Proposition 3.10, printed pp. 10–11: lower main term is “XV(z_0,h*)V_1(z_1,z_0)V_2(z_2,z_0) f(σ_1,σ_2)” plus remainder R−. Here “f(σ_1,σ_2)=sup{ f_1(s_1)F_2(s_2)+f_2(s_2)F_1(s_1)−F_1(s_1)F_2(s_2): s_1/σ_1+s_2/σ_2=1, s_i≥β_i }.” Remainder has Σ_{d_1d_2≤D^(1+ε), d_i|P_i(z_i)} τ(d_1d_2)^4 |r(d_1,d_2)|. So the asserted shorthand is directionally right but omits factors/optimization.
- Section 5, latest version: semi-linear functions are given as F(s)=2(e^γ/(πs))^(1/2), 0<s≤2, and f(s)=(e^γ/(πs))^(1/2)·2 arccosh(√s), 1≤s≤3. The text explicitly cites “[4, (14.2)–(14.3)]” for β=1; [4] is Friedlander–Iwaniec, *Opera de Cribro*, Theorem 11.12 is cited for the beta-sieve result. It cites [4, (12.1)–(12.2)] for the β=2 linear sieve functions. Equations (14.2)–(14.3) are not themselves the theorem number.

### 2. Ford, *Sieve Methods*
- Theorem 2.4: PDF p. 16 (printed page; theorem starts at PDF text around line 921); theorem begins “Let A be a sieve problem, let z≥2 and assume (g), (r) and (V).” This is a standard upper-bound sieve theorem, with its full hypotheses essential.
- Theorem 3.4: PDF p. 33; heading reads “Theorem 3.4 (Bombieri-A.I.Vinogradov, 1965). For every A>0 there is a B>0…” The result is the average prime-distribution estimate over q≤x^(1/2)/(log x)^B.
- (7.6): PDF p. 78; defines EH(θ) using a maximum over y≤x and moduli q≤x^θ with the prime progression discrepancy and an ≪_A x/(log x)^A bound. Conjecture 7.3: “We have EH(θ) for every θ<1.”

### 3. Hyperbolic prime number theorem
- A(θ), printed p. 3: “E(x,Q) ≪ x(log x)^(−A) holds for Q=x^(θ−ε), for every ε>0 and A>0…”
- Theorem 2, p. 3: “If A(θ) holds for some θ, less than 1 but sufficiently close to 1,” then c₁x/log x < π_Γ(x) < c₂x/log x; the prose says the upper bound is unconditional.
- Theorem 3, p. 4: the corresponding sum over determinant-one quadruples of Λ(x₁²+x₂²+x₃²+x₄²) is ≍x.
- Theorem 4, p. 5: “S(x)=Σ_{n≤x} r(n−2)r(n+2)Λ(n) ≍ x.” The paper says this holds under the same assumption as Theorem 2. This confirms the claimed analytic content but corrects the theorem association: Theorem 4 is the requested r-correlation statement; Theorem 3 is the four-square/determinant form.

### 6. Rosser–Schoenfeld
- Corollary 1 to Theorem 2, (3.6), printed p. 69: “π(x)<1.25506 x/log x for 1<x.”
- Theorem 9, (3.32), printed p. 71: “θ(x)<1.01624 x for 0<x.” Both quoted constants/ranges are verified, but the theorem associations must be given separately.

### 7. Elsholtz–Tao
- §1, p. 3: “Type I solution if n divides x but is coprime to y,z” and “Type II solution if n divides y,z but is coprime to x.”
- Proposition 1.6, p. 7: “For any odd perfect square n, we have f_I(n)=f_II(n)=0.” So it is not the Type I/II characterization.
- Theorem 1.1, pp. 4–5: average bounds on f_I and f_II; for primes, lower and upper bounds are as stated in the table. It yields “N(log N)^2 ≪ Σ_{p≤N}f(p) ≪ N(log N)^2 log log N.”
- §1, around p. 8: “any primitive residue class n=r mod 840 is solvable”; this is a polynomial solvability result and not a claim the six Mordell residue classes fail.

### 9. Computation
- Salez paper, title page/abstract: “checking up to n=10^17.” Elsholtz–Tao Table 1, p. 3, independently lists Salez (2014), 10^17.
- Mihnea–Dumitru, p. 1: “We improved this bound to p≤10^18 by extending this approach with S_29”; the paper says it checked the remaining candidates up to that bound.
