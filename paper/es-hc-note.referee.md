## Numbered issues

1. **MAJOR — False primorial assertion and unsupported quantitative consequence.** §5, lines 1001–1003, immediately after Proposition 5.1: “The primes produced are ≡1 modulo every prime ℓ≤X+1, so log p≥(1+o(1))X.” This is false for the stated `Q=lcm(24,c(c+1):c∈C_X)`. For **X=10**, `C_X={4,7,8}` and **Q=2520**, not divisible by 11. This is not merely a hypothetical loophole: **s=58422** gives the seven prime values, in the order `p,F_4,G_4,F_7,G_7,F_8,G_8`,

   `147223441, 29444689, 117778753, 18402931, 128820511, 16358161, 130865281`.

   Here `p≡2 (mod 11)`, and all the hypotheses of the construction hold. I independently checked primality of all seven numbers. The discrepancy is asymptotic, not an endpoint error: an odd prime `ℓ≡3 (mod 8)` divides Q precisely when `3ℓ−1≤X` (apart from the fixed factor 24). Its earliest eligible occurrence in c or c+1 is at `c=3ℓ−1`. Primes in classes 1 and 5 occur up to X+1, and those in class 7 up to X. Higher prime powers contribute o(X), so PNT modulo 8 gives

   `log Q=(3/4+1/12+o(1))X=(5/6+o(1))X`.

   Thus the immediate consequence is only `log p≥(5/6+o(1))X`; the stronger coefficient 1 needs a different argument. **Fix:** replace the two assertions by this corrected consequence, or enlarge Q in Proposition 5.1 to `lcm(Q,∏_{ℓ≤X+1}ℓ)`, which preserves the admissibility and divisor arguments and makes the stated consequence valid. Replace “this is forced” by “a logarithmic restriction is forced”: Theorem W proves coefficient 1/4, not 1. The earlier correction to `log Q` in `Z53_HCSHIFTS.md` §2.2 and its review has regressed in this note.

2. **MINOR — Type I/II attribution silently drops the prime/coprimality restriction.** Introduction, lines 190–193: the unqualified assertion that the solutions in Proposition 2.2(c) are Type I or II is false for the composite n allowed by that proposition. ET Proposition 2.7 also explicitly requires `abd` coprime to n in `4abcd=a+b+nc`, a hypothesis omitted from the description. **Fix:** begin “For n=p prime, the solutions …”; in the c=1 comparison identify `(a,b,d)=(u,v,t/4)` and retain the coprimality condition (automatic here at primes). ## Proofs independently checked

The following is a proof audit, not an inference from the numerical checker.

### Words, reciprocity, and explicit classes (§§2–3)

- **Lemma 2.1:** expanding `(tu−1)(tv−1)` gives `t(tuv−u−v)+1`; t is nonzero. Positivity excludes `tu=1`. Ordering u≤v gives `v(tu−2)≤n`; the exceptional `tu=2` cases give v=n+1 or n+2. All coordinate bounds follow.
- **Proposition 2.2:** a product divisible by 8 has a factor divisible by 4, proving the odd-coordinate direction. For the converse, `4|tu−1` forces t,u odd; the only unresolved case is `tu≡5 (mod 8)`, v odd. Then u≡3 or 7 modulo 8 and t≡−n modulo 8. I recomputed all three displayed rational sums and both u/v exchanges: their common-denominator numerators reduce respectively to `tuv`, `t(n+v)=tuA*`, and `v(tn+1)=vAA*`. All denominators are positive. No coprimality is needed for these identities.
- **Lemma 2.3:** U follows from `v(tc−1)=n+c`. On T, `de=cn+1` and `d≡−1 (mod c)` force `e≡−1 (mod c)`, giving positive integral u,v. Neither converse loses a positivity or divisibility condition.
- **Proposition 2.6:** the word for c=3 is valid for every odd p; admissibility selects p≡5 modulo 8. At c=4, a 3-mod-4 divisor exists exactly when a 3-mod-4 prime factor exists. The three excluded endpoints p,p+1,p+2 establish HC≤p−1 when finite.
- **Lemma 2.7:** valid for **all positive n≡1 (mod 4)**, not merely primes or n≡1 modulo 8. Since `(c,d)=1`, either line makes n a unit modulo the odd part d′. Reciprocity gives `(d/n)=(2/n)^a(−c/d′)`. For c=4k, d is 3 modulo 4 and `(k/d)=1`, including the powers of 2 when 8|c. For odd c≡−n modulo 8, `(−c/d′)=(d′/c)=−(2/c)^a`, and `(2/c)=(2/n)`. Both cases give −1. The coprimality conclusion is established, not assumed. Corollary 2.8's square, smooth-support, and Dirichlet consequences follow.
- **Proposition 3.1:** all six third coordinates are positive integers under the listed CRT conditions. In particular the c=7 T-line uses d=20 and `p≡17 (mod 20)`. The missing residues are exactly p≡1 modulo 8, p≡1 modulo 3, p≡±1 modulo 5, and p≡1,2,4 modulo 7; CRT gives precisely the six listed classes modulo 840.

### Two shifts and sieve inputs (§4.1–4.3)

- **Lemma 4.1:** immediate from the c=4 criterion and the gap between 4 and 7 in C_p; infinity causes no exception.
- **Remark 4.2:** determinant is `4(x²+y²)−(u²+v²)=15`, and squared Frobenius norm is `8(x²+y²)+2(u²+v²)=16p+34`. The two lattice congruences make all four inverse coordinates integral. Conversely norm±2 determinant recover both equations. For odd N, primitive sum-of-two-squares representation is equivalent to N∈S: an inert prime forces a common divisor, while choosing one Gaussian prime above each split prime gives a primitive representation. The warning that r(N)>0 alone is weaker is correct.
- **Theorem Q:** both shifts are 5 times an odd number of the form a²+1, excluding all inert prime factors. Primality forces 3|m, and 4|m gives p≡1 modulo 16. The irreducible polynomial 720k²+1 has no fixed prime divisor. Only its prime-value infinitude requires a conjecture.
- **Lemma 4.4:** `E(x;q)≪x/φ(q)` for q≤x. The Euler factor for `∑k^{2ω(q)}/φ(q)` is exactly `1+k²ℓ/(ℓ−1)²`. Cauchy–Schwarz therefore absorbs any fixed divisor weight using arbitrary logarithmic savings. This is enough for both EH and BV uses.
- **Lower-bound sieve inequality, label `eq:lowersieve` (current equation (6)):** sum the lower weights over each element's squarefree small-prime support; their pointwise sum is at most its survival indicator. Expanding and using `|λ⁻|≤1` proves the stated inequality with no missing positivity assumption on λ⁻.
- **Lemma 4.7:** the Bernoulli-product weights w(n) are nonnegative and sum to one; hence `V⁻≤V≤V⁺`. Combined with the beta bounds at fixed s this really does imply `f≤1≤F` (the inequality directions are correct). The delay equations then give F′≤0 and `(√s(F−f))′≤0` for s>2. Thus the stated 1.51 bound follows from `2√(e^γ/π)<1.51`; it is deliberately loose. Finally `arccosh y≥√(y²−1)/y` proves the lower bound on f. The numerics 1.505… and `22801=151²` are consistent.
- **Proposition 4.8:** the integer sieve has root counts 1 on split primes, 2 at 3, and 3 at inert primes ≥7, hence dimension 2. The mod-8 restriction gives X=x/8. The remainders are at most `3^ω(d)` and sum to `O(z log²z)`. With z=x¹/⁴ the omitted small primes and remainder are negligible. Ford's actual theorem allows precisely this remainder range.

### Theorem E: all five steps

1. The prime-sequence density is `ν(d)/φ(d)`, with ν(3)=1 and ν(q)=2 for other inert primes; CRT uses **8d**, and X=li(x)/4. Both roots are units. The dimension is **1**, not 1/2.
2. Taking D=x¹⁻η/8 keeps all moduli within the assumed endpoint EH range. Weighted remainders follow from Lemma 4.4. For fixed η and sufficiently large x, `2+η≤s≤2.25` and `f_1(s)≥0.79η`. The resulting coefficient c₁ can be chosen independently of η≤0.1.
3. Each shift is 1 modulo 4, so the total inert-prime multiplicity is even. Since `z³>4x+1`, a bad shift has exactly two such factors, **allowing equality**, with cofactor m∈S and m≤5x²η.
4. On integers k, the switched roots are `0,4/a,15/(4a)` for U, or `0,1/a,−15/a` for T, with the third root restricted to inert primes ≥7. There are no collisions there. At 3 there are two roots, at 2 one; small primes dividing a divide m and are split primes. Deleting their extra root gives precisely `h(m)=∏(ℓ−1)/(ℓ−2)`. The universal root envelope has dimension **5/2** and an absolute product-condition constant. Thus Ford's constant is independent of η,m,q. The total elementary remainder is `O(x^.61 log²x)`. The reciprocal q-sum is O(η), and `∑_{m≤5x²η,m∈S}h(m)/m≪√(η log x)` follows directly from the Euler factors `1+1/(ℓ−2)`. This proves an **absolute** C₂ in the O(η³/²) loss. For T the integrality condition on k can safely be dropped in an upper bound; odd-prime roots remain the ones stated.
5. The η versus η³/² subtraction and final choice of η₀ work. All sufficiently-large-x thresholds may depend on the eventually fixed η. No switched prime-distribution hypothesis or generalized EH is used.

### Theorem U: vector sieve and switching

- **Local system and remainder:** for inert q≥7 the two conditions are incompatible if q divides both divisor coordinates; at 3 they coincide with density 1/2. Thus h(q,q)=0 or 1/2 as stated, and A2 holds with C₁=5. Each component has dimension **1/2**. Compatible pairs give a single unit class modulo **8 lcm(d₁,d₂)**; incompatible pairs have both count and remainder zero. For squarefree l, at most `3^ω(l)` pairs have that lcm. Since `τ(d₁d₂)≤4^ω(l)`, the weighted remainder is bounded by a sum with weight `768^ω(l)`. Lemma 4.4 absorbs it under BV. The inequality `8D^(1+ε)≤x^(1/2−ε)` holds eventually for the stated positive ε. There is no unaccounted multiplicity or modulus-8 loss.
- **Main product:** division by W(z₁)W(z₂) gives 9/8 at 3 and `q²(q−3)/(q−1)³=1+O(q⁻²)` at the other inert primes. The product is positive and convergent. The large-prime correction tends to 1.
- **Allocation:** `s₁=(1−η/2)/(1−η)∈[1+η/2,7/6]`. The bracket is at least `f(s₁)−F(1)·1.51/√S₀`, hence at least `0.59F(1)√η`; the advertised 0.5 leaves a margin. Although S₀ and σ₂ can be very large, they are fixed as x→∞. NX's bounded-parameter condition does not require uniform error constants as η→0. Choosing η against the switched absolute constant is therefore legitimate.
- **Parity and switching:** four inert factors already exceed x+4; even multiplicity leaves zero or two. The switched interval endpoint `(x+4)/(mq)` is correct. With w=x¹/¹⁶, the universal integer-sieve envelope again has dimension 5/2, independently of δ; the actual product is `≪h(m)W(z₂)/log²x` with an absolute constant. Pair-count errors are `O(x^.6875 log²x)=O(x^.7)`.
- **Sums and uniformity:** Mertens gives the displayed bound `(2/3)(η−log m/log x)_++O(1/log x)`. The Euler-product bound for H(t), followed by partial summation, gives O(η³/²√log x); the atom m=1 is included in the integral. The extra endpoint interval and the O(1/log x) term are lower order. Since θ₁∈[3/8,1/2), converting to XW(z₁)W(z₂) preserves an **absolute** K₇. It does not depend on S₀ or δ. The final η choice is not circular. The even bounded Ω conclusion and the exchanged-shift variant follow with only absolute changes of constants.
- The final symmetric-vector threshold `cosh²(1/2)` is correctly scoped. Positivity requires `arccosh√s₁+arccosh√s₂>1`; concavity on [1,2] makes the symmetric allocation optimal within that range. This is not a universal sieve barrier.

### Dickson and covering obstructions (§§5–6)

- **Proposition 5.1 itself is correct.** All forms have positive integral slopes and common value 1 at s=0, proving admissibility of their product. Large prime quotients are 1 modulo c. Any divisor of `(c+1)F_c` or `(c+1)G_c` is congruent to a divisor e of c+1, and no such e is −1 modulo c for c≥4. Issue 1 concerns the subsequent paragraph, not this proof.
- **Theorem W is correct with the usual positive-integer factor exponents.** Nonconstant factors permit choosing a member for which all prime quotients exceed a fixed ℓ. Selecting `c=4k≤X` with c≡−p modulo ℓ forces ℓ into a fixed constant, hence fixes the residue of **every** member. The CRT product separates distinct positive members by its size; all but the least exceed it. PNT yields coefficient 1/4. Neither the proof nor the surrounding caveat bounds the actual HC of the members. Stating explicitly `e_{x,i}∈N` would remove a harmless implicit convention.
- **Theorem N, Steps 1–4:** pinned valuations force s|c+1; reciprocity forces L>1; multiplying `Ls≡−1` by m=(c+1)/s gives L≡−m modulo c. The ℓ-adic precision bounds exclude excessive j and give `c+R≡0 (mod L)` or `Rc+1≡0 (mod L)`. CRT is primitive because R is positive and smaller than every ℓ∈Λ, so Dirichlet supplies infinitely many primes.
- **Steps 5–6:** writing h=i+1 on U, the introduced g is simply s. The equations become `c(sk−h)=h+sR` and `c(sk−n)=s+n`, respectively. Positivity makes the left parenthesis an integer ≥1; `s≤sk` then gives `L≤R+1+2R/h≤3R+1` on U and `L≤2R+(R+1)/n≤3R+1` on T. Both contradict L≥ℓ₀. No squarefreeness or non-residue assumption on R is hidden here.
- **Corollary 6.1 and Remark 6.2:** the least-non-residue argument using `ceil(ℓ/n)n−ℓ` proves `n(n−1)<ℓ`; the four small cases and the stated range finish it. Taking Y to include the finite support produces the non-square class modulo 840ℓ. The examples for 3,5,7 are legitimate certificate classes. The many-non-residue specialization and the least-prime-non-residue-11 consequence are correctly restricted to finite certificate families, not all possible HC bounds.
