\title[A sub-exponential Erd\H{o}s--Straus witness bound]{Large Erd\H{o}s--Straus witnesses II:\\
the multiplier modulus is infinitely often $\exp\big((\log p)^{1/4-o(1)}\big)$}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{Draft v5, October 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
For a prime $p$ let $W(p)$ be the least modulus $M\equiv3\pmod4$ of a
``multiplier'' congruence that yields a solution of $4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$.  We
prove $W(p)\ge\exp\big(c(\log p)^{1/4}(\log\log p)^{-1/4}\big)$ for infinitely
many primes $p$ in Mordell's hard classes, assuming Gallagher's prime number
theorem for the family of all characters and the Nair--Tenenbaum bound for
short sums of multiplicative functions of polynomials; more precisely, the
least hard prime with $W(p)>T$ is at most $\exp(O((\log T)^4\log\log T))$.
The proof quarantines $n$ to a \emph{random square class} modulo a growing
modulus (the residues of the multiplier congruences are Jacobi
non-residues, so no congruence is ever forced), controls the cost by a
$\beta$-weighted local lemma and a supermartingale bookkeeping, and transfers
the resulting Haar density to primes through the one-sided $\ell^2$ sandwich
of Bazzi and Razborov, an energy bound for avoidance indicators of cylinder
events, and a linear transfer theorem based on Gallagher's theorem.
We then describe the surrounding picture.  The profinite density
$\delta^*(T)$ of the integers with $W(n)>T$ satisfies
$(\log T)^3\ll\log(1/\delta^*(T))\ll(\log T)^3(\log\log T)^5$ (upper bound modulo
Nair--Tenenbaum; lower bound via a Janson-type inequality for one-hot
product spaces with a $1/\log\log T$ loss, and without loss using the atoms
of a companion note on the exceptional set).  Typical primes have
$\#\{p\le x:\ W(p)>T\}\ll\pi(x)e^{-c(\log T)^3}$ for $\log T\le c_1(\log x)^{1/4}$.  The
exponent $1/4$ is the limit of the minorant architecture: on every fibre
$n\equiv r\pmod Q$ with $\log Q\le T^{0.05}$, no minorant of $\mathbf 1[W(n)>T]$ of level
$\exp(c(\log T)^4/\log\log T)$ (or $\exp(c(\log T)^4)$, using the companion note) has
positive Haar mean.  This follows from a planting argument, the dual of a
lower-bound sieve of dimension $\asymp(\log T)^3$, and the planted law is
exponentially pseudorandom against classes and characters of every
modulus.  Hence linear certificates with full-orbit uniform accounting
cannot pass $1/4$ whatever the prime input (Bombieri--Vinogradov,
Elliott--Halberstam, GRH below $x$, Siegel-model main terms, each through its
consequences for prime counts at the scale $x$).  Conversely, a
Linnik-type hypothesis for sifted sets gives
$W(p)\ge\exp((\log p)^{1/3-o(1)})$ infinitely often, the exponent suggested by the
Haar density, while a Hardy--Littlewood-type hypothesis for product sets
certifies only $W(p)\ge(\log p)^{2+o(1)}$.  All results are proved modulo the cited
theorems listed in the text; the companion note is internally proved but
not externally refereed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture asserts that $4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ is solvable
in positive integers for every $n\ge2$.  It suffices to treat primes.  It has
been verified for $n\le10^{18}$ (\cite{MD}; \cite{Salez} for $10^{17}$), and
Mordell's identities settle every prime outside the six classes
$p\equiv1^2,11^2,13^2,17^2,19^2,23^2\pmod{840}$ \cite{Mordell} (cf.\
\cite{Oblath}).  We call the primes in these classes \emph{hard}.  For the
exceptional set see Vaughan \cite{Vaughan} and \cite{PW}; for the average
number of solutions see \cite{ET}.

For $M\equiv3\pmod4$ put $A_M=(M+1)/4$.  If $uvw=A_M$ and
$n\equiv-uv^{-1}\pmod M$, then $s=(nv+u)/M$ is a positive integer and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident}
 \frac4n=\frac1{suw}+\frac1{nsvw}+\frac1{nuvw}
\end{equation}
(direct verification; this is the classical multiplier family, cf.\
\cite{Vaughan,ET}).  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
 W(n)=\min\{M\equiv3\ (4):\ n\equiv-uv^{-1}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ M)\ \text{for some}\ uvw=A_M\},
\end{equation}
with $W(n)=+\infty$ if no such $M$ exists.  Thus $W(p)\le T$ provides an
explicit representation with modulus at most $T$.  Every prime
$p\equiv1\pmod{\lcm\{M\le T\}}$ has $W(p)>T$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:one}), so
Linnik's theorem gives $W(p)\gg\log p$ infinitely often.  In
\cite{Paper1} we proved that for every fixed $k$,
$W(p)\ge(\log p)^{k}e^{-C_k\log\log p/\log\log\log p}$ for infinitely many
hard primes.  Here we prove a sub-exponential rate.

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:G} and~\ref{thm:NT}]\label{thm:main}
There is an absolute constant $c>0$ such that for infinitely many hard primes $p$
\[
 W(p)\ \ge\ \exp\Big(c\,\frac{(\log p)^{1/4}}{(\log\log p)^{1/4}}\Big).
\]
More precisely, for every sufficiently large $T$ there is a hard prime $p$
with $p>T$, $W(p)>T$ and $\log p\le C(\log T)^{4}\log\log T$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:G} is Gallagher's prime number theorem for the family of
all primitive characters of conductor at most $Q_G$ \cite{Gallagher}, quoted
from \cite[Thm.~28.19]{MV3} together with the standard facts on exceptional
zeros (Theorem~\ref{thm:xz}).  No hypothesis on Siegel zeros is needed.
Theorem~\ref{thm:NT} is the Nair--Tenenbaum bound \cite{NT} for sums of
multiplicative functions of several polynomials, quoted in the form of
Henriot \cite{Henriot}; it is used once, to bound Haar-weighted divisor sums
over the moduli $M=4n-1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:masses}).  The Elsholtz--Tao
estimates used in the third version are not needed.  The constants in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} are effective, except that we have not checked whether
the implied constants in \cite{NT} are; the results proved modulo \cite{TQ}
below have ineffective constants.  We make no numerical claim.

\emph{Typical size.}  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is about the extreme primes.  For
the typical prime, $W(p)$ is small: large values have a cubic-exponential
tail.

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo \cite{TQ} and Page's theorem]\label{thm:typicalintro}
There are absolute $c,c_1>0$ such that, uniformly for $x\ge3$ and
$\log T\le c_1(\log x)^{1/4}$,
\[
 \#\{p\le x:\ W(p)>T\}\ \ll\ \pi(x)\,e^{-c(\log T)^3}.
\]
\end{theorem}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:typical}.  It uses the atom family of the companion
note \cite{TQ} (internally proved, not externally refereed; inputs
Bombieri--Vinogradov, Brun--Titchmarsh and Shiu) and, for small $T$, the prime
number theorem in progressions with a Landau--Page term.  At
$\log T\asymp(\log x)^{1/4}$ it is the prime case of the exceptional-set bound
$E(N)\ll Ne^{-c(\log N)^{3/4}}$ of \cite{TQ}.  Together with
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} it describes both ends of the range: for
$\log T\le c_1(\log x)^{1/4}$ the primes $p\le x$ with $W(p)>T$ have relative frequency
at most $e^{-c(\log T)^3}$, and once $\log x\ge C(\log T)^4\log\log T$ such primes
exist.

\emph{The Haar exponent.}  Let $\delta^*(T)$ be the Haar measure of
$\{n\in\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times:\ W(n)>T\}$ relative to the class $n\equiv1\pmod{24}$
(\S\ref{sec:moment}).

\begin{theorem}[Haar exponent $3$; proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:NT} and \cite{TQ}, see below]\label{thm:haar}
For large $T$,
\[
 c\,(\log T)^3\ \le\ \log\frac1{\delta^*(T)}\ \le\ C(\log T)^3(\log\log T)^5 .
\]
The upper bound is proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:NT}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:haarupper}).  The lower bound is proved modulo \cite{TQ}
(Proposition~\ref{prop:haarnoloss}); with $(\log T)^3/\log\log T$ in place of
$(\log T)^3$ it is proved modulo the fundamental lemma of sieve theory alone
(Theorem~\ref{thm:haarlower}).
\end{theorem}

\emph{The ceiling.}  The exponent of $\log p$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
governed not by the Haar density but by the level of the minorant used to
transfer it to primes, and this level cannot be lowered.

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:xz}, \ref{thm:G} and the fundamental lemma; sharpened modulo \cite{TQ}]\label{thm:ceilingintro}
There are absolute $c,T_0>0$ such that for $T\ge T_0$, every modulus $Q$ with
$\log Q\le T^{0.05}$ and every unit $r$ modulo $Q$: if $B$ is a linear
combination of functions $n\mapsto\phi(n\bmod qQ)$ with
$\log q\le c(\log T)^4/\log\log T$, and $B(n)\le\mathbf 1[W(n)>T]$ for all units
$n\equiv r\pmod Q$, then the Haar mean of $B$ on this class is $\le0$.  Modulo
\cite{TQ}, the same holds with $\log q\le c(\log T)^4$.
\end{theorem}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling} and Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling4}.
Consequently (Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling}) every argument that certifies a
hard prime with $W(p)>T$ through a minorant of positive Haar mean and a
transfer theorem costing $\log x\gg\log(\text{level})$, as ours does, has
$\log x\gg(\log T)^4/\log\log T$ (and $\gg(\log T)^4$ modulo \cite{TQ}): such
arguments cannot give $W(p)\ge\exp((\log p)^{1/4+\delta})$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is optimal among them up to a factor
$(\log\log p)^{1/2}$ in $\log W(p)$, or $(\log\log p)^{1/4}$ modulo \cite{TQ}.  It
also shows that the energy bound used for the sandwich cannot be sharpened
for this purpose (Corollary~\ref{cor:notail}).  The level $(\log T)^4$ is the
product of the log-size $\log T$ of the moduli and the dimension $(\log T)^3$ of
the sieve formed by the events with one large prime factor.  The same
product, read at the budget $\log N$ of an integer sieve instead of the
budget $\log x$ of a prime transfer, gives the exceptional-set exponent $3/4$
of \cite{TQ} (Remark~\ref{rem:unified}).

\emph{Beyond the ceiling.}  The planted law behind
Theorem~\ref{thm:ceilingintro} is exponentially pseudorandom against
residue classes, Dirichlet characters and additive characters of
\emph{every} modulus (Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo}).  So every minorant $B$ of any
level has $\E B\le e^{-c(\log T)^4/\log\log T}\|B\|_\times$, where $\|B\|_\times$ is the $\ell^1$
norm of its coefficients in classes and characters
(Theorem~\ref{thm:wiener}).  As a consequence, linear certificates with
full-orbit uniform error accounting cannot beat the exponent $1/4$, whatever
true prime input they use, including Bombieri--Vinogradov,
Elliott--Halberstam, GRH restricted to moduli $\le x$, and Siegel-model main
terms, each used through its consequences for the unweighted prime counts
at the scale $x$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:orbit}, Proposition~\ref{prop:EH}).  If primes
behaved like a set of Haar density $\delta^*(T)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:haar} would
suggest $\log W(p)\approx(\log p)^{1/3}$ for the extremal primes.  We isolate a
hypothesis that gives this.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional on Hypothesis LS; proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:NT}]\label{thm:LSintro}
Assume that every system of unit-class congruences $n\not\equiv a_E\pmod{m_E}$ with
$m_E\le T$ whose solution set has Haar density $\delta>0$ contains a prime $p>T$
with $\log p\le C(\log T+\log(1/\delta))$ (Hypothesis LS, ``Linnik's theorem for
sifted sets'').  Then $W(p)\ge\exp\big(c_C(\log p)^{1/3}(\log\log p)^{-5/3}\big)$ for
infinitely many hard primes $p$.
\end{theorem}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:LS}.  For a single residue class LS is Linnik's
theorem.  It is not reachable by the linear certificates above
(\S\ref{sec:wiener}), and the analogous hypothesis restricted to CRT product
sets (a Hardy--Littlewood-type singular series) gives
$W(p)\ge(\log p)^{2-o(1)}$ infinitely often and, applied to product subsets of
the avoiding set, cannot certify more than $W(p)\ge(\log p)^{2+o(1)}$ (modulo the
Barban--Davenport--Halberstam theorem)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:product}).  Altogether, with
$\Lc=\log T$: the extremal primes have $\Lc\ge(\log p)^{1/4-o(1)}$ (proved), the
minorant method stops at $(\log p)^{1/4+o(1)}$ (proved, within its scope), the
Haar heuristic and LS give $(\log p)^{1/3-o(1)}$, and typical primes have
$W(p)>T$ with relative frequency at most $e^{-c\Lc^3}$.

\emph{Versions.}  The first version of this note had exponent $1/14$; it used
the Thorner--Zaman theorem \cite{TZ} through the transfer theorem of
\cite{Paper1}.  The second had exponent $1/7$: it replaced Thorner--Zaman by
the linear transfer theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} here), and bounded
the approximation error by Håstad's switching lemma.  The third had exponent
$1/5$: it replaced the switching lemma by the energy bound
(\S\ref{sec:energy}) and used a graded class-of-one quarantine, whose cost
was bounded with four results of Elsholtz and Tao.  The fourth version
reached $1/4$ by a new quarantine: a random square class, a $\beta$-weighted
local lemma and a supermartingale bookkeeping (\S\ref{sec:system}) with Haar
masses bounded by the Nair--Tenenbaum theorem (\S\ref{sec:moment}); it added
the Haar lower bound (\S\ref{sec:haarlower}) and the ceiling
(\S\ref{sec:ceiling}).  The present fifth version leaves the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} unchanged and adds: the typical size, the Haar lower
bound without logarithmic loss and the ceiling at level $(\log T)^4$, all via
the atoms of \cite{TQ} (\S\ref{sec:atoms34}); the Wiener-norm barrier and its
consequences for linear certificates (\S\ref{sec:wiener}); and the
conditional exponent $1/3$ (\S\ref{sec:LS}).

\subsection*{The method}
Fix $T$.  We restrict $n$ to a class $n\equiv r\pmod Q$, with
$Q=8\prod_\ell\ell^{a_\ell}$ and $r$ a quadratic residue modulo every prime of $Q$.
Then $W(n)>T$ whenever $n$ avoids a finite family of congruence \emph{events}
$n\equiv-4D\pmod{m}$, one for each surviving pair $(M,D)$ with $M\le T$,
$D\mid A_M^2$, where $m$ is the part of $M$ not absorbed by $Q$.  The key
observation (Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi}) is that the Jacobi symbol
$(\frac{-4D}M)$ is always $-1$; so if every prime of $M$ divides $Q$, the
event is excluded, and no event is ever forced, whatever square class $r$
we choose.  For $r=1$ this is the class-of-one quarantine of \cite{Paper1};
Mordell's hard classes are the case $Q=840$.

We build $(Q,r)$ by a random process (Lemma~\ref{lem:book}).  While the
weighted mass $\tilde w_\ell=\sum_{E\ni\ell}\beta^{|\supp E|}\PP(E)$ at a prime $\ell\le Y=\Lc^{O(1)}$
exceeds $\eta=\frac34\log\beta$, we reveal one more digit of $n$ at $\ell$,
uniformly among the squares.  The $\beta$-weighted local lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:betalll}) needs exactly $\tilde w_\ell\le\eta$ at every
coordinate.  With $\beta=1+1/\log\Lc$ the threshold is $\asymp1/\log\Lc$ at every
prime and every level, instead of $\asymp\log\ell/\Lc$ in the previous version.
The masses form supermartingales (a step at a new prime at most doubles the
mean of an event's probability, and this is paid by a factor $2^{\omega_Y(M)}$),
so by optional stopping the expected cost is a Haar-weighted divisor sum,
$\ll\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5$ by the Nair--Tenenbaum theorem.  Primes $\ell>Y$ are never
quarantined; a second-moment potential for pairs of events shows that their
masses are small with high probability.  The class of one, used before,
inflates the masses by $\gcd(M,4D+1)$, which costs a factor $\Lc$ and requires
the Elsholtz--Tao estimates.

To find a \emph{prime} in the avoiding set we need a function
$B=\sum_ic_i\mathbf 1[n\equiv b_i\ (d_i)]$ with $B\le\mathbf 1[\text{avoid}]$
pointwise, mean $\gg\delta$ (the Haar density of the avoiding set on the
fibre), Haar $\ell^1$ norm $\E|B|$ comparable to its mean, and small moduli
$d_i$.  The linear transfer theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}) then gives a
prime $p\equiv r\pmod Q$ with $\log p\ll\log Z$, where $Z$ is $Q$ times the
largest $d_i$.  It is Linnik's theorem with weights: the function
$B(n)\mathbf 1[n\equiv r\ (Q)]$ is expanded in characters, every coefficient is
bounded by the Haar $\ell^1$ norm of $B$, whatever the number of cells, and
Gallagher's theorem bounds the contribution of all characters of conductor
at most a power of $Z$ at once.  Since $r$ is a square, every real character
that factors through $(\mathbb Z/Q)^\times$ is $1$ at $r$, so the exceptional-zero
analysis is the same as for the class of one.

As in the previous versions, $B$ is the one-sided $\ell^2$ sandwich of Bazzi
\cite{Bazzi}, in the form of Razborov \cite{Razborov} (Lemma~\ref{lem:brw}).
For events $E_1,\dots,E_m$ with indicators $A_i$ and arbitrary functions $u_j$,
\[
 B=1-\sum_iA_i\Big(1-\sum_{j<i}A_ju_j\Big)^2\ \le\ \mathbf 1[\text{no }E_i\text{ occurs}],
\]
and, if each $u_j$ depends only on the coordinates not fixed by $E_j$,
$\E[F-B]\le m^2\sum_j\PP(E_j)\,\E(F^{(j)}-u_j)^2$, where $F^{(j)}$ is the
indicator that no earlier event occurs, conditioned on $E_j$.  The $u_j$ are
truncations of the Efron--Stein decomposition of $F^{(j)}$, and the error is
an Efron--Stein tail, which we bound by the energy bound of the previous
version (Theorem~\ref{thm:C1}): if $F$ is the avoidance indicator of any
family of cylinder events on a finite product probability space, and
$\lambda_v\ge1$ are weights with $\prod_{v\in\supp E}\lambda_v\le2$ for every event
$E$, then
\[
 \sum_{U}\Big(\prod_{v\in U}\lambda_v\Big)\,\|F^{=U}\|_2^2\ \le\ 1 .
\]
Applied to the base-$\ell$ digits of the residues, with weights adapted to
the moduli (Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration}), it shows that truncating $F^{(j)}$
to moduli at most $e^{\tau}$ costs $2^{-\tau/(2\log T)}$.  So moduli of size
$\log d_i\ll\Lc\cdot(S+\Lc)$ suffice, where $S\ll\Lc^3\log\Lc$ is the residual mass.
Altogether $\log Z\ll\Lc^4\log\Lc$.  The ceiling (Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling})
shows that the level $\Lc S$ is forced up to a factor $(\log\Lc)^2$: conditioned on the small primes,
the events with one large prime factor form a sieve of dimension
$\asymp\Lc^3/\log\Lc$ on the primes above $T^{0.6}$, and a planting lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:planting}), the dual of a lower-bound sieve, shows that no
function of lower level can be a minorant with positive mean.

\subsection*{Relation to the literature}
\emph{The energy bound.}  The nearest prior art known to us is Lecomte and
Tan \cite{LT}, who bound the Fourier coefficients of a DNF on $\{\pm1\}^n$ by
the probability that a set $S$ is covered by the terms satisfied at a random
point; this is the device of our Lemma~\ref{lem:cover}.  Their cover counts
are unsigned, and their degree concentration still comes from Håstad's
switching lemma, with an unspecified constant.  In Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} the
cover counts are signed, so that they vanish when a satisfied event misses
the set; the polarization identity (Lemma~\ref{lem:polar}) and the
deletion--contraction bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:theta}) then give the constant
$1$, on any finite product probability space.  In Boolean language
(Remark~\ref{rem:boolean}): every width-$k$ DNF $g:\{\pm1\}^n\to\{\pm1\}$
has $\sum_{|S|>t}\hat g(S)^2\le4\cdot2^{-(t+1)/k}$, under any product measure,
while the switching-lemma route gives $2\cdot2^{-t/(20k)}$ \cite[\S4.4]{OD}.
The base $2^{-1/k}$ is sharp over general product spaces
(Remark~\ref{rem:sharp}).  Theorem~\ref{thm:C1} and its corollaries are new to
us, but our literature search was partial (we checked \cite{LT}, \cite{OD} and
some lecture notes; we did not search the literature on switching-lemma
variants, Fourier growth or hypercontractivity with $\rho>1$
systematically), and we make no priority claim.

\emph{The local lemmas.}  The $\beta$-weighted local lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:betalll}) is the asymmetric local lemma of
\cite[Lemma~5.1.1]{AS} with the choice $x_E=\beta^{|\supp E|}\PP(E)$; the
conditional form for events outside the family is standard (cf.\
\cite{HSS}).  Theorem~\ref{thm:janson} combines the lopsided local lemma of
Erd\H{o}s and Spencer \cite{ErdosSpencer} with the proof scheme of Janson's
inequality \cite{Janson}, which needs increasing events and Harris'
inequality; for atomic events on one-hot coordinates we use instead the
correlation inequality of Lemma~\ref{lem:compat}.  We did not find this form
in the literature, but did not search systematically.  The planting
lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:planting}) is the linear-programming dual of
lower-bound sieves; constructions of $k$-wise independent laws with
extremal all-zero or all-one probabilities of the same kind appear in
\cite{BGP,PYY}, and we claim no novelty for it.  Its use to bound the level
of minorants of a concrete arithmetic indicator, on every fibre of modulus
$\log Q\le T^{0.05}$, is new to us.

\emph{The rest.}  The sandwich (Lemma~\ref{lem:brw}) is Razborov's identity.
To our knowledge tools from the theory of bounded independence
(``polylogarithmic independence fools DNF and $\mathrm{AC}^0$''
\cite{Bazzi,Razborov,Braverman}) have not been used before to construct
sieve minorants for primes.  Gallagher's theorem is classically the engine of
Linnik's theorem on the least prime in a single progression.  Here it serves
as a transfer device for an arbitrary signed combination of progressions; the
cost is governed by the Haar $\ell^1$ norm of the minorant and its largest
modulus, not by the number of cells.  The nearest classical analogues are
bounds for the least prime in a union of residue classes, or in a Chebotarev
class of an abelian extension (Lagarias--Montgomery--Odlyzko \cite{LMO};
Thorner--Zaman \cite{TZcheb}); these concern nonnegative indicators.  We
have not searched systematically for an earlier use of Gallagher's theorem
in this role.  Earlier uses of the switching lemma in number theory known to
us run in the opposite direction: they show that the M\"obius function is
uncorrelated with bounded-depth circuits \cite{Green,Bourgain}.  The Haar
side, a local-lemma lower bound for the density of the uncovered set of a
congruence system, has relatives in the work on covering systems:
Filaseta, Ford, Konyagin, Pomerance and Yu \cite{FFKPY}, who to our knowledge
already use the local lemma for this purpose, Hough \cite{Hough} and
Balister, Bollob\'as, Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba \cite{BBMST}.  We do not
use those results.  The Jacobi-symbol property of the multiplier classes
(Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi}) is implicit in the classical fact that the
identities cover all non-residue classes; its use to quarantine to random
square classes is ours.  The divisor-sum bounds use Nair--Tenenbaum
\cite{NT,Henriot} in a standard way.  Hypothesis LS (\S\ref{sec:LS}) is a
uniform version of Linnik's theorem for sifted sets; we did not search the
literature for it systematically and claim no priority for the formulation.

\section{Atoms, square classes and the local lemma}\label{sec:system}

For $M\equiv3\pmod4$ put $\cR(M)=\{-4D\bmod M:\ D\mid A_M^2\}$.  Note that
$\gcd(A_M,M)=1$ (as $4A_M=M+1$), so every element of $\cR(M)$ is a unit
class.

\begin{lemma}[Proved; cf.\ {\cite{Paper1}}]\label{lem:atoms}
$\{-uv^{-1}\bmod M:\ uvw=A_M\}=\cR(M)$.  Consequently, for every integer $n$,
$W(n)>T$ if and only if $n\bmod M\notin\cR(M)$ for every $M\le T$ with
$M\equiv3\pmod4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $4A_M\equiv1\pmod M$, from $uvw=A_M$ we get $v^{-1}\equiv4uw$, hence
$-uv^{-1}\equiv-4u^2w$ and $D=u^2w$ divides $A_M^2$.  Conversely let
$D\mid A_M^2$.  For each prime $q$ with $q^a\Vert A_M$ and $q^d\Vert D$
($d\le2a$) put $u_q=\max(0,d-a)$, $w_q=d-2u_q$, $v_q=a-u_q-w_q$; these are
non-negative, so $u=\prod q^{u_q}$, $v=\prod q^{v_q}$, $w=\prod q^{w_q}$
satisfy $uvw=A_M$ and $u^2w=D$.
\end{proof}

An \emph{atom} is a pair $(M,D)$ with $M\le T$, $M\equiv3\pmod4$ and
$D\mid A_M^2$.  There are at most $T^2$ atoms, since $\tau(A_M^2)\le2A_M\le T$.

\begin{lemma}[Event classes are Jacobi non-residues; proved]\label{lem:jacobi}
Let $(M,D)$ be an atom and let $d$ be the squarefree part of $D$.  Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] every prime $\ell\mid M$ satisfies $\ell\nmid 2D$ and
$\big(\frac{-4D}{\ell}\big)=\big(\frac{-d}{\ell}\big)$;
\item[(b)] the Jacobi symbol $\big(\frac{-4D}{M}\big)$ equals $-1$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, if $r$ is an integer coprime to $M$ that is a quadratic residue
modulo every prime dividing $M$, then $r\not\equiv-4D\pmod M$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) $\gcd(M,4A_M)=1$ and every prime of $D$ divides $A_M$, so $\ell\nmid2D$;
and $-4D=-d\cdot(2t)^2$ for an integer $t$.  (b) By (a),
$\big(\frac{-4D}M\big)=\big(\frac{-d}M\big)=\big(\frac{-1}M\big)\big(\frac dM\big)=-\big(\frac dM\big)$,
as $M\equiv3\pmod4$.  Every prime $p\mid d$ has $v_p(D)$ odd and
$v_p(D)\le2v_p(A_M)$, so $p\mid A_M$ and $M=4A_M-1\equiv-1\pmod p$.  For odd
$p$, quadratic reciprocity, with $(M-1)/2$ odd, gives
$\big(\frac pM\big)=\big(\frac Mp\big)(-1)^{(p-1)/2}=\big(\frac{-1}p\big)(-1)^{(p-1)/2}=1$.
If $p=2$, then $2\mid A_M$, so $M\equiv7\pmod8$ and $\big(\frac2M\big)=1$.  Hence
$\big(\frac dM\big)=1$.  For the consequence,
$\big(\frac rM\big)=\prod_{\ell\mid M}\big(\frac r\ell\big)^{v_\ell(M)}=1\ne\big(\frac{-4D}M\big)$.
\end{proof}

We checked (a) and (b) by computer on all $2\,401\,032$ atoms with
$M\le2\cdot10^5$ (no failures; this check is not used).  The lemma is the
mechanism behind two classical facts: the identities \eqref{eq:ident} cover
the non-residue classes, and Mordell's hard classes modulo $840$ are exactly
the six square classes.  For $r=1$ it contains Lemma~\ref{lem:one} below.

\begin{lemma}[Class of one; proved]\label{lem:one}
$1\notin\cR(M)$ for every $M\equiv3\pmod4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi} with $r=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Square-class quarantine and residual system]\label{def:system}
For an odd prime $\ell\le T$ let $f_\ell=\lfloor\Lc/\log\ell\rfloor\ge1$ be the
largest exponent with $\ell^{f_\ell}\le T$.  A \emph{square-class quarantine}
is a pair $(Q,r)$, where $Q=8\prod_\ell\ell^{a_\ell}$ with integers
$0\le a_\ell\le f_\ell$ for the odd primes $\ell\le T$, and $r$ is a unit modulo
$Q$ with $r\equiv1\pmod8$ that is a quadratic residue modulo every odd prime
dividing $Q$.  The \emph{coordinate primes} are the odd $\ell\le T$ with
$a_\ell<f_\ell$; write $\cP$ for their set.  For $\ell\in\cP$ let
\[
 \Omega_\ell=\{x\in(\mathbb Z/\ell^{f_\ell})^\times:\ x\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell^{a_\ell})\}
\]
(all units if $a_\ell=0$), with the uniform measure, and let $\PP$, $\E$ be
the product measure on $\Omega=\prod_{\ell\in\cP}\Omega_\ell$, with coordinates
$X_\ell$.  An atom \emph{survives} $(Q,r)$ if $-4D\equiv r\pmod{\gcd(M,Q)}$ and
$M\nmid Q$.  Its \emph{support} is $\supp(M,D)=\{\ell:\ v_\ell(M)>a_\ell\}$, a
nonempty subset of $\cP$, with $s(M,D)=|\supp(M,D)|\le\omega(M)$; its
\emph{modulus} is $\prod_{\ell\in\supp}\ell^{v_\ell(M)}$, and its \emph{event} is
\[
 E_{M,D}=\{X_\ell\equiv-4D\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell^{v_\ell(M)})\ \text{for all}\ \ell\in\supp(M,D)\}.
\]
For $\beta\ge1$ the \emph{weighted fibre mass} at $\ell\in\cP$ and the
weighted total mass are
\[
 \tilde w_\ell=\sum_{\substack{(M,D)\ \text{surviving}\\ \ell\in\supp(M,D)}}\beta^{s(M,D)}\PP(E_{M,D}),\qquad
 S_\beta=\sum_{\substack{(M,D)\\ \text{surviving}}}\beta^{s(M,D)}\PP(E_{M,D}),
\]
where surviving atoms are counted individually (two atoms may give the same
event).  For an integer $n$ we put $X_\ell(n)=n\bmod\ell^{f_\ell}$, and $F(n)=1$
if $n$ lies in no event of a surviving atom, $F(n)=0$ otherwise; on
$\Omega$, $F$ is the indicator that no event $E_{M,D}$ occurs.
\end{definition}

Every event is nonempty: $-4D$ is a unit modulo $\ell$, and
$-4D\equiv r\pmod{\ell^{a_\ell}}$ by survival.  The modulus is at most $M\le T$.
The class-of-one quarantines of \cite{Paper1} and of the previous versions
are the case $r=1$.

\begin{lemma}[Reduction and weights; proved]\label{lem:system}
Let $(Q,r)$ be a square-class quarantine.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $n\equiv r\pmod Q$ and $F(n)=1$, then $W(n)>T$.
\item[(ii)] For every surviving atom,
\[
 \PP(E_{M,D})=\frac{\gcd(M,Q)}M\prod_{\ell\in\supp(M,D),\ a_\ell=0}\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}.
\]
\item[(iii)] If $105\mid Q$, then $r\bmod 840$ is one of Mordell's six
hard classes, so every prime $p\equiv r\pmod Q$ is hard.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $M\le T$, $M\equiv3\pmod4$, and suppose $n\equiv-4D\pmod M$ with
$D\mid A_M^2$.  Reducing modulo $\gcd(M,Q)$ gives $-4D\equiv r$.  If $M\mid Q$,
then $r\equiv-4D\pmod M$, and every prime of $M$ divides $Q$, so $r$ is a
quadratic residue modulo it; this contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi}.
Otherwise the atom survives, and $n$ lies in its event, so $F(n)=0$.
Conclude with Lemma~\ref{lem:atoms}.
(ii) Let $\ell\in\supp$ and $v=v_\ell(M)$.  If $a_\ell=0$, the class of $-4D$
modulo $\ell^v$ has probability $1/\varphi(\ell^v)=\ell^{-v}\cdot\ell/(\ell-1)$ in
$\Omega_\ell$.  If $a_\ell\ge1$, then $|\Omega_\ell|=\ell^{f_\ell-a_\ell}$, and the class
(which lies in $\Omega_\ell$ by survival) has $\ell^{f_\ell-v}$ elements, so
probability $\ell^{a_\ell-v}$.  As $M$ is odd,
$\gcd(M,Q)=\prod_{\ell\mid M}\ell^{\min(v_\ell(M),a_\ell)}$, and
$\min(v_\ell(M),a_\ell)=v_\ell(M)$ for $\ell\notin\supp$.
(iii) A unit modulo $840$ that is $\equiv1\pmod8$ and a square modulo $3$, $5$
and $7$ is a square modulo $840$, and there are $1\cdot1\cdot2\cdot3=6$ such
classes; they are the six listed classes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The graded class-of-one quarantine]\label{rem:graded}
In the previous version $r=1$, and the exponents $a_\ell$ were raised while
the fibre mass exceeded $c(a_\ell+1)\log\ell/\Lc$.  With $r=1$ a surviving atom
has $\gcd(M,Q)\mid g=\gcd(M,4D+1)$, so by (ii) its weight is up to $g/M$
instead of the Haar weight $1/\varphi(M)$.  Summed over the atoms, the Haar
weights give $O(\Lc^3\log\Lc)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:masses}), while for the
inflated weights we could only prove $O(\Lc^4)$, and that needed the
Elsholtz--Tao divisor-sum estimates \cite{ET}.  The
threshold $\propto\log\ell/\Lc$ cost a further factor $\Lc$ in the
quarantine.  A \emph{random} square class removes the inflation in
expectation (Lemma~\ref{lem:book}), Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi} guarantees that
no event is ever forced, and the $\beta$-weighted local lemma
(Lemma~\ref{lem:betalll}) replaces the log-weighted threshold by a constant
one.  The graded class-of-one quarantine is therefore superseded, and we
omit it.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The local lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Local lemma with conditioning; classical]\label{lem:lll}
Let $\mathcal A$ be a finite family of events in a product space, each
depending on a finite set of coordinates; write $A'\sim A$ if they share a
coordinate.  Suppose $x_A\in[0,1)$ satisfy
$\PP(A)\le x_A\prod_{A'\sim A,\,A'\ne A}(1-x_{A'})$.  Then
$\PP(\bigcap_{\mathcal A}\bar A)\ge\prod_{\mathcal A}(1-x_A)$, and
$\PP(A\mid\bigcap_{A'\in\mathcal S}\bar A')\le x_A$ for every
$\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal A$ with $A\notin\mathcal S$.  Moreover, for every
event $B$ depending on a set $J$ of coordinates and every
$\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal A$,
$\PP(B\mid\bigcap_{\mathcal S}\bar A)\le\PP(B)\prod_{A\in\Gamma(B)}(1-x_A)^{-1}$, where
$\Gamma(B)$ is the set of $A\in\mathcal S$ depending on some coordinate in $J$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first two statements are \cite[Lemma~5.1.1]{AS} and its proof.  For the
third (cf.\ \cite[Thm.~2.1]{HSS}), $B$ is independent of
$\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal S\setminus\Gamma(B)}\bar A$, so
\[
 \PP\Big(B\ \Big|\ \bigcap_{\mathcal S}\bar A\Big)
 \le\frac{\PP(B)\,\PP(\bigcap_{\mathcal S\setminus\Gamma(B)}\bar A)}{\PP(\bigcap_{\mathcal S}\bar A)}
 =\frac{\PP(B)}{\PP(\bigcap_{\Gamma(B)}\bar A\mid\bigcap_{\mathcal S\setminus\Gamma(B)}\bar A)},
\]
and the chain rule with the second statement bounds the denominator below by
$\prod_{\Gamma(B)}(1-x_A)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$\beta$-weighted local lemma; proved]\label{lem:betalll}
Let $\mathcal A$ be a finite family of events on a product probability
space, each determined by the coordinates in a nonempty set $\supp A$, and
put $s(A)=|\supp A|$.  Let $1<\beta\le e^{1/3}$, $\eta=\frac34\log\beta$, and
$\tilde w_v=\sum_{A\ni v}\beta^{s(A)}\PP(A)$, the sum over the $A$ with
$v\in\supp A$.  Suppose that $\tilde w_v\le\eta$ for every coordinate $v$.
Then
\[
 \PP\Big(\bigcap_{\mathcal A}\bar A\Big)\ \ge\ \exp\Big(-\frac43\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\beta^{s(A)}\PP(A)\Big),
\]
and for every event $B$ determined by a coordinate set $J$ and every
$\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal A$,
$\PP(B\mid\bigcap_{\mathcal S}\bar A)\le\beta^{|J|}\PP(B)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $x_A=\beta^{s(A)}\PP(A)$.  Every $A$ has a coordinate $v\in\supp A$, so
$x_A\le\tilde w_v\le\eta\le1/4$.  For $0\le x\le1/4$,
$-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)\le\frac43x$.  For any coordinate set $J$, each $A'$
sharing a coordinate with $J$ is counted in some $\tilde w_v$, $v\in J$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:betaprod}
 \prod_{A'\text{ meets }J}(1-x_{A'})\ \ge\ \exp\Big(-\frac43\sum_{v\in J}\tilde w_v\Big)\ \ge\ \exp\Big(-\frac43|J|\eta\Big)=\beta^{-|J|}.
\end{equation}
With $J=\supp A$ this gives $\PP(A)=x_A\beta^{-s(A)}\le x_A\prod_{A'\sim A}(1-x_{A'})$,
the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:lll}.  Its conclusions give
$\PP(\bigcap\bar A)\ge\prod(1-x_A)\ge\exp(-\frac43\sum x_A)$ and, with
\eqref{eq:betaprod}, the conditional bound.
\end{proof}

The criterion is per coordinate, with a threshold that depends neither on
the coordinate nor on the level of its quarantine; the price is the weight
$\beta^{s(A)}$.  We shall take $\beta=1+1/\log\Lc$, so that $\beta^{\omega(M)}$
is $O(1)$ on average, and $\eta\asymp1/\log\Lc$.

\subsection*{The square-class process}
Fix a parameter $Y\ge7$ and $\beta=1+1/\log\Lc$, $\eta=\frac34\log\beta$.  The
\emph{square-class process} is the following random sequence of square-class
quarantines $(Q_i,r_i)$.  Start from $Q=8$, $r=1$.  Then make three
\emph{forced} steps at $\ell=3,5,7$ with $a=0$, and afterwards, while some
odd prime $\ell\le Y$ with $\ell\in\cP$ has $\tilde w_\ell>\eta$ (computed for the
current quarantine), make a step at the least such $\ell$.  A \emph{step} at
$(\ell,a)$, where $a=a_\ell<f_\ell$ is the current exponent, replaces $a_\ell$ by
$a+1$ and $r$ by a class $r'\bmod Q\ell$ with $r'\equiv r\pmod Q$, chosen
uniformly among the classes of the current fibre at $\ell$ that are squares
modulo $\ell$: if $a=0$ this is uniform among the $(\ell-1)/2$ nonzero squares
modulo $\ell$, and if $a\ge1$ among the $\ell$ lifts of $r\bmod\ell^a$ to
$\ell^{a+1}$, all of which are squares.  Each step raises some $a_\ell<f_\ell$
with $\ell\le Y$, so the process stops after at most $\sum_{\ell\le Y}f_\ell$
steps.

Every $(Q_i,r_i)$ is a square-class quarantine.  After the forced steps,
$840\mid Q$ and $r\equiv1\pmod{24}$, since $1$ is the only nonzero square
modulo $3$.  Primes $\ell>Y$ are never stepped.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi}, no
atom ever has all its coordinates quarantined and matched: the final system
contains no event of probability one.

\emph{Notation.}  At stage $i$ let $\mu_i$ be the uniform probability on the
units $n$ with $n\equiv r_i\pmod{Q_i}$ (on $\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times$, or modulo
any multiple of $\lcm(Q_i,M)$), and for an atom $E=(M,D)$ put
$p_i(E)=\mu_i(n\equiv-4D\ (\mathrm{mod}\ M))$.  So $p_i(E)=\PP(E_{M,D})$ if the
atom survives $(Q_i,r_i)$, and $p_i(E)=0$ otherwise (if $M\mid Q_i$ the class
is excluded by Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi}).  Let $P_H(E)=1/\varphi(M)$ be the Haar
probability, $s_i(E)$ the support size at stage $i$, and
$u_i(E)=\#\{\ell\le Y:\ \ell\mid M,\ a_\ell(i)=0\}$.  Write $\omega_Y(M)$ for the
number of primes $\ell\le Y$ dividing $M$, and $M_Y$ for the $Y$-smooth part of
$M$.

\begin{lemma}[Bookkeeping for the square-class process; proved]\label{lem:book}
Consider the square-class process with parameter $Y$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For every fixed $\phi(E)\ge0$, $G_i=\sum_Ep_i(E)2^{u_i(E)}\phi(E)$ is
a supermartingale, and $G_0=\sum_EP_H(E)2^{\omega_Y(M)}\phi(E)$.
\item[(b)] With $Q_{\rm end}$ the final modulus,
\[
 \E[\log Q_{\rm end}]\ \le\ \log840+\frac1\eta\sum_EP_H(E)\,2^{\omega_Y(M)}\beta^{\omega(M)}\log M_Y .
\]
\item[(c)] $\E\big[\sum_E\beta^{s_{\rm end}(E)}p_{\rm end}(E)\big]\le S_H^\beta:=\sum_EP_H(E)2^{\omega_Y(M)}\beta^{\omega(M)}$.
\item[(d)] For every prime $\ell>Y$, $\E[\tilde w_\ell(\mathrm{end})^2]\le B_2(\ell)$, where
\begin{multline*}
 B_2(\ell)=\sum_{\substack{E,E'\\ \ell\mid M,\ \ell\mid M'}}\beta^{\omega(M)+\omega(M')}2^{\omega_Y(M)+\omega_Y(M')}P_H(E)P_H(E')\\
 \times\varphi\Big(\frac{\gcd(M,M')}{\ell^{\min(v_\ell(M),v_\ell(M'))}}\Big).
\end{multline*}
Hence $\PP(\exists\,\ell>Y:\ \tilde w_\ell(\mathrm{end})>\eta)\le\eta^{-2}\sum_{\ell>Y}B_2(\ell)$.
\end{enumerate}
All sums are over atoms $E=(M,D)$, $E'=(M',D')$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Consider a step at $(\ell,a)$.  Atoms with $\ell\nmid M$, or with
$v_\ell(M)\le a$, are unaffected: their class modulo $\ell^{v_\ell(M)}$ is
already determined by the fibre, and the step only refines it at higher
levels.  Let $v_\ell(M)>a$.  If $a\ge1$, the new class is uniform among the
$\ell$ classes of the fibre at level $a+1$, so $\E[p_{i+1}(E)\mid\text{past}]=p_i(E)$,
and $u$ is unchanged.  If $a=0$, the conditional probability of the event
becomes $\ell^{1-v}$ (with $v=v_\ell(M)$) if $r'\equiv-4D\pmod\ell$ and $0$
otherwise; the class $r'\bmod\ell$ is uniform on the $(\ell-1)/2$ squares, so
\begin{multline*}
 \E[p_{i+1}(E)\mid\text{past}]=p_i(E)\,\varphi(\ell^v)\,\ell^{1-v}\,\frac{2}{\ell-1}\,\mathbf 1\Big[\Big(\frac{-4D}\ell\Big)=1\Big]\\
 =\Big(1+\Big(\frac{-d}\ell\Big)\Big)p_i(E)\le2p_i(E)
\end{multline*}
by Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi}(a), while $u$ drops by one if $\ell\le Y$ (forced
steps and all other steps have $\ell\le Y$).  So each term
$p_i(E)2^{u_i(E)}\phi(E)$ is a supermartingale.  At the start $Q=8$, $r=1$
and $M$ is odd, so $p_0(E)=P_H(E)$; and $u_0(E)=\omega_Y(M)$.

(b) For a pair $(\ell,a)$ with $\ell\le Y$ let $\tau_{\ell,a}$ be the stage at
which the (unforced) step at $(\ell,a)$ is taken, $\infty$ if it is never taken.
At that stage $\tilde w_\ell>\eta$, so
\begin{multline*}
 \log\ell\cdot\mathbf 1[\tau_{\ell,a}<\infty]\le\frac{\log\ell}\eta\,G^{(\ell,a)}_{\tau_{\ell,a}}\mathbf 1[\tau_{\ell,a}<\infty],\\
 G^{(\ell,a)}_i=\sum_{E:\ v_\ell(M)\ge a+1}p_i(E)2^{u_i(E)}\beta^{\omega(M)},
\end{multline*}
since at stage $\tau_{\ell,a}$ the atoms with $\ell\in\supp E$ are those with
$v_\ell(M)\ge a+1$, and $\beta^{s}p\le\beta^{\omega}2^up$.  The stopping times
are bounded, and optional stopping for the nonnegative supermartingale
$G^{(\ell,a)}$ (part (a) with $\phi=\beta^{\omega(M)}\mathbf 1[v_\ell(M)\ge a+1]$)
gives $\E[G^{(\ell,a)}_{\tau}\mathbf 1[\tau<\infty]]\le G^{(\ell,a)}_0$.  Summing over
$\ell\le Y$ and $a<v_\ell(M)$, and using $\sum_{\ell\le Y}v_\ell(M)\log\ell=\log M_Y$,
gives the claim; the forced steps cost $\log105$, and $\log Q\le\log8+\log105+\sum_{\rm steps}\log\ell$.

(c) Part (a) with $\phi=\beta^{\omega(M)}$, and $s_{\rm end}\le\omega$.

(d) The prime $\ell$ is never stepped, so for $\ell\mid M$,
$p_i(E)=p_i(E^{(\ell)})/\varphi(\ell^{v_\ell(M)})$, where $E^{(\ell)}$ is the event
with its condition at $\ell$ removed, and
\[
 \tilde w_\ell^2\le\sum_{E,E'}\beta^{\omega(M)+\omega(M')}p(E)p(E'),
\]
the sum over
atoms with $\ell\mid M$, $\ell\mid M'$.  For two events $F,F'$ (conditions at sets
of primes, with given residues) put $\Pi_i=p_i(F)p_i(F')R_i$, where
$R_i=\prod_{\ell'}\rho_i(\ell')$ over the primes $\ell'$ constrained by both, and
$\rho_i(\ell')$ is the number of classes modulo $\ell'^{j}$ in the current
fibre, $j\le\min(v_{\ell'}(F),v_{\ell'}(F'))$ being the depth to which the two
prescribed residues agree ($\rho_i(\ell')=1$ once $a_{\ell'}\ge j$).  Then
$p_i(F)p_i(F')\le\Pi_i$, and $\Pi_i2^{u_i(F)+u_i(F')}$ is a supermartingale.
Indeed, consider a step revealing level $a+1$ at $\ell'$.  If neither event
constrains $\ell'$ at this level, nothing changes.  If only one does, its
factor behaves as in (a), and its $\rho$ is already $1$.  If both do and they
agree at this level, both probabilities are multiplied by the same factor
$N\cdot\mathbf 1[\text{match}]$, where $N$ is the number of classes of the
current fibre at this level ($N=\ell'-1$ if $a=0$, $N=\ell'$ if $a\ge1$), and
$\rho$ is divided by $N$; so $\Pi\mapsto N\mathbf 1[\text{match}]\Pi$, whose mean
is $\Pi$ if $a\ge1$ and $(1+(\frac{-d}{\ell'}))\Pi\le2\Pi$ if $a=0$, in which
case both $u$'s drop.  If both constrain $\ell'$ and disagree at this level,
they cannot both match, so $\Pi\mapsto0$.  At the start
$R_0\le\prod\varphi(\ell'^{j})\le\varphi(\gcd(M_F,M_{F'}))$, so
$\E[\Pi_{\rm end}]\le2^{\omega_Y(M_F)+\omega_Y(M_{F'})}P_H(F)P_H(F')\varphi(\gcd(M_F,M_{F'}))$.
Apply this to $F=E^{(\ell)}$, $F'=E'^{(\ell)}$, multiply by
$\varphi(\ell^{v_\ell(M)})^{-1}\varphi(\ell^{v_\ell(M')})^{-1}$ and sum.  The last claim
is Chebyshev's inequality and a union bound.
\end{proof}

Part (d) is the reason for the random class: the second moment of the mass
at a prime that is never quarantined is controlled by a Haar
pair-correlation sum, a deterministic divisor sum in which $\gcd(M,4D+1)$ does
not occur.

\section{Haar masses and the upper bound on the Haar side}\label{sec:moment}

The only analytic input of this section is the following theorem.  For
fixed $k\ge1$, $A,B\ge1$ and $\varepsilon>0$, let $\cM_k(A,B,\varepsilon)$ be the
class of non-negative functions $F$ of $k$ variables with
\[
 F(a_1b_1,\dots,a_kb_k)\le\min\big(A^{\Omega(a_1\cdots a_k)},\,B(a_1\cdots a_k)^\varepsilon\big)F(b_1,\dots,b_k)
\]
whenever $\gcd(a_1\cdots a_k,b_1\cdots b_k)=1$.

\begin{theorem}[Nair--Tenenbaum {\cite[Thm.~1]{NT}}; cited in the form of {\cite[(1.1)]{Henriot}}]\label{thm:NT}
Let $Q_1,\dots,Q_k\in\mathbb Z[X]$ be pairwise coprime irreducible polynomials,
$Q=Q_1\cdots Q_k$ of degree $g$, without fixed prime divisor.  Let
$\rho_{Q_j}(n)$ and $\rho(n)$ be the numbers of zeros of $Q_j$ and of $Q$
modulo $n$.  Let $0<\alpha,\delta<1$, $A,B\ge1$, $0<\varepsilon\le\alpha\delta/(12g^2)$ and
$F\in\cM_k(A,B,\varepsilon)$.  Then, uniformly in $x\ge c_0\|Q\|^\delta$ and
$x^\alpha<y\le x$,
\begin{multline*}
 \sum_{x<n\le x+y}F\big(|Q_1(n)|,\dots,|Q_k(n)|\big)\ \ll\ y\prod_{p\le x}\Big(1-\frac{\rho(p)}p\Big)\\
 \times\sum_{n_1\cdots n_k\le x}F(n_1,\dots,n_k)\frac{\rho_{Q_1}(n_1)\cdots\rho_{Q_k}(n_k)}{n_1\cdots n_k},
\end{multline*}
where $c_0$ and the implied constant depend only on $k,g,A,B,\varepsilon,\alpha,\delta$
and the discriminant of $Q$.
\end{theorem}

We use it with $k=2$, $Q_1(n)=n$, $Q_2(n)=4n-1$: these are coprime and
irreducible, $Q=4n^2-n$ has discriminant $1$ and no fixed prime divisor
($Q(1)=3$, $Q(2)=14$), $\rho(2)=1$, $\rho(p)=2$ for odd $p$, $\rho_{Q_1}\equiv1$
and $\rho_{Q_2}(m)=\mathbf 1[m\text{ odd}]$.  We take $y=x$, $\alpha=\delta=1/2$ and
$\varepsilon=1/200$.  (Henriot notes that NT's own class $\cM_k$ requires the
inequality whenever $\gcd(a_i,b_i)=1$ for each $i$, so the class above is
slightly larger and Theorem~\ref{thm:NT} is Henriot's reading of NT's proof.
Our functions $F$, products of multiplicative functions of separate
variables, lie in NT's original class, and NT's own range
($\varepsilon<1/(8g^2)$, $x^{4g^2\varepsilon}\le y\le x$) covers $\varepsilon=1/200$, $g=2$, $y=x$; so
for our use the citation rests on \cite[Thm.~1]{NT} alone.)

Throughout, $\beta=1+1/\log\Lc$, $2\le Y\le T$, $H(M)=\beta^{\omega(M)}2^{\omega_Y(M)}$, and
\[
 w(M)=\tau(A_M^2)\,H(M)\,\frac M{\varphi(M)}\quad(M\equiv3\ (4)),\qquad w(M)=0\ \text{otherwise}.
\]
Summing over $D\mid A_M^2$ gives $\sum_DH(M)P_H(E_{M,D})=w(M)/M$.  Sums over $M$
run over $M\le T$.

\begin{lemma}[Haar masses; proved, (A) modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:NT}]\label{lem:masses}
Let $2\le Y\le T$ and $\beta=1+1/\log\Lc$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)] We have
\begin{gather*}
 S_H^\beta=\sum_M\frac{w(M)}M\ll\Lc^3\log Y,\\ \sum_EP_H(E)2^{\omega_Y(M)}\beta^{\omega(M)}\log M_Y\ll\Lc^3(\log Y)^4 .
\end{gather*}
\item[(B)] $\sum_{\ell>Y\ \text{prime}}B_2(\ell)\le\frac{\Lc+1}Y\,\Xi$, where
$\Xi=\sum_Mw(M)^2\tau(M)\omega(M)/M\ll\Lc^{C_0}$ for an absolute constant $C_0$,
uniformly in $2\le Y\le T$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(A) Write $n=A_M$, so $M=4n-1$, and $f_2(m)=H(m)m/\varphi(m)$, a
multiplicative function with $f_2(p^k)=\beta\,2^{[p\le Y]}p/(p-1)\le6$ for odd $p$
(as $\beta\le2$).  The function $F(n_1,n_2)=\tau(n_1^2)f_2(n_2)$ satisfies
$F(a_1b_1,a_2b_2)=\tau(a_1^2)f_2(a_2)F(b_1,b_2)$ when
$\gcd(a_1a_2,b_1b_2)=1$, and $\tau(a_1^2)f_2(a_2)$ is at most
$8^{\Omega(a_1a_2)}$ and at most $B(a_1a_2)^{1/200}$ for an absolute $B$ (divisor bound).  So
$F\in\cM_2(8,B,1/200)$, uniformly in $T$ and $Y$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:NT} on
$x<n\le2x$ gives
\begin{multline*}
 \sum_{x<n\le2x}\tau(n^2)f_2(4n-1)\ll\frac x{(\log x)^2}\sum_{n_1\le x}\frac{\tau(n_1^2)}{n_1}\sum_{n_2\le x}\frac{f_2(n_2)}{n_2}\\
 \ll\frac x{(\log x)^2}(\log x)^3(\log x)^\beta(\log Y)^\beta ,
\end{multline*}
by the Euler products $\prod_{p\le x}(1+3/p+\dots)$ and
\[
 \prod_{p\le Y}\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{p-1}+O(p^{-2})\Big)\prod_{Y<p\le x}\Big(1+\frac\beta{p-1}+O(p^{-2})\Big).
\]
Since $(\log x)^{\beta-1},(\log Y)^{\beta-1}\le\Lc^{1/\log\Lc}=e$, this is
$\ll x(\log x)^2\log Y$.  As $\tau(A_M^2)H(M)/\varphi(M)=\tau(n^2)f_2(M)/M$ with
$M\asymp x$, the block contributes $\ll\Lc^2\log Y$; the $O(\Lc)$ dyadic blocks
(and the bounded range $x<c_0$) give the first bound.  For the second, use
$\log M_Y\le\log Y\cdot\Omega(M_Y)\le\log Y\cdot\tau(M_Y)$ (as
$\sum k_i\le\prod(k_i+1)-1$), and repeat the argument with $f_2(m)\tau(m_Y)$ in
place of $f_2$.  Its values at $p^k$ are at most $6(k+1)\le12^k$ and
$\ll_\varepsilon p^{k\varepsilon}$, so the class condition holds uniformly; its Euler
factor at $p\le Y$ is $1+4\beta/(p-1)+O(p^{-2})$, so
$\sum_{n_2\le x}f_2(n_2)\tau((n_2)_Y)/n_2\ll(\log x)(\log Y)^3$, and the bound
$\Lc^3(\log Y)^4$ follows.

(B) For an atom pair with $\ell\mid M$, $\ell\mid M'$ write $M=\ell m$,
$M'=\ell m'$.  Then $\gcd(M,M')/\ell^{\min(v_\ell(M),v_\ell(M'))}$ divides $\gcd(m,m')$, so
its $\varphi$ is at most $\gcd(m,m')=\sum_{e\mid(m,m')}\varphi(e)$.  Summing over
$D,D'$,
\[
 B_2(\ell)\le\sum_e\varphi(e)V(e\ell)^2,\qquad V(q)=\sum_{q\mid M}\frac{w(M)}M=\sum_{k\le T/q}\frac{w(qk)}{qk}.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz in $k$, $V(q)^2\le(\Lc+1)\sum_kw(qk)^2/(q^2k)$.  Put $N=e\ell k$:
the term $\varphi(e)w(N)^2/(e^2\ell^2k)$ equals $(w(N)^2/N)\varphi(e)/(e\ell)\le(w(N)^2/N)/\ell$,
and for fixed $\ell$ there are at most $\tau(N)$ factorisations $N=e\ell k$.  So
\[
 \sum_{\ell>Y}B_2(\ell)\le(\Lc+1)\sum_N\frac{w(N)^2\tau(N)}N\sum_{\ell\mid N,\ \ell>Y}\frac1\ell\le\frac{\Lc+1}Y\,\Xi .
\]
For $\Xi$, use $\omega\le\tau$, $2^{\omega_Y}\le2^\omega$ and Cauchy--Schwarz with
$M=4n-1\ge3n$:
\begin{multline*}
 \Xi\le\Big(\sum_{n\le T}\frac{\tau(n^2)^4}n\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{m\le4T}\frac{g(m)^2}m\Big)^{1/2},\\
 g(m)=\beta^{2\omega(m)}4^{\omega(m)}\Big(\frac m{\varphi(m)}\Big)^2\tau(m)^2 .
\end{multline*}
Both are mean values of non-negative multiplicative functions $f$ with
$f(p^k)\le C(k+1)^C$ for an absolute $C$; bounding them by
$\prod_{p\le4T}(1+f(p)/p+f(p^2)/p^2+\dots)$ gives $\ll\Lc^{C_0}$, with
$C_0$ absolute (since $\beta\le2$) and independent of $Y$.
\end{proof}

The proof of (A) is the only place where Theorem~\ref{thm:NT} is used.  All
masses here are Haar masses, so the Elsholtz--Tao estimates are not needed.

Let $\delta^*(T)$ be the Haar measure of $\{n\in\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times:\ W(n)>T\}$
relative to the class $n\equiv1\pmod{24}$, that is, the proportion, among the
units modulo $L=\lcm(24,\,M\le T)$ that are $\equiv1\pmod{24}$, of those with
$W(n)>T$ (the condition $W(n)>T$ depends only on $n\bmod L$).  On all of
$\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times$ the proportion is exactly half of this, because the atom
$(3,1)$ excludes $n\equiv2\pmod3$.

\begin{theorem}[Haar side, upper bound; proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:NT}]\label{thm:haarupper}
For large $T$ there is a square-class quarantine $(Q,r)$ with $840\mid Q$,
$r\equiv1\pmod{24}$, all prime factors of $Q$ at most $Y:=\Lc^{C_0+4}$ (with
$C_0$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:masses}), and, with $\beta=1+1/\log\Lc$ and
$\eta=\frac34\log\beta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:goodQ}
 \tilde w_\ell\le\eta\ \ (\ell\in\cP),\qquad S_\beta\le C\Lc^3\log\Lc,\qquad \log Q\le C\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5,
\end{equation}
where $C$ is absolute.  Consequently $\delta=\E F\ge\exp(-\frac43S_\beta)$ and
\[
 \log\frac1{\delta^*(T)}\ \le\ \log\varphi(Q)+\frac43S_\beta\ \ll\ \Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Run the square-class process with this $Y$.  As $1/\eta\asymp\log\Lc$ and
$\log Y\asymp\log\Lc$, Lemma~\ref{lem:book}(b,c) and Lemma~\ref{lem:masses}(A)
give $\E[\log Q_{\rm end}]\ll\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5$ and
$\E[S_\beta(\mathrm{end})]\ll\Lc^3\log\Lc$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:book}(d) and
Lemma~\ref{lem:masses}(B),
$\PP(\exists\ell>Y:\ \tilde w_\ell>\eta)\le\eta^{-2}(\Lc+1)\Lc^{C_0}/Y\le1/4$ for
large $T$.  By Markov's inequality each of the events
$\log Q_{\rm end}>4\E[\log Q_{\rm end}]$ and $S_\beta>4\E[S_\beta]$ has probability at
most $1/4$.  So some realisation $(Q,r)$ avoids all three.  In it every
coordinate $\ell\le Y$ has $\tilde w_\ell\le\eta$ because the process stopped, and
every $\ell>Y$ by the choice of realisation; this is \eqref{eq:goodQ}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:betalll} gives $\delta\ge\exp(-\frac43S_\beta)$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:system}(i), the units $n\equiv r\pmod Q$ with $F(n)=1$ have
$W(n)>T$.  Among the units $\equiv1\pmod{24}$ the class $r\bmod Q$ has relative
Haar measure $\varphi(24)/\varphi(Q)$, as $r\equiv1\pmod{24}$, and within it the
residues $n\bmod\ell^{f_\ell}$, $\ell\in\cP$, are independent and uniform on
$\Omega_\ell$.  So $\delta^*(T)\ge(\varphi(24)/\varphi(Q))\,\delta$.
\end{proof}

Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:haarlower} below, this determines the
Haar exponent, $\log\log(1/\delta^*(T))=(3+o(1))\log\Lc$.  The quarantine costs
$\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5$; the class-of-one route of the previous version gave
$\log(1/\delta^*(T))\ll\Lc^5\log\Lc$ modulo the Elsholtz--Tao estimates.

\section{A Janson-type inequality and the lower bound on the Haar side}\label{sec:haarlower}

In this section $(X_v)$ are independent random variables with finite or
countable ranges, and an \emph{atomic event} is an event
$E=\{X_v=a_{E,v}\ \text{for}\ v\in S_E\}$ with $S_E$ finite and single
values $a_{E,v}$.  For a family $\mathcal F$ put
$\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal F)=\bigcap_{E\in\mathcal F}\bar E$.  Two atomic events
\emph{conflict} if they prescribe different values on some common
coordinate (then $E\cap E'=\emptyset$), and \emph{share a bit} if
$S_E\cap S_{E'}\ne\emptyset$ and they agree on all common coordinates (then
$\PP(E\cap E')>0$).  Harris' inequality is not available here: for one-hot
variables $\PP(X=a\mid X\ne a')>\PP(X=a)$.  The following lemmas replace it.

\begin{lemma}[Compatible events; proved]\label{lem:compat}
Let $A$ be atomic and let $\mathcal C$ be a family of atomic events none of
which conflicts with $A$.  Then $\PP(A\cap\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal C))\le\PP(A)\PP(\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal C))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition on $A$.  For $C\in\mathcal C$, $C\cap A=A\cap C'$, where $C'$ is $C$
with the coordinates in $S_A$ deleted; $C'$ is independent of
$(X_v)_{v\in S_A}$ and $C'\supseteq C$.  So
$\PP(\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal C)\mid A)=\PP(\bigcap\bar C')\le\PP(\bigcap\bar C)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lopsided local lemma; proved, standard]\label{lem:lopsided}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a family of atomic events, $\Gamma(E)$ the set of
$E'\in\mathcal F$ conflicting with $E$, and suppose $x_E\in(0,1)$ satisfy
$\PP(E)\le x_E\prod_{E'\in\Gamma(E)}(1-x_{E'})$.  Then for every atomic event $A$
and every $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal F$,
\[
 \PP(A\mid\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal S))\le\PP(A)\prod_{E'\in\mathcal S\text{ conflicting with }A}(1-x_{E'})^{-1}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:compat}, $\PP(E\mid\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal S'))\le\PP(E)$ whenever
no event of $\mathcal S'$ conflicts with $E$, so the lopsided local lemma of
Erd\H{o}s and Spencer \cite{ErdosSpencer} applies with the graph $\Gamma$; its
proof gives $\PP(E\mid\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal S))\le x_E$ for $E\notin\mathcal S$.  For
$A$, let $\mathcal S_1$ be the events of $\mathcal S$ conflicting with $A$ and
$\mathcal S_2=\mathcal S\setminus\mathcal S_1$.  Then
$\PP(A\mid\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal S))\le\PP(A\mid\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal S_2))/\PP(\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal S_1)\mid\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal S_2))$;
the numerator is at most $\PP(A)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:compat}, and the chain
rule bounds the denominator below by $\prod_{\mathcal S_1}(1-x_E)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Janson-type inequality for atomic events; proved]\label{thm:janson}
Under the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:lopsided} put
$K=\max_{E\in\mathcal F}\prod_{E'\in\Gamma(E)}(1-x_{E'})^{-1}$,
$\mu=\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}\PP(E)$, and
$\Delta=\sum\PP(E\cap E')$ over the unordered pairs of distinct events of
$\mathcal F$ that share a bit.  Then
\[
 -\log\PP(\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal F))\ \ge\ \mu-K\Delta,\qquad
 -\log\PP(\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal F))\ \ge\ \min\Big(\frac\mu2,\frac{\mu^2}{4K\Delta}\Big).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Order $\mathcal F=\{E_1,\dots,E_N\}$ and put $B_i=\mathrm{Av}(\{E_j:\ j<i\})$, so
$-\log\PP(\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal F))=-\sum_i\log(1-\PP(E_i\mid B_i))\ge\sum_i\PP(E_i\mid B_i)$.
Let $B_i^d=\mathrm{Av}(\{E_j:\ j<i,\ S_j\cap S_i=\emptyset\})\supseteq B_i$.  Then
$\PP(E_i\mid B_i)\ge\PP(E_i)\PP(B_i\mid E_i)/\PP(B_i^d)$.  Given $E_i$, the
events $E_j$ ($j<i$) conflicting with $E_i$ are impossible, those sharing a bit
become $E_j'$ ($E_j$ with the coordinates of $S_i$ deleted, so
$\PP(E_i)\PP(E_j')=\PP(E_i\cap E_j)$), and $B_i^d$ is independent of $E_i$.  Hence
\[
 \PP(B_i\mid E_i)\ge\PP(B_i^d)-\sum_{j<i,\ j\text{ shares a bit with }i}\PP(E_j'\cap B_i^d).
\]
$B_i^d$ is the avoidance event of a subfamily of $\mathcal F$, and every event
conflicting with $E_j'$ conflicts with $E_j$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:lopsided}
gives $\PP(E_j'\mid B_i^d)\le K\PP(E_j')$.  Therefore
$\PP(E_i\mid B_i)\ge\PP(E_i)-K\sum_{j<i,\,j\sim i}\PP(E_i\cap E_j)$, and summing over
$i$ gives the first bound.  For the second, a subfamily $\mathcal F'$
satisfies the hypothesis with the same $x_E$ and the same or smaller $K$,
and $\PP(\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal F))\le\PP(\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal F'))$.  For a random
$\mathcal F'$ with inclusion probability $p$, $\E\mu'=p\mu$ and
$\E\Delta'=p^2\Delta$, so some $\mathcal F'$ has $\mu'-K\Delta'\ge p\mu-p^2K\Delta$; take
$p=\min(1,\mu/(2K\Delta))$.
\end{proof}

$\Delta$ counts only compatible pairs; conflicting pairs, which carry the
structure ``one coordinate, many residues'', enter only through $K$.  If
$x_E=2\PP(E)$, $\PP(E)\le1/8$ and $\sum_{E'\in\Gamma(E)}\PP(E')\le1/8$ for all $E$,
the hypothesis holds and $K\le e^{1/3}$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:janson} was
checked by exact enumeration on small random instances (not used).  We did
not find it in the literature in this form; the Janson inequality itself
\cite{Janson} needs positively correlated (increasing) events, and the
lopsided local lemma alone gives upper bounds on $\PP(E\mid\cdot)$, not lower
bounds.  We make no priority claim.

\subsection*{The lower bound}
For the ES events the coordinates are $X_\ell=n\bmod\ell^{f}$ ($f$ large) for
odd primes $\ell$, independent and uniform on units (on $X_3\equiv1\pmod3$ in
the normalisation of $\delta^*(T)$), and
$E_{M,D}=\bigcap_{\ell\mid M}\{X_\ell\equiv-4D\ (\ell^{v_\ell(M)})\}$.  For squarefree $M$
with $3\nmid M$ these are atomic events in the variables $X_\ell\bmod\ell$, and
$\PP(E_{M,D})=1/\varphi(M)$.  Distinct pairs $(M,-4D\bmod M)$ are distinct
events.

\begin{theorem}[Haar side, lower bound; proved modulo the fundamental lemma]\label{thm:haarlower}
There are absolute constants $c>0$ and $T_0$ such that for $T\ge T_0$
\[
 \log\frac1{\delta^*(T)}\ \ge\ c\,\frac{\Lc^3}{\log\Lc} .
\]
\end{theorem}

The only external input is the fundamental lemma of sieve theory
\cite[Lemma~6.3, Cor.~6.10]{FI} (cited), used for a lower bound for rough numbers in
progressions to small moduli.  $T_0$ is astronomically large.

\emph{The family.}  Put $y=\Lc^5$, $N_0=\Lc^2$, and for an integer $D$ write
$D^*=\prod_pp^{\lceil v_p(D)/2\rceil}$.  Then $D\mid A^2\iff D^*\mid A$, and each
$n\ge1$ equals $D^*$ for exactly $2^{\omega(n)}$ integers $D$ (write $D=\kappa t^2$
with $\kappa$ squarefree; then $D^*=\kappa t$).  Let $\mathcal F$ be the set of
events $E_{M,D}$ with
\begin{multline*}
 \sqrt T\le M\le T,\quad M\ \text{squarefree with all prime factors}>y,\quad M\equiv3\ (4),\\
 n:=D^*\in[N_0,T^{1/10}],\quad n\mid A_M .
\end{multline*}
We use: (F1) $D\le n^2\le T^{1/5}<M/4$, so different $D$ give different
residues $-4D\bmod M$, and $\PP(E_{M,D})=1/\varphi(M)$; (F2) for $y$-rough squarefree
$m\le T$, $\omega(m)\le\Lc/\log y$ and $\varphi(m)\ge m/2$; (F3)
$\sum_{n\le x}2^{\omega(n)}/n\le(1+\log x)^2$ and $\sum_{n\le x}2^{\omega(n)}\le x(1+\log x)$;
(F4) for a progression $v\equiv c\pmod d$ in $[v_0,V]$ with least element
$v_1\ge v_0$, $\sum1/v\le1/v_1+\log(V/v_0)/d$; (F5) if $\omega(m)y^{-1}\le1$, then
$\sum_{g\mid m,\ g>1,\ g\ \text{squarefree and } y\text{-rough}}g^{-1}\le(1+1/y)^{\omega(m)}-1\le2\omega(m)/y$.

\begin{lemma}[Mass; proved modulo the fundamental lemma]\label{lem:hmass}
We have $\mu=\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}\PP(E)\ge c_1\Lc^3/\log y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By (F1), $\mu\ge\sum_{N_0\le n\le T^{1/10}}2^{\omega(n)}\sum_M1/M$, over $y$-rough squarefree
$M\in[\sqrt T,T]$ with $M\equiv-1\pmod{4n}$; this congruence implies $M\equiv3\pmod4$
and $n\mid A_M$.  For a dyadic block $(X,2X]\subseteq[\sqrt T,T]$, sift the
progression (of length $X/(4n)\ge X^{4/5}/4$) by the primes $p<y$, $p\nmid2n$,
at level $X^{1/2}$; as $s=\log X/(2\log y)\to\infty$, the fundamental lemma gives at
least $c_2X/(n\log y)$ $y$-rough elements.  Those divisible by $p^2$ with $p\ge y$
number at most $X/(4n(y-1))+\pi(\sqrt{2X})$, which is negligible as $n\le X^{1/5}$.  So each
of the $\gg\Lc$ blocks contributes $\ge c_2/(4n\log y)$, and
$\sum_{N_0\le n\le T^{1/10}}2^{\omega(n)}/n\gg\Lc^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local-lemma hypothesis; proved]\label{lem:hlll}
For $T\ge T_0$, every $E\in\mathcal F$ has $\PP(E)\le1/8$ and
$\sum_{E'\in\Gamma(E)}\PP(E')\le1/8$.  Hence Theorem~\ref{thm:janson} applies with
$x_E=2\PP(E)$ and $K\le e^{1/3}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\PP(E)\le2/\sqrt T$.  Put $w_q=\sum_{E'\in\mathcal F:\,q\mid M'}\PP(E')$.  Events
conflicting with $E$ share a prime $q\mid M$, so
$\sum_{\Gamma(E)}\PP\le(\Lc/\log y)\max_{q>y}w_q$.  Writing $M'=qv$ with
$qv\equiv-1\pmod{4n'}$ and $qv\ge\sqrt T$, (F2) and (F4) give
$w_q\le(2/q)\sum_{D'}(\Lc/(4n')+\min(1,q/\sqrt T))$.  By (F3) the first part is at
most $\Lc^3/(2q)$, and the second at most $4\Lc T^{-2/5}$.  So
$\sum_{\Gamma(E)}\PP\le(\Lc/\log y)(\Lc^3/(2y)+4\Lc T^{-2/5})=O(1/(\Lc\log\Lc))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pair sum; proved]\label{lem:hpair}
$\Delta=\sum_{\text{bit-sharing pairs}}\PP(E\cap E')\le C\Lc^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $E=E_{M,D}$, $E'=E_{M',D'}$ be distinct and share a bit; put
$g=\gcd(M,M')>1$, $M=gv$, $M'=gv'$.  Then $g$ is $y$-rough, so $g>y$, and
compatibility means $g\mid D-D'$.  Also $M\ne M'$: otherwise $M\mid D-D'$ and
$E=E'$ by (F1).  By (F2), $\PP(E\cap E')=1/(\varphi(g)\varphi(v)\varphi(v'))\le8/(gvv')$.
For fixed $g$ and $D$ the admissible $v$ satisfy $gv\equiv-1\pmod{4n}$ and
$gv\ge\sqrt T$, so by (F4)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmagn}
 \sigma(g,n):=\sum_v\frac1v\le\frac\Lc{4n}+\min\Big(1,\frac g{\sqrt T}\Big).
\end{equation}
(a) $D=D'$.  Then $v\ne v'$ and $v\equiv v'\equiv-g^{-1}\pmod{4n}$.  For fixed $(v,n)$,
$g$ runs over a progression modulo $4n$ with $g>y$, so $\sum_g1/g\le1/y+\Lc/(4n)$;
and $\sum_{v<v',\ v'\equiv v\ (4n)}1/(vv')\le(1+\Lc)^2/(4n)$.  So
$\Delta_a\le8\sum_{N_0\le n\le T^{1/10}}2^{\omega(n)}(1/y+\Lc/(4n))(1+\Lc)^2/(4n)\ll\Lc^4/y+\Lc^3\log N_0/N_0\ll\Lc\log\Lc$.

(b) $D\ne D'$.  Now $g\mid m=|D-D'|$ with $0<m<T^{1/5}$, so $g<T^{1/5}$ and
\eqref{eq:sigmagn} gives $\sigma(g,n)\le\Lc/(4n)+T^{-3/10}$.  Summing over $g$
with (F5),
\[
 \Delta_b\le\frac{2\Lc}y\Big[\frac{\Lc^2}2\Big(\sum_D\frac1n\Big)^2+4T^{-3/10}\Lc\,\#\cD\sum_D\frac1n+8T^{-3/5}(\#\cD)^2\Big],
\]
where $\cD$ is the set of $D$ with $D^*\le T^{1/10}$, so $\sum_D1/n\le(1+\Lc)^2$ and
$\#\cD\le T^{1/10}(1+\Lc)$ by (F3).  So $\Delta_b\ll\Lc^7/y+T^{-1/10}\Lc^5\ll\Lc^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:haarlower}]
The events of $\mathcal F$ involve only primes $>y>3$, so they are independent
of $n\bmod24$, and $\delta^*(T)\le\PP(\mathrm{Av}(\mathcal F))$.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:janson} and Lemmas~\ref{lem:hmass}--\ref{lem:hpair},
$\log(1/\delta^*(T))\ge\mu-e^{1/3}\Delta\ge c_1\Lc^3/(5\log\Lc)-C\Lc^2$.
\end{proof}

Two restrictions make the pair sum small: $M\ge\sqrt T\ge(D^*)^5$ makes the
first terms of the progressions in \eqref{eq:sigmagn} harmless, and
$D^*\ge\Lc^2$ removes the small-$D$ hubs ($D=1$ has residue $-4$ at every
prime), whose same-$D$ pairs would make $\Delta_a\asymp\mu$.  The factor
$\log\Lc$ is the price of $y$-roughness.  A Monte Carlo estimate of
$\delta^*(T)$ for $1023\le T\le32767$ fits
$\log(1/\delta^*(T))\approx0.07\,\Lc^3/\log\Lc$ (evidence only, not used; at these $T$
it cannot distinguish $\Lc^3/\log\Lc$ from, say, $\Lc^{2.5}$).

\section{The analytic input: Gallagher's theorem}\label{sec:analytic}

For a Dirichlet character $\chi$ put $\vartheta(x;\chi)=\sum_{p\le x}\chi(p)\log p$, and
let $E_0(\chi)=1$ if $\chi$ is the trivial character modulo $1$ and
$E_0(\chi)=0$ otherwise.  $\sum^*_{\chi\bmod q}$ is a sum over primitive
characters.

\begin{theorem}[Landau--Page; classical, {\cite[Ch.~14]{Dav}}, {\cite[Cor.~11.10]{MV1}}]\label{thm:xz}
There are absolute effective positive constants $c_1,c_2$ such that, for
every $Y\ge3$, $\prod_{q\le Y}\prod^*_{\chi\bmod q}L(s,\chi)$ has at most one zero
in the region $\Re s>1-c_1/\log Y$, $|\Im s|\le Y$.  If this zero $\beta_1$
exists, it is real and simple, and its character $\chi_1$ is quadratic.
Moreover, every real zero $\beta$ of $L(s,\chi)$, for $\chi$ a real primitive
character of conductor $q\ge3$, satisfies
$1-\beta\ge c_2^{-1}q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{-2}$.
\end{theorem}

The first part is the classical zero-free region with the Landau--Page
theorem.  The height restriction matters: without it the statement would be
a zero-free region of width $\asymp1/\log Y$ uniformly in $|\Im s|$, which is
not known.  The ``Exceptional Zero Statement'' (28.61)--(28.62) of
\cite{MV3}, which refers to \cite[Cor.~11.10]{MV1}, omits the height
restriction.  The second part is the effective Page-type bound
\cite[Ch.~14]{Dav}, \cite[(28.62)]{MV3}.  We use Theorem~\ref{thm:xz} only
for \emph{real} zeros: for the uniqueness of a real zero close to $1$, for the
quadraticity of its character, and for the lower bound for $1-\beta$.

\begin{theorem}[Gallagher {\cite{Gallagher}}; cited in the form of {\cite[Thm.~28.19]{MV3}}]\label{thm:G}
There are absolute constants $c\ge1$ and $\kappa_0\ge3$ with the following
property.  Let $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$ be fixed and $1<Q_G^{6c}\le x$.  Then
\[
 \sum_{q\le Q_G}\ \sideset{}{^*}\sum_{\chi\bmod q}\big|\vartheta(x;\chi)-E_0(\chi)x\big|
 \ \ll_\kappa\ x\exp\Big(-\frac{\log x}{\kappa\log Q_G}\Big)+\Big(\frac{\log x}{\log Q_G}\Big)^2\frac x{Q_G},
\]
unless $\prod_{q\le Q_G}\prod^*_{\chi\bmod q}L(s,\chi)$ has a real zero $\beta_1$ with
$1-\beta_1<1/(\kappa\log Q_G)$.  In that case the term of the exceptional
character $\chi_1$ on the left is replaced by
$|\vartheta(x;\chi_1)+x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1|$, and the right-hand side by
\[
 (1-\beta_1)(\log x)\Big(x\exp\Big(-\frac{\log x}{\log Q_G}\Big)+\frac{x\log x}{Q_G\log Q_G}\Big).
\]
\end{theorem}

\emph{Source caveat.}  We quote \cite{MV3}, an unpublished draft (third volume
of Montgomery--Vaughan), archived by us.  We have not seen Gallagher's paper
\cite{Gallagher}, so we cite it without a theorem number.  The printed proof
of \cite[Thm.~28.19]{MV3} has visible slips.  Its case split is at
$1-\beta_1<1/(3\kappa_0\log Q_G)$, so it establishes the statement for
$\kappa=3\kappa_0$.  The statement for every larger $\kappa$ follows from that
case.  Let $\kappa\ge3\kappa_0$, and suppose a real zero satisfies
$1/(\kappa\log Q_G)\le1-\beta_1<1/(3\kappa_0\log Q_G)$.  Then
$x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1\le2x\exp(-\log x/(\kappa\log Q_G))$, and
$(1-\beta_1)(\log x)\,x\exp(-\log x/\log Q_G)\ll x\exp(-\log x/(2\log Q_G))$.
So the exceptional form of the bound implies the non-exceptional one with
parameter $\kappa$.  We therefore fix
\[
 \kappa:=\max(3\kappa_0,\,1/c_1).
\]
An exceptional zero as in Theorem~\ref{thm:G} is then a real zero in the
region of Theorem~\ref{thm:xz} with $Y=Q_G$, so it is unique and $\chi_1$ is
real.  Write $C_G$ for the implied constant in Theorem~\ref{thm:G} with this
$\kappa$.

Gallagher's original statement may carry a range condition of the form
$\exp(\sqrt{\log x})\le Q_G\le x^{b}$; we have not checked this.  Our use is
robust to such a condition.  In the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer},
$\log x/\log Q_G=\kappa L\ge6c$, and $\log x\ge(\kappa L)^2$ by the
hypothesis of that theorem, so $Q_G\ge\exp(\sqrt{\log x})$.  In Lemma~\ref{lem:cuprimes}, $Q_G=4T^{\varepsilon}$ and
$x\le T^{0.7}$, so again $\exp(\sqrt{\log x})\le Q_G\le x$.  The statement
itself is the standard form of Gallagher's prime number theorem for the
whole family of characters of conductor $\le Q_G$, which underlies the usual
proofs of Linnik's theorem.

\section{A linear transfer theorem}\label{sec:transfer}

\begin{theorem}[Linear transfer; proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:xz} and~\ref{thm:G}]\label{thm:transfer}
Let $T\ge1$ and $Q\ge2$ with $8\mid Q$, and let $r$ be a unit modulo $Q$ with
$r\equiv1\pmod8$ that is a quadratic residue modulo every odd prime dividing
$Q$.  Let $B(n)=\sum_{i\in I}c_i\mathbf 1[n\equiv b_i\ (d_i)]$ be a
finite real combination of \emph{consistent unit cells}: $\gcd(b_i,d_i)=1$ and
$b_i\equiv r\pmod{\gcd(d_i,Q)}$.  Assume that
$B(n)\le\mathbf 1[W(n)>T]$ for every $n\equiv r\pmod Q$ coprime to all $d_i$.  Put
$D=\lcm_id_i$, $N_0=\lcm(Q,D)$, $H=\{n\in(\mathbb Z/N_0)^\times:\ n\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Q)\}$, and let $\E_H$
be the mean over $H$.  Put
\[
 Z=Q\max_id_i,\quad \mu=\E_H B,\quad
 A=\frac{\E_H|B|}{\mu},
\]
and assume $\mu>0$, $A\le Z^{1/4}$, and the \emph{twist condition}: for every
real primitive character $\psi$ of conductor $f>1$ with $\gcd(f,Q)=1$ and
$f\mid d_i$ for some $i$,
\[
 |\E_H[B\psi]|\le\mu/4.
\]
There is an absolute effective constant $C_2$ such that, if
\[
 \log x\ \ge\ C_2(1+\log A)\log Z,
\]
then some prime $p\le x$ with $p\equiv r\pmod Q$ and $p\nmid D$ has $W(p)>T$.
\end{theorem}

In the second version $r=1$ and the cells were coprime to $Q$; then $\E_H$ is the
mean over the units modulo $D$, and $\mu=\sum_ic_i/\varphi(d_i)$.  Partial
quarantines produce cells whose moduli share primes with $Q$; consistency
means that each cell meets the class $r\bmod Q$.  The hypotheses on $r$ are
used only in (c) below: every real character that factors through
$(\mathbb Z/Q)^\times$ takes the value $1$ at $r$, exactly as at $1$.

Neither the $\ell^1$ mass $\sum_i|c_i|/\varphi(d_i)$ nor the number of cells
enters; only the Haar quantity $A\ge1$ does.

\begin{proof}
\emph{Parameters.}  Put $L=2c+\log(400C_G(A+1))$ and $Q_G=x^{1/(\kappa L)}$.  Since
$A\ge1$ we have $L\le C_3(1+\log A)$ for an absolute $C_3$.  If $C_2$ is large
enough, the hypothesis gives the following, using $A\le Z^{1/4}$ and $Z\ge2$:
$\log Q_G\ge(C_2/(\kappa C_3))\log Z$, hence $Q_G\ge Z$ and
$Q_G\ge10^4C_G(A+1)(\kappa L)^2$; $Q_G^{6c}\le x$ (as $\kappa L\ge6c$);
$\log x\ge16$; $\log x\ge(\kappa L)^2$ (as
$(1+\log A)^2\le(1+\log A)(1+\tfrac14\log Z)$); and $x\ge C_4AZ^4$, with
$C_4$ as large as needed below.

\emph{Character expansion.}  The function $f(n)=B(n)\mathbf 1[n\equiv r\ (Q)]$
on units modulo $N_0$ satisfies $f(n)=\sum_{\chi\bmod N_0}c(\chi)\chi(n)$.  The
reduction map $(\mathbb Z/N_0)^\times\to(\mathbb Z/Q)^\times$ is onto, and $H$ is the fibre
over $r$, so $|H|=\varphi(N_0)/\varphi(Q)$ and
\[
 c(\chi)=\frac1{\varphi(N_0)}\sum_{n\in H}B(n)\bar\chi(n)=\frac{\E_H[B\bar\chi]}{\varphi(Q)} .
\]
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $c(\chi)=0$ unless the conductor of $\chi$ divides $\lcm(d_i,Q)$ for
some $i$.  Indeed, by consistency and the Chinese remainder theorem,
$\mathbf 1[n\equiv b_i\ (d_i)]\mathbf 1[n\equiv r\ (Q)]$ is the indicator of one unit
class modulo $\lcm(d_i,Q)$, and its $\chi$-coefficient vanishes unless $\chi$ is
trivial on $\{n\equiv1\ (\lcm(d_i,Q))\}$.  So $c(\chi)\ne0$ implies that the
conductor of $\chi$ is at most $Qd_i\le Z\le Q_G$.
\item[(b)] $|c(\chi)|\le\E_H|B|/\varphi(Q)=A\mu/\varphi(Q)$, and
$c(\chi_0)=\mu/\varphi(Q)$ for the principal character $\chi_0$.
\item[(c)] Let $\chi$ be real with $c(\chi)\ne0$, and let $\psi^*$ be the real
primitive character inducing it, of conductor $f$.  Write $f=f_1f_2$, where
$f_1$ is composed of primes dividing $Q$ and $\gcd(f_2,Q)=1$, and
$\psi^*=\psi_1\psi_2$ accordingly.  A real primitive conductor is $2^ef'$
with $e\in\{0,2,3\}$ and $f'$ odd and squarefree; as $8\mid Q$, $f_1\mid Q$, and
$\psi_1$ is a product of a character modulo $8$ and Legendre symbols modulo
odd primes of $Q$.  On $H$ we have $n\equiv r\pmod Q$, so
$\psi_1(n)=\psi_1(r)=1$, as $r\equiv1\pmod8$ and $r$ is a quadratic residue
modulo every odd prime of $Q$.  So $\chi=\psi_2$ on $H$.  If $f_2=1$, then $\chi=1$
on $H$ and $c(\chi)=\mu/\varphi(Q)$.
If $f_2>1$, then $c(\chi)=\E_H[B\psi_2]/\varphi(Q)$.  A cell with $f_2\nmid d_i$
has $\E_H[\mathbf 1[n\equiv b_i\ (d_i)]\psi_2]=0$: there is a prime $p\mid f_2$ with
$p\nmid d_iQ$; on $H$ the residue of $n$ modulo $p^{v_p(N_0)}$ is uniform among
units and independent of $n$ modulo $N_0/p^{v_p(N_0)}$, and the $p$-component of
$\psi_2$ is a nontrivial character modulo $p$.  So $f_2\mid d_i$ for some $i$,
$\gcd(f_2,Q)=1$, and the twist condition gives $|c(\chi)|\le\mu/(4\varphi(Q))$.
\end{enumerate}
Let $\chi^*$ be the primitive character inducing $\chi$.  For a fixed modulus
the map $\chi\mapsto\chi^*$ is injective, so by (a) at most
$\sum_{r\le Z}\varphi(r)\le Z^2$ characters have $c(\chi)\ne0$.

\emph{The prime sum.}  Let
$S(x)=\sum_{p\le x,\ p\nmid N_0}f(p)\log p=\sum_\chi c(\chi)\vartheta(x;\chi)$.  Since
$|\vartheta(x;\chi)-\vartheta(x;\chi^*)|\le\log N_0\le\log Q+\log D\le2Z$ (as
$D\mid\lcm(1,\dots,\max d_i)$, so $\log D\le\psi(\max d_i)<1.04\max d_i$ by
Rosser--Schoenfeld \cite{RS}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sx}
 S(x)=\frac{\mu x}{\varphi(Q)}+\sum_{\chi:\,c(\chi)\ne0}c(\chi)\big(\vartheta(x;\chi^*)-E_0(\chi^*)x\big)+R_1,
\end{equation}
where $|R_1|\le Z^2\cdot(A\mu/\varphi(Q))\cdot2Z=2AZ^3\mu/\varphi(Q)$.  Here
$E_0(\chi^*)=1$ only for $\chi=\chi_0$, and $|R_1|\le\mu x/(400\varphi(Q))$ as
$x\ge800AZ^3$.

\emph{Case 0: no exceptional zero} in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:G}.  The
$\chi^*$ in \eqref{eq:Sx} are distinct primitive characters of conductor
$\le Q_G$.  By (b) and Theorem~\ref{thm:G}, the middle sum is at most
\[
 \frac{A\mu}{\varphi(Q)}\,C_Gx\Big(e^{-L}+\frac{(\kappa L)^2}{Q_G}\Big)
 \le\frac{\mu x}{\varphi(Q)}\Big(\frac1{400}+\frac1{10^4}\Big)\le\frac{\mu x}{200\,\varphi(Q)},
\]
using $\log x/\log Q_G=\kappa L\ge L$.  So $S(x)>0$.

\emph{Exceptional case:} a zero $\beta_1$ with character $\chi_1$ and
$1-\beta_1<1/(\kappa\log Q_G)$.  Put $u=(1-\beta_1)\log x$; then
$u<\log x/(\kappa\log Q_G)=L$, and $\beta_1>1/2$.  The replaced right-hand side
of Theorem~\ref{thm:G} is $C_Gux(e^{-\kappa L}+\kappa L/Q_G)$.  Since
$u\le\min(u,1)\,L$ (recall $L\ge2$), its product with $A\mu/\varphi(Q)$ is at
most
\[
 \frac{\mu x}{\varphi(Q)}\min(u,1)\cdot C_GAL\Big(e^{-\kappa L}+\frac{\kappa L}{Q_G}\Big)
 \le\frac{\mu x}{200\,\varphi(Q)}\min(u,1).
\]
Here $C_GALe^{-3L}\le C_GAe^{-L}\le1/400$, and
$C_GA\kappa L^2/Q_G\le10^{-4}$.  If no $\chi$ with $c(\chi)\ne0$ has
$\chi^*=\chi_1$, this bounds the middle sum of \eqref{eq:Sx}, and $S(x)>0$
as in Case~0.  Otherwise, by injectivity, exactly one such $\chi$ exists.  It
is real, because $\chi_1$ is; hence $c(\chi)$ is real, as $B$ and $\chi$ are.
Let $f_2$ be as in (c).
Its term is
$c(\chi)\big(\vartheta(x;\chi_1)+x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1\big)-c(\chi)x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1$.
The first part is covered by the bound just proved.

\emph{Case A: $f_2=1$.}  Then $c(\chi)=\mu/\varphi(Q)$, and the
conductor $q_1$ of $\chi_1$ divides $Q$, so $q_1\le Z$.  The main terms combine
to $\lambda\mu x/\varphi(Q)$ with $\lambda=1-x^{\beta_1-1}/\beta_1$.  Since
$1/\beta_1\le1+2(1-\beta_1)$, $1-e^{-u}\ge(1-e^{-1})\min(u,1)$ and
$2(1-\beta_1)e^{-u}=2ue^{-u}/\log x\le2\min(u,1)/\log x$,
\[
 \lambda\ \ge\ 1-e^{-u}-2(1-\beta_1)e^{-u}\ \ge\ (0.63-2/16)\min(u,1)\ \ge\ \min(u,1)/2 .
\]
So the error from Theorem~\ref{thm:G} is at most $\lambda\mu x/(100\varphi(Q))$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:xz}, $u\ge16(1-\beta_1)\ge16/(c_2Z^{1/2}(\log Z)^2)$, so
$1/\lambda\le2+c_2Z^{1/2}(\log Z)^2/8\le C_5Z$.  Hence
$|R_1|\le2AZ^3\mu/\varphi(Q)\le\lambda\mu x/(100\varphi(Q))$ once
$x\ge200C_5AZ^4$.  Altogether $S(x)\ge(1-2/100)\lambda\mu x/\varphi(Q)>0$.

\emph{Case B: $f_2>1$.}  By (c),
$|c(\chi)x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1|\le(\mu/(4\varphi(Q)))\cdot2x=\mu x/(2\varphi(Q))$, and
$S(x)\ge(1-1/2-1/200-1/400)\mu x/\varphi(Q)>0$.

In all cases $S(x)>0$.  So some prime $p\le x$ with $p\nmid N_0$ and
$p\equiv r\pmod Q$ has $B(p)>0$, and then $W(p)>T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Assessment]\label{rem:linear}
This is Linnik's theorem with weights.  Every character coefficient is
bounded by $\E_H|B|/\varphi(Q)$, not by the $\ell^1$ mass of a cell
representation.  Theorem~\ref{thm:G} controls all characters of conductor
$\le Q_G$ simultaneously, so the number of moduli $d_i$ never multiplies the
error.  In \cite{Paper1} and in the first version of this note, each
progression was instead treated separately with the Thorner--Zaman theorem
\cite{TZ}.  That costs $\log x\gg K\max(\log Z,K)$ with
$K=1+\log(\sum|c_i|/\varphi(d_i)\,/\,\mu)$, which here is of order
$(\log T)^{14}$ instead of $(\log T)^7$ in the setting of the second
version.  No lower bound for $1-\beta_1$ is
needed in Case~B.  In Case~A we use the effective bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:xz} only to absorb the term $R_1$.
\end{remark}
\section{An energy bound for avoidance indicators}\label{sec:energy}

This section is self-contained and does not use arithmetic.

\emph{Setting.}  Let $\Omega=\prod_{v\in V_0}\Omega_v$ be a finite product of
finite sets with a product probability measure $\pi=\bigotimes_v\pi_v$; write
$\PP$, $\E$ and $\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{L^2(\pi)}$.  A \emph{cylinder event} is a set
$E=\{X_v=\sigma_v\ (v\in\supp E)\}$ with $\supp E\subseteq V_0$ and
$\sigma\in\prod_{\supp E}\Omega_v$.  For a finite family $\cE$ of cylinder events
(repetitions allowed) let $F=F_\cE=\prod_{E\in\cE}(1-\mathbf 1_E)$ be its
\emph{avoidance indicator}.

\emph{Efron--Stein decomposition} (classical; \cite{ES,Hoeffding},
\cite[\S8.3]{OD}).  For $\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ and $W\subseteq V_0$ let
$\E[\varphi\mid X_W]$ be the conditional expectation given the coordinates in
$W$, and
\[
 \varphi^{=U}=\sum_{W\subseteq U}(-1)^{|U\setminus W|}\,\E[\varphi\mid X_W].
\]
Then $\varphi=\sum_U\varphi^{=U}$, the components are mutually orthogonal,
$\varphi^{=U}$ depends only on $X_U$, and $\E_u\varphi^{=U}=0$ for $u\in U$, where
$\E_u$ averages over the coordinate $u$.  Put $L_u=I-\E_u$ and
$L_V=\prod_{u\in V}L_u$; then $L_V\varphi=\sum_{U\supseteq V}\varphi^{=U}$.  For a
down-closed family $\cD$ of subsets of $V_0$, the function
$\varphi^{\cD}=\sum_{U\in\cD}\varphi^{=U}$ is a linear combination of the
$\E[\varphi\mid X_W]$, $W\in\cD$ (by M\"obius inversion), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \E(\varphi-\varphi^{\cD})^2=\sum_{U\notin\cD}\|\varphi^{=U}\|^2 .
\end{equation}
For $\cD=\{U:\ |U|\le t\}$ this is the \emph{energy above level $t$},
$\energy(\varphi;t)$; it is the least $\E(\varphi-g)^2$ over sums $g$ of
functions of at most $t$ coordinates.

\begin{definition}[Energy generating function]
For weights $\lambda_v\ge1$ put $\mu_v=\lambda_v-1$ and
\[
 G_\varphi(\lambda)=\sum_{U\subseteq V_0}\lambda^U\,\|\varphi^{=U}\|^2
 =\sum_{V\subseteq V_0}\mu^V\,\|L_V\varphi\|^2 .
\]
\end{definition}

The second form follows from the identity $\lambda^U=\sum_{V\subseteq U}\mu^V$ and
$\|L_V\varphi\|^2=\sum_{U\supseteq V}\|\varphi^{=U}\|^2$.  For every $\tau$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:markov}
 \sum_{U:\ \lambda^U>e^\tau}\|\varphi^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ e^{-\tau}G_\varphi(\lambda).
\end{equation}
For a single event $E$ with $\pi_E=\PP(E)$ one computes
$G_{1-\mathbf 1_E}=1-2\pi_E+\pi_E\prod_{v\in\supp E}(\lambda_v-\mu_v\pi_v(\sigma_v))$, so for
small $\pi_v(\sigma_v)$ the condition $\lambda^{\supp E}\le2$ below is necessary for
$G\le1$.

\emph{Hypergraphs.}  A hypergraph $\Ho$ is a finite set of subsets
(\emph{edges}) of $V_0$.  For $V\subseteq V_0$ put
\[
 N_\Ho(V)=\sum_{\mathcal J\subseteq\Ho:\ \bigcup_{J\in\mathcal J}(J\cap V)=V}(-1)^{|\mathcal J|},\qquad
 Q_\mu(\Ho)=\sum_{V\subseteq V_0}\mu^V\,N_\Ho(V)^2 .
\]
$N_\Ho(V)$ depends only on the traces $J\cap V$, and it is unchanged if
edges are repeated or replaced by edges with the same trace: a class of
$m\ge1$ edges with equal traces contributes $\sum_{j\ge1}\binom mj(-1)^j=-1$,
exactly like one edge.  If some edge misses $V$, then $N_\Ho(V)=0$ (toggle
that edge).

\begin{lemma}[Cover bound; proved]\label{lem:cover}
In the Setting, for every $V\subseteq V_0$,
\[
 \|L_VF\|^2\ \le\ \E_x\big[N_{\Ho(x)}(V)^2\big],\qquad
 \Ho(x)=\{\supp E:\ E\in\cE,\ x\in E\}.
\]
Consequently $G_F(\lambda)\le\E_x\big[Q_\mu(\Ho(x))\big]$ for all weights $\lambda_v\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $x=(\sigma,y)$ with $\sigma\in\Omega_V$, $y\in\Omega_{V_0\setminus V}$.  The operator
$L_V$ acts on the $V$-coordinates only, so
$\|L_VF\|^2=\E_y\|L_Vg_y\|^2_{L^2(\Omega_V)}$, where $g_y=F(\cdot,y)$.  Now $g_y$
is the avoidance indicator on $\Omega_V$ of the restricted events: those
$E\in\cE$ whose conditions off $V$ hold at $y$, restricted to $\supp E\cap V$.
Expand $g_y=\sum_{\mathcal J}(-1)^{|\mathcal J|}\mathbf 1_{\mathcal J}$ over subfamilies
$\mathcal J$ of restricted events, where $\mathbf 1_{\mathcal J}$ is the indicator
of their intersection, a cylinder on $\Omega_V$ (or empty).  $L_V$ kills every
function that does not depend on all coordinates of $V$, hence every
cylinder whose support is a proper subset of $V$.  A cylinder with
support $V$ is a point indicator $\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma\}}$.  So $L_Vg_y=L_Vc_y$, where
\[
 c_y(\sigma)=\sum_{\mathcal J:\ \supp\mathbf 1_{\mathcal J}=V,\ \mathbf 1_{\mathcal J}=\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma\}}}(-1)^{|\mathcal J|}.
\]
As $L_V$ is an orthogonal projection in $L^2(\pi_V)$,
$\|L_Vc_y\|^2\le\E_\sigma c_y(\sigma)^2$.  The subfamilies $\mathcal J$ counted in
$c_y(\sigma)$ are exactly the subfamilies of events that hold at $x=(\sigma,y)$
whose traces on $V$ cover $V$.  Two events that hold at $x$ and have the
same support are the same cylinder, so by the remark above
$c_y(\sigma)=N_{\Ho(x)}(V)$.  Taking $\E_y$ gives the first claim.  Multiply by
$\mu^V$ and sum over $V$ for the second.
\end{proof}

The suppression is built in: $N_{\Ho(x)}(V)=0$ as soon as some event that
holds at $x$ misses $V$.

For a finite multiset $\cC$ of subsets of $V_0$ (possibly empty ones) put
\[
 \Theta_\lambda(\cC)=\sum_{\mathcal J\subseteq\cC}(-1)^{|\mathcal J|}\lambda^{\bigcup\mathcal J}\qquad(\Theta_\lambda(\emptyset)=1).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Polarization; proved]\label{lem:polar}
Let $\lambda_v\ge1$, $\mu_v=\lambda_v-1$, and let $P\subseteq V_0$ be random with
$\PP(v\in P)=\mu_v/\lambda_v$ independently.  For every hypergraph $\Ho$,
\[
 Q_\mu(\Ho)=\E_P\big[\Theta_\lambda(\Ho_P)^2\big],\qquad \Ho_P=\{J\in\Ho:\ J\cap P=\emptyset\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\tau(R)=\mathbf 1[R\cap J\ne\emptyset\ \forall J\in\Ho]$ for $R\subseteq V_0$, and
$\hat\tau(V)=\sum_{R\subseteq V}(-1)^{|V\setminus R|}\tau(R)$.  Expanding
$\tau(R)=\sum_{\mathcal J\subseteq\Ho}(-1)^{|\mathcal J|}\mathbf 1[R\cap\bigcup\mathcal J=\emptyset]$,
and using that the M\"obius transform of $R\mapsto\mathbf 1[R\cap U=\emptyset]$ is
$V\mapsto(-1)^{|V|}\mathbf 1[V\subseteq U]$, we get $\hat\tau(V)=(-1)^{|V|}N_\Ho(V)$.  Let
\[
 \mathcal T(p)=\sum_V\hat\tau(V)p^V=\sum_R\tau(R)\prod_{v\in R}p_v\prod_{v\notin R}(1-p_v)
\]
be the multilinear extension of $\tau$.  If the $p_v$ are independent with
mean $0$ and variance $\mu_v$, distinct monomials are orthogonal, and
$\E[\mathcal T(p)^2]=\sum_V\mu^V\hat\tau(V)^2=Q_\mu(\Ho)$.  Take $p_v=1$ with
probability $\mu_v/\lambda_v$ and $p_v=-\mu_v$ otherwise (mean
$\mu_v/\lambda_v-\mu_v/\lambda_v=0$, variance $\mu_v/\lambda_v+\mu_v^2/\lambda_v=\mu_v$), and
$P=\{v:p_v=1\}$.  Only the $R\supseteq P$ contribute; writing $R=P\cup B$ with
$B\subseteq P^c$,
\[
 \mathcal T(p)=\sum_{B\subseteq P^c}(-\mu)^B\lambda^{P^c\setminus B}\,\tau(P\cup B).
\]
Now $\tau(P\cup B)=\sum_{\mathcal J\subseteq\Ho_P}(-1)^{|\mathcal J|}\mathbf 1[B\cap\bigcup\mathcal J=\emptyset]$,
and for $U\subseteq P^c$,
\[
 \sum_{B\subseteq P^c\setminus U}(-\mu)^B\lambda^{P^c\setminus B}=\lambda^U\prod_{v\in P^c\setminus U}(\lambda_v-\mu_v)=\lambda^U .
\]
Hence $\mathcal T(p)=\Theta_\lambda(\Ho_P)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Matching bound; proved]\label{lem:theta}
If every $J\in\cC$ has $w_J:=\lambda^J\le2$, then for every matching
$\cM\subseteq\cC$ (pairwise disjoint members)
\[
 |\Theta_\lambda(\cC)|\le\prod_{J\in\cM}(w_J-1);\qquad\text{in particular }|\Theta_\lambda(\cC)|\le1 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $v\in V_0$ let $\cC-v$ be the members not containing $v$ and
$\cC/v=\{J\setminus\{v\}:\ J\in\cC\}$.  Splitting $\mathcal J$ according to whether
it contains a member through $v$,
\[
 \Theta(\cC)=\Theta(\cC-v)+\lambda_v\big[\Theta(\cC/v)-\Theta(\cC-v)\big]=\lambda_v\Theta(\cC/v)-\mu_v\Theta(\cC-v).
\]
Both families again have all weights at most $2$.  We induct on
$|\bigcup\cC|$.  If $\emptyset\in\cC$, then $\Theta(\cC)=0$ (toggling the empty
member pairs the terms), and the claim holds.  If $\cC=\emptyset$, then
$\Theta=1$ and $\cM=\emptyset$.  This covers the base case $\bigcup\cC=\emptyset$.  If
$\cM=\emptyset$ and $\cC\ne\emptyset$, it suffices to prove the bound for $\cM=\{J\}$
with any $J\in\cC$, as $w_J-1\le1$.  So let $J_0\in\cM$ be nonempty, $v\in J_0$,
and $\Pi'=\prod_{J\in\cM\setminus\{J_0\}}(w_J-1)$.  In $\cC/v$ the family
$(\cM\setminus\{J_0\})\cup\{J_0\setminus\{v\}\}$ is a matching, so
$|\Theta(\cC/v)|\le(w_{J_0}/\lambda_v-1)\Pi'$.  In $\cC-v$ the family
$\cM\setminus\{J_0\}$ is a matching, so $|\Theta(\cC-v)|\le\Pi'$.  Therefore
$|\Theta(\cC)|\le(w_{J_0}-\lambda_v)\Pi'+\mu_v\Pi'=(w_{J_0}-1)\Pi'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Energy bound; proved]\label{thm:C1}
\textup{(a)} If every edge of a hypergraph $\Ho$ has $\lambda^J\le2$, then
$Q_\mu(\Ho)\le\prod_{J\in\cM}(\lambda^J-1)$ for every matching $\cM\subseteq\Ho$; in
particular $Q_\mu(\Ho)\le1$.
\textup{(b)} In the Setting, if $\lambda_v\ge1$ and $\lambda^{\supp E}\le2$ for every
$E\in\cE$, then
\[
 G_F(\lambda)=\sum_U\lambda^U\|F^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ 1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) By Lemma~\ref{lem:polar} and Lemma~\ref{lem:theta} applied to $\Ho_P$ with the
matching $\cM\cap\Ho_P$, $Q_\mu(\Ho)\le\E_P\prod_{J\in\cM\cap\Ho_P}(\lambda^J-1)^2$.  The
members of $\cM$ are disjoint, so the events $\{J\cap P=\emptyset\}$ are independent,
with probability $\prod_{v\in J}\lambda_v^{-1}=1/w_J$.  Hence
$Q_\mu(\Ho)\le\prod_{J\in\cM}[(1-1/w_J)+(w_J-1)^2/w_J]=\prod_{J\in\cM}(w_J-1)$.
(b) Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} and (a), since $\Ho(x)$ consists of supports of events.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Energy tail; proved]\label{cor:tail}
In the Setting, if every event of $\cE$ has $|\supp E|\le k$ ($k\ge1$), then for
$f\in\{F,1-F\}$ and every integer $t\ge0$,
\[
 \energy(f;t)=\sum_{|U|>t}\|f^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ 2^{-(t+1)/k}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:C1}(b) with $\lambda_v=2^{1/k}$ and \eqref{eq:markov};
$(1-F)^{=U}=-F^{=U}$ for $U\ne\emptyset$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sharpness; proved]\label{rem:sharp}
Over general product spaces the base $2^{-1/k}$ cannot be improved.  Take
$m$ events on pairwise disjoint sets of $k$ coordinates, each fixing at every
coordinate of its support a value of probability $p$; put $\pi=p^k$.  As the
factors $1-\mathbf 1_{E_i}$ are independent, $F^{=U}=\prod_i(1-\mathbf 1_{E_i})^{=U\cap\supp E_i}$.
For $U$ the union of the supports of $j$ of the events this gives
$\|F^{=U}\|^2=[\pi(1-p)^k]^j(1-\pi)^{2(m-j)}$, and $|U|=jk$.  Hence
\[
 \energy(F;jk-1)\ \ge\ \binom mj\big[\pi(1-p)^k\big]^j(1-\pi)^{2(m-j)}
 \ \longrightarrow\ e^{-2s}\frac{s^j}{j!}
\]
as $p\to0$ with $m=\lfloor s/\pi\rfloor$.  With $s=j/2$ and $j!\le e\,j^{j+1/2}e^{-j}$,
the limit is at least $2^{-j}/(e\sqrt j)=2^{-(t+1)/k}/(e\sqrt j)$ for $t=jk-1$.  So no
bound $C\rho^{-(t+1)/k}$ with $\rho>2$ holds for all such systems (with alphabets
of size about $1/p$), and at most a factor of order $\sqrt{t/k}$ could be
gained.  The condition $\lambda^{\supp E}\le2$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:C1} is necessary, by the single-event formula above.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Boolean form and comparison; proved, except the cited constant]\label{rem:boolean}
A width-$k$ DNF $g:\{\pm1\}^n\to\{\pm1\}$ is $1-2F$ for the family of its terms
(a term with a literal set is a cylinder event).  As $g^{=U}=-2F^{=U}$ for
$U\ne\emptyset$, Corollary~\ref{cor:tail} gives, for every product measure on
$\{\pm1\}^n$ (Efron--Stein weights; Fourier weights for the uniform measure),
\[
 \sum_{|S|>t}\hat g(S)^2\ \le\ 4\cdot2^{-(t+1)/k},
\]
i.e.\ $g$ is $\varepsilon$-concentrated up to degree $k\log_2(4/\varepsilon)$.  The
switching-lemma route of Linial, Mansour and Nisan gives
$\sum_{|S|\ge t}\hat g(S)^2\le2\cdot2^{-t/(20k)}$ \cite[\S4.4]{OD}, \cite{LMN}.  Also
$\lambda^{|U|}\ge1+|U|\ln\lambda$ gives total influence
$\sum_U|U|\,\|F^{=U}\|^2\le(k/\ln2)\PP(F=0)$.  The same bounds hold for $q$-ary
DNFs with literals $x_v\in S_v$ (split each term into single-value cylinders
on the same support; $F$ and the supports do not change).  We have not found Theorem~\ref{thm:C1} in the
literature; see the introduction for what we checked.
\end{remark}

\emph{Digit filtrations.}  For the application we need weights that grow
along a filtration of each coordinate.  Suppose that each coordinate
$X_\ell$ ($\ell\in\cP$, a finite index set) is a tuple of independent
\emph{digits} $(\delta_{\ell,i})_{i_0(\ell)\le i<f_\ell}$, with arbitrary distributions,
and that the coordinates are independent; the Efron--Stein decomposition
$F=\sum_UF^{=U}$ below is taken over the digits, so $U$ is a set of pairs
$(\ell,i)$.  For such $U$ write $U_\ell=\{i:(\ell,i)\in U\}$.  We say that an
event $E$ is a \emph{digit-prefix event} if, for some integers $v_\ell(E)\ge0$,
it fixes exactly the digits $\delta_{\ell,i}$ with $i_0(\ell)\le i<v_\ell(E)$, for each
$\ell$ (to arbitrary values).  Such an event is a cylinder event on the digits.

\begin{theorem}[Filtration energy bound; proved]\label{thm:filtration}
In this situation let $\lambda_\ell\ge1$ ($\ell\in\cP$), and suppose that every event
of $\cE$ is a digit-prefix event with $\prod_\ell\lambda_\ell^{2v_\ell(E)}\le2$.  Then
\[
 \sum_U\Big(\prod_{\ell:\ U_\ell\ne\emptyset}\lambda_\ell^{1+\max U_\ell}\Big)\|F^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ 1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Lambda_{\ell,i}=\lambda_\ell^{i+1}$, $\Lambda_{\ell,i_0(\ell)-1}=1$, and
$\mu'_{\ell,j}=\Lambda_{\ell,j}-\Lambda_{\ell,j-1}\ge0$ for $i_0(\ell)\le j<f_\ell$.  For such $j$ let
$P_{\ell,j}=I-\E_{B_{\ell,j}}$, where $\E_{B_{\ell,j}}$ averages over the \emph{tail block}
$B_{\ell,j}=(\delta_{\ell,i})_{i\ge j}$.  Averaging over a digit in $U$ kills $F^{=U}$, and
$F^{=U}$ does not depend on digits outside $U$; hence $P_{\ell,j}F^{=U}=F^{=U}$ if
$U_\ell\ne\emptyset$ and $\max U_\ell\ge j$, and $P_{\ell,j}F^{=U}=0$ otherwise.  Call a set
$V$ of pairs $(\ell,j)$ a \emph{selection} if it contains at most one pair for
each $\ell$, and put $L'_V=\prod_{(\ell,j)\in V}P_{\ell,j}$.  Then
$\|L'_VF\|^2=\sum_{U\succ V}\|F^{=U}\|^2$, where $U\succ V$ means $\max U_\ell\ge j$
for all $(\ell,j)\in V$.  For fixed $U$, the sum of $\mu'^V$ over the
selections $V\prec U$ factorises as
$\prod_\ell\big(1+\sum_{i_0(\ell)\le j\le\max U_\ell}\mu'_{\ell,j}\big)=\prod_{\ell:\,U_\ell\ne\emptyset}\Lambda_{\ell,\max U_\ell}$.
So the left side of the claim equals $\sum_V\mu'^V\|L'_VF\|^2$, the sum over
selections.

Fix a selection $V$ and pass to the coarser product space whose coordinates
are, for $(\ell,j)\in V$, the head $(\delta_{\ell,i})_{i<j}$ and the tail block
$B_{\ell,j}$, and, for the other $\ell$, the whole $X_\ell$.  There $L'_V$ is the
operator $L_{V'}$ of the Setting for the set $V'$ of tail blocks.  Each event
$E$ is a disjoint union of cylinder events on the coarse coordinates with
the same coarse support (enumerate the unfixed digits of each coarse
coordinate that $E$ involves), and splitting every event in this way does
not change $F$.  Exactly one piece of $E$ holds at $x$ if $E$ does.  The
trace of the coarse support of $E$ on $V'$ is the set of $B_{\ell,j}$ with
$(\ell,j)\in V$ and $j<v_\ell(E)$.  Identify $B_{\ell,j}$ with the pair $(\ell,j)$ and put
$\hat E=\{(\ell,j):\ i_0(\ell)\le j<v_\ell(E)\}$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} and the
remark on traces,
\[
 \|L'_VF\|^2\le\E_x\big[N_{\hat\Ho(x)}(V)^2\big],\qquad \hat\Ho(x)=\{\hat E:\ E\in\cE,\ x\in E\}.
\]
Summing over selections and adding the nonnegative terms of all other sets
$V$ of pairs, the left side of the claim is at most
$\E_x[Q_{\mu'}(\hat\Ho(x))]$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:C1}(a) it remains to show
$\prod_{(\ell,j)\in\hat E}(1+\mu'_{\ell,j})\le2$ for every event.  Fix $\ell$ and write
$\lambda=\lambda_\ell$, $v=v_\ell(E)$, $i_0=i_0(\ell)$, and assume $v>i_0$.  Here
$1+\mu'_{\ell,i_0}=\lambda^{i_0+1}$ and $1+\mu'_{\ell,j}=1+\lambda^j(\lambda-1)$ for $j>i_0$.  The
hypothesis gives $\lambda^{2v}\le2$, so $\lambda^j\le\lambda^v\le\sqrt2<\lambda+1$ for $j<v$, i.e.\
$1+\lambda^j(\lambda-1)\le\lambda^2$.  The factor of $\ell$ is therefore at most
$\lambda^{i_0+1}\lambda^{2(v-1-i_0)}\le\lambda^{2v}$, and the product over $\ell$ is at most
$\prod_\ell\lambda_\ell^{2v_\ell(E)}\le2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Modulus-weighted tail; proved]\label{cor:modtail}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration} let the $\ell\in\cP$ be primes and
$\lambda_\ell=2^{\log\ell/(2\Lc)}$, and suppose $\prod_\ell\ell^{v_\ell(E)}\le T$ for every
event.  For a set $U$ of digits put $m_U=\prod_{\ell:\,U_\ell\ne\emptyset}\ell^{1+\max U_\ell}$.
Then for every $\tau\ge0$
\[
 \sum_{U:\ m_U>e^\tau}\|F^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ 2^{-\tau/(2\Lc)} .
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\prod_\ell\lambda_\ell^{2v_\ell(E)}=2^{\log\prod_\ell\ell^{v_\ell(E)}/\Lc}\le2$, and the weight of $U$
in Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration} is $2^{\log m_U/(2\Lc)}$.
\end{proof}

The cost of a junta is thus measured by the modulus $m_U$ a function of the
digits in $U$ needs, not by the number of coordinates.  For the
Erd\H{o}s--Straus events (digits in base $\ell$) this removes both the
binary width and the support size from the junta modulus.

\section{The residual system and the sandwich minorant}\label{sec:brw}

Throughout this section $T$ is large, $\beta=1+1/\log\Lc$, $\eta=\frac34\log\beta$,
and $(Q,r)$ is the square-class quarantine of Theorem~\ref{thm:haarupper};
$E_1,\dots,E_m$ ($m\le T^2$) are the events of the surviving atoms, in some
order, $A_i=\mathbf 1_{E_i}$, $F_{<i}=\prod_{j<i}(1-A_j)$, $F=F_{<m+1}$,
$S=\sum_i\PP(E_i)\le S_\beta$ and $\delta=\E F$.  By \eqref{eq:goodQ} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:betalll}, every subfamily of $\{E_i\}$ satisfies the hypothesis
of Lemma~\ref{lem:betalll}, $\delta\ge e^{-4S_\beta/3}$, and for $T$ large
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lllhyp}
 \eta\le0.19\qquad(\text{as soon as }\Lc\ge33).
\end{equation}

\emph{Digits.}  For $\ell\in\cP$ write $x\in\Omega_\ell$ in base $\ell$ as
$x=\sum_{i<f_\ell}\delta_{\ell,i}\ell^i$.  The digits $\delta_{\ell,i}$ with $i<a_\ell$ are those of
$r$, hence constant on $\Omega_\ell$.  The others are independent and uniform:
$\delta_{\ell,0}$ on $\{1,\dots,\ell-1\}$ if $a_\ell=0$, and $\delta_{\ell,i}$ on $\{0,\dots,\ell-1\}$
for $\max(a_\ell,1)\le i<f_\ell$; indeed $x\mapsto(\delta_{\ell,i})_{i\ge a_\ell}$ is a bijection
from $\Omega_\ell$ onto the product of these ranges.  So the free digits are
those with $i\ge i_0(\ell):=a_\ell$, and $E_{M,D}$ is a digit-prefix event with
$v_\ell(E_{M,D})=v_\ell(M)$ for $\ell\in\supp(M,D)$ and $v_\ell(E_{M,D})=0$ otherwise.  A
function of the digits $\delta_{\ell,i}$ with $i\le i_\ell$ depends only on $X_\ell$ modulo
$\ell^{1+i_\ell}$.

\emph{Cells.}  A \emph{consistent unit cell} is a class $b\bmod d$ with $d$
composed of primes of $\cP$, $d\mid\prod_{\ell\in\cP}\ell^{f_\ell}$, $\gcd(b,d)=1$ and
$b\equiv r\pmod{\gcd(d,Q)}$; on $\Omega$ it is the event
$\{X_\ell\equiv b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell^{v_\ell(d)})\ \forall\ell\mid d\}$, and on $\mathbb Z$ the
class itself.  The events $E_{M,D}$ are consistent unit cells (by survival).
The intersection of two consistent unit cells is empty or a consistent unit
cell whose modulus is the lcm of the two, both on $\Omega$ and on $\mathbb Z$.
A function on $\Omega$ that depends only on $(X_\ell\bmod\ell^{b_\ell})_\ell$ is a linear
combination of consistent unit cells of modulus $\prod_\ell\ell^{b_\ell}$ (the
indicators of the points of its level sets).

\begin{lemma}[Haar means; proved]\label{lem:haar}
Let $Q'=Q\ell'$ with $\ell'$ a prime not dividing $Q\prod_{\ell\in\cP}\ell$, and $r'$
the class modulo $Q'$ with $r'\equiv r\pmod Q$ and $r'\equiv1\pmod{\ell'}$ (or
$Q'=Q$, $r'=r$).  Let $\varphi$ be a combination of consistent unit cells with
$\lcm$ of moduli $D$.  With $H$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} for $Q'$,
$r'$ and $D$, $\E_H\varphi=\E\varphi$.  The same holds for $\varphi\psi$, where $\psi$
is a character modulo a squarefree $f\mid D$ coprime to $Q$, viewed on $\Omega$
through $X_\ell\bmod\ell$ for $\ell\mid f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $N_0=\lcm(Q',D)$.  By the Chinese remainder theorem the uniform measure on
$H$ is the product of the uniform measures on
$H_p=\{x\in(\mathbb Z/p^{v_p(N_0)})^\times:\ x\equiv r'\ (p^{v_p(Q')})\}$.  For $\ell\in\cP$ with
$\ell\mid D$, $v_\ell(Q')=a_\ell$ and $v_\ell(N_0)\le f_\ell$, and reduction maps the uniform
measure on $\Omega_\ell$ to the uniform measure on $H_\ell$ (all fibres have the
same size).  The other primes of $N_0$ do not divide $D$, and $\varphi$, $\psi$ do
not depend on them.
\end{proof}

For $j\le m$ let $F^{(j)}$ be the function of the digits not fixed by $E_j$
obtained from $F_{<j}$ by giving the digits fixed by $E_j$ their values on
$E_j$.  Then $A_jF_{<j}=A_jF^{(j)}$.  At $\ell$, the digits not fixed by $E_j$ are
those with $i\ge i_0^{(j)}(\ell):=\max(a_\ell,v_\ell(E_j))$, and $F^{(j)}$ is the avoidance
indicator of the restrictions of the events $E_i$ ($i<j$) compatible with
$E_j$.  Each restriction fixes the digits $i_0^{(j)}(\ell)\le i<v_\ell(E_i)$, so it is a
digit-prefix event for the filtration starting at $i_0^{(j)}$, with the same
$v_\ell(E_i)$ and $\prod_\ell\ell^{v_\ell(E_i)}\le M_i\le T$.  (A restriction may fix
nothing; then $F^{(j)}\equiv0$.)

\begin{lemma}[Bazzi--Razborov sandwich {\cite{Bazzi,Razborov}}; proved]\label{lem:brw}
Let $u_1,\dots,u_m$ be arbitrary real functions, $v_i=\sum_{j<i}A_ju_j$, and
\[
 B=1-\sum_{i\le m}A_i(1-v_i)^2 .
\]
Then $B\le F$ pointwise, and with $e_j=F_{<j}-u_j$,
\[
 F-B=\sum_iA_i\Big(\sum_{j<i}A_je_j\Big)^2,\qquad \E[F-B]\le m^2\sum_{j\le m}\E[A_je_j^2].
\]
If $u_j$ is a function of the digits not fixed by $E_j$, then
$\E[A_je_j^2]=\PP(E_j)\,\E(F^{(j)}-u_j)^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity $1-F=\sum_iA_iF_{<i}$ holds pointwise: the sum counts the first
occurring event.  Put $f_{<i}=1-F_{<i}=\sum_{j<i}A_jF_{<j}$.  If $F_{<i}=1$,
then $A_j=0$ for all $j<i$, so $v_i=0$ and $A_i(1-v_i)^2=A_i=A_iF_{<i}$.  If
$F_{<i}=0$, then $f_{<i}=1$, and $A_i(1-v_i)^2-A_iF_{<i}=A_i(f_{<i}-v_i)^2$.  In
both cases $A_i(1-v_i)^2-A_iF_{<i}=A_i(f_{<i}-v_i)^2$, and summing over $i$,
\[
 F-B=\sum_iA_i\big[(1-v_i)^2-F_{<i}\big]=\sum_iA_i(f_{<i}-v_i)^2\ \ge0,
\]
with $f_{<i}-v_i=\sum_{j<i}A_j(F_{<j}-u_j)$.  By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$(\sum_{j<i}A_je_j)^2\le m\sum_{j<i}A_je_j^2$.  Drop $A_i\le1$ and sum over
$i$.  For the last claim, $A_je_j^2=A_j(F^{(j)}-u_j)^2$, and $F^{(j)}-u_j$ is
independent of $A_j$.
\end{proof}

This is Razborov's simplification \cite{Razborov} of Bazzi's argument
\cite{Bazzi}.  The choice of $u_j$ as an $\ell^2$-approximation of the
``conditional survival function'' $F^{(j)}$ is the one used there.  We
include the short proof for completeness.  The identity and the inequality
$B\le F$ use only that each $A_i$ takes values in $\{0,1\}$, so they also hold
for the functions on $\mathbb Z$ obtained from cell expansions.

\emph{Choice of $u_j$.}  Fix $\tau\ge0$.  For $j\le m$ let $\cD_j$ be the family of
sets $U$ of digits not fixed by $E_j$ with $m_U\le e^\tau$, where
$m_U=\prod_{\ell:\,U_\ell\ne\emptyset}\ell^{1+\max U_\ell}$; it is down-closed.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uj}
 u_j=\big(F^{(j)}\big)^{\cD_j}=\sum_{U\in\cD_j}\big(F^{(j)}\big)^{=U},
\end{equation}
the Efron--Stein decomposition being taken over the digits not fixed by $E_j$.

\begin{lemma}[Approximation error; proved]\label{lem:EL}
With $u_j$ as in \eqref{eq:uj}, $\E[F-B]\le m^2S\,2^{-\tau/(2\Lc)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:energy} and Corollary~\ref{cor:modtail}, applied to $F^{(j)}$ with
the filtration starting at $i_0^{(j)}$ (the digits are indexed by their
absolute position, so $m_U$ is the modulus needed),
$\E(F^{(j)}-u_j)^2\le2^{-\tau/(2\Lc)}$.  Conclude with Lemma~\ref{lem:brw}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cell structure of $B$; proved]\label{lem:size}
With $u_j$ as in \eqref{eq:uj}, $B$ is a finite real combination of consistent
unit cells of modulus at most $T^3e^{2\tau}$, composed of primes of $\cP$.
The identity $B=1-\sum_iA_i(1-v_i)^2$ also holds for the expanded cell
combination evaluated at any integer $n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:energy}, $u_j$ is a combination of the functions
$\E[F^{(j)}\mid\delta_W]$, $W\in\cD_j$.  Such a function depends only on
$X_\ell\bmod\ell^{1+\max W_\ell}$ for the $\ell$ with $W_\ell\ne\emptyset$, so it is a combination
of consistent unit cells of modulus $m_W\le e^\tau$.  Expanding,
\[
 B=1-\sum_iA_i+2\sum_i\sum_{j<i}A_iA_ju_j-\sum_i\sum_{j,j'<i}A_iA_jA_{j'}u_ju_{j'} .
\]
Each term is a product of at most three events (moduli $\le T$) and at most
two $u$'s, so every cell is an intersection of consistent unit cells of total
modulus at most $T^3e^{2\tau}$.  The last claim holds because the
intersection identities hold on $\mathbb Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$\ell^1$-tightness; proved]\label{lem:l1}
If $B\le F$ pointwise, $F\ge0$ and $\E[F-B]\le\eta\,\E B$, then
$\E|B|\le(1+2\eta)\E B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$B^-:=\max(-B,0)\le F-B$ since $F\ge0$, and $\E|B|=\E B+2\E B^-$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Twist; proved]\label{lem:twist}
Assume $\E[F-B]\le\delta/100$, and write $B$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:size}, with
$\mu=\E B$.  Let $\psi$ be a real primitive character of conductor $f>1$ with
$\gcd(f,Q)=1$ and $f\mid d_i$ for some cell modulus $d_i$.  Then $\mu\ge0.99\,\delta$
and $|\E[B\psi]|\le\mu/4$, where $\psi$ is viewed on $\Omega$ through
$X_\ell\bmod\ell$, $\ell\mid f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\mu=\E F-\E[F-B]\ge0.99\,\delta$.  As $2\nmid f$, $f$ is squarefree.  Its prime
factors divide $d_i$, so they lie in $\cP$, and $a_\ell=0$ for them as
$\gcd(f,Q)=1$.  So $\psi=\prod_{\ell\mid f}\psi_\ell(X_\ell\bmod\ell)$ with nontrivial
characters $\psi_\ell$ of the uniformly distributed digits $\delta_{\ell,0}$.  Now
\[
 |\E[B\psi]|\le\E|B-F|+|\E[F\psi]|=\E[F-B]+|\E[F\psi]| .
\]
Fix $\ell_0\mid f$ and let $F'$ be the avoidance indicator of the events $E_i$
with $\ell_0\notin\supp E_i$.  Given the other coordinates $X_{-\ell_0}$, we have
$F=F'\cdot\mathbf 1[X_{\ell_0}\notin\mathrm{Forb}]$, where $\mathrm{Forb}$ is the union of the
classes $X_{\ell_0}\equiv-4D_i\pmod{\ell_0^{v_{\ell_0}(M_i)}}$ over the events $E_i\ni\ell_0$
whose other conditions hold.  Write $E_i\setminus\ell_0$ for the event given by those
other conditions, and $\pi_i$ for the $\Omega_{\ell_0}$-probability of the class at $\ell_0$,
so that $\PP(E_i)=\PP(E_i\setminus\ell_0)\pi_i$.  Since $\E_{X_{\ell_0}}\psi_{\ell_0}=0$,
\begin{align*}
 |\E[F\psi]|&=\Big|\E\Big[F'\textstyle\prod_{\ell\ne\ell_0}\psi_\ell\ \E_{X_{\ell_0}}\big[\psi_{\ell_0}\,\mathbf 1[X_{\ell_0}\in\mathrm{Forb}]\big]\Big]\Big|\\
 &\le\sum_{i:\,\ell_0\in\supp E_i}\PP(F'=1,\ E_i\setminus\ell_0)\,\pi_i .
\end{align*}
The event $E_i\setminus\ell_0$ is determined by the $s(E_i)-1$ coordinates of
$\supp E_i\setminus\{\ell_0\}$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:betalll}, applied to the family
defining $F'$, gives
$\PP(E_i\setminus\ell_0\mid F'=1)\le\beta^{s(E_i)-1}\PP(E_i\setminus\ell_0)$.  Hence
\[
 |\E[F\psi]|\le\beta^{-1}\sum_{i:\,\ell_0\in\supp E_i}\beta^{s(E_i)}\PP(E_i)\,\E F'=\beta^{-1}\tilde w_{\ell_0}\E F'\le\eta\,\E F' .
\]
The same bound gives $\E F'-\E F=\E[F'\mathbf 1[X_{\ell_0}\in\mathrm{Forb}]]\le\eta\,\E F'$, so
$\E F'\le\delta/(1-\eta)$.  By \eqref{eq:lllhyp},
$|\E[B\psi]|\le(0.01+\eta/(1-\eta))\delta\le0.245\,\delta\le0.245\,\mu/0.99<\mu/4$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the main theorems}\label{sec:proof}

\begin{theorem}[Assembly; proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:G} and~\ref{thm:NT}]\label{thm:assembly}
Let $T$ be large, let $(Q,r)$ be the square-class quarantine of
Theorem~\ref{thm:haarupper}, put $S_1=C\Lc^3\log\Lc$ (the bound for $S_\beta$ in
\eqref{eq:goodQ}) and
\[
 \tau=2\Lc\big\lceil\log_2\big(100\,T^4(S_1+1)e^{3S_1}\big)\big\rceil .
\]
Then there is a hard prime $p$ with $p>T$, $W(p)>T$ and
$\log p\le C_6\log Z$, where $C_6$ is absolute and
\[
 \log Z\le\log Q+4\tau+6\Lc+1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take the residual system of $(Q,r)$; $m\le T^2$.  If $m=0$, then $F\equiv1$; we
put $B=1$ (one cell of modulus $1$), so that $\mu=A=1$ and the twist condition
is vacuous, and go directly to the transfer step below.  Otherwise define $B$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:brw} with $u_j$ as in \eqref{eq:uj}.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:EL}, the
choice of $\tau$, $S\le S_\beta\le S_1$ and $\delta\ge e^{-4S_\beta/3}\ge e^{-3S_1}$,
\[
 \E[F-B]\le m^2S\,2^{-\tau/(2\Lc)}\le\frac{S\,e^{-3S_1}}{100(S_1+1)}\le\frac{e^{-3S_1}}{100}\le\frac\delta{100}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:twist} gives $\mu=\E B\ge0.99\,\delta>0$, and, as
$\E[F-B]\le\mu/99$, Lemma~\ref{lem:l1} gives $\E|B|\le1.03\,\mu$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:size}, $B$ is a combination of consistent unit cells of
moduli $d_i\le T^3e^{2\tau}$.

\emph{Pointwise inequality on integers.}  For every integer $n$ the values
$A_i(n)\in\{0,1\}$ satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:brw}, and the cell
expansion of $B$ is valid on $\mathbb Z$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:size}).  So
$B(n)\le F(n)$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:system}(i), $B(n)\le\mathbf 1[W(n)>T]$ for
every $n\equiv r\pmod Q$.

\emph{Transfer.}  Let $R=\max(T,\max_id_i)$, choose a prime
$\ell_{\rm aux}\in(R,2R]$, put $Q'=Q\ell_{\rm aux}$, and let $r'$ be the class modulo $Q'$
with $r'\equiv r\pmod Q$ and $r'\equiv1\pmod{\ell_{\rm aux}}$.  Then $8\mid Q'$,
$r'\equiv1\pmod8$, and $r'$ is a quadratic residue modulo every odd prime of
$Q'$.  As $\ell_{\rm aux}>\max d_i$ is prime, $\gcd(d_i,Q')=\gcd(d_i,Q)$, so the cells
are consistent for $(Q',r')$, and no cell or event involves $\ell_{\rm aux}$.  The
inequality $B\le\mathbf 1[W>T]$ holds on $n\equiv r'\pmod{Q'}$.  The functions $B$
and $|B|$ depend only on $X\bmod D$, so both are combinations of consistent
unit cells (point cells modulo $D$), and by Lemma~\ref{lem:haar}, $\E_HB=\mu$ and
$\E_H|B|/\mu\le1.03\le Z^{1/4}$.  A real primitive character of conductor
$f>1$ coprime to $Q'$ is coprime to $Q$, so Lemmas~\ref{lem:twist}
and~\ref{lem:haar} give the twist condition.  Here
$\log Z\le\log Q+\log(2R)+\log\max d_i\le\log Q+\log2+2(3\Lc+2\tau)$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} with $\log x=C_2(1+\log1.03)\log Z$ gives a prime
$p\equiv r'\pmod{Q'}$ with $W(p)>T$ and $\log p\le C_6\log Z$.  Since
$\ell_{\rm aux}\mid p-1$, $p>\ell_{\rm aux}>T$; and $p\equiv r\pmod Q$ with $840\mid Q$, so $p$ is
hard by Lemma~\ref{lem:system}(iii).
\end{proof}

The prime $\ell_{\rm aux}$ is needed for infinitely many \emph{distinct} primes:
$W(p)>T$ alone does not force $p>T$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By \eqref{eq:goodQ}, $\tau\ll\Lc(\Lc+S_1)\ll\Lc^4\log\Lc$ and
$\log Q\ll\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5$.  So $\log Z\ll\Lc^4\log\Lc$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:assembly} gives a hard prime $p>T$ with $W(p)>T$ and
$\log p\le C\Lc^4\log\Lc$.  As $\Lc<\log p$, this gives
$\Lc^4\ge\log p/(C\log\log p)$, i.e.\
$W(p)>T\ge\exp\big(c(\log p)^{1/4}(\log\log p)^{-1/4}\big)$.  Applying this to a
sequence $T_1<T_2<\dots$ with $T_{j+1}$ larger than the prime found for $T_j$
gives infinitely many distinct primes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the exponent comes from; Assessment]\label{rem:exponent}
The certified bound is $\log p\ll\log Z\ll\log Q+\tau$.  The quarantine term
is $\log Q\ll\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5$, of the order of the Haar density
(Theorems~\ref{thm:haarupper} and~\ref{thm:haarlower}).  The bottleneck is
the junta term $\tau\asymp\Lc(S_1+\Lc)\asymp\Lc^4\log\Lc$: the cells of the
sandwich have modulus about $e^{\Lc S}$.  In the previous version both terms
were of order $\Lc^5$ (up to $\log\Lc$).  Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} shows
that the junta term cannot be reduced within the present architecture.
\end{remark}

\section{The ceiling of the minorant architecture}\label{sec:ceiling}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} passes through a minorant $B\le F$ of
positive Haar mean whose cells have modulus $e^{O(\Lc S)}$.  Here we show
that no minorant of smaller level exists, dense or sparse, on any fibre
whose modulus satisfies $\log Q\le T^{0.05}$ (for larger $Q$ the transfer
itself is too expensive, Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling}).

\emph{Setting.}  Fix a modulus $Q$ and a unit $r$ modulo $Q$, and consider the
\emph{fibre} $\{n\in\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times:\ n\equiv r\ (Q)\}$ with its Haar
probability.  Let $F(n)=\mathbf 1[W(n)>T]$.  A function on the fibre has
\emph{level} at most $D$ if it is a linear combination of functions
$n\mapsto\phi(n\bmod qQ)$ with $q\le D$.  Every cell of modulus at most $D$
and every character of conductor at most $D$ (restricted to the fibre) has
level at most $D$; the minorant of Theorem~\ref{thm:assembly} has level at
most $\max_id_i\le T^3e^{2\tau}$.  All functions below depend on $n$ modulo a
fixed finite modulus, so all probability spaces are finite.

\begin{lemma}[Planting; proved]\label{lem:planting}
Let $b_1,\dots,b_N$ be independent bits with $\PP(b_i=1)=p_i\in[0,1)$, and law
$\nu_0$.  Put $r_i=p_i/(1-p_i)$, $R=\sum_ir_i$ and $r^*=\max_ir_i$.  Let $k\ge0$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plant}
 R\ \ge\ (k+1)+(2k+1)r^* .
\end{equation}
Then there is a probability law $\nu$ on $\{0,1\}^N$ with the same marginals as
$\nu_0$ on every set of at most $k$ coordinates and $\nu(b=0)=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $1_y$ for the configuration with ones exactly on $y\subseteq[N]$, and
$P_0=\nu_0(1_\emptyset)=\prod(1-p_i)$, so $\nu_0(1_y)=P_0\prod_{i\in y}r_i$.  Let
$e_a$ denote the elementary symmetric functions.  If $e_{k+1}(r)=0$, at most
$k$ of the $r_i$ are positive and $R\le kr^*$, contradicting \eqref{eq:plant}.
For $|J|=k+1$ put $w_J=\prod_{i\in J}r_i/e_{k+1}(r)$, so $\sum_Jw_J=1$, and
\[
 \nu=\nu_0+P_0\sum_{|J|=k+1}w_J\sigma_J,\qquad \sigma_J=\sum_{y\subseteq J}(-1)^{|y|+1}\delta_{1_y}.
\]
\emph{Marginals.}  For $|K|\le k$ and $z\subseteq K$, the $\sigma_J$-mass of
$\{x:\ x_K=1_z\}$ is $[z\subseteq J]\sum_{y'\subseteq J\setminus K}(-1)^{|z|+|y'|+1}=0$, as
$J\setminus K\ne\emptyset$.  In particular ($K=\emptyset$) $\nu$ has total mass $1$.
\emph{Zero.}  $\nu(1_\emptyset)=P_0-P_0\sum_Jw_J=0$.
\emph{Positivity.}  Only $y\subseteq J$ with $|y|=j\ge2$ even receives negative
charge, of total $P_0\sum_{J\supseteq y}w_J=P_0\prod_{i\in y}r_i\,e_{k+1-j}(s)/e_{k+1}(r)$, where
$s=(r_i)_{i\notin y}$.  So it suffices that $e_{k+1-j}(s)\le e_{k+1}(r)$.  Now
$e_{k+1}(r)\ge e_{k+1}(s)$, and $e_a(s)\ge e_{a-1}(s)$ for $1\le a\le k+1$: each
$a$-set arises $a$ times as $I\cup\{i\}$ with $|I|=a-1$, $i\notin I$, so
$a\,e_a(s)\ge e_{a-1}(s)(\sum s-(a-1)r^*)$, and
$\sum s-(a-1)r^*\ge R-(j+a-1)r^*\ge R-(2k+1)r^*\ge k+1\ge a$.  Chain
$a=k+2-j,\dots,k+1$.
\end{proof}

This is the dual (linear programming) side of lower-bound sieves:
minorants of degree $k$ of $\mathbf 1[b=0]$ with positive mean exist only if
no such $\nu$ exists (the duality itself is standard, cf.\ \cite[Thm.~5]{Tao254A}).  It is close in spirit to work on $k$-wise independent
laws with extremal probabilities \cite{BGP,PYY}; we claim no novelty for
it.  In the Poisson-like model with $R$ of order $S$, Bonferroni inequalities
of order $\asymp S$ do give positive minorants, so the order $k\asymp R$ is
right.

\begin{lemma}[Level barrier; proved]\label{lem:barrier}
Let independent coordinates be split into small ones $X_s$ and big ones
$(X_b)_{b\in\mathfrak B}$.  Let $\mathcal F$ be a family of events, each of the
form $E=\{X_s\in\Sigma_E\}\cap\{X_{b(E)}\in\Gamma_E\}$ with exactly one big
coordinate $b(E)$, and let $F$ be the indicator that no event of a family
$\mathcal E\supseteq\mathcal F$ occurs.  For a value $x_s$ put
$\Omega_b(x_s)=\bigcup_{E:\,b(E)=b,\,x_s\in\Sigma_E}\Gamma_E$,
$p_b(x_s)=\PP(X_b\in\Omega_b(x_s))$, $r_b=p_b/(1-p_b)$ and $R(x_s)=\sum_br_b(x_s)$.
Suppose $p_b(x_s)\le p^*<1$ for all $b$, $x_s$, put $r^*=p^*/(1-p^*)$, and
suppose $R(x_s)\ge(k+1)+(2k+1)r^*$ for \emph{every} $x_s$.  Let $B$ be a linear
combination of functions each depending on $X_s$ and on at most $k$ big
coordinates.  If $B\le F$ pointwise, then $\E B\le0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Define a law $\nu$: draw $x_s$ from its true law; draw bits $(b_b)_b$ from the
law of Lemma~\ref{lem:planting} for the independent bits
$\mathbf 1[X_b\in\Omega_b(x_s)]$ (bits with $p_b=0$ have $w_J=0$ and are never
set); given the bits, draw each $X_b$ independently from its true law
conditioned on $X_b\in\Omega_b(x_s)$ (bit $1$) or $X_b\notin\Omega_b(x_s)$ (bit $0$).
For a function $\phi$ of $x_s$ and of the big coordinates in a set $K$ with
$|K|\le k$: given $x_s$, the bits on $K$ are independent with the true
marginals, so $(X_b)_{b\in K}$ has its true product law, and
$\E_\nu\phi=\E\phi$.  Hence $\E B=\E_\nu B\le\E_\nu F$.  Under $\nu$ some bit is
$1$, so some event of $\mathcal F$ occurs and $F=0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The ES instance}
Fix a small absolute $\varepsilon\in(0,1/20]$ (chosen in Lemma~\ref{lem:cuprimes}), and put
$X=T^\varepsilon$, $V=X^{1/3}$, $L=\log X=\varepsilon\Lc$ and $y=\Lc^6$.  With
$D^*$ as in \S\ref{sec:haarlower}, let $\mathcal F^*$ be the set of events
$E_{v\ell,D}$ with
\begin{multline*}
 n_D:=D^*\in[V,X],\quad v\le V\ \text{squarefree and $y$-rough},\\
 \ell\in(T^{0.6},T^{0.7}]\ \text{prime},\quad v\ell\equiv-1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4n_D).
\end{multline*}
The congruence gives $v\ell\equiv3\pmod4$ and $n_D\mid A_{v\ell}$, i.e.\ $D\mid A_{v\ell}^2$;
and $D\le n_D^2\le T^{2\varepsilon}$.  On a fibre $n\equiv r\pmod Q$ the \emph{big}
coordinates are the $X_\ell=n\bmod\ell$ for primes $\ell>T^{0.6}$ with $\ell\nmid Q$.
Everything else is small: the residues of $n$ modulo the primes $\le T^{0.6}$
and modulo $2$, the coordinates fixed by the fibre (constants), and, for big
$\ell$, the higher $\ell$-adic digits of $n$ beyond $n\bmod\ell$.  On the units these
higher digits are uniform and independent of $X_\ell$, so all coordinates are
independent, as Lemma~\ref{lem:barrier} requires.  A modulus $q\le D$ has at
most $\log D/(0.6\Lc)$ \emph{distinct} prime factors $>T^{0.6}$, and a function
of $n\bmod qQ$ depends on $X_\ell$ only for these $\ell$ (whatever their exponent
in $q$).  So a function of level at most $D$ is a combination of functions
of the small coordinates and of at most $k=\lfloor\log D/(0.6\Lc)\rfloor$ big
ones.

\begin{lemma}[Unique small part; proved]\label{lem:unique}
For each pair $(\ell,D)$ there is at most one $v$ with $E_{v\ell,D}\in\mathcal F^*$.
Every event of $\mathcal F^*$ has exactly one big coordinate (if $\ell\nmid Q$),
$p_\ell(x)\le p^*:=T^{-1/2}$ for all $\ell$ and $x$, and, for every small
configuration $x$,
\[
 \sum_{\ell>T^{0.6}}p_\ell(x)=\sum_v\sum_{\substack{D:\ n_D\in[V,X]\\ 4D\equiv-x\ (v)}}c(v,n_D),\qquad
 c(v,n)=\sum_{\substack{\ell\in(T^{0.6},T^{0.7}]\\ \ell\equiv-v^{-1}\ (4n)}}\frac1{\ell-1},
\]
with $v$ over the $y$-rough squarefree $v\le V$, $c(v,n)=0$ if $\gcd(v,2n)>1$,
and $p_\ell(x)$ computed with $X_\ell$ uniform on the units modulo $\ell$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$v\equiv-\ell^{-1}\pmod{4n_D}$ and $1\le v\le V\le n_D<4n_D$, so $v$ is determined by
$(\ell,n_D)$.  As $v<T^{0.6}<\ell$, $\ell$ is the only prime factor of $v\ell$ above
$T^{0.6}$.  Given $x$, the events at $\ell$ whose small part holds are the
$E_{v\ell,D}$ with $x\equiv-4D\pmod v$, and distinct $D$ give distinct classes
$-4D\bmod\ell$, as $0<|D-D'|<T^{2\varepsilon}<\ell$.  So
$p_\ell(x)=(\ell-1)^{-1}\#\{D:\ v(\ell,D)\text{ exists},\ x\equiv-4D\ (v)\}$; this is at
most $X(1+L)/(\ell-1)\le T^{-1/2}$ by (F3).  Sum over $\ell$ and group by $v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Class-uniform primes; proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:xz} and~\ref{thm:G}]\label{lem:cuprimes}
There is an absolute $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that for $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$ and $T$ large the
following holds.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:G} with $Q_G=4X$, let $q_1$ be the
conductor of the exceptional character if there is one, and put
$\mathcal N_{\rm exc}=\{n:\ q_1\mid4n\}$ if $q_1$ exists and $q_1>\Lc^{1.9}$, and
$\mathcal N_{\rm exc}=\emptyset$ otherwise.  Then $c(v,n)\ge0.13/\varphi(4n)$ for every
$n\in[V,X]\setminus\mathcal N_{\rm exc}$ and every $v\le V$ with $\gcd(v,2n)=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $q=4n\le Q_G$ and $b\equiv-v^{-1}\pmod q$.  Each $\chi\bmod q$ is induced by a
primitive $\chi^*$, distinct $\chi$ give distinct $\chi^*$, and
$|\vartheta(x;\chi)-\vartheta(x;\chi^*)|\le\log q$.  By orthogonality and
Theorem~\ref{thm:G}, for $x\in[T^{0.6},T^{0.7}]$,
\[
 \Big|\vartheta(x;q,b)-\frac x{\varphi(q)}+[q_1\mid q]\,\chi_1(b)\frac{x^{\beta_1}}{\beta_1\varphi(q)}\Big|\le\frac{\mathrm{Err}_G(x)}{\varphi(q)}+\log q,
\]
where $\mathrm{Err}_G(x)$ is the right-hand side of Theorem~\ref{thm:G} (times
$C_G$), and the bracket is present only in the exceptional case.  The range
condition $Q_G^{6c}\le x$ holds for $\varepsilon\le0.05/c$, and
$\log x/\log Q_G\ge0.6/(\varepsilon+o(1))$.  Without an exceptional zero,
$\mathrm{Err}_G(x)\le C_Gx(e^{-0.6/(\kappa(\varepsilon+o(1)))}+o(1))$.  With one,
$(1-\beta_1)\log x\le\log x/(\kappa\log Q_G)\le0.7/(\kappa(\varepsilon+o(1)))$, so
$\mathrm{Err}_G(x)\le C_Gx(0.7/(\kappa\varepsilon))(e^{-0.6/(\varepsilon+o(1))}+o(1))$.  Both are at most
$x/16$ for $\varepsilon$ small, and $\log q=o(x/\varphi(q))$.  If $q_1\mid q$ and
$q_1\le\Lc^{1.9}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:xz} gives $1-\beta_1\gg\Lc^{-0.95}(\log\Lc)^{-2}$, so
$x^{\beta_1-1}=o(1)$; if $q_1\mid q$ and $q_1>\Lc^{1.9}$, then $n\in\mathcal N_{\rm exc}$.  So
$\vartheta(t;q,b)\in[\frac78-o(1),\frac98+o(1)]\,t/\varphi(q)$ on $[T^{0.6},T^{0.7}]$, and
partial summation against $1/(t\log t)$ gives
$c(v,n)\ge\sum1/\ell\ge\frac78\log\frac76/\varphi(q)-O(1/(\Lc\varphi(q)))\ge0.13/\varphi(q)$.
\end{proof}

If $q_1>\Lc^{1.9}$, put $q''=q_1/\gcd(q_1,4)\ge q_1/4$; then $n\in\mathcal N_{\rm exc}$ if
and only if $q''\mid n$, and by the divisor bound
$\tau(q'')/q''\le q''^{-0.95}\le4\Lc^{-1.8}$ for $T$ large.

\begin{lemma}[Class-uniform $D$; proved]\label{lem:cuD}
For $T$ large, every odd squarefree $y$-rough $v\le V$ and every unit $a$
modulo $v$,
\[
 W(v,a):=\sum_{\substack{D:\ n_D\in[V,X]\setminus\mathcal N_{\rm exc}\\ D\equiv a\ (v)}}\frac1{\varphi(4n_D)}\ \ge\ \frac{L^2}{200\,v}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $D=\kappa t^2$ with $\kappa$ squarefree, so $n_D=\kappa t$.  Keep only $t\le X^{1/6}$
with $(t,v)=1$ and $\kappa\in(K_1,K_2]$, $K_1=X^{2/3}$, $K_2=X/t$; then
$n_D\in[V,X]$, and $D\equiv a\pmod v$ means $\kappa\equiv b:=at^{-2}\pmod v$.  As
$\varphi(4n)\le2n$, $W(v,a)\ge H_{\rm all}-H_{\rm exc}$, where $H_{\rm all}$ and
$H_{\rm exc}$ are the sums of $1/(2\kappa t)$ over the kept pairs, with all $n$
and with $n\in\mathcal N_{\rm exc}$ respectively.  For a unit $b$ modulo $v$, the
number of squarefree $\kappa\le K$ with $\kappa\equiv b\pmod v$ is
$\sigma_vK/v+O(\sqrt K)$ with $\sigma_v=\sum_{(d,v)=1}\mu(d)/d^2\ge6/\pi^2$ (write
$\mu^2(\kappa)=\sum_{d^2\mid\kappa}\mu(d)$).  Partial summation gives
$\sum_{\kappa\in(K_1,K_2],\,\kappa\equiv b}1/\kappa\ge(\sigma_v/v)\log(X^{1/3}/t)-O(K_1^{-1/2})$.  For
$y$-rough $v$,
$\sum_{t\le X^{1/6},(t,v)=1}t^{-1}\log(X^{1/3}/t)\ge L^2/24-O(\omega(v)L^2/y)-O(L)\ge L^2/48$.
So $H_{\rm all}\ge\frac12\cdot\frac6{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{L^2}{48v}-O(LX^{-1/3})$.  If
$\mathcal N_{\rm exc}\ne\emptyset$: $q''\mid\kappa t$ forces $q''/(q'',t)\mid\kappa$, which with
$\kappa\equiv b\pmod v$ is one class modulo $vq''/(q'',t)$ or empty (empty if
a prime of $v$ divides $q''/(q'',t)$, since $b$ is a unit modulo $v$; otherwise
the Chinese remainder theorem gives one class), so by (F4)
the $1/\kappa$-sum is at most $1/K_1+(q'',t)L/(vq'')$.  Since
$\sum_{t\le X}(q'',t)/t\le\tau(q'')(1+L)$, we get
$H_{\rm exc}\le L^2\tau(q'')/(vq'')+O(LX^{-2/3})\le4L^2\Lc^{-1.8}/v$.  As
$X^{-1/3}\le1/v$, $W(v,a)\ge(L^2/v)(6/(96\pi^2)-o(1))\ge L^2/(200v)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No positive low-level minorant; proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:xz}, \ref{thm:G} and the fundamental lemma]\label{thm:ceiling}
There are absolute constants $c,\varepsilon>0$ such that for $T$ large, every modulus
$Q$ with $\log Q\le T^{0.05}$ and every unit $r$ modulo $Q$ the following holds.
If $B$ is a function on the fibre $n\equiv r\pmod Q$ of level at most $D$, with
$B\le F$ pointwise and $\log D\le c\,\Lc^4/\log\Lc$, then $\E B\le0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use $\mathcal F^*$ without the events whose $\ell$ divides $Q$; each of its events
has exactly one big coordinate, and $p^*\le T^{-1/2}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:unique}).
Fix a small configuration $x$; its entries are units.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:unique},
$R(x)\ge\sum_\ell p_\ell(x)\ge\sum_v\sum_{D\equiv-x/4\ (v),\ n_D\notin\mathcal N_{\rm exc}}c(v,n_D)-\omega_{\rm big}(Q)p^*$, where $\omega_{\rm big}(Q)\le\log Q/(0.6\Lc)\le T^{0.05}$ counts the prime factors of $Q$ above $T^{0.6}$.
For $D\equiv-x/4\pmod v$, $D$ is a unit modulo $v$, so $\gcd(v,2n_D)=1$, and
Lemmas~\ref{lem:cuprimes} and~\ref{lem:cuD} give
\[
 R(x)\ \ge\ 0.13\cdot\frac{L^2}{200}\sum_{\substack{v\le V\\ y\text{-rough, squarefree}}}\frac1v-T^{0.05}T^{-1/2}\ \ge\ \mu^*:=c_9\,\varepsilon^3\frac{\Lc^3}{\log\Lc},
\]
where the $v$-sum is $\ge c_3\log V/\log y$ by the fundamental lemma (as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:hmass}; squarefreeness costs $O(\Lc/y)$).  With
$k=\lfloor\log D/(0.6\Lc)\rfloor$ we have $(k+1)+(2k+1)r^*\le(k+1)(1+4p^*)\le\mu^*$ if
$c\le0.3c_9\varepsilon^3$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:barrier}, applied with $\mathcal E$ the set of
all events with $M\le T$, gives $\E B\le0$.
\end{proof}

The mechanism: conditioned on the coordinates below $T^{0.6}$, what remains
is a sieve on the big primes with removed classes of total density
$\gg\Lc^3/\log\Lc$ (the bulk of the ES mass comes from moduli with one large
prime factor), so a sieve of dimension $\kappa\asymp\Lc^3/\log\Lc$ with all moduli
above $T^{0.6}$.  Lower-bound sieves of dimension $\kappa$ need level about
$z^{\kappa}$, here $e^{\asymp\Lc^4/\log\Lc}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:planting} is the elementary
form of this, and the uniqueness family makes $R(x)$ large for \emph{every}
small configuration, not only on average.

\begin{corollary}[The $1/4$ ceiling; proved implication]\label{cor:ceiling}
Consider any argument that produces a hard prime $p$ with $W(p)>T$ from a
function $B\le F$ on a fibre $n\equiv r\pmod Q$, of level at most $Z$ and with
$\E B>0$, through a transfer theorem whose hypothesis requires
$\log x\ge C\log Z$ for the search bound $x$ (with $Z\ge Q$).  Every such
argument has $\log x\gg\Lc^4/\log\Lc$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, and hence
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, is of this type, whatever the minorant
(sandwich, Selberg, Bonferroni, dense or sparse, any value of $A$).  So such
arguments cannot give $W(p)\ge\exp((\log p)^{1/4+\delta})$ for any $\delta>0$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is optimal among them up to a factor
$(\log\log p)^{1/2}$ in $\log W(p)$.  Modulo \cite{TQ}, Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling4}
improves $\Lc^4/\log\Lc$ to $\Lc^4$ and the factor to $(\log\log p)^{1/4}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\log Q>T^{0.05}$, then $\log x\ge C\log Z\ge C\log Q>e^{0.05\Lc}$.  Otherwise
Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling} forces $\log Z>c\Lc^4/\log\Lc$.  In both cases
$\log x\gg\Lc^4/\log\Lc$, so the certified statement ``some hard $p\le x$ has
$W(p)>T$'' holds at best with $\Lc\ll(\log x\log\log x)^{1/4}$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Scope.}  Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} says nothing about the true size of
the least such prime; nothing about prime input other than low-conductor
minorants (bilinear or Type~II information, parity-sensitive input, or
mixtures of majorants and minorants); nothing about transfers whose
positivity comes from an exceptional-character term rather than from
$\E B>0$ (such transfers can only work at Siegel scales); and nothing about
functions that are not of bounded level in the above sense.  Section~\ref{sec:wiener}
extends the obstruction to minorants of unbounded level, to Siegel-model
main terms and to equidistribution hypotheses, for linear certificates with
full-orbit uniform accounting; Section~\ref{sec:LS} gives a hypothesis
outside that class which reaches $1/3$.

\begin{corollary}[The energy tail cannot be improved; proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:xz}, \ref{thm:G}, \ref{thm:NT} and the fundamental lemma]\label{cor:notail}
On the fibre of Theorem~\ref{thm:haarupper}, there is no bound
$\E(F^{(j)}-u_j)^2\le2^{-\tau/\rho}$ for all $j$ and all $\tau$, with $u_j$ as in
\eqref{eq:uj} and $\rho\le c'\Lc/(\log\Lc)^2$ ($c'$ small absolute).  So the
weights of Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration} are optimal for this purpose up to a
factor $(\log\Lc)^2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Such a bound gives $\E[F-B]\le m^2S2^{-\tau/\rho}\le\delta/100$ for some
$\tau\ll\rho(S_1+\Lc)\ll\rho\Lc^3\log\Lc$, so $\E B\ge0.99\delta>0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:twist}),
and $B$ has level at most $T^3e^{2\tau}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:size}).  For $c'$ small
this contradicts Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The heuristic exponent $1/3$; Assessment]\label{rem:third}
By Theorems~\ref{thm:haarupper} and~\ref{thm:haarlower},
$\log(1/\delta^*(T))=\Lc^{3+o(1)}$.  If the least prime in the avoiding set
behaved like the least prime in a set of Haar density $\delta^*$ (as for a
single progression, by Linnik's theorem), one would expect a hard prime with
$W(p)>T$ and $\log p\ll\Lc^{3+o(1)}$, i.e.\ $\log W(p)\approx(\log p)^{1/3}$
infinitely often.  This is a heuristic, not a claim.  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} it cannot be reached by a level-$Z$ minorant
with a transfer costing $\log x\gg\log Z$: one would need prime-detecting
input for a sifted set of sieve dimension $\asymp\Lc^3$ at level well below
its sieving limit, that is, information beyond minorants of bounded level,
such as bilinear (Type~II) estimates or other parity-sensitive input
adapted to the ES system.  We do not know how to provide it.
Section~\ref{sec:wiener} makes this precise for linear certificates, and
Section~\ref{sec:LS} shows that a Linnik-type hypothesis for sifted sets
(Theorem~\ref{thm:LS}) gives the exponent $1/3$ up to logarithms.
\end{remark}

\section{One-big-prime atoms: the Haar exponent without loss, typical size, and the ceiling at level \texorpdfstring{$\Lc^4$}{L\textasciicircum 4}}\label{sec:atoms34}

This section imports one input from the companion note \cite{TQ}, which
proves the exceptional-set bound $E(N)\ll N\exp(-c(\log N)^{3/4})$.  That note is
\emph{internally proved, not externally refereed}; results below that use it
are labelled ``modulo \cite{TQ}''.  Its analytic inputs are the
Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem, the Brun--Titchmarsh inequality and Shiu's
theorem, and its constants are not asserted to be effective.

\emph{The atoms of \cite{TQ}.}  Fix $\varkappa=1/480$ (any fixed
$\varkappa\in(0,1/240)$ would do).  For $X$ large put $t=\log X$,
$K_X=\lfloor X^\varkappa\rfloor$, $\mathcal K=\{k\le K_X:\ k\equiv1\ (4)\}$ and
$L_K=\lcm\mathcal K$.  The family $\mathcal A_X$ of \cite[(4)]{TQ} consists of
quadruples $(k,\ell,u,v)$ with $k\in\mathcal K$, $\ell$ a prime in a dyadic block
$I_j\subseteq(X^{1/2},X]$, $H<u,v\le z_j=x_j^{1/6}$ (with $H=K_X^{10}$ and $x_j$ the left end
of $I_j$), $(u,v)=(uv,k)=1$, a bound on $\omega(uv)$, and $4uv\mid k\ell+1$; the
atom is the class $E_A=\{n\equiv-uv^{-1}\pmod{k\ell}\}$, and
$H_X(n)=\sum_A\mathbf 1_{E_A}(n)$.  With $M=k\ell\equiv3\pmod4$ and
$w=(M+1)/(4uv)$ we have $uvw=A_M$, so $E_A$ is one of the multiplier
congruences in \eqref{eq:W}: if $n\in E_A$ then $W(n)\le k\ell\le K_XX$.  We
use three facts from \cite{TQ}:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(A1)] \cite[Lemma~2.2]{TQ}: at a fixed $\ell$ the atoms have pairwise
distinct residues modulo $\ell$, and there are at most $z_j^2\le\ell^{1/3}$ of
them; the residue $-uv^{-1}\bmod\ell$ is a unit, since $uv<\ell$.  An atom is
\emph{active} on the class $c=n\bmod L_K$ iff $k\mid u+cv$.
\item[(A2)] \cite[Cor.~4.3 and Lemma~3.2]{TQ}: with $f_c(\ell)$ the number of
atoms at $\ell$ active on $c$ and $\mu_c=\sum_\ell f_c(\ell)/\ell$, there are
constants $a,C_u>0$ with $a\,t^3\le\mu_c\le C_ut^3$ for every unit $c$ modulo $L_K$
and all large $X$.
\item[(A3)] \cite[Lemma~8.1 and Thm.~8.2]{TQ}: with $y=Bt^3$, $r$ the least even
integer $\ge D_Bt^3$ ($B,D_B$ as there; $D_B$ may be enlarged), $S_y(n)=\mathbf 1[(n,P_y)=1]$
and $Q_r(h)=\sum_{j\le r}(-1)^j\binom hj$, the function $\nu_X=S_y\,Q_r(H_X)$ is
$\ge0$ on $\mathbb Z$, $Q_r(0)=1$, $Q_r(h)\le\mathbf 1[h=0]+\binom h{r+1}$, and $\nu_X$ is a
combination $\sum_ic_i\mathbf 1[n\equiv a_i\ (q_i)]$ with $\log\max q_i\le C't^4$ and
$\sum_i|c_i|\le e^{C't^4}$; moreover $\sum_{n\le N}\nu_X(n)\ll Ne^{-c_at^3}$ whenever
$\log N\ge C_0t^4$.
\end{itemize}
Throughout, $X=T^{1/(1+\varkappa)}$, so that $K_XX\le T$ and $t\asymp\Lc$; and $\PP_*$,
$\E_*$ denote Haar measure on $\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times$.  Under $\PP_*$ the coordinate
$c=n\bmod L_K$ is a unit, and the residues $n\bmod\ell$, $\ell\in(X^{1/2},X]$ (all
coprime to $L_K$), are independent of $c$ and of each other, each uniform on
$(\mathbb Z/\ell)^\times$.  By (A1), given $c$, $H_X=\sum_\ell I_\ell$ with independent
Bernoulli variables $I_\ell$ of mean $f_c(\ell)/(\ell-1)$.

\begin{proposition}[Haar lower bound without loss; proved modulo \cite{TQ}]\label{prop:haarnoloss}
For large $T$, $\PP_*(H_X=0)\le e^{-at^3}$ and $\delta^*(T)\le e^{-at^3}$.  Hence
$\log(1/\delta^*(T))\gg\Lc^3$, and with Theorem~\ref{thm:haarupper},
$\Lc^3\ll\log(1/\delta^*(T))\ll\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given $c$, $\PP_*(H_X=0\mid c)=\prod_\ell(1-f_c(\ell)/(\ell-1))\le e^{-\mu_c}\le e^{-at^3}$ by
(A2).  This holds for every unit $c$, hence also after conditioning on
$n\equiv1\pmod{24}$, which restricts only $c\bmod3$ (note $9\in\mathcal K$) and the
independent coordinate $n\bmod8$.  If $W(n)>T$ then $n$ lies in no atom, since
atoms are multiplier congruences of modulus $\le K_XX\le T$; so $\delta^*(T)\le
\PP_*(H_X=0\mid n\equiv1\ (24))\le e^{-at^3}$.
\end{proof}

This removes the factor $1/\log\Lc$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:haarlower}, at the
price of the inputs of \cite{TQ} and ineffective constants;
Theorem~\ref{thm:haarlower} uses only the fundamental lemma.  The reason is
structural: each atom of \cite{TQ} has one large prime and a small
coordinate $c$ that is revealed, so the events are conditionally
independent and an exact product replaces the Janson-type inequality.

\begin{theorem}[Typical size; proved modulo \cite{TQ} and Page's theorem]\label{thm:typical}
There are absolute $c,c_1>0$ such that, uniformly for $x\ge3$ and
$3\le T\le\exp(c_1(\log x)^{1/4})$,
\[
 \#\{p\le x\ \text{prime}:\ W(p)>T\}\ \ll\ \pi(x)\,e^{-c(\log T)^3}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Here ``Page's theorem'' is the classical prime number theorem in
progressions, uniform for $q\le\exp(c_2\sqrt{\log x})$, with the possible
exceptional term \cite[Ch.~20]{Dav}: for $(a,q)=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:page}
 \psi(x;q,a)=\frac x{\varphi(q)}-\mathbf 1[q_1\mid q]\,\frac{\chi_1(a)x^{\beta_1}}{\varphi(q)\beta_1}+O\big(xe^{-c_3\sqrt{\log x}}\big),
\end{equation}
where $\chi_1$ (primitive modulo $q_1$) is the possible exceptional real
character for this range.  We recalled this form without re-checking it
against the source; any version with this range and error suffices.
The Siegel--Walfisz theorem would not: the moduli below have size
$\exp(C(\log\log x)^{4/3})$.

\begin{proof}
We may assume $T\ge T_0$.  For a prime $p>y$ with $W(p)>T$ we have $S_y(p)=1$ and
$H_X(p)=0$, so $\nu_X(p)=1$; hence the count is at most
$y+\sum_{p\le x}\nu_X(p)$, and $y=Bt^3$ is negligible.  Choose $c_1$ so that
$\log x\ge C_0t^4$.

\emph{Case A: $c_at^3\ge2\log\log x$.}  As $\nu_X\ge0$, (A3) gives
$\sum_{p\le x}\nu_X(p)\le\sum_{n\le x}\nu_X(n)\ll xe^{-c_at^3}\le xe^{-c_at^3/2}/\log x$.

\emph{Case B: $c_at^3<2\log\log x$.}  Then $t^4\ll(\log\log x)^{4/3}$, so every
modulus $q_i$ and the sum $\sum_i|c_i|$ are at most $\exp(O((\log\log x)^{4/3}))$,
inside the range of \eqref{eq:page} for large $x$.  Since $0\le\nu_X\le\sum|c_i|$,
\begin{gather*}
 \sum_{p\le x}\nu_X(p)\le\sqrt x\sum_i|c_i|+\frac2{\log x}\sum_{n\le x}\nu_X(n)\Lambda(n),\\
 \sum_{n\le x}\nu_X(n)\Lambda(n)=\sum_ic_i\psi(x;q_i,a_i).
\end{gather*}
Terms with $(a_i,q_i)>1$ count only powers of the primes dividing $(a_i,q_i)$
and contribute $O(\sum|c_i|\log q_i(\log x)^2)$.  For the others insert
\eqref{eq:page}.  Put $\E_*\nu_X=\sum_{(a_i,q_i)=1}c_i/\varphi(q_i)$ and
$\tilde\chi_1(n)=\chi_1(n\bmod q_1)$ on $\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times$.  Since
$\int_{n\equiv a\ (q)}\tilde\chi_1\,d\PP_*$ is $\chi_1(a)/\varphi(q)$ if $q_1\mid q$ and $0$
otherwise ($\chi_1$ is primitive), the main terms add up to
$x\,\E_*[\nu_X(1-\epsilon\tilde\chi_1)]$ with $\epsilon=x^{\beta_1-1}/\beta_1\in[0,2]$.  As
$\nu_X\ge0$ on $\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times$, this is at most $3x\E_*\nu_X$.  So
$\sum_{p\le x}\nu_X(p)\le(6x/\log x)\E_*\nu_X+O(xe^{-c_3\sqrt{\log x}/2})$.
On units $S_y=1$.  Given $c$, $\E_*[(H_X)_m\mid c]=m!\,e_m\big((f_c(\ell)/(\ell-1))_\ell\big)\le(2\mu_c)^m\le(2C_ut^3)^m$
for the falling factorials, so by (A3) and Proposition~\ref{prop:haarnoloss},
\[
 \E_*\nu_X\le\PP_*(H_X=0)+\frac{\E_*(H_X)_{r+1}}{(r+1)!}\le e^{-at^3}+\Big(\frac{2eC_ut^3}{r+1}\Big)^{r+1}\le2e^{-at^3}
\]
once $D_B\ge\max(2e^2C_u,a)$.  The error term is $\ll\pi(x)e^{-at^3}$ because
$e^{at^3}\le(\log x)^{2a/c_a}$.  In both cases the count is
$\ll\pi(x)e^{-\min(a,c_a/2)t^3}$, and $t\asymp\Lc$.
\end{proof}

The normalisation by $\pi(x)$ rather than $x$ matters when $\Lc^3\lesssim\log\log x$.
At $T=\exp(c_1(\log x)^{1/4})$ the theorem gives the prime case of the
exceptional-set bound of \cite{TQ}; it is a reproof, not an improvement.  It
is the opposite inequality to Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: an upper bound for
the number of primes with $W(p)>T$ needs a \emph{majorant}, and for majorants
the exceptional character costs only the factor $1+\epsilon\le3$; Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
needs a minorant.  Heuristically (Assessment) the count is
$\pi(x)e^{-\Lc^{3+o(1)}}$ up to $\Lc\approx(\log x)^{1/3}$.  We expect (Assessment, not
checked) that a matching lower bound in the range
$\log T\le c(\log x)^{1/4}/(\log\log x)^{O(1)}$ could be derived from the main term of
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, except when an exceptional character divides the
quarantine modulus; we have not pursued this.

The same atoms sharpen the ceiling of Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling}.

\begin{proposition}[Ceiling at level $c\Lc^4$; proved modulo \cite{TQ}]\label{prop:ceiling4}
There are absolute $c,T_0>0$ such that for $T\ge T_0$, every modulus $Q$ with
$\log Q\le T^{0.05}$ and every unit $r$ modulo $Q$: if $B$ is a function on the
fibre $n\equiv r\pmod Q$ of level at most $D$, with $B\le F$ pointwise and
$\log D\le c\Lc^4$, then $\E B\le0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:barrier} with big coordinates $X_\ell=n\bmod\ell$ for the
primes $\ell\in(X^{1/2},X]$ with $\ell\nmid Q$, and all other coordinates small
(including $c=n\bmod L_K$, the residues at all other primes, and the higher
$\ell$-adic digits at big $\ell$, as in \S\ref{sec:ceiling}).  As there, a function
of level $\le D$ reads at most $k=\lfloor2\log D/t\rfloor$ big coordinates, since each
big prime exceeds $X^{1/2}$.  Let $\mathcal F$ be the set of atoms with $\ell\nmid Q$;
each reads $c$ (small, as $k\mid L_K$) and one big $X_\ell$, and
$\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal E$, the set of all multiplier congruences with $M\le T$.  Fix a
small configuration; its $c$ is a unit.  By (A1), $p_\ell=f_c(\ell)/(\ell-1)\le
\ell^{1/3}/(\ell-1)\le2X^{-1/3}=:p^*$.  At most $2\log Q/t\le T^{0.05}$ big primes
divide $Q$, so by (A2)
\[
 R\ \ge\ \sum_{\ell\nmid Q}p_\ell\ \ge\ \mu_c-T^{0.05}p^*\ \ge\ at^3/2
\]
for $T\ge T_0$, for \emph{every} small configuration.  Since $r^*\le4X^{-1/3}$,
$(k+1)+(2k+1)r^*\le2(k+1)\le at^3/2$ once $\log D\le c\Lc^4$ with $c$ small.  Lemma~\ref{lem:barrier}
gives $\E B\le0$.
\end{proof}

Compared with Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling}, the factor $1/\log\Lc$ disappears,
and the inputs (Gallagher's theorem, Landau--Page, the fundamental lemma)
are replaced by those of \cite{TQ} (with an ineffective $T_0$).  In
Corollary~\ref{cor:ceiling} one may therefore replace $\Lc^4/\log\Lc$ by $\Lc^4$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is optimal among the arguments described there up to
a factor $(\log\log p)^{1/4}$ in $\log W(p)$.

\begin{remark}[One sieve limit behind two ceilings; proved parts and Assessment]\label{rem:unified}
The atoms of \cite{TQ} form a system with one big prime per event, big
cost $\asymp\Lc$ and conditional mass $P\asymp\Lc^3$ uniformly in the small
coordinates.  For such a system, order-$k$ certificates are trivial below
$k\asymp P$ on \emph{both} sides (proved, no novelty claimed): a minorant of
$\mathbf 1[\text{no event}]$ depending on at most $k\le0.6P-1$ big coordinates has
conditional mean $\le0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:planting}); a majorant of order $k$ saves
at most $k\log(C(P+4k)/k)+O(k+\log k)$ in $\log(1/\E)$, if $p^*\le1/4$ (the weighted
$k$-ary comparison and mean cost of \cite[Thm.~8.5 and Cor.~8.6]{SL}, via the
thinned law and binomial extrapolation of \cite[Lemmas~8.3--8.4]{SL}, applied
fibrewise; their core is \cite{PYY,BGP}); and the Bonferroni truncations
$Q_k(H)$ of order $k\ge e^2P$ achieve both (as minorant for odd $k$, as majorant
for even $k$).  So the critical level is $\asymp\Lc\cdot\Lc^3=\Lc^4$.  A budget
$\lambda\asymp\log N$ (rounding to integers) then exploits $\Lc\asymp\lambda^{1/4}$ and saves
$\asymp\lambda^{3/4}$: this is the exceptional-set exponent $3/4$ of \cite{TQ}, which is
sharp for coefficient-sum congruence majorants \cite[Thm.~10.14]{SL} (for CRT
majorants that are $\ge0$ on $\mathbb Z$, with coefficient sum $<N$ and all family primes
$\le N^A$; for Case-A classes modulo Elsholtz--Tao \cite[\S7]{ET}).  A
budget $\lambda\asymp\log x$ (transfer to primes $\le x$) exploits $\Lc\lesssim\lambda^{1/4}$:
the exponent $1/4$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, sharp in the sense of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling4}.  Within the stated scopes of these two
ceilings this is a conjunction of proved statements; as a description of
``the'' mechanism it is an Assessment.  The full avoidance indicator is not
a one-big-prime system: on the minorant side the subfamily is used only
for the obstruction (as $F\le\mathbf 1[\text{no event of }\mathcal F]$), while the positive
minorant of Theorem~\ref{thm:assembly} is built differently.
\end{remark}

\section{Beyond bounded level: a Wiener-norm barrier for linear certificates}\label{sec:wiener}

Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling} concerns minorants of bounded level.  Here we show
that the planted law behind it is exponentially pseudorandom against
residue classes and characters of \emph{every} modulus, and deduce that a
large class of linear certificates, whatever the prime input, cannot
beat the exponent $1/4$.  The results are from the working note \cite{O15}
(internally reviewed); we give the two short key proofs and state the rest
with pointers.

\emph{Setting.}  Fix $Q$ with $\log Q\le T^{0.05}$, a unit $r$, the fibre
$H=\{n\equiv r\ (Q)\}$ with Haar probability $\PP=\PP_H$, and $F=\mathbf 1[W>T]$.  In this
section the big coordinate $X_\ell$ ($\ell>T^{0.6}$ prime, $\ell\nmid Q$) is the whole
$\ell$-adic component of $n$ (modulo a power of $\ell$ large enough for all
functions considered); the events of $\mathcal F^*$ read it only modulo $\ell$, so the
quantities $p_\ell(x)$ and the bound $R(x)\ge\mu^*$ of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling} are unchanged, with $p^*\le T^{-1/2}$.  Put $k+1=\lfloor\mu^*/2\rfloor$;
then $R(x)\ge(k+1)+(2k+1)r^*$ for every small configuration $x$.  Let $\nu$ be the
planted law of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:barrier} for $\mathcal F^*$ and this $k$,
and $\rho=\nu-\PP$.  Then $F=0$ $\nu$-almost surely.

A \emph{reduced product} is a function $h=h_s(x_s)\prod_{b\in I}h_b(X_b)$, $I$ a finite
set of big coordinates, with $|h_s|,|h_b|\le1$ and $|\E h_b|\le1/4$ for $b\in I$.
Restricted to $H$, the indicator of a residue class, a Dirichlet character
and an additive character $n\mapsto e(an/q)$ of any modulus are reduced products
(up to a unimodular constant): the local factor at a big $b\mid q$ is a class
indicator of mean $\le1/(b-1)$, a non-trivial character of mean $0$, or a
non-trivial additive character of mean $c_{b^v}(a')/\varphi(b^v)$, of modulus
$\le1/(b-1)$; trivial factors are dropped from $I$.

\begin{lemma}[Pseudorandomness of the planted law; proved]\label{lem:pseudo}
For every reduced product $h$, $|\E_\rho h|\le(4r^*)^{k+1}$, and $\E_\rho h=0$ if $|I|\le k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x_s$ and use the notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:planting} for the bits
$\mathbf 1[X_b\in\Omega_b(x_s)]$.  Given the bits the big coordinates are independent;
put $\beta_b=\E[h_b\mid X_b\in\Omega_b]$ and $\gamma_b=\E[h_b\mid X_b\notin\Omega_b]$ ($\beta_b=\gamma_b=1$ for
$b\notin I$).  The conditional perturbation is $P_0\sum_{|J|=k+1}w_J\sigma_J$, and
\begin{align*}
 \E_{\sigma_J}\Big[\prod_{b\in I}h_b\Big]&=\sum_{y\subseteq J}(-1)^{|y|+1}\prod_{b\in y}\beta_b\prod_{b\in J\setminus y}\gamma_b\prod_{b\in I\setminus J}\gamma_b\\
 &=-\prod_{b\in J}(\gamma_b-\beta_b)\prod_{b\in I\setminus J}\gamma_b .
\end{align*}
For $b\in J\setminus I$ the factor $\gamma_b-\beta_b$ vanishes, so only $J\subseteq I$
contribute; none if $|I|\le k$.  Now $|\gamma_b-\beta_b|\le2$, and
$\E h_b=(1-p_b)\gamma_b+p_b\beta_b$ gives $|\gamma_b|\le(1/4+p_b)/(1-p_b)<1/2$.  Next
$w_J\le r^{*\,k+1}/e_{k+1}(r)$, and $e_{k+1}(r)\ge1$: the chain in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:planting} with $s=r$ gives $a\,e_a(r)\ge(k+1)e_{a-1}(r)$ for $a\le k+1$.  With
$m=|I|$, $\sum_{J\subseteq I,|J|=k+1}\prod_{b\in I\setminus J}|\gamma_b|\le\binom m{k+1}2^{-(m-k-1)}\le2^{k+1}$.  As
$P_0\le1$ and $|h_s|\le1$, the conditional bound is $r^{*\,k+1}2^{k+1}2^{k+1}$; average over
$x_s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Wiener-norm barrier; proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:xz}, \ref{thm:G} and the fundamental lemma]\label{thm:wiener}
There are absolute $c,T_0>0$ such that for $T\ge T_0$, every $Q$ with
$\log Q\le T^{0.05}$ and every unit $r$: if $B\le F$ on $H$ and $B=\sum_ic_ih_i$ is
any finite combination of reduced products, then
\[
 \E_HB\ \le\ \eta\sum_i|c_i|,\qquad \eta=\exp\big(-c\Lc^4/\log\Lc\big).
\]
Only the $h_i$ involving at least $k+1\asymp\Lc^3/\log\Lc$ big primes, hence of modulus
$>T^{0.6(k+1)}$, contribute.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\E_HB=\E_\nu B-\E_\rho B\le\E_\nu F-\E_\rho B=-\E_\rho B\le\sum_i|c_i|(4r^*)^{k+1}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo}, and $(4r^*)^{k+1}\le(8T^{-1/2})^{\mu^*/2-1}\le\exp(-c\Lc\mu^*)$.
\end{proof}

In particular $\|F\|_\times\ge\E_HF/\eta$ for the $\ell^1$ norm $\|\cdot\|_\times$ over such
representations: the avoidance indicator costs $e^{c\Lc^4/\log\Lc}$ times its
mean.  Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling} is the case $|I|\le k$.  (With the atoms of
\S\ref{sec:atoms34} in place of $\mathcal F^*$, the same argument gives
$\eta=e^{-c\Lc^4}$ modulo \cite{TQ}; we do not use this.)

\emph{Linear certificates} \cite[Def.~2.1]{O15}.  Let $\mathfrak h$ be a family of
reduced products on $H$ with moduli in a finite set, $N_x$ the number of
primes $p\le x$ in $H$ coprime to these moduli, and $m_x$ their counting
measure.  A nonnegative measure $m$ on $H$ of mass $N_x$ is
\emph{$\mathfrak h$-consistent at accuracy $\mathfrak E$} if $|\int h\,dm-N_x\E_Hh|\le\mathfrak E$ for all $h\in\mathfrak h$.  A
\emph{linear certificate} concludes ``$\int F\,dm_x>0$'' from the facts that $m_x$ is
nonnegative of mass $N_x$ and $\mathfrak h$-consistent at accuracy $\mathfrak E$, and from
nothing else (in particular not from its atomicity, integrality or
support); it is valid iff every consistent $m$ has $\int F\,dm>0$.  Every
minorant transfer is one: from $B=\sum c_ih_i\le F$ it concludes
$\sum_pF(p)\ge N_x\E_HB-\mathfrak E\sum|c_i|$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo} the diffuse measure
$N_x\nu$ is consistent at every accuracy $\mathfrak E\ge N_x(4r^*)^{k+1}$, for every family
$\mathfrak h$, and has $\int F\,d(N_x\nu)=0$; so valid linear certificates need
$\mathfrak E<N_x\eta$ \cite[Thm.~2.2]{O15}.  On the other hand, for a modulus $q>x$ no
bound that is uniform over a translation orbit can be small: if $\mathfrak h$
contains all classes in the fibre modulo $q$, then $\mathfrak E\ge1/2$; if it contains all
Dirichlet characters (or all additive characters) modulo $q$, then
$\mathfrak E^2\ge N_x/2$ (\cite[Lemma~2.3]{O15}, by orthogonality, as soon as $N_x$ is at most half
the number of units of the fibre modulo $q$).

\begin{corollary}[Full-orbit uniform linear certificates are capped at $1/4$; proved implication in {\cite[Cor.~2.4]{O15}}, same inputs; sketch here]\label{cor:orbit}
Let a linear certificate use a family $\mathfrak h$ of residue classes, Dirichlet
characters or additive characters which contains, with each member of
modulus $>x$, its whole translation orbit, with one error bound per orbit.
If it certifies a prime $p\le x$ in $H$ with $W(p)>T$, then
$\log x\gg\Lc^4/\log\Lc$.  Hence no such certificate, whatever true prime input
it uses (Bombieri--Vinogradov, Elliott--Halberstam, GRH for the characters
it uses, Siegel-zero main terms as below), gives $W(p)\ge\exp((\log p)^{1/4+\delta})$.
\end{corollary}

The proof \cite[Cor.~2.4]{O15} splits $B$ into members with at most $k$ big
primes, on which $\E_\rho=0$, and deep members, whose moduli exceed $x^2$ if
$\log x\le c\Lc^4/\log\Lc$; each deep orbit costs at least $1/2$ (classes) or
$\sqrt{N_x/2}$ (characters), while by Theorem~\ref{thm:wiener} it contributes at most
$\eta$ per unit of coefficient to the mean, so positivity needs $N_x\eta>1/2$.
One-sided accounting (dropping deep classes with positive coefficients)
and Hölder-averaged accounting over an orbit do not help
\cite[Cor.~2.4 and Prop.~2.5]{O15}.  \emph{Siegel zeros} (proved in \cite[Thm.~3.1]{O15}, same inputs; statement only here): for
any real character $\chi_1$ and $\epsilon\in[0,1]$, the reweighted fake $(1-\epsilon\chi_1)\nu$
is nonnegative, has $F=0$ almost surely, and agrees with the Siegel-model
law $(1-\epsilon\chi_1)\PP$ to within $2(4r^*)^{k+1}$ on every class, Dirichlet character
and additive character.  Let $s$ be the number of big primes dividing the
conductor of $\chi_1$.  The total masses differ by $\epsilon|\E_\rho\chi_1|\le(4r^*)^{k+1}$, and
agree exactly when $s\le k$, the relevant case; otherwise the certificate
framework must let the total mass vary within the accuracy.  If $B$ has
level $\le T^{0.6(k-s)}$, then $\E_{(1-\epsilon\chi_1)\PP}B\le0$ exactly.
So an exceptional term in the prime law does not rescue a minorant
transfer of this kind.

\begin{proposition}[Equidistribution hypotheses cap at $1/4$; proved implication, same inputs]\label{prop:EH}
Let $N$ be the number of primes $p\le x$ in $H$, and for a modulus $q$ let $N_{x,q}$
be the number of those coprime to $q$.  Let $\mathcal I$ be any set of statements
$|\sum_{p\le x,\,p\in H,\,p\nmid q}h(p)-N_{x,q}\E_Hh|\le\mathfrak E_h$ for residue classes, Dirichlet
characters or additive characters $h$ of moduli $q\le x$, individually with
$\mathfrak E_h\ge2\log x$, or summed over a set of moduli with an allowance at least
$2\log x$ times the number of terms (for example Bombieri--Vinogradov, the
Elliott--Halberstam conjecture at any level $\theta<1$, GRH restricted to
moduli $\le x$), in the form of their consequences for the unweighted counts
at the given $x$ (information about several scales or the weight $\log p$ uses
the position of the primes in $[1,x]$ and is outside the framework; see the
Scope paragraph below).  A linear certificate (with $N$ as the total mass) that uses
$\mathcal I$ and produces a prime $p\le x$ in $H$ with $W(p)>T$ needs
$\log x\ge c\Lc^4/\log\Lc$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\log x<0.6\Lc(k+1)$.  A modulus $q\le x$ has at most $k$ big prime
factors, so every $h$ occurring in $\mathcal I$ involves at most $k$ big coordinates on
$H$, and $\E_\nu h=\E_Hh$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo}).  The measure $m=N\nu$ on $H$ is
nonnegative of mass $N$ and gives $h$ the value $N\E_Hh$, which differs from the
centring $N_{x,q}\E_Hh$ by at most $N-N_{x,q}\le\omega(q)\le2\log x$, as $|h|\le1$.  Summing
gives the averaged statements.  Thus $m$ satisfies $\mathcal I$, while $\int F\,dm=0$.
\end{proof}

For Bombieri--Vinogradov or Elliott--Halberstam at level $\theta<1$ the extra
allowance $2x^\theta\log x$ is far below the usual $x(\log x)^{-A}$.
Raising the level of distribution only changes $c$: beyond level $x$ the
primes are not equidistributed in the orbit-uniform sense above.  Full
GRH is \emph{not} covered by Proposition~\ref{prop:EH}: it also asserts
$|\sum_{p\le x}\chi(p)|\ll\sqrt x\log^2(qx)$ for moduli $q>x$, and whether the planted
measure satisfies these bounds is open; Corollary~\ref{cor:orbit} covers
such characters only when they are used orbit-uniformly.

\emph{Scope} \cite[\S6]{O15}.  Not covered: certificates that use the
atomicity, integrality or support of the prime measure (for instance that
classes modulo $q>x$ whose least positive member exceeds $x$ are empty, i.e.
minorants valid only on $[1,x]$ or only on primes; single moduli
$\le x^{1/5}/(QT)$ are excluded in \cite[Prop.~5.2]{O15}); non-periodic families inside
the avoiding set; non-linear arguments (pair correlations, density
increments); and whether Type~II information can help.  On the integer
side, the Type~I sums produced by Vaughan- or Heath-Brown-type identities
are blocked only under full-orbit uniform accounting
\cite[Prop.~4.1]{O15}, which is not the natural accounting for integers.  Our
reading (Assessment) is that $1/4$ is a sieve-\emph{dimension} barrier
(dimension $\kappa\asymp\Lc^3/\log\Lc$, sifting limit $\beta_\kappa\asymp\kappa$, $\log z\asymp\Lc$), not a
parity barrier.

\section{The exponent \texorpdfstring{$1/3$}{1/3} under a Linnik-type hypothesis for sifted sets}\label{sec:LS}

By \S\ref{sec:wiener}, the heuristic exponent $1/3$ (Remark~\ref{rem:third}) is out
of reach of linear certificates with standard prime input.  Here we
identify a natural hypothesis that gives it.  The material is from
\cite{O16} (internally reviewed).

A \emph{unit-class sieve system} with moduli $\le T$ is a finite family $\mathcal E$ of
events $\{n\equiv a_E\pmod{m_E}\}$ with $2\le m_E\le T$ and $(a_E,m_E)=1$; no condition is
imposed on their number, overlaps or dimension.  Its \emph{avoiding set} is
$S(\mathcal E)=\{n\in\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times:\ n\not\equiv a_E\ (m_E)\ \forall E\}$, and $\delta(\mathcal E)$ is its Haar
measure (the proportion of units modulo $\lcm(m_E)$ in no event).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Hypothesis LS($C$)} (Linnik's theorem for sifted
sets; a conjecture).  For every $T\ge3$ and every unit-class sieve system $\mathcal E$
with moduli $\le T$ and $\delta(\mathcal E)>0$ there is a prime $p>T$ avoiding $\mathcal E$ with
\[
 \log p\ \le\ C\big(\log T+\log(1/\delta(\mathcal E))\big).
\]

For a single residue class (all other classes modulo $q$ removed, $T=q$,
$\delta=1/\varphi(q)$) this is Linnik's theorem.  LS is the log-scale form, not the
Cram\'er form, of the model in which a sifted set behaves like a residue
class of the same density, for the purpose of containing one prime.  The
term $C\log T$ cannot be dropped: shifted Jacobsthal configurations (one
class modulo each prime $\le z$) have $\delta\asymp1/\log z$ and least avoiding
integers of size $z^{1+o(1)}$.  The restriction to moduli $\le T$ matters: for an
arbitrary set of density $\delta$ modulo $Q$ no bound $p\ll\delta^{-1}(\log Q)^{O(1)}$ holds
(take the units in $(Q(1-\delta),Q)$).

\begin{theorem}[LS implies exponent $1/3$; proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:NT}, conditional on LS($C$)]\label{thm:LS}
Assume LS($C$).  For every large $T$ there is a hard prime $p>T$ with
$W(p)>T$ and $\log p\ll_C\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5$.  Consequently, for infinitely many hard
primes,
\[
 W(p)\ \ge\ \exp\big(c_C(\log p)^{1/3}(\log\log p)^{-5/3}\big).
\]
LS($C$) is used only for one system $\mathcal E_T$ for each $T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal E_T$ consist of the events $n\equiv-4D\pmod M$ for all atoms $(M,D)$ with
$M\le T$ (unit classes, as $\gcd(4D,M)=1$), and the events $n\equiv c\pmod{840}$ for
the unit classes $c$ that are not squares modulo $840$.  Its moduli are
$\le T$.  Let $(Q,r)$ be the quarantine of Theorem~\ref{thm:haarupper}.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:system}(i) the units $n\equiv r\pmod Q$ with $F(n)=1$ have $W(n)>T$,
so by Lemma~\ref{lem:atoms} they avoid every atom; as $840\mid Q$ and $r$ is a
square modulo $840$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:system}(iii)), they lie in $S(\mathcal E_T)$.  The
class $r\bmod Q$ has Haar measure $1/\varphi(Q)$ and within it $\E F=\delta\ge e^{-4S_\beta/3}$, so
by \eqref{eq:goodQ}
\[
 \log\frac1{\delta(\mathcal E_T)}\ \le\ \log\varphi(Q)+\tfrac43S_\beta\ \ll\ \Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5 .
\]
LS($C$) gives a prime $p>T$ avoiding $\mathcal E_T$ with $\log p\ll_C\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5$.  It
is a square modulo $840$, hence hard, and avoids every atom with $M\le T$, so
$W(p)>T$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:atoms}.  As $p>T$, letting $T\to\infty$ gives infinitely
many primes, and $\Lc\gg(\log p)^{1/3}(\log\log p)^{-5/3}$.
\end{proof}

More generally, LS with $C(\cdot)$ replaced by an increasing function
$\Phi(\log T+\log(1/\delta))$ gives $\log p\le\Phi(C_0\Lc^3(\log\Lc)^5)$; for instance $\Phi(\lambda)=\lambda^\theta$
gives the exponent $1/(3\theta)$.  LS is used far below the sieve limit: the
system has dimension $\kappa\asymp\Lc^3/\log\Lc$ on the big primes, LS asks for
$\log x\asymp\log(1/\delta)\asymp\kappa\log\Lc$ (up to logarithms), while a lower-bound sieve needs
$\log x\gg\kappa\log T$.  This ratio $\log T$ versus $\log\log T$ per unit of dimension is
the gap between $1/4$ and $1/3$.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:EH} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:orbit}, LS cannot be derived through linear certificates
from equidistribution hypotheses; it is a beyond-the-barrier hypothesis.
The planted measures of \S\ref{sec:wiener} do not refute it: they are
reweightings of Haar measure onto the complement of the avoiding set, not
sequences of primes.

\emph{Other strengths} \cite[\S\S2,5]{O16}.  (i) A Cram\'er form
($\log p\le\log(1/\delta)+A\log T$) implies LS($\max(1,A)$); a log-scale counting form
$\#\{T<p\le x:\ p\in S(\mathcal E)\}\ge c_0\delta^C\pi(x)$ for $\log x\ge C(\log T+\log(1/\delta))$ implies
LS($C'$) for some $C'=C'(C,c_0)$.  (ii) Main-term forms are too strong: in the integer analogue
(events $n\equiv0\pmod\ell$, $\ell\le z$, at $x=z^u$) the count is $(e^\gamma\omega(u)+o(1))\delta x$ with
Buchstab's function $\omega$, not $\sim\delta x$ (proved, classical); for primes a
uniform $\kappa$-tuple heuristic predicts a compounding deficit $(e^\gamma\omega(u))^\kappa$
(Assessment).  (iii) LS holds, by the Brun--Titchmarsh inequality, for
systems of total event mass at most $(1-\varepsilon)/2$ (proved); the ES system is
very far from this case.

\emph{Hypotheses for product sets do not suffice.}  A \emph{product system} has
only prime-power moduli, so that $S(\mathcal E)=\prod_\ell A_\ell$ is a CRT product with
$\delta=\prod_\ell|A_\ell|/\varphi(\ell^{e_\ell})$, a singular series.  Let HL$_{\rm prod}$($C$) be LS($C$)
restricted to product systems; it has the shape of a Hardy--Littlewood or
Bateman--Horn statement, but is a least-prime hypothesis for growing
systems, not a standard fixed-polynomial statement.  Let
$F_T=\{n\in\hat{\mathbb Z}^\times:\ W(n)>T\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Product subsets of the avoiding set are thin {\cite[Prop.~4.1, Cor.~4.2]{O16}}]\label{prop:product}
(a) (Proved.) There are product sets $S\subseteq F_T$, inside the hard classes,
with $\log(1/\delta(S))\le T^{1/2+o(1)}$.  (b) (Proved in \cite{O16} modulo the
Barban--Davenport--Halberstam theorem \cite[Ch.~29]{Dav}; ineffective; sketch below.)  Every
product set $S\subseteq F_T$ has $\log(1/\delta(S))\gg T^{1/2}(\log T)^{-8}$.  Consequently
HL$_{\rm prod}$($C$) implies $W(p)\ge(\log p)^{2-o(1)}$ for infinitely many hard $p$, and
no application of HL$_{\rm prod}$ to a product subset of $F_T$ certifies more
than $W(p)\ge(\log p)^{2+o(1)}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of (a)]
Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and $y=T^{1/2+\varepsilon}$.  Take $A_2=\{1\bmod8\}$ and, for odd $\ell\le y$, the
quadratic residues (lifted to $\ell^{e_\ell}$).  An atom $(M,D)$ all of whose primes are
$\le y$ is avoided: $(n/M)=1$ for $n\in S$ while $(-4D/M)=-1$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:jacobi}).  Every other atom has exactly one prime $\ell>y$, to the first
power; let $B_\ell$ be the set of its classes $-4D\bmod\ell$ and $A_\ell=(\mathbb Z/\ell)^\times\setminus B_\ell$
for $y<\ell\le T$.  Then $S$ avoids all atoms, and
$|B_\ell|\le\sum_{v\le T/\ell}\tau(A_{v\ell}^2)\le T^{1/2-\varepsilon+o(1)}\le\ell/2$, so
$\log(1/\delta(S))\le\pi(y)\log2+O(1)+\sum_{\ell>y}2|B_\ell|/(\ell-1)\le T^{1/2+\varepsilon+o(1)}$.
\end{proof}

Part (b) \cite[Prop.~4.1(b)]{O16} uses only the atoms $(\ell_1\ell_2,q)$ with
primes $\ell_1\equiv1$, $\ell_2\equiv3\pmod4$ in $(\sqrt T/2,\sqrt T]$ and a prime
$q\mid(\ell_1\ell_2+1)/4$: since $S$ is a product, each such atom must be blocked
at $\ell_1$ or at $\ell_2$, which forces $\gg T^{1/2}(\log T)^{-2}$ blocked classes per
prime on average once the primes $\ell$ are equidistributed modulo $4q$ on
average over $q\le\sqrt T(\log T)^{-6}$.  So the ES avoiding set, of density
$e^{-\Lc^{3+o(1)}}$, exceeds its best product subset by a factor $e^{T^{1/2+o(1)}}$:
hypotheses that see only prime-by-prime local structure are useless here,
and the hypothesis must apply to non-product sifted sets, as LS does.
