\title[A sub-exponential Erd\H{o}s--Straus witness bound]{Large Erd\H{o}s--Straus witnesses II:\\
the multiplier modulus is infinitely often $\exp\big((\log p)^{1/5-o(1)}\big)$}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{Draft v3, 5 October 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
For a prime $p$ let $W(p)$ be the least modulus $M\equiv3\pmod4$ of a
``multiplier'' congruence that yields a solution of $4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$.  In
earlier work we showed that $W(p)$ exceeds every fixed power of $\log p$ for
infinitely many primes $p$ in Mordell's hard classes.  Here we prove
$W(p)\ge\exp\big(c(\log p)^{1/5}(\log\log p)^{-1/5}\big)$ for infinitely many
hard primes, assuming Gallagher's prime number theorem for the family of all
characters and four results of Elsholtz and Tao on divisor sums of
polynomials; more precisely, the least hard prime with $W(p)>T$ is at most
$\exp(O((\log T)^5\log\log T))$.  Without the Elsholtz--Tao input we get
$\log W(p)\ge(1/\log2-o(1))\log\log p\cdot\log\log\log p$ infinitely often.
The class of one modulo a \emph{graded} set of prime powers leaves a residual
congruence system; the exponent at each prime is raised until the mass of the
system at that prime falls below a threshold proportional to the logarithm of
the prime power it would consume.  The local lemma then gives its avoiding
set positive Haar density, and the cost of the quarantine is controlled by a
moment of the number of prime-power divisors of the moduli, which we bound
with the Elsholtz--Tao divisor sums in progressions.  To transfer the density to primes we use
the one-sided $\ell^2$ sandwich of Bazzi and Razborov and a linear transfer
theorem based on Gallagher's theorem, as in the previous version.  The new
ingredient is a sharp energy bound for avoidance indicators: for any family
of cylinder events on a finite product probability space and any weights
$\lambda_v\ge1$ with $\prod_{v\in\supp E}\lambda_v\le2$ for every event $E$,
the Efron--Stein energies satisfy $\sum_U\prod_{v\in U}\lambda_v\|F^{=U}\|_2^2\le1$.
It replaces the binary encoding, Håstad's switching lemma and the
Linial--Mansour--Nisan argument of the previous version, and, applied to
the base-$\ell$ digits of the residues, it measures the cost of the
approximation in the size of the moduli instead of the number of primes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture asserts that $4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ is solvable
in positive integers for every $n\ge2$.  It suffices to treat primes.  It has
been verified for $n\le10^{18}$ (\cite{MD}; \cite{Salez} for $10^{17}$), and
Mordell's identities settle every prime outside the six classes
$p\equiv1^2,11^2,13^2,17^2,19^2,23^2\pmod{840}$ \cite{Mordell} (cf.\
\cite{Oblath}).  We call the primes in these classes \emph{hard}.  For the
exceptional set see Vaughan \cite{Vaughan} and \cite{PW}; for the average
number of solutions see \cite{ET}.

For $M\equiv3\pmod4$ put $A_M=(M+1)/4$.  If $uvw=A_M$ and
$n\equiv-uv^{-1}\pmod M$, then $s=(nv+u)/M$ is a positive integer and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident}
 \frac4n=\frac1{suw}+\frac1{nsvw}+\frac1{nuvw}
\end{equation}
(direct verification; this is the classical multiplier family, cf.\
\cite{Vaughan,ET}).  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
 W(n)=\min\{M\equiv3\ (4):\ n\equiv-uv^{-1}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ M)\ \text{for some}\ uvw=A_M\},
\end{equation}
with $W(n)=+\infty$ if no such $M$ exists.  Thus $W(p)\le T$ provides an
explicit representation with modulus at most $T$.  Every prime
$p\equiv1\pmod{\lcm\{M\le T\}}$ has $W(p)>T$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:one}), so
Linnik's theorem gives $W(p)\gg\log p$ infinitely often.  In
\cite{Paper1} we proved that for every fixed $k$,
$W(p)\ge(\log p)^{k}e^{-C_k\log\log p/\log\log\log p}$ for infinitely many
hard primes.  Here we prove a sub-exponential rate.

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:G} and~\ref{thm:ETall}]\label{thm:main}
There is an absolute constant $c>0$ such that for infinitely many hard primes $p$
\[
 W(p)\ \ge\ \exp\Big(c\,\frac{(\log p)^{1/5}}{(\log\log p)^{1/5}}\Big).
\]
More precisely, for every sufficiently large $T$ there is a prime
$p\equiv1\pmod{840}$ with $p>T$, $W(p)>T$ and $\log p\le C(\log T)^{5}\log\log T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:G}]\label{thm:uncond}
For infinitely many hard primes $p$,
\[
 \log W(p)\ \ge\ \Big(\frac1{\log2}-o(1)\Big)\log\log p\cdot\log\log\log p .
\]
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:G} is Gallagher's prime number theorem for the family of
all primitive characters of conductor at most $Q_G$ \cite{Gallagher}, quoted
from \cite[Thm.~28.19]{MV3} together with the standard facts on exceptional
zeros (Theorem~\ref{thm:xz}).  No hypothesis on Siegel zeros is needed.
Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall} collects four results of Elsholtz and Tao
\cite{ET}: their Proposition~1.4, Theorem~7.1, Corollary~7.4 and the
estimate (7.10).  They are used only in \S\ref{sec:moment}, to bound the
total mass of the residual system by $O((\log T)^4)$ and a moment of the
number of prime-power divisors of its moduli by $O((\log T)^4\log\log T)$.
Without them the same argument gives Theorem~\ref{thm:uncond}.  All
constants are effective, except that we have not checked whether the
implied constants in \cite{ET} are.  We make no numerical claim.  We also
obtain a Haar-side bound (Corollary~\ref{cor:haar}): the set of integers
$n$ with $W(n)>T$ has density at least $\exp(-O((\log T)^5\log\log T))$,
modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}.

\emph{Versions.}  The first version of this note had exponent $1/14$; it used
the Thorner--Zaman theorem \cite{TZ} through the transfer theorem of
\cite{Paper1}.  The second had exponent $1/7$: it replaced Thorner--Zaman by
the linear transfer theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} here, in a simpler
form), and bounded the approximation error by Håstad's switching lemma.  The
present version keeps the transfer theorem and the sandwich, and changes
three things: the switching lemma is replaced by an energy bound
(\S\ref{sec:energy}), the quarantine is graded (\S\ref{sec:system}), and the
cost of the quarantine is bounded through a moment estimate
(\S\ref{sec:moment}).  Håstad's switching lemma is no longer used.

\subsection*{The method}
Fix $T$.  We impose $n\equiv1$ modulo $Q=8\prod_\ell\ell^{a_\ell}$ for suitable
exponents $a_\ell\ge0$.  Then $W(n)>T$ whenever $n$ avoids a finite family
of congruence \emph{events} $n\equiv-4D\pmod{r}$, one for each surviving pair
$(M,D)$, where $r$ is the part of $M$ not absorbed by $Q$.  Unlike in
\cite{Paper1} and the previous version, a prime $\ell$ is not quarantined
all at once to $n\equiv1\pmod{\ell^{e}}$ with $\ell^e\le T<\ell^{e+1}$.  We
raise $a_\ell$ one step at a time, as long as the Haar mass $w_\ell$ of the
events that still involve $\ell$ exceeds $\theta_\ell=c(a_\ell+1)\log\ell/\log T$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:gradedq}).  Since every event comes from a modulus $M\le T$,
the total mass seen by one event is then at most $c$, which is what the local
lemma needs.  The cost $\log Q$ is at most $(\log T)/c$ times the moment
$\sum_{(M,D)}(g/M)\,h(M)$, where $g=\gcd(M,4D+1)$ and $h(M)=\sum_{\ell^i\mid M}1/i$.
We bound this moment by $O((\log T)^4\log\log T)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:omega}), so
$\log Q\ll(\log T)^5\log\log T$.

To find a \emph{prime} in the avoiding set we need a function
$B=\sum_ic_i\mathbf 1[n\equiv b_i\ (d_i)]$ with $B\le\mathbf 1[\text{avoid}]$
pointwise, mean $\gg\delta$ (the Haar density of the avoiding set), Haar
$\ell^1$ norm $\E|B|$ comparable to its mean, and small moduli $d_i$.  The
linear transfer theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}) then gives a prime $p$
with $\log p\ll\log Z$, where $Z$ is $Q$ times the largest $d_i$.  It is
Linnik's theorem with weights: the function $B(n)\mathbf 1[n\equiv1\ (Q)]$ is
expanded in characters, every coefficient is bounded by the Haar $\ell^1$
norm of $B$, whatever the number of cells, and Gallagher's theorem bounds
the contribution of all characters of conductor at most a power of $Z$ at once.

As in the previous version, $B$ is the one-sided $\ell^2$ sandwich of Bazzi
\cite{Bazzi}, in the form of Razborov \cite{Razborov} (Lemma~\ref{lem:brw}).
For events $E_1,\dots,E_m$ with indicators $A_i$ and arbitrary functions $u_j$,
\[
 B=1-\sum_iA_i\Big(1-\sum_{j<i}A_ju_j\Big)^2\ \le\ \mathbf 1[\text{no }E_i\text{ occurs}],
\]
and $\E[F-B]\le m^2\sum_j\PP(E_j)\,\E(F^{(j)}-u_j)^2$, where $F^{(j)}$ is the
indicator that no earlier event occurs, conditioned on $E_j$.  We need the
$u_j$ to be combinations of classes with small moduli.  The natural choice
is a truncation of the Efron--Stein decomposition of $F^{(j)}$, and the error
is then an Efron--Stein tail.  The previous version bounded this tail by
encoding residues in binary and applying Håstad's switching lemma
\cite{Hastad,LMN}.  Here we prove (Theorem~\ref{thm:C1}): if $F$ is the
avoidance indicator of any family of cylinder events on a finite product
probability space, and $\lambda_v\ge1$ are weights with
$\prod_{v\in\supp E}\lambda_v\le2$ for every event $E$, then
\[
 \sum_{U}\Big(\prod_{v\in U}\lambda_v\Big)\,\|F^{=U}\|_2^2\ \le\ 1 .
\]
With $\lambda_v=2^{1/k}$ this gives the tail $2^{-(t+1)/k}$ beyond level $t$ for
events on at most $k$ coordinates (Corollary~\ref{cor:tail}), with no
dependence on the alphabet sizes.  Applied to the base-$\ell$ digits of the
residues, with weights adapted to the moduli (Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration}),
it shows that truncating $F^{(j)}$ to moduli at most $e^{\tau}$ costs
$2^{-\tau/(2\log T)}$.  So moduli of size $\log d_i\ll\log T\cdot(S+\log T)$
suffice, where $S\ll(\log T)^4$ is the total mass.  Altogether
$\log Z\ll(\log T)^5\log\log T$.

\subsection*{Relation to the literature}
\emph{The energy bound.}  The nearest prior art known to us is Lecomte and
Tan \cite{LT}, who bound the Fourier coefficients of a DNF on $\{\pm1\}^n$ by
the probability that a set $S$ is covered by the terms satisfied at a random
point; this is the device of our Lemma~\ref{lem:cover}.  Their cover counts
are unsigned, and their degree concentration still comes from Håstad's
switching lemma, with an unspecified constant.  In Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} the
cover counts are signed, so that they vanish when a satisfied event misses
the set; the polarization identity (Lemma~\ref{lem:polar}) and the
deletion--contraction bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:theta}) then give the constant
$1$, on any finite product probability space.  In Boolean language
(Remark~\ref{rem:boolean}): every width-$k$ DNF $g:\{\pm1\}^n\to\{\pm1\}$
has $\sum_{|S|>t}\hat g(S)^2\le4\cdot2^{-(t+1)/k}$, under any product measure,
while the switching-lemma route gives $2\cdot2^{-t/(20k)}$ \cite[\S4.4]{OD}.
The base $2^{-1/k}$ is sharp over general product spaces
(Remark~\ref{rem:sharp}).  Theorem~\ref{thm:C1} and its corollaries are new to
us, but our literature search was partial (we checked \cite{LT}, \cite{OD} and
some lecture notes; we did not search the literature on switching-lemma
variants, Fourier growth or hypercontractivity with $\rho>1$
systematically), and we make no priority claim.

\emph{The rest.}  The sandwich (Lemma~\ref{lem:brw}) is Razborov's identity.
To our knowledge tools from the theory of bounded independence
(``polylogarithmic independence fools DNF and $\mathrm{AC}^0$''
\cite{Bazzi,Razborov,Braverman}) have not been used before to construct
sieve minorants for primes.  Gallagher's theorem is classically the engine of
Linnik's theorem on the least prime in a single progression.  Here it serves
as a transfer device for an arbitrary signed combination of progressions; the
cost is governed by the Haar $\ell^1$ norm of the minorant and its largest
modulus, not by the number of cells.  The nearest classical analogues are
bounds for the least prime in a union of residue classes, or in a Chebotarev
class of an abelian extension (Lagarias--Montgomery--Odlyzko \cite{LMO};
Thorner--Zaman \cite{TZcheb}); these concern nonnegative indicators.  We
have not searched systematically for an earlier use of Gallagher's theorem
in this role.  Earlier uses of the switching lemma in number theory known to
us run in the opposite direction: they show that the M\"obius function is
uncorrelated with bounded-depth circuits \cite{Green,Bourgain}.  The Haar
side, a local-lemma lower bound for the density of the uncovered set of a
congruence system, has relatives in the work on covering systems:
Filaseta, Ford, Konyagin, Pomerance and Yu \cite{FFKPY}, who to our knowledge
already use the local lemma for this purpose, Hough \cite{Hough} and
Balister, Bollob\'as, Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba \cite{BBMST}.  We do not
use those results.  The moment bound of \S\ref{sec:moment} uses the
parametrisation and the divisor-sum estimates of Elsholtz and Tao
\cite{ET}; the splitting of the prime powers at the scale of the parameter
$c$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:rough}) is ours.

\section{Atoms and the graded quarantine}\label{sec:system}

For $M\equiv3\pmod4$ put $\cR(M)=\{-4D\bmod M:\ D\mid A_M^2\}$.  Note that
$\gcd(A_M,M)=1$ (as $4A_M=M+1$), so every element of $\cR(M)$ is a unit
class.

\begin{lemma}[Proved; cf.\ {\cite{Paper1}}]\label{lem:atoms}
$\{-uv^{-1}\bmod M:\ uvw=A_M\}=\cR(M)$.  Consequently, for every integer $n$,
$W(n)>T$ if and only if $n\bmod M\notin\cR(M)$ for every $M\le T$ with
$M\equiv3\pmod4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $4A_M\equiv1\pmod M$, from $uvw=A_M$ we get $v^{-1}\equiv4uw$, hence
$-uv^{-1}\equiv-4u^2w$ and $D=u^2w$ divides $A_M^2$.  Conversely let
$D\mid A_M^2$.  For each prime $q$ with $q^a\Vert A_M$ and $q^d\Vert D$
($d\le2a$) put $u_q=\max(0,d-a)$, $w_q=d-2u_q$, $v_q=a-u_q-w_q$; these are
non-negative, so $u=\prod q^{u_q}$, $v=\prod q^{v_q}$, $w=\prod q^{w_q}$
satisfy $uvw=A_M$ and $u^2w=D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Class of one; proved]\label{lem:one}
$1\notin\cR(M)$ for every $M\equiv3\pmod4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $-4D\equiv1$ then $D\equiv-A_M\pmod M$ (multiply by $A_M$).  Since
$4A_M^2\equiv A_M$, the map $D\mapsto A_M^2/D$ preserves this congruence, so
we may assume $D\le A_M$; then $0<D+A_M\le2A_M<M$, while $M\mid D+A_M$.
\end{proof}

An \emph{atom} is a pair $(M,D)$ with $M\le T$, $M\equiv3\pmod4$ and
$D\mid A_M^2$.  There are at most $T^2$ atoms, since $\tau(A_M^2)\le2A_M\le T$.
For an atom put $g=g(M,D)=\gcd(M,4D+1)$.

\begin{definition}[Graded quarantine and residual system]\label{def:system}
For an odd prime $\ell\le T$ let $f_\ell=\lfloor\Lc/\log\ell\rfloor\ge1$ be the
largest exponent with $\ell^{f_\ell}\le T$.  A \emph{graded quarantine} is a
choice of integers $0\le a_\ell\le f_\ell$ for the odd primes $\ell\le T$; put
$Q=8\prod_\ell\ell^{a_\ell}$.  The \emph{coordinate primes} are the odd
$\ell\le T$ with $a_\ell<f_\ell$; write $\cP$ for their set.  For $\ell\in\cP$ let
\[
 \Omega_\ell=\{x\in(\mathbb Z/\ell^{f_\ell})^\times:\ x\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell^{a_\ell})\}
\]
(all units if $a_\ell=0$), with the uniform measure, and let $\PP$, $\E$ be
the product measure on $\Omega=\prod_{\ell\in\cP}\Omega_\ell$, with coordinates
$X_\ell$.  An atom \emph{survives} $Q$ if $\gcd(M,Q)\mid4D+1$ and $M\nmid Q$.  Its
\emph{support} is $\supp(M,D)=\{\ell:\ v_\ell(M)>a_\ell\}$, which is a nonempty
subset of $\cP$, its \emph{modulus} is $r=\prod_{\ell\in\supp}\ell^{v_\ell(M)}$, and
its \emph{event} is
\[
 E_{M,D}=\{X_\ell\equiv-4D\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ell^{v_\ell(M)})\ \text{for all}\ \ell\in\supp(M,D)\}.
\]
The \emph{fibre mass} at $\ell\in\cP$ and the total mass are
\[
 w_\ell(Q)=\sum_{(M,D)\ \text{surviving},\ \ell\in\supp(M,D)}\PP(E_{M,D}),\qquad
 S(Q)=\sum_{(M,D)\ \text{surviving}}\PP(E_{M,D}),
\]
where surviving atoms are counted individually (two atoms may give the same
event).  For an integer $n$ we put $X_\ell(n)=n\bmod\ell^{f_\ell}$, and $F(n)=1$
if $n\not\equiv-4D\pmod r$ for every surviving atom, $F(n)=0$ otherwise; on
$\Omega$, $F$ is the indicator that no event $E_{M,D}$ occurs.
\end{definition}

Every event is nonempty: $-4D$ is a unit modulo $\ell$, and
$-4D\equiv1\pmod{\ell^{a_\ell}}$ by survival.  The modulus satisfies $r\le M\le T$.
The quarantine of \cite{Paper1} and of the previous version is the special
case $a_\ell\in\{0,f_\ell\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Reduction and weights; proved]\label{lem:system}
In Definition~\ref{def:system}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If $n\equiv1\pmod Q$ and $F(n)=1$, then $W(n)>T$.
\item[(ii)] For every surviving atom,
\[
 \PP(E_{M,D})=\frac{\gcd(M,Q)}M\prod_{\ell\in\supp(M,D),\ a_\ell=0}\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}
 \ \le\ \frac gM\prod_{\ell\mid M,\ a_\ell=0}\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}.
\]
\item[(iii)] If $\Lc\ge2$ and $a_\ell\ge1$ for every odd prime $\ell\le\Lc$, then
$\PP(E_{M,D})\le C_1g/M$ for every surviving atom, where $C_1=e^2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $M\le T$, $M\equiv3\pmod4$, and suppose $n\equiv-4D\pmod M$ with
$D\mid A_M^2$.  Reducing modulo $\gcd(M,Q)$ gives $-4D\equiv1$, i.e.\
$\gcd(M,Q)\mid4D+1$.  If $M\mid Q$, then $n\equiv1\pmod M$, which contradicts
Lemma~\ref{lem:one}.  Otherwise the atom survives, and $n\equiv-4D\pmod r$ as
$r\mid M$, so $F(n)=0$.  Conclude with Lemma~\ref{lem:atoms}.
(ii) Let $\ell\in\supp$ and $v=v_\ell(M)$.  If $a_\ell=0$, the class of $-4D$
modulo $\ell^v$ has probability $1/\varphi(\ell^v)=\ell^{-v}\cdot\ell/(\ell-1)$ in
$\Omega_\ell$.  If $a_\ell\ge1$, then $|\Omega_\ell|=\ell^{f_\ell-a_\ell}$, and the class
(which lies in $\Omega_\ell$) has $\ell^{f_\ell-v}$ elements, so probability
$\ell^{a_\ell-v}$.  As $M$ is odd, $\gcd(M,Q)=\prod_{\ell\mid M}\ell^{\min(v_\ell(M),a_\ell)}$,
and $\min(v_\ell(M),a_\ell)=v_\ell(M)$ for $\ell\notin\supp$.  This gives the identity.
Survival gives $\gcd(M,Q)\mid g$.
(iii) Every prime $\ell$ with $\ell\mid M$ and $a_\ell=0$ satisfies $\ell>\Lc$, so
$\ell/(\ell-1)\le1+1/(\Lc-1)$.  As $M$ is odd, $\omega(M)\le\log M/\log3\le\Lc$.
The product in (ii) is therefore at most $\exp(\Lc/(\Lc-1))\le e^2$.
\end{proof}

Write $h(M)=\sum_{\ell^i\mid M,\ i\ge1}1/i=\sum_{\ell\mid M}H_{v_\ell(M)}$, where
$H_v=\sum_{i\le v}1/i$, and put
\[
 S_0=\sum_{(M,D)}\frac gM,\qquad \Omega_0=\sum_{(M,D)}\frac gM\,h(M),
\]
both sums over all atoms.

\begin{lemma}[Graded iterated quarantine; proved]\label{lem:gradedq}
Let $\Lc\ge2$ and $0<c\le1/8$.  Start from $a_\ell=1$ for the odd primes
$\ell\le\max(\Lc,7)$ and $a_\ell=0$ for all other odd $\ell$.  While some
$\ell\in\cP$ has
\[
 w_\ell(Q)\ >\ \theta_\ell:=c\,(a_\ell+1)\frac{\log\ell}{\Lc},
\]
raise $a_\ell$ by one.  The procedure stops, and the final quarantine satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\sum_{\ell\in\supp(M,D)}w_\ell(Q)\le c$ for every surviving atom, and
$w_\ell(Q)\le c$ for every $\ell\in\cP$;
\item[(b)] $\PP(E_{M,D})\le C_1g/M$ for every surviving atom, and $S(Q)\le C_1S_0$;
\item[(c)] $840\mid Q$ and $\log Q\le1.02\,\Lc+7+(C_1\Lc/c)\,\Omega_0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each step raises some $a_\ell<f_\ell$, so the procedure stops.  Exponents only
grow, so Lemma~\ref{lem:system}(iii) applies at every stage, which gives
(b).  (a): at the end every $\ell\in\cP$ has $w_\ell\le\theta_\ell$.  If
$\ell\in\supp(M,D)$, then $\ell\in\cP$ and $a_\ell+1\le v_\ell(M)$, so
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in\supp(M,D)}w_\ell\le\frac c\Lc\sum_{\ell\mid M}v_\ell(M)\log\ell=\frac{c\log M}{\Lc}\le c .
\]
Also $w_\ell\le\theta_\ell\le cf_\ell\log\ell/\Lc\le c$, as $a_\ell<f_\ell$ and
$\ell^{f_\ell}\le T$.
(c) $840=8\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7$ divides the starting $Q$, whose logarithm is
at most $\log(8\cdot105)+\vartheta(\Lc)\le1.02\,\Lc+7$ by $\vartheta(x)<1.01624\,x$
\cite{RS}.  A step at $\ell$ from $a$ to $a+1$, taken at a stage with
quarantine $Q'$, has $\log\ell<\Lc\,w_\ell(Q')/(c(a+1))$, and by (b) at that stage,
$w_\ell(Q')\le C_1\sum_{(M,D):\,v_\ell(M)\ge a+1}g/M$.  Each pair $(\ell,a)$ is stepped
at most once.  Summing over all steps,
\[
 \log Q-\log Q_{\rm start}\le\frac{C_1\Lc}c\sum_{(M,D)}\frac gM\sum_{\ell\mid M}\sum_{a=0}^{v_\ell(M)-1}\frac1{a+1}
 =\frac{C_1\Lc}c\,\Omega_0 .\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The local lemma needs only that the total mass seen by each event is small
(Corollary~\ref{cor:lll}), not that each $w_\ell$ is at most a fixed fraction
of $1/k$, where $k$ bounds the number of primes in a support.  Charging the
threshold to the prime power $\ell^{a_\ell+1}$ that a coordinate consumes lets
the budget $\log M\le\Lc$ do the work of the width $k$.  In particular no
block $\{\ell\le z\}$ of fully quarantined small primes is needed.  The ratio
of the cost of a step to its threshold is $\Lc/(c(a+1))$, uniformly in $\ell$.

\section{The mass and the charge moment}\label{sec:moment}

In this section we bound $S_0$ and $\Omega_0$.  We use four results of
Elsholtz and Tao.  For a polynomial $P$ and $m\ge1$ let $\rho_P(m)$ be the
number of roots of $P$ in $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$, and for integers $\kappa,a\ge1$ let
$\rho_{\kappa a}(m)$ be the number of $x\bmod m$ with $\kappa ax^2+1\equiv0\pmod m$.

\begin{theorem}[Elsholtz--Tao {\cite{ET}}; cited]\label{thm:ETall}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textup{(\cite[Prop.~1.4]{ET})} For $A,B>1$ and every positive
integer $\kappa\ll(AB)^{O(1)}$,
$\sum_{a\le A}\sum_{b\le B}\tau(\kappa ab^2+1)\ll AB\log(A+B)\log(1+\kappa)$.
\item[(b)] \textup{(\cite[Thm.~7.1]{ET})} Let $N>1$, and let $P$ be a polynomial of degree $D_P$ whose
coefficients are non-negative integers at most $N^{l}$, with
$\rho_P(p^j)\le C$ for all primes $p$ and all $j\ge1$.  Then
$\sum_{n\le N}\tau(P(n))\ll_{D_P,l,C}N\sum_{m\le N}\rho_P(m)/m$.
\item[(c)] \textup{(\cite[Cor.~7.4]{ET})} If $a,b,N$ are natural numbers with
$a,b\ll N^{O(1)}$, then $\sum_{n\le N}\tau(an+b)\ll\tau(\gcd(a,b))\,N\log N$.
\item[(d)] \textup{(\cite[(7.10)]{ET})} For $2\le A\le B$ and $\kappa\ge1$,
$\sum_{a\le A}\sum_{m\le B}\rho_{\kappa a}(m)/m\ll A\log B\log(1+\kappa)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

We use (a) and (d) only with $\kappa=4$.  Elsholtz and Tao state (d) inside
the case $A\le B$ of their proof of (a), and we use it only in that range.

\begin{lemma}[Prime-power counts; proved]\label{lem:h}
\textup{(a)} $h(xy)\le h(x)+h(y)$ for all $x,y\ge1$.
\textup{(b)} For $Y\ge2$ and $N\ge1$, $\sum_{q\mid N,\ q>Y}1/i\le\log N/\log Y$, where $q=\ell^i$.
\textup{(c)} $h(M)\le\log M/\log3$ for odd $M$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) $h=\sum_\ell H_{v_\ell}$, and $H_{x+y}-H_x=\sum_{x<i\le x+y}1/i\le H_y$.
(b) Fix $\ell\mid N$ with $v=v_\ell(N)$.  Each $i\le v$ with $\ell^i>Y$ has
$1/i<\log\ell/\log Y$, so these terms contribute at most $v\log\ell/\log Y$.  Sum
over $\ell$.  (c) $h(M)\le\sum_\ell v_\ell(M)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Parametrisation; proved, cf.\ {\cite[\S2]{ET}}]\label{lem:param}
Let $(M,D)$ be an atom with $D\le A_M$.  There are unique integers
$a,b,d\ge1$ with $d$ squarefree, $D=da^2$, $A_M=dab$ and $a\le b$.  Put
$P=4a^2d+1=4D+1$, $e=g(M,D)$, $f=P/e$, $c=(a+b)/e$ and $N=M/e$.  Then $c$ is a
positive integer, $f\mid P$, and
\[
 N=4acd-f,\qquad acd\le N\le T,\qquad a^2d\le T,\qquad \frac gM=\frac1N .
\]
The map $(M,D)\mapsto(a,c,d,f)$ is injective.  The involution
$D\mapsto A_M^2/D$ of the divisors of $A_M^2$ preserves $g(M,D)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $D=da^2$ with $d$ squarefree.  Comparing valuations, $da^2\mid A_M^2$ if
and only if $da\mid A_M$; so $A_M=dab$, and $D\le A_M$ if and only if $a\le b$.
As $M=4dab-1$, $\gcd(M,4dab)=1$.  Since $M+4D+1=4da(a+b)$, $e\mid4da(a+b)$
and hence $e\mid a+b$, so $c$ is an integer; $e\mid P$ by definition.  Next,
$e\cdot4acd=4ad(a+b)=(P-1)+(M+1)=ef+eN$, so $4acd=f+N$.  From $b\ge a$,
$eN=4abd-1\ge4a^2d-1=ef-2$, so $N\ge f-2/e$; then
$2N\ge N+f-2/e=4acd-2/e$, and $N\ge2acd-1\ge acd$.  Also $a^2d=D\le A_M\le T$,
$N\le M\le T$ and $g/M=e/(eN)$.  The quadruple determines $e=P/f$, then
$b=ce-a$, $D$ and $A_M$, hence the atom.  For the involution: $\gcd(A_M,M)=1$
and $4A_M\equiv1\pmod M$, so $4A_M^2/D+1\equiv(4D)^{-1}(1+4D)\pmod M$, and
$g$ is unchanged.
\end{proof}

The involution exchanges $D<A_M$ with $D>A_M$ and preserves $M$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
 S_0\le2\sum_{(a,c,d,f)}\frac1N,\qquad
 \Omega_0\le2\sum_{(a,c,d,f)}\frac{h(eN)}N\le2(\Sigma_I+\Sigma_{II}),\qquad
 \Sigma_I=\sum\frac{h(e)}N,\quad\Sigma_{II}=\sum\frac{h(N)}N,
\end{equation}
with all sums over the quadruples of Lemma~\ref{lem:param} and
$e=P/f$; we used Lemma~\ref{lem:h}(a).  For dyadic $A,B,C\in\{2^j:\ j\ge0\}$
the \emph{block} $(A,C,B)$ consists of the quadruples with $a\in[A,2A)$,
$c\in[C,2C)$, $d\in[B,2B)$.  Only blocks with $A^2B\le T$ and $ACB\le T$ occur.
There are $O(\Lc^2)$ pairs $(A,B)$ and $O(\Lc)$ values of $C$.

\begin{lemma}[Block mass; proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}(a)]\label{lem:block}
For every block,
\[
 \sum_{(a,c,d,f)\in(A,C,B)}\frac1N\ \le\ \sum_{a\in[A,2A),\,d\in[B,2B)}\frac{2\tau(4a^2d+1)}{ad}\ \ll\ \log(A+B+2).
\]
Consequently $S_0\ll\Lc^4$.  Unconditionally, $S_0\le2(1+\Lc)^3\tau^*(4T+1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$1/N\le1/(acd)$, $\sum_{c\in[C,2C)}1/c\le1+\log2<2$, and for fixed $(a,d)$ there
are $\tau(P)$ choices of $f$.  In the block $1/(ad)\le1/(AB)$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}(a) with $\kappa=4$ and Elsholtz--Tao's $(a,b,A,B)$
equal to our $(d,a,2B,2A)$ gives
$\sum_{a<2A,\,d<2B}\tau(4a^2d+1)\ll AB\log(A+B+2)$.  Sum over the $O(\Lc^3)$
blocks, using $\log(A+B+2)\ll\Lc$.  For the unconditional bound, use
$\tau(P)\le\tau^*(4T+1)$ and $\sum_{a,c,d\le T}1/(acd)\le(1+\Lc)^3$ in
\eqref{eq:split}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The rough part; proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}(a)]\label{lem:rough}
For every block $(A,C,B)$, with $Y=\max(C,2)$,
\[
 \sum_{(a,c,d,f)\in(A,C,B)}\frac{h(N)}N\ \ll\ \log(A+B+2)\Big(\log\log(C+2)+1+\frac\Lc{\log Y}\Big).
\]
Hence $\Sigma_{II}\ll\Lc^4\log\Lc$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$N$ divides the odd number $M$, so only odd $q=\ell^i$ divide $N$.  Write
$h(N)=\sum_{q\mid N}1/i$ and split at $q\le C$ and $q>Y$; for $C=1$ the
first part is empty.

\emph{Small $q$.}  Fix $(a,d,f)$ and $q=\ell^i\le C$.  If $\ell\mid ad$, then
$P\equiv1\pmod\ell$, so $\ell\nmid f$ and $N\equiv-f\not\equiv0\pmod\ell$.
Otherwise $q\mid N=4ad\,c-f$ confines $c$ to one class modulo $q$, which has
at most $C/q+1\le2C/q$ elements in $[C,2C)$, and each term has
$1/N\le1/(adC)$.  So the contribution of $(a,d,f)$ is at most
$(2/(ad))\sum_{q\le C}1/(iq)\le(2/(ad))(\log\log(C+2)+O(1))$ by Mertens'
theorem.  Summing over the $\tau(P)$ choices of $f$ and over $(a,d)$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:block} gives $\ll\log(A+B+2)(\log\log(C+2)+1)$.

\emph{Large $q$.}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:h}(b) and $N\le T$,
$\sum_{q\mid N,\,q>Y}1/i\le\Lc/\log Y$ pointwise, and Lemma~\ref{lem:block}
bounds the mass of the block.

\emph{Sum.}  With $C=2^j$, $0\le j\le\Lc/\log2$,
$\sum_j(\log\log(2^j+2)+1+\Lc/\log\max(2^j,2))\ll\Lc\log\Lc$, and
$\sum_{(A,B)}\log(A+B+2)\ll\Lc^3$.
\end{proof}

The prime powers $q\le C$ are summed along the $c$-progression only where a
block contains a full period.  The larger ones are paid pointwise, at the
cost $\Lc/\log C$; this is large only for small $c$, and every dyadic
$c$-scale carries the same mass bound, so the cost averages to
$\Lc^{-1}\sum_{j\le\Lc}\Lc/j\asymp\log\Lc$.

For the smooth part note that $\sum_{e\mid P}h(e)=\sum_{q\mid P}(1/i)\,\tau(P/q)$
(write $e=qe'$ with $e'\mid P/q$).  For a pair $(A,B)$ put $Z_0=\max(A,B)$ and
\[
 R(A,B)=\sum_{a\in[A,2A),\ d\in[B,2B)}\frac1{ad}\sum_{q\mid P}\frac1i\,\tau(P/q),\qquad P=4a^2d+1 .
\]

\begin{lemma}[Prime powers of $4a^2d+1$; proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}]\label{lem:smooth}
$R(A,B)\ll\log(Z_0+2)\log\log(Z_0+16)$, uniformly in $A,B\ge1$.  Hence
$\Sigma_I\ll\Lc^4\log\Lc$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every $q=\ell^i\mid P$ is odd and has $\ell\nmid ad$, since $P$ is odd and
$P\equiv1\pmod\ell$ if $\ell\mid ad$.

\emph{Large $q$ ($q>Z_0^{1/2}$), and all $q$ if $Z_0<16$.}  Use
$\tau(P/q)\le\tau(P)$.  As $P<32Z_0^3$, Lemma~\ref{lem:h}(b) gives
$\sum_{q\mid P,\,q>Z_0^{1/2}}1/i\le2\log(32Z_0^3)/\log Z_0\ll1$ for $Z_0\ge16$, and
for $Z_0<16$, $h(P)\le\log_2P\ll1$.  So this part is
$\ll\sum\tau(P)/(ad)\ll\log(Z_0+2)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}(a), as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:block}.

\emph{Small $q$, case $A\ge B$} (so $Z_0=A\ge16$ and $q\le A^{1/2}$).  Fix $d<2B$
with $\ell\nmid d$.  The congruence $4dx^2+1\equiv0\pmod q$ has at most two
roots $x_0\in[0,q)$ ($\ell$ odd, $\ell\nmid4dx_0$, Hensel).  For each, write
$a=qa'+x_0$; then $1\le a'\le N':=\lfloor2A/q\rfloor$, as $a\ge A\ge q>x_0$.  Put
\[
 P^\flat(a')=\frac{P(qa'+x_0)}q=4dq\,a'^2+8dx_0\,a'+\frac{4dx_0^2+1}q .
\]
Its coefficients are non-negative integers at most $8dq\le16A^{3/2}\le N'^5$
(as $N'\ge A^{1/2}$ and $A\ge16$).  It takes odd values, so
$\rho_{P^\flat}(2^j)=0$.  For an odd prime $p\ne\ell$, $a'\mapsto qa'+x_0$ is a
bijection modulo $p^j$, so $\rho_{P^\flat}(p^j)=\rho_{4d}(p^j)\le2$.  For $p=\ell$,
each of the at most two roots of $4dx^2+1$ modulo $\ell^{i+j}$ fixes $a'$
modulo $\ell^j$, so $\rho_{P^\flat}(\ell^j)\le2$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}(b)
(degree $2$, $l=5$, $C=2$) and multiplicativity,
\[
 \sum_{a'\le N'}\tau(P^\flat(a'))\ll N'\sum_{m\le N'}\frac{\rho_{P^\flat}(m)}m
 \le\frac{2A}q\Big(1+\frac2{\ell-1}\Big)\sum_{m\le2A}\frac{\rho_{4d}(m)}m .
\]
With $1/(ad)\le1/(AB)$ and at most two roots,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{a,d:\ q\mid P}}\frac{\tau(P/q)}{ad}\ll\frac1{qB}\sum_{d<2B}\sum_{m\le2A}\frac{\rho_{4d}(m)}m\ll\frac{\log A}q,
\]
by Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}(d) with $\kappa=4$ and Elsholtz--Tao's $(A,B)$
equal to our $(2B,2A)$, which satisfies $2\le2B\le2A$.

\emph{Small $q$, case $B>A$} (so $Z_0=B\ge16$ and $q\le B^{1/2}$).  Fix $a<2A$
with $\ell\nmid a$.  Then $q\mid P$ means $d\equiv d_0\pmod q$, where
$d_0\equiv-(4a^2)^{-1}$ and $0<d_0<q$.  Write $d=qd'+d_0$ with
$1\le d'\le N'':=\lfloor2B/q\rfloor$ (as $d\ge B\ge q$).  Then
$P/q=4a^2d'+b_a$ with $b_a=(4a^2d_0+1)/q\le4a^2$, and $\gcd(4a^2,b_a)=1$: $b_a$
is odd, and a prime dividing $a$ and $b_a$ divides $4a^2d_0+1$.  Both
coefficients are at most $16B^2\le N''^6$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}(c),
$\sum_{d'\le N''}\tau(4a^2d'+b_a)\ll N''\log N''\le(2B/q)\log(2B)$.  With
$1/(ad)\le1/(aB)$ we get
$\sum_{a,d:\,q\mid P}\tau(P/q)/(ad)\ll(\log B/q)\sum_{a\in[A,2A)}1/a\ll\log B/q$.

\emph{Sum over small $q$.}  $\sum_{q\le Z_0^{1/2}}(1/i)\log Z_0/q\ll\log Z_0\log\log Z_0$.

\emph{$\Sigma_I$.}  We have $1/N\le1/(acd)$ and $\sum_{c\in[C,2C)}1/c<2$, and each
$(a,d,e)$ with $e\mid P$ occurs at most once for each $c$.  So
$\Sigma_I\le\sum_C\sum_{(A,B)}2R(A,B)\ll\Lc\cdot\Lc^2\cdot\Lc\log\Lc$, as
$Z_0\le T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Charge moment; proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:ETall}]\label{thm:omega}
$S_0\ll\Lc^4$ and $\Omega_0\ll\Lc^4\log\Lc$.  Unconditionally,
$\Omega_0\le\Lc S_0$ and $\log S_0\le(\log2+o(1))\Lc/\log\Lc$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:block}, \eqref{eq:split} and Lemmas~\ref{lem:rough}
and~\ref{lem:smooth}.  Unconditionally, $h(M)\le\Lc$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:h}(c),
and $\log\tau^*(x)\le(\log2+o(1))\log x/\log\log x$ \cite[Thm.~317]{HW}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Assessment]\label{rem:mean}
Theorem~\ref{thm:omega} bounds the moment, not the $g/M$-weighted mean
$\Omega_0/S_0$ of $h(M)$; that would need $S_0\gg\Lc^4$, which we have not
proved.  Numerically (atoms with $D\le A_M$, $T=10^4,10^5,10^6$) the mean is
$2.16$, $2.40$, $2.62$, which tracks $\log\log T$, and the splitting
$h(eN)\le h(e)+h(N)$ loses about $2\%$.  This is evidence only.
\end{remark}

\section{The analytic input: Gallagher's theorem}\label{sec:analytic}

For a Dirichlet character $\chi$ put $\vartheta(x;\chi)=\sum_{p\le x}\chi(p)\log p$, and
let $E_0(\chi)=1$ if $\chi$ is the trivial character modulo $1$ and
$E_0(\chi)=0$ otherwise.  $\sum^*_{\chi\bmod q}$ is a sum over primitive
characters.

\begin{theorem}[Landau--Page; classical, {\cite[Ch.~14]{Dav}}, {\cite[Cor.~11.10]{MV1}}]\label{thm:xz}
There are absolute effective positive constants $c_1,c_2$ such that, for
every $Y\ge3$, $\prod_{q\le Y}\prod^*_{\chi\bmod q}L(s,\chi)$ has at most one zero
in the region $\Re s>1-c_1/\log Y$, $|\Im s|\le Y$.  If this zero $\beta_1$
exists, it is real and simple, and its character $\chi_1$ is quadratic.
Moreover, every real zero $\beta$ of $L(s,\chi)$, for $\chi$ a real primitive
character of conductor $q\ge3$, satisfies
$1-\beta\ge c_2^{-1}q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{-2}$.
\end{theorem}

The first part is the classical zero-free region with the Landau--Page
theorem.  The height restriction matters: without it the statement would be
a zero-free region of width $\asymp1/\log Y$ uniformly in $|\Im s|$, which is
not known.  The ``Exceptional Zero Statement'' (28.61)--(28.62) of
\cite{MV3}, which refers to \cite[Cor.~11.10]{MV1}, omits the height
restriction.  The second part is the effective Page-type bound
\cite[Ch.~14]{Dav}, \cite[(28.62)]{MV3}.  We use Theorem~\ref{thm:xz} only
for \emph{real} zeros: for the uniqueness of a real zero close to $1$, for the
quadraticity of its character, and for the lower bound for $1-\beta$.

\begin{theorem}[Gallagher {\cite{Gallagher}}; cited in the form of {\cite[Thm.~28.19]{MV3}}]\label{thm:G}
There are absolute constants $c\ge1$ and $\kappa_0\ge3$ with the following
property.  Let $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$ be fixed and $1<Q_G^{6c}\le x$.  Then
\[
 \sum_{q\le Q_G}\ \sideset{}{^*}\sum_{\chi\bmod q}\big|\vartheta(x;\chi)-E_0(\chi)x\big|
 \ \ll_\kappa\ x\exp\Big(-\frac{\log x}{\kappa\log Q_G}\Big)+\Big(\frac{\log x}{\log Q_G}\Big)^2\frac x{Q_G},
\]
unless $\prod_{q\le Q_G}\prod^*_{\chi\bmod q}L(s,\chi)$ has a real zero $\beta_1$ with
$1-\beta_1<1/(\kappa\log Q_G)$.  In that case the term of the exceptional
character $\chi_1$ on the left is replaced by
$|\vartheta(x;\chi_1)+x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1|$, and the right-hand side by
\[
 (1-\beta_1)(\log x)\Big(x\exp\Big(-\frac{\log x}{\log Q_G}\Big)+\frac{x\log x}{Q_G\log Q_G}\Big).
\]
\end{theorem}

\emph{Source caveat.}  We quote \cite{MV3}, an unpublished draft (third volume
of Montgomery--Vaughan), archived by us.  We have not seen Gallagher's paper
\cite{Gallagher}, so we cite it without a theorem number.  The printed proof
of \cite[Thm.~28.19]{MV3} has visible slips.  Its case split is at
$1-\beta_1<1/(3\kappa_0\log Q_G)$, so it establishes the statement for
$\kappa=3\kappa_0$.  The statement for every larger $\kappa$ follows from that
case.  Let $\kappa\ge3\kappa_0$, and suppose a real zero satisfies
$1/(\kappa\log Q_G)\le1-\beta_1<1/(3\kappa_0\log Q_G)$.  Then
$x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1\le2x\exp(-\log x/(\kappa\log Q_G))$, and
$(1-\beta_1)(\log x)\,x\exp(-\log x/\log Q_G)\ll x\exp(-\log x/(2\log Q_G))$.
So the exceptional form of the bound implies the non-exceptional one with
parameter $\kappa$.  We therefore fix
\[
 \kappa:=\max(3\kappa_0,\,1/c_1).
\]
An exceptional zero as in Theorem~\ref{thm:G} is then a real zero in the
region of Theorem~\ref{thm:xz} with $Y=Q_G$, so it is unique and $\chi_1$ is
real.  Write $C_G$ for the implied constant in Theorem~\ref{thm:G} with this
$\kappa$.

Gallagher's original statement may carry a range condition of the form
$\exp(\sqrt{\log x})\le Q_G\le x^{b}$; we have not checked this.  Our use is
robust to such a condition.  In the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer},
$\log x/\log Q_G=\kappa L\ge6c$, and $\log x\ge(\kappa L)^2$ by the
hypothesis of that theorem, so $Q_G\ge\exp(\sqrt{\log x})$.  The statement
itself is the standard form of Gallagher's prime number theorem for the
whole family of characters of conductor $\le Q_G$, which underlies the usual
proofs of Linnik's theorem.

\section{A linear transfer theorem}\label{sec:transfer}

\begin{theorem}[Linear transfer; proved modulo Theorems~\ref{thm:xz} and~\ref{thm:G}]\label{thm:transfer}
Let $T\ge1$ and $Q\ge2$ with $8\mid Q$.  Let $B(n)=\sum_{i\in I}c_i\mathbf 1[n\equiv b_i\ (d_i)]$ be a
finite real combination of \emph{consistent unit cells}: $\gcd(b_i,d_i)=1$ and
$b_i\equiv1\pmod{\gcd(d_i,Q)}$.  Assume that
$B(n)\le\mathbf 1[W(n)>T]$ for every $n\equiv1\pmod Q$ coprime to all $d_i$.  Put
$D=\lcm_id_i$, $N_0=\lcm(Q,D)$, $H=\{n\in(\mathbb Z/N_0)^\times:\ n\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Q)\}$, and let $\E_H$
be the mean over $H$.  Put
\[
 Z=Q\max_id_i,\quad \mu=\E_H B,\quad
 A=\frac{\E_H|B|}{\mu},
\]
and assume $\mu>0$, $A\le Z^{1/4}$, and the \emph{twist condition}: for every
real primitive character $\psi$ of conductor $f>1$ with $\gcd(f,Q)=1$ and
$f\mid d_i$ for some $i$,
\[
 |\E_H[B\psi]|\le\mu/4.
\]
There is an absolute effective constant $C_2$ such that, if
\[
 \log x\ \ge\ C_2(1+\log A)\log Z,
\]
then some prime $p\le x$ with $p\equiv1\pmod Q$ and $p\nmid D$ has $W(p)>T$.
\end{theorem}

In the previous version the cells were coprime to $Q$; then $\E_H$ is the
mean over the units modulo $D$, and $\mu=\sum_ic_i/\varphi(d_i)$.  Graded
quarantines produce cells whose moduli share primes with $Q$; consistency
means that each cell meets the class $1\bmod Q$.

Neither the $\ell^1$ mass $\sum_i|c_i|/\varphi(d_i)$ nor the number of cells
enters; only the Haar quantity $A\ge1$ does.

\begin{proof}
\emph{Parameters.}  Put $L=2c+\log(400C_G(A+1))$ and $Q_G=x^{1/(\kappa L)}$.  Since
$A\ge1$ we have $L\le C_3(1+\log A)$ for an absolute $C_3$.  If $C_2$ is large
enough, the hypothesis gives the following, using $A\le Z^{1/4}$ and $Z\ge2$:
$\log Q_G\ge(C_2/(\kappa C_3))\log Z$, hence $Q_G\ge Z$ and
$Q_G\ge10^4C_G(A+1)(\kappa L)^2$; $Q_G^{6c}\le x$ (as $\kappa L\ge6c$);
$\log x\ge16$; $\log x\ge(\kappa L)^2$ (as
$(1+\log A)^2\le(1+\log A)(1+\tfrac14\log Z)$); and $x\ge C_4AZ^4$, with
$C_4$ as large as needed below.

\emph{Character expansion.}  The function $f(n)=B(n)\mathbf 1[n\equiv1\ (Q)]$
on units modulo $N_0$ satisfies $f(n)=\sum_{\chi\bmod N_0}c(\chi)\chi(n)$.  The
reduction map $(\mathbb Z/N_0)^\times\to(\mathbb Z/Q)^\times$ is onto with kernel $H$, so
$|H|=\varphi(N_0)/\varphi(Q)$ and
\[
 c(\chi)=\frac1{\varphi(N_0)}\sum_{n\in H}B(n)\bar\chi(n)=\frac{\E_H[B\bar\chi]}{\varphi(Q)} .
\]
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $c(\chi)=0$ unless the conductor of $\chi$ divides $\lcm(d_i,Q)$ for
some $i$.  Indeed, by consistency and the Chinese remainder theorem,
$\mathbf 1[n\equiv b_i\ (d_i)]\mathbf 1[n\equiv1\ (Q)]$ is the indicator of one unit
class modulo $\lcm(d_i,Q)$, and its $\chi$-coefficient vanishes unless $\chi$ is
trivial on $\{n\equiv1\ (\lcm(d_i,Q))\}$.  So $c(\chi)\ne0$ implies that the
conductor of $\chi$ is at most $Qd_i\le Z\le Q_G$.
\item[(b)] $|c(\chi)|\le\E_H|B|/\varphi(Q)=A\mu/\varphi(Q)$, and
$c(\chi_0)=\mu/\varphi(Q)$ for the principal character $\chi_0$.
\item[(c)] Let $\chi$ be real with $c(\chi)\ne0$, and let $\psi^*$ be the real
primitive character inducing it, of conductor $f$.  Write $f=f_1f_2$, where
$f_1$ is composed of primes dividing $Q$ and $\gcd(f_2,Q)=1$, and
$\psi^*=\psi_1\psi_2$ accordingly.  A real primitive conductor is $2^ef'$
with $e\in\{0,2,3\}$ and $f'$ odd and squarefree; as $8\mid Q$, $f_1\mid Q$, so
$\psi_1=1$ on $H$ and $\chi=\psi_2$ on $H$.  If $f_2=1$, then $\chi$ is trivial on
$H$, so it is induced from a character modulo $Q$ and $c(\chi)=\mu/\varphi(Q)$.
If $f_2>1$, then $c(\chi)=\E_H[B\psi_2]/\varphi(Q)$.  A cell with $f_2\nmid d_i$
has $\E_H[\mathbf 1[n\equiv b_i\ (d_i)]\psi_2]=0$: there is a prime $p\mid f_2$ with
$p\nmid d_iQ$; on $H$ the residue of $n$ modulo $p^{v_p(N_0)}$ is uniform among
units and independent of $n$ modulo $N_0/p^{v_p(N_0)}$, and the $p$-component of
$\psi_2$ is a nontrivial character modulo $p$.  So $f_2\mid d_i$ for some $i$,
$\gcd(f_2,Q)=1$, and the twist condition gives $|c(\chi)|\le\mu/(4\varphi(Q))$.
\end{enumerate}
Let $\chi^*$ be the primitive character inducing $\chi$.  For a fixed modulus
the map $\chi\mapsto\chi^*$ is injective, so by (a) at most
$\sum_{r\le Z}\varphi(r)\le Z^2$ characters have $c(\chi)\ne0$.

\emph{The prime sum.}  Let
$S(x)=\sum_{p\le x,\ p\nmid N_0}f(p)\log p=\sum_\chi c(\chi)\vartheta(x;\chi)$.  Since
$|\vartheta(x;\chi)-\vartheta(x;\chi^*)|\le\log N_0\le\log Q+\log D\le2Z$ (as
$D\mid\lcm(1,\dots,\max d_i)$, so $\log D\le\psi(\max d_i)<1.04\max d_i$ by
Rosser--Schoenfeld \cite{RS}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sx}
 S(x)=\frac{\mu x}{\varphi(Q)}+\sum_{\chi:\,c(\chi)\ne0}c(\chi)\big(\vartheta(x;\chi^*)-E_0(\chi^*)x\big)+R_1,
\end{equation}
where $|R_1|\le Z^2\cdot(A\mu/\varphi(Q))\cdot2Z=2AZ^3\mu/\varphi(Q)$.  Here
$E_0(\chi^*)=1$ only for $\chi=\chi_0$, and $|R_1|\le\mu x/(400\varphi(Q))$ as
$x\ge800AZ^3$.

\emph{Case 0: no exceptional zero} in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:G}.  The
$\chi^*$ in \eqref{eq:Sx} are distinct primitive characters of conductor
$\le Q_G$.  By (b) and Theorem~\ref{thm:G}, the middle sum is at most
\[
 \frac{A\mu}{\varphi(Q)}\,C_Gx\Big(e^{-L}+\frac{(\kappa L)^2}{Q_G}\Big)
 \le\frac{\mu x}{\varphi(Q)}\Big(\frac1{400}+\frac1{10^4}\Big)\le\frac{\mu x}{200\,\varphi(Q)},
\]
using $\log x/\log Q_G=\kappa L\ge L$.  So $S(x)>0$.

\emph{Exceptional case:} a zero $\beta_1$ with character $\chi_1$ and
$1-\beta_1<1/(\kappa\log Q_G)$.  Put $u=(1-\beta_1)\log x$; then
$u<\log x/(\kappa\log Q_G)=L$, and $\beta_1>1/2$.  The replaced right-hand side
of Theorem~\ref{thm:G} is $C_Gux(e^{-\kappa L}+\kappa L/Q_G)$.  Since
$u\le\min(u,1)\,L$ (recall $L\ge2$), its product with $A\mu/\varphi(Q)$ is at
most
\[
 \frac{\mu x}{\varphi(Q)}\min(u,1)\cdot C_GAL\Big(e^{-\kappa L}+\frac{\kappa L}{Q_G}\Big)
 \le\frac{\mu x}{200\,\varphi(Q)}\min(u,1).
\]
Here $C_GALe^{-3L}\le C_GAe^{-L}\le1/400$, and
$C_GA\kappa L^2/Q_G\le10^{-4}$.  If no $\chi$ with $c(\chi)\ne0$ has
$\chi^*=\chi_1$, this bounds the middle sum of \eqref{eq:Sx}, and $S(x)>0$
as in Case~0.  Otherwise, by injectivity, exactly one such $\chi$ exists.  It
is real, because $\chi_1$ is; hence $c(\chi)$ is real, as $B$ and $\chi$ are.
Let $f_2$ be as in (c).
Its term is
$c(\chi)\big(\vartheta(x;\chi_1)+x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1\big)-c(\chi)x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1$.
The first part is covered by the bound just proved.

\emph{Case A: $f_2=1$.}  Then $c(\chi)=\mu/\varphi(Q)$, and the
conductor $q_1$ of $\chi_1$ divides $Q$, so $q_1\le Z$.  The main terms combine
to $\lambda\mu x/\varphi(Q)$ with $\lambda=1-x^{\beta_1-1}/\beta_1$.  Since
$1/\beta_1\le1+2(1-\beta_1)$, $1-e^{-u}\ge(1-e^{-1})\min(u,1)$ and
$2(1-\beta_1)e^{-u}=2ue^{-u}/\log x\le2\min(u,1)/\log x$,
\[
 \lambda\ \ge\ 1-e^{-u}-2(1-\beta_1)e^{-u}\ \ge\ (0.63-2/16)\min(u,1)\ \ge\ \min(u,1)/2 .
\]
So the error from Theorem~\ref{thm:G} is at most $\lambda\mu x/(100\varphi(Q))$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:xz}, $u\ge16(1-\beta_1)\ge16/(c_2Z^{1/2}(\log Z)^2)$, so
$1/\lambda\le2+c_2Z^{1/2}(\log Z)^2/8\le C_5Z$.  Hence
$|R_1|\le2AZ^3\mu/\varphi(Q)\le\lambda\mu x/(100\varphi(Q))$ once
$x\ge200C_5AZ^4$.  Altogether $S(x)\ge(1-2/100)\lambda\mu x/\varphi(Q)>0$.

\emph{Case B: $f_2>1$.}  By (c),
$|c(\chi)x^{\beta_1}/\beta_1|\le(\mu/(4\varphi(Q)))\cdot2x=\mu x/(2\varphi(Q))$, and
$S(x)\ge(1-1/2-1/200-1/400)\mu x/\varphi(Q)>0$.

In all cases $S(x)>0$.  So some prime $p\le x$ with $p\nmid N_0$ and
$p\equiv1\pmod Q$ has $B(p)>0$, and then $W(p)>T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:linear}
This is Linnik's theorem with weights.  Every character coefficient is
bounded by $\E_H|B|/\varphi(Q)$, not by the $\ell^1$ mass of a cell
representation.  Theorem~\ref{thm:G} controls all characters of conductor
$\le Q_G$ simultaneously, so the number of moduli $d_i$ never multiplies the
error.  In \cite{Paper1} and in the first version of this note, each
progression was instead treated separately with the Thorner--Zaman theorem
\cite{TZ}.  That costs $\log x\gg K\max(\log Z,K)$ with
$K=1+\log(\sum|c_i|/\varphi(d_i)\,/\,\mu)$, which here is of order
$(\log T)^{14}$ instead of $(\log T)^7$ in the setting of the previous
version.  No lower bound for $1-\beta_1$ is
needed in Case~B.  In Case~A we use the effective bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:xz} only to absorb the term $R_1$.
\end{remark}
