\title[An energy bound for avoidance indicators]{An energy bound for avoidance indicators,\\
and Fourier tails of DNFs over product spaces}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{Draft v1, 5 October 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Let $F$ be the indicator that none of a finite family of cylinder events
occurs, on an arbitrary finite product probability space, and let
$F=\sum_UF^{=U}$ be its Efron--Stein decomposition.  We prove that
\[
 \sum_U\Big(\prod_{v\in U}\lambda_v\Big)\|F^{=U}\|_2^2\ \le\ 1
\]
for all weights $\lambda_v\ge1$ such that $\prod_{v\in\supp E}\lambda_v\le2$ for every
event $E$.  There is no dependence on the number of events, the alphabet
sizes or the measure.  In particular every width-$k$ DNF
$g:\{\pm1\}^n\to\{\pm1\}$ satisfies $\sum_{|S|>t}\hat g(S)^2\le4\cdot2^{-(t+1)/k}$, under
every product measure; so $g$ is $\varepsilon$-concentrated up to degree
$k\log_2(4/\varepsilon)$.  The proof does not use a switching lemma.  It bounds the
Efron--Stein energies by signed cover counts of the events that hold at a
random point, writes the resulting hypergraph functional as the second moment
of an alternating union sum under a random deletion, and bounds that sum by
deletion--contraction.  Over general product spaces the base $2^{-1/k}$ is
sharp, and the threshold $2$ is necessary.  We also give a version with
weights that grow along a filtration of each coordinate, and we explain the
application that motivated it: the junta term in a lower bound for the
least ``multiplier modulus'' of the Erd\H{o}s--Straus equation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Let $\Omega=\prod_{v\in V_0}\Omega_v$ be a finite product of finite sets with a
product probability measure $\pi=\bigotimes_v\pi_v$.  A \emph{cylinder event}
fixes the values of the coordinates in a set $\supp E\subseteq V_0$, its
\emph{support}.  For a finite family $\cE$ of cylinder events let
\[
 F=F_\cE=\prod_{E\in\cE}(1-\mathbf 1_E)
\]
be its \emph{avoidance indicator}, and let $F=\sum_{U\subseteq V_0}F^{=U}$ be the
Efron--Stein (Hoeffding) decomposition of $F$ in $L^2(\pi)$
\cite{ES,Hoeffding}, \cite[\S8.3]{OD}.  For the uniform measure on
$\{\pm1\}^n$ this is the Fourier expansion, $F^{=U}=\hat F(U)\chi_U$.  We
study the weighted energy
\[
 G_F(\lambda)=\sum_{U\subseteq V_0}\lambda^U\|F^{=U}\|^2,\qquad \lambda^U=\prod_{v\in U}\lambda_v,\quad\lambda_v\ge1 .
\]
For constant weights $\lambda_v=\rho^2$ it is $\|T_\rho F\|^2$, where $T_\rho$
multiplies the component $F^{=U}$ by $\rho^{|U|}$; so we are asking for the
largest noise operator \emph{with $\rho>1$} that still maps every avoidance indicator
(of a suitable family) into the unit ball of $L^2$.  (It is not a contraction
in $L^2$: $\|T_\rho F\|>\|F\|$ for nonconstant $F$.)

\begin{theorem}[Energy bound]\label{thm:main}
Let $\lambda_v\ge1$ $(v\in V_0)$, and suppose that $\lambda^{\supp E}\le2$ for every
$E\in\cE$.  Then
\[
 G_F(\lambda)=\sum_U\lambda^U\|F^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ 1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

The hypothesis is on single events only.  There is no condition on the
number of events, on their masses or overlaps, on the alphabet sizes
$|\Omega_v|$ or on the measure.  The constant $2$ cannot be raised
(Proposition~\ref{prop:single}).  Since
$\sum_{U:\lambda^U>e^\tau}\|F^{=U}\|^2\le e^{-\tau}G_F(\lambda)$, the theorem is a family of
tail bounds, one for each choice of weights.  Constant weights give the
following.

\begin{corollary}[Energy tail]\label{cor:tail}
If every event of $\cE$ has $|\supp E|\le k$ $(k\ge1)$, then for
$f\in\{F,1-F\}$ and every integer $t\ge0$,
\[
 \energy(f;t):=\sum_{|U|>t}\|f^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ 2^{-(t+1)/k}\,\PP(F=0)\big(2-\PP(F=0)\big)\ \le\ 2^{-(t+1)/k}.
\]
\end{corollary}

A width-$k$ DNF is $g=2F-1$ for the family of its terms (with the convention
$-1=\mathrm{True}$), and the corollary becomes a statement about its Fourier
tail.

\begin{corollary}[Fourier tails of DNFs]\label{cor:dnf}
Let $g:\{\pm1\}^n\to\{\pm1\}$ be computed by a DNF of width $k$, and let $\pi$ be
any product measure on $\{\pm1\}^n$ (Efron--Stein components in $L^2(\pi)$;
Fourier coefficients if $\pi$ is uniform).  Then for every integer $t\ge0$
\[
 W^{>t}[g]:=\sum_{|U|>t}\|g^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ 4\,p(2-p)\cdot2^{-(t+1)/k}\ \le\ 4\cdot2^{-(t+1)/k},
\]
where $p=\PP[g=-1]$.
So $g$ is $\varepsilon$-concentrated up to degree $k\log_2(4/\varepsilon)$.  Moreover
$\sum_U2^{|U|/k}\|g^{=U}\|^2\le1+4p$, and the total influence
$\mathbf I[g]:=\sum_U|U|\,\|g^{=U}\|^2$ is at most $(4k/\ln2)\,p$.  The same holds for $q$-ary DNFs, with literals $x_v\in S_v$,
under any product measure on $\prod_v[q_v]$.
\end{corollary}

\emph{Comparison.}  The classical bound goes back to Linial, Mansour and Nisan
\cite{LMN}, via H{\aa}stad's switching lemma \cite{Hastad}: width-$w$ DNFs are
$\varepsilon$-concentrated up to degree $O(w\log(1/\varepsilon))$.  In the form of
O'Donnell's book \cite[\S4.4]{OD} it reads $W^{\ge t}[g]\le2\cdot2^{-t/(20w)}$
(we quote the constant $20$ from memory and could not re-check it).  It has
been used in this form, with an unspecified constant $C$, in later work on
DNFs, for instance by Mansour \cite{Mansour} (from memory) and by Lecomte
and Tan \cite[Fact~6]{LT}.  Corollary~\ref{cor:dnf} has the constant $1$ in the
exponent and holds for arbitrary product measures and alphabets.  Over
general product spaces the base $2^{-1/k}$ is sharp: no bound
$C\rho^{-t/k}$ with $\rho>2$ holds, and at most a factor of order $\sqrt{t/k}$
can be gained (Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}).  On the uniform cube we only
know that the true rate lies between $3^{-t/k}$ and $2^{-t/k}$
(Example~\ref{ex:parity}).  The influence bound is \emph{not} a gain: an
elementary certificate argument gives $\mathbf I[g]\le2kp$ on the uniform cube
and $\sum_v\|L_vg\|^2\le4kp$ under any product measure
(Remark~\ref{rem:influence}), so our $(4k/\ln2)p$ is weaker by the factor
$2/\ln2$, resp.\ $1/\ln2$.  Sharper bounds without the factor $p$ are known
(from memory: $\mathbf I\le2w$ is usually attributed to Boppana \cite{Boppana},
and we recall that Amano \cite{Amano} improved it to $\mathbf I\le w$).  We state the
influence bound only because it falls out.

The nearest prior art we know is the paper of Lecomte and Tan \cite{LT}.
They bound $|\hat g(S)|$ by $2^{|S|}$ times the probability that $S$ is covered
by the terms satisfied at a random point.  This is the device of our
Lemma~\ref{lem:cover}, but there are three differences: our cover counts are
\emph{signed}, and vanish as soon as a satisfied term misses $S$; we work in
$L^2$ with all levels and weights at once; and our degree concentration does
not go through the switching lemma.  The two combinatorial steps of the proof,
a polarization identity (Lemma~\ref{lem:polar}) and a deletion--contraction
bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:theta}), have no counterpart there.

\emph{What we checked.}  We have no internet access while writing this
draft, and our literature search is partial.  Earlier, \cite{LT} and Tal
\cite{Tal} were checked online; in \cite{Tal} we found no explicit-constant
tail bound for width-$w$ DNFs.  Everything else above is quoted from memory and is
marked as such.  We did not search systematically through the switching-lemma
literature (sharp variants of H{\aa}stad's lemma, Rossman's entropy proof),
the literature on Fourier growth, or work on noise operators with $\rho>1$.
We believe Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and the constant $1$ in
Corollary~\ref{cor:dnf} are new, but we make no priority claim.

\emph{Proof outline.}  Write $G_F=\sum_V\mu^V\|L_VF\|^2$ with $\mu_v=\lambda_v-1$ and
$L_V=\prod_{v\in V}(I-\E_v)$.  Section~\ref{sec:cover} bounds $\|L_VF\|^2$ by the
mean square of a signed count $N_{\Ho(x)}(V)$ of covers of $V$ by the events
that hold at $x$, and so reduces Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to the hypergraph
inequality $Q_\mu(\Ho)=\sum_V\mu^VN_\Ho(V)^2\le1$.  Section~\ref{sec:hyper} proves
it: $Q_\mu(\Ho)$ is the second moment of the alternating sum
$\Theta_\lambda(\Ho_P)=\sum_{\cJ\subseteq\Ho_P}(-1)^{|\cJ|}\lambda^{\bigcup\cJ}$ over the edges that
miss a random set $P$, and deletion--contraction gives $|\Theta|\le1$.  Nothing
in the proof is specific to two-point spaces or to the uniform measure.

\emph{Organisation.}  Section~\ref{sec:prelim} sets up notation.
Sections~\ref{sec:cover} and~\ref{sec:hyper} prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
Section~\ref{sec:dnf} proves the corollaries, Section~\ref{sec:sharp} discusses
sharpness, failed strengthenings and open problems, and
Section~\ref{sec:filtration} gives the filtration variant.
Section~\ref{sec:app} sketches the application to the Erd\H{o}s--Straus
equation \cite{Subexp}.  The exact-arithmetic script
\texttt{scripts/energy\_note\_check.py} checks the main inequalities on small
cases (Section~\ref{sec:check}).

\section{Setting and elementary facts}\label{sec:prelim}

\emph{Product spaces.}  Throughout, $\Omega=\prod_{v\in V_0}\Omega_v$ is a finite product
of finite sets, $V_0$ is finite, and $\pi=\bigotimes_v\pi_v$ is a product
probability measure.  We write $\PP$, $\E$, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and
$\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{L^2(\pi)}$.  For $W\subseteq V_0$ we write $x_W$ for the restriction
of $x\in\Omega$ to $W$, $\Omega_W=\prod_{v\in W}\Omega_v$ and $\pi_W=\bigotimes_{v\in W}\pi_v$.  A
\emph{cylinder event} is a set $E=\{x:\ x_v=\sigma_v\ (v\in\supp E)\}$ with
$\supp E\subseteq V_0$ and $\sigma\in\Omega_{\supp E}$.  A family $\cE$ of cylinder events is
finite, and repetitions are allowed.  Its avoidance indicator is
$F=\prod_{E\in\cE}(1-\mathbf 1_E)$, and $h=1-F$ is the indicator that some event occurs.
For weights $\lambda_v\ge1$ we put $\mu_v=\lambda_v-1\ge0$, $\lambda^U=\prod_{v\in U}\lambda_v$,
$\mu^U=\prod_{v\in U}\mu_v$, and $w_E=\lambda^{\supp E}$ (the \emph{weight} of $E$).

\emph{Efron--Stein decomposition} (classical; \cite{ES,Hoeffding},
\cite[\S8.3]{OD}).  For $\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ and $W\subseteq V_0$ let
$\E[\varphi\mid x_W]$ be the conditional expectation given the coordinates in
$W$, and
\[
 \varphi^{=U}=\sum_{W\subseteq U}(-1)^{|U\setminus W|}\,\E[\varphi\mid x_W].
\]
Then $\varphi=\sum_U\varphi^{=U}$, the components are mutually orthogonal,
$\varphi^{=U}$ depends only on $x_U$, and $\E_u\varphi^{=U}=0$ for $u\in U$, where
$\E_u$ averages over the coordinate $u$ (with respect to $\pi_u$).  Put
$L_u=I-\E_u$ and $L_V=\prod_{u\in V}L_u$.  The $L_u$ are commuting orthogonal
projections, and $L_V\varphi=\sum_{U\supseteq V}\varphi^{=U}$.  If $\cD$ is a down-closed
family of subsets of $V_0$, then $\varphi^{\cD}=\sum_{U\in\cD}\varphi^{=U}$ is a linear
combination of the functions $\E[\varphi\mid x_W]$, $W\in\cD$ (by M\"obius
inversion), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \E(\varphi-\varphi^{\cD})^2=\sum_{U\notin\cD}\|\varphi^{=U}\|^2 .
\end{equation}
For $\cD=\{U:\ |U|\le t\}$ this is the \emph{energy above level $t$},
$\energy(\varphi;t)$.  It is the least value of $\E(\varphi-g)^2$ over sums $g$
of functions that each depend on at most $t$ coordinates.  For $\pm1$-valued
$g$ we also write $W^{>t}[g]=\energy(g;t)$, as in \cite{OD}.

\begin{definition}[Energy generating function]
For weights $\lambda_v\ge1$,
\[
 G_\varphi(\lambda)=\sum_{U\subseteq V_0}\lambda^U\|\varphi^{=U}\|^2 .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Elementary identities]\label{lem:ident}
For every $\varphi$ and all weights $\lambda_v\ge1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $G_\varphi(\lambda)=\sum_{V\subseteq V_0}\mu^V\|L_V\varphi\|^2$;
\item[(b)] $\sum_{U:\ \lambda^U>e^\tau}\|\varphi^{=U}\|^2\le e^{-\tau}G_\varphi(\lambda)$ for every real $\tau$;
\item[(c)] $G_{1-\varphi}=1-2\E\varphi+G_\varphi$; in particular $G_F\le1$ if and only if
$G_h\le2\E h$;
\item[(d)] if $\varphi(x)=\prod_{v}\varphi_v(x_v)$ is a product, then
$G_\varphi(\lambda)=\prod_vG_{\varphi_v}(\lambda_v)$, where $G_{\varphi_v}(\lambda_v)=(\E\varphi_v)^2+\lambda_v\operatorname{Var}\varphi_v$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Expand $\lambda^U=\prod_{v\in U}(1+\mu_v)=\sum_{V\subseteq U}\mu^V$ and use
$\|L_V\varphi\|^2=\sum_{U\supseteq V}\|\varphi^{=U}\|^2$.  (b) is Markov's inequality.
(c) $(1-\varphi)^{=U}=-\varphi^{=U}$ for $U\ne\emptyset$ and $(1-\varphi)^{=\emptyset}=1-\E\varphi$.
(d) For a product, $\varphi^{=U}=\prod_{v\in U}(\varphi_v-\E\varphi_v)\prod_{v\notin U}\E\varphi_v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A single event; the threshold $2$]\label{prop:single}
Let $E$ fix $x_v=\sigma_v$ on $\supp E$, put $p_v=\pi_v(\sigma_v)$ and
$\pi_E=\PP(E)=\prod_{v\in\supp E}p_v$.  Then
\[
 G_{1-\mathbf 1_E}(\lambda)=1-2\pi_E+\pi_E\prod_{v\in\supp E}\big(\lambda_v-\mu_vp_v\big).
\]
Hence, for fixed weights with $w_E>2$, $G_{1-\mathbf 1_E}(\lambda)>1$ as soon as all
$p_v>0$ are small enough.  So the constant $2$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
cannot be replaced by any larger number, even for a single event.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:ident}(d), with $\varphi_v=\mathbf 1[x_v=\sigma_v]$,
$G_{\mathbf 1_E}=\prod_v(p_v^2+\lambda_vp_v(1-p_v))=\pi_E\prod_v(\lambda_v-\mu_vp_v)$.  Then use
Lemma~\ref{lem:ident}(c).  The product tends to $w_E$ as $p\to0$.
\end{proof}

On the uniform cube $\{\pm1\}^n$ the single-event condition is the weaker
$\prod_v(1+\lambda_v)/2\le2$.  The threshold $w_E\le2$ is forced by large
alphabets or strongly biased measures.

\section{The cover bound}\label{sec:cover}

A \emph{hypergraph} $\Ho$ is a finite set of subsets (\emph{edges}) of $V_0$; the
empty edge is allowed.  For $V\subseteq V_0$ let
\[
 N_\Ho(V)=\sum_{\cJ\subseteq\Ho:\ \bigcup_{J\in\cJ}(J\cap V)=V}(-1)^{|\cJ|},\qquad
 Q_\mu(\Ho)=\sum_{V\subseteq V_0}\mu^V\,N_\Ho(V)^2 .
\]
So $N_\Ho(V)$ is a signed count of the subfamilies whose traces cover $V$.  It
depends only on the multiset of traces $J\cap V$.  It does not change if an
edge is replaced by several edges with the same trace: a class of $m\ge1$
edges with equal traces contributes $\sum_{j\ge1}\binom mj(-1)^j=-1$ to the choice
of whether the class meets $\cJ$, exactly like a single edge.  If some edge
misses $V$, then $N_\Ho(V)=0$: toggling that edge is a sign-reversing
involution on the covering subfamilies.  Also $N_\Ho(\emptyset)=\mathbf 1[\Ho=\emptyset]$.
For example, $Q_\mu(\{J\})=\lambda^J-1$, and for pairwise disjoint edges
$Q_\mu(\{J_1,\dots,J_m\})=\prod_i(\lambda^{J_i}-1)$.

\begin{lemma}[Cover bound]\label{lem:cover}
For every family $\cE$ of cylinder events and every $V\subseteq V_0$,
\[
 \|L_VF\|^2\ \le\ \E_x\big[N_{\Ho(x)}(V)^2\big],\qquad
 \Ho(x)=\{\supp E:\ E\in\cE,\ x\in E\}.
\]
Consequently $G_F(\lambda)\le\E_x\big[Q_\mu(\Ho(x))\big]$ for all weights $\lambda_v\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $x=(\sigma,y)$ with $\sigma\in\Omega_V$ and $y\in\Omega_{V^c}$, $V^c=V_0\setminus V$.  The
operator $L_V$ acts on the $V$-coordinates only, so
$\|L_VF\|^2=\E_y\|L_Vg_y\|^2_{L^2(\pi_V)}$ with $g_y=F(\cdot,y)$.  Now $g_y$ is the
avoidance indicator on $\Omega_V$ of the \emph{restricted} events: those $E\in\cE$
whose conditions off $V$ hold at $y$, restricted to $\supp E\cap V$.  Expand
\[
 g_y=\prod_E(1-\mathbf 1_{E|_y})=\sum_{\cJ}(-1)^{|\cJ|}\mathbf 1_\cJ
\]
over subfamilies $\cJ$ of restricted events, where $\mathbf 1_\cJ$ is the indicator
of their intersection.  This intersection is a cylinder on $\Omega_V$ with
support $\bigcup_{E\in\cJ}(\supp E\cap V)$, or empty.  The operator $L_V$ kills every
function that does not depend on all coordinates in $V$, hence every
cylinder whose support is a proper subset of $V$.  A cylinder with support
$V$ is a point indicator $\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma\}}$.  Therefore $L_Vg_y=L_Vc_y$, where
\[
 c_y(\sigma)=\sum_{\cJ:\ \supp\mathbf 1_\cJ=V,\ \mathbf 1_\cJ=\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma\}}}(-1)^{|\cJ|}.
\]
Since $L_V$ is an orthogonal projection in $L^2(\pi_V)$,
$\|L_Vc_y\|^2\le\E_\sigma c_y(\sigma)^2$.  The subfamilies counted in $c_y(\sigma)$ are
exactly the subfamilies of events that hold at $x=(\sigma,y)$ whose traces on
$V$ cover $V$.  Two events that hold at $x$ and have the same support are the
same cylinder.  By the remark on repeated traces, $c_y(\sigma)=N_{\Ho(x)}(V)$.
Taking $\E_y$ gives the first claim.  For the second, multiply by $\mu^V$, sum
over $V$ and use Lemma~\ref{lem:ident}(a).
\end{proof}

The lemma is an equality for $V=\emptyset$ (both sides equal $\E F$).  The only
loss is the projection step $\|L_Vc\|\le\|c\|$.  Two features matter below.
First, the suppression is built in: $N_{\Ho(x)}(V)=0$ as soon as some event
that holds at $x$ misses $V$.  Second, the right-hand side no longer involves
the measure, except through the law of $\Ho(x)$; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} will
follow from a bound on $Q_\mu$ that is uniform over all hypergraphs whose edges
have weight at most $2$.

\begin{remark}[Unsigned counts are too weak]\label{rem:unsigned}
Lecomte and Tan \cite[Fact~9]{LT} bound $|\hat g(S)|$ for a DNF $g$ on the uniform
cube by $2^{|S|}\PP_x[S$ is covered by the terms satisfied by $x]$; we quote
the form from \cite{LT}, checked earlier.  The indicator ``$S$ is covered'' does
not see cancellations.  For example, if $\Ho(x)$ contains a singleton $\{u\}$
and an edge containing a set $V\not\ni u$, then $N_{\Ho(x)}(V)=0$, while
$\mathbf 1[V\text{ is covered}]=1$, so the bound of \cite{LT} charges $2^{|V|}$ at such
points.
\end{remark}

\section{The hypergraph inequality}\label{sec:hyper}

For a finite multiset $\cC$ of subsets of $V_0$ (empty members allowed) put
\[
 \Theta_\lambda(\cC)=\sum_{\cJ\subseteq\cC}(-1)^{|\cJ|}\lambda^{\bigcup\cJ}\qquad(\Theta_\lambda(\emptyset)=1),
\]
the sum running over sub-multisets (members are distinguishable).

\begin{lemma}[Polarization]\label{lem:polar}
Let $\lambda_v\ge1$, $\mu_v=\lambda_v-1$, and let $P\subseteq V_0$ be random with
$\PP(v\in P)=\mu_v/\lambda_v$, independently for different $v$.  For every
hypergraph $\Ho$,
\[
 Q_\mu(\Ho)=\E_P\big[\Theta_\lambda(\Ho_P)^2\big],\qquad \Ho_P=\{J\in\Ho:\ J\cap P=\emptyset\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\tau(R)=\mathbf 1[R\cap J\ne\emptyset\ \text{for all}\ J\in\Ho]$ for $R\subseteq V_0$, and
$\hat\tau(V)=\sum_{R\subseteq V}(-1)^{|V\setminus R|}\tau(R)$.  By inclusion--exclusion,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tauIE}
 \tau(R)=\sum_{\cJ\subseteq\Ho}(-1)^{|\cJ|}\mathbf 1\big[R\cap{\textstyle\bigcup}\cJ=\emptyset\big].
\end{equation}
The M\"obius transform of $R\mapsto\mathbf 1[R\cap U=\emptyset]$ is
$V\mapsto(-1)^{|V|}\mathbf 1[V\subseteq U]$, so $\hat\tau(V)=(-1)^{|V|}N_\Ho(V)$.  Let
\[
 \mathcal T(p)=\sum_V\hat\tau(V)p^V=\sum_R\tau(R)\prod_{v\in R}p_v\prod_{v\notin R}(1-p_v)
\]
be the multilinear extension of $\tau$.  If the $p_v$ are independent random
variables with mean $0$ and variance $\mu_v$, then distinct monomials are
orthogonal and $\E[\mathcal T(p)^2]=\sum_V\mu^V\hat\tau(V)^2=Q_\mu(\Ho)$, whatever the
laws of the $p_v$.  Take $p_v=1$ with probability $\mu_v/\lambda_v$ and $p_v=-\mu_v$
otherwise.  The mean is $\mu_v/\lambda_v-\mu_v/\lambda_v=0$ and the variance is
$\mu_v/\lambda_v+\mu_v^2/\lambda_v=\mu_v$.  Let $P=\{v:\ p_v=1\}$.  In the second form of $\mathcal T$,
the factor $1-p_v$ vanishes for $v\in P$, so only the $R\supseteq P$ contribute;
writing $R=P\cup B$ with $B\subseteq P^c$, and using $1-p_v=\lambda_v$ for $v\notin P$,
\[
 \mathcal T(p)=\sum_{B\subseteq P^c}(-\mu)^B\lambda^{P^c\setminus B}\,\tau(P\cup B).
\]
An edge that meets $P$ meets $P\cup B$, so \eqref{eq:tauIE} for the hypergraph
$\Ho_P$ gives $\tau(P\cup B)=\sum_{\cJ\subseteq\Ho_P}(-1)^{|\cJ|}\mathbf 1[B\cap\bigcup\cJ=\emptyset]$.  For
$U\subseteq P^c$,
\[
 \sum_{B\subseteq P^c\setminus U}(-\mu)^B\lambda^{P^c\setminus B}=\lambda^U\prod_{v\in P^c\setminus U}(\lambda_v-\mu_v)=\lambda^U .
\]
Hence $\mathcal T(p)=\sum_{\cJ\subseteq\Ho_P}(-1)^{|\cJ|}\lambda^{\bigcup\cJ}=\Theta_\lambda(\Ho_P)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Matching bound]\label{lem:theta}
If every member of $\cC$ has $w_J:=\lambda^J\le2$, then for every matching
$\cM\subseteq\cC$ \textup{(}pairwise disjoint members\textup{)}
\[
 |\Theta_\lambda(\cC)|\le\prod_{J\in\cM}(w_J-1);\qquad\text{in particular }|\Theta_\lambda(\cC)|\le1 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $v\in V_0$ let $\cC-v$ be the multiset of members not containing $v$, and
$\cC/v=\{J\setminus\{v\}:\ J\in\cC\}$ (as a multiset).  Split the sum over $\cJ$
according to whether $\cJ$ contains a member through $v$.  If it does, then
$\lambda^{\bigcup\cJ}=\lambda_v\lambda^{\bigcup(\cJ/v)}$; the terms without such a member form
$\Theta(\cC-v)$ both in $\Theta(\cC)$ and in $\Theta(\cC/v)$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dc}
 \Theta(\cC)=\Theta(\cC-v)+\lambda_v\big[\Theta(\cC/v)-\Theta(\cC-v)\big]=\lambda_v\Theta(\cC/v)-\mu_v\Theta(\cC-v).
\end{equation}
Both families again have all weights at most $2$, since $w_{J\setminus v}=w_J/\lambda_v$ for
$J\ni v$ and the other members are unchanged.
We induct on $|\bigcup\cC|$.  If $\emptyset\in\cC$, then $\Theta(\cC)=0$ (toggling the
empty member pairs the terms), and the claim holds.  If $\cC=\emptyset$, then
$\Theta=1$ and $\cM=\emptyset$.  This covers the base case $\bigcup\cC=\emptyset$.  If
$\cM=\emptyset$ and $\cC\ne\emptyset$, it suffices to prove the bound for $\cM=\{J\}$ with
any $J\in\cC$, since $w_J-1\le1$.  So let $J_0\in\cM$ be nonempty, $v\in J_0$, and
$\Pi'=\prod_{J\in\cM\setminus\{J_0\}}(w_J-1)$.  In $\cC/v$ the family
$(\cM\setminus\{J_0\})\cup\{J_0\setminus\{v\}\}$ is a matching, so by induction
$|\Theta(\cC/v)|\le(w_{J_0}/\lambda_v-1)\Pi'$.  In $\cC-v$ the family $\cM\setminus\{J_0\}$ is a
matching, so $|\Theta(\cC-v)|\le\Pi'$.  By \eqref{eq:dc},
$|\Theta(\cC)|\le(w_{J_0}-\lambda_v)\Pi'+\mu_v\Pi'=(w_{J_0}-1)\Pi'$.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis $w_J\le2$ enters only through $w_J-1\le1$ and its stability
under contraction.  Without it $\Theta$ is unbounded: for $m$ disjoint
singletons of weight $\lambda$, $\Theta=(1-\lambda)^m$.

\begin{theorem}[Hypergraph inequality]\label{thm:Q}
If every edge of a hypergraph $\Ho$ has $w_J=\lambda^J\le2$, then
\[
 Q_\mu(\Ho)\le\prod_{J\in\cM}(w_J-1)\quad\text{for every matching }\cM\subseteq\Ho;
\]
in particular $Q_\mu(\Ho)\le1$, and $Q_\mu(\Ho)\le\min_{J\in\Ho}w_J-1$ if $\Ho\ne\emptyset$.
Equality holds if $\Ho=\cM$ is itself a matching.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:polar}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:theta} applied to $\Ho_P$ with
the matching $\cM\cap\Ho_P$,
$Q_\mu(\Ho)\le\E_P\prod_{J\in\cM\cap\Ho_P}(w_J-1)^2$.  The members of $\cM$ are disjoint,
so the events $\{J\cap P=\emptyset\}$, $J\in\cM$, are independent, with probabilities
$\prod_{v\in J}(1-\mu_v/\lambda_v)=1/w_J$.  Hence
\[
 Q_\mu(\Ho)\le\prod_{J\in\cM}\Big[\Big(1-\frac1{w_J}\Big)+\frac{(w_J-1)^2}{w_J}\Big]=\prod_{J\in\cM}(w_J-1).
\]
The equality case is the example after the definition of $Q_\mu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:cover}, $G_F(\lambda)\le\E_x[Q_\mu(\Ho(x))]$.  Every edge of $\Ho(x)$
is the support of an event, so it has weight at most $2$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:Q} gives $Q_\mu(\Ho(x))\le1$ for every $x$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:Q} with the matching bound gives slightly more:
$G_F(\lambda)\le\E_x\min_{\cM}\prod_{J\in\cM}(w_J-1)$, the minimum over matchings in $\Ho(x)$.
It would be interesting to know whether the minimum over matchings can be
replaced by a fractional-matching version (Conjecture~\ref{conj:fm}).

\section{Energy tails and DNFs}\label{sec:dnf}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:tail}]
Take $\lambda_v=\lambda=2^{1/k}$ for all $v$; then $w_E=\lambda^{|\supp E|}\le2$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives $G_F(\lambda)\le1$.  Let $a=\PP(F=0)=\E h$.  Since
$\|F^{=\emptyset}\|^2=(\E F)^2=(1-a)^2$,
\[
 \sum_{U\ne\emptyset}\lambda^{|U|}\|F^{=U}\|^2\le1-(1-a)^2=a(2-a).
\]
For $t\ge0$ every $U$ with $|U|>t$ is nonempty and has $\lambda^{|U|}\ge2^{(t+1)/k}$, so
$\energy(F;t)\le2^{-(t+1)/k}a(2-a)$.  Finally $(1-F)^{=U}=-F^{=U}$ for $U\ne\emptyset$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:dnf}]
Let $g$ be computed by the DNF $T_1\vee\dots\vee T_m$ with $-1=\mathrm{True}$, and let
$E_i$ be the set of inputs that satisfy the term $T_i$.  A term is a conjunction
of at most $k$ literals on distinct variables (a term containing $x_v$ and
$\bar x_v$ is never satisfied and may be dropped), so $E_i$ is a cylinder event
with $|\supp E_i|\le k$.  Then $g=2F-1$ with $F=\prod_i(1-\mathbf 1_{E_i})$, and
$p=\PP[g=-1]=\PP(F=0)$.  As $g^{=U}=2F^{=U}$ for $U\ne\emptyset$, the first claim is
Corollary~\ref{cor:tail} multiplied by $4$, and $p(2-p)\le1$.  If
$4\cdot2^{-(d+1)/k}\le\varepsilon$, i.e.\ $d+1\ge k\log_2(4/\varepsilon)$, then $W^{>d}[g]\le\varepsilon$; the
least such integer $d$ is at most $k\log_2(4/\varepsilon)$.

For the weighted sum, $\|g^{=\emptyset}\|^2=(1-2p)^2$ and, with $\lambda=2^{1/k}$,
\[
 \sum_{U\ne\emptyset}\lambda^{|U|}\|g^{=U}\|^2=4(G_h-p^2),
\]
where $G_h=G_F-1+2p\le2p$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ident}(c).  So the sum is at most $(1-2p)^2+8p-4p^2=1+4p$.
For the influence, $\sum_U(\lambda^{|U|}-1)\|F^{=U}\|^2=G_F-\E F\le p$, and
$\lambda^{|U|}-1\ge|U|\ln\lambda=|U|\ln2/k$.  Hence
\[
 \mathbf I[g]=\sum_U|U|\,\|g^{=U}\|^2=4\sum_U|U|\,\|F^{=U}\|^2\le(4k/\ln2)\,p .
\]

For a $q$-ary DNF, a term $\bigwedge_{v\in S}(x_v\in S_v)$ is the disjoint union of the
cylinder events $\{x_S=\sigma\}$, $\sigma\in\prod_{v\in S}S_v$, which all have support $S$.
Replacing each term by these events changes neither $F$ nor the supports.
\end{proof}

For a width-$k$ CNF $h$, $-h$ is a width-$k$ DNF and $W^{>t}[h]=W^{>t}[-h]$; so the
same bounds hold with $p=\PP[h=+1]$ (the probability that $h$ is false), and
the bound $4\cdot2^{-(t+1)/k}$ holds as stated.  Here
$\mathbf I[g]=\sum_U|U|\,\|g^{=U}\|^2=\sum_v\|L_vg\|^2$ is the Efron--Stein total influence; it is
the usual total influence for the uniform measure.

\begin{remark}[The influence bound is elementary]\label{rem:influence}
Let $g$ be a width-$k$ DNF and $p=\PP[g=-1]$.  If $g(x)=-1$, fix a term satisfied at
$x$; changing a coordinate outside it leaves $g=-1$.  So the fibre of $x$ in
direction $v$ is non-constant for at most $k$ coordinates $v$.  Since
$\|L_vg\|^2=\E\operatorname{Var}_{x_v}(g)$ and $\operatorname{Var}_{x_v}(g)=4q(1-q)\le4q$ on a non-constant fibre, with
$q$ the conditional probability of $g=-1$, we get $\sum_v\|L_vg\|^2\le4kp$ under any
product measure.  On the uniform cube, $\mathbf I[g]$ is the mean number of
sensitive coordinates; every sensitive edge has exactly one endpoint with
$g=-1$, so $\mathbf I[g]\le2kp$.  Corollary~\ref{cor:dnf} gives the weaker $(4k/\ln2)p$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Non-uniform widths]\label{rem:widths}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} also handles terms of very different widths, and
weights adapted to the variables.  For instance, if every term contains at
most $k_1$ variables from a set $A$ and at most $k_2$ from its complement,
then $\lambda_v=2^{1/(2k_1)}$ on $A$ and $2^{1/(2k_2)}$ off $A$ are admissible.  This
gives $\sum\|F^{=U}\|^2\le2^{-\tau}$ over the $U$ with
$|U\cap A|/k_1+|U\setminus A|/k_2>2\tau$.  The weights used in
Section~\ref{sec:app} are of this kind, with $\log\lambda_v$ proportional to the
logarithm of the size of the alphabet at $v$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What is not claimed]\label{rem:notclaimed}
Corollary~\ref{cor:dnf} concerns concentration on low \emph{degree} only.  It
says nothing about the number of significant coefficients (Mansour's
sparsity bound and Mansour's conjecture \cite{Mansour}, \cite{LT}), and it does
not give the random-restriction (decision-tree depth) form of the switching
lemma.  It does not apply to depth-$3$ circuits or to $\mathrm{AC}^0$; we do
not know whether the method extends to them.
\end{remark}

\section{Sharpness, limits of the method, open problems}\label{sec:sharp}

\begin{proposition}[The base $2^{-1/k}$ is sharp]\label{prop:sharp}
Fix $k\ge1$ and $j\ge1$, and let $t=jk-1$.  For every $\eta>0$ there is a family of
cylinder events of width $k$, on a product space with all alphabets of size
$2$ and a suitable product measure, or on $[q]^n$ with the uniform measure
and $q$ large, such that
\[
 \energy(F;t)\ \ge\ \frac{2^{-(t+1)/k}}{e\sqrt j}-\eta .
\]
Consequently, no bound $\energy(F;t)\le C\rho^{-(t+1)/k}$ with $\rho>2$ holds for all
width-$k$ systems, and the same is true for $W^{>t}[g]$ of width-$k$ DNFs
under biased product measures on $\{\pm1\}^n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $m$ events on pairwise disjoint sets $S_1,\dots,S_m$ of $k$ coordinates,
each fixing at every coordinate of its support a value of probability $p$
(the value $-1$ of a $p$-biased bit, or one value of $[q]$ with $q=1/p$), and
put $\pi=p^k$.  The factors $1-\mathbf 1_{E_i}$ are independent, so
$F^{=U}=\prod_i(1-\mathbf 1_{E_i})^{=U\cap S_i}$.  If $U$ is the union of $j$ of the $S_i$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:ident}(d) gives $\|F^{=U}\|^2=[\pi(1-p)^k]^j(1-\pi)^{2(m-j)}$, and
$|U|=jk$.  Hence
\[
 \energy(F;jk-1)\ \ge\ \binom mj\big[\pi(1-p)^k\big]^j(1-\pi)^{2(m-j)}
 \ \longrightarrow\ e^{-2s}\frac{s^j}{j!}
\]
as $p\to0$ with $m=\lfloor s/\pi\rfloor$.  With $s=j/2$ and $j!\le e\,j^{j+1/2}e^{-j}$ the
limit is at least $2^{-j}/(e\sqrt j)$, and $2^{-j}=2^{-(t+1)/k}$.
\end{proof}

So at most a factor of order $\sqrt{t/k}$ could be gained in
Corollary~\ref{cor:tail} over general product spaces.  Numerically, the
supremum over $m$ of $\energy(F;jk-1)\,2^{j}$ in this family is about
$2/\sqrt{2\pi j}$ for small $p$ (an earlier internal computation; we do not
prove it).  Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:single} this shows that both
the threshold $2$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and the base of
Corollary~\ref{cor:tail} are optimal at the level of generality of the
theorem.

\begin{example}[The uniform cube]\label{ex:parity}
On $\{\pm1\}^n$ with the uniform measure the extremal families above are not
available ($p=1/2$), and the truth may be better.  Let $g$ be the OR of $s$
parities on pairwise disjoint blocks $B_1,\dots,B_s$ of $k$ variables.  Each parity
is a width-$k$ DNF (with $2^{k-1}$ terms), so $g$ is a width-$k$ DNF.  If the
$i$-th parity is ``true'' when $\chi_{B_i}=-1$, then $F=\prod_i(1+\chi_{B_i})/2$, and
$\energy(F;jk-1)=4^{-s}\sum_{i\ge j}\binom si$.  Taking $s$ near $4j/3$ and using
Stirling's formula, $\max_s4^{-s}\binom sj=3^{-j+o(j)}$.  So the exponential rate of
the best width-$k$ bound on the uniform cube lies between $3^{-t/k}$ and
$2^{-t/k}$.  We do not know where.  In random and hill-climbing searches over
small Boolean systems (earlier internal computations) we found nothing
better than this example.
\end{example}

\emph{No one-event-at-a-time proof.}  It is natural to try to prove
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} by adding the events one at a time.  The following
example shows that this cannot work in the obvious form.  Adding a further
event of weight at most $2$ to a system can \emph{increase} $G_F$.

\begin{example}[Adding an event can increase $G_F$]\label{ex:mono}
Let $n=2$, $\Omega=[8]^2$ with the uniform measure, $\lambda_1=\lambda_2=\sqrt2$, and let
$\cE$ consist of the $49$ point events $\{x=(a,b)\}$ with $a\ne0\ne b$.  Let
$A=\{x=(0,0)\}$.  All events have weight $2$, and
$G_{F_\cE}<G_{F_{\cE\cup\{A\}}}$; numerically the values are $0.31571$ and
$0.31757$.  (The exact values are computed in Section~\ref{sec:check}.)  A
similar example exists on $[5]^3$, using the full-width cylinders with an
even number $\ge2$ of coordinates different from $0$.
\end{example}

Also the inequality fails beyond the threshold.  For $n$ uniform bits, the
OR of the $n$ literals $x_v=-1$ and $\lambda_v=4$ give $G_F=(5/4)^n$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ident}(d) and Proposition~\ref{prop:single}.  We state two
strengthenings that are supported by numerical evidence but are open.

\begin{conjecture}[Excess weights]\label{conj:cexp}
For every family of cylinder events and all weights $\lambda_v\ge1$,
\[
 G_F(\lambda)\le\prod_{E\in\cE}\big(1+\PP(E)(w_E-2)_+\big).
\]
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Fractional matchings]\label{conj:fm}
If every edge of $\Ho$ has $w_J\le2$, then $Q_\mu(\Ho)\le\prod_{J\in\Ho}(w_J-1)^{y_J}$ for
every fractional matching $y$ ($y_J\ge0$, $\sum_{J\ni v}y_J\le1$ for all $v$).
\end{conjecture}

Both were tested numerically on small random systems (several thousand
cases on $[q]^n$ with $q\le5$, $n\le9$; floating-point internal computations,
with a numerical linear program over $y$ for Conjecture~\ref{conj:fm}; not part
of this note), without a violation.  For Conjecture~\ref{conj:cexp} only the
weaker form $G_F\le\exp\big(\sum_E\PP(E)(w_E-2)_+\big)$ was tested.  For
Conjecture~\ref{conj:fm} equality (up to rounding) is frequent.  Example~\ref{ex:mono} shows that a proof of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:cexp} by induction on the number of events would
need a stronger induction hypothesis.

\emph{Open problems.}  (1) Determine the optimal rate for width-$k$ DNFs on
the uniform cube (Example~\ref{ex:parity}).  (2) Is there an analogue of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for depth-$3$ circuits, or for $\mathrm{AC}^0$, with a
weight condition on the bottom layer?  (3) Does the signed cover count give
sparsity bounds in the direction of Mansour's conjecture?

\section{Weights along a filtration}\label{sec:filtration}

In Theorem~\ref{thm:main} the cost of a component $F^{=U}$ is a product over
the coordinates in $U$.  In the application the natural cost of a function of
a residue $x_\ell\bmod\ell^f$ depends on \emph{how many} base-$\ell$ digits it reads,
and an event that fixes $x_\ell\bmod\ell^v$ fixes the first $v$ digits.  We now
prove the corresponding variant.

\emph{Setting.}  Let $\cP$ be a finite index set.  Each coordinate $X_\ell$
($\ell\in\cP$) is a tuple of independent \emph{digits} $(\delta_{\ell,i})_{i_0(\ell)\le i<f_\ell}$ with
arbitrary distributions on finite sets, and the coordinates are independent.
The Efron--Stein decomposition $F=\sum_UF^{=U}$ is taken over the digits, so $U$
is a set of pairs $(\ell,i)$; we write $U_\ell=\{i:(\ell,i)\in U\}$.  An event $E$ is a
\emph{digit-prefix event} if, for some integers $0\le v_\ell(E)\le f_\ell$, it fixes exactly the
digits $\delta_{\ell,i}$ with $i_0(\ell)\le i<v_\ell(E)$, for each $\ell$, to arbitrary values (no
digit of $X_\ell$ if $v_\ell(E)\le i_0(\ell)$).  The offsets are integers $i_0(\ell)\ge0$.  Such
an event is a cylinder event on the digits.  (The offset $i_0(\ell)$ allows digits
below $i_0(\ell)$ to be fixed in advance; they are not coordinates.)

\begin{theorem}[Filtration energy bound]\label{thm:filtration}
Let $\lambda_\ell\ge1$ $(\ell\in\cP)$, and suppose that every event of $\cE$ is a digit-prefix
event with $\prod_\ell\lambda_\ell^{2v_\ell(E)}\le2$.  Then
\[
 \sum_U\Big(\prod_{\ell:\ U_\ell\ne\emptyset}\lambda_\ell^{1+\max U_\ell}\Big)\|F^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ 1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

Applied directly, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with digit weights would charge a set
$U$ for each digit in it, and the event weights would grow with the number
of fixed digits.  Here the charge for $U_\ell$ depends only on its \emph{deepest}
digit, which is what a function of $X_\ell$ that reads $U_\ell$ costs, and the
hypothesis loses only the factor $2$ in the exponent.

\begin{proof}
Write $\Lambda_{\ell,i}=\lambda_\ell^{i+1}$ for $i\ge i_0(\ell)$, $\Lambda_{\ell,i_0(\ell)-1}=1$, and
$\mu'_{\ell,j}=\Lambda_{\ell,j}-\Lambda_{\ell,j-1}\ge0$ for $i_0(\ell)\le j<f_\ell$.  For such $j$ let
$P_{\ell,j}=I-\E_{B_{\ell,j}}$, where $\E_{B_{\ell,j}}$ averages over the \emph{tail block}
$B_{\ell,j}=(\delta_{\ell,i})_{i\ge j}$.  Averaging over a digit in $U$ kills $F^{=U}$, and
$F^{=U}$ does not depend on the digits outside $U$.  Hence $P_{\ell,j}F^{=U}=F^{=U}$ if
$U_\ell\ne\emptyset$ and $\max U_\ell\ge j$, and $P_{\ell,j}F^{=U}=0$ otherwise.  Call a set $V$ of
pairs $(\ell,j)$ a \emph{selection} if it contains at most one pair for each $\ell$,
and put $L'_V=\prod_{(\ell,j)\in V}P_{\ell,j}$.  Then $\|L'_VF\|^2=\sum_{U\succ V}\|F^{=U}\|^2$, where
$U\succ V$ means $\max U_\ell\ge j$ for all $(\ell,j)\in V$.  For fixed $U$, the sum of
$\mu'^V$ over the selections $V$ with $U\succ V$ factorises and telescopes:
\[
 \prod_\ell\Big(1+\sum_{i_0(\ell)\le j\le\max U_\ell}\mu'_{\ell,j}\Big)=\prod_{\ell:\,U_\ell\ne\emptyset}\Lambda_{\ell,\max U_\ell}.
\]
So the left side of the claim equals $\sum_V\mu'^V\|L'_VF\|^2$, the sum over
selections.

Fix a selection $V$ and pass to the coarser product space whose coordinates
are, for $(\ell,j)\in V$, the head $(\delta_{\ell,i})_{i<j}$ and the tail block $B_{\ell,j}$, and,
for the other $\ell$, the whole $X_\ell$.  There $L'_V$ is the operator $L_{V'}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} for the set $V'$ of tail blocks.  Each event $E$ is a
disjoint union of cylinder events on the coarse coordinates with the same
coarse support: enumerate the values of the unfixed digits of each coarse
coordinate that $E$ involves.  Splitting every event in this way does not
change $F$, and exactly one piece of $E$ holds at $x$ if $E$ does.  The trace
of the coarse support of $E$ on $V'$ is the set of the $B_{\ell,j}$ with $(\ell,j)\in V$
and $j<v_\ell(E)$.  Identify $B_{\ell,j}$ with the pair $(\ell,j)$ and put
$\hat E=\{(\ell,j):\ i_0(\ell)\le j<v_\ell(E)\}$; then this trace is $\hat E\cap V$.  Since
$N$ depends only on traces, Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} gives
\[
 \|L'_VF\|^2\le\E_x\big[N_{\hat\Ho(x)}(V)^2\big],\qquad \hat\Ho(x)=\{\hat E:\ E\in\cE,\ x\in E\}.
\]
Sum over selections and add the nonnegative terms of all other sets $V$ of
pairs: the left side of the claim is at most $\E_x[Q_{\mu'}(\hat\Ho(x))]$.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:Q} it remains to show that
$\prod_{(\ell,j)\in\hat E}(1+\mu'_{\ell,j})\le2$ for every event.  Fix $\ell$, write $\lambda=\lambda_\ell$,
$v=v_\ell(E)$, $i_0=i_0(\ell)$, and assume $v>i_0$.  Here $1+\mu'_{\ell,i_0}=\lambda^{i_0+1}$, and
$1+\mu'_{\ell,j}=1+\lambda^j(\lambda-1)$ for $j>i_0$ (if $v\le i_0$ the factor of $\ell$ is $1$).  The hypothesis gives $\lambda^{2v}\le2$, so
$\lambda^j\le\lambda^v\le\sqrt2<\lambda+1$ for $j<v$, which is equivalent to $1+\lambda^j(\lambda-1)\le\lambda^2$.
The factor of $\ell$ is therefore at most $\lambda^{i_0+1}\lambda^{2(v-1-i_0)}\le\lambda^{2v}$, and the
product over $\ell$ is at most $\prod_\ell\lambda_\ell^{2v_\ell(E)}\le2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Modulus-weighted tail]\label{cor:modtail}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration} let the $\ell\in\cP$ be primes, let $T>1$, $\Lc=\log T$,
and suppose that $\prod_\ell\ell^{v_\ell(E)}\le T$ for every event.  For a set $U$ of digits put
$m_U=\prod_{\ell:\,U_\ell\ne\emptyset}\ell^{1+\max U_\ell}$.  Then for every $\tau\ge0$
\[
 \sum_{U:\ m_U>e^\tau}\|F^{=U}\|^2\ \le\ 2^{-\tau/(2\Lc)} .
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $\lambda_\ell=2^{\log\ell/(2\Lc)}$.  Then
$\prod_\ell\lambda_\ell^{2v_\ell(E)}=2^{\log\prod_\ell\ell^{v_\ell(E)}/\Lc}\le2$, and the weight of $U$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration} is $2^{\log m_U/(2\Lc)}$; apply Lemma~\ref{lem:ident}(b).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The factor $2$ in the exponent]\label{rem:rho}
The proof gives more.  If $v>i_0$, the factor of $\ell$ is
\[
 \lambda^{i_0+1}\prod_{i_0<j<v}(1+\lambda^j(\lambda-1))\le\exp\Big(\lambda^{i_0+1}-1+\sum_{i_0<j<v}\lambda^j(\lambda-1)\Big)=\exp(\lambda^v-1).
\]
So it suffices that $\sum_\ell(\lambda_\ell^{v_\ell(E)}-1)\le\ln2$ for every event.  In
Corollary~\ref{cor:modtail} take $\lambda_\ell=2^{\log\ell/(\rho\Lc)}$ and put
$x_\ell=v_\ell(E)\log\ell/\Lc$, so that $\sum_\ell x_\ell\le1$.  The function $x\mapsto2^{x/\rho}-1$ is
convex and vanishes at $0$, so it is superadditive, and
$\sum_\ell(2^{x_\ell/\rho}-1)\le2^{1/\rho}-1$.  This is at most $\ln2$ when
$\rho\ge\rho_0:=1/\log_2(1+\ln2)=1.3163\ldots$, and then the tail in
Corollary~\ref{cor:modtail} is at most $2^{-\tau/(\rho\Lc)}$.
\end{remark}

\section{Application: Erd\H{o}s--Straus witness moduli}\label{sec:app}

This section explains where Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration} is used; the details
are in \cite{Subexp}, and nothing here is needed for the rest of the note.
The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture asserts that $4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ has a solution
in positive integers for every $n\ge2$; it is open.  For $M\equiv3\pmod4$ the
classical multiplier identity gives a solution for every $n$ with
$n\equiv-uv^{-1}\pmod M$ for some factorisation $uvw=(M+1)/4$.  Let $W(n)$ be the
least such $M$ ($+\infty$ if there is none).  In \cite{Subexp} it is shown, assuming
Gallagher's prime number theorem for the family of all characters and four
estimates of Elsholtz and Tao on divisor sums, that
$W(p)\ge\exp\big(c(\log p)^{1/5}(\log\log p)^{-1/5}\big)$ for infinitely many primes $p$.
This is an $\Omega$-result about one particular family of explicit
solutions, and says nothing about whether the conjecture holds.

The structure of the argument is as follows.  Fix $T$ and $\Lc=\log T$.  After a
\emph{quarantine} $n\equiv1\pmod Q$, the condition $W(n)>T$ holds as soon as $n$
avoids a finite family of congruence events $n\equiv b_i\pmod{r_i}$ with
$r_i\le T$, at most $T^2$ of them.  For $n$ distributed by Haar measure on the
unit group $\widehat{\mathbb Z}^\times$, conditioned on the quarantine, the residues
$X_\ell=n\bmod\ell^{f_\ell}$ at the primes involved are independent.  Each $X_\ell$ is
uniform on the units modulo $\ell^{f_\ell}$, or on a fibre $1+\ell^a\mathbb Z$ modulo $\ell^{f_\ell}$ if
$\ell$ is quarantined with $a=a_\ell\ge1$.  On these sets the uniform measure is the
product of the uniform distributions of the free base-$\ell$ digits (digit $0$
ranging over the nonzero residues in the unit case), so the digits are
independent coordinates.  Moreover an event $n\equiv b\pmod r$ is a digit-prefix event with
$\prod_\ell\ell^{v_\ell(E)}=r\le T$.  So the avoidance indicator $F$ is in the setting of
Corollary~\ref{cor:modtail}, and so is each conditioned function $F^{(j)}$
(the indicator that no earlier event occurs, restricted to the cylinder of
the $j$-th event): there the digit offsets $i_0(\ell)$ are raised.

To find a \emph{prime} $p$ with $W(p)>T$ one needs a minorant
$B\le F$ that is a combination of congruence classes of small modulus, has
mean comparable to $\E F$, and has small $\ell^1$ norm.  \cite{Subexp} uses the
one-sided $\ell^2$ sandwich of Bazzi \cite{Bazzi} in the form of Razborov
\cite{Razborov}:
\begin{align*}
 B&=1-\sum_i\mathbf 1_{E_i}\Big(1-\sum_{j<i}\mathbf 1_{E_j}u_j\Big)^2\le F,\\
 \E[F-B]&\le m^2\sum_j\PP(E_j)\,\E\big[(F^{(j)}-u_j)^2\big],
\end{align*}
for any functions $u_j$ of the coordinates not fixed by $E_j$.  The natural
choice of $u_j$ is the truncation of the Efron--Stein decomposition of $F^{(j)}$
to digit sets $U$ with $m_U\le e^\tau$.  By \eqref{eq:energy} it is a combination
of classes of modulus at most $e^\tau$, and by Corollary~\ref{cor:modtail} its
error is at most $2^{-\tau/(2\Lc)}$.  The sandwich needs the error to be about
$e^{-O(S)}m^{-2}$, where $S$ is the total mass of the events.  So
$\tau\ll\Lc(S+\Lc)$ suffices; in \cite{Subexp} $S\ll\Lc^4$ (using the
Elsholtz--Tao input), and the junta moduli satisfy $\log d_i\ll\Lc^5$.  The
quarantine costs $\log Q\ll\Lc^5\log\Lc$, and a linear transfer theorem based on
Gallagher's theorem then produces a prime $p$ with $\log p\ll\Lc^5\log\Lc$.

An earlier version of \cite{Subexp} encoded the residues in binary and used
the switching-lemma tail of LMN instead.  There the junta modulus picked up
factors for the width of the events and for the bit length, and it was one
of the two terms of the largest order (the other being the quarantine).  With
the energy bound the junta term is smaller than the quarantine term.  The
exponent $1/5$ needs two further ingredients of \cite{Subexp} (a graded
quarantine and a moment bound), and we do not attribute it to
Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration} alone.  Two features of the energy bound matter
here: the events are congruences modulo integers up to $T$, so the alphabets
are huge and the measure is far from the Boolean setting, and the cost of a
junta must be measured by its modulus rather than by the number of primes
it involves.

\section{Computational checks}\label{sec:check}

The proofs above are complete.  The script
\path{scripts/energy_note_check.py} is a sanity check of the statements on small cases.  It uses exact rational
arithmetic throughout; where a statement involves an irrational number, the
comparison is rewritten as an exact rational one (for instance
$W^{>t}\le c\,2^{-(t+1)/k}$ as $(W^{>t})^k2^{t+1}\le c^k$).  With seed $1$ it checks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)] Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, Lemma~\ref{lem:ident}(a) and
Lemma~\ref{lem:cover} (for every $V$) on $150$ random systems on
$\prod_v[q_v]$ ($n\le4$ coordinates, $q_v\le5$), with random rational product measures
and random rational weights in $[1,2]$;
\item[(B)] the identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:polar}, the two formulas for $N_\Ho$, and
the matching bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:theta} and Theorem~\ref{thm:Q}
(including the equality case) on $300$ random weighted hypergraphs on at most
$5$ vertices;
\item[(C)] Corollary~\ref{cor:dnf} for \emph{every} Boolean function on $4$ bits
under the uniform measure, with $k$ its minimal DNF width: the tail bound for
all $t$ (exactly), the bounds $1+4p$ and $(4k/\ln2)p$ at the endpoint
$\lambda=2^{1/k}$ (exactly for $k=1$, in floating point with slack $10^{-12}$
otherwise), and $G_F(\lambda)\le1$ at a rational $\lambda\le2^{1/k}$; the largest value
of $W^{>t}[g]\,2^{(t+1)/k}/(4p(2-p))$ is $0.680$;
\item[(D)] the same statements on $150$ random DNFs under biased product
measures on $\{\pm1\}^n$ and $q$-ary DNFs with set-valued literals;
\item[(E)] Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration} on $150$ random digit-prefix systems in
bases $2$ and $3$, with random offsets $i_0(\ell)\in\{0,1\}$ and random digit
distributions;
\item[(F)] the level polynomial of Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp} against brute
force, Corollary~\ref{cor:tail} along that family, the formula of
Example~\ref{ex:parity}, and the exact sign in Example~\ref{ex:mono}.  The
values $(\max_s\energy(F;jk-1))^{1/j}$ of Example~\ref{ex:parity} ($0.299$, $0.311$,
$0.319$ for $j=10,20,40$) are printed, not checked.
\end{enumerate}
The run takes about six minutes on one core; its output is in the file
\path{data/energy_note/check_seed1.txt}.
