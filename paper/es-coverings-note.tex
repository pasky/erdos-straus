\begin{abstract}
The Elsholtz--Tao families of residue classes on which the
Erd\H{o}s--Straus equation $4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ is solvable by polynomials
are clopen subsets of $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.  By compactness and
Dirichlet's theorem, all but finitely many primes of a given set admit
a finite covering by polynomial identities exactly when its set of accumulation points in
$\widehat{\mathbb Z}^\times$ contains no \emph{sterile} point, i.e.\ no
point outside every class.  The only sterile points known to us are the square
points, which give the classical Mordell--Schinzel obstruction.  We
collect what is known about coverings of sets of primes that avoid the
square points by a non-residue condition at one further prime~$r$.  For
Type~I coverings of the hard primes with least non-residue $r$, a covering
exists for $r=5$ (of height $10$), and the least covering height equals
the limsup of the Type~I depth under Hypothesis~H.  For $r=7$ every
covering has height greater than $1.32\cdot10^{12}$ (finite computation),
because of an explicit ``sign point'' at which the two lowest admissible
$2$-adic levels are proved empty.  For $r=13$ we give an explicit
finite-exception theorem, a repackaging of Salez's level sieve, and a
candidate sterile point that lies in no class of modulus at most $10^6$.
For $r=17$ the classes meeting the $17$-generic line are balls around
solutions of $4/17^K=1/x+1/y+1/z$; an explicit bound on the number of
such solutions would produce a sterile point and rule out every finite
polynomial covering of the Mordell-hard primes with least non-residue
$17$.  The bound needed is a discrete-logarithm counting problem, which we
leave open.  Nothing here proves or disproves the Erd\H{o}s--Straus
conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture (ES) asserts that for every integer
$n\ge2$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ES}
 \frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z
\end{equation}
has a solution in positive integers.  It is open; it suffices to treat
primes.  The oldest approach is by \emph{polynomial identities}: on a
residue class one writes down $x,y,z$ as polynomials in $n$.  Mordell's
identities settle every prime outside the six square classes
$1,121,169,289,361,529$ modulo $840$ \cite{Mordell}, and Mordell and
Schinzel showed that a square class can never be settled this way
\cite{Mordell,Schinzel}.  Elsholtz and Tao essentially classified the classes
that are solvable by polynomials, by seven families \cite[Prop.~1.9]{ET}, and
remarked that the odd-square vanishing ``rules out \dots\ a finite set of
covering congruence strategies'' \cite[p.~6]{ET}.  Salez \cite{Salez}
and, later, Mihnea and Bogdan \cite{MD} sieve with these classes to great depth.

This note looks at finite coverings from the profinite side.  Every ET
class is a clopen subset of $\Zh$, so for a closed target set compactness
reduces the existence of a finite covering to a pointwise question: is
every point in some class?  A unit point that lies in no ET class is
called \emph{sterile}.  Square points (squares of units at every prime)
are sterile; this is the Mordell--Schinzel obstruction.  Once the target
excludes them, for instance by asking $p$ to be a non-residue modulo one
further prime $r$, the question is whether \emph{other} sterile points
exist.  We consolidate the project's internally reviewed work on this
question.

\subsection*{Results}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Framework} (\S\ref{sec:frame}, \status{proved}).  For a set
$\mathcal P$ of primes with accumulation set $\mathcal P'\subset\Zhu$, a
finite covering of all but finitely many $p\in\mathcal P$ by primitive
classes solvable by polynomials exists if and only if $\mathcal P'$ has
no sterile point (Proposition~\ref{prop:compact}).  For a single explicit
point only one direction holds (Remark~\ref{rem:direction}).
\item \emph{Square points} (\S\ref{sec:square}).  Square points are
sterile (\status{proved}; classical).  The project's procedure-level
generalisation, Theorem~C of \cite{PS}, is stated with its
H-conditional label and hedged novelty.
\item \emph{Type~I and the least non-residue} (\S\ref{sec:typeI}).  For
the Type~I depth $\ckmin(p)$ of hard primes with least non-residue $r$:
$C(5)=10$ (\status{proved}); under Hypothesis~H the limsup $C^*(r)$
equals the least height of a finite Type~I covering, and it is infinite
if and only if a Type~I-sterile point exists (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}).  At
the sign point $\hat x_9$ no certificate has its smaller divisor below
$10^{12}$ (\status{certified}), so every Type~I covering for $r=7$ has
height $>1.32\cdot10^{12}$, and $C(7)>1.32\cdot10^{12}$ under H.  The
$2$-adic levels $5$ and $6$ are empty (\status{proved}), the Type~I-sterile
set is nowhere dense (\status{proved}), and a measure criterion would give
$C(7)=\infty$ under H (\status{proved} reduction; hypothesis open).
\item \emph{$r=13$} (\S\ref{sec:mordell}).  Primes with $(p/13)=-1$
satisfy ES outside six classes modulo $720720$ (\status{proved} by
certified finite computation; an explicit packaging of Salez's sieve).
The point $x^*$ with $x^*_{11}=x^*_{13}=2$ lies in no ET class of modulus
$\le10^6$ (\status{certified}); that it is sterile is a conjecture.
\item \emph{$r=17$} (\S\ref{sec:r17}).  On the $17$-generic line the
classes become balls around solutions of $4/17^K=1/x+1/y+1/z$
(\status{proved}); levels $\le5$ leave $67.7\%$ of each non-residue cell
uncovered (\status{certified}); an explicit tail bound would give a sterile
point and rule out every finite polynomial covering of the Mordell-hard
primes with $n_p=17$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:tail17}, \status{proved}
reduction; Corollary~\ref{cor:17}, \status{conditional}).  The missing
bound is a discrete-logarithm count (\status{Assessment}).
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:open} lists open problems.  No sterile point other than
a square point is known to us.  A sterile point would show that a particular
set of primes cannot be handled by finitely many polynomial identities;
it would say nothing about whether ES holds for those primes.

\section{Coverings as clopen classes}\label{sec:frame}

\subsection{The seven families}
We use the families of Elsholtz and Tao \cite[Prop.~1.9]{ET}.  Each is
indexed by a triple $\kappa$ of positive integers subject to a side
condition, and defines a set of residue classes:
\begin{center}
\small\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
family & parameters & side condition & class\\
\midrule
I1 & $a,d,f$ & $f\mid 4a^2d+1$ & $n\equiv-f \pmod{4ad}$\\
I2 & $a,c,f$ & $(4ac,f)=1$ & $n\equiv-f\ (4ac)$, $n\equiv -c/a\ (f)$\\
I3 & $c,d,f$ & $(4cd,f)=1$ & $n\equiv-f\ (4cd)$, $n^2\equiv-4c^2d\ (f)$\\
I4 & $a,b,e$ & $e\mid a+b$, $(e,4ab)=1$ & $n\equiv-1/e \pmod{4ab}$\\
II1 & $a,b,e$ & $e\mid a+b$, $(e,4ab)=1$ & $n\equiv-e\pmod{4ab}$\\
II2 & $a,d,f$ & $4ad\mid f+1$ & $n\equiv-4a^2d\pmod f$\\
II3 & $a,d,e$ & $(4ad,e)=1$ & $n\equiv-4a^2d-e\pmod{4ade}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
On its class each family carries explicit solutions.  With
$\pi^{I}(a,b,c,d)=(abdn,acd,bcd)$ and $\pi^{II}(a,b,c,d)=(abd,acdn,bcdn)$
\cite[\S2]{ET}, the remaining coordinates are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
I1 & $e=(4a^2d+1)/f$, $b=(ne+1)/(4ad)$, $c=(n+f)/(4ad)$\\
I2 & $d=(n+f)/(4ac)$, $b=(na+c)/f$\\
I3 & $a=(n+f)/(4cd)$, $b=\bigl(n+(n^2+4c^2d)/f\bigr)/(4cd)$\\
I4 & $c=(a+b)/e$, $d=(ne+1)/(4ab)$\\
II1 & $c=(a+b)/e$, $d=(n+e)/(4ab)$\\
II2 & $c=(f+1)/(4ad)$, $b=(nc+a)/f$\\
II3 & $c=(n+4a^2d+e)/(4ade)$, $b=ce-a=(n+e)/(4ad)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
and the solution is $\pi^I$ for I1--I4 and $\pi^{II}$ for II1--II3.

\begin{lemma}[\status{proved}; {\cite[\S\S2, 10]{ET}}]\label{lem:solve}
Let $\kappa$ be an admissible parameter triple of one of the seven
families and let $n\ge1$ be an integer in its class.  Then the coordinates
above are positive integers, and the resulting $(x,y,z)$ is a solution of
$4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ in positive integers.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity $4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ follows from the defining relations of
the varieties $\Sigma^I_n$, $\Sigma^{II}_n$ of \cite[(2.1)--(2.9),
(2.13)--(2.21)]{ET}.  Integrality on the class is a direct check; for
example in I3, $n\equiv-f\pmod{4cd}$ gives $a\in\Z$, the second
congruence gives $Y:=(n^2+4c^2d)/f\in\Z$, and $fY\equiv f^2\pmod{4cd}$
with $f$ invertible modulo $4cd$ gives $Y\equiv f\equiv -n$, so $b\in\Z$.
With $\kappa$ fixed, every coordinate is a polynomial in $n$ with
non-negative coefficients that is positive at $n=1$, so all coordinates
are positive for $n\ge1$.
\end{proof}

The positivity statement for all $n\ge 1$ (not only for large $n$) was
checked in the project's review of \cite{PM}; the degrees of
$(x,y,z)$ in $n$ are at most~$4$.

ET call a primitive residue class \emph{solvable by polynomials} if there
are polynomials $P_1,P_2,P_3$ with rational coefficients, taking positive
integer values at all sufficiently large $n$ of the class, with
$4/n=\sum 1/P_i(n)$ identically.  Lemma~\ref{lem:solve} says that every
primitive class contained in one of the seven families is solvable by
polynomials.  We use the converse in the following form.

\begin{citedthm}[{\cite[Prop.~1.9]{ET}}]\label{cit:ET19}
If a primitive residue class is solvable by polynomials, then all
sufficiently large primes in it lie in the union of finitely many classes
of the seven families.
\end{citedthm}

(ET state this separately for Type~I and Type~II solvable classes, with
the families I1--I4 and II1--II3 respectively; every solvable primitive
class is of one of the two types \cite[p.~8]{ET}.)

\subsection{Profinite language}
Let $\Zh=\prod_q\Z_q$ be the profinite completion of $\Z$, with unit group
$\Zhu=\prod_q\Z_q^\times$.  Every class of the table is defined by
congruences modulo an integer $M(\kappa)$ (for I3, modulo $4cdf$); we
write $\Cl(\kappa)\subset\Zh$ for the set of $x\in\Zh$ satisfying them.
It is open and closed (\emph{clopen}), and an integer $n$ lies in
$\Cl(\kappa)$ exactly when it lies in the class.  We call $\Cl(\kappa)$
an \emph{ET class}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:sterile}
A point $x\in\Zhu$ is \emph{sterile} if it lies in no ET class.  For a
subfamily $\mathcal K$ of parameter triples, $x$ is
\emph{$\mathcal K$-sterile} if it lies in no $\Cl(\kappa)$,
$\kappa\in\mathcal K$.
\end{definition}

Let $\mathcal P$ be an infinite set of primes and $\mathcal P'$ its set of
accumulation points in $\Zh$.

\begin{lemma}[\status{proved}]\label{lem:accum}
$\mathcal P'$ is a compact subset of $\Zhu$.  If
$\Sigma=\{x\in\Zhu: x\bmod M\in R\}$ for a set $R$ of reduced residues
modulo $M$, and $\mathcal P(\Sigma)$ is the set of primes $p\nmid M$ with
$p\bmod M\in R$, then $\mathcal P(\Sigma)'=\Sigma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $x\in\mathcal P'$, then for each prime $q$ and each $k$ infinitely many
$p\in\mathcal P$ satisfy $p\equiv x\pmod{q^k}$; all but one of them are
prime to $q$, so $x_q\in\Z_q^\times$.  Derived sets in the compact metric
space $\Zh$ are closed, hence compact.  For the second statement, a
point of $\mathcal P(\Sigma)'$ is congruent modulo $M$ to some element of
$\mathcal P(\Sigma)$ and, by the first statement, is a unit, so it lies
in $\Sigma$.  Conversely every
neighbourhood $x+Q\Zh$ ($M\mid Q$) of $x\in\Sigma$ is a reduced class
modulo $Q$ and contains infinitely many primes by Dirichlet's theorem;
they lie in $\mathcal P(\Sigma)$.
\end{proof}

Such a $\Sigma$ is clopen in $\Zhu$, and by compactness every clopen
subset of $\Zhu$ has this form for some $M$.  Note that $\Zhu$ is closed
but not open in $\Zh$, so ``clopen'' always refers to the topology of
$\Zhu$.  The sets of primes studied below are all of the form $\mathcal
P(\Sigma)$.  The prime $p$ itself is never a unit of $\Z_p$, which is why
we work with accumulation points rather than with the primes as points of
$\Zh$.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}]\label{prop:compact}
Let $\mathcal P$ be an infinite set of primes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If no point of $\mathcal P'$ is sterile, then finitely many ET
classes contain all but finitely many $p\in\mathcal P$.  For those $p$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:solve} gives an explicit solution of
$4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$.
\item[(b)] If some $x\in\mathcal P'$ is sterile, then no finite set of ET
classes contains all but finitely many $p\in\mathcal P$.  Moreover no
finite set of primitive residue classes, each solvable by polynomials,
contains all but finitely many $p\in\mathcal P$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence all but finitely many elements of $\mathcal P$ lie in finitely many
primitive classes solvable by polynomials if and only if $\mathcal P'$
contains no sterile point.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) The ET classes are open and cover the compact set $\mathcal P'$, so
finitely many of them cover it; let $U$ be their union.  If
$\mathcal P\setminus U$ were infinite, it would have an accumulation
point, which lies in $\mathcal P'\subset U$; but $U$ is open and misses
$\mathcal P\setminus U$, a contradiction.

(b) Removing finitely many elements from $\mathcal P$ does not change
$\mathcal P'$.  If finitely many ET classes contain a cofinite subset of
$\mathcal P$, their union is closed and therefore contains $x$, which
contradicts sterility.  Next let $C_1,\dots,C_s$ be primitive classes
solvable by polynomials whose union contains a cofinite subset of
$\mathcal P$.  Viewed in $\Zh$, their union is closed, so $x\in C_j$ for
some $j$.  By Cited Theorem~\ref{cit:ET19} there are ET classes
$K_1,\dots,K_t$ containing all large primes of $C_j$.  Their union is
closed and misses $x$, so some neighbourhood $V=x+Q\Zh\subset C_j$
misses it.  As $x$ is a unit, $V$ is a reduced class modulo $Q$, and by
Dirichlet's theorem it contains infinitely many primes.  These lie in
$C_j$ and in no $K_i$, a contradiction.  The final equivalence combines
(a), (b) and Lemma~\ref{lem:solve}: each ET class is a finite union of
residue classes modulo $M(\kappa)$; the non-primitive ones contain at
most finitely many primes, and each primitive one is solvable by
polynomials by Lemma~\ref{lem:solve}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Direction of the implications]\label{rem:direction}
The equivalence concerns the \emph{existence} of a sterile point of
$\mathcal P'$.  For a \emph{given} point $x$ only one direction holds:
if $x$ is sterile there is no finite covering, but if $x$ lies in some
ET class another point of $\mathcal P'$ may still be sterile.  An earlier
internal draft of \cite{PM} said ``equivalently'' here; this was false
and was corrected in review.  A weaker statement is available for every
explicit point: if $x\in\mathcal P'$ lies in no ET class of modulus at
most $Y$, then every finite ET covering of a cofinite subset of $\mathcal
P$ uses a class of modulus greater than $Y$.  Indeed the classes of
modulus $\le Y$ in the covering miss a neighbourhood of $x$, which
contains infinitely many elements of $\mathcal P$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Heights]\label{rem:heights}
The same compactness argument works for any subfamily $\mathcal K$ and
any function $\kappa\mapsto\operatorname{ht}(\kappa)$.  Let
$\mathcal K_X$ be the triples of height at most $X$ and
$U_X=\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{\kappa\in\mathcal K_X}\Cl(\kappa)$ for a
compact $\Sigma$.  The $U_X$ are closed and decreasing.  If each is
nonempty, the finite intersection property gives a point of $\bigcap_X
U_X$, which is $\mathcal K$-sterile.  So either some finite subfamily of
bounded height covers $\Sigma$, or $\Sigma$ has a $\mathcal K$-sterile
point.  The set of $\mathcal K$-sterile points of $\Sigma$ is
$\bigcap_XU_X$, a closed set.
\end{remark}

\section{Square points and the odd-square obstruction}\label{sec:square}

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; known]\label{prop:square}
Let $x\in\Zhu$ be a \emph{square point}: $x_q\in(\Z_q^\times)^2$ for every
prime $q$.  Then $x$ is sterile.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $x=1$ this is elementary: if $1\in\Cl(\kappa)$ then the integer $n=1$
lies in the class, and Lemma~\ref{lem:solve} gives positive integers with
$4=1/x+1/y+1/z\le3$.  In general, suppose $x\in\Cl(\kappa)$ with
$\Cl(\kappa)$ defined modulo $M$.  For each $q\mid M$ the component
$x_q$ is the square of a unit, so by the Chinese remainder theorem there
is an integer $m$ prime to $M$ with $m^2\equiv x\pmod M$.  The primitive
class $m^2\bmod M$ is then contained in the class of $\kappa$ and is
solvable by polynomials (Lemma~\ref{lem:solve}).  This contradicts the
theorem of Mordell and Schinzel that a primitive class whose residue is a
perfect square is not solvable by polynomials
\cite{Mordell,Schinzel}, \cite[p.~8]{ET}.
\end{proof}

The last step is the classical obstruction; ET remark that it also
follows from their vanishing result at odd squares \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}, which goes back to
Schinzel and Yamamoto \cite{Yamamoto}.  The input is one quadratic
character computation; Bright and Loughran \cite{BL} identify it with the
Brauer--Manin obstruction of the Erd\H{o}s--Straus surfaces.

Call a prime $p$ \emph{Mordell-hard} if $p\bmod 840$ is a square,
$p\bmod 840\in\{1,121,169,289,361,529\}$.  Every primitive class modulo
$840$ that is not a square is solvable by polynomials (Mordell; see
\cite[p.~8]{ET}).  The set $\Sigma_{\rm hard}\subset\Zhu$ of square
classes modulo $840$ contains the point $1$, so by
Propositions~\ref{prop:compact}(b) and~\ref{prop:square} the Mordell-hard
primes admit no finite covering by polynomial identities.  Only the
elementary case $x=1$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:square} is used here, so
this statement depends on Cited Theorem~\ref{cit:ET19} but not on the
Mordell--Schinzel theorem.  This is the
classical form of the obstruction, and it is the reason why every finite
covering statement below restricts the hard primes further by a
\emph{non-residue} condition at some additional prime $r$; the resulting
target sets contain no square point.  Whether they contain other sterile
points is the question of this note.

\subsection{The procedure-level form}
The project's working note \cite{PS} proves a version of the odd-square
obstruction for procedures.  We only state it informally and refer to
\cite[\S\S1--2]{PS} and \cite{PObs} for the model of computation.
A \emph{witness-producing program} takes a prime $p$, performs ring
operations, floor divisions, eventual-sign comparisons, full
factorisations and loops over the lists so produced, and outputs a
solution or FAIL; it is \emph{correct} if every solution it outputs is
genuine, and \emph{bounded} if it uses only these operations, each loop
running over a list fixed at loop entry (so it halts on every input; the
number of steps may grow with $p$).  Let $\mathcal H$ be the set of
primitive irreducible $h\in\Z[X]$ with positive leading coefficient
(denoted $\mathcal P$ in \cite{PS}).  For $P\in\Z[X]$ a point $q^*\in\Zh$ is
\emph{square-mimicking} for $P$ if $P(q^*_\ell)$ is the square of a unit
of $\Z_\ell$ for every prime $\ell$.

\begin{citedthm}[Formal odd-square principle {\cite[Theorem~C,
Corollary~C1]{PS}}; \status{conditional} on Hypothesis~H]\label{cit:C}
Let $P=uX+v\in\mathcal H$ be linear and $q^*$ square-mimicking for $P$
and nondegenerate for every $h\in\mathcal H$ in the sense of
\cite[\S1.2]{PS}.  If a correct witness-producing program has a defined and
finite formal run at $(P,q^*)$, then it outputs FAIL at every admissible
$q$ (\status{proved}); under Schinzel's Hypothesis~H \cite{SS} for an
explicit finite family there are infinitely many admissible $q$, and
$p=P(q)\equiv1\pmod{24}$.  In particular, under H for one explicit
finite family, every correct bounded witness-producing program fails for
infinitely many primes $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$.
\end{citedthm}

A finite list of ET classes, each tested by a congruence and followed by
the identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:solve}, is such a bounded program, and
its failure at the rational square-mimicking point $1$ is
Proposition~\ref{prop:square} for $x=1$, unconditionally.  Cited Theorem~\ref{cit:C} does
not reprove the unconditional statement for general square points; it
extends the obstruction from congruence coverings to all bounded
procedures, at the price of Hypothesis~H.  \emph{Novelty.} Theorem~C is
the procedure-level, H-conditional generalisation of known obstructions
(Mordell and Schinzel on square classes, ET Prop.~1.6 and the remark on
\cite[p.~6]{ET} that ``a finite set of covering congruence strategies''
must fail, Yamamoto, Bright--Loughran); its unconditional instances are
known in substance.  The project's novelty audit rates it as a partial
novelty only.

\begin{remark}[\status{Assessment}]\label{rem:notsquare}
The target sets of \S\S\ref{sec:typeI}--\ref{sec:r17} require $x_r$ to be a
non-square for some prime $r$, so they contain no point that is
square-mimicking for $P=X$, and neither Proposition~\ref{prop:square} nor
Cited Theorem~\ref{cit:C} applies to them.  The candidate sterile points
below (the sign point $\hat x_9$, the point $x^*$ for $r=13$, the
$17$-generic line) are therefore not explained by the odd-square
obstruction.  If they are sterile, the reason is a different, rigidity
type of obstruction (\S\ref{sec:typeI}); this is a heuristic reading,
not a theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Type~I coverings and the least non-residue}\label{sec:typeI}

\subsection{Slices, certificates and the depth $\ckmin$}
In this section a prime $p$ is \emph{hard} if $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$, and
$n_p$ denotes the least quadratic non-residue modulo $p$ (a prime $\ge5$).
For an odd prime $p$ let
\[
\mathcal B_p=\{(c,k): 1\le k\le\lfloor 2p/3\rfloor,\ 1\le c\le
\lfloor (2p+k)/4k\rfloor,\ (p,ck)=1\},
\]
put $N_{c,k}=p^2+4ck^2$,
and let $M_{c,k}(p)$ be the number of divisors $D$ of $N_{c,k}$ with
$D\equiv-p\pmod{4ck}$.  The Type~I solutions of $4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$ (those
with $p$ dividing exactly one denominator) correspond to the pairs
$((c,k),D)$ of this kind \cite[\S\S26, 36]{Notes}.  In ET's coordinates a Type~I
solution has $(c,d,f)_{\rm ET}=(k,c,D)$, and fixing $(c,k,D)$ gives
exactly a class of family I3.  Write $s=\sqf(c)$ for the squarefree part
of $c$ and $\chi_s$ for the Kronecker character of $\Q(\sqrt{-s})$.  If
$\chi_s(p)=1$ then $M_{c,k}(p)=0$ \cite[Thm.~48.1]{Notes} (genus theory;
the slice is \emph{forced}).  For hard $p$ this applies to
$s\in\{1,2,3,6\}$, and to every $c$ all of whose prime factors are
residues modulo $p$.  Put
\[
\ckmin(p)=\min\{ck:(c,k)\in\mathcal B_p,\ M_{c,k}(p)>0\},
\]
the least height of a Type~I solution in these coordinates.  By the
forcing just described, $\ckmin(p)\ge n_p$ for hard $p$.  For a prime
$r\ge5$ put
\[
C(r)=\sup\{\ckmin(p): p\text{ hard},\ n_p=r\},\qquad
C^*(r)=\limsup_{p\text{ hard},\ n_p=r}\ckmin(p).
\]

A \emph{certificate} is a triple $\kappa=(c,k,F)$ of positive integers
with $(F,4ck)=1$ and $\sqf(c)\notin\{1,2,3,6\}$; its \emph{height} is
$ck$, and its class is the ET class (family I3)
\[
\Cl(c,k,F)=\{x\in\Zh: x\equiv-F\pmod{4ck},\ x^2\equiv-4ck^2\pmod F\}.
\]
If $(c,k)\in\mathcal B_p$, then $M_{c,k}(p)\ge1$ if and only if
$p\in\Cl(c,k,F)$ for some $F$, and the possible $F$ are exactly the
divisors counted by $M_{c,k}(p)$ \cite[Lemma~0.1]{PT2}.  Let
$\Sigma_r\subset\Zhu$ be the clopen set of $x$ with $x\equiv1\pmod{24}$,
$x\bmod\ell$ a non-zero square for the primes $5\le\ell<r$, and $x\bmod r$
a non-square.  By quadratic reciprocity a prime $p>r$ is hard with
$n_p=r$ if and only if $p\bmod 24\prod_{\ell\le r}\ell$ lies in
$\Sigma_r$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:accum} these primes accumulate exactly on
$\Sigma_r$.  A \emph{Type~I covering of height $\le X$} is a finite set of
certificates of height $\le X$ whose classes cover $\Sigma_r$; let
$X_r\in\N\cup\{\infty\}$ be the least height of a Type~I covering.

The other Type~I families I1, I2, I4 also give Type~I solutions, but in
each of them $c$ or $k$ grows linearly with $p$ \cite[\S6.1]{PT1}; only
family I3 with fixed $(c,k)$ bounds $\ckmin$.

\subsection{The case $r=5$}

\begin{theorem}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Thm.~6.1]{PT1}}]\label{thm:C5}
Every hard prime $p$ with $n_p=5$ has $\ckmin(p)\le10$, and
$\ckmin(p)=5$ if $p\equiv2\pmod5$.  The bound is attained at $p=193$, so
$C(5)=10$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Here $p\equiv1\pmod 8$, $p\equiv1\pmod3$ and $p\equiv2,3\pmod5$, so
$p\equiv17$ or $33\pmod{40}$; all slices below lie in $\mathcal B_p$ as
$p\ge73$.  If $p\equiv17\pmod{40}$, then $-p\equiv3\pmod{20}$ and
$p^2+20\equiv0\pmod3$, so $D=3$ is a target divisor of the slice $(5,1)$
and $\ckmin(p)\le5$.  If $p\equiv33\pmod{40}$, then $-p\equiv7\pmod{40}$.
As $p$ runs over the units modulo $7$, $p^2\in\{1,2,4\}$, while
$-20\equiv1$, $-40\equiv2$, $-80\equiv4\pmod 7$.  So $7$ divides one of
$p^2+20$, $p^2+40$, $p^2+80$, and $D=7$ is a target divisor of $(5,1)$,
$(10,1)$ or $(5,2)$.  Finally $\ckmin(193)=10$ by direct computation,
and $\ckmin(p)\ge n_p=5$ always.
\end{proof}

The proof is a Type~I covering of $\Sigma_5$ of height $10$ with
$F\in\{3,7\}$.  ES itself is classical for $p\equiv2,3\pmod 5$; the
content is the bounded depth.

\subsection{Compactness and Hypothesis~H}

\begin{theorem}[{\cite[Thm.~A]{PT2}}]\label{thm:A}
Let $r\ge5$ be prime.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \status{(proved)} If $X_r<\infty$, then
$\ckmin(p)\le X_r$ for every hard $p$ with $n_p=r$ beyond an explicit
bound, so $C^*(r)\le X_r$.
\item[(ii)] \status{(conditional on H)} If no Type~I covering of height
$\le X$ exists, then, under Hypothesis~H for an explicit finite family
$\mathcal P_X$ of polynomials, there are infinitely many hard $p$ with
$n_p=r$ and $\ckmin(p)>X$.
\item[(iii)] \status{(proved)} $X_r=\infty$ if and only if $\Sigma_r$
contains a point lying in no class $\Cl(\kappa)$ of a certificate (a
\emph{Type~I-sterile point}).  \status{(conditional on H)} Under H,
$C^*(r)=X_r$; in particular
$C^*(r)=\infty$ if and only if a Type~I-sterile point exists.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(i) Let $\mathcal K$ be a covering of height $X_r$ and $F_{\max}$ its
largest $F$.  For a prime $p>\max(2X_r,F_{\max},r)$ that is hard with
$n_p=r$, $p$ is prime to all moduli of $\mathcal K$, so its residue is that
of a point of $\Sigma_r$ and lies in some $\Cl(\kappa)$.  For $ck\le
X_r<p/2$ one checks $(c,k)\in\mathcal B_p$, and $M_{c,k}(p)\ge1$.

(iii) The first statement is Remark~\ref{rem:heights} with the
height $ck$.  The second follows from (i) and (ii).

(ii) By compactness some $x^*\in\Sigma_r$ lies in no class of height
$\le X$.  Fix a prime $B\ge\max(X,r,2m+2)$, where $m$ is the number of
slices of height $\le X$.  At each prime $q\le B$ at most one value
$V=ck^2$ has $x^{*2}_q+4V=0$; perturbing $x^*_q$ by a high power of $q$
makes every $v_q(N_{c,k})$ finite and constant on a small class
$\mathcal C\ni$ (perturbed point) $\subset\Sigma_r$.  Let $f_{c,k}$ be the
resulting fixed $B$-smooth part of $N_{c,k}$.  A divisor $d\mid f_{c,k}$
in the target class would give a certificate containing $x^*$, so none
exists.  The family $\mathcal P_X$ consists of $Qt+a$ (the class
$\mathcal C$) and the quadratics $((Qt+a)^2+4ck^2)/f_{c,k}$; it has no
fixed prime divisor.  Under H all its members are prime for infinitely
many $t$, and then every divisor of $N_{c,k}=f_{c,k}R_{c,k}$ is $d$ or
$dR_{c,k}$ with $d\mid f_{c,k}$; both are excluded from the target class.
So $M_{c,k}(p)=0$ for all slices of height $\le X$.  Full details are in
\cite[\S1]{PT2}.
\end{proof}

Forcedness is not needed in (ii): forced slices simply contain no
certificate class point of $\Sigma_r$.  A Type~I-sterile point need not be
sterile in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:sterile}; it may lie in a
class of another family.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} concerns the depth
$\ckmin$, not ES.

\begin{theorem}[\status{conditional} on H; {\cite[Thm.~2.1]{PT1}}]\label{thm:unbounded}
Let $g\ge1$, $D(g)=\sum_{m\le g}\tau(m)$, and let $r\ge100D(g)^2g^3$ be
prime.  Under Hypothesis~H for an explicit family of $1+D(g)$
polynomials, there are infinitely many hard $p$ with $n_p=r$ and
$\ckmin(p)>g\,n_p$.  Consequently, under H for a finite family depending
on $\varepsilon$, $\ckmin(p)\ge n_p^{2-\varepsilon}$ for infinitely many
hard $p$.
\end{theorem}

The construction is the one of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(ii) at the point
$x_q=1$ ($q\ne r$), $x_r=b$ with a suitable non-residue $b$.  So no bound
$\ckmin(p)\le f(n_p)$ with $f(r)\le r^{2-\varepsilon}$ for all large $r$
can be proved without refuting H for an explicit family.  For fixed $r$,
formal evaluations at H-generic points give explicit lower bounds
\cite[\S6.2]{PT1}: under H there are infinitely many hard $p$ with
$n_p=7$ and $\ckmin(p)=539$, and with $n_p=11$ and $\ckmin(p)>3000$.
Unconditionally (the same refinement argument plus Dirichlet), every
Type~I covering of $\Sigma_{11}$ has height $>3000$
(\status{certified} by finite computation; two independent engines).  For
$r=7$ these bounds are superseded by \S\ref{subsec:sign}.

\subsection{The sign point}\label{subsec:sign}

\begin{lemma}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Lemma~2.1 and \S4]{PT2}}]\label{lem:squareoff}
Let $x\in\Sigma_r$ with $x_q\in(\Z_q^\times)^2$ for every prime $q\ne r$.
Then a certificate $(c,k,F)$ with $x\in\Cl(c,k,F)$ has $v_r(c)$ odd.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\chi_s(x)$ is determined by $x\bmod 4s$, and equals $-1$ exactly when
$r\mid s$.  If $\chi_s(x)=1$ and $x\in\Cl(c,k,F)$, the open set
$\Cl(c,k,F)\cap\{\chi_s=1\}\cap\Sigma_r$ contains primes $p$ with
$(c,k)\in\mathcal B_p$ and $M_{c,k}(p)\ge1$ (Dirichlet), contradicting
forcing.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
For $w\in\Z_2$ with $w\equiv9\pmod{16}$ and a prime $r\equiv3\pmod4$, the
\emph{sign point} $\hat x_w=\hat x^{(r)}_w\in\Zhu$ has components
$w$ at $2$, $-1$ at $r$ and $1$ at every other prime.  For $r=7$ we write
$\hat x_9$ for $w=9$.
\end{definition}

The sign point lies in $\Sigma_r$ because $-1$ is a non-square modulo
$r$; for $r\equiv1\pmod4$ no such point exists.  At $\hat x_w$ the odd
components satisfy $\hat x_q^2=1$, so $N_{c,k}(\hat x)=1+4ck^2$ at every
odd prime, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:squareoff} a certificate at $\hat x_w$
is an integer solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signcert}
\begin{gathered}
F\mid1+4ck^2,\qquad F\equiv-1\pmod{m'},\qquad F\equiv1\pmod{r^{v_r(ck)}},\\
F\equiv-w\pmod{2^{v_2(4ck)}},\qquad v_r(c)\text{ odd},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $m'$ is the part of $ck$ prime to $2r$.  Write $c=2^\alpha r^ac'$,
$k=2^\gamma r^bk'$ with $c'k'$ prime to $2r$, $t=v_2(4ck)=2+\alpha+\gamma$,
and $e=(1+4ck^2)/F$.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Lemma~3.1, Prop.~4.1]{PT2}}]\label{prop:signpoint}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $r\equiv7\pmod 8$, no certificate at $\hat x^{(r)}_w$ has
$F^2=1+4ck^2$.
\item[(b)] If $r\equiv3\pmod8$, then $(r(r+1)/4,2,2r+1)$ is a certificate
at every $\hat x^{(r)}_w$, of height $r(r+1)/2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(b) $v_r(c)=1$, $1+4ck^2=1+4r(r+1)=(2r+1)^2$, the $2$-part of $4ck$ is $8$,
and $F=2r+1$ satisfies $F\equiv1\pmod r$, $F\equiv-1\pmod{(r+1)/4}$,
$-F\equiv1\pmod 8$.  (a) If $F^2=1+4ck^2$, then from \eqref{eq:signcert}
$m'\mid F+1$ and $r^{a+b}\mid F-1$; as $\gcd(F-1,F+1)=2$ this gives
$F+1=2^ic'k'^2$, $F-1=2^jr^{a+2b}$ with $i+j=2+\alpha+2\gamma$,
$\min(i,j)=1$ and $s=a+2b$ odd.  If $j=1$, then $2^{i-1}c'k'^2=1+r^s$ and
$v_2(1+r^{s})=v_2(1+r)\ge3$ for odd $s$ when $r\equiv7\pmod8$, so
$\alpha+2\gamma=i-1\ge3$, $\alpha+\gamma\ge2$ and the $2$-adic modulus
$2^{2+\alpha+\gamma}$ is at least $16$; as $r^s\equiv r\pmod{16}$ and
$2r\equiv14\pmod{16}$, $-F=-1-2r^s\equiv1\pmod{16}$, against
$w\equiv9\pmod{16}$; the cases $i=1$ lead
to $-F\not\equiv1\pmod 8$ or to a parity contradiction.  See
\cite[Lemma~3.1]{PT2}.
\end{proof}

So sign points survive the square family exactly for $r\equiv7\pmod8$:
$r=7,23,31,47,\dots$.  At the other natural candidate, the
\emph{residue-one point} ($x_q=1$ for $q\ne7$), every non-square $x_7$ is
already covered by $(7,3,11)$, $(7,3,23)$ or $(14,2,15)$
\cite[Prop.~2.3]{PT2}.

\begin{lemma}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Lemmas~1.1--1.2]{PT3}}]\label{lem:fgrade}
Let $(c,k,F)$ be a certificate at $\hat x_w$ and $f\in\{F,e\}$.  Then
$m'\mid f+1$, $r^{a+b}\mid f-1$, $f\equiv-w\pmod{2^t}$ if $f=F$ and
$wf\equiv-1\pmod{2^t}$ if $f=e$, and $4ck^2\equiv-1\pmod f$.  Conversely
such data with $a$ odd and $a,c',k'\ge1$ give a certificate.  If $w$ is a
positive integer, each $f$ belongs to finitely many certificates, all
explicitly computable from the factorisation of $(f+1)_{\rm odd}\,r^{v_r(f-1)}$.  If $ck\le X$
and $r\mid c$, then $\min(F,e)<2X/\sqrt r+1$.
\end{lemma}

The finiteness needs $w$ with finite $2$-adic depth $v_2(f+w)$,
$v_2(wf+1)$: at $w=-71$ the triples $(14\cdot2^{35j},8192,71)$ are
certificates for all $j\ge0$.  For $w=9$ the lemma reduces the search to
a sieve over $f$, at all heights simultaneously.

\begin{computation}[\status{certified}; {\cite[Comp.~3.2]{PT2},
\cite[Comp.~2.1, 2.3]{PT3}}]\label{comp:sign}
Exhaustive searches at the sign point give the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] No certificate at $\hat x_9$ ($r=7$) has $\min(F,e)<10^{12}$.
Two independent $f$-graded engines cover $f<10^{11}$; the range
$[10^{11},10^{12})$ rests on one engine.  An earlier height-graded search
($7.6\cdot10^9$ slices with $ck\le3\cdot10^9$) was independently re-run to
its full range, and cross-checked by a factoring engine and a complete
parametric enumeration on smaller ranges.  (Programs:
\texttt{typei3\_fsearch.c}, \texttt{review\_typei3\_fs.c},
\texttt{typei2\_signcheck.c}.)
\item[(b)] For $r=23$, $31$, $47$ and $w=9$ no certificate has
$\min(F,e)<10^{11}$ (two engines for $r=23$, one for $r=31,47$).
\end{enumerate}
On ten test points (six of them carrying certificates, $64$ certificates
with $ck\le2\cdot10^5$ in all) the $f$-graded and the height-graded
engines produce identical certificate sets; at the point $w=-15$ both
sets are first restricted to certificates without the divisor $15$, which
has infinite $2$-adic depth there \cite[\S2]{PT3}.
\end{computation}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:C7}
\status{(certified)} Every Type~I covering of $\Sigma_7$ has height
$>1.32\cdot10^{12}$; for $r=23,31,47$ every Type~I covering of
$\Sigma_r$ has height $>2.39\cdot10^{11}$, $>2.78\cdot10^{11}$,
$>3.42\cdot10^{11}$ respectively.  \status{(conditional on H)}
There are infinitely many hard $p$ with $n_p=7$ and
$\ckmin(p)>1.32\cdot10^{12}$; so $C(7)>1.32\cdot10^{12}$, and similarly
for $r=23,31,47$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:fgrade}, a certificate of height $\le X$ at $\hat x_9$
has $\min(F,e)<2X/\sqrt7+1$; with Computation~\ref{comp:sign} there is
none with $X\le1.32\cdot10^{12}$.  A covering of $\Sigma_7$ must contain
$\hat x_9$.  The conditional part is Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(ii) with the
point $x^*=\hat x_9$.
\end{proof}

The previous bounds (under H) were $C(7)\ge539$ \cite{PT1} and
$C(7)>3\cdot10^9$ \cite{PT2}; the census maximum of $\ckmin$ over hard $p<10^7$ with
$n_p=7$ is $76$ (\status{evidence}).

\subsection{Low $2$-adic levels}
The certificates at the sign point can also be attacked by descent.

\begin{lemma}[Vieta descent; \status{proved}; {\cite[Lemma~5.1]{PT3}}]\label{lem:vieta}
Let $B,K,\delta,F,e\ge1$ be integers with $Fe=1+BK^2$ and $e-F=BK\delta$.
Then $F\equiv e\equiv1\pmod{B\delta}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induction on $K$.  If $F=1$ then $K=\delta$ and $e=1+B\delta^2$.
Otherwise put $\rho=K-\delta F$.  From $F^2+BK\delta F=1+BK^2$ we get
$F^2-1=BK\rho$, so $1\le\rho<K$.  Put $F'=F-B\rho\delta$; then
$F'F=1+B\rho^2>0$ and $F-F'=B\rho\delta$, and induction applies to
$(F',F)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Cor.~5.2, Props.~5.3--5.5]{PT3}}]\label{prop:levels}
Let $w\equiv9\pmod{16}$ and $r=7$.  Every certificate at $\hat x_w$ has
$t\ge5$ and $\alpha+2\gamma\ge7$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:fgrade} and $w\equiv w^{-1}\pmod{16}$, $e\equiv F$
modulo $2^{\min(t,4)}$ times the odd part of $ck$, and $F,e\not\equiv
1\pmod 4$; $F\neq e$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:signpoint}(a).  If $t\le4$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:vieta} with $B=4c$ gives $F\equiv1\pmod4$; if
$\alpha+2\gamma\le4$ the same holds after the integral rescaling
$c\mapsto c/4^{t-4}$, $k\mapsto2^{t-4}k$.  At levels
$\alpha+2\gamma\in\{5,6\}$ the descent stays integral and forces
$c=2^\alpha7^a$.  At level $5$ the chain of the descent, read modulo $16$,
only reaches divisors $\equiv\pm1\pmod{16}$, while certificates need
$\equiv7$; this deduction is due to the internal reviewer.  At level $6$
the chain is a Pell orbit; a $2$-adic index condition forces its index
to be an odd multiple of $3$, whence $D+3$ divides the relevant Lucas
number ($D=7^a\delta^2$), and a prime $q\mid(D+3)/2$ gives
$F\equiv1$ and $F\equiv-1\pmod q$ simultaneously.  Details and
machine checks are in \cite[\S5]{PT3}.
\end{proof}

At level $\alpha+2\gamma=7$ the descent leaves $\Z$, and $34$ near misses
(certificates except for the $2$-adic condition) with $-F\equiv1\pmod8$
and $ck\le10^8$ exist there; this level is open.

\subsection{Scope of a sterility proof}

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Prop.~3.1]{PT3}}]\label{prop:nowhere}
The set of Type~I-sterile points of $\Sigma_7$ is closed and has empty
interior.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Closedness is Remark~\ref{rem:heights}.  Let $U=\{y\equiv
x\pmod Q\}\subset\Sigma_7$ be a basic neighbourhood, $Q=2^j7^i\prod_{q\in
P}q^{e_q}$.  Pick a prime $p_0\notin P\cup\{2,7\}$ and, by Dirichlet, a
prime $F\notin P\cup\{2,7,p_0\}$ with $F\equiv3\pmod4$,
$F\equiv-x_7\pmod7$ and $(-7p_0/F)=1$; these are compatible because
$(-7/F)=(F/7)=(-x_7/7)=1$ for non-square $x_7$.  Then $\Cl(7p_0,1,F)$
meets $U$ by the Chinese remainder theorem.
\end{proof}

So no sterility proof can consist of exhibiting one ambient congruence
cylinder: a sterile point, if it exists, is a limit of covered points.
This does not exclude arguments within the thin sign fibre
$\Phi=\{\hat x_w: w\in9+16\Z_2\}$, which itself has empty interior.

\begin{remark}[Measure route; \status{proved} reduction and
\status{evidence}]\label{rem:measure}
Theorem~\ref{thm:A}(iii) needs \emph{some} Type~I-sterile point, not
$\hat x_9$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:fgrade}, the certificates having a given
$f\equiv7\pmod{16}$ remove from $\Phi$ at most two $2$-adic balls of
radius $2^{-t_{\min}(f)}$, with $t_{\min}(f)\ge5$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:levels}; other $f$ remove nothing.  Let $U_Y$ be the
part of $\Phi$ surviving all $f<Y$.  If
\[
\mu(U_Y)>\sum_{f\ge Y}2^{5-t_{\min}(f)}
\]
(Haar measure on $w$, normalised to $1$), then $\Phi$ contains a
Type~I-sterile point, and $C^*(7)=\infty$ under H.  Numerically
$\mu(U_{10^{10}})\ge0.6086$, the bound including the error of truncating
the $2$-adic depth at $40$; and $t_{\min}(f)$ grows like
$\tfrac12\log_2f$ in the data (\status{evidence}).  An explicit bound
for the number of $f$ with given $t_{\min}$ that makes
$\sum_{f\ge10^{10}}2^{5-t_{\min}(f)}<0.6$ would therefore close the
question; summability alone does not suffice (the tail must beat the
computed $\mu(U_Y)$), let alone a bound $o(2^T)$.  No such bound is
known.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[{\cite[Conj.~3.4]{PT2}}]\label{conj:sign}
The sign point $\hat x_9$ is Type~I-sterile.  Then, under H,
$C(7)=C^*(7)=\infty$.
\end{conjecture}

The evidence is the search range of Computation~\ref{comp:sign}, the
empty levels of Proposition~\ref{prop:levels}, and the decay of the
near-miss mass (\status{evidence}).  Among integral points
$(x_2,x_7)=(w,z)$ with $w\equiv9\pmod{16}$, $|w|\le200$, $|z|\le60$ (all
other components $1$), the $26$ that survive the complete enumeration of certificates at
$\{2,7\}$-generic points up to the cutoff of \cite[\S2.2]{PT2} (parameter
$\Lambda\le3\cdot10^8$ there) all have $z=-1$; this is how the
sign point was found.  For $r\equiv1\pmod 4$ no sign point exists, and
$r=5$ is the one value with a known finite covering.

\section{Mordell-type coverings modulo a further prime}\label{sec:mordell}

We now return to all seven families and to ES itself.  For a prime $r\ge
11$ let $\Sigma_r\subset\Zhu$ (the \emph{main variant}) be the set of
units $x$ with $x\equiv1\pmod{24}$, $x$ a square modulo $5$ and $7$
(Mordell-hard) and $x$ a non-square modulo $r$, and let
$\Sigma_r^{\rm np}\subset\Sigma_r$ also require $x$ to be a non-zero square
modulo every prime $5\le\ell<r$.  By reciprocity the primes $p>r$ in
$\Sigma_r^{\rm np}$ are the Mordell-hard primes with $n_p=r$.
(Throughout this section $\Sigma_r$ denotes this set, not the set of
\S\ref{sec:typeI}.)

\subsection{A finite-exception theorem for $r=13$}

\begin{theorem}[\status{proved} by finite computation; {\cite[Thm.~3.1]{PM}}]\label{thm:r13}
Let $p$ be a prime with $(p/13)=-1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $(p/11)=+1$, then $4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$ has a solution in
positive integers unless $p\equiv112561\pmod{240240}$, i.e.
$p\equiv1\pmod{16\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7}$, $p\equiv9\pmod{11}$,
$p\equiv7\pmod{13}$.
\item[(b)] Without a condition at $11$, the same holds unless
$p\bmod720720\in\{112561,352801,380881,418321,473761,483841\}$.
\item[(c)] If $(p/11)=+1$, the exceptions of (a) shrink to $p\bmod
720720\in\{112561,352801\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $p$ is not a square modulo $840$, Mordell's identities apply.
Otherwise $p\bmod L$ represents a point of $\Sigma_{13}^{\rm np}$
($L=240240$) or of $\Sigma_{13}$ ($L=720720$).  Explicit lists of $20$
resp.\ $31$ ET classes, with moduli dividing $L$, cover every such
residue except the listed ones; Lemma~\ref{lem:solve} then gives the
solution for every $n\ge1$ in the class.  Part (c) combines the two
lists: the third lift $593041$ of $112561$ modulo $720720$ is covered by
the second list.  The certificates are the files
\texttt{cert\_r13\_np\_240240.json} and
\texttt{cert\_r13\_main\_720720.json} in \texttt{data/mordell/}; the stand-alone checker
\texttt{mordell\_\allowbreak check.py} verifies the polynomial identities,
positivity and the integer-valuedness of all coordinates on every class
residue.  An independent checker written from scratch in review
(\texttt{review\_\allowbreak mordell\_\allowbreak check.py}) confirms both certificates.
\end{proof}

\emph{Novelty.} Theorem~\ref{thm:r13} is an explicit packaging of the
level sieve of Salez \cite[\S\S3--4]{Salez}, extended by Mihnea and
Bogdan \cite{MD}.  Salez sieves with seven modular equations, including
filters with composite moduli, and tabulates the residues not certified
at levels $840,9240,120120,\dots$; using all ET classes of modulus
dividing $L$ reproduces his counts $34$ (at $9240$) and $192$ (at
$120120$) exactly.  Salez's residue set modulo $120120$ already contains
only $7$ classes with $(p/13)=-1$, so a statement of the shape of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r13} modulo $120120$ follows from his 2014 data.
Theorem~\ref{thm:r13} is the same sieve one level deeper ($2^4$, $3^2$),
sliced by $(p/13)$, with certificates that can be checked independently.
The exceptional classes carry $1/360$ (a) and $6/2160$ (b) of the
relevant residues; deeper levels shrink them (to $1412$ residues modulo
$2^4 3^2\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13^2\cdot17\cdot19\cdot23$ with moduli
$\le10^8$ in case (b)), but apparently never to zero
(\status{evidence}).

\subsection{Generic points and boxes}
For a finite set $T$ of primes call $x\in\Zhu$ \emph{$T$-generic} if
$x_q=1$ for every $q\notin T$.  A class of modulus $M=M_TN$, with $M_T$
the $T$-part, meets the $T$-generic points exactly when its residue
satisfies the class condition at $n=1$ away from $T$; its trace on the
$T$-generic points is then a box (a finite union of products of balls)
in $\prod_{q\in T}\Z_q$.  In ET's coordinates this says that the
non-constant coordinates of the family are $\Z[1/T]$-integral at $n=1$
\cite[\S2.1]{PM}.  For the families II1, I4 and II2 the conditions take
rigid forms, e.g.\ II1 and I4 at a $T$-generic point amount to
$4iabk=F(a+b+k)$ with $k=(a+b)/e$, $e+1=4iab/F$ and $F$ the $T$-part of
$4ab$; so for each $T$-level $F$ there are finitely many boxes
(\status{proved}; the II1, I4 and II2 rows were re-derived in the review
of \cite{PM}, and the full table for $T=\{17\}$, all seven families, in
the review of \cite{PM17}).

At the $13$-generic points of $\Sigma_{13}$ every non-residue class is
covered at $13$-adic level one: $x_{13}\equiv2$ by II2 $(a,d,f)=(9,2,143)$,
$x_{13}\equiv7$ by II2 $(2,2,143)$, and $x_{13}\equiv5,6,8,11$ by II2
classes with $f=39$ \cite[\S1]{PM} (the cases $x_{13}\equiv2,5,7$ were
re-checked in review).  So hard
points for $r=13$ need special residues at $11$ as well.  For
$T=\{11,13\}$ the classes of modulus $\le10^6$ leave uncovered only cells
above $(x_{11},x_{13})\equiv(2,2)\pmod{(11,13)}$ (\status{evidence}).

\subsection{The candidate sterile point $x^*$}

\begin{computation}[\status{certified}; {\cite[Comp.~4.1, \S4.1]{PM}}]\label{comp:xstar}
Let $x^*\in\Zhu$ have $x^*_{11}=x^*_{13}=2$ and $x^*_q=1$ for every other
prime $q$.  Then $x^*\in\Sigma_{13}$ and
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $x^*$ lies in no ET class of modulus $\le10^6$ (two
independent engines, \texttt{mordell\_point.py} and the reviewer's
\texttt{review\_mordell\_point.py});
\item[(b)] $x^*$ lies in no II1, I4 or II2 class whose modulus has
$\{11,13\}$-part dividing $11^4\cdot13^4$, with unrestricted remaining
part (complete rigid enumeration; re-checked by a second engine for every
level dividing $11^3\cdot13^3$ except $11^3\cdot13^3$ itself; that level
and the levels with exponent $4$ rest on one engine).
\end{enumerate}
\end{computation}

\begin{corollary}[\status{proved} from Computation~\ref{comp:xstar}(a)]
Every finite set of ET classes containing all but finitely many
Mordell-hard primes $p$ with $(p/13)=-1$ contains a class of modulus
$>10^6$.  The same holds for the primes with
$(p/11)=(p/13)=-1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Remark~\ref{rem:direction}: $x^*$ is an accumulation point of these
primes by Lemma~\ref{lem:accum} ($(2/11)=(2/13)=-1$).
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[{\cite[Conj.~4.2]{PM}}]\label{conj:xstar}
The point $x^*$ is sterile.
\end{conjecture}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:compact}(b), Conjecture~\ref{conj:xstar}
\emph{implies} that no finite set of primitive classes solvable by
polynomials contains all but finitely many Mordell-hard primes with
$(p/11)=(p/13)=-1$, so that the method of Theorem~\ref{thm:r13}(b), a
finite list of polynomial classes, cannot be extended to cover all such
primes up to finitely many exceptions.  This would say nothing about
whether ES holds for these primes.  The conjecture is not equivalent to
the non-existence of such a covering (Remark~\ref{rem:direction}).

\emph{Why the odd-square obstruction does not explain $x^*$.}  The point
$x^*$ is not square-mimicking, as $x^*_{11}$ and $x^*_{13}$ are
non-residues; so Proposition~\ref{prop:square} and Cited
Theorem~\ref{cit:C} do not apply (\status{proved}, trivially).  That the
mechanism is instead the rigidity of classes at $T$-generic points, as for
the sign point, is an \status{Assessment}: Jacobi-parity arguments of the
kind in \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET} force only an odd total $\{11,13\}$-valuation
of the relevant parameters, which can be satisfied.

\emph{Data} (\status{evidence}).  With all-family boxes of modulus
$\le10^6$ and the rigid II boxes, $24.8\%$ of the $(2,2)$ cell remains
uncovered at resolution $11^4\cdot13^4$, and the exponent-$4$ rigid level
barely changes this.  For the np variant no $T$-generic obstruction with
$|T|\le3$ was found; a best-first tree search over $\Sigma_{13}^{\rm np}$
stopped at $30000$ nodes with open Haar mass $4.1\cdot10^{-7}$, so that
variant is undecided.

\section{The $17$-generic line}\label{sec:r17}

For $r=17$ the candidate sterile set is one-dimensional, and the classes
meeting it can be described exactly.  A point $x\in\Zhu$ is
\emph{$17$-generic} if $x_q=1$ for all $q\ne17$; write $x=x(u)$ with
$u=x_{17}\in\Z_{17}^\times$.  If $u$ is a non-residue modulo $17$ then
$x(u)\in\Sigma_{17}^{\rm np}$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:accum} the
Mordell-hard primes with $n_p=17$ accumulate at $x(u)$.  The non-residue
cells $C_5=\{u\equiv5\}$ and $C_7=\{u\equiv7\pmod{17}\}$ are exchanged by
$u\mapsto1/u$ ($5\cdot7\equiv1$).  A class of modulus $17^kN$ with
$17\nmid N$ contains $x(u)$ if and only if its residue $\rho$ satisfies
$\rho\equiv1\pmod N$ and $u\equiv\rho\pmod{17^k}$; it then contributes the
\emph{box} $\rho+17^k\Z_{17}$ of \emph{level} $k$.  We use Haar measure
$\mu$ on $\Z_{17}$, so $\mu(C_v)=1/17$.  All results of this section are
from \cite{PM17} and its review.

\subsection{Box types}

\begin{lemma}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Lemmas~1.1, 1.3]{PM17}}]\label{lem:boxtypes}
At level $F=17^k$ the boxes of the seven families are exactly the boxes
of the following types (all parameters positive integers, ${}'$ the
$17$-free part):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(P)] $(a,d,f)$ with $17\nmid f$, $f\mid4a^2d+1$, $4(ad)'\mid f+1$,
$(ad)_{17}=F$; box $-f\pmod F$ (from I1, I3 with $17\mid cd$, II3 with
$17\mid ad$);
\item[(Q)] $(a,d,f)$ with $f=Fg$, $17\nmid g$, $4ad\mid f+1$, $g\mid
4a^2d+1$; box $-4a^2d\pmod F$ (II2, II3 with $17\mid e$), and its inverse
$-1/(4a^2d)$ (I2 with $17\mid f$);
\item[(U)] $(a,b,e)$ with $e\mid a+b$, $(e,4ab)=1$, $4(ab)'\mid e+1$,
$(ab)_{17}=F$; box $-e\pmod F$ (II1, I2 with $17\mid ac$), and its
inverse $-1/e$ (I4).
\end{enumerate}
The type I3 with $17\mid f$ (boxes $\pm\sqrt{-4a^2d}$) never meets the
$17$-generic line.  (Q)-data exist only at odd levels $k$, with $-4a^2d$
a non-residue modulo $17$; the set of (P)-boxes is closed under
inversion; no class contains the whole line (no level-$0$ box).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the parity statements]
For a (Q)-datum write $f=4adm-1=17^kg$.  As $f\equiv-1\pmod{4d}$, Jacobi
reciprocity gives $(d/f)=1$, and $(-1/f)=-1$, so $(-d/f)=-1$.  As
$g\mid4a^2d+1$, $-d(2a)^2\equiv1\pmod g$, so $(-d/g)=1$.  Hence
$(-d/17)^k=-1$: $k$ is odd and $-d$, hence $-4a^2d$, is a non-residue
modulo $17$, so $n^2\equiv-4a^2d\pmod{17^k}$ has no root.  A level-$0$
box would give positive integers with $4/1=1/x+1/y+1/z$, by the rigid
forms \cite[Cor.~2.4]{PM17}, which is impossible.  The type
classification is read off from the generic-point conditions family by
family; see \cite[\S1]{PM17}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Boxes are Erd\H{o}s--Straus solutions at prime powers}
In ET's coordinates, $\Sigma^I_n$ is given by $4abcd=n(a+b)+c$ and
$\Sigma^{II}_n$ by $4abcd=a+b+nc$, with $e=(a+b)/c$ and the further
relations of \cite[\S2]{ET}.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Lemmas~2.1--2.3, 5.1, 5.2]{PM17}}]\label{prop:boxES}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Put $b=(f+1)/(4ad)$.  Then $(a,d,f=17^kg)$ is a (Q)-datum if
and only if $(a,b,(a+b)/g,d)$ is an $\N$-point of $\Sigma^I_{17^k}$ with
$e=g$ prime to $17$.  The box is $-4a^2d\equiv-a/b\pmod{17^k}$.
\item[(b)] A (U)-datum $(a,b,e)$ of level $k$ gives the solution
$4/17^k=1/(iab)+1/(iac)+1/(ibc)$, $c=(a+b)/e$, $i=(e+1)F/(4ab)$.
Every (U)-box meeting $C_5\cup C_7$ lies in a (P)-box of level $\le k$
with the same centre, so the type (U) adds no measure.
\item[(c)] Let $(a,d,f)$ be a (P)-datum of level $k$, $a=17^\alpha a'$,
$d=17^\delta d'$, $n=a'd'$, $f=4ni-1$, $(4a^2d+1)/f=4nj-1$.  Then
$(i,j,a',d')$ is an $\N$-point of $\Sigma^{II}_{17^K}$ with
$K=k+\alpha\in[k,2k]$ and $17\nmid a'd'$.  Conversely every $\N$-point of
$\Sigma^{II}_{17^K}$ with $17\nmid cd$ yields (P)-boxes, nested, the
largest of level $\lceil K/2\rceil$, with centre $-f_{\rm ET}$; if
$17\nmid b$ (as for every box meeting the non-residue cells) the centre is
$\equiv-a/b$.
$\Sigma^{II}_{17^K}$ has no such $\N$-points for even $K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of (c)]
Since $(ad)_{17}=F=17^k$ and $(ad)'=n$, we have $4a^2d=4aFn$, so
$(4ni-1)(4nj-1)=4aFn+1$; expanding gives $4nij-i-j=aF=17^Ka'$, i.e.
$4\,i\,j\,a'\,d'=i+j+17^Ka'$, which is the defining equation of
$\Sigma^{II}_{17^K}$ for $(i,j,a',d')$.  The converse uses
$4c^2dN+1=f(4bcd-1)$ \cite[(2.21)]{ET}.  The centre is $-a/b$ because
$bf=Nc+a$ \cite[(2.20)]{ET}.  Even $K$ are excluded by the reciprocity
argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:boxtypes} applied to $f\mid
4\cdot17^Ka'^2d'+1$, $f\equiv-1\pmod{4d'}$.
\end{proof}

So the boxes on the $17$-generic line are balls around the rationals
$-a/b$ built from solutions of $4/17^K=1/x+1/y+1/z$: Type~I points at
full level for (Q), Type~II points at \emph{half} level for (P).  The
half level is what makes (P) critical below.

\subsection{Exact low levels}

\begin{computation}[\status{certified}; {\cite[Comp.~3.1]{PM17}}]\label{comp:17}
The boxes of level $\le5$ (and the (Q), (U) boxes of level $7$) were
enumerated completely, using the bounds of \cite[Lemma~2.8]{ET}, by two
independent engines (\texttt{m17\_enum.c} and the reviewer's
\texttt{review\_m17\_enum.c}); the union in $C_5$ was computed exactly
(two $17$-adic balls are nested or disjoint).  The covered fraction of
$C_5$ is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
level $k$ & boxes meeting $C_5$ & covered fraction after level $k$\\
\midrule
1 & 0 & 0\\
2 & 4 (P) & 0.235294\\
3 & 32 & 0.314879\\
4 & 50 (P) & 0.321799\\
5 & 158 & 0.322793\\
7 (Q, U only) & 240 & 0.322797\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
and identically for $C_7$.  So the boxes of level $\le5$ leave exactly
the fraction $56561/83521=0.67720\ldots$ of each cell uncovered.
Completeness was also checked against a brute force that reads the
classes literally off \cite[Prop.~1.9]{ET} for all moduli $\le10^6$ and
all boxes of level $\le3$ ($168$ boxes in all cells, $0$ missing); the boxes of levels
$4$ and $5$ rest on the two enumeration engines only.  Moreover the point $x(5)$ lies in no ET class of modulus
$\le10^6$ (two engines) and in no II1, I4 or II2 class with $17$-part
dividing $17^4$ \cite[Comp.~5.1]{PM}.
\end{computation}

The numbers of data are $D_P(K)=2,32,121,258,604$ for $K=1,3,5,7,9$ and
$D_Q(k)=2,73,245,707$, $D_U(k)=4,68,310,826$ for $k=1,3,5,7$; the measure
added per level is $0.235$, $0.080$, $0.0069$, $0.0010$ at levels $2$--$5$
(\status{evidence} of decay, from three ratios only).

\subsection{The tail}
Let $B_k=4D_Q(k)+2D_U(k)+2D_P(2k-1)$ be the resulting bound for the
number of boxes of level $k$ (two orientations; $D_P(2k)=0$).

\begin{theorem}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Thm.~4.1]{PM17}}]\label{thm:tail17}
If
\[
\sum_{k\ge6}17^{1-k}B_k<\frac{56561}{83521},
\]
then $C_5$ and $C_7$ contain sterile points $u$: $x(u)$ lies in no ET
class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The boxes of level $\ge6$ cover at most $\sum_{k\ge6}B_k17^{-k}$, less
than the measure $\frac{56561}{83521}\cdot\frac1{17}$ left by the levels
$\le5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\status{conditional} on the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:tail17}]\label{cor:17}
No finite set of primitive residue classes solvable by polynomials
contains all but finitely many of the Mordell-hard primes with $n_p=17$
(primes $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ with $(p/17)=-1$ and $(p/q)=+1$ for
$q=5,7,11,13$).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact}(b) with the sterile point $x(u)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[{\cite[Conj.~4.3]{PM17}}]\label{conj:17}
The cell $C_5$ (equivalently $C_7$) contains a sterile point.
\end{conjecture}

\emph{Status of the hypothesis.}  The series converges
(\status{proved}, non-explicitly): the proof of \cite[Prop.~1.7]{ET}
counts $\N$-points and gives $D_Q(k)\ll17^{(3/5+o(1))k}$ and
$D_P(K)\ll17^{(2/5+o(1))K}$, and the bound $f(n)\ll_\varepsilon
n^{2/3+\varepsilon}$ of Browning and Elsholtz \cite{BE} handles
(U).  The constants are not explicit, so this gives no level the
computation reaches.  The critical family is (P): its boxes have weight
$\asymp N^{-1/2}$ at ES level $N=17^K$, against the count
$N^{2/5+o(1)}$, a margin of only $1/10$; even $D_P(K)\le17^{2K/5}$ with
constant $1$ would leave a tail $\approx0.84$ from $K\ge11$, more than
$0.677$.  An $\N$-point of $\Sigma^{II}_N$ with $a\le b$ is determined by
$(a,b)$, so it would suffice (\status{Assessment}: this is the natural
route, not a proved necessity) to have an explicit bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pcount}
\#\{(a,b):a\le b,\ ab\le17^K,\ (-17^K\bmod 4ab)\mid a+b\}\le
C\cdot17^{(1/2-\delta)K}
\end{equation}
with explicit $C$ and $\delta>0$, together with weaker explicit bounds
for (Q), (U).  The data fit $D_P(K)\approx K^3$
($2,32,121,258,604$ against $1,27,125,343,729$; \status{evidence}), far
below what is needed: $D_Q+D_U\le17^{k/2}$ for $k\ge8$ and $D_P(K)\le
17^{K/4}$ for $K\ge11$ would make the tail $<0.01$.

\begin{assessment}[Discrete logarithms]
With $(s,t)=(c,d)$ the (P)-data of level $K$ are exactly the $(a,s,t)$
with $17\nmid st$, $f=4ast-1\mid s\cdot17^K+a$ and
$b=(s17^K+a)/f\ge a$ \cite[\S6]{PM17}.  As $(s,f)=1$ the condition is
$17^K\equiv-a/s\pmod f$: a triple with $(a,f)=1$ is ever admissible if
and only if $-a/s\in\langle17\rangle\subset(\Z/f)^\times$, and then for
the $K$ in one class modulo $\operatorname{ord}_f17$ that satisfy the
size condition $4a^2t\le17^K+2a/s$.  If $17^K$ is modelled as
equidistributed modulo $f$, $E[D_P(K)]\approx\sum1/(4ast)\asymp K^3$, as
observed.  So \eqref{eq:Pcount} is an Artin-type equidistribution
question for one base and many moduli.  Divisor-bound methods (ET,
Lenstra's bound on divisors in residue classes \cite{Lenstra},
Coppersmith--Howgrave-Graham--Nagaraj \cite{CHN}, explicit divisor bounds
of Nicolas--Robin type \cite{NR}) treat $17^K$ as an arbitrary integer; they give
$N^{2/5+o(1)}$ non-explicitly and $O(N)$ explicitly, and we see no way
for them to reach $N^{1/2-\delta}$ explicitly.  A digit-set (Cantor)
construction that avoids boxes with small integer centres was tested and
fails: the normalised centres of new boxes have maxima $0.86$--$0.99$ at
levels $3$--$7$.
\end{assessment}

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}

\begin{problem}
Is there a sterile point in $\Zhu$ that is not a square point?
Equivalently, is there a clopen set $\Sigma\subset\Zhu$ without square
points such that no finite set of polynomial identities covers all but
finitely many of the primes $\mathcal P(\Sigma)$?  (The square points form
a closed set, so a non-square sterile point has a clopen neighbourhood
$\Sigma$ without square points; conversely a clopen $\Sigma$ is defined
modulo some $M$.  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:accum} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact}.)
Each of Conjectures~\ref{conj:xstar} and~\ref{conj:17} would give one;
Conjecture~\ref{conj:sign} would give a Type~I analogue.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:sign}, or find a certificate at $\hat x_9$.
Two routes are visible: the $2$-adic level $\alpha+2\gamma=7$, where the
Vieta descent leaves $\Z$ and the reduced pairs must be classified as
orbits of a norm equation over $\Z[1/2]$; and an explicit bound for the
near-miss count of Remark~\ref{rem:measure}, small enough that the tail
beats $\mu(U_{10^{10}})$, which would give a
Type~I-sterile point of the sign fibre (not necessarily $\hat x_9$).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Is $X_r<\infty$ for any prime $r\ge7$?  The project's weakly supported
guess is that $r=5$ is the only value of $n_p$ with a finite Type~I
covering.  For $r\equiv1\pmod4$ there is no sign point; $r=13$ is the
first open case of that kind.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Prove the explicit count \eqref{eq:Pcount} for some $\delta>0$ and
explicit $C$, or any other bound that makes the series of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tail17} smaller than $56561/83521$.  By the Assessment
of \S\ref{sec:r17} this is a question about how often $-a/s$ lies in the
subgroup generated by $17$ modulo $4ast-1$, and where its discrete
logarithm falls.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Decide whether $x^*$ is sterile (Conjecture~\ref{conj:xstar}), and
whether the np variant $\Sigma^{\rm np}_{13}$ has a sterile point at all.
A finite-level reduction analogous to Theorem~\ref{thm:tail17} for the
two-dimensional $\{11,13\}$-generic set is not available.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
Describe the set of sterile points.  For the Type~I family on
$\Sigma_7$ it is closed and nowhere dense (Proposition~\ref{prop:nowhere});
is the same true for all seven families on $\Sigma_r$, and does it have
Haar measure zero?  The data for the sign fibre and the $17$-generic line
suggest sterile sets of positive measure \emph{within} these thin fibres
(\status{evidence}).
\end{problem}
