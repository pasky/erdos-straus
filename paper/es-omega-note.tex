\title[Large Erd\H{o}s--Straus witnesses]{Large Erd\H{o}s--Straus witnesses:\\
the multiplier parameter exceeds every power of $\log p$}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{Draft, 2 October 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
For a prime $p$ let $W(p)$ be the least modulus $M\equiv3\pmod4$ of a
``multiplier'' congruence that forces a solution of $4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$, and
let $\ckmin(p)$ be the least product $ck$ of a positive Type~I slice.  Both
are expected to be at most a power of $\log p$ for almost all $p$, and are
known to exceed a constant times $\log p$ for infinitely many $p$.  We prove
that for every fixed $k$,
$W(p)\ge(\log p)^{k}\exp(-C_k\log\log p/\log\log\log p)$ for infinitely many
primes $p$ in Mordell's hard classes; equivalently, the least hard prime with
$W(p)>T$ is at most $\exp(T^{o(1)})$.  The class of one is imposed modulo the
primes up to $T^{1/k+o(1)}$, so that the residual congruence system consists
of events on at most $k-1$ free primes.  We build a pointwise minorant of its
void indicator by inclusion--exclusion truncated by the number of primes in
the support, not by the number of events, and we organise the events in
\emph{levels} by support size.  Each level is sieved inside the levels below
it; by a conditional form of the local lemma its truncation, and hence the
codegree threshold it needs, depends only on its own mass.  Heavy vertex sets
(``hubs'', which do occur) are moved to lower levels by a first-moment
argument, and the bottom level, a graph, needs no codegree condition at all.
The minorant is transferred to primes by the Linnik-range prime number
theorem of Thorner and Zaman, whose relative error term absorbs a possible
Siegel zero.  As a warm-up we give the simpler prime-local construction,
which reaches exponent~$2$ and cannot go further.  For the slice parameter we
prove that congruence methods certify exactly the least quadratic
non-residue, giving $\ckmin(p)\gg\log p\log\log\log p$ infinitely often (via
Lau--Wu, after Graham--Ringrose).  On the Haar side we prove
$\log(1/\delta^*(T))\le T^{o(1)}$ for the avoider density (polylogarithmic
modulo Elsholtz--Tao), identify the single-prime atoms with points of
Elsholtz--Tao's Type~I variety, and show that an individual Type~I bound
$n^{\eta}$ would give the Haar exponent $\eta/(1+\eta)$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture asserts that $4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ is solvable
in positive integers for every $n\ge2$.  It suffices to treat primes, it has
been verified for $n\le10^{18}$ (\cite{MD}; \cite{Salez} for
$10^{17}$; see \cite{PW}, \S6), and Mordell's identities settle every prime outside the six
classes $p\equiv1^2,11^2,13^2,17^2,19^2,23^2\pmod{840}$ \cite{Mordell} (cf.\ also \cite{Oblath}); we
call such primes \emph{hard}.  On the exceptional-set side, Vaughan
\cite{Vaughan} proved that the number $E(N)$ of $n\le N$ without a
representation satisfies $E(N)\ll N\exp\{-c(\log N)^{2/3}\}$; see
\cite{PW} for a uniform modern account and for many exceptions to the
analogous statement with large numerators, and \cite{ET} for the average
number of solutions, which over primes $p\le N$ is $(\log N)^3$ up to a factor
$\log\log N$.

This note is about the \emph{pointwise size} of the smallest witness.  Two
natural witness parameters are the following.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{The multiplier parameter.}  For $M\equiv3\pmod4$ put
$A_M=(M+1)/4$.  If $uvw=A_M$ and $n\equiv-uv^{-1}\pmod M$, then
$s=(nv+u)/M$ is a positive integer and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ident}
 \frac4n=\frac1{suw}+\frac1{nsvw}+\frac1{nuvw}
\end{equation}
(direct verification; this is the classical multiplier family, cf.
\cite{Vaughan,ET}).  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
 W(n)=\min\{M\equiv3\ (4):\ n\equiv-uv^{-1}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ M)\ \text{for some}\ uvw=A_M\},
\end{equation}
with $W(n)=+\infty$ if no such $M$ exists.  Thus $W(p)\le T$ supplies an
explicit representation with a modulus at most $T$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{The slice parameter.}  A Type~I solution with
parameters $(a,b,c,k)$ satisfies $p(a+b)=k(4abc-1)$ and gives
$4/p=1/(ack)+1/(bck)+1/(pabc)$.  For an admissible pair $(c,k)$ (see
\S\ref{sec:typeI}) let $M_{c,k}(p)$ be the number of divisors of
$p^2+4ck^2$ lying in the class $-p\pmod{4ck}$; the slice is positive iff
$M_{c,k}(p)>0$ (see \S\ref{sec:typeI}).  Put $\ckmin(p)=\min\{ck:\ M_{c,k}(p)>0,\ \sfree(c)\notin\{1,2,3,6\}\}$
(the cores $1,2,3,6$ are excluded because they are deterministic on hard
primes).

\smallskip
Both parameters are expected to be small for almost all primes.  For $W$,
tails of the form
$\#\{p\le N:W(p)>T\}\ll N\exp\{-c(\log T)^2\log\log T\}$ (in the range
$\log N\gg(\log T)^3\log\log T$) are obtained in companion work whose
assembly is still under internal review; we do not use them.  In the other
direction, the class of one (every prime $p\equiv1\pmod{\lcm\{M\le T\}}$ has
$W(p)>T$) together with Linnik's theorem shows $W(p)\gg\log p$
infinitely often.  With the smaller modulus $\lcm(24,\ M\le T,\ M\equiv3\ (4))$
and Chang's least-prime bound for smooth moduli \cite{Chang}, the constant
becomes $5/8$ (\S\ref{sec:limits}).  The same is true for $\ckmin$ via
genus characters.  Whether $W(p)/\log p$ is unbounded was open.

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:TZ}; effective for each fixed $k$]\label{thm:main}
For every integer $k\ge3$ there is a constant $C_k$ such that for infinitely
many hard primes $p$
\[
 W(p)\ \ge\ (\log p)^k\exp\Big(-C_k\,\frac{\log\log p}{\log\log\log p}\Big).
\]
More precisely, for every $k\ge3$ and every sufficiently large $T$ there is a
prime $p\equiv1\pmod{840}$ with $W(p)>T$ and
$\log p\le T^{1/k}\exp(O_k(\log T/\log\log T))$.  In particular the least hard
prime $p$ with $W(p)>T$ is at most $\exp(T^{o(1)})$.
\end{theorem}

In particular the statement ``$W(p)\le(\log p)^A$ for all large $p$'' is
false for every $A$.  The cited input (Theorem~\ref{thm:TZ}) is Thorner and
Zaman's prime number theorem in the Linnik range \cite{TZ}, including the
McCurley zero-free region statement quoted there.  No hypothesis on Siegel
zeros is needed: the exceptional zero is absorbed by a uniqueness lemma
across the family of moduli (Lemma~\ref{lem:severe}) and a two-case
analysis (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}).

The proof has two layers.  The analytic layer (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer})
turns any congruence minorant of $\mathbf 1[W(n)>T]$ with controlled modulus,
$\ell^1$-mass and twisted means into a prime.  The combinatorial layer
produces such minorants.  The simplest one is \emph{prime-local}: the class of
one modulo all primes up to $T^{1/2+o(1)}$ leaves a single forbidden set at each
larger prime, and a Brun minorant applies; this gives exponent~$2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:two}), which is the limit of every prime-local design
(Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}).  To go further we quarantine only the primes
up to $y=T^{1/k+o(1)}$ (plus $T^{o(1)}$ further primes, chosen by an iterated
quarantine so that every free prime carries little mass); then moduli with up
to $k-1$ free prime factors produce events on up to $k-1$ primes.  These
cluster at ``hub'' residues on which many events fire together, so the usual
Bonferroni truncation by the number of events fails
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bonf}).  We truncate instead by the number of
\emph{primes} in the support (\S\ref{sec:supp}).  For events on at most two
primes the error is controlled by a pseudoforest bound after quarantining
high-degree hub residues (\S\ref{sec:pseudo}); this already gives
exponent~$3$ (the case $k=3$).  For larger supports a hypergraph moment bound
(\S\ref{sec:hyper}) needs \emph{codegree} conditions at the scale
$1/L$ of the truncation level $L$, and some vertex pairs (the classes of
small-height rationals such as $-4$, $-4d^2$, $-2/3$) genuinely violate them.
The remedy (\S\ref{sec:multi}) is to sieve in levels: events on $r$ primes form
level $r$, the graph level~$2$ is outermost, and each level is composed inside
the lower ones coefficient by coefficient.  A conditional local lemma shows
that the error of level $r$ is relative to the probability of avoiding the
lower levels, so its truncation, and hence its codegree threshold, depends only
on its own mass; heavy vertex sets are moved down to the level of their size by
Markov's inequality, and level~$2$ needs no codegree condition.  No arithmetic
bound on codegrees is used.  The prime side thus matches the Haar (profinite)
side, where an iterated quarantine gives $\log(1/\delta^*(T))\le T^{o(1)}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:iterq}).

For the slice parameter the picture is different: the only congruence
mechanism is the genus character, and it certifies exactly the least
quadratic non-residue $n_p$.

\begin{theorem}[Proved; (b) modulo {\cite[Prop.~5.1]{LW}}]\label{thm:slice-intro}\mbox{}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textup{(Proved.)} $\ckmin(p)\ge n_p$ for every prime
$p\equiv1\pmod{24}$, and every fixed-period congruence minorant for
$\{\ckmin(p)>T\}$ is supported, among large primes, on primes with $n_p>T$
\textup{(Corollary~\ref{cor:exactly})}.
\item[(b)] \textup{(Proved modulo \cite[Prop.~5.1]{LW}.)}
$\ckmin(p)\gg\log p\cdot\log\log\log p$ for infinitely many hard primes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Thus a congruence proof of $\ckmin(p)>(\log p)^{1+\delta}$ infinitely often
would improve the best known unconditional (Graham--Ringrose) and
GRH-conditional (Montgomery) $\Omega$-results for $n_p$, and would exceed the
random-model prediction $\max_{p\le x}n_p\asymp\log x\log\log x$.

Finally, on the Haar (profinite) side, let $\delta^*(T)$ be the Haar measure
of the set of $n\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}^\times$ with $n\equiv1\pmod{24}$ and
$W(n)>T$, normalised within the class $1\pmod{24}$.  The class of one gives
$\log(1/\delta^*(T))\le(2/3+o(1))T$, and a crude prime-local bound gives
$T^{1/2+o(1)}$.

\begin{theorem}[Proved]\label{thm:haar-intro}
$\log(1/\delta^*(T))\le T^{1/3+o(1)}$.
\end{theorem}

We do not pursue heuristics about the true size of $\delta^*(T)$ here.

\section{The atoms of the multiplier problem}\label{sec:atoms}

For $M\equiv3\pmod4$ put $\cR(M)=\{-4D\bmod M:\ D\mid A_M^2\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:atoms}
$\{-uv^{-1}\bmod M:\ uvw=A_M\}=\cR(M)$.  Consequently, for every integer $n$,
\[
 W(n)>T\iff n\bmod M\notin\cR(M)\ \text{for every}\ M\le T,\ M\equiv3\ (4).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $4A_M\equiv1\pmod M$ and $\gcd(A_M,M)=1$, from $uvw=A_M$ we get
$v^{-1}\equiv4uw$, hence $-uv^{-1}\equiv-4u^2w$ and $D=u^2w$ divides $A_M^2$.
Conversely let $D\mid A_M^2$.  For each prime $q$ with $q^a\Vert A_M$ and
$q^d\Vert D$ ($d\le2a$) put $u_q=\max(0,d-a)$, $w_q=d-2u_q$, $v_q=a-u_q-w_q$;
these are non-negative, so $u=\prod q^{u_q}$, $v=\prod q^{v_q}$,
$w=\prod q^{w_q}$ satisfy $uvw=A_M$ and $u^2w=D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Class of one; proved]\label{lem:one}
$1\notin\cR(M)$ for every $M\equiv3\pmod4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $-4D\equiv1$ then $D\equiv-A_M\pmod M$.  The map $D\mapsto A_M^2/D$
preserves this congruence, so we may assume $D\le A_M$; then
$0<D+A_M\le2A_M<M$ while $M\mid D+A_M$.
\end{proof}

\section{The prime-local residual system}\label{sec:local}

Fix $T$ large and put $\Lc=\log T$,
\begin{gather*}
 y=\sqrt T\,\exp(3\Lc/\log\Lc),\\
 Q=\lcm\big(24,\ \ell^{e_\ell}:\ \ell\le y\ \text{prime},\ \ell^{e_\ell}\le T\ \text{maximal}\big),
\end{gather*}
and let $\cU$ be the set of primes in $(y,T]$.  Then $\log Q\le2y$ for large
$T$ (only $\ell\le\sqrt T$ have $e_\ell\ge2$), and $840\mid Q$.  For
$\ell\in\cU$ let
\begin{gather*}
 F_\ell=\{-4D\bmod\ell:\ m\le T/\ell,\ m\ell\equiv3\ (4),\ D\mid A_{m\ell}^2,\
 m\mid 4D+1\},\\
 g(\ell)=\frac{|F_\ell|}{\ell-1},\qquad S=\sum_{\ell\in\cU}g(\ell).
\end{gather*}
All elements of $F_\ell$ are units.

\begin{lemma}[Prime-local reduction; proved]\label{lem:local}
If $n\equiv1\pmod Q$ and $n\bmod\ell\notin F_\ell$ for all $\ell\in\cU$,
then $W(n)>T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $M\le T$, $M\equiv3\pmod4$.  If every prime factor of $M$ is at most $y$,
then $M\mid Q$, so $n\equiv1\pmod M$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:one} applies.
Otherwise let $\ell>y$ divide $M$; since $\ell^2>T$ we have $\ell\Vert M$, and
$m=M/\ell<T/y\le y$, so $m\mid Q$ and $n\equiv1\pmod m$.  If
$n\equiv-4D\pmod M$ with $D\mid A_M^2$, then $m\mid4D+1$ and
$n\bmod\ell\in F_\ell$.  Now use Lemma~\ref{lem:atoms}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local sizes; proved]\label{lem:sizes}
$g(\ell)\le2T\tau^*(T)^2/y^2$, where $\tau^*(T)=\max_{n\le T}\tau(n)$.  In
particular $\max_\ell g(\ell)\le1/16$ for large $T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each $m\le T/\ell$ there are at most $\tau(A^2)\le\tau(A)^2$ choices of
$D$, and $1/(\ell-1)\le2/\ell$.  Since $\log\tau^*(T)\le(\log2+o(1))\Lc/\log\Lc$
\cite[Thm.~317]{HW}, the bound is $\le2\exp((2\log2-6+o(1))\Lc/\log\Lc)$.
\end{proof}

The next lemma is the key arithmetic input: the congruence $m\mid4D+1$
saves a factor $m$.

\begin{lemma}[Mass bound; proved]\label{lem:mass}
With $X=(T+1)/4$,
\begin{align*}
 S&\le\tfrac43(3+\log X)\sum_{\substack{sr^2\le X\\ s\ \text{squarefree}}}\frac{\tau(4sr^2+1)}{sr}\\
 &\le 2(3+\Lc)(1+\Lc)^2\tau^*(T+2)=\exp\Big(O\Big(\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}\Big)\Big).
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Clearly $S\le\sum 1/(\ell-1)$ over triples $(m,\ell,D)$ as in the definition
of $F_\ell$; put $A=A_{m\ell}$.  Since $4A\equiv1$ and $4D\equiv-1\pmod m$, we
have $4A^2D^{-1}\equiv-1\pmod m$, so $D\mapsto A^2/D$ preserves $m\mid4D+1$
and exchanges $D>A$ with $D<A$; hence it suffices to count $D\le A$ and
double.  Write $D=sr^2$ with $s$ squarefree.  Then $D\mid A^2$ iff $sr\mid A$;
write $A=srk$, so that $D\le A$ iff $k\ge r$.  The map
$(m,\ell,D)\mapsto(m,s,r,k)$ is injective, and
$1/(\ell-1)\le2/\ell=2m/(4A-1)\le2m/(3A)$.

From $m\mid4sr^2+1$ and $m\mid m\ell=4srk-1$ we get $m\mid4sr(r+k)$, and
$\gcd(m,4sr)=1$, so
\[
 m\mid r+k .
\]
Since $k\ge r\ge1$, the least admissible $k$ is at least $\max(r,m-r)\ge m/2$,
so $\sum_{k\equiv-r\,(m),\ k\le X}1/k\le(3+\log X)/m$.  Summing over $m\mid4sr^2+1$
gives the first bound; the second follows from $\sum_{s\le X}1/s\cdot\sum_{r\le\sqrt X}1/r\le(1+\Lc)^2$
and $4sr^2+1\le T+2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Without the congruence $m\mid r+k$ the same count gives $S\le T^{1/2-\eta+o(1)}$
for $y=T^{1/2+\eta}$, and the transfer below then only yields $W(p)\gg\log p$.
Numerically $S\approx 6.5,\ 13.6,\ 23.9,\ 38.5$ for $T=10^3,\dots,10^6$ (with
$y=\sqrt T$), i.e.\ $S$ is polylogarithmic in practice.  These values
illustrate $S$ only: the parameter $y=\sqrt T$ does \emph{not} instantiate
Theorem~\ref{thm:two}.  At $y=\sqrt T$ one has $\max g\approx0.08$--$0.33>1/16$,
and the twist condition of \S\ref{sec:proof} can fail (numerically it does
at $T=10^4$).  The bound $g\le1/16$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:sizes}, and hence
Theorem~\ref{thm:two}, is asymptotic: with the $y$ above, $y<T$ (so that
$\cU\ne\emptyset$) only when $\log T>e^6\approx403$.
\end{remark}

\section{The analytic input}\label{sec:analytic}

\begin{theorem}[Thorner--Zaman {\cite[Cor.~1.4 and Rem.~1.5]{TZ}}; cited]\label{thm:TZ}
There are absolute effectively computable constants $c_4>0$, $C_0$ such
that for coprime $a,q$ with $q\ge2$ and $x\ge q^{12}$,
\begin{gather*}
 \sum_{\substack{p\le x\\ p\equiv a\,(q)}}\log p=\frac{\lambda x}{\varphi(q)}\,(1+\varepsilon),\\
 |\varepsilon|\le C_0\Big(e^{-c_4\log x/\log q}+e^{-c_4(\log x)^{3/5}/(\log\log x)^{1/5}}\Big),
\end{gather*}
where $\lambda=1-\chi_1(a)x^{\beta_1-1}/\beta_1$ if the exceptional zero $\beta_1$
for the modulus $q$ exists and $\lambda=1$ otherwise.  Here, following
McCurley \cite{McC} as quoted in \cite[p.~1]{TZ}, $\prod_{\chi\bmod q}L(s,\chi)$
has no zero in $\Re s\ge1-1/(13\log(q(|\Im s|+3)))$ apart from at most one
real zero $\beta_1$, which is simple and belongs to a unique real character
$\chi_1$; also $0<\lambda<2$.
\end{theorem}

We used the arXiv version; we did not compare the numbering with the
published version.  We also use the quoted McCurley statement for moduli
other than those of Theorem~\ref{thm:TZ}; alternatively the classical
Landau--Page theorem \cite[Ch.~14]{Dav} with a constant $c\le1/13$ in place
of $1/13$ works verbatim.

\begin{lemma}[One severe character per family; proved modulo the McCurley statement]\label{lem:severe}
Let $\cQ$ be a finite set of moduli, all $\le Z$.  Call $(\chi^*,\beta)$
\emph{severe} if $\chi^*$ is a real primitive character whose conductor $q^*$
divides some $q\in\cQ$, $L(\beta,\chi^*)=0$ and $\beta\ge1-1/(13\log(3Z^2))$.
Then there is at most one severe pair.  If $(\chi^*,\beta^*)$ is severe and
$q^*\mid q\in\cQ$, then $\beta_1=\beta^*$ and $\chi_1$ is induced by $\chi^*$ for
the modulus $q$.  If $q\in\cQ$ and no severe pair has $q^*\mid q$, then any exceptional
$\beta_1$ of $q$ satisfies $x^{\beta_1-1}/\beta_1\le2\exp(-\log x/(13\log3Z^2))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $(\chi_a,\beta_a),(\chi_b,\beta_b)$ are severe with conductors dividing
$q_a,q_b\in\cQ$, put $q'=\lcm(q_a,q_b)\le Z^2$.  Both characters induce
characters mod $q'$ with the same zeros in $\Re s>0$, and both $\beta$ lie in
McCurley's region for $q'$; uniqueness there gives $\beta_a=\beta_b$ and
$\chi_a=\chi_b$.  The second claim holds because $\beta^*$ lies in McCurley's
region for $q\le Z$.  For the third, an exceptional zero of $q$ in
$[1-1/(13\log 3Z^2),1)$ would be severe; also $\beta_1\ge1-1/(13\log6)>1/2$.
\end{proof}

\section{A transfer theorem}\label{sec:transfer}

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:TZ}]\label{thm:transfer}
Let $T\ge1$, $Q\ge2$, and let $B(n)=\sum_{i\in I}c_i\,\mathbf 1[n\equiv b_i\ (d_i)]$
be a finite real combination with $\gcd(d_i,Q)=\gcd(b_i,d_i)=1$ and
$B(n)\le\mathbf 1[W(n)>T]$ for all $n\equiv1\pmod Q$.  Put
$Z=Q\max d_i$, $\mu=\sum c_i/\varphi(d_i)$, $M_1=\sum|c_i|/\varphi(d_i)$, and assume
$\mu>0$ and the \emph{twist condition}: for every real primitive character
$\psi$ of conductor $f>1$ with $\gcd(f,Q)=1$ and $f\mid d_i$ for some $i$,
\[
 |\mu_\psi|\le\mu/4,\qquad \mu_\psi=\sum_{i:\,f\mid d_i}c_i\psi(b_i)/\varphi(d_i).
\]
Let $K=1+\log(M_1/\mu)$.  There is an absolute $C_1$ such that if
$\log x\ge C_1K\max(\log Z,K)$, then some prime $p\le x$, $p\equiv1\pmod Q$,
has $W(p)>T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $q_i=Qd_i$ and let $a_i$ be the class $\equiv1\ (Q)$, $\equiv b_i\ (d_i)$;
it is a unit class.  Then
$\sum_{p\le x,\,p\equiv1(Q)}\log p\,B(p)=\sum_ic_i\theta(x;q_i,a_i)$.  Since
$M_1\ge\mu$ we have $K\ge1$, so taking $C_1\ge12$ gives
$\log x\ge12\log Z$, i.e.\ $x\ge Z^{12}\ge q_i^{12}$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:TZ} applies to every term.  Put
$E_1=e^{-c_4\log x/\log Z}+e^{-c_4(\log x)^{3/5}/(\log\log x)^{1/5}}$ and
$E_2=e^{-\log x/(13\log3Z^2)}$.  Since $\gcd(Q,d_i)=1$ we have
$\varphi(q_i)=\varphi(Q)\varphi(d_i)$, and $q_i\le Z$, so
\[
 \sum_ic_i\theta(x;q_i,a_i)=\frac{x}{\varphi(Q)}\sum_i\frac{c_i\lambda_i(1+\varepsilon_i)}{\varphi(d_i)},\qquad
 |\varepsilon_i|\le C_0E_1,
\]
with $\lambda_i$ the factor of Theorem~\ref{thm:TZ} for $(q_i,a_i)$.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:severe} to $\cQ=\{q_i\}$; a severe $\beta$ satisfies
$\beta\ge1-1/(13\log 6)>1/2$.  Write the conductor of a severe $\chi^*$ as $q^*=q^*_Qq^{*\prime}$ with
$q^*_Q$ supported on the primes of $Q$ and $\gcd(q^{*\prime},Q)=1$, and
$\chi^*=\chi^*_Q\chi^{*\prime}$.

\emph{Case A: a severe pair exists with $q^*\mid Q$.}  Then $q^*\mid q_i$ for
every $i$.  By the second claim of Lemma~\ref{lem:severe}, every $\lambda_i$ is
formed with the same pair $(\chi^*,\beta^*)$, so
$\lambda_i=1-\chi^*(a_i)x^{\beta^*-1}/\beta^*=1-x^{\beta^*-1}/\beta^*=:\lambda_*$,
because $a_i\equiv1\pmod{q^*}$.  Moreover $\lambda_*>0$: since $\log x\ge12\log Z>2$ and
$1/2<\beta^*<1$,
$\log\beta^*\ge1-1/\beta^*>2(\beta^*-1)>(\beta^*-1)\log x$, i.e.\
$x^{\beta^*-1}/\beta^*<1$.
So
$\sum c_i\theta\ge(x\lambda_*/\varphi(Q))(\mu-C_0E_1M_1)$.

\emph{Case B: otherwise.}  If a severe $\chi^*$ exists, then $q^*$ divides some
$Qd_j$ with $\gcd(d_j,Q)=1$, so $q^*_Q\mid Q$.  Hence $q^*\mid q_i$ iff
$q^{*\prime}\mid d_i$, and in Case~B we have $q^{*\prime}>1$.  For such $i$,
$\chi_1=\chi^*$ on units mod $q_i$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:severe}), and, as
$a_i\equiv1\pmod{q^*_Q}$ and $a_i\equiv b_i\pmod{q^{*\prime}}$,
$\chi^*(a_i)=\chi^*_Q(1)\chi^{*\prime}(b_i)=\psi(b_i)$ with $\psi=\chi^{*\prime}$.  So
$\lambda_i=1-\psi(b_i)x^{\beta^*-1}/\beta^*$.  This $\psi$ is real primitive (the
coprime-conductor factors of a real primitive character are real primitive), of
conductor $q^{*\prime}>1$ coprime to $Q$ and dividing some $d_i$, so the twist
condition applies to it.  For all other $i$ (and for all $i$ if there is no
severe pair), $|\lambda_i-1|\le x^{\beta_1-1}/\beta_1\le2E_2$ by the third claim of
Lemma~\ref{lem:severe}.  Since $\lambda_i<2$ and
$x^{\beta^*-1}/\beta^*\le1$, splitting $\lambda_i=1+(\lambda_i-1)$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum c_i\theta&\ge\frac{x}{\varphi(Q)}\big(\mu-2|\mu_\psi|-M_1(2C_0E_1+2E_2)\big)\\
 &\ge\frac{x}{\varphi(Q)}\big(\mu/2-M_1(2C_0E_1+2E_2)\big),
\end{align*}
using $|\mu_\psi|\le\mu/4$.  Both brackets are positive once
$E_1,E_2\le\mu/(8(C_0+1)M_1)=e^{1-K}/(8(C_0+1))$.  This holds if $C_1$ is large:
$\log x/\log Z\ge C_1K$, $(\log x)^{3/5}/(\log\log x)^{1/5}\ge(\log x)^{1/2}\ge
C_1^{1/2}K$ and $\log x/(13\log3Z^2)\ge C_1K/52$ (as $Z\ge2$), so each of the
three exponentials is at most $e^{-cC_1^{1/2}K}$ with $c>0$ absolute.  Hence
$\sum_p\log p\,\mathbf 1[W(p)>T]\ge\sum_p\log p\,B(p)>0$.
\end{proof}

No lower bound for $1-\beta^*$ is used: in Case~A the exceptional factor
$\lambda_*$ multiplies main term and error alike.  A prime number theorem
valid only for $q\le\exp(c\sqrt{\log x})$ would force $\log x\gg(\log Z)^2\ge T$;
the Linnik-range uniformity is essential.

\section{Warm-up: exponent two by a prime-local design}\label{sec:proof}

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:TZ}; effective]\label{thm:two}
For infinitely many hard primes $p$,
$W(p)\ge(\log p)^2\exp(-C\log\log p/\log\log\log p)$.  More precisely, for
every large $T$ there is a prime $p\equiv1\pmod Q$ \textup{(}$Q$ as in
\S\ref{sec:local}\textup{)} with $W(p)>T$ and
$\log p\le T^{1/2}\exp(O(\log T/\log\log T))$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} supersedes this; we include it because its minorant is
a plain Brun truncation and it isolates the arithmetic input
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mass}) that the main proof reuses.

\begin{proof}

Let $J$ be the least even integer $\ge22S+10$ and
\begin{gather*}
 B(n)=\sum_{P\subseteq\cU,\ |P|\le J-1}(-1)^{|P|}\prod_{\ell\in P}\mathbf 1[n\bmod\ell\in F_\ell]
 =\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}(-1)^j\binom{N(n)}j,\\
 N(n)=\#\{\ell\in\cU:\ n\bmod\ell\in F_\ell\}.
\end{gather*}
\emph{Pointwise bound.}  For $N\ge1$,
$\sum_{j\le J-1}(-1)^j\binom Nj=(-1)^{J-1}\binom{N-1}{J-1}\le0$; hence
$B\le\mathbf 1[N=0]\le\mathbf 1[W>T]$ on $n\equiv1\ (Q)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:local}.
Expanding $\mathbf 1[n\bmod\ell\in F_\ell]=\sum_{a\in F_\ell}\mathbf 1[n\equiv a\ (\ell)]$ puts $B$
in the form of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, with squarefree moduli
$d_P=\prod_{\ell\in P}\ell\le T^{J}$ coprime to $Q$.

\emph{Mean and mass.}  With $e_j$ the elementary symmetric functions of the
$g(\ell)$, $\mu=\sum_{j<J}(-1)^je_j$ and $M_1=\sum_{j<J}e_j\le e^S$.  Let $N$ be a sum
of independent Bernoulli$(g(\ell))$ variables; the identity above and
$\binom{N-1}{J-1}\le\binom NJ$ give
$|\mu-V|\le e_J\le(eS/J)^J\le e^{-2J}$, where $V=\prod(1-g(\ell))\ge e^{-2S}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sizes}).  So $\mu\ge0.99V$ and $\log(M_1/\mu)\le3S+1$.

\emph{Twist.}  If $\psi$ is real primitive of conductor $f>1$, coprime to $Q$,
dividing some $d_P$, then $f=\prod_{\ell\in P_f}\ell$ with $r=|P_f|\ge1$ and
$\psi=(\cdot/f)$.  With $h(\ell)=\sum_{a\in F_\ell}(a/\ell)/(\ell-1)$, $|h|\le g$,
\[
 \mu_\psi=(-1)^r\prod_{\ell\in P_f}h(\ell)\sum_{P'\subseteq\cU\setminus P_f,\ |P'|\le J-1-r}(-1)^{|P'|}\prod_{P'}g ,
\]
which vanishes if $r\ge J$.  The inner sum is $V'+\varepsilon'$ with
$V'=V/\prod_{P_f}(1-g)\le(16/15)^rV$ and $|\varepsilon'|\le e_{J-r}\le S^{J-r}/(J-r)!$, so,
with $g\le1/16$,
$|\mu_\psi|\le15^{-r}V+16^{-r}S^{J-r}/(J-r)!$.  The first term is $\le V/15$.
If $r\le J/2$, the second is $\le(eS/(J-r))^{J-r}\le(2eS/J)^{J/2}\le(e/11)^{J/2}
\le e^{-0.69J}\le e^{-15S-6}$, as $\tfrac12\log(11/e)>0.69$ and $J\ge22S+10$.  If
$r>J/2$, it is $\le16^{-J/2}e^S\le e^{-1.38(22S+10)+S}\le e^{-15S-6}$.  Since
$e^{-15S}\le e^{-2S}\le V$, we get $|\mu_\psi|\le(1/15+e^{-6})V\le0.07V\le\mu/4$.

\emph{Moduli.}  $\log Z\le\log Q+J\Lc\le3y$ because $S=T^{o(1)}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mass}).  Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} gives a prime $p\equiv1\pmod Q$
with $W(p)>T$ and $\log p\le C_1(3S+2)\cdot3y\le\sqrt T\exp((3+\log2+o(1))\Lc/\log\Lc)$.
Since $840\mid Q$ the prime is hard, and $p>Q>T$ gives infinitely many distinct
primes.  Inverting: if $\Lc\ge3\log\log p$ then already $W(p)>T\ge(\log p)^3$;
otherwise $\Lc/\log\Lc\ll\log\log p/\log\log\log p$ and
$\Lc\ge2\log\log p-O(\log\log p/\log\log\log p)$.  All inputs are effective.
\end{proof}

\section{Support-truncated inclusion--exclusion}\label{sec:supp}

\emph{Setting.}  Let $\mathcal P$ be a finite set of primes and
$X=(X_\ell)_{\ell\in\mathcal P}$ independent random variables.  Let $\mathcal E$ be
a finite family of events; each $E\in\mathcal E$ has a nonempty support
$\supp E\subseteq\mathcal P$ and depends only on
$(X_\ell)_{\ell\in\supp E}$.  For an outcome $x$ put
\begin{gather*}
 A(x)=\{E\in\mathcal E:\ E\text{ occurs}\},\quad
 V(x)=\bigcup_{E\in A(x)}\supp E,\quad N(x)=|V(x)|,\\
 a_\ell(x)=\#\{E\in A(x):\ \ell\in\supp E\}.
\end{gather*}
For $L\ge0$ and $F\subseteq\mathcal E$ with $\supp F=\bigcup_{E\in F}\supp E$, define
\begin{gather*}
 B_L(x)=\sum_{F\subseteq A(x),\ |\supp F|\le L}(-1)^{|F|},\\
 G_{L+1}(x)=e_{L+1}\big(a(x)\big)=\sum_{P\subseteq\mathcal P,\ |P|=L+1}\ \prod_{\ell\in P}a_\ell(x).
\end{gather*}
The truncation is by the number of \emph{primes} involved, not by the number of
events; this is what defeats the hub configurations of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bonf}.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:closed}
Call $W\subseteq\mathcal P$ independent at $x$ if no $E\in A(x)$ has
$\supp E\subseteq W$.  If $A(x)=\emptyset$ then $B_L(x)=1$.
Otherwise
\[
 B_L(x)=\sum_{\substack{W\subsetneq V(x)\ \text{independent at }x\\ |W|\le L}}
 (-1)^{L-|W|}\binom{N-|W|-1}{L-|W|},
\]
and in particular $B_L(x)=0$ whenever $1\le N(x)\le L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x$, write $A,V,N$.  For $U\subseteq V$ the alternating sum over all
$F\subseteq\{E\in A:\supp E\subseteq U\}$ is
$I(U)=\mathbf 1[\text{no }E\in A\text{ has }\supp E\subseteq U]$, so by M\"obius
inversion on the Boolean lattice
$\sum_{F\subseteq A,\ \supp F=U}(-1)^{|F|}=\sum_{W\subseteq U}(-1)^{|U\setminus W|}I(W)$.
Summing over $U\subseteq V$ with $|U|\le L$,
\[
 B_L=\sum_{W\subseteq V,\ I(W)=1,\ |W|\le L}\ \sum_{j=0}^{L-|W|}(-1)^j\binom{N-|W|}j .
\]
The inner sum is $(-1)^{L-|W|}\binom{N-|W|-1}{L-|W|}$ if $|W|<N$ and $1$ if $W=V$;
and $I(V)=1$ iff $A=\emptyset$.  If $N\le L$ every binomial
$\binom{N-|W|-1}{L-|W|}$ vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:pointwise}
For every $x$ and $L\ge0$,
\begin{gather*}
 B^*_L(x):=B_L(x)-4^{L+1}G_{L+1}(x)\le\mathbf 1[A(x)=\emptyset],\\
 |B_L(x)-\mathbf 1[A(x)=\emptyset]|\le4^{L+1}G_{L+1}(x).
\end{gather*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $A=\emptyset$ then $a\equiv0$ and $B_L=1$.  Let $A\ne\emptyset$.  If $N\le L$
then $B_L=0$.  If $N\ge L+1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:closed} and Vandermonde give
$|B_L|\le\sum_{w\le L}\binom Nw\binom N{L-w}=\binom{2N}L$.  For
$f(N)=\binom{2N}L/\binom N{L+1}$ one computes
\[
 \frac{f(N+1)}{f(N)}=\frac{2(2N+1)(N-L)}{(2N+2-L)(2N+1-L)},
\]
and with $t=N-L\ge1$ the denominator exceeds the numerator by
$L^2+3L+4t+2$; so
$\binom{2N}L\le f(L+1)\binom N{L+1}\le4^{L+1}\binom N{L+1}$.  Finally
$\binom N{L+1}=e_{L+1}(\mathbf 1[\ell\in V])\le e_{L+1}(a)$.
\end{proof}

For a finite combination $\Phi=\sum_ic_i\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_i}$ of indicators of
cells $\mathcal C_i$ (a cell being a product of single values of finitely many
coordinates) we write $M_1(\Phi)=\sum_i|c_i|P(\mathcal C_i)$, after merging equal
cells; for congruence classes this is the $M_1$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:meanmass}
Let $z\ge16$ and $\Lambda_z=\log E\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P}(1+za_\ell)$.  Then
$E\,G_{L+1}\le z^{-L-1}e^{\Lambda_z}$ and
$E|B^*_L-\mathbf 1[A=\emptyset]|\le2\cdot4^{L+1}z^{-L-1}e^{\Lambda_z}$; for $z=16$
this is $2\cdot4^{-L-1}e^{\Lambda_{16}}$.  If every event is a union of cells,
then $B_L$ is a combination of cells on supports of size $\le L$ with
$M_1(B_L)\le E\prod_\ell(1+2a_\ell)$, and $G_{L+1}$ is a nonnegative
combination of cells on supports of size $\le k(L+1)$, $k=\max|\supp E|$,
with $M_1(G_{L+1})=E\,G_{L+1}$.  If moreover no event contains a cell with a
non-unit coordinate, then only unit cells have nonzero coefficient.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since all $a_\ell\ge0$, we have $e_{L+1}(a)\le z^{-L-1}\prod_\ell(1+za_\ell)$
termwise.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise},
\[
 E|B^*_L-\mathbf 1[A=\emptyset]|\le E|B_L-\mathbf 1[A=\emptyset]|+4^{L+1}E\,G_{L+1}
 \le2\cdot4^{L+1}E\,G_{L+1}.
\]

Write $B_L=\sum_{F\subseteq\mathcal E,\,|\supp F|\le L}(-1)^{|F|}\prod_{E\in F}\mathbf 1_E$
and group by $U=\supp F$.  On a cell $c$ of the coordinates in $U$, let
$A_U(c)$ be the set of events with support in $U$ that contain $c$, and
$I_c(W)=\mathbf 1[\text{no }E\in A_U(c)\text{ has }\supp E\subseteq W]$.  The
coefficient of $c$ is
\[
 \kappa(U,c)=\sum_{F\subseteq A_U(c),\ \supp F=U}(-1)^{|F|}
 =\sum_{W\subseteq U}(-1)^{|U\setminus W|}I_c(W)
\]
by the M\"obius inversion of Lemma~\ref{lem:closed}; so $|\kappa(U,c)|\le2^{|U|}$.
If some $\ell\in U$ lies in the support of no event of $A_U(c)$, then
$I_c(W)=I_c(W\cup\{\ell\})$ and the terms cancel in pairs, so $\kappa(U,c)=0$; in
particular $\kappa(U,c)=0$ unless $c$ is a unit cell, under the last
hypothesis.  For the remaining cells, every $\ell\in U$ is covered by an event
occurring on $c$, so $\mathbf 1_c\le\prod_{\ell\in U}a_\ell$ on $c$.  Hence
\begin{align*}
 M_1(B_L)&\le\sum_U2^{|U|}P(\text{every }\ell\in U\text{ covered})\\
 &\le\sum_U2^{|U|}E\prod_{\ell\in U}a_\ell=E\prod_\ell(1+2a_\ell).
\end{align*}
Finally $G_{L+1}=\sum_{|P|=L+1}\prod_{\ell\in P}\sum_{E\ni\ell}\mathbf 1_E$ expands
into products of event indicators with coefficient $+1$, so its mass equals its
mean.
\end{proof}

\section{A pseudoforest bound for the exponential moment}\label{sec:pseudo}

\emph{Graph-type systems.}  Each $X_\ell$ takes values in a finite set
$\Omega_\ell$.  The events are of two kinds.  \emph{Singles}: one set
$S_\ell\subseteq\Omega_\ell$ per $\ell$, with $g_\ell=P(X_\ell\in S_\ell)$ and
$S_1=\sum_\ell g_\ell$.  \emph{Edges}: a \emph{vertex} is a pair $v=(\ell,V_v)$
with $V_v\subseteq\Omega_\ell$, distinct vertices at the same $\ell$ being
disjoint, and $p(v)=P(X_\ell\in V_v)$; an edge is an unordered pair $\{u,w\}$ of
vertices at distinct primes, occurring iff $X_{\ell_u}\in V_u$ and
$X_{\ell_w}\in V_w$.  The edge graph is simple: edges are distinct pairs of
vertices (for congruences, distinct classes modulo $\ell\ell'$), repeated atoms
being merged.  Put
\[
 S_2=\sum_{\text{edges}}p(u)p(w),\quad \deg(v)=\sum_{w:\{v,w\}\text{ edge}}p(w),\quad
 w_\ell=\sum_{\text{edges at }\ell}p(u)p(w).
\]
Then $a_\ell=s_\ell+d_\ell$ with $s_\ell=\mathbf 1[X_\ell\in S_\ell]$ and $d_\ell$ the
number of occurring edges at $\ell$.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:pseudo}
Let $z\ge1$ and suppose $\deg(v)\le\delta\le e^{-3z-2}$ for every vertex.  Then
$\Lambda_z\le zS_1+16e^{6z+2}S_2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use $1+z(s+d)\le(1+zs)(1+zd)$ and expand
$\prod_\ell(1+zd_\ell)=\sum_{(U,f)}z^{|U|}\mathbf 1[f(\ell)\text{ occurs for all }\ell\in U]$,
where $f$ assigns to each $\ell\in U$ an edge at a vertex of $\ell$.  Let
$F=f(U)$.  If $F$ occurs, its vertices lie at distinct primes, and each
component $K$ of $F$ has $|E(K)|\le|V(K)|$ (each edge of $K$ is chosen by a prime
whose vertex lies in $K$); so $F$ is a pseudoforest.  For fixed $F$, the weight
of the pairs $(U,f)$ with $f(U)=F$ is at most
$\prod_{v\in V(F)}(1+z\deg_F(v))\le e^{2z|V(F)|}$.  For the singles,
independence gives
$E[\prod_{\ell\in U_s}s_\ell\,\mathbf 1[F\text{ occurs}]]\le\prod_{\ell\in U_s\setminus\pi(F)}g_\ell\cdot P(F\text{ occurs})$,
$\pi(F)$ being the primes of $V(F)$; summing $z^{|U_s|}$ over $U_s$ costs
$(1+z)^{|\pi(F)|}\prod_\ell(1+zg_\ell)$.  Hence
\begin{align*}
 E\prod_\ell(1+za_\ell)&\le e^{zS_1}\sum_Fe^{3z|V(F)|}P(F\text{ occurs})\\
 &\le e^{zS_1}\exp\Big(\sum_Ke^{3z|V(K)|}P(K\text{ occurs})\Big),
\end{align*}
$K$ running over connected pseudoforests; here we used that $P(F)$ is the
product over components when these lie on disjoint primes and $0$ otherwise.
A connected pseudoforest on $v$ vertices is a spanning tree $T$ plus at most one
of $\le v^2/2$ further pairs, and $P(K)\le P(T)\le\prod_{w\in T}p(w)$.  Encode a
tree rooted at $v_0$ by its child sets $C_1,\dots,C_v$ in breadth-first order
(children ordered by a fixed total order); every tree is recovered once for
each choice of root, so summing over roots overcounts by the factor $v$.
Summing over $C_i\subseteq N(v_i)$ with $|C_i|=c_i$ gives at most
$\deg(v_i)^{c_i}/c_i!$, and for $i=1$, $c_1\ge1$, at most
$\deg(v_0)\delta^{c_1-1}/c_1!$; and
$\sum_{c_1+\dots+c_v=v-1}\prod1/c_i!=v^{v-1}/(v-1)!\le e^v$.  So the trees with
$v\ge2$ vertices contribute at most
$\sum_{v_0}p(v_0)\deg(v_0)e^v\delta^{v-2}=2S_2e^v\delta^{v-2}$, and
\[
 \sum_Ke^{3z|V(K)|}P(K)\le2S_2e^{6z+2}\sum_{j\ge0}\big(3+2j+\tfrac{j^2}2\big)(e^{3z+1}\delta)^j\le16e^{6z+2}S_2,
\]
as $e^{3z+1}\delta\le e^{-1}$ and $\sum_j(3+2j+j^2/2)e^{-j}<7.6$.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis is a bound on \emph{vertex degrees}; configurations in which a
complete bipartite graph of events fires (the hubs of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bonf}) are not excluded.  It is enforced as follows.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:hub}
For $\delta>0$ let $H=\{v:\deg(v)>\delta\}$.  Replace $S_\ell$ by
$S_\ell^+=S_\ell\cup\bigcup_{v\in H\text{ at }\ell}V_v$ and delete the edges with an
endpoint in $H$.  Then the absence of every new event implies the absence of
every old event; every remaining vertex has degree $\le\delta$;
$g_\ell^+\le g_\ell+w_\ell/\delta$, $S_1^+\le S_1+2S_2/\delta$ and $S_2^+\le S_2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An old edge at $v\in H$ cannot occur without the new single at $\ell_v$.
Degrees only decrease.  By Markov,
$\sum_{v\in H\text{ at }\ell}p(v)\le\sum_{v\text{ at }\ell}p(v)\deg(v)/\delta=w_\ell/\delta$,
and $\sum_\ell w_\ell=2S_2$.
\end{proof}

\section{An abstract criterion}\label{sec:crit}

\begin{lemma}[Local lemma with conditioning; classical, proof included]\label{lem:lll}
Let $\mathcal A$ be finitely many events in a product probability space, each
depending on a finite set of coordinates; write $A'\sim A$ if they share a
coordinate.  Suppose $x_A\in[0,1)$ satisfy
$P(A)\le x_A\prod_{A'\sim A}(1-x_{A'})$.  Then
$P(\bigcap_{\mathcal A}\bar A)\ge\prod_{\mathcal A}(1-x_A)$, and for every event $B$
depending on a set of coordinates,
$P(B\mid\bigcap_{\mathcal A}\bar A)\le P(B)\prod_{A\in\Gamma(B)}(1-x_A)^{-1}$, where
$\Gamma(B)$ is the set of $A\in\mathcal A$ sharing a coordinate with $B$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first statement is \cite[Lemma~5.1.1]{AS}, whose proof shows
$P(A\mid\bigcap_{A'\in\mathcal S}\bar A')\le x_A$ for every $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal A$
with $A\notin\mathcal S$.  For the second (cf.\ \cite[Thm.~2.1]{HSS}), $B$ is
independent of $\bigcap_{A\notin\Gamma(B)}\bar A$, so
\[
 P\Big(B\ \Big|\ \bigcap_{\mathcal A}\bar A\Big)
 \le\frac{P(B)\,P(\bigcap_{A\notin\Gamma(B)}\bar A)}{P(\bigcap_{\mathcal A}\bar A)}
 =\frac{P(B)}{P(\bigcap_{\Gamma(B)}\bar A\mid\bigcap_{A\notin\Gamma(B)}\bar A)},
\]
and the chain rule with the displayed claim bounds the denominator below by
$\prod_{\Gamma(B)}(1-x_A)$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Congruence systems.}  Fix $Q$ with $2\mid Q$, a finite set $\mathcal P$ of
primes coprime to $Q$, and exponents $e_\ell\ge1$; put $X_\ell=n\bmod\ell^{e_\ell}$.
The Haar measure is the uniform measure on $\prod_\ell(\mathbb Z/\ell^{e_\ell})^\times$;
in it Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} computes $\mu$.  A single is a set $S_\ell$ of
unit classes mod $\ell^{e_\ell}$, a vertex a unit class mod $\ell$ (so
$p(v)=1/(\ell-1)$), an edge a unit class mod $\ell\ell'$.  The pointwise
statements of \S\ref{sec:supp} hold for every integer $n$ (non-unit residues
trigger no event).

\begin{theorem}[Proved]\label{thm:crit}
Let $\delta=e^{-50}$ and consider a graph-type congruence system with
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(G)] $g_\ell\le1/32$ for all $\ell\in\mathcal P$;
\item[(W)] $w_\ell\le\delta/32$ for all $\ell\in\mathcal P$;
\item[(I)] for every integer $n\equiv1\pmod Q$ at which no single and no edge
occurs, $W(n)>T$.
\end{enumerate}
Put $\Sigma=S_1+S_2$.  Then there is a minorant $B$ as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} (moduli coprime to $Q$, unit classes,
$B(n)\le\mathbf 1[W(n)>T]$ for $n\equiv1\ (Q)$) whose moduli are products of at
most $C(\Sigma+1)$ of the $\ell^{e_\ell}$, with $\mu\ge\exp(-C(\Sigma+1))$,
$\log(M_1/\mu)\le C(\Sigma+1)$, and $|\mu_\psi|\le\mu/4$ for every real primitive
$\psi$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}.  Here $C$ is absolute.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Hubs.}  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:hub} with $\delta$: by (W),
$g_\ell^+\le1/16$, $S_1^+\le S_1+2S_2/\delta$, all degrees are $\le\delta$, and
(I) persists.  Let $A(n)$ be the occurring events of the new system.

\emph{Local lemma.}  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:lll} to all singles and edges with
$x_E=2P(E)\le1/8$.  For a single at $\ell$ the neighbours are the edges at $\ell$ and
$\prod(1-x)\ge1-\sum x\ge1-2w_\ell\ge1/2$; for an edge at $\ell,\ell'$ the
neighbours are the two singles and the edges at $\ell$ or $\ell'$, and
$\prod(1-x)\ge(7/8)^2(1-2w_\ell-2w_{\ell'})\ge1/2$.  So the hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:lll} holds, and since $1-x\ge e^{-1.1x}$ for $0\le x\le1/8$ and
$\sum_Ex_E=2(S_1^++S_2^+)\le2(S_1^++S_2)$,
\[
 P(A=\emptyset)\ge\prod_E(1-x_E)\ge e^{-\lambda},\qquad\lambda=3(S_1^++S_2).
\]

\emph{Minorant.}  With $\Lambda=\Lambda_{16}\le16S_1^++16e^{98}S_2$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo} with $z=16$, $\delta=e^{-50}=e^{-3z-2}$), let $L$ be least
with $4^{L+1}\ge200e^{\Lambda+\lambda}$ and $B=B^*_L$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise}), expanded into unit classes
(Lemma~\ref{lem:meanmass}; every event is a set of unit classes).  Then
$B\le\mathbf 1[A=\emptyset]\le\mathbf 1[W>T]$ on $n\equiv1\ (Q)$, by (I).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:meanmass},
$E|B-\mathbf 1[A=\emptyset]|\le2\cdot4^{-L-1}e^\Lambda\le e^{-\lambda}/100\le P(A=\emptyset)/100$,
so $\mu=E\,B\ge0.99e^{-\lambda}$; and
$M_1\le M_1(B_L)+4^{L+1}M_1(G_{L+1})\le e^\Lambda+4^{L+1}16^{-L-1}e^\Lambda\le2e^\Lambda$,
so $\log(M_1/\mu)\le\Lambda+\lambda+1$.  Each term involves at most $2(L+1)$
primes, and $\Lambda,\lambda,L$ are $O(\Sigma+1)$.

\emph{Twist.}  Let $\psi$ be real primitive of conductor $f>1$, $\gcd(f,Q)=1$,
$f\mid d_i$ for some $i$.  As $2\mid Q$, $f$ is odd, hence squarefree, and its
primes lie in $\mathcal P$; fix one, $\ell_0$.  If $f\nmid d_i$, some prime of $f$
does not divide $d_i$ and the character sum over that coordinate vanishes;
hence $\mu_\psi=E[B\psi]$ (Haar) and
\[
 |\mu_\psi|\le E|B-\mathbf 1[A=\emptyset]|+|E[\mathbf 1[A=\emptyset]\psi]|.
\]
Let $\bar A'$ be the event that no event whose support avoids $\ell_0$ occurs;
it depends on $X_{-\ell_0}=(X_\ell)_{\ell\ne\ell_0}$ only.  Then
$\mathbf 1[A=\emptyset]=\mathbf 1_{\bar A'}\cdot\mathbf 1[X_{\ell_0}\notin\mathrm{Forb}]$, where
$\mathrm{Forb}=\mathrm{Forb}(X_{-\ell_0})$ is the union of $S^+_{\ell_0}$ and of the
vertices at $\ell_0$ joined to an occurring vertex.  Write
$\psi=\chi_0(X_{\ell_0})\psi'(X_{-\ell_0})$ with $\chi_0=(\cdot/\ell_0)$.  Conditioning on
$X_{-\ell_0}$ and using independence and $E\chi_0(X_{\ell_0})=0$,
\[
 \big|E_{X_{\ell_0}}[\mathbf 1[X_{\ell_0}\notin\mathrm{Forb}]\chi_0]\big|
 =\big|E_{X_{\ell_0}}[\mathbf 1[X_{\ell_0}\in\mathrm{Forb}]\chi_0]\big|
 \le P_{X_{\ell_0}}(\mathrm{Forb}),
\]
so $|E[\mathbf 1[A=\emptyset]\psi]|\le P(\bar A'\cap\{X_{\ell_0}\in\mathrm{Forb}\})$, which is at most
\[
 P(\bar A')\Big(g^+_{\ell_0}+\sum_{\{u,w\},\,u\text{ at }\ell_0}p(u)P(w\mid\bar A')\Big)
 \le P(\bar A')\big(\tfrac1{16}+1.22w_{\ell_0}\big)<0.064\,P(\bar A').
\]
Here the single at $\ell_0$ and $X_{\ell_0}$ are independent of $\bar A'$ and of
$X_{\ell_w}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:lll}, applied to the family defining $\bar A'$ and
$B=\{X_{\ell_w}\in V_w\}$ (neighbours: the single and the edges at $\ell_w$, with
$\prod(1-x)\ge(7/8)(15/16)>0.82$), gives $P(w\mid\bar A')\le1.22p(w)$.  As
$P(A=\emptyset)=P(\bar A')-P(\bar A'\cap\{X_{\ell_0}\in\mathrm{Forb}\})\ge0.936P(\bar A')$, we get
$|\mu_\psi|<0.08P(A=\emptyset)<\mu/4$.
\end{proof}

\section{Private covers and a hypergraph moment bound}\label{sec:hyper}

\emph{Hypergraph-type systems.}  As in \S\ref{sec:pseudo}, with singles
$S_\ell$ and vertices $v=(\ell,V_v)$ (vertices at one prime pairwise
disjoint), but with \emph{hyperedges} in place of edges: a hyperedge is a set
of $2,\dots,k$ vertices at distinct primes, occurring iff all its vertices are
realised, so $P(e)=\prod_{v\in e}p(v)$.  Repeated hyperedges are allowed (they
only enlarge the quantities below).  Put $S_H=\sum_eP(e)$,
$w_\ell=\sum_{e\text{ at }\ell}P(e)$, and for a set $O$ of vertices at distinct
primes the \emph{codegree}
\[
 \Delta_O=\sum_{e\supsetneq O}\ \prod_{v\in e\setminus O}p(v),\qquad
 \Delta^{(i)}=\max_{|O|=i}\Delta_O;
\]
thus $\Delta_{\{v\}}=\deg(v)$.

For a set $P$ of primes, a \emph{private cover} of $P$ is a set $\mathcal C$ of
events with $P\subseteq\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal C}\supp E$ such that every
$E\in\mathcal C$ has a prime of $\supp E\cap P$ lying in the support of no other
member of $\mathcal C$.  A family is \emph{private} if each member has a prime
lying in no other member.  Put $\pi(\mathcal C)=\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal C}\supp E$ and
\[
 G^{\rm cov}_u(x)=\sum_{|P|=u}\ \sum_{\mathcal C\text{ private cover of }P}\mathbf 1[\text{every }E\in\mathcal C\text{ occurs at }x].
\]

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:cov}
For every $x$ and $u,L\ge0$: $\binom{N(x)}u\le G^{\rm cov}_u(x)$; hence
Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise} holds with $G^{\rm cov}_{L+1}$ in place of $G_{L+1}$.
If every event is a union of cells, then
$M_1(B_L)\le\sum_{u\le L}2^uE\,G^{\rm cov}_u$ and $M_1(G^{\rm cov}_u)=E\,G^{\rm cov}_u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every $P\subseteq V(x)$ is covered by occurring events, and a minimal covering
subfamily is a private cover (a member without a private prime could be
removed).  This gives the first claim, and the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise} goes through verbatim.  In the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:meanmass}, a cell $c$ on $U$ with $\kappa(U,c)\ne0$ has every
prime of $U$ covered by events supported in $U$ occurring on $c$; a minimal
such subfamily is a private cover of $U$, so
$\sum_c|\kappa(U,c)|P(c)\le2^{|U|}\sum_{\mathcal C\text{ priv.\ cover of }U}P(\mathcal C\text{ occurs})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:hypmom}
Let $U_0\ge1$, $\rho\ge1$ and put
$D=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}(kU_0)^j\Delta^{(j+1)}$.  If $D\le[2ek\rho^k]^{-1}$, then
\[
 \sum_{\mathcal C\text{ private},\ |\mathcal C|\le U_0}\rho^{|\pi(\mathcal C)|}P(\mathcal C\text{ occurs})
 \le\exp\big(\rho S_1+2ek\rho^kS_H\big).
\]
In particular, for $w\ge0$ and $\rho=1+w$,
$\sum_{u\le U_0}w^uE\,G^{\rm cov}_u\le\exp((1+w)S_1+2ek(1+w)^kS_H)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The last claim follows from the first, because a private family privately
covers only subsets of $\pi(\mathcal C)$, so pointwise
$\sum_{u\le U_0}w^uG^{\rm cov}_u\le\sum_{\mathcal C\text{ private},|\mathcal C|\le U_0}(1+w)^{|\pi(\mathcal C)|}\mathbf 1[\mathcal C\text{ occurs}]$.

A single in a private family has its only prime private, so the singles sit
on primes disjoint from the rest and contribute at most
$\prod_\ell(1+\rho g_\ell)\le e^{\rho S_1}$.  The hyperedge part splits into
connected components, each private; two components using the same prime with
different vertices cannot occur together, and otherwise $P$ and
$\rho^{|\pi|}$ are multiplicative over components.  So the hyperedge part is
at most $\exp(\sum_K\rho^{|V(K)|}P(K))$ over connected private $K$ with
$h=|K|\le U_0$ hyperedges, $|V(K)|\le kh$.

\emph{Exploration.}  Pick a first hyperedge $e_1\in K$ and declare its
vertices discovered; process discovered vertices in breadth-first order (ties
by a fixed order), attaching at a vertex $v$ as \emph{children} all
not-yet-attached hyperedges of $K$ containing $v$.  Every hyperedge is attached
($K$ is connected), and a child $e$ of $v$ has a private vertex, which is
therefore new.  If $O_e$ denotes $v$ together with the old vertices of $e$,
$|O_e|=j+1$, the weight of $e$ beyond $O_e$ is the product of $p$ over its new
vertices, and the sum over admissible $e$ is at most
$\sum_j\binom{kh}j\Delta^{(j+1)}\le D$.  To make the encoding independent of
$K$, record the child sets in $N:=kh$ fixed breadth-first slots, padding
unused slots with empty sets; $K\mapsto(e_1,\text{child sets})$ is injective.

\emph{Counting.}  The first hyperedge contributes
$\sum_{e_1}P(e_1)\cdot\#\text{roots}\le kS_H$ (the root index only
overcounts).  A slot with $c$ children contributes at most $D^c/c!$: the
product of the sibling weights is $\prod p(u)$ over all vertices they bring,
independent of order, so the sum over sets is $1/c!$ times the sum over
ordered tuples, which is at most $D^c$ since each next child has a private,
hence new, vertex and its old vertices lie in a pool of at most $kh$
vertices.  The number of weighted compositions is
$\sum_{c_1+\dots+c_N=h-1}\prod1/c_i!=N^{h-1}/(h-1)!\le e(ke)^{h-1}$.  Hence
$\sum_{|K|=h}\rho^{|V(K)|}P(K)\le\rho^{kh}\,e\,kS_H\,(keD)^{h-1}$, and summing
over $h\ge1$ with $\rho^kkeD\le1/2$ gives at most $2ek\rho^kS_H$.
\end{proof}

The factor $(kU_0)^j$ in $D$ is real: a cluster of hyperedges reusing $j+1$
old vertices out of many costs only $\Delta^{(j+1)}$ per hyperedge but can be
placed in many ways.  For the multiplier problem some pair codegrees are of
constant size (Remark~\ref{rem:hubs}), so Lemma~\ref{lem:hypmom} cannot be
applied to the whole system with $U_0$ of the size of the total mass; this is
what \S\ref{sec:multi} circumvents.

\section{Multilevel minorants}\label{sec:multi}

\emph{Levels.}  Consider a congruence system as in \S\ref{sec:crit} whose
events are singles and hyperedges of $2,\dots,k$ vertices at distinct primes,
all vertices being unit classes mod $\ell^{e_\ell}$.  Its events are
partitioned into \emph{levels}: level~$2$ consists of the singles and the
edges, and level $r$ ($3\le r\le k$) of hyperedges with $r$ vertices.  Let
$F_r$ be the indicator that no event of level $r$ occurs, $F_{<r}=\prod_{2\le
s<r}F_s$, $\mathcal A_{<r}=\{F_{<r}=1\}$, and let $\Sigma_r$ be the mass of level
$r$ ($\Sigma_2=S_1+S_2$).  We assume throughout
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(P$_k$)] the total mass of all events at every prime is at most $1/(64k)$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:compare}
Under \textup{(P$_k$)}, with $x_E=2P(E)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $P(\mathcal A_{<r+1})\ge P(\mathcal A_{<r})\prod_{E\in\text{level }r}(1-x_E)\ge P(\mathcal A_{<r})e^{-3\Sigma_r}$ for every $r$, and $P(\mathcal A_{<3})\ge e^{-3\Sigma_2}$;
\item[(b)] for every $r$ and every event $B$ depending on the coordinates in a
set $U$ of primes, $P(B\cap\mathcal A_{<r})\le P(\mathcal A_{<r})P(B)e^{|U|/2}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An event meets at most $k$ primes, so the $x$-sum over its neighbours is at
most $k\cdot2/(64k)=1/32$ and the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:lll} holds for
the union of all levels.  Its proof gives $P(E\mid\bigcap_{E'\in\mathcal S}\bar E')\le
x_E$ for all $\mathcal S$; the chain rule over the events of level $r$, conditioned
on $\mathcal A_{<r}$ and the earlier ones, gives (a), using $1-x\ge e^{-1.1x}$.
For (b) apply the second part of Lemma~\ref{lem:lll} to the levels $<r$:
$\sum_{A\in\Gamma(B)}x_A\le|U|/32$, so
$\prod_{\Gamma(B)}(1-x_A)^{-1}\le e^{1.2|U|/32}\le e^{|U|/2}$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Constants.}  $z=16$, $\delta=e^{-50}$ (level~$2$, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}); $w=16$, $\rho=17e^{1/2}$ and
$\delta_r=[4er\rho^r]^{-1}$ (levels $\ge3$).  For $\hat S\ge1$ put
$\Lambda_3=6e\rho^3\hat S$, let $L_3$ be least with
$4^{L_3+1}\ge400e^{\Lambda_3+3\hat S}$, and $t(\hat S)=\delta_3/(3(L_3+1))$.

\begin{theorem}[Two-level minorant; proved]\label{thm:twolevel}
Take a system with levels $2$ and $3$ only, satisfying \textup{(P$_3$)}, and
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(D2)] every level-$2$ vertex has level-$2$ degree $\le\delta$;
\item[(D3)] for some $\hat S\ge\Sigma_3$, every level-$3$ vertex has degree
$\le\delta_3$ and every pair of level-$3$ vertices has codegree
$\le t(\hat S)$.
\end{enumerate}
Put $\Sigma=\Sigma_2+\hat S$.  There is a real combination $B$ of unit cells
with $B\le F_2F_3$ pointwise, $E[F_2F_3-B]\le P(\mathcal A_{<4})/100$ (so
$\mu=E\,B\ge0.99e^{-3\Sigma}$), $\log(M_1/\mu)\le C(\Sigma+1)$, every cell on at most
$C(\Sigma+1)$ primes, and $|E[B\psi]|<\mu/4$ for every real primitive $\psi$ as
in Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}.  Here $C$ is absolute.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Level~$3$.}  Let $B_3=B_{L_3}-4^{L_3+1}G^{\rm cov}_{L_3+1}$ for the
level-$3$ events (Lemma~\ref{lem:cov}); pointwise $B_3\le F_3$ and
$|F_3-B_3|\le2\cdot4^{L_3+1}G^{\rm cov}_{L_3+1}$.  Multiply the pointwise
inequality in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:hypmom} by $F_2$ and take
expectations; Lemma~\ref{lem:compare}(b) with $U=\pi(\mathcal C)$ gives
$E[F_2\mathbf 1[\mathcal C]]\le P(\mathcal A_{<3})P(\mathcal C)e^{|\pi(\mathcal C)|/2}$, and
$(1+w)e^{1/2}=\rho$.  By (D3),
$D=\deg+3(L_3+1)\Delta^{(2)}\le2\delta_3=[2e\cdot3\rho^3]^{-1}$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:hypmom} (with $U_0=L_3+1$, no singles) gives
$E[F_2G^{\rm cov}_{L_3+1}]\le16^{-L_3-1}P(\mathcal A_{<3})e^{\Lambda_3}$ and therefore
\[
 E[F_2(F_3-B_3)]\le2\cdot4^{-L_3-1}P(\mathcal A_{<3})e^{\Lambda_3}\le P(\mathcal A_{<3})e^{-3\hat S}/200\le P(\mathcal A_{<4})/200
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:compare}(a).  Without the tilt, $M_1(B_3)\le2e^{\Lambda_3}$ by
Lemmas~\ref{lem:cov} and~\ref{lem:hypmom}, and every cell of $B_3$ lies on at
most $3(L_3+1)$ primes.

\emph{Level~$2$, cell by cell.}  Write $B_3=\sum_ic_i\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_i}$ with cells
$\mathcal C_i$ on prime sets $P_i$.  If $\mathcal C_i$ realises a level-$2$ single at a prime of
$P_i$, or both ends of a level-$2$ edge inside $P_i$, then $F_2=0$ on $\mathcal C_i$;
put $\beta_i=\alpha_i=0$.  Otherwise, on $\mathcal C_i$, $F_2$ equals the void
indicator $F_2^{(i)}$ of the \emph{conditioned} level-$2$ system on
$\mathcal P\setminus P_i$: the original singles and edges there, plus the induced
singles $\{w:\{v,w\}\text{ an edge},\ v\text{ realised by }\mathcal C_i\}$, of total
mass $\le\delta|P_i|$; degrees remain $\le\delta$.  Lemmas~\ref{lem:pointwise},
\ref{lem:meanmass} and~\ref{lem:pseudo} give, with
$\Lambda_2=16(S_1+3\delta(L_3+1))+16e^{98}S_2$ and every $L$, functions
$B^{(i)}_L$, $G^{(i)}_{L+1}$ with $|B^{(i)}_L-F_2^{(i)}|\le4^{L+1}G^{(i)}_{L+1}$,
$E\,G^{(i)}_{L+1}\le16^{-L-1}e^{\Lambda_2}$ and $M_1(B^{(i)}_L)\le e^{\Lambda_2}$.  Let
$L_2$ be least with $4^{L_2+1}\ge800e^{\Lambda_2+\Lambda_3+3\Sigma}$, put
$\beta_i,\alpha_i=B^{(i)}_{L_2}\mp4^{L_2+1}G^{(i)}_{L_2+1}$ (so
$\beta_i\le F_2^{(i)}\le\alpha_i$), and
\[
 B=\sum_{c_i>0}c_i\beta_i\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_i}-\sum_{c_i<0}|c_i|\alpha_i\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_i}.
\]
Then $B\le\sum_ic_iF_2\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_i}=F_2B_3\le F_2F_3$.  As $\beta_i,\alpha_i$ do
not depend on the coordinates in $P_i$,
\[
 E[F_2F_3-B]\le E[F_2(F_3-B_3)]+\sum_i|c_i|P(\mathcal C_i)\,2\cdot4^{-L_2-1}e^{\Lambda_2}
 \le\frac{P(\mathcal A_{<4})}{200}+\frac{e^{-3\Sigma}}{200}\le\frac{P(\mathcal A_{<4})}{100},
\]
since $P(\mathcal A_{<4})\ge e^{-3\Sigma}$.  Further
$M_1(B)\le M_1(B_3)\cdot2e^{\Lambda_2}\le4e^{\Lambda_2+\Lambda_3}$, every cell lies
on at most $3(L_3+1)+2(L_2+1)$ primes, and $L_3,\Lambda_3=O(\hat S+1)$,
$\Lambda_2,L_2=O(\Sigma+1)$.

\emph{Twist.}  As in Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}, $\mu_\psi=E[B\psi]$ and
$|E[B\psi]|\le E[F-B]+|E[F\psi]|$ with $F=F_2F_3$.  For a prime $\ell_0\mid f$ let
$\bar A'$ be the event that no event (of either level) avoiding $\ell_0$ occurs.
Then $|E[F\psi]|\le P(\bar A'\cap\{\text{an event at }\ell_0\text{ occurs}\})$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:lll} applied to the family defining $\bar A'$ (the rest of an
event through $\ell_0$ meets at most two further primes, with neighbour product
$\ge(1-1/96)^2$) this is at most $1.1\cdot P(\bar A')/192<0.01P(\bar A')$.  Hence
$|\mu_\psi|<0.03P(\mathcal A_{<4})<\mu/4$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Pushing heavy sets down.}  Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel} assumes small
codegrees only for level~$3$, and only at the scale $t(\hat S)$, which depends
on $\hat S$ alone.  Heavy pairs are moved into level~$2$, whose own
truncation is unconstrained.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:push}
Start from one system of singles, edges and $3$-vertex hyperedges with masses
$S_1,S_2,S_H$, in which every prime carries total mass $\le c_0$.  Let
$\hat S\ge S_H$, $t=t(\hat S)$, and $c_0\le\delta\delta_3t/1728$.  Perform in
order: \textup{(a)} every vertex of hyperedge-degree $>\delta_3$ becomes a
single and the hyperedges through it are deleted; \textup{(b)} every vertex
pair $O$ with codegree $\Delta_O>t$ becomes an edge \textup{(}merged with an
existing edge if any\textup{)} and the hyperedges containing it are deleted;
\textup{(c)} every vertex of edge-degree $>\delta$ becomes a single and the
edges and hyperedges through it are deleted.  The result is a two-level system
satisfying \textup{(P$_3$)}, \textup{(D2)} and \textup{(D3)} with this $\hat S$;
if no event of it occurs, no original event occurs; and with $S_2^{(b)}$ the
edge mass after \textup{(b)},
\[
 \Sigma_3\le S_H,\quad S_2^{(b)}\le S_2+3S_H/t,\quad
 S_1^{\rm new}\le S_1+3S_H/\delta_3+2S_2^{(b)}/\delta .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Deletions only lower degrees and codegrees, so after \textup{(c)} the
conditions (D2), (D3) hold (pushed pairs have codegree $0$).  Every deleted
event contains a new single or edge, so it cannot occur when no new event
occurs.  Markov's inequality, per prime: \textup{(a)} adds at most
$w^{(3)}_\ell/\delta_3$, since $\sum_{v\text{ at }\ell}p(v)\deg(v)=w^{(3)}_\ell$;
\textup{(b)} adds at most $\sum_{O\ni v\text{ at }\ell}P(O)\Delta_O/t=2w^{(3)}_\ell/t$;
\textup{(c)} adds at most $w^{(2),(b)}_\ell/\delta$.  So the new per-prime total is
at most $c_0(2+1/\delta_3+(1+2/t)(1+1/\delta))\le9c_0/(t\delta\delta_3)\le1/192$,
which is (P$_3$).  The global bounds are the same sums over $\ell$, with
$\sum_\ell w^{(3)}_\ell=3S_H$ and $\sum_\ell w^{(2)}_\ell=2S_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proved]\label{cor:crit3}
Consider a congruence system of singles, edges and $3$-vertex hyperedges
satisfying \textup{(I)} of Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}, with
$\hat S\ge S_1+S_2+S_H$ and every prime carrying total mass
$\le c_0(\hat S):=\delta\delta_3t(\hat S)/1728$.  Then there is a minorant $B$
as in Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, with the twist condition, such that
$\log(M_1/\mu)$ and the number of prime powers in each modulus are
$\le C(\hat S^2+1)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:push}, then Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel} with
$\Sigma\le S_1+3\hat S/\delta_3+(1+2/\delta)S_2^{(b)}+\hat S=O(\hat S/t)=O(\hat S^2+1)$;
(I) transfers since $B\le F_2F_3\le\mathbf 1[\text{no original event}]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$k$-level minorant; proved]\label{thm:klevel}
For every $k\ge3$ there are constants $C_k,A_k$ with the following property.
Consider a congruence system of singles and hyperedges with $2,\dots,k$
vertices satisfying \textup{(I)} of Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}, with total mass
$\le\hat S$ $(\hat S\ge1)$ and every prime carrying total mass
$\le c_k(\hat S):=\hat S^{-A_k}/C_k$.  Then there is a minorant $B$ as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, with the twist condition, such that
$\log(M_1/\mu)$ and the number of prime powers in each modulus are at most
$C_k\hat S^{A_k}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put events with $r$ vertices into level $r$ ($3\le r\le k$) and singles and
edges into level~$2$.

\emph{Step A (push-down, top to bottom).}  For $r=k,k-1,\dots,3$ in this order,
let $\Sigma_r$ be the mass of level $r$ at the moment it is processed (it
contains the sets pushed into it from above; later steps only delete events
of level $\ge r$ or add events below, so $\Sigma_r$ stays an upper bound), and
put
\begin{gather*}
 H_r=k\sum_{s>r}(L_s+1),\qquad \hat S_{\ge r}=\sum_{s\ge r}(\Sigma_s+1),\qquad
 \Lambda_r=2er\rho^r(\Sigma_r+H_r+1),\qquad \mathfrak M_{r+1}=\prod_{s>r}4e^{\Lambda_s},\\
 L_r=\min\{L:\ 4^{L+1}\ge200k\,\mathfrak M_{r+1}e^{H_r/2+\Lambda_r+3\hat S_{\ge r}}\},\qquad
 N_r=r(L_r+1)+H_r .
\end{gather*}
Then: \textup{(a)} every vertex of level-$r$ degree $>\delta_r/2$ becomes a
single; \textup{(b)} for $1\le j\le r-2$, every vertex set $O$ with $|O|=j+1$
and $\Delta^{(r)}_O>\delta_r(4N_r)^{-j}/(2r)$ becomes an event of level $j+1$;
every level-$r$ event containing a pushed vertex or set is deleted.  Finally,
level-$2$ vertices of degree $>\delta$ become singles, deleting the events
through them.  As in Lemma~\ref{lem:push}, no deleted event can occur when no
remaining event occurs, and by Markov a set pushed from level $r$ carries
per-prime mass $\le2^rw^{(r)}_\ell(4N_r)^j\,2r/\delta_r$ and total mass
$\le2^r\Sigma_r(4N_r)^r\,2r/\delta_r$.  By downward induction all of
$L_r,N_r,H_r,\Sigma_r$ are $\le C_k\hat S^{A_k}$, and for $c_k(\hat S)$ small
enough (P$_k$) holds at the end.

\emph{Step B (composition, top level innermost).}  Let $B_{\ge k}$ be the
minorant $B_{L_k}-4^{L_k+1}G^{\rm cov}_{L_k+1}$ of $F_k$.  Given
$B_{\ge r+1}=\sum_ic_i\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_i}$ with cells $\mathcal C_i$ on $P_i$,
$|P_i|\le H_r$, form the level-$r$ system conditioned on $\mathcal C_i$: events
meeting $P_i$ are realised, vanish, or lose their realised vertices, the last
becoming \emph{induced} events of smaller support; identical induced events are
merged (this changes neither the void indicator nor any bound below).  If an
event is fully realised, put $\beta_i=\alpha_i=0$; otherwise let
$\beta_i,\alpha_i=B_{L_r}\mp4^{L_r+1}G^{\rm cov}_{L_r+1}$ for the conditioned void
indicator $F_r^{(i)}$, and put
$B_{\ge r}=\sum_{c_i>0}c_i\beta_i\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_i}-\sum_{c_i<0}|c_i|\alpha_i\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_i}$.
Level~$2$ is treated in the same way with
Lemmas~\ref{lem:pointwise}--\ref{lem:pseudo}, at the least $L_2$ with
$4^{L_2+1}\ge200k\,\mathfrak M_3e^{\Lambda_2+3\hat S_{\ge2}}$, where
$\Lambda_2=16(S_1+\delta H_2)+16e^{98}S_2$ (induced singles have mass
$\le\delta H_2$).  Finally $B=B_{\ge2}$.

\emph{Conditioned codegrees.}  An induced event $e\setminus F'$ ($F'$ its realised
fixed vertices) contains $O$ iff $e\supseteq O\cup F'$; there are at most
$H_r\le N_r$ fixed vertices.  By (a) and (b), and $H_r/(4N_r)\le1/4$,
\[
 \Delta'_v\le\frac{\delta_r}2+\sum_{i\ge1}\frac{H_r^i\delta_r}{2r(4N_r)^i}\le\delta_r,\qquad
 \Delta'_O\le\sum_{i\ge0}\frac{H_r^i\delta_r}{2r(4N_r)^{|O|+i-1}}\le\frac{\delta_r}{r(4N_r)^{|O|-1}}\quad(|O|\ge2).
\]
As $r(L_r+1)\le N_r$, Lemma~\ref{lem:hypmom} applies with $U_0=L_r+1$:
$\sum_j(rU_0)^j\Delta'^{(j+1)}\le\delta_r+\sum_{j\ge1}4^{-j}\delta_r/r\le2\delta_r=[2er\rho^r]^{-1}$.
The induced events have mass at most
$\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\binom hi\max_{|F'|=i}\Delta^{(r)}_{F'}\le h\delta_r/2+\frac{\delta_r}{2r}\sum_{i\ge2}h^i(4N_r)^{1-i}\le h\delta_r$
with $h\le H_r$ fixed vertices; so the conditioned masses are $\le\Sigma_r+H_r$,
and the tilted moment is $\le e^{\Lambda_r}$.

\emph{Errors.}  With the true lower indicators,
\[
 E[F_{<r}B_{\ge r}]\ge E[F_{<r}F_rB_{\ge r+1}]-\sum_i|c_i|\,E\big[F_{<r}\mathbf 1_{\mathcal C_i}|\beta_i/\alpha_i-F_r^{(i)}|\big],
\]
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:compare}(b), applied to $\mathcal C_i\cap\{\mathcal C\text{ occurs}\}$ on
$P_i\cup\pi(\mathcal C)$, the last expectation is at most
$2\cdot4^{L_r+1}P(\mathcal A_{<r})P(\mathcal C_i)e^{|P_i|/2}16^{-L_r-1}e^{\Lambda_r}$.
Since $M_1(B_{\ge r+1})\le\mathfrak M_{r+1}$ (each level multiplies the mass by
$\le4e^{\Lambda_s}$), the choice of $L_r$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:compare}(a) bound the sum
by $P(\mathcal A_{<r})e^{-3\hat S_{\ge r}}/(100k)\le P(\text{all})/(100k)$, where
$P(\text{all})=E\prod_rF_r$; level~$2$ contributes the same amount in Haar
measure.  Telescoping from $r=2$ to $r=k$ gives $B\le\prod_rF_r$ pointwise and
$E[\prod_rF_r-B]\le P(\text{all})/100$, so
$\mu\ge0.99P(\text{all})\ge0.99e^{-3\hat S_{\ge2}}$ and $M_1(B)\le\mathfrak M_2$.  The
twist is handled as in Theorem~\ref{thm:twolevel}: the rest of an event through
$\ell_0$ meets at most $k-1$ further primes, and (P$_k$) makes the conditional
factor $\le1.1$.  All quantities are $\le C_k\hat S^{A_k}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why levels are needed; proved for the stated lower bound]\label{rem:hubs}
Every atom is $(M,D)=(4sab-1,sa^2)$ with $s$ squarefree, and its class is
$-4D\equiv-a/b\equiv-4sa^2\equiv-1/(4sb^2)\pmod M$.  Fixing two of $s,a,b$ and
letting the third run over a progression gives a class which is the same
rational at every $M$ in a progression modulo $4sa$, $4sb$ or $4ab$.  For
$y=T^\theta$, $\theta<1/3$, and distinct primes $\ell_1,\ell_2\in(y,T^{1/3}]$,
the prime number theorem in progressions shows that such a pair of vertices
has codegree $\ge(\log(1/(3\theta))-o(1))/\varphi(4n)$, $n=sa,sb,ab$
respectively (take $M=\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3$ with $\ell_3\in(y,T^{1/3}]$ in one class
mod $4n$).  The number of such classes with $\varphi(4n)\le X$ grows like
$X\log X$, so codegrees $\le1/L$ cannot be enforced inside one truncation of
level $L\asymp$ total mass: removing the heavy pairs costs more than $L$.  In
Theorem~\ref{thm:klevel} these pairs become level-$2$ edges, their vertices
have level-$2$ degree $\asymp1$ and become singles, and their cost enters only
$L_2$.
\end{remark}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:main}

For a set $\Pi$ of primes and $M\le T$, $M\equiv3\pmod4$, write $M=m_\Pi r_\Pi$ with
$m_\Pi$ the $\Pi$-part.  An atom $(M,D)$, $D\mid A_M^2$, \emph{survives} $\Pi$ if
$m_\Pi\mid4D+1$ and $r_\Pi>1$; its Haar weight is $1/\varphi(r_\Pi)$.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:qmass}
For every set $\Pi$ of primes,
\[
 S_{\rm tot}(\Pi)=\sum_{\text{atoms surviving }\Pi}\frac1{\varphi(r_\Pi)}\le\exp\Big(O\Big(\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}\Big)\Big).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use $1/\varphi(r)\ll\log\log T\cdot m/M$ with $m=m_\Pi$ and follow the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:mass}: the involution preserves $m\mid4D+1$ because $m\mid M$;
writing $D=sr'^2$, $A=sr'k$ with $r'\le k\le X$ gives $m\mid r'+k$; for fixed
$(s,r')$ each $k$ determines $M$ and hence $m$, and $m\mid4sr'^2+1$.  So the
sum is $\ll\log\log T\,(3+\Lc)\sum_{sr'^2\le X}\tau(4sr'^2+1)/(sr')$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Proved]\label{prop:hmin}
For all large $T$, with $y=T^{1/3}\exp(2\Lc/\log\Lc)$, there are $Q$ with $840\mid Q$ and $\log Q\le5y$,
and a minorant $B$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} with $\mu>0$, the twist
condition, and $\log\max d_i$, $\log(M_1/\mu)$ both at most
$\exp(O(\Lc/\log\Lc))$.  In particular $H_{\min}(\theta)$
\textup{(}Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:min}\textup{)} holds for every $\theta\ge1/3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\tau^*=\max_{n\le T+2}\tau(n)$, so that
$\tau^*\le\exp((\log2+o(1))\Lc/\log\Lc)$, and
\[
 y=T^{1/3}\exp(2\Lc/\log\Lc),\qquad\Pi_0=\{\ell\le y\}.
\]
For $\ell>y$ let $g^{(0)}_\ell$ be the Haar measure of the classes mod
$\ell^{e_\ell}$ ($e_\ell$ maximal with $\ell^{e_\ell}\le T$) hit by atoms surviving
$\Pi_0$ with $r_{\Pi_0}\in\{\ell,\ell^2\}$, and let
$\mathcal B=\{\ell>y:\ g^{(0)}_\ell>1/64\}$, so $|\mathcal B|\le64S_{\rm tot}(\Pi_0)$.  Put
$\Pi=\Pi_0\cup\mathcal B$, $Q=\lcm(24,\ \ell^{e_\ell}:\ \ell\in\Pi)$, and
$\mathcal P=\{y<\ell\le T\}\setminus\mathcal B$.  Every atom surviving $\Pi$ has
$r_\Pi\le T$ with all prime factors in $\mathcal P$, hence $>T^{1/3}$, so
$r_\Pi\in\{\ell,\ell^2,\ell\ell'\}$: it contributes the class $-4D\bmod r_\Pi$ to
the single $S_\ell$ (lifted mod $\ell^{e_\ell}$) or the edge $-4D\bmod\ell\ell'$.

\emph{(I).}  Let $n\equiv1\pmod Q$ and $M\le T$, $M\equiv3\pmod 4$.  Every
$\ell^v\Vert M$ with $\ell\in\Pi$ has $\ell^v\le T$, so $m_\Pi\mid Q$.  If $r_\Pi=1$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:one} applies.  If $n\equiv-4D\pmod M$ with $D\mid A_M^2$, then
$m_\Pi\mid4D+1$, and reducing mod $r_\Pi$ shows that a single or edge occurs.
Conclude with Lemma~\ref{lem:atoms}.

\emph{(W).}  An edge at $\ell$ comes from $M=m\ell\ell'$ with $m\le T/(\ell\ell')$,
and each $M$ carries $\le\tau^{*2}$ atoms; so
$w_\ell\le\sum_{\ell'>y}(T/(\ell\ell'))\tau^{*2}\cdot4/(\ell\ell')\le8\tau^{*2}T/y^3
=\exp((2\log2-6+o(1))\Lc/\log\Lc)$.

\emph{(G).}  An atom with $r_\Pi\in\{\ell,\ell^2\}$ either survives $\Pi_0$ with the
same rough part (weight $\le1/64$ in total) or has $M=mb\ell$ with $b\in\mathcal B$
($b\ell^2,b^2\ell>y^3>T$).  The latter add at most
$2|\mathcal B|\tau^{*2}T/y^3=\exp((3\log2-6+o(1))\Lc/\log\Lc)$.  So $g_\ell\le1/32$.

\emph{Sizes.}  $\Sigma\le S_{\rm tot}(\Pi)=\exp(O(\Lc/\log\Lc))$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:qmass}, and $\log Q\le(\pi(y)+|\mathcal B|)\Lc+\log24\le5y$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:crit} now gives $B$ with
$K=1+\log(M_1/\mu)$ and $\log\max d_i$ both at most $\exp(O(\Lc/\log\Lc))$.
The conditions of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:min} hold for every $\theta\ge1/3$ and
$\varepsilon>0$, since $\exp(O(\Lc/\log\Lc))=T^{o(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Take $Q$, $B$, $y$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:hmin} and put $K=1+\log(M_1/\mu)$.
As in Theorem~\ref{thm:Hmin}, adjoin to $Q$ a prime $\ell_0\in(R,2R]$,
$R=\max(T,\max d_i)$; then $\log Z\le6y$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} gives a
prime $p\equiv1\pmod{Q\ell_0}$ with $W(p)>T$ and
$\log p\le C_1K\max(\log Z,K)\le T^{1/3}\exp(O(\Lc/\log\Lc))$.  It is hard as
$840\mid Q$, and $p>\ell_0>T$ gives infinitely many distinct primes.
Inverting: if $\Lc\ge4\log\log p$ then $W(p)>T\ge(\log p)^4$; otherwise
$\Lc/\log\Lc\ll\log\log p/\log\log\log p$, and
$\Lc\ge3\log\log p-O(\log\log p/\log\log\log p)$.  All inputs are effective.
\end{proof}

The constants are astronomically lossy ($\delta=e^{-50}$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}); the statement is purely asymptotic.  Exponent~$3$
uses $y\ge T^{1/3+o(1)}$ twice: for the crude per-prime bounds (W), (G), and to
ensure that all events have at most two primes.

\section{Limits of the method}\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{Complete certificates}

A \emph{complete certificate} for $W>T$ is a reduced class $c\bmod Q$ all of
whose primes have $W(p)>T$.  The class of one modulo
$L^*(T)=\lcm(24,\ M\le T,\ M\equiv3\ (4))$ is one, with
$\log L^*(T)=(2/3+o(1))T$.

\begin{proposition}[Proved]\label{prop:complete}
Every complete certificate has $\log Q\ge(2/3-o(1))T$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) If a prime $\ell\equiv3\pmod4$, $\ell\le T$, does not divide $Q$, then $c$
is compatible with the atom $-4\in\cR(\ell)$ ($D=1$), and Dirichlet's theorem
gives primes with $W\le\ell$; so $\prod_{\ell\le T,\ell\equiv3(4)}\ell\mid Q$.
(ii) Since $\cR(3)=\{2\}$, $3\mid Q$ and $c\equiv1\pmod3$.  (iii) Let
$\ell\equiv1\pmod4$, $\ell\le T/3$, and suppose a prime $r\equiv2\pmod3$ divides
$A=(3\ell+1)/4$.  The atom $M=3\ell$, $D=r$ has class $-4r\equiv1\pmod3$; if
$\ell\nmid Q$ it is compatible with $c$, a contradiction.  (iv) The primes
$\ell\equiv1\pmod4$ for which $(3\ell+1)/4$ has no prime factor $\equiv2\pmod3$
avoid the classes $\ell\equiv-3^{-1}\pmod r$ for all primes $r\equiv2\pmod 3$,
$r\ge5$; by the prime number theorem in progressions their logarithmic
weight up to $X$ is $o(X)$.  Hence $\log Q\ge T/2+T/6-o(T)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The previous record; proved modulo {\cite[Cor.~11]{Chang}}]\label{rem:record}
Put $L^*(T)=\lcm(24,\ M\le T,\ M\equiv3\ (4))$.  Every prime $p\equiv1\pmod{L^*(T)}$
has $W(p)>T$, by Lemmas~\ref{lem:atoms} and~\ref{lem:one}.  A prime
$\ell\le T/3$ divides some such $M$ (namely $\ell$ or $3\ell$).  A prime
$\ell\in(T/3,T]$ divides one iff $\ell\equiv3\pmod4$.  Higher prime powers
contribute $O(\sqrt T\log T)$.  Hence $\log L^*(T)=(2/3+o(1))T$.  Every prime
factor $\ell$ of $L^*(T)$ has $\log\ell\le\log T=o(\log L^*(T))$, so Chang's bound
gives a prime $p\equiv1\pmod{L^*(T)}$ with $\log p\le(12/5+o(1))\log L^*(T)$,
i.e.\ $W(p)>T\ge(5/8-\varepsilon)\log p$.  As $840\mid L^*(T)$ and $p>L^*(T)>T$,
this holds for infinitely many hard primes.

Proposition~\ref{prop:complete} bounds $\log Q$, not $p$: a class $c\ne1$ may
contain primes below $Q$.  What it shows is that a complete certificate whose
prime is supplied by a least-prime theorem, which certifies $\log p$ only
through an upper bound $O(\log Q)$, certifies no more than $W(p)\gg\log p$;
with Chang's exponent, no more than $5/8$ by this route.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Prime-local designs}

Call a design \emph{prime-local} if it imposes $n\equiv1\pmod{\ell^{e_\ell}}$ for
$\ell$ in a set $\Pi$ and every $M\le T$, $M\equiv3\pmod4$, has at most one prime
factor outside $\Pi$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:two} uses such a design.

\begin{proposition}[Proved]\label{prop:ceiling}
In a prime-local design, $\Pi$ contains every odd prime $\le\sqrt{T/3}$ with at
most one exception.  Hence every prime it produces has
$\log p\ge(1/\sqrt3-o(1))\sqrt T$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For odd primes $\ell_1<\ell_2\le\sqrt{T/3}$ outside $\Pi$, one of
$\ell_1\ell_2,3\ell_1\ell_2$ is $\equiv3\pmod4$ and $\le T$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The hypothesis \texorpdfstring{$H_{\min}$}{H\_min} and the hub obstruction}

\begin{hypothesis}[$H_{\min}(\theta)$; proved for $\theta\ge1/3$ \textup{(}Proposition~\ref{prop:hmin}\textup{)}, open for $\theta<1/3$]\label{hyp:min}
For every $\varepsilon>0$ and large $T$ there are $Q$ with $840\mid Q$,
$\log Q\le T^{\theta+\varepsilon}$, and a minorant $B$ as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} with $\log\max d_i\le T^{\theta+\varepsilon}$,
$\log(M_1/\mu)\le T^{\varepsilon}$, $\mu>0$, and the twist condition.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:TZ}]\label{thm:Hmin}
$H_{\min}(\theta)$ implies $W(p)>(\log p)^{1/\theta-\varepsilon}$ for infinitely
many hard primes, for every $\varepsilon>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Adjoin to $Q$ a prime $\ell_0\in(R,2R]$, $R=\max(T,\max d_i)$; this keeps all
hypotheses and forces $p>T$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} gives
$\log p\le T^{\theta+2\varepsilon+o(1)}$.
\end{proof}

$H_{\min}$ is a purely combinatorial statement about congruences.  The
Proposition~\ref{prop:hmin} (via Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}) establishes it for
every $\theta\ge1/3$ (indeed with $T^{o(1)}=\exp(O(\log T/\log\log T))$).  For
$\theta<1/2$ the residual atoms involve several free primes, and plain
event-level Bonferroni truncation over the raw atom list fails:

\begin{proposition}[Proved]\label{prop:bonf}
Fix $0<\theta<1/2$, put $y=T^\theta$, and impose the class of one modulo all
prime powers $\le T$ of primes $\le y$.  Take as events all atoms
$(M,c)$, $c\in\cR(M)$, compatible with this, and let $N(n)$ count those
containing $n$.  Then for large $T$ and every even $J$ with
$(4.2\theta^2+o(1))(\log T)^2\le J\le y$, the Bonferroni minorant
$B_J=\sum_{j<J}(-1)^j\binom Nj$ has negative mean in the CRT measure on the
class $1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The mean is $P(N=0)-E[\binom{N-1}{J-1}\mathbf 1_{N\ge1}]$.  Let
$r=\lceil\sqrt{2J-1}\rceil\le\sqrt{2y}+1$, and take $r$ primes $\equiv1\pmod4$
and $r$ primes $\equiv3\pmod4$ in $(y,2y]$; there are enough of them.  If
$n\equiv-4$ modulo all of them, then for every cross pair $M=\ell\ell'\le4y^2\le T$
we have $M\equiv3\pmod 4$, and $D=1$ gives the atom $-4\bmod M$.  So
$N\ge r^2\ge2J-1$, on a set of measure $\ge(2y)^{-2r}$.  Hence
\[
 E\tbinom{N-1}{J-1}\ge(2y)^{-2r}\tbinom{2J-2}{J-1}
 \ge\exp\big((J-1)\log4-\log(2J-1)-2r(\theta\log T+\log2)\big),
\]
which exceeds $1>P(N=0)$ once $J>(2\sqrt2\theta/\log4)^2(\log T)^2(1+o(1))$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:bonf} is an obstruction only to truncation by the
number of events.  On the hub configuration of its proof, $r^2$ events fire on
$2r$ primes; the error of the support truncation (Lemma~\ref{lem:pointwise}) is
at most $\binom{4r}{L}\le16^r$ there, against probability $(2y)^{-2r}$.

\emph{Below $\theta=1/3$.}  The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses
$y\ge T^{1/3+o(1)}$ for the crude per-prime bounds (W), (G) and to ensure that
events involve at most two primes.  For smaller $\theta$ one would need a
per-prime mass bound of the kind discussed in \S\ref{sec:haar} and a hypergraph
version of Lemma~\ref{lem:pseudo}; we have neither.

\section{The slice parameter}\label{sec:typeI}

For a prime $p$ let $\cB_p$ be the set of pairs $(c,k)$ with
$1\le k\le\lfloor2p/3\rfloor$, $1\le c\le\lfloor(2p+k)/(4k)\rfloor$, $(p,ck)=1$;
put $h=4ck$, $N_{c,k}=p^2+4ck^2$, $s=\sfree(c)$, and let $\chi_s=(\Delta_s/\cdot)$,
where $\Delta_s<0$ is the discriminant of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-s})$; its
conductor divides $4s\mid h$.  $M_{c,k}(p)$ is the number of divisors of
$N_{c,k}$ in the class $-p\pmod h$.  The slice $(c,k)$ is \emph{positive} if
$p(a+b)=k(4abc-1)$ has a solution in positive integers $a,b$.  This happens iff
$M_{c,k}(p)>0$.  Both directions rest on the identity, for $e=ha-p$, $f=hb-p$,
\[
 (ha-p)(hb-p)-N=h\big(4abck-p(a+b)\big)-4ck^2=h\big(k(4abc-1)-p(a+b)\big),
\]
so $ef=N$ iff $k(4abc-1)=p(a+b)$.  If $e\equiv-p\pmod h$ is a positive divisor
of $N$, then $f=N/e\equiv p^2(-p)^{-1}\equiv-p\pmod h$ (as $\gcd(p,h)=1$), so
$a=(p+e)/h$, $b=(p+f)/h$ are positive integers with $ef=N$.  Conversely, if
$k(4abc-1)=p(a+b)$ then $pa,pb<4abck$, so $e=ha-p>0$ and $f=hb-p>0$; and $ef=N$,
$e\equiv-p\pmod h$.

\begin{lemma}[Genus obstruction; proved]\label{lem:genus}
If $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$, $(c,k)\in\cB_p$ and $\chi_s(p)=1$, then $M_{c,k}(p)=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $c=st^2$.  Every prime $q\mid N$ is odd, prime to $stk$, and
$(p(2tk)^{-1})^2\equiv-s\pmod q$, so $\chi_s(q)=1$; hence $\chi_s(e)=1$ for every
divisor $e$ of $N$.  But $\chi_s(-p)=\chi_s(-1)\chi_s(p)=-1$, and $\chi_s$ is
defined modulo $h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A sufficient prime factor; proved]\label{lem:goodprime}
If a prime $q$ divides $N_{c,k}$ and $q\equiv-p\pmod h$, then $M_{c,k}(p)\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$q$ itself is a divisor in the class $-p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:np}
Let $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ and suppose $(\ell/p)=1$ for every prime $5\le\ell\le B$.
Then $\ckmin(p)>B$.  In particular $\ckmin(p)\ge n_p$, the least quadratic
non-residue modulo $p$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $ck\le B$, $s\le B$ and
$\chi_s(p)=(-s/p)=(-1/p)\prod_{q\mid s}(q/p)=1$, since
$(-1/p)=(2/p)=(3/p)=1$.  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:genus}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Proved]\label{prop:QR}
Let $24\mid L$ and let $a\bmod L$ be a reduced class with $a\equiv1\pmod{24}$
such that every sufficiently large prime $p\equiv a\pmod L$ has $\ckmin(p)>T$.
Then every prime $5\le\ell\le T$ divides $L$ and $(a/\ell)=1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\ell\nmid L$ or $(a/\ell)=-1$.  Choose $c_0\bmod4\ell$ with
$c_0\equiv a\pmod{\gcd(L,4\ell)}$, $c_0\equiv1\pmod4$, $(c_0/\ell)=-1$; for
$n\equiv1\pmod 4$, $\chi_\ell(n)=(n/\ell)$, so $\chi_\ell(c_0)=-1$.  Take a prime
$q\equiv-c_0\pmod{4\ell}$, $q\nmid L$.  Then $\chi_\ell(q)=\chi_\ell(-1)\chi_\ell(c_0)=1$,
so $-4\ell\equiv\rho^2\pmod q$ for some $\rho\not\equiv0$.  The conditions
$p\equiv a\ (L)$, $p\equiv c_0\ (4\ell)$, $p\equiv\rho\ (q)$ are compatible and
define a reduced class, which contains infinitely many primes.  For these,
$q\mid p^2+4\ell$ and $q\equiv-p\pmod{4\ell}$, so $M_{\ell,1}(p)\ge1$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:goodprime}), and $(\ell,1)\in\cB_p$ for large $p$, whence
$\ckmin(p)\le\ell\le T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proved]\label{cor:exactly}
Let $B(n)$ be a finite combination of residue classes with
$B(p)\le\mathbf 1[\ckmin(p)>T]$ for all large primes $p$ in a reduced class
$\equiv1\pmod{24}$.  Then every large prime $p$ in that class with $B(p)>0$ has
$n_p>T$.  Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:np}: the depth certifiable by
congruences is exactly $n_p$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$B$ is periodic modulo some $L$, which we take divisible by the modulus of the given class (hence by $24$).  If $B(p)>0$ then $B$ is positive on the
whole class $p\bmod L$, which lies in the given class.  So every large prime in
the class $p\bmod L$ has $\ckmin>T$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:QR} gives
$(p/\ell)=1$, i.e.\ $(\ell/p)=1$ by reciprocity ($p\equiv1\pmod4$), for
$5\le\ell\le T$; also $(2/p)=(3/p)=1$ as $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$.  Hence $n_p>T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Proved modulo {\cite[Prop.~5.1]{LW}}]\label{thm:slice}
$\ckmin(p)\gg\log p\cdot\log\log\log p$ for infinitely many hard primes $p$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lau and Wu \cite[Prop.~5.1]{LW} (following Graham--Ringrose \cite{GR}; the
numbering is that of the authors' preprint, and the published numbering was not
compared) show the following.  They phrase it via $\chi_p(q)=1$ with
$\chi_p=(p/\cdot)$, which for $p\equiv1\pmod4$ equals $(q/p)$ by reciprocity,
including $q=2$.  The statement:
for small fixed $\delta>0$ and $y(x)=\delta\log x\log_3x$ there are
$x_n\to\infty$ and primes $p\in(x_n^{1/2},x_n\log x_n]$ with $p\equiv1\pmod4$
and $(q/p)=1$ for all primes $q\le y(x_n)$.  Such $p$ are $\equiv1\pmod{24}$
and, since $(5/p)=(7/p)=1$, lie in a Mordell class.  Lemma~\ref{lem:np} gives
$\ckmin(p)>y(x_n)\ge(\delta/2)\log p\log_3p$.  Effectivity is not claimed.
\end{proof}

Under GRH, Montgomery's $n_p=\Omega(\log p\log\log p)$ would give the
corresponding bound, and Ankeny's $n_p\ll(\log p)^2$ caps what congruences can
certify; the random model predicts $\max_{p\le x}n_p\asymp\log x\log\log x$
(statements as quoted in \cite{LW}).

\begin{corollary}[Proved modulo Theorem~\ref{thm:TZ}]\label{cor:joint}
For infinitely many hard primes $p$, jointly $W(p)\ge(\log p)^{3-o(1)}$ and
$\ckmin(p)\ge(\log p)^{1-o(1)}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The primes $p$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} satisfy $p\equiv1\pmod Q$, where $24\mid Q$
and every prime $\ell\le y=T^{1/3}\exp(2\Lc/\log\Lc)$ divides $Q$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:hmin}).  So $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ and $(p/\ell)=1$, hence
$(\ell/p)=1$ by reciprocity ($p\equiv1\bmod4$), for every odd $\ell\le y$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:np} gives $\ckmin(p)>y$, and $\log p\le T^{1/3}\exp(O(\Lc/\log\Lc))$
gives $y\ge(\log p)^{1-o(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\section{The Haar side}\label{sec:haar}

Impose the class of one at all primes $\le z$ ($z\ge3$), i.e.\ $n\equiv1\pmod{Q_z}$
with $Q_z$ defined like $Q$.  For $M\le T$ write $M=mr$ with $m$ the $z$-smooth
and $r$ the $z$-rough part.  If $r>1$, the surviving atoms of $M$ are the
$D\mid A_M^2$ with $m\mid4D+1$, each giving the event $n\equiv-4D\pmod r$ of
conditional Haar probability $1/\varphi(r)$.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:struct}
If $M\equiv3\pmod4$, $D\mid A_M^2$ and $M=mr$ with $m\mid4D+1$, then $m\le r^2+1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the involution of Lemma~\ref{lem:mass} assume $D\le A$, $D=sr'^2$,
$A=sr'k$, $k\ge r'$.  As there, $m\mid r'+k\le2k$, so
$r=(4sr'k-1)/m\ge2sr'-1/(2k)$, i.e.\ $r\ge2sr'$, and
$m\le4sr'^2+1\le r^2+1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:globalmass}
For every $3\le z\le T$, the total mass $S_{\rm tot}=\sum1/\varphi(r)$ over all
surviving atoms is $\exp(O(\log T/\log\log T))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use $1/\varphi(r)\ll\log\log T\cdot m/M$ and repeat the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:mass} with $\ell$ replaced by $r$; every surviving atom has
$m\mid\gcd(4D+1,M)$, hence $m\mid r'+k$.
\end{proof}

(Using \cite[Prop.~1.4]{ET} on dyadic blocks one gets
$S_{\rm tot}\ll(\log T)^4\log\log T$; this is not needed below.)

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:haar-intro}]
Fix $\varepsilon>0$, put $y=T^{1/3+\varepsilon}$ and $z=y$.  Every rough part is
$\ell$, $\ell^2$ or $\ell_1\ell_2$ (as $y^3>T$).  Use the independent coordinates
$X_\ell=n\bmod\ell^{e_\ell}$.  Single atoms form forbidden sets $G_\ell$ of
measure $g_\ell$, with $1\notin G_\ell$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:one}) and
$\sum g_\ell\le S_{\rm tot}$.

\emph{Bad primes.}  $B=\{\ell:\ g_\ell>1/4\}$ has $|B|\le4S_{\rm tot}=T^{o(1)}$.
Impose also $n\equiv1\pmod{\ell^{e_\ell}}$ for $\ell\in B$ (cost $\le|B|\log T$).
Pairs inside $B$ die by Lemma~\ref{lem:one}; a pair atom $(\ell b,a)$ with
$b\in B$ becomes the residue $a\bmod\ell$ at $\ell$ if $a\equiv1\ (b)$ and dies
otherwise.  The enlarged sets satisfy
$g'_\ell\le1/4+|B|\tau^2_{\max}T/(\ell y(\ell-1))\le1/2$, with
$\tau^2_{\max}=\max_{A\le T}\tau(A^2)=T^{o(1)}$, and
$\sum(g'_\ell-g_\ell)\le|B|\tau^2_{\max}T/y^2=T^{1/3-2\varepsilon+o(1)}$.

\emph{Good pairs.}  Under the product measure $\mu'$ uniform on the allowed
residues, a good pair event has $\mu'(E)\le4/((\ell_1-1)(\ell_2-1))$, and the
per-prime weight is
$w'_\ell\le16\tau^2_{\max}T/(\ell^2y)\le T^{-3\varepsilon+o(1)}$.  The asymmetric local
lemma \cite[Lemma~5.1.1]{AS} applies to $\mu'$.  Here $\mu'$ is the Haar
measure conditioned on a product set, hence a product measure, and a pair event
is mutually independent of all events sharing no prime with it.  With
$x_E=2\mu'(E)$ we have
$\sum_{E'\sim E}x_{E'}\le2(w'_{\ell_1}+w'_{\ell_2})=T^{-3\varepsilon+o(1)}$, so
$\prod_{E'\sim E}(1-x_{E'})\ge1/2$ for large $T$.  The lemma gives
$\mu'(\text{no good pair event})\ge\prod(1-x_E)\ge\exp(-16S_{\rm tot})$.

\emph{Collecting.}  With $Q'=Q_y\prod_{b\in B}b^{e_b}$,
\begin{align*}
\delta^*(T)&\ge\frac{8}{\varphi(Q')}\prod(1-g'_\ell)\,e^{-16S_{\rm tot}}\\
&\ge\exp\big(-\pi(y)\log T-|B|\log T-18S_{\rm tot}-T^{1/3-2\varepsilon+o(1)}\big)\\
&=\exp\big(-T^{1/3+\varepsilon+o(1)}\big).
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

The exponent $1/3$ reflects the crude bound $N(M)\le\tau(A^2)$ for multi-prime
atoms at a fixed prime.

Let $w_\ell(T,z)=\sum1/\varphi(r_E)$ over the distinct surviving events $E$ whose
rough part is divisible by $\ell$.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:ppl}
Let $3\le z\le T$.  If $w_\ell(T,z)\le\log z/(8\log T)$ for all primes
$z<\ell\le T$, then
$\delta^*(T)\ge(8/\varphi(Q_z))\exp(-4S_{\rm tot})$, so
$\log(1/\delta^*(T))\le\pi(z)\log T+4S_{\rm tot}+O(1)$, with $S_{\rm tot}$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:globalmass}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the local lemma \cite[Lemma~5.1.1]{AS} on the coordinates $X_\ell$,
$\ell>z$ (uniform on units), to all distinct surviving events, with
$x_E=2/\varphi(r_E)\le1/2$ and $E\sim E'$ when the rough parts share a prime.  An
event has at most $\log T/\log z$ primes, so
$\prod_{E'\sim E}(1-x_{E'})\ge\exp(-4\sum_{\ell\mid r_E}w_\ell)\ge e^{-1/2}\ge1/2$, and
the lemma gives probability $\ge\prod(1-x_E)\ge\exp(-4S_{\rm tot})$ of no event.
The class of one modulo $Q_z$ has relative measure $\varphi(24)/\varphi(Q_z)$, and
$\log\varphi(Q_z)\le\pi(z)\log T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conditional; the implication is proved modulo {\cite[Prop.~1.4]{ET}}]\label{rem:pp}
Suppose the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:ppl} holds with $z=(\log T)^C$ for all
large $T$.  Then $\log(1/\delta^*(T))\ll(\log T)^{\max(C+1,4)+o(1)}$, since
$S_{\rm tot}\ll(\log T)^4\log\log T$ by \cite[Prop.~1.4]{ET}.  Without \cite{ET},
Lemma~\ref{lem:globalmass} gives only $\delta^*(T)\ge\exp(-T^{o(1)})$ by this
argument.  We do not know how to prove the per-prime hypothesis.
\end{remark}

For $y=T^{1/3+o(1)}$ the Haar bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:haar-intro} has a
prime-side counterpart: Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, through the pointwise minorant
of Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}.  The local lemma by itself does not provide a
pointwise minorant.

\subsection*{Single-prime atoms and the Type~I variety}
Following \cite[\S2]{ET}, let $\Sigma_I^n$ be the set of sextuples
$(a,b,c,d,e,f)$ satisfying their identities (2.1)--(2.9), among them
\[
 4abd=ne+1,\quad ce=a+b,\quad 4acd=n+f,\quad ef=4a^2d+1,\quad bf=na+c,
\]
and put $F_I(n)=\#\{(a,b,c,d,e,f)\in\mathbb N^6\cap\Sigma_I^n:\ a\le b\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Proved]\label{lem:dict}
Let $r>1$ be odd.  The atoms $(M,D)$ with $M=mr\equiv3\pmod4$, $D\mid A_M^2$,
$m\mid4D+1$ and $D\le A_M$ \textup{(}any $m\ge1$\textup{)} correspond
bijectively to the points of $\mathbb N^6\cap\Sigma_I^r$ with $a\le b$ and $d$
squarefree, via $D=sr'^2$, $A_M=sr'k$ \textup{(}$s$ squarefree\textup{)} and
\[
 (a,b,c,d,e,f)=\big(r',\,k,\,(r'+k)/m,\,s,\,m,\,(4D+1)/m\big).
\]
The class of the atom is $-4D\equiv-a/b\pmod r$.  Hence the number of distinct
classes modulo $r$ carried by atoms with rough part $r$, under any class-of-one
quarantine, is at most $2F_I(r)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:mass}, $m\mid r'+k$, so $c\in\mathbb N$.  Then
$4abd=4A_M=mr+1$, $ce=a+b$ and $ef=4sr'^2+1$; by direct computation these three
identities (with nonzero entries) imply the others, e.g.\
$e\cdot4acd=4a^2d+4abd=(ef-1)+(ne+1)$ and $e\cdot bf=a(ne+1)+b=e(an+c)$.
Conversely a point with $a\le b$ and $d$ squarefree gives $D=da^2\le A=dab$,
$M=ne=4A-1$, $m=e\mid4D+1$.  The triple $(s,r',k)$ determines $D$ and $A$,
hence $M$; and $4A\equiv1\pmod r$ gives $-4D\equiv-r'/k$.  Partners $A^2/D$ at
most double the count.
\end{proof}

So the single-prime forbidden sets are governed by the Type~I variety
$\Sigma_I^r$.  Modulo the proof of \cite[Prop.~1.7]{ET} (its Lemma~2.8 and \S3),
which bounds the relevant points, $F_I(n)\le n^{3/5+O(1/\log\log n)}$; ET remark
that $3/5$ appears to be the limit of divisor-bound arguments.  The following
elementary bound is not used below.

\begin{proposition}[Proved]\label{prop:a1}
For a prime $\ell$, the points of $\mathbb N^6\cap\Sigma_I^\ell$ with $a=1$ number
$\ll\sqrt\ell\log\ell$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A point is determined by $(a,c,f)$, via $4acd=\ell+f$, $ef=4a^2d+1$, $b=ce-a$.
First $\ell\nmid c$: otherwise $\ell$ divides $y=acd$, $z=bcd$ and $x=abd\ell$ in
$4/\ell=1/x+1/y+1/z$, giving $4\le3$.  So $g=\gcd(c,f)$, which divides $\ell a+c$
(as $f\mid\ell a+c$ by $bf=\ell a+c$) and $\ell+f$ (as $c\mid\ell+f$), divides $\ell$
and hence equals $1$.  Then $f\mid a\ell+c$ and $c\mid a(\ell+f)$ give
$cf\mid a(\ell+f)+c$, so $(c-a)(f-1)\le a(\ell+1)$.  With $a=1$: either
$f\le1+\sqrt{\ell+1}$, and then $c\mid\ell+f$; or $c\le1+\sqrt{\ell+1}$, and then
$f\mid\ell+c$.  So the count is at most $2\sum_{j\le2+\sqrt\ell}\tau(\ell+j)$, and
$\tau(n)\le2\#\{\delta\mid n:\delta\le\sqrt n\}$ gives
$\sum_{j\le2+\sqrt\ell}\tau(\ell+j)\le2\sum_{\delta\le\sqrt{2\ell}}(3+\sqrt\ell)/\delta+O(\sqrt\ell)
\ll\sqrt\ell\log\ell$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Proved implication]\label{thm:eta}
Suppose $F_I(n)\le n^{\eta+o(1)}$ for all $n$, for some $0\le\eta\le1$, and put
$\kappa=\eta/(1+\eta)$.  Then $w_\ell(T,z)\le T^{\kappa+o(1)}/\ell$ for all $z$ and
all primes $\ell\le T$, and $\log(1/\delta^*(T))\le T^{\kappa+o(1)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:dict} and the crude count, the number of events with rough
part $r$ is at most $\min(2F_I(r),\tau^{*2}T/r)$, with $\tau^*$ as in
\S\ref{sec:main}.  Hence
\[
 w_\ell\ll\log\log T\sum_{r\le T,\ \ell\mid r}\frac{\min(2r^{\eta+o(1)},\tau^{*2}T/r)}r .
\]
Splitting at $R=T^{1/(1+\eta)}$ gives
$w_\ell\ll R^{\eta}T^{o(1)}/\ell+\tau^{*2}T/(\ell R)=T^{\kappa+o(1)}/\ell$.  With
$z=T^{\kappa+\varepsilon}$ the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:ppl} holds for large $T$,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:ppl} with $S_{\rm tot}=T^{o(1)}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:globalmass}) gives $\log(1/\delta^*)\le\pi(z)\log T+T^{o(1)}$.
\end{proof}

With $\eta=3/5$ this gives $\kappa=3/8$, weaker than
Theorem~\ref{thm:haar-intro}; $\eta=1/2$ gives exactly $1/3$.  So individual
Type~I bounds beat the exponent $1/3$ only if $\eta<1/2$, well beyond
\cite[Prop.~1.7]{ET}.  The single-prime part of the per-prime hypothesis is
not the bottleneck (Theorem~\ref{thm:haar-intro} absorbs it by quarantining
$T^{o(1)}$ bad primes); what is needed is control of
$\sum_{r\le T,\ \ell\mid r}F_I(r)/r$ uniformly in $\ell$, a progression average of
Type~I counts (Assessment).  The next theorem makes this route unnecessary.

\subsection*{Iterated quarantine}
For an atom $(M,D)$ and a set $\Pi$ of primes let $m_\Pi$ be the $\Pi$-part of $M$
and $r_\Pi=M/m_\Pi$; the atom \emph{survives} $\Pi$ if $m_\Pi\mid4D+1$ and $r_\Pi>1$.
Put $S^*=\sum_{(M,D)}\max_\Pi\mathbf 1[\text{survives }\Pi]/\varphi(r_\Pi)$.

\begin{theorem}[Proved; the polylogarithmic form modulo {\cite[Prop.~1.4]{ET}}]\label{thm:iterq}
\[
 \log(1/\delta^*(T))\ll(\log T)^3(S^*+1).
\]
Hence $\log(1/\delta^*(T))\le\exp(O(\log T/\log\log T))=T^{o(1)}$, and, modulo
\cite[Prop.~1.4]{ET}, $\log(1/\delta^*(T))\ll(\log T)^7/\log\log T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $(M,D)$ survives $\Pi$ then $m_\Pi\mid g=\gcd(M,4D+1)$, so
$1/\varphi(r_\Pi)\ll\log\log T\cdot g/M$; the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:mass} with
$m=g$ (the involution preserves $g$, $g\mid r'+k$, $g\mid4sr'^2+1$) bounds $S^*$
as in Lemma~\ref{lem:globalmass}, giving $\exp(O(\log T/\log\log T))$, and
$\ll(\log T)^4\log\log T$ by \cite[Prop.~1.4]{ET}.  Let $z=(\log T)^2$,
$k=\lfloor\log T/\log z\rfloor$, $c_0=1/(8k)$, $\Pi_0=\{\ell\le z\}$, and let
$w_\ell(\Pi)$ be the Haar mass of the distinct events surviving $\Pi$ whose
rough part is divisible by $\ell$.  Repeatedly add to $\Pi$ every prime
$\ell\notin\Pi$ with $w_\ell(\Pi)>c_0$.  If $\ell$ is added at some stage, $c_0$
is less than the sum of the weights $1/\varphi(r_{\Pi})$ of atoms with
$\ell\mid r_\Pi$; each atom has at most $k$ primes above $z$ and weight at most
its contribution to $S^*$, so at most $kS^*/c_0$ primes are added.  At the end
every free prime has $w_\ell\le c_0$, every event has at most $k$ free primes,
and the local lemma with $x_E=2P(E)$ applies
($\sum_{E'\sim E}x_{E'}\le2kc_0=1/4$), giving probability
$\ge\exp(-4S^*)$ of no event.  The class of one modulo the quarantined prime
powers costs $\le(\pi(z)+8k^2S^*)\log T$.
\end{proof}

This improves Theorem~\ref{thm:haar-intro}, and it makes the per-prime
hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:ppl} and of Remark~\ref{rem:pp} unnecessary.
On the prime side it supplies the per-prime conditions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:crit}, at the cost of $T^{o(1)}$ further quarantined primes.
Below $\theta=1/3$ the remaining obstacle there is the control of pair
codegrees (Assessment).  For $T\le10^6$ the iteration adds a few hundred
primes in a single round, and the resulting certificates give
$\log(1/\delta^*(10^6))\le3.6\cdot10^3$ (EVIDENCE).


\section{Summary of status}

\begin{itemize}
\item Proved: Lemmas~\ref{lem:atoms}--\ref{lem:mass}, \ref{lem:closed}--\ref{lem:hub},
\ref{lem:qmass}, \ref{lem:genus}--\ref{lem:np}, \ref{lem:struct}, \ref{lem:globalmass},
\ref{lem:ppl}, \ref{lem:dict}; Lemma~\ref{lem:lll} (classical); Theorem~\ref{thm:crit};
Proposition~\ref{prop:hmin}, i.e.\ $H_{\min}(\theta)$ for $\theta\ge1/3$;
Propositions~\ref{prop:complete}, \ref{prop:ceiling}, \ref{prop:bonf}, \ref{prop:QR};
Corollary~\ref{cor:exactly}; Theorem~\ref{thm:haar-intro} (with the classical
local lemma and divisor bound).
\item Proved modulo Thorner--Zaman \cite{TZ} (with the McCurley statement quoted there):
Lemma~\ref{lem:severe}, Theorems~\ref{thm:transfer}, \ref{thm:main}, \ref{thm:two},
\ref{thm:Hmin}, Corollary~\ref{cor:joint}; hence the falsity of ``$W(p)\le(\log p)^A$ for all large $p$'' for every $A<3$.
\item Proved modulo Lau--Wu \cite[Prop.~5.1]{LW}: Theorem~\ref{thm:slice}.
\item Proved modulo Chang \cite[Cor.~11]{Chang}: the $5/8$ record
(Remark~\ref{rem:record}).
\item Proved: Theorem~\ref{thm:iterq} ($\log(1/\delta^*)\le T^{o(1)}$); its polylogarithmic
form modulo \cite[Prop.~1.4]{ET}.
\item Proved modulo the proof of \cite[Prop.~1.7]{ET}: $F_I(n)\le n^{3/5+o(1)}$.
Proved implication: Theorem~\ref{thm:eta}.
\item Conditional on the per-prime hypothesis, and proved modulo
\cite[Prop.~1.4]{ET}: the polylogarithmic Haar bound of Remark~\ref{rem:pp}.
\item Open: $H_{\min}(\theta)$ for $\theta<1/3$; an unconditional
polylogarithmic Haar bound; any bound $W(p)>(\log p)^{3+\delta}$ or
$\ckmin(p)>(\log p)^{1+\delta}$ infinitely often.
\end{itemize}
Nothing here bears on the truth of the Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture: a prime with
large $W(p)$ or $\ckmin(p)$ may well have other representations.
