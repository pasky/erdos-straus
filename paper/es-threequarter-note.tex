\begin{abstract}
Let $E(N)$ be the number of $n\leq N$ for which $4/n$ is not a sum of three
unit fractions.  Vaughan (1970) proved $E(N)\ll N\exp\{-c(\log N)^{2/3}\}$.
We prove
\[
 E(N)\ll N\exp\{-c(\log N)^{3/4}\},
\]
with an absolute, ineffective constant $c>0$.  We are not aware of an
earlier improvement of the exponent $2/3$.  The forced residue classes are
those of the classical Type~II identity, but a prime $\ell\leq X$ is used
together with a \emph{multiplier} $k\leq X^\kappa$, giving classes modulo
$k\ell$.
% (R289 repair) R288 m1: define the conditioning modulus and separate the analytic inputs.
Conditionally on $n\bmod L_K$, where $L_K$ is the least common multiple of
the multipliers, the hits at different primes are independent.
Brun--Titchmarsh gives an expected number of hits $\ll(\log X)^3$ in every
fibre.  Bombieri--Vinogradov, Shiu's theorem and Cauchy--Schwarz give a
lower bound $\gg(\log X)^3$ outside an exponentially rare set of fibres,
after conditioning on $n$ having no small prime factors.  For Vaughan's
classes the corresponding count is
$\asymp(\log X)^2$.  Instead of
the large sieve we count on $[1,N]$ an explicit nonnegative majorant made
of congruence classes, namely a small-prime selector times an even
Bonferroni truncation.  Its coefficient ledger costs
$\exp\{O((\log X)^4)\}$, and balancing gives the exponent $3/4$.  This note
has been checked internally only; it is not a proof of the Erd\H{o}s--Straus
conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Call a positive integer $n$ \emph{representable} if there are positive
integers $x_1,x_2,x_3$, not necessarily distinct, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ES}
 \frac4n=\frac1{x_1}+\frac1{x_2}+\frac1{x_3}.
\end{equation}
The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture asserts that every $n\geq2$ is
representable.  Put
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E(N)&=\#\{1\leq n\leq N:n\text{ not representable}\},\\
 E_{\rm pr}(N)&=\#\{p\leq N\text{ prime}:p\text{ not representable}\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
(Here $n=1$ is not representable; this is irrelevant for a density bound.)

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There are absolute constants $c_*,C_*>0$ such that for every real $N\geq3$
\[
 E_{\rm pr}(N)\leq E(N)\leq C_*N\exp\{-c_*(\log N)^{3/4}\}.
\]
The constants are ineffective, because the proof uses the
Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Background}
Vaughan \cite{Vaughan} proved $E(N)\ll N\exp\{-c(\log N)^{2/3}\}$.  This
is still the bound quoted by Elsholtz and Tao \cite[\S1]{ET} and in the
survey of Bloom and Elsholtz \cite{BE}.  Pomerance and Weingartner
\cite[Thm.~1.3]{PW} give a version that is uniform in the numerator $m$:
for $4\leq m\leq(\log N)^2$, at most $N\exp\{-C(\log^2N/\varphi(m))^{1/3}\}$
integers $n\leq N$ have $m/n$ not a sum of three unit fractions.  For fixed
$m$ the exponent is again $2/3$.
% (R289 repair) R288 m5: retain the numerator range of the cited result.
For sums of $k\geq3$ unit fractions representing $m/n$, with $m>k$,
Elsholtz's bound has exponent $1-1/(2^{k-1}-1)$ \cite{Elsholtz},
\cite[\S on exceptional sets]{BE}.  This is $2/3$ for $k=3$; larger $k$ is
a different problem.  A companion internal note \cite{LL} obtains the
intermediate bound $N\exp\{-c(\log N)^{2/3}(\log\log N)^{1/3}\}$ by the
large sieve with multipliers $k\ll\log N$.  A search of the literature up
to 2026 found no improvement of the exponent $2/3$ for three unit fractions.
This is a report of a search, not a priority claim.

\subsection*{Where the exponents come from}
For a prime $\ell\equiv3\pmod4$, every divisor pair of $(\ell+1)/4$
forces a residue class modulo $\ell$ to consist of representable integers.
On average over primes $\ell\leq X$ the mass of these classes is
$\sum_\ell\omega(\ell)/\ell\asymp(\log X)^2$.  Heuristically, to turn
mass $\mu$ at primes $\leq X$ into a saving $\e^{-c\mu}$, a sieve of this
kind must
control intersections of about $\mu$ classes.  Such intersections have
moduli of size up to $X^{O(\mu)}$, and this costs $\exp\{O(\mu\log X)\}$
in the error terms.  With $t=\log X$ and $\mu\asymp t^{\mathcal B}$, the
balance $t^{\mathcal B+1}\asymp\log N$ gives a saving
$(\log N)^{\mathcal B/(\mathcal B+1)}$.  Vaughan's large sieve has
$\mathcal B=2$, hence the exponent $2/3$.

The point of this note is $\mathcal B=3$.  For a multiplier $k\equiv1\pmod4$
up to $K=X^\kappa$, factorizations of $(k\ell+1)/4$ force classes modulo
$k\ell$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:identity}).  Conditionally on $n\bmod k$, such a
class is a class modulo $\ell$.  Summed over $k$, the extra mass is the
factor $\sum_k\varphi(k)/k^2\asymp\log K\asymp t$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:supply}).  There is a difficulty: $L_K=\lcm\{k\leq K:
k\equiv1\ (4)\}$
satisfies $\log L_K\asymp K$.
% (R289 repair) R289 item 1: the obstruction concerns the particular fibrewise evaluation.
Thus $L_K$ is far larger than $N$, and the fibrewise large-sieve evaluation
of \cite{LL} ceases to be useful.  We avoid any such splitting.
The majorant of Section~\ref{sec:assembly} is a fixed finite
signed combination of congruence classes.  Its mean is computed exactly in
a finite CRT probability space.  The fibres modulo $L_K$ appear only
inside that computation, and its count on $[1,N]$ costs one unit per term.
The terms number $\exp\{O(t^4)\}$, so $t\asymp(\log N)^{1/4}$ and the
saving is $t^3\asymp(\log N)^{3/4}$.

\begin{remark}[the method limit]\label{rem:limit}
% (R289 repair) R288 m3: distinguish the companion cap from this note's proved claims.
Internal, unrefereed companion work \cite{KA3,SL} claims that $3/4$ is
optimal for the architecture used here.  This optimality assertion is
\emph{conditional on the proofs in those notes}; it is not established
here.  More precisely, consider any
nonnegative CRT majorant that is $\geq1$ on all integers avoiding a finite
family of forced classes (the classes
$\mathcal R(M)=\{-4D\bmod M:D\mid((M+1)/4)^2\}$ of
Remark~\ref{rem:supply}, and the $(a,D)$ and Case~A classes in the
notation of \cite{KA3}) and of selector classes $0\bmod p$, with all
primes involved $\leq N^{A}$ for a fixed $A$ and no condition on the
moduli, and that is used through $N\E\nu+\sum|{\rm coefficients}|$ with
coefficient sum $<N$.  Under those companion results, every such majorant
saves at most $C_A(\log N)^{3/4}$; see \cite[Cor.~4.2]{KA3} and
\cite[Thm.~10.14]{SL}; the Case~A part uses \cite[Prop.~1.4, \S7]{ET}.
The majorant of Theorem~\ref{thm:assembly} is of this form, since its
atoms are classes in $\mathcal R(k\ell)$.  These results do not cover large sieves of super-polynomial
level, hybrid interval methods, or non-CRT inputs.  Nothing in this remark
is used below.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Parameters}
Fix $\kappa=1/480$; any fixed $0<\kappa<1/240$ would do.  Throughout,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
 \begin{gathered}
 t=\log X,\quad K=\lfloor X^\kappa\rfloor,\quad H=K^{10},\\
 \K(K)=\{1\leq k\leq K:k\equiv1\pmod4\},\quad
 L_K=\lcm_{k\in\K(K)}k.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The constants are chosen in the following order.  First come the absolute
analytic constants (Shiu, Brun--Titchmarsh, Bombieri--Vinogradov with a
fixed exponent) and the lattice threshold $K_0$.  Then the void constant
$c_v$ is chosen (Section~\ref{sec:void}), then the selector constant $B$
in $y=Bt^3$, then the Bonferroni constant $D_B$, then the ledger constant
$C_L$, and finally $\alpha$ in $t=\alpha(\log N)^{1/4}$.  Every later
``sufficiently large'' threshold depends only on the constants already
chosen, and none depends on a residue class, a subfamily of multipliers,
a moment order, or $N$.  We write $A\ll B$ with constants that are
absolute in this sense.

The guiding distinction is between using prime distribution at scale $X$
to \emph{construct} classes and counting those classes on \emph{all}
integers up to $N$.  No prime-counting estimate is ever used at scale $N$.
Integer counts in residue classes, each with error at most one, take its
place.

\section{Forced classes and a fixed atom family}\label{sec:atoms}
\begin{lemma}[multiplier identity]\label{lem:identity}
Let $k,\ell$ be positive integers with $k\ell\equiv3\pmod4$, and let
$(k\ell+1)/4=uvw$ with positive integers $u,v,w$.  If $n\geq1$ satisfies
$nv\equiv-u\pmod{k\ell}$, then $s=(nv+u)/(k\ell)$ is a positive integer and
\[
 \frac4n=\frac1{suw}+\frac1{nsvw}+\frac1{nuvw}.
\]
Thus every positive integer in the class $-uv^{-1}\pmod{k\ell}$ is
representable.  The inverse exists because $uvw$ is coprime to $k\ell$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Over the common denominator $nsuvw$ the numerators add up to
$nv+u+s=s(k\ell+1)=4suvw$.
\end{proof}
For $k=1$ these are the classes used by Vaughan; the solvability criterion
is classical in the form ``$4abd-1\mid b+nc$'' (see \cite[Prop.~2.7]{ET},
where it is attributed to \cite{Vaughan}).  The multiplier $k$ only changes
which moduli $M=k\ell$ are used.

\subsection*{Blocks and atoms}
Put $x_j=2^jX^{1/2}$ and use the disjoint blocks
\[
 I_j=(x_j,\min(2x_j,X)],\qquad
 0\leq j<\Bigl\lceil\frac{\log X}{2\log2}\Bigr\rceil,
\]
with $z_j=x_j^{1/6}$.  A block is \emph{full} if $2x_j\leq X$.  Lower
estimates use only full blocks, and upper estimates enlarge the last block
to a full one.  There are $\asymp t$ blocks, and $t/2\leq\log x_j\leq t$.
Since $\kappa<1/240$, for all sufficiently large $X$ every block satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:floor}
 z_j\geq X^{1/12}>K^{20}=H^2,
 \qquad \Lambda_j:=\log(z_j/H)\geq\tfrac12\log z_j.
\end{equation}

Let $\A_X$ be the set of quadruples $A=(k,\ell,u,v)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atomdef}
 \begin{gathered}
 k\in\K(K),\quad \ell\in I_j\text{ prime for some }j,\quad H<u,v\leq z_j,\\
 (u,v)=(uv,k)=1,\quad 4uv\mid k\ell+1.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Divisibility forces $\ell\equiv3\pmod4$.  To each atom $A$ attach the class
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinder}
 E_A=\{n:n\equiv-uv^{-1}\pmod{k\ell}\},\qquad
 H_X(n)=\sum_{A\in\A_X}\ind_{E_A}(n).
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} with $w=(k\ell+1)/(4uv)$, a non-representable
$n$ has $H_X(n)=0$.  The family $\A_X$ is fixed: it does not depend on $N$
or on any residue class.

\subsection*{The probability space}
Let $2\leq y<K$ and $P_y=\prod_{p\leq y}p$.  We use the uniform measure on
$\mathbb Z/\mathcal M\mathbb Z$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:space}
 \mathcal M=\lcm(L_K,P_y)\prod_{\substack{X^{1/2}<\ell\leq X\\\ell\
 \mathrm{prime}}}\ell .
\end{equation}
Every class $a\bmod q$ with $q\mid\mathcal M$ has probability $1/q$.  The
size of $\mathcal M$ is irrelevant: it only defines probabilities, and
$[1,N]$ is never partitioned modulo $\mathcal M$.  We write
$c=n\bmod L_K$ and call $\{n:n\equiv c\ (L_K)\}$ the \emph{fibre} of $c$.

\begin{lemma}[distinctness and conditional independence]\label{lem:CRT}
For all sufficiently large $X$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Distinct atoms with the same prime $\ell$ have distinct classes
modulo $\ell$.
\item Conditionally on $c=n\bmod L_K$, and on any event determined by
$n\bmod P_y$, the residues $n\bmod\ell$ for the primes
$X^{1/2}<\ell\leq X$ are independent and uniform.
\item An atom $A$ meets the fibre of $c$ if and only if $k\mid u+cv$.
In that case $E_A\cap\{n\equiv c\ (L_K)\}=\{n\equiv c\ (L_K),\
n\equiv-uv^{-1}\ (\ell)\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, in the fibre of $c$, $H_X=\sum_\ell I_\ell$ is a sum of
independent Bernoulli variables, with $\Prob(I_\ell=1\mid c)=f_c(\ell)/\ell$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fc}
 f_c(\ell)=\#\{A\in\A_X:\ell_A=\ell,\ k_A\mid u_A+cv_A\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(1) Let $(k,\ell,u,v)$ and $(k',\ell,u',v')$ have the same class modulo
$\ell$, with $\ell\in I_j$.  Then $\ell\mid uv'-u'v$ and
$|uv'-u'v|<z_j^2<x_j<\ell$, so $uv'=u'v$.  Both fractions are reduced, so
$(u,v)=(u',v')$.  Next $k\ell\equiv k'\ell\equiv-1\pmod{4uv}$ and
$(\ell,4uv)=1$ give $k\equiv k'\pmod{4uv}$.  Since $4uv>4H^2>K$, $k=k'$.
(2) The primes $\ell>X^{1/2}>K>y$ are coprime to $\lcm(L_K,P_y)$, so this
is the Chinese remainder theorem.  (3) Since $k\mid L_K$, the condition
$n\equiv-uv^{-1}\pmod k$ is determined by $c$, and it holds if and only if
$k\mid u+cv$.  By (1) the active atoms at $\ell$ occupy $f_c(\ell)$
distinct classes modulo $\ell$, and at most one of them contains $n$.
\end{proof}
Note that $H_X(n)=\sum_\ell I_\ell(n)$ holds identically and not just in
distribution: at each $\ell$ at most one atom can contain $n$.

\section{Analytic inputs and lattice sums}\label{sec:lattice}
\subsection*{Prime distribution}
We use Brun--Titchmarsh in the form of Montgomery and Vaughan
\cite[Thm.~2]{MV}: for $1\leq q<Y$ and $(a,q)=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BT}
 \pi(x+Y;q,a)-\pi(x;q,a)\leq\frac{2Y}{\varphi(q)\log(Y/q)}.
\end{equation}
With $Y=x$ and $q\leq4x^{1/3}$ the right side is at most
$4x/(\varphi(q)\log x)$ for large $x$.

For $(a,q)=1$ put
\[
 E_x(q,a)=\pi(2x;q,a)-\pi(x;q,a)-\frac{\li(2x)-\li(x)}{\varphi(q)},
 \qquad E^*_x(q)=\max_{(a,q)=1}|E_x(q,a)|.
\]
By the Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem, for every fixed $R>0$ there are
$A_R,C_R,x_R$ such that for $x\geq x_R$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BV}
 \sum_{q\leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-A_R}}E^*_x(q)\leq C_Rx(\log x)^{-R}.
\end{equation}
Davenport \cite[Ch.~28]{Davenport} states the theorem for
$\max_{y\leq x}\max_a|\psi(y;q,a)-y/\varphi(q)|$.  The form with $\pi$
and $\li$ follows by partial summation, since prime powers contribute
negligibly, and taking two endpoints costs a factor $2$.  The constants
are ineffective.  Only moduli $q\leq4x^{1/3}$ occur below, and they lie
below the level $x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-A_R}$ for large $x$.

\subsection*{Shiu's theorem}
Let $F\geq0$ be multiplicative with $F(p^a)\leq A_1^a$ for all prime
powers, and $F(n)\leq A_2(\epsilon)n^\epsilon$ for every $\epsilon>0$.
Fix $0<\alpha_s,\beta_s<1/2$.  Shiu \cite[Thm.~1]{Shiu} proves that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Shiu}
 \sum_{\substack{V-Y<n\leq V\\n\equiv a\ (q)}}F(n)
 \ll\frac{Y}{\varphi(q)\log V}
 \exp\Bigl\{\sum_{p\leq V,\ p\nmid q}\frac{F(p)}p\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
% (R289 repair) R288 m4: match Shiu's asymptotic threshold and fixed-function dependence.
for $V\geq V_0(\alpha_s,\beta_s,F)$, uniformly for $0<a<q$, $(a,q)=1$,
$q<Y^{1-\alpha_s}$ and $V^{\beta_s}<Y\leq V$.  The implied constant depends
only on $\alpha_s,\beta_s$ and $F$.  We use it only for
\[
 F(n)=2^{\omega(n)}\frac n{\varphi(n)},\qquad F(p^a)=\frac{2p}{p-1}\leq4,
 \qquad F(n)\leq4^{\omega(n)}\leq\tau(n)^2\ll_\epsilon n^\epsilon,
\]
in \emph{boxes} $(U,\frac{21}{20}U]$ with $U\geq H=K^{10}$ and moduli
$q\leq K^2$.  We take $V=\frac{21}{20}U$, $Y=\frac1{20}U$ and
$\alpha_s=\beta_s=\frac14$.  Then $q\leq K^2\leq U^{1/5}<Y^{3/4}$ and
$V^{1/4}<Y$ as soon as $K$ is large.  Since $U\geq H=K^{10}\to\infty$,
every box also lies beyond the fixed threshold $V_0$.  An interval
$(H,z]$ is covered by consecutive boxes starting at $U=H$, and the last
box is enlarged.  This
uses $O(\log z)$ boxes, all with endpoints $\leq\frac{21}{20}z$.  The
modulus $q=1$ is excluded in \cite{Shiu}.  For it we use instead the
elementary bound $\sum_{n\leq V}F(n)\ll V\log V$, which follows from
$F=1*h$, where $h\geq0$ is supported on squarefree integers and
$h(p)=(p+1)/(p-1)$.  Indeed
$\sum_{n\leq V}F(n)\leq V\sum_{d\leq V}h(d)/d\ll V\log V$.

\subsection*{Lattice sums}
For $k\geq1$, $(c,k)=1$ and $1\leq H<z$, put $\Lambda=\log(z/H)$ and
\[
 \Sset(k,c;H,z)=\{(u,v):H<u,v\leq z,\ (u,v)=1,\ (v,k)=1,\
 u\equiv-cv\ (\mathrm{mod}\ k)\}.
\]
Then $(u,k)=1$ automatically.

\begin{lemma}[lattice sums; cf.\ {\cite[Lemma~3.1]{LL}}]\label{lem:lattice}
There are absolute $K_0,C_1$ such that for every $K\geq K_0$, every
$1\leq k\leq K$, every $(c,k)=1$ and every $z\geq H^2$, where $H=K^{10}$,
\begin{align}
 \sum_{\Sset(k,c;H,z)}\frac1{uv}
 &\geq\frac14\frac{\varphi(k)}{k^2}\Lambda^2,\label{eq:latlower}\\
 \sum_{\Sset(k,c;H,z)}\frac1{\varphi(u)\varphi(v)}
 &\leq C_1\frac{\varphi(k)}{k^2}\Lambda^2.\label{eq:latupper}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $H'\geq1$, $z'>H'$, $\Lambda'=\log(z'/H')$ and $(a,k)=1$, comparison
with integrals and M\"obius inversion give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:apone}
 \sum_{\substack{H'<u\leq z'\\u\equiv a\ (k)}}\frac1u
 =\frac{\Lambda'}k+O\Bigl(\frac1{H'}\Bigr),\qquad
 \sum_{\substack{H'<v\leq z'\\(v,k)=1}}\frac1v
 =\frac{\varphi(k)}k\Lambda'+O\Bigl(\frac{\tau(k)}{H'}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
including empty progressions.  Hence the double sum $T(H',z')$ over the
conditions of $\Sset$ without $(u,v)=1$ is
$\frac{\varphi(k)}{k^2}\Lambda'^2+O(\Lambda'\tau(k)/H')$ for
$\Lambda'\geq1$.  Remove $(u,v)=1$ by M\"obius inversion.  A common divisor
$d$ is coprime to $k$, and $(u,v)=(du',dv')$ ranges over the same
conditions with $(H/d,z/d]$.  For $d\leq H$ use the formula for $T$.  For
$d>H$ use $T(H/d,z/d)\leq(1+\log z)^2$ and $\log z\leq2\Lambda$.  This
gives
\[
 \sum_{\Sset}\frac1{uv}=\frac{\varphi(k)}{k^2}\Lambda^2
 \sum_{\substack{d\leq H\\(d,k)=1}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^2}
% (R289 repair) R288 m7: the small-divisor error sums only over d <= H.
 +O\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda\tau(k)\log H}{H}+\frac{\Lambda^2}{H}\Bigr).
\]
The coefficient is $\prod_{p\nmid k}(1-p^{-2})+O(H^{-1})\geq6/\pi^2-O(H^{-1})$.
Because $k/\varphi(k)\leq2^{\omega(k)}\leq\tau(k)\leq2\sqrt k$, the error
relative to $\varphi(k)\Lambda^2/k^2$ is $O(k\tau(k)^2/H)=O(K^2/H)$.  This
proves \eqref{eq:latlower} for $K\geq K_0$.

For \eqref{eq:latupper} drop $(u,v)=1$ and write
$1/\varphi(m)=m^{-1}\sum_{d\mid m}\mu^2(d)/\varphi(d)$.  If $d\mid u$ and
$u\equiv a\pmod k$ with $(a,k)=1$, then $(d,k)=1$.  By \eqref{eq:apone} for
$d\leq H$, and the bound $\sum_{m\leq z/d}1/m\leq1+\log z$ for $d>H$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\substack{H<u\leq z\\u\equiv a\ (k)}}\frac1{\varphi(u)}
 &\ll\frac\Lambda k\sum_d\frac{\mu^2(d)}{d\varphi(d)}
 +\frac1H\sum_{d\leq H}\frac{\mu^2(d)}{\varphi(d)}
 +(1+\log z)\sum_{d>H}\frac1{d\varphi(d)}\\
 &\ll\frac\Lambda k+\frac{\log H}H+\frac{\Lambda}{\sqrt H},
 \end{aligned}
\]
using $\varphi(d)\gg\sqrt d$.  In the same way
$\sum_{H<v\leq z,(v,k)=1}1/\varphi(v)\ll\frac{\varphi(k)}k\Lambda
+\tau(k)\log H/H+\Lambda/\sqrt H$.  Since $\varphi(k)/k\geq(2k)^{-1/2}$,
$\tau(k)\leq2\sqrt k$ and $\Lambda\geq10\log K$, the secondary terms are
dominated by the main terms.  Multiply the two bounds, applying the first
with $a\equiv-cv$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[multiplier harmonic sum; cf.\ {\cite[Lemma~3.2]{LL}}]\label{lem:h}
For every subset $\J\subseteq\K(K)$ put
$h(\J)=\sum_{k\in\J}\varphi(k)/k^2$.  For $K\geq5$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hfull}
 h(\K(K))=\frac2{\pi^2}\log K+O(1),
\end{equation}
and for every prime $p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hremove}
 \sum_{\substack{k\leq K\\p\mid k}}\frac{\varphi(k)}{k^2}\leq\frac{1+\log K}p.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
From $\varphi(k)/k^2=k^{-1}\sum_{d\mid k}\mu(d)/d$ and $k\equiv1\pmod4$,
\[
 h(\K(K))=\sum_{\substack{d\leq K\\d\ \mathrm{odd}}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^2}
 \Bigl(\frac14\log\frac Kd+O(1)\Bigr),
\]
and $\sum_{d\ \mathrm{odd}}\mu(d)/d^2=8/\pi^2$.  For \eqref{eq:hremove}
use $\varphi(k)/k^2\leq1/k$ and write $k=pm$.
\end{proof}

\section{Cubic supply in every fibre}\label{sec:supply}
For $\J\subseteq\K(K)$ let $L_\J=\lcm_{k\in\J}k$.  For $(c,L_\J)=1$ and
integers $u,v$ put
\[
 r_\J(u,v;c)=\#\{k\in\J:k\mid u+cv\}.
\]
If $(u,v)=1$ and $k\mid u+cv$ with $(c,k)=1$, then $(uv,k)=1$, so
$(u,v)\in\Sset(k,c;H,z)$ whenever $H<u,v\leq z$.

\begin{lemma}[weighted second incidence moment]\label{lem:wsecond}
There is an absolute $C_2$ such that for $K\geq K_0$, $H=K^{10}$,
$z\geq H^2$, every $\J\subseteq\K(K)$ and every $(c,L_\J)=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wsecond}
 \sum_{\substack{H<u,v\leq z\\(u,v)=1}}
 \frac{2^{\omega(uv)}\,r_\J(u,v;c)^2}{\varphi(4uv)}
 \leq C_2(\log z)^4(1+\log K)^3.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For coprime $u,v$ we have $\varphi(4uv)\geq2\varphi(u)\varphi(v)$.  This
is an equality if $uv$ is odd.  If $u=2^am$ with $a\geq1$ and $m$ odd,
then $\varphi(4u)=2^{a+1}\varphi(m)=4\varphi(u)$.  Expanding the square,
a pair $k,k'\in\J$ contributes through the single condition
$d=[k,k']\mid u+cv$, where $d\leq K^2$ and $(c,d)=1$.  For coprime $u,v$
this condition forces $(v,d)=1$.  We may therefore drop $(u,v)=1$ but
keep $(v,d)=1$, and then the residue $u\equiv-cv\pmod d$ is reduced.  Put
$F(n)=2^{\omega(n)}n/\varphi(n)$, so that
$2^{\omega(n)}/\varphi(n)=F(n)/n$.  The pair contributes at most
\[
 \frac12\sum_{\substack{H<v\leq z\\(v,d)=1}}\frac{F(v)}v
 \sum_{\substack{H<u\leq z\\u\equiv-cv\ (d)}}\frac{F(u)}u .
\]
Now cover $(H,z]$ by boxes and take $d\geq2$.  In a box $(U,\frac{21}{20}U]$
we apply \eqref{eq:Shiu}.  There $\sum_{p\leq V}F(p)/p=2\log\log V+O(1)$,
and $\exp\{-\sum_{p\mid d}2/(p-1)\}\leq(\varphi(d)/d)^2$, because
$\log(1-1/p)\geq-1/(p-1)$.  So the inner sum over one box is
$\ll U^{-1}\cdot U(\log U)\varphi(d)^{-1}(\varphi(d)/d)^2=(\log U)\varphi(d)/d^2$.
For $d=1$ the same bound $\ll\log U$ follows from the elementary estimate
for $q=1$.  Summing over $O(\log z)$ boxes, the inner sum is
$\ll(\log z)^2\varphi(d)/d^2$, uniformly in the residue.  For the outer
sum, partial summation from $\sum_{n\leq V}F(n)\ll V\log V$ gives
$\sum_{v\leq z}F(v)/v\ll(\log z)^2$.  Each pair therefore contributes
$\ll(\log z)^4\varphi(d)/d^2$.  Finally, $\varphi(m)/m^2\leq1/m$ and
$[k,k']=kk'/(k,k')$.  Writing $k=ga$, $k'=gb$ with $g=(k,k')$ and then
dropping $(a,b)=1$,
\[
 \sum_{k,k'\leq K}\frac{\varphi([k,k'])}{[k,k']^2}
 \leq\sum_{g\leq K}\frac1g\Bigl(\sum_{a\leq K/g}\frac1a\Bigr)^2
 \leq\sum_{g\leq K}\frac{(1+\log(K/g))^2}g\ll(1+\log K)^3.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

For $1\in\J\subseteq\K(K)$ and $(c,L_\J)=1$, and for a prime $\ell\in I_j$,
let $f_{c;\J}(\ell)$ be the number of triples $(k,u,v)$ with $k\in\J$,
$(u,v)\in\Sset(k,c;H,z_j)$ and $4uv\mid k\ell+1$.

\begin{theorem}[supply]\label{thm:supply}
There are $a_h,A_h>0$ and $X_h$, depending only on $\kappa$ and the
absolute analytic constants, such that for every $X\geq X_h$, every
$1\in\J\subseteq\K(K)$ and every $c$ with $(c,L_\J)=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supply}
 a_ht^2h(\J)\leq\sum_{X^{1/2}<\ell\leq X}\frac{f_{c;\J}(\ell)}{\ell}
 \leq A_ht^2h(\J).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a block $(x,2x]$ with $z=x^{1/6}$, so that $4uv\leq4x^{1/3}$.  By
\eqref{eq:floor}, $\Lambda\geq\frac12\log z=\frac1{12}\log x$.  For $k\in\J$
and $(u,v)\in\Sset(k,c;H,z)$ we have $(k,4uv)=1$, and $4uv\mid k\ell+1$
is the condition $\ell\equiv-k^{-1}\pmod{4uv}$, a reduced class.  Hence
\[
 \sum_{x<\ell\leq2x}f_{c;\J}(\ell)
 =\sum_{k\in\J}\sum_{(u,v)\in\Sset(k,c;H,z)}
 \bigl[\pi(2x;4uv,-k^{-1})-\pi(x;4uv,-k^{-1})\bigr].
\]

\emph{Upper bound} (for every block, the last one enlarged).
By \eqref{eq:BT}, $\varphi(4uv)\geq2\varphi(u)\varphi(v)$ and
\eqref{eq:latupper}, the sum is
$\ll(x/\log x)\sum_{k\in\J}\varphi(k)k^{-2}(\log z)^2\ll x\log x\,h(\J)$.
Divide by $x$ and sum over the $O(t)$ blocks.

\emph{Lower bound} (full blocks).  Write each bracket as
$(\li(2x)-\li(x))/\varphi(4uv)+E_x(4uv,-k^{-1})$.  Since
$\li(2x)-\li(x)\geq x/\log(2x)$ and $\varphi(4uv)\leq4uv$,
\eqref{eq:latlower} bounds the main terms below by
\[
 \frac{x}{\log2x}\sum_{k\in\J}\sum_{\Sset(k,c;H,z)}\frac1{4uv}
 \geq\frac{x\Lambda^2h(\J)}{16\log2x}\gg x\,t\,h(\J).
\]
For the error terms, group the triples by $q=4uv$.  The triples with a
given $(u,v)$ number $r_\J(u,v;c)$, and each triple with modulus $q$ has
error $|E_x(q,-k^{-1})|\leq E_x^*(q)$.  Put
$W(q)=\sum_{4uv=q}r_\J(u,v;c)$, summed over coprime $H<u,v\leq z$.  There
are at most $2^{\omega(q/4)}$ ordered coprime pairs with $uv=q/4$, so
$W(q)^2\leq2^{\omega(q/4)}\sum_{4uv=q}r_\J(u,v;c)^2$ by Cauchy--Schwarz.
By \eqref{eq:BT}, $E_x^*(q)\ll x/(\varphi(q)\log x)$ for $q\leq4x^{1/3}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:wsecond} gives
\[
 \sum_qW(q)^2E^*_x(q)\ll\frac x{\log x}
 \sum_{\substack{H<u,v\leq z\\(u,v)=1}}
 \frac{2^{\omega(uv)}r_\J(u,v;c)^2}{\varphi(4uv)}\ll x\,t^6,
\]
using $\log z\asymp\log x\asymp t$ and $\log K\leq\kappa t$.  By
Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{eq:BV} with $R=26$,
\[
 \Bigl|\sum_{\rm triples}E_x\Bigr|\leq\sum_qW(q)E^*_x(q)
 \leq\Bigl(\sum_qW(q)^2E^*_x(q)\Bigr)^{1/2}
 \Bigl(\sum_{q\leq4x^{1/3}}E^*_x(q)\Bigr)^{1/2}\ll x\,t^{-10}.
\]
Since $1\in\J$ gives $h(\J)\geq1$, this is $o(x\,t\,h(\J))$.  Divide by
$2x$ and sum over the $\asymp t$ full blocks.  No constant depends on $c$,
$\J$ or $x$.
\end{proof}

The Bombieri--Vinogradov input is used through the maximum $E_x^*(q)$
over all reduced classes.  Therefore \eqref{eq:supply} holds for every
choice of $c$ and $\J$, including choices that depend on the data, with no
averaging over $c$.  The multiplicity $W(q)$ can be as large as a power of
$X$.  It is paid for in mean square by its own weight $1/\varphi(q)$,
which is the role of Lemma~\ref{lem:wsecond}.

\begin{corollary}[fibre masses]\label{cor:fibremass}
Let $X\geq X_h$.  For every residue $c\bmod L_K$, reduced or not, put
\[
 \J_c=\{k\in\K(K):(k,c)=1\},\qquad
 \mu_c=\sum_{X^{1/2}<\ell\leq X}\frac{f_c(\ell)}\ell,
\]
with $f_c$ as in \eqref{eq:fc}.  Then $f_c=f_{c;\J_c}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibremass}
 a_ht^2h(\J_c)\leq\mu_c\leq A_ht^2h(\J_c)\leq C_ut^3
\end{equation}
with $C_u$ depending only on $\kappa$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
An atom active in the fibre of $c$ has $k\mid u+cv$ with
$(u,k)=1$.  Hence $c$ is a unit modulo $k$, that is, $k\in\J_c$, and
$(u,v)\in\Sset(k,c;H,z_j)$.  Conversely, every triple counted by
$f_{c;\J_c}(\ell)$ is an atom active in the fibre.  Moreover $1\in\J_c$
and $(c,L_{\J_c})=1$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:supply} applies with
$\J=\J_c$.  Finally $h(\J_c)\leq h(\K(K))\ll\log K\leq\kappa t$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:h}.
\end{proof}

\section{Factorial moments}\label{sec:moments}
For $y\geq2$ let $S_y(n)=\ind_{(n,P_y)=1}=\sum_{d\mid P_y}\mu(d)\ind_{d\mid n}$,
and write $(h)_m=h(h-1)\cdots(h-m+1)$.

\begin{theorem}[upper factorial moments]\label{thm:moments}
For every $X\geq X_h$, every real $2\leq y<K$ and every integer $m\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
 \E\bigl((H_X)_m\bigm|S_y=1\bigr)\leq(C_ut^3)^m .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The pair $(c,S_y)$ is a function of $n\bmod\lcm(L_K,P_y)$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:CRT}, conditionally on $c$ and $S_y=1$ we can write
$H_X=\sum_\ell I_\ell$, where the $I_\ell$ are independent and $I_\ell$ is
Bernoulli with mean $p_\ell=f_c(\ell)/\ell$.  For $0/1$ variables,
$(\sum_\ell I_\ell)_m$ is the sum of $\prod_{i\leq m}I_{\ell_i}$ over ordered
$m$-tuples of distinct primes.  Hence
\[
 \E\bigl((H_X)_m\bigm|c,S_y=1\bigr)
 =\sum_{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_m\ \mathrm{distinct}}\prod_{i=1}^mp_{\ell_i}
 \leq\mu_c^m\leq(C_ut^3)^m
\]
by Corollary~\ref{cor:fibremass}, which holds for every $c$.  Averaging
over $c$ gives \eqref{eq:moments}.
\end{proof}
The base $C_u$ does not depend on $m$; this matters because we shall take
$m\asymp t^3$.  The bound uses the upper estimate in \emph{every} fibre,
including fibres where $c$ is not reduced modulo $L_K$.  No Poisson
approximation is involved.

\section{An exponentially small conditioned void}\label{sec:void}
If $c$ is divisible by many primes dividing $L_K$, the multiplier set
$\J_c$ is small, and then the lower bound in \eqref{eq:fibremass} is weak.
Under the uniform measure such fibres have positive density.  For
example, $Q\mid c$ for a fixed odd squarefree $Q$ has probability $1/Q$.
A small-prime selector makes the \emph{low-mass} fibres exponentially rare.
Fibres with $(c,L_K)>1$ caused by a few large primes remain common, but
they are harmless.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selector}
 y=Bt^3,\qquad S_y(n)=\ind_{(n,P_y)=1}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $B$ and large $X$ we have $2\leq y<K$.  Every non-representable
integer without prime factors $\leq y$, in particular every
non-representable prime $>y$, has $S_y=1$.

\begin{lemma}[conditioned void]\label{lem:void}
There are $c_v>0$ and $B_0$, depending only on $\kappa$, such that for
every fixed $B\geq B_0$ and all $X\geq X_v(B)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:void}
 \Prob(H_X=0\mid S_y=1)\leq\e^{-c_vt^3}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 Z(c)=\sum_{\substack{y<p\leq K,\ p\mid L_K\\p\mid c}}\frac1p .
\]
The divisibility $p\mid c$ is meaningful because $p\mid L_K$.
Conditionally on $S_y=1$, the events $p\mid n$ for the primes $p>y$ with
$p\mid L_K$ are independent with probabilities $1/p$, by the CRT, because
$S_y$ depends only on $n\bmod P_y$.  Since $0<y/p<1$ and
$\e^s-1\leq(\e-1)s$ on $[0,1]$,
\[
 \E(\e^{yZ}\mid S_y=1)=\prod_{\substack{y<p\leq K\\p\mid L_K}}
 \Bigl(1+\frac{\e^{y/p}-1}p\Bigr)
 \leq\exp\Bigl\{(\e-1)y\sum_{n>y}n^{-2}\Bigr\}\leq\e^{4},
\]
using $\sum_{n>y}n^{-2}\leq1/\lfloor y\rfloor\leq2/y$.  Therefore
$\Prob(Z>\eta\mid S_y=1)\leq\e^{4-\eta y}$ for every $\eta>0$.

By \eqref{eq:hfull} there are absolute $a_0>0$ and $K_1$ with
$h(\K(K))\geq a_0\log K$ for $K\geq K_1$.  On $S_y=1$ no prime $p\leq y$ with
$p\mid L_K$ divides $c$.  A multiplier $k\notin\J_c$ therefore has a prime factor $p$
with $y<p\leq K$, $p\mid L_K$ and $p\mid c$.  By \eqref{eq:hremove},
\[
 h(\J_c)\geq h(\K(K))-\sum_{\substack{y<p\leq K,\ p\mid L_K\\p\mid c}}
 \frac{1+\log K}p\geq(a_0-2Z(c))\log K .
\]
Put $\eta=a_0/4$.  On the event $Z(c)\leq\eta$ we get
$h(\J_c)\geq\frac{a_0}2\log K\geq\frac{a_0\kappa}4t$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:CRT} and Corollary~\ref{cor:fibremass},
\[
 \Prob(H_X=0\mid c,S_y=1)=\prod_\ell\Bigl(1-\frac{f_c(\ell)}\ell\Bigr)
 \leq\e^{-\mu_c}\leq\e^{-a_ht^2h(\J_c)}\leq\e^{-a_vt^3},
\]
where $a_v=a_ha_0\kappa/4$.
Choose $B_0=2a_v/\eta$.  For $B\geq B_0$, averaging over $c$ gives
$\Prob(H_X=0\mid S_y=1)\leq\e^{-a_vt^3}+\e^{4-2a_vt^3}\leq\e^{-a_vt^3/2}$
for large $X$.  Take $c_v=a_v/2$.
\end{proof}
A Markov bound for $Z$ would be far too weak here.  The exponential moment
at parameter $y\asymp t^3$ makes the bad fibres ($Z(c)>\eta$) as rare as the required
saving.  The price is the expansion of $S_y$ into $2^{\pi(y)}$ classes,
which is affordable.  Revealing $c$ is an operation inside the CRT
computation only.  It does not expand anything into the $L_K$ progressions
modulo $L_K$.

\section{Bonferroni assembly on the integers}\label{sec:assembly}
\begin{lemma}[even Bonferroni majorant]\label{lem:Bonferroni}
For an even integer $r\geq0$ and an integer $h\geq0$ let
$Q_r(h)=\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^j\binom hj$.  Then $Q_r(0)=1$,
$Q_r(h)=\binom{h-1}r\geq0$ for $h\geq1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bonferroni}
 \ind_{h=0}\leq Q_r(h)\leq\ind_{h=0}+\binom h{r+1}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $h\geq1$, Pascal's rule telescopes the sum to $(-1)^r\binom{h-1}r$.
This vanishes for $h\leq r$, and for $h\geq r+1$ it is at most
$\frac h{r+1}\binom{h-1}r=\binom h{r+1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[assembly]\label{thm:assembly}
% (R289 repair) R288 m6: make the constant dependencies precise.
Let $B\geq B_0$ be fixed and $y=Bt^3$.  There are constants $c_a,D_B>0$
depending only on $\kappa$, and a constant $C_L>0$ and a threshold $X_a$
depending only on $\kappa$ and $B$, with the following property.
For $X\geq X_a$, let $r$ be the least even integer $\geq D_Bt^3$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:majorant}
 \nu_X(n)=S_y(n)\,Q_r(H_X(n)).
\end{equation}
Then $\nu_X\geq0$ on $\mathbb Z$, and $\nu_X(n)\geq1$ for every
non-representable $n$ with $(n,P_y)=1$.  Moreover $\E\nu_X\leq\e^{-c_at^3}$,
and for every real $N\geq1$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integermean}
 \sum_{1\leq n\leq N}\nu_X(n)\leq N\e^{-c_at^3}+\e^{C_Lt^4}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Mean.}  By \eqref{eq:Bonferroni}, Lemma~\ref{lem:void} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:moments}, using $m!\geq(m/\e)^m$,
\[
 \E(Q_r(H_X)\mid S_y=1)\leq\e^{-c_vt^3}
 +\frac{\E((H_X)_{r+1}\mid S_y=1)}{(r+1)!}
 \leq\e^{-c_vt^3}+\Bigl(\frac{\e C_ut^3}{r+1}\Bigr)^{r+1}.
\]
Choose a constant $D_B>\e C_u$ such that
$D_B\log(D_B/(\e C_u))\geq2c_v$.  The function
$m\mapsto m\log(m/(\e C_ut^3))$ is increasing for $m\geq C_ut^3$, and
$r+1\geq D_Bt^3$, so the last term is at most $\e^{-2c_vt^3}$.
Multiplying by $\Prob(S_y=1)\leq1$ gives $\E\nu_X\leq\e^{-c_at^3}$ with
$c_a=c_v/2$ for large $X$.  The
nonnegativity and the lower bound for non-representable $n$ follow from
Lemma~\ref{lem:Bonferroni} and $H_X(n)=0$ (Section~\ref{sec:atoms}).

\emph{Expansion into classes.}  For every integer $n$,
\[
 \binom{H_X(n)}j=\sum_{\substack{\mathcal B\subseteq\A_X\\|\mathcal B|=j}}
 \prod_{A\in\mathcal B}\ind_{E_A}(n),
\]
so that
\[
 \nu_X(n)=\sum_{d\mid P_y}\sum_{j=0}^r\sum_{|\mathcal B|=j}
 (-1)^j\mu(d)\,\ind_{d\mid n}\prod_{A\in\mathcal B}\ind_{E_A}(n).
\]
Each product is the indicator of the empty set or of a single class
modulo $q=\lcm(d,k_A\ell_A:A\in\mathcal B)$, and $q$ divides $\mathcal M$.
Hence $\E\nu_X$ is exactly the sum of the coefficients divided by these
moduli.  The number of terms, which bounds the absolute coefficient sum, is
\[
 T_{\rm abs}\leq2^{\pi(y)}\sum_{j=0}^r\binom{|\A_X|}j
 \leq2^{\pi(y)}(r+1)|\A_X|^r .
\]
For a crude inventory, an atom is determined by $k\leq K$, $\ell\leq X$
and $u,v\leq X^{1/6}$, so $|\A_X|\leq KX^{4/3}\leq\e^{(4/3+\kappa)t}$.
With $r\leq D_Bt^3+2$ and $\pi(y)\leq y=Bt^3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:termcount}
 \log T_{\rm abs}\leq Bt^3\log2+\log(r+1)+r(4/3+\kappa)t\leq C_Lt^4
\end{equation}
for a suitable $C_L=C_L(\kappa,B)$ and large $X$.  The exponent is
$O(rt)$ and not $O(r)$: every factor $\binom{\cdot}{j}$ costs a choice
of atoms among $\e^{O(t)}$.

\emph{Counting.}  For every modulus $q\geq1$ and every class $a\bmod q$,
\[
 \#\{1\leq n\leq N:n\equiv a\ (q)\}=N/q+\theta,\qquad|\theta|\leq1 .
\]
This also holds when $q>N$.  Summing over the terms with their signed
coefficients gives
$\sum_{n\leq N}\nu_X(n)=N\E\nu_X+O^*(T_{\rm abs})$, where $|O^*(T)|\leq T$.
This proves \eqref{eq:integermean}.
\end{proof}
No prime-counting estimate enters at scale $N$, and the residue classes
modulo $L_K$ are never enumerated: the term moduli are at most
$P_y(KX)^r=\e^{O(t^4)}$, while $L_K$ itself plays a role only in the
computation of $\E\nu_X$.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:all}
\begin{proof}[Primes]
Let $N$ be large, $L=\log N$, and take $t=\alpha L^{1/4}$ and $X=\e^t$,
where $\alpha>0$ is fixed with $C_L\alpha^4\leq1/2$.  Every
non-representable prime $p>y$ satisfies $\nu_X(p)\geq1$.  By
\eqref{eq:integermean},
\[
 E_{\rm pr}(N)\leq y+N\e^{-c_a\alpha^3L^{3/4}}+\e^{C_L\alpha^4L}
 \leq B\alpha^3L^{3/4}+N\e^{-c_a\alpha^3L^{3/4}}+N^{1/2}.
\]
This is $\ll N\exp\{-c_a\alpha^3L^{3/4}\}$.  Enlarging the constant covers
bounded $N\geq3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[All denominators]
If $p\mid n$ and $4/p=\sum_i1/x_i$, then $4/n=\sum_i1/((n/p)x_i)$.  Hence
every prime factor of a non-representable $n$ is non-representable, and
the non-representable integers lie in the multiplicative semigroup
generated by the non-representable primes (together with $1$).

By the prime case there are $c_0>0$ and $x_0\geq\e$ with
$E_{\rm pr}(x)\leq x\e^{-c_0(\log x)^{3/4}}$ for $x\geq x_0$.  Fix
$0<\eta_0<c_0$, put $L=\log x$ and $\delta=\eta_0L^{-1/4}$, and assume $x$
is so large that $\delta<1/4$.  Rankin's trick gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rankin}
 E(x)\leq\sum_{\substack{n\leq x\\n\ \text{non-repr.}}}\Bigl(\frac xn\Bigr)^{1-\delta}
 \leq x^{1-\delta}\prod_{\substack{p\leq x\\p\ \text{non-repr.}}}
 (1-p^{-1+\delta})^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The prime powers $p^a$ with $a\geq2$ contribute $O(\sum_pp^{-3/2})=O(1)$ to
the logarithm of the product, and the primes below $x_0$ contribute
$O_{x_0}(1)$.  By partial summation,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{x_0<p\leq x\\p\ \text{non-repr.}}}p^{-1+\delta}
 \leq\e^{\delta L-c_0L^{3/4}}+\int_{\log x_0}^{L}\e^{\delta s-c_0s^{3/4}}\,ds .
\]
For $s\leq L$ we have $\delta s=\eta_0sL^{-1/4}\leq\eta_0s^{3/4}$.  So the
first term is at most $1$, and the integral is at most
$\int_0^\infty\e^{-(c_0-\eta_0)s^{3/4}}ds<\infty$.  The product in
\eqref{eq:Rankin} is therefore bounded uniformly in $x$, and
$E(x)\ll x^{1-\delta}=x\exp\{-\eta_0(\log x)^{3/4}\}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Remarks}\label{sec:remarks}
\begin{remark}[comparison with Vaughan's argument]\label{rem:vaughan}
Vaughan uses the classes of Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} with $k=1$ and
Montgomery's large sieve with prime moduli (we follow the account in
\cite{PW}, \cite{LL}; the following balance is heuristic).  With $k=1$ only, the mass
in \eqref{eq:supply} is $\asymp t^2$.
% (R289 repair) R289 item 1: this is a sufficient scale for the standard evaluation, not a universal necessity.
In the standard Euler-product/Rankin-tail evaluation, taking $\log Q$
at least a suitable constant times $\mu\log X$ yields a gain
$\exp\{c\mu\}$.  With $Q\leq N^{1/2}$, this choice gives the balance
$t^3\asymp\log N$ and the saving $(\log N)^{2/3}$.  Here the extra factor
$h(\K(K))\asymp\log K\asymp t$
in the mass is paid for by the same kind of budget: the Bonferroni degree
is $r\asymp\mu$ and each degree costs a factor $\e^{O(t)}$ in
\eqref{eq:termcount}.  The balance becomes $t^4\asymp\log N$.  The
particular fibrewise large-sieve implementation of \cite{LL} pays for
splitting $[1,N]$ modulo $L_K$ and ceases to be useful when $L_K>N$.
Since $\log L_K\asymp K$, that implementation is limited to
$K\ll\log N$ and to a gain of $(\log\log N)^{1/3}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[total identity-class supply]\label{rem:supply}
For $M\equiv3\pmod4$ put $A=(M+1)/4$ and
$\mathcal R(M)=\{-uv^{-1}\bmod M:uvw=A\}$, the set of classes forced by
Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} with $k\ell$ replaced by $M$.  Then
$\mathcal R(M)=\{-4D\bmod M:D\mid A^2\}$.  Indeed $-uv^{-1}\equiv-4u^2w$
because $4uvw\equiv1$, so one may take $D=u^2w$.  Conversely, if
$p^e\Vert A$ and $p^d\Vert D$, give $u$, $w$, $v$ the exponents
$\lfloor d/2\rfloor$, $d-2\lfloor d/2\rfloor$ and the remainder.
Divisors $D<A$ give distinct classes, since $0<4D<M$, so
$\frac12(\tau(A^2)-1)\leq|\mathcal R(M)|\leq\tau(A^2)$.  From
$\tau(n^2)=\sum_{d^2\mid n}\mu(d)\tau_3(n/d^2)$ and
$\sum_{m\leq x}\tau_3(m)\sim\frac12x\log^2x$ one gets
$\sum_{M\leq Q,\,M\equiv3\,(4)}|\mathcal R(M)|/M\asymp(\log Q)^3$.  So the
supply of all identity classes, over all moduli and with distinct
residues, has exponent three, and the multipliers of this note reach
that order of magnitude.  Combined with the heuristic balance above, this
explains the value $3/4$.
% (R289 repair) R288 m3: preserve the qualification when recalling the companion cap.
The corresponding method-limit statement is conditional on the internal
companion results quoted in Remark~\ref{rem:limit}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[numerator $m$]
% (R289 repair) R288 m2 and R289 item 2: remove the unproved uniform formula and identify its separate source.
For a numerator $m\geq4$, the algebraic identity has the analogue
$k\ell+1=m\,uvw$ with $(k,m)=1$, and the prime-counting moduli become
$muv$.  A separate internal, unrefereed note \cite[Thm.~A]{MN} records an
extension to $m/n$, including uniform dependence on $m$, proved relative
to the present note.  That extension is conditional here on the companion
argument: its analytic uniformity requires additional work, does not
follow merely from the identity substitution, and is not claimed or
proved in this note.
\end{remark}
