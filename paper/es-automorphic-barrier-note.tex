\begin{abstract}
For a prime $p$ we collect exact reformulations of the Erd\H{o}s--Straus
equation $4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$ in automorphic and arithmetic-geometric
language. Type~I solutions are $\SL_2(\Z)$ matrices with a prescribed
entry, equivalently Heegner points of discriminant $-4d$ in a thin wedge,
whose discriminant-$(-4dp^2)$ forms are $T_p$-Hecke translates. Type~II
solutions are Heegner points of discriminant $-4dp$ lying on an exact
shell around a cuspidal geodesic. Further reformulations use orders in the
quaternion algebra $B_{p,\infty}$, the algebra $(-d,-p)_\Q$, a CM torus,
and an isotropic ternary form. The Bright--Loughran Brauer class is a
genus character; this is folklore and goes back to Yamamoto. All these
encodings reduce to one statement, which we prove: once two of the
Elsholtz--Tao coordinates are fixed, the solutions are either a finite
union of congruence classes of $p$, or the divisors of one explicit integer
$N_\theta(p)$ in one residue class. Every representative-independent
invariant of the automorphic objects is congruence or genus data. We then
record negative results, each with an exact scope. Fixed ternary embeddings
reduce to Mordell coverings. Any detector must work at main term
$p^{o(1)}$. Thickening a target is the same as averaging over $n$. Local,
genus and spinor solubility hold even at odd squares. Under Schinzel's
Hypothesis~H, every argument that certifies the equation through a fixed
finite set of slices fails infinitely often. We give the quantitative gaps
to known equidistribution theorems, list the escape hatches that remain
open, and report numerical evidence: $f_I(p)\ge1$ and $f_{II}(p)\ge1$ for
all primes $p\le10^8$. The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture is \emph{not}
proved here. The negative statements concern the routes examined and known
technology. They are not impossibility theorems for the conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture (ES) asserts that for every integer
$n\ge2$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:es}
 \frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z,\qquad x,y,z\in\N,
\end{equation}
is soluble. It suffices to treat primes $n=p$, and by Mordell's
identities only primes in six classes modulo $840$ (the squares) remain
open. ES has been verified to very large bounds and holds for almost all
$n$ in a strong quantitative sense, but it is open for an individual large
prime.

\subsection*{Why automorphic and geometric input}
In earlier internal work of this campaign
(\cite{PO}, and Theorems~M and~C of \cite{PS}) we proved that
\emph{congruence-covering} arguments cannot settle ES pointwise. More
precisely, under Schinzel's Hypothesis~H, every correct bounded
witness-producing procedure built from ring operations, floor division,
sign tests and factorisations of the integers it meets fails for
infinitely many primes $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ \cite[Thm~C]{PS}. This is the
procedure-level form of the classical odd-square obstructions (Yamamoto,
Mordell--Schinzel, Elsholtz--Tao \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}, Bright--Loughran
\cite[Cor.~1.4]{BL}). It leaves room for input that is \emph{not}
congruence data: spectral, automorphic or arithmetic-geometric
information about a fixed prime $p$. This note collects what such input
can see.

\subsection*{What this note contains}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Exact dictionaries} (\S\ref{sec:dict}, \status{proved}). Type~I
and Type~II solutions are rewritten as Heegner points, $\SL_2$ matrices,
Hecke translates, shells around cuspidal geodesics, orders in definite
quaternion algebras, divisors of an element of a CM field, and points on
an isotropic ternary quadric. Several of these are folklore. We mark
attributions explicitly; in particular, the identification of the
Bright--Loughran Brauer class with a genus character is \emph{not} new
(Yamamoto 1965, via \cite[App.~A]{BL}).
\item \emph{The divisor-class reduction} (\S\ref{sec:reduction},
\status{proved}). Each encoding is a ``slice'' of the Elsholtz--Tao
variety. Fixing two coordinates leaves either a congruence condition on
$p$ or the divisors of one integer $N_\theta(p)$ in one residue class
(Lemma~\ref{lem:slices}, Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}).
\item \emph{Negative results with exact scope} (\S\ref{sec:negative}):
ternary embeddings, the scale constraint, thickening, local solubility at
odd squares, geometry, and Hypothesis-H-conditional emptiness of single
targets.
\item \emph{Quantitative gaps} to known equidistribution theorems
(\S\ref{sec:gaps}, \status{Assessment}), \emph{open problems}
(\S\ref{sec:open}), and \emph{numerical evidence} (\S\ref{sec:numerics}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{What this note does not contain}
ES is not proved here, conditionally or unconditionally. We know of no
standard conjecture (GRH, GLH, Ramanujan, Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture,
twisted Linnik--Selberg) that is known to imply ES. The literature survey
behind this note \cite{LA} found no prior pointwise result for ES,
conditional or not. Every negative statement below concerns the specific
encodings and mechanisms analysed, or ``known technology''. None is a
theorem that ES cannot be proved by automorphic methods. Words like
``barrier'' are used in that mechanism-scoped sense; where they summarise
more than a proved statement, they are labelled \status{Assessment}.

\section{Exact dictionaries}\label{sec:dict}

\subsection{The Elsholtz--Tao normal form}
For an odd prime $p$, $p$ divides exactly one or exactly two of $x,y,z$ in
any solution of \eqref{eq:es} (it cannot divide all three, since
$1/x'+1/y'+1/z'=4$ has no positive solution). Following \cite[\S2]{ET}
and \cite[\S2.0]{RT2}, put
\begin{align*}
V_I(p)&=\{(a,b,c,d,e)\in\N^5:\ 4abd=pe+1,\ a+b=ce\},\\
V_{II}(p)&=\{(a,b,c,d,e)\in\N^5:\ 4abd=p+e,\ a+b=ce\}.
\end{align*}
An element of $V_I(p)$ gives the solution $(abdp,acd,bcd)$, and an element
of $V_{II}(p)$ gives $(abd,acdp,bcdp)$: indeed
$(c+p(a+b))/(abcdp)=c(1+pe)/(abcdp)=4/p$ and
$(cp+a+b)/(abcdp)=c(p+e)/(abcdp)=4/p$. Conversely every solution arises
in this way \cite[Props.~2.2, 2.6]{ET}. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VIVII}
\mathrm{ES}(p)\iff V_I(p)\cup V_{II}(p)\neq\emptyset.
\end{equation}
On $V_I(p)$ one has $p\nmid cd$ \cite[Prop.~2.1]{O130}, \cite[\S5]{O131}.
The counts $f_I(p)$, $f_{II}(p)$ count primitive tuples ($\gcd(a,b,c)=1$)
\cite[(2.10)]{ET}. A non-primitive tuple $(ga',gb',gc',d)$ is the primitive
tuple $(a',b',c',g^2d)$, so existence questions are insensitive to
primitivity.

\subsection{Type~I: $\SL_2$ matrices, Heegner wedges and the Hecke operator $T_p$}
The following is \cite[Lemmas~2.1--2.2, 2.4]{O123}; parts (i)--(ii) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI} are rearrangements of \cite[\S2]{ET}.

\begin{lemma}[\status{proved}]\label{lem:typeI}
Let $a,c,d,f\ge1$ with $p\nmid cd$. The following are equivalent:
\textup{(i)} $p=4acd-f$ and $f\mid 4a^2d+1$;
\textup{(ii)} $f\mid ap+c$ and $4ac\mid p+f$;
\textup{(iii)} $f\mid p^2+4c^2d$ and $f\equiv-p\pmod{4cd}$.
In that case $e=(4a^2d+1)/f$, $b=(ap+c)/f$ and
$g=(p^2+4c^2d)/f=4bcd-p$ are integers, $4abd-pe=1$, $a+b=ce$, and
\[\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}4ad&p\\ e&b\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_2(\Z).\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): $c(4a^2d+1)=a\cdot4acd+c=a(p+f)+c$, so
$f\mid ap+c$. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i): with $4acd=p+f$ the same identity gives
$f\mid c(4a^2d+1)$, and $\gcd(f,c)\mid\gcd(4acd-p,c)\mid p$; if $p\mid f$
and $p\mid c$ then $p\mid cd$, which is excluded. (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii):
modulo $f$ we have $4acd\equiv p$, hence
$4c^2d(4a^2d+1)\equiv p^2+4c^2d$, and $\gcd(f,4c^2d)\mid p$, which is $1$
unless $p\mid cd$. For the identities, put $e=(4a^2d+1)/f$ and $b=ce-a$.
Then $bf=c(4a^2d+1)-a(4acd-p)=ap+c$ (so $b\ge1$),
$4abd=e\cdot4acd-4a^2d=e(p+f)-4a^2d=pe+1$, and
$(4acd-p)(4bcd-p)=4c^2d(4abd-pe)+p^2=p^2+4c^2d$, using $a+b=ce$; see
\cite[(2.8)--(2.9)]{ET}.
\end{proof}

Write $Q_-=[f,-4ad,de]$ (discriminant $-4d$) and $Q'=[f,4cd,dg]$
(discriminant $-4dp^2$).

\begin{proposition}[Hecke form; \status{proved}]\label{prop:hecke}
For every Type~I tuple,
$Q'(x,y)=Q_-(x+2bd\,y,\ p\,y)$, i.e.
$Q'=Q_-\circ\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2bd\\0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Thus the discriminant-$(-4dp^2)$ form of a Type~I solution is a
$T_p$-Hecke translate of a level-$d$ Heegner form of discriminant $-4d$,
and the roots satisfy $z_{Q'}=p\,z_{Q_-}-2bd$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The middle coefficient is $2f\cdot2bd-4adp=4d(bf-ap)=4cd$. The last is
$4b^2d^2f-8abd^2p+dep^2=d\bigl(4bd(ap+c)-8abdp+ep^2\bigr)
=d\bigl(4bcd-p(4abd-pe)\bigr)=d(4bcd-p)=dg$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the wedge; \status{proved}, {\cite[Prop.~3.1]{O123}}]\label{prop:wedge}
Let $\Lambda_d=\{z=(2ad+i\sqrt d)/f:\ a,f\ge1,\ f\mid4a^2d+1\}$, the roots
of the forms $[f,-4ad,de]$. For $z=x+iy\in\Lambda_d$ and $c\ge1$,
\[\frac{2c\sqrt d\,x-\sqrt d-p\,y}{y}=4acd-f-p\in\Z.\]
Hence $(a,c,d,f)$ is a Type~I tuple of $p$ if and only if $z$ lies in the
wedge $R_c=\{x+iy:\ |2c\sqrt d\,x-\sqrt d-py|<y\}$ at the cusp $1/(2c)$.
The edges of $R_c$ are hypercycles of the geodesic $\{\operatorname{Re}z=1/(2c)\}$,
and $R_c$ is a tube of hyperbolic width
$\operatorname{arsinh}\frac{p+1}{2c\sqrt d}-\operatorname{arsinh}\frac{p-1}{2c\sqrt d}
\approx 2/\sqrt{p^2+4c^2d}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $z\in\Lambda_d$ we have $x/y=2a\sqrt d$ and $\sqrt d/y=f$; substitute.
An integer of absolute value $<1$ is $0$. The width is the difference of the
distances of the two edges from the geodesic.
\end{proof}

The target is therefore at the \emph{Planck scale} $\asymp1/p$ of the
relevant discriminant. The same holds in the $T_p$-translated picture,
where the wedge sits at $-1/(2a)$ \cite[Prop.~3.2]{O123}.

\begin{lemma}[genus condition; \status{proved}; classical]\label{lem:genusI}
If $(a,c,d,f)$ is a Type~I tuple of an odd $n$ with $\gcd(n,2cd)=1$, then
$\chi_{-4d}(-n)=1$. For $n=p$ prime this says $\leg{-d}{p}=-1$: $p$ is
inert in $\Q(\sqrt{-d})$. For an odd square $n=m^2$ the condition fails for
every $d$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every prime $q\mid f$ divides $n^2+4c^2d$ and is prime to $2cd$, so
$-4c^2d\equiv n^2$ is a non-zero square mod $q$, and $\chi_{-4d}(f)=1$. As
$f\equiv-n\pmod{4cd}$ and $\chi_{-4d}$ has period dividing $4d$,
$\chi_{-4d}(-n)=\chi_{-4d}(f)=1$. For $n=m^2$,
$\chi_{-4d}(-m^2)=\chi_{-4d}(-1)=-1$. For $n=p$,
$\chi_{-4d}(-p)=-\chi_{-4d}(p)=-\leg{-d}{p}$.
\end{proof}
This is the Type~I half of \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}, read at a fixed level.
Any fixed-$p$ main term at level $d$ must therefore carry the factor
$(1+\chi_{-4d}(-p))/2$.

\subsection{Type~II: determinant one at level $dp$, and shells}
The following is \cite[Lemma~1.1, Props.~2.1, 2.5, 7.1, Lemma~6.1]{O128}.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}]\label{prop:typeII}
Let $c,d\ge1$, $M=4cd$ and $N_{c,d}=4c^2dp+1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Type~II tuples of $p$ with these $(c,d)$ correspond to
factorisations $ff'-4c^2(dp)=1$ with $f\equiv f'\equiv-1\pmod{M}$, via
$f=4acd-1$, $f'=4bcd-1$. Equivalently, $f$ runs over the divisors of
$N_{c,d}$ with $f\equiv-1\pmod M$ (the cofactor is then automatically
$\equiv-1$).
\item[(b)] The map $(c,f)\mapsto Q=[f,4cdp,dpf']$ sends these solutions
injectively to Heegner forms of discriminant $-4dp$ at level $dp$. For
fixed $(p,d)$ the corresponding points of $\HH$ are pairwise at hyperbolic
distance with $\cosh\ge3/2$.
\item[(c)] In the determinant-$p$ coordinates $Q=[f,4ad,de]$ (level one,
discriminant $-4dp$), with $S_c=[0,2c,1]$ and the bilinear form
$b(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)$ of $q([A,B,C])=B^2-4AC$, one has
$q(Q)=-4dp$, $q(S_c)=4c^2$ and $b(Q,S_c)=4$. Thus the Type~II solutions
are embeddings of the binary lattice $4\cdot[-dp,1,c^2]$ (discriminant
$4c^2dp+1$ before scaling) into the trace-zero lattice of signature $(2,1)$.
Geometrically, a Type~II point is a Heegner point of discriminant $-4dp$ at
hyperbolic distance exactly $\operatorname{arsinh}\bigl(1/(2c\sqrt{dp})\bigr)$
from the cuspidal geodesic $(\infty,-1/(2c))$: it lies on a \emph{shell}.
\item[(d)] (genus lemma) $(p/f)=(p/f')=-1$ (Jacobi symbols).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof of (a), (c), (d)]
(a) $(4acd-1)(4bcd-1)=4c^2d(4abd-e)+1=4c^2dp+1$ using $a+b=ce$ and
$4abd=p+e$. Conversely, given $f\mid N_{c,d}$ with $f\equiv-1\pmod M$, the
cofactor $f'$ satisfies $ff'\equiv1$, hence $f'\equiv-1\pmod M$; put
$a=(f+1)/M$, $b=(f'+1)/M$; expanding $ff'=N_{c,d}$ gives
$4abcd=a+b+cp$, so $c\mid a+b$ and $e=(a+b)/c=4abd-p$.
(c) With $e=(p+4a^2d)/f$ (an integer, \cite[(2.19)]{ET}),
$q(Q)=16a^2d^2-4def=-4dp$ and
$b(Q,S_c)=2\cdot4ad\cdot2c-4f=4(4acd-f)=4$. The distance formula is
\cite[Prop.~2.5]{O128}.
(d) $ef=p+4a^2d$ gives $(2ad)^2\equiv-dp\pmod f$ with $\gcd(dp,f)=1$, so
$(-dp/f)=1$. Since $f\equiv-1\pmod{4d}$ and $\chi_{-4d}(-1)=-1$,
$(-d/f)=\chi_{-4d}(f)=-1$, hence $(p/f)=-1$. The same argument applies to
$f'$.
\end{proof}
Part (b) is \cite[Prop.~2.1, Lemma~2.2]{O128}; the separation is the
lemma $\cosh\operatorname{dist}-1=\operatorname{disc}(Q-Q')/(8dp)$ of
\cite[Lemma~2.2]{TH} applied at level $dp$. Part (d) recovers the Type~II
half of \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}: at an odd square $n=s^2$ one has
$(s^2/f)=1$. As for Type~I, it is a level-wise reading of a classical
fact; see Proposition~\ref{prop:brauer}.

\subsection{The Brauer class is a genus character (folklore)}
Bright and Loughran \cite{BL} study the surface
$U_n:4u_1u_2u_3=n(u_1u_2+u_1u_3+u_2u_3)$ and its integral model. Over $\Q$
the group $\Br\tilde U_n/\Br\Q$ is $\Z/2$, generated by the transcendental
quaternion algebra $\alpha=(-u_1/u_3,-u_2/u_3)$ \cite[Cor.~2.7]{BL}. They
show that the positive integral points avoid the Brauer--Manin obstruction
for every $n$ \cite[Thm.~1.1]{BL}, and that $\alpha$ obstructs strong
approximation \cite[Thm.~1.2, Cor.~1.4]{BL}.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; folklore, Yamamoto \cite{Ya} via {\cite[App.~A]{BL}}]\label{prop:brauer}
\textup{(a)} On the Type~I locus at level $d$, the condition imposed by
$\alpha$ is $\leg{-ab}{p}=\leg{-d}{p}=-1$, i.e. Lemma~\ref{lem:genusI}.
For $\leg{-d}{p}=+1$ the positive adelic Brauer set of the level-$d$
chart is empty.
\textup{(b)} On the Type~II locus, $\alpha$ imposes
$(f/p)=-(-1/p)$, which is equivalent to Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(d)
and holds automatically. So the Brauer--Manin obstruction never removes a
Type~II level.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
(a) $4abd=pe+1$ gives $(abd/p)=1$, so $(-ab/p)=(-d/p)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:genusI}
gives $(-d/p)=-1$. For the local statement, $\operatorname{inv}_p\alpha=(-ab/p)$
on $p$-adic points of the chart, $\operatorname{inv}_\infty\alpha=-1$ on the
positive part, and $\operatorname{inv}_\ell\alpha=+1$ for $\ell\ne p$
\cite[Lemmas~3.1, 3.5, 3.8]{BL}.
(b) $e\equiv4abd\pmod p$ gives $(-ab/p)=(-de/p)$, and $ef\equiv4a^2d\pmod p$
gives $(e/p)=(d/p)(f/p)$. Since $f\equiv3\pmod4$, reciprocity gives
$(f/p)=(p/f)(-1/p)$. Details: \cite[Props.~5.1, 5.2]{O126}.
\end{proof}
Nothing here is new. Part (a) is the classical reason why the
Mordell/Elsholtz--Tao families $n\equiv-f\pmod{4ad}$, $f\mid4a^2d+1$, cover
only non-residue classes. Part (b) is Yamamoto's lemma $(p/q)=-1$ for
$q=4abd-1$ in Mordell's notation, as derived from $\alpha$ in
\cite[App.~A]{BL}. (Yamamoto's paper was not accessed directly.)

\subsection{Quaternion orders}
For $d,m\ge1$ let $\Lambda_{d,m}=\Z\langle u,v\rangle$ with $u^2=-d$,
$v^2=-m$, $uv+vu=1$. It is an order $\Z+\Z u+\Z v+\Z uv$ in the definite
algebra $B_{d,m}\cong(-d,-d(4dm-1))_\Q$ (replace $v$ by $2dv+u$, which
anticommutes with $u$), of reduced discriminant $4dm-1$: the Gram matrix of
the reduced trace on $(1,u,v,uv)$ has determinant $-(4dm-1)^2$
\cite[Lemma~1.2]{O130}.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Prop.~1.3]{O130}}]\label{prop:quat}
Let $(a,b,c,d,e)\in V_I(p)$ and $m=ab$. Then $B_{d,m}\cong B_{p,\infty}$, the
quaternion algebra ramified exactly at $p$ and $\infty$, and $\Lambda_{d,m}$
is an order of reduced discriminant $4abd-1=pe$ in it. Consequently there is
a supersingular elliptic curve over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ with
endomorphisms $u,v$ satisfying $u^2=-d$, $v^2=-ab$, $uv+vu=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Compute the Hilbert symbols of $(-d,-dpe)$. At $\ell\nmid2dpe$ they are
trivial. At $p$: $p\nmid e$ (if $p\mid e$ then $-4a^2d\equiv1\pmod p$,
contradicting $(-d/p)=-1$), so the symbol is $(-d/p)=-1$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:genusI}. At odd $\ell\mid e$: $-d\equiv(2a)^{-2}$ is a square
mod $\ell$ ($e\mid4a^2d+1$), so the symbol is $1$. At odd $\ell\mid d$:
$pe\equiv-1\pmod\ell$ gives $1$. At $\infty$ it is $-1$, and the product
formula gives $2$. The last sentence is Deuring's correspondence.
\end{proof}
This was checked on all Type~I tuples with $p<3000$ (\status{evidence},
\cite{O130}). In the language of Gross--Zagier \cite{GZ}, the identity
$(16abd-4)/4=pe$ has the shape $(D_1D_2-x^2)/4$ with $D_1=-4d$, $D_2=-4ab$,
$x=2$, so the CM points of these discriminants have a common supersingular
reduction. This is a \emph{formal analogy only}: the theorem of \cite{GZ}
assumes coprime fundamental discriminants, and $-4d,-4ab$ are never
coprime. No Gross--Zagier formula is used.

\begin{lemma}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Lemma~3.1]{O130}}]\label{lem:dp}
In $B=(-d,-p)_\Q$ with $i^2=-d$, $j^2=-p$, $k=ij=-ji$, Type~II at level $d$
holds if and only if, for some $a,b,c,e\ge1$,
\[(2a\,i+j)(2b\,i-j)=-e(1+2c\,k).\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The left side is $-4abd-2(a+b)k+p=-(4abd-p)-2(a+b)k$; compare coefficients
with $4abd-p=e$, $a+b=ce$.
\end{proof}
Taking reduced norms gives $\Nm(2ai+j)=4a^2d+p=ef$ and
$\Nm(1+2ck)=1+4c^2dp=ff'$, the integers of the ternary form below and of
the shells of Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}. The novelty of the quaternion
dictionary is \emph{unverified}: the survey \cite{LA} found no prior
occurrence, but we do not claim it is new.

\subsection{The CM torus}
Let $p\nmid2cd$, $K=\Q(\sqrt{-d})$, $\alpha=p+2c\sqrt{-d}$, so
$\Nm\alpha=p^2+4c^2d$.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Lemma~5.1, Thm.~5.2]{O131}}]\label{prop:cm}
\textup{(i)} $\mathfrak a\mapsto\Nm\mathfrak a$ is a bijection from the
ideal divisors of $(\alpha)$ in $\Z[\sqrt{-d}]$ to the positive divisors of
$\Nm\alpha$.
\textup{(ii)} The number $T_{c,d}(p)$ of ordered Type~I tuples with these
$(c,d)$ equals $\#\{\mathfrak a\mid(\alpha):\Nm\mathfrak a\equiv-p\pmod{4cd}\}$, and
\[T_{c,d}(p)=\frac1{\varphi(4cd)}\sum_{\chi\bmod4cd}\bar\chi(-p)\,\sigma_\chi(p^2+4c^2d),
\qquad\sigma_\chi(N)=\sum_{f\mid N}\chi(f).\]
\textup{(iii)} Only Hecke characters of the form $\chi\circ\Nm$ occur. In
particular every class-group character of $K$ that is not a genus
character has coefficient $0$, and pairing $\chi\leftrightarrow\chi\chi_{-4d}$
produces the factor $1+\chi_{-4d}(-p)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:genusI}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
$\alpha$ is primitive since $\gcd(p,2c)=1$. Every prime $\ell\mid\Nm\alpha$
is odd and prime to $d$, so it is unramified and not inert, and it splits.
Exactly one of the two primes above $\ell$ divides $\alpha$, to the full
power. This gives (i). Then (ii) is Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}(iii) plus
orthogonality on $(\Z/4cd)^\times$, and (iii) is genus theory.
\end{proof}
Thus the dihedral (Frobenius) information carried by the class of
$(f,\alpha)$ in the ring class field is invisible in the count.

\subsection{The isotropic ternary form $Q_d$}
Let $Q_d(e,f,a)=ef-4da^2$.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Prop.~6.2]{O124}}, {\cite[\S6]{RT1}}]\label{prop:Qd}
\textup{(i)} Type~II at level $d$ with parameter $a$ holds if and only if
$Q_d(e,f,a)=p$ with $4ad\mid f+1$ and $(f+1)e/(4ad)>a$, i.e.
$p+4a^2d$ has a divisor $f\equiv-1\pmod{4ad}$ with large enough cofactor.
\textup{(ii)} $Q_d$ represents every integer primitively over $\Z$
($(e,f,a)=(n,1,0)$), so local conditions, genus and spinor genus impose
nothing; all the arithmetic sits in the side condition $4ad\mid f+1$.
\textup{(iii)} The side condition is not a finite union of lattice cosets:
its slice at $a=a_0$ has $f$-period $4a_0d$, which is unbounded.
\textup{(iv)} On such points the cofactor $f=4acd-1$ is a Type~II
\emph{multiplier witness modulus}; in particular $f\ge W(p)$, the least
such modulus.
\end{proposition}
Among all the parametrisations of \cite[\S2]{ET}, $Q_d$ is the only
rank-$3$ quadratic piece (\status{certified} by a systematic search,
\cite[Comp.~6.1]{O124}, reproduced independently in its review).

\section{The divisor-class reduction}\label{sec:reduction}
This section is \cite[\S2]{RT2}. A \emph{slice} is a choice
$\theta$ of two of the coordinates $\{a,b,c,d,e\}$ together with prescribed
values for them; $V_\theta(p)$ denotes the elements of $V_I(p)$ (or
$V_{II}(p)$) with these values. By the symmetry $a\leftrightarrow b$ the
coordinate pairs are $\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{a,d\},\{a,e\},\{c,d\},\{c,e\},\{d,e\}$.

\begin{lemma}[slice table; \status{proved}, elementary]\label{lem:slices}
For each type and each slice $\theta$ exactly one of the following holds.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(C)] \emph{Congruence slice:} $V_\theta(p)\neq\emptyset$ if and only if
$p$ lies in an explicit finite union of residue classes modulo a modulus
depending only on $\theta$.
\item[(D)] \emph{Divisor slice:} there are an integer $N_\theta(p)$ (a
polynomial in $p$ of degree $\le2$, possibly divided by a constant depending
on $\theta$), a modulus $q_\theta$ and a residue set $R_\theta(p)\subset\Z/q_\theta$
such that $V_\theta(p)$ is in bijection with
$\{f\ge1:\ f\mid N_\theta(p),\ f\bmod q_\theta\in R_\theta(p)\}$. The set
$R_\theta(p)$ depends only on $\theta$, on $p\bmod q_\theta$ and, in the
$\{d,e\}$ rows, on $N_\theta(p)\bmod q_\theta$.
\end{itemize}
The rows are as follows (Type~I rows containing $c$ or $d$ are for
$p\nmid cd$; otherwise the fibre is empty).
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llllll}
\toprule
type & $\theta$ & kind & $N_\theta(p)$ & divisor $f$ & class condition\\
\midrule
I & $\{c,d\}$ & D & $p^2+4c^2d$ & $4acd-p$ & $f\equiv-p\ (4cd)$\\
I & $\{a,c\}$ & D & $ap+c$ & $4acd-p$ & $f\equiv-p\ (4ac)$\\
I & $\{a,e\}$ & D & $(pe+1)/4a$ & $b$ & $f\equiv-a\ (e)$\\
I & $\{d,e\}$ & D & $m=(pe+1)/4d$ & $a$ & $f^2\equiv-m\ (e)$, $(f,e)=1$\\
I & $\{a,d\}$ & C & $4a^2d+1$ & $4acd-p$ & $p\equiv-f\ (4ad)$\\
I & $\{a,b\},\{c,e\}$ & C & --- & --- & $p$ mod $4ab$, resp.\ $4a(ce-a)$\\
II & $\{c,d\}$ & D & $4c^2dp+1$ & $4acd-1$ & $f\equiv-1\ (4cd)$\\
II & $\{a,c\}$ & D & $cp+a$ & $4acd-1$ & $f\equiv-1\ (4ac)$\\
II & $\{a,d\}$ & D & $p+4a^2d$ & $4acd-1$ & $f\equiv-1\ (4ad)$\\
II & $\{a,e\}$ & D & $(p+e)/4a$ & $b$ & $f\equiv-a\ (e)$\\
II & $\{d,e\}$ & D & $m=(p+e)/4d$ & $a$ & $f^2\equiv-m\ (e)$, $(f,e)=1$\\
II & $\{a,b\},\{c,e\}$ & C & --- & --- & $p$ mod $4ab$, resp.\ $4a(ce-a)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In the $\{a,e\}$ and $\{d,e\}$ rows, $N_\theta(p)$ is required to be an
integer (otherwise the fibre is empty).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each D row is an identity plus a converse.
\emph{I$\{c,d\}$} is Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}(iii). For the converse, the
cofactor $f'=N/f$ satisfies $f'\equiv p^2(-p)^{-1}\equiv-p\pmod{4cd}$; put
$a=(f+p)/4cd$, $b=(f'+p)/4cd$; expanding $ff'=N$ gives
$p(a+b)=c(4abd-1)$, so $c\mid a+b$ and $e=(a+b)/c=(4abd-1)/p$.
\emph{II$\{c,d\}$} is Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(a).
\emph{I$\{a,c\}$} is Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}(ii).
\emph{II$\{a,c\}$, II$\{a,d\}$}: with $f=4acd-1$ one has $ef=4a^2d+p$, and
$c(4a^2d+p)=a(f+1)+cp$, so $f\mid cp+a$ (as $\gcd(f,c)=1$). Conversely put
$d=(f+1)/4ac$, resp.\ $c=(f+1)/4ad$, then $e=(p+4a^2d)/f$ and $b=ce-a$.
\emph{$\{a,e\}$, $\{d,e\}$}: with $M=pe+1$ (Type~I) or $M=p+e$ (Type~II)
the defining equations read $bd=M/4a$, resp.\ $ab=M/4d$, together with
$e\mid a+b$. For $\gcd(a,e)=1$, $e\mid a+m/a$ is equivalent to
$e\mid a^2+m$, and every element has $\gcd(a,e)=1$: for Type~I since
$a\mid pe+1$; for Type~II since $\gcd(a,e)\mid p$ and $p\nmid e$ (if
$p\mid e$ then $4abd=p+e\le2e\le2(a+b)$ forces $a=b=d=1$ and then
$4=p+e\ge2p$, impossible).
\emph{C rows}: for I$\{a,d\}$ the fibre is $\{f\mid4a^2d+1: f\equiv-p\ (4ad)\}$,
a condition on $p\bmod 4ad$; for $\{a,b\}$, $e$ runs over the divisors of
$a+b$ and the condition is $4ab\mid pe+1$ (resp.\ $p+e$); for $\{c,e\}$,
$a$ runs over $[1,ce-1]$ with condition $4a(ce-a)\mid pe+1$ (resp.\ $p+e$).
\end{proof}
All bijections were verified mechanically against a brute-force
enumeration of $V_I(p)\cup V_{II}(p)$ for every prime $5\le p<200$
(\status{certified}, \cite[Lemma~2.1]{RT2}; script
\texttt{scripts/rt2\_slices.py}).

\begin{theorem}[divisor-class reduction]\label{thm:reduction}
Consider the encodings of \S\ref{sec:dict}:
E1 the Type~I $\SL_2$/Heegner wedge (Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge},
$\theta=\{c,d\}$; its $T_p$-translated form, per tube, $\theta=\{a,d\}$);
E2 the Type~II shells (Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}, $\theta=\{c,d\}$);
E3 the quaternion order of Proposition~\ref{prop:quat} ($\theta=\{d,e\}$);
E4 the $(-d,-p)$ factorisation of Lemma~\ref{lem:dp} ($\theta=\{d,e\}$;
the norms of $2ai+j$ give $\theta=\{a,d\}$);
E5 the CM torus (Proposition~\ref{prop:cm}, $\theta=\{c,d\}$);
E6 the ternary form $Q_d$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:Qd}, $\theta=\{a,d\}$).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \status{(proved)} The E-solutions with parameter $\theta$ are in
bijection with $V_\theta(p)$. Hence they correspond to the divisors of the
single integer $N_\theta(p)$ lying in $R_\theta(p)$, or (C rows) to a
congruence condition on $p$.
\item[(ii)] \status{(proved, relative to the cited internal statements)}
Every representative-independent invariant of these objects is congruence
or genus data. Specifically: for E5 only genus characters have non-zero
coefficient (Proposition~\ref{prop:cm}). For E3 the collision datum is a
function of $\theta$, of $p\bmod4d$, of $(-d/p)$ and of $(-d/\ell)$ for
$\ell\mid e$, and it holds automatically on every admissible $\theta$
\cite[Prop.~2.2]{O130}. For E1, E2 and E6 the property of being an ES point
belongs to the representative, not to the CM point or $\Gamma$-orbit: each
class has infinitely many representatives of the required shape and only
finitely many good ones (\cite[Prop.~4.1]{O126}; \cite[Prop.~8.2]{O128}; the
stabiliser of $S_c$ in $\SL_2(\Z)$ is $\pm I$). For E4 the ES elements lie
on the line $\{2xi\pm j\}$, on which the reduced norm is the one-variable
polynomial $4dx^2+p$ \cite[Prop.~3.2]{O130}.
\item[(iii)] \status{(conditional on Hypothesis~H)} Let $\Theta_0$ be a finite
set of slice parameters, of any types and rows, fixed independently of
$p$. Any argument that certifies $\mathrm{ES}(p)$ by exhibiting an
E-solution with parameter in $\Theta_0$ fails for infinitely many primes
$p\equiv1\pmod{24}$. This holds whatever auxiliary automorphic, spectral
or geometric information the argument uses.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) Each source file proves the exact lift from its object to $V_\theta(p)$
(Propositions~\ref{prop:wedge}, \ref{prop:typeII}, \ref{prop:quat},
\ref{prop:cm}, \ref{prop:Qd} and Lemma~\ref{lem:dp}); then apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:slices}. (ii) is a collection of the cited statements.
(iii) The argument can only succeed at primes with
$\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta_0}V_\theta(p)\ne\emptyset$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:slices}
this predicate is decided, together with a witness, by the bounded and
correct procedure ``for $\theta\in\Theta_0$: test $p\bmod q_\theta$; factor
$N_\theta(p)$ (with floor division for the $\{\cdot,e\}$ rows); test each
divisor against $R_\theta(p)$''. By \cite[Thm.~C]{PS}, under Hypothesis~H
every such procedure fails for infinitely many $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[scope]\label{rem:scope}
Part (iii) says that whatever an automorphic input ``knows'', if it
certifies ES through a fixed finite set of fibres it is no stronger than
factoring finitely many fixed polynomials in $p$, and under H that fails.
It does \emph{not} say that automorphic methods cannot aggregate over a
parameter set $\Theta(p)$ that grows with $p$; Kuznetsov's formula and
Heegner equidistribution aggregate over levels and discriminants in
exactly this way. Aggregation over $\theta$ is legitimate, since $\theta$
is a coordinate of the solution and not fixed by the problem. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:slices} it amounts to controlling, at one prime $p$, the
divisor events of a growing family $\{N_\theta(p)\}_{\theta\in\Theta(p)}$
jointly. The quantitative obstacles to doing so are in
\S\S\ref{sec:negative}--\ref{sec:gaps}, and the open routes in
\S\ref{sec:open}. Unconditional quantitative versions of (iii) exist for
specific growing boxes. For the Type~II box $\{acd\le T/4\}$ one uses
$f=4acd-1\ge W(p)$ together with the $\Omega$-result
$W(p)>\exp\bigl(c(\log p)^{1/4}(\log\log p)^{-1/4}\bigr)$ for infinitely
many $p$ (\cite{PO}, modulo a theorem of Thorner and Zaman). For Type~I
heights there is $ck_{\min}(p)\gg\log p\cdot\log\log\log p$ infinitely
often (internal file \texttt{POINTWISE\_OMEGA.md}, Thm.~8.5, modulo
Lau--Wu). Both are cited as summarised in \cite[\S2.2]{RT2}.
\end{remark}

\section{Negative results with exact scope}\label{sec:negative}
Each result in this section concerns a precisely defined mechanism. None of
them says that ES is false or unprovable.

\subsection{Ternary and higher-rank embeddings}
Fix $k\ge3$, a non-degenerate integral quadratic form $Q$ in $k$ variables,
and integers $\kappa\ge1$, $\eta$, all independent of $p$.

\begin{definition}[{\cite[Def.~1.1]{O124}}]
An \emph{embedding of complexity $(J,D)$} is a family of pieces
$(S_j,\Phi_j)_{j\le J}$, where $S_j\subseteq\Z^k$ is an \emph{arbitrary}
subset and $\Phi_j=(X_j,Y_j,Z_j)$ is a triple of rational functions of
degree $\le D$. It is \emph{valid} if for every prime $p$, every $j$ and
every $v\in S_j$ with $Q(v)=\kappa p+\eta$, $\Phi_j(v)\in\N^3$ solves
\eqref{eq:es} for $n=p$. Put $R(p)=\bigcup_j\{v\in S_j: Q(v)=\kappa p+\eta\}$.
A piece is \emph{degenerate} if $X_j,Y_j,Z_j\in\Q(Q)$.
\end{definition}
This covers every statement of the form ``$\kappa p+\eta$ is represented by
$Q$ with side conditions, hence ES$(p)$ by an explicit piecewise rational
recipe'' of fixed complexity. It does not cover recipes of unbounded
algebraic complexity, or families with a parameter that grows with $p$.

\begin{theorem}[\status{proved}, relative to Pila \cite{Pi}, Mordell--Schinzel \cite{Mo,Sch} and {\cite[Thm.~1.1]{ET}}; {\cite[Thm.~4.1, Cor.~4.2]{O124}}]\label{thm:ternary}
Let $Q$ be positive definite. There is a finite union $U$ of primitive
classes modulo some $q^*$, containing no square class, such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] for every large prime $p\notin U$,
$\#R(p)\ll f(p)\,p^{(k-2)/2-1/4+\varepsilon}$, where $f(p)$ is the total number
of solutions;
\item[(b)] for $0<\varepsilon_0<1/4$ the primes $p\le N$, $p\notin U$, with
$\#R(p)\ge c\,p^{(k-2)/2-\varepsilon_0}$ number $\ll N^{3/4+\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon}$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, suppose a representation theorem (circle method for $k\ge5$,
Kloosterman/$\delta$-method for $k=4$, Duke \cite{Du}, Duke--Schulze-Pillot
\cite{DSP} and Blomer-type results for $k=3$) gives
$\#R(p)\ge c\,p^{(k-2)/2-\varepsilon_0}$ on a union $\mathcal P_R$ of primitive
classes, outside $o(\pi(N))$ exceptions. Then $\mathcal P_R\subseteq U$:
the representation input proves ES only for primes that a finite
polynomial (Mordell-type) covering already handles.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
A degenerate piece produces an ES solution depending only on $p$. By a
rationality and integrality argument it is a polynomial identity on
finitely many classes, which by Mordell--Schinzel are not square classes;
this defines $U$. A non-degenerate piece is non-constant on the quadric
$Q=m$, $m=\kappa p+\eta$, outside finitely many $p$. Group the points of
$R_j(p)$ by the value of a non-constant coordinate $X_j$; there are at
most $f(p)$ values. Each group lies on a hypersurface section of the
bounded quadric $Q=m$. For $k=3$ such a section is a curve whose real
components have degree $\ge2$ (no real line lies on a bounded quadric). By
Pila's bound it has $\ll m^{1/4+\varepsilon}$ integer points in a box of side
$\asymp m^{1/2}$. For $k\ge4$ one slices by the last $k-3$ coordinates. This
gives (a). Then (b) follows from (a) and the first-moment
bound $\sum_{p\le N}f(p)\ll N\log^2N\log\log N$ \cite[Thm.~1.1]{ET}, and the
corollary from Dirichlet's theorem.
\end{proof}
The same holds for line-free indefinite ternary forms \cite[\S5]{O124}. The
theorem does \emph{not} say that an embedding proves ES only on $U$. A
non-degenerate piece supported on a \emph{thin} set can cover a positive
proportion of primes. An example from \cite[\S4]{O124}: $Q=\sum_{i\le5}v_i^2$,
with $Q(v)=p+1$, the piece $v_1=1$, $4\mid Q(v)$, and the output
$((p+1)/4+v_1-1,\ p(p+1)/2,\ p(p+1)/2)$. What it excludes is that a
representation \emph{theorem}, which produces only sets of the generic
size, does any work outside $U$. The only rank-$3$ quadratic piece
of ES is $Q_d$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:Qd}). It represents everything and is
isotropic, and its side condition is not mild. \status{evidence}: every
Mordell-hard prime $\le10^8$ is solved through $Q_d$ with $d\le8$,
$a\le22$ \cite[\S8]{O124}.

\subsection{The scale constraint}
\begin{definition}[{\cite[Def.~2.2]{O127}}]
An \emph{ES-detector} assigns to each large prime $p$ a finite target
$\cT_p$ and a map $\Phi_p:\cT_p\to\mathrm{Sol}(p)$ whose fibres have size
$\le\mu(p)$. A hypothesis $\mathsf H$ \emph{drives} it if $\mathsf H$ implies
$\#\cT_p\ge M(p)-R(p)$ with explicit $M(p)>R(p)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}, relative to {\cite[Thm.~1.1, Prop.~1.7]{ET}}; {\cite[Prop.~2.3]{O127}}, {\cite[Prop.~2.4, \S5.5]{RT1}}]\label{prop:scale}
If $\mathsf H$ drives a detector with $R(p)\le M(p)/2$, then
$M(p)\le2\mu(p)f(p)$. Here
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $f(p)\le p^{3/5+O(1/\log\log p)}$ for every $p$ \cite[Prop.~1.7]{ET};
\item[(b)] for each $\delta>0$, $f(p)\le p^{\delta}$ outside a set of primes
with counting function $O_\delta(N^{1-\delta}\log^3N)$;
\item[(c)] for any $\xi(p)\to\infty$, $f(p)\le\xi(p)\log^3p\log\log p$ outside a
set of primes of relative density $o(1)$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, if $\mu(p)\le p^{o(1)}$, then $M(p)\le p^{o(1)}$ for almost
all $p$, and no detector with $M(p)\ge p^\eta$ for all large $p$ can be
driven.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$M/2\le\#\cT_p\le\mu(p)f(p)$. Part (a) is \cite[Prop.~1.7]{ET}. Parts (b)
and (c) follow from Markov's inequality applied dyadically to
$\sum_{p\le N}f(p)\ll N\log^2N\log\log N$ \cite[Thm.~1.1]{ET}.
\end{proof}
Every natural parametrisation of solutions by lattice points, Heegner
points or matrices is $O(1)$-to-one, or $p^{o(1)}$-to-one by the divisor
bound. So any equidistribution statement whose main term for the target is
a power of $p$ is excluded as a driver, unless it detects only a coarse
invariant of solutions (fibres $\mu\ge p^{\eta-o(1)}$). In that case the
lifting step from the invariant to an integer solution must be supplied
separately. This applies in particular to small-scale equidistribution of
Heegner or CM points in families with $|D|\ge p^\kappa$ through a
$p^{o(1)}$-to-one encoding \cite[Cor.~4.1]{O127}. With the Type~I bound of
\cite{TH} in place of \cite[Thm.~1.1]{ET}, Markov's inequality gives
$f_I(p)\le\psi\cL^3$ for all but a proportion $O(1/\psi)$ of primes
\cite[Thm.~5.1]{O123}. A Planck-scale certificate must therefore have
\emph{polylogarithmic} accuracy.

\begin{proposition}[local data of moderate modulus; \status{proved}; {\cite[Prop.~7.3]{O127}}, {\cite[Lemma~5.1]{RT1}}]\label{prop:localsize}
Let $S'(n)$ count the solutions of the $a=1$ Type~I slice
($tn+1=mm''$, $m\equiv m''\equiv-1\pmod t$, $4\mid m$) with $\gcd(n,c)=1$.
Let $\mathsf H(n)$ be any predicate depending only on the dyadic range
$n\in(X,2X]$ and on $n\bmod Q(X)$, where $Q(X)\le X^{1/2-\varepsilon}$. If
$\mathsf H$ holds at one large prime $p\in(X,2X]$ that is a square modulo
$Q(X)$, then it holds at an odd square in $(X,2X]$, where $S'=0$. Hence
``$\mathsf H(n)\Rightarrow S'(n)\ge1$'' is false for any such $\mathsf H$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The odd $m\equiv m_0\pmod{2Q}$ with $m_0^2\equiv p\pmod Q$ give $\asymp X^{1/2}/Q$
squares in the class of $p$. For odd $n=m^2$, a slice solution with
$\gcd(n,c)=1$ is a Type~I solution, which cannot exist by
\cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}.
\end{proof}
This does not apply to ``$\mathsf H(n)\Rightarrow\mathrm{ES}(n)$'': odd
squares satisfy ES, through solutions that are of neither type.
