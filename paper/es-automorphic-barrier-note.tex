\begin{abstract}
For a prime $p$ we collect exact reformulations of the Erd\H{o}s--Straus
equation $4/p=1/x+1/y+1/z$ in automorphic and arithmetic-geometric
language. Type~I solutions are $\SL_2(\Z)$ matrices with a prescribed
entry, equivalently Heegner points of discriminant $-4d$ in a thin wedge,
whose discriminant-$(-4dp^2)$ forms are $T_p$-Hecke translates. Type~II
solutions are Heegner points of discriminant $-4dp$ lying on an exact
shell around a cuspidal geodesic. Further reformulations use orders in the
quaternion algebra $B_{p,\infty}$, the algebra $(-d,-p)_\Q$, a CM torus,
and an isotropic ternary form. The Bright--Loughran Brauer class is a
genus character; this is folklore and goes back to Yamamoto. All these
encodings reduce to one statement, which we prove: once two of the
Elsholtz--Tao coordinates are fixed, the solutions are either a finite
union of congruence classes of $p$, or the divisors of one explicit integer
$N_\theta(p)$ in one residue class. The only representative-independent
invariants of the automorphic objects that enter the count of solutions
are congruence or genus data. We then
record negative results, each with an exact scope. Fixed ternary embeddings
reduce to Mordell coverings. Any detector must work at main term
$p^{o(1)}$. Thickening a target is the same as averaging over $n$. The
Type~II lattice problem is everywhere locally soluble, and globally soluble
up to one sign, even at odd squares. Under Schinzel's
Hypothesis~H, every argument that certifies the equation through a fixed
finite set of slices fails infinitely often. Two non-counting routes
also fail, with exact scope: covering certificates from the prime factors
below the modulus fail for a positive proportion of primes, and
power-size fibres (algebraic or bounded-weight orbit recipes) only rescale
the count. We give the quantitative gaps
to known equidistribution theorems, list the escape hatches that remain
open, and report numerical evidence: $f_I(p)\ge1$ and $f_{II}(p)\ge1$ for
all primes $p\le10^8$. The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture is \emph{not}
proved here. The negative statements concern the routes examined and known
technology. They are not impossibility theorems for the conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture (ES) asserts that for every integer
$n\ge2$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:es}
 \frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z,\qquad x,y,z\in\N,
\end{equation}
is soluble. It suffices to treat primes $n=p$, and by Mordell's
identities only primes in six classes modulo $840$ (the squares) remain
open. ES has been verified to very large bounds and holds for almost all
$n$ in a strong quantitative sense, but it is open for an individual large
prime.

\subsection*{Why automorphic and geometric input}
In earlier internal work of this campaign
(\cite{PO}, and Theorems~M and~C of \cite{PS}) we proved that
\emph{congruence-covering} arguments cannot settle ES pointwise. More
precisely, under Schinzel's Hypothesis~H, every correct bounded
witness-producing procedure built from ring operations, floor division,
sign tests and factorisations of the integers it meets fails for
infinitely many primes $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ \cite[Thm~C]{PS}. This is the
procedure-level form of the classical odd-square obstructions (Yamamoto,
Mordell--Schinzel, Elsholtz--Tao \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}, Bright--Loughran
\cite[Cor.~1.4]{BL}). It leaves room for input that is \emph{not}
congruence data: spectral, automorphic or arithmetic-geometric
information about a fixed prime $p$. This note collects what such input
can see.

\subsection*{What this note contains}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Exact dictionaries} (\S\ref{sec:dict}, \status{proved}). Type~I
and Type~II solutions are rewritten as Heegner points, $\SL_2$ matrices,
Hecke translates, shells around cuspidal geodesics, orders in definite
quaternion algebras, divisors of an element of a CM field, and points on
an isotropic ternary quadric. Several of these are folklore. We mark
attributions explicitly; in particular, the identification of the
Bright--Loughran Brauer class with a genus character is \emph{not} new
(Yamamoto 1965, via \cite[App.~A]{BL}).
\item \emph{The divisor-class reduction} (\S\ref{sec:reduction},
\status{proved}). Each encoding is a ``slice'' of the Elsholtz--Tao
variety. Fixing two coordinates leaves either a congruence condition on
$p$ or the divisors of one integer $N_\theta(p)$ in one residue class
(Lemma~\ref{lem:slices}, Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}).
\item \emph{Negative results with exact scope} (\S\ref{sec:negative}):
ternary embeddings, the scale constraint, thickening, local solubility at
odd squares, geometry, and Hypothesis-H-conditional emptiness of single
targets.
\item \emph{Two non-counting escapes that fail} (\S\ref{sec:longshots}):
deterministic covering in the $e$-slices (Theorem~\ref{thm:transparent})
and fibred encodings (Theorem~\ref{thm:fibred}).
\item \emph{Quantitative gaps} to known equidistribution theorems
(\S\ref{sec:gaps}, \status{Assessment}), \emph{open problems}
(\S\ref{sec:open}), and \emph{numerical evidence} (\S\ref{sec:numerics}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{What this note does not contain}
ES is not proved here, conditionally or unconditionally. We know of no
standard conjecture (GRH, GLH, Ramanujan, Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture,
twisted Linnik--Selberg) that is known to imply ES. The literature survey
behind this note \cite{LA} found no prior pointwise result for ES,
conditional or not. Every negative statement below concerns the specific
encodings and mechanisms analysed, or ``known technology''. None is a
theorem that ES cannot be proved by automorphic methods. Words like
``barrier'' are used in that mechanism-scoped sense; where they summarise
more than a proved statement, they are labelled \status{Assessment}.

\section{Exact dictionaries}\label{sec:dict}

\subsection{The Elsholtz--Tao normal form}
For an odd prime $p$, $p$ divides exactly one or exactly two of $x,y,z$ in
any solution of \eqref{eq:es} (it cannot divide all three, since
$1/x'+1/y'+1/z'=4$ has no positive solution). Following \cite[\S2]{ET}
and \cite[\S2.0]{RT2}, put
\begin{align*}
V_I(p)&=\{(a,b,c,d,e)\in\N^5:\ 4abd=pe+1,\ a+b=ce\},\\
V_{II}(p)&=\{(a,b,c,d,e)\in\N^5:\ 4abd=p+e,\ a+b=ce\}.
\end{align*}
An element of $V_I(p)$ gives the solution $(abdp,acd,bcd)$, and an element
of $V_{II}(p)$ gives $(abd,acdp,bcdp)$: indeed
$(c+p(a+b))/(abcdp)=c(1+pe)/(abcdp)=4/p$ and
$(cp+a+b)/(abcdp)=c(p+e)/(abcdp)=4/p$. Conversely every solution arises
in this way \cite[Props.~2.2, 2.6]{ET}. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VIVII}
\mathrm{ES}(p)\iff V_I(p)\cup V_{II}(p)\neq\emptyset.
\end{equation}
On $V_I(p)$ one has $p\nmid cd$ \cite[Prop.~2.1]{O130}, \cite[\S5]{O131}.
The counts $f_I(p)$, $f_{II}(p)$ count primitive tuples ($\gcd(a,b,c)=1$)
\cite[(2.10)]{ET}. A non-primitive tuple $(ga',gb',gc',d)$ is the primitive
tuple $(a',b',c',g^2d)$, so existence questions are insensitive to
primitivity.

\subsection{Type~I: $\SL_2$ matrices, Heegner wedges and the Hecke operator $T_p$}
The following is \cite[Lemmas~2.1--2.2, 2.4]{O123}; parts (i)--(ii) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI} are rearrangements of \cite[\S2]{ET}.

\begin{lemma}[\status{proved}]\label{lem:typeI}
Let $a,c,d,f\ge1$ with $p\nmid cd$. The following are equivalent:
\textup{(i)} $p=4acd-f$ and $f\mid 4a^2d+1$;
\textup{(ii)} $f\mid ap+c$ and $p+f=4acd$;
\textup{(iii)} $f\mid p^2+4c^2d$ and $p+f=4acd$.
In particular, (ii) holds for some $d$ iff $f\mid ap+c$ and $4ac\mid p+f$, and
(iii) holds for some $a$ iff $f\mid p^2+4c^2d$ and $f\equiv-p\pmod{4cd}$.
In that case $e=(4a^2d+1)/f$, $b=(ap+c)/f$ and
$g=(p^2+4c^2d)/f=4bcd-p$ are integers, $4abd-pe=1$, $a+b=ce$, and
\[\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}4ad&p\\ e&b\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_2(\Z).\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): $c(4a^2d+1)=a\cdot4acd+c=a(p+f)+c$, so
$f\mid ap+c$. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i): with $4acd=p+f$ the same identity gives
$f\mid c(4a^2d+1)$, and $\gcd(f,c)\mid\gcd(4acd-p,c)\mid p$; if $p\mid f$
and $p\mid c$ then $p\mid cd$, which is excluded. (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii):
modulo $f$ we have $4acd\equiv p$, hence
$4c^2d(4a^2d+1)\equiv p^2+4c^2d$, and $\gcd(f,4c^2d)\mid p$, which is $1$
unless $p\mid cd$. For the identities, put $e=(4a^2d+1)/f$ and $b=ce-a$.
Then $bf=c(4a^2d+1)-a(4acd-p)=ap+c$ (so $b\ge1$),
$4abd=e\cdot4acd-4a^2d=e(p+f)-4a^2d=pe+1$, and
$(4acd-p)(4bcd-p)=4c^2d(4abd-pe)+p^2=p^2+4c^2d$, using $a+b=ce$; see
\cite[(2.8)--(2.9)]{ET}.
\end{proof}

Write $Q_-=[f,-4ad,de]$ (discriminant $-4d$) and $Q'=[f,4cd,dg]$
(discriminant $-4dp^2$).

\begin{proposition}[Hecke form; \status{proved}]\label{prop:hecke}
For every Type~I tuple,
$Q'(x,y)=Q_-(x+2bd\,y,\ p\,y)$, i.e.
$Q'=Q_-\circ\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2bd\\0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Thus the discriminant-$(-4dp^2)$ form of a Type~I solution is a
$T_p$-Hecke translate of a level-$d$ Heegner form of discriminant $-4d$,
and the roots satisfy $z_{Q'}=p\,z_{Q_-}-2bd$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The middle coefficient is $2f\cdot2bd-4adp=4d(bf-ap)=4cd$. The last is
$4b^2d^2f-8abd^2p+dep^2=d\bigl(4bd(ap+c)-8abdp+ep^2\bigr)
=d\bigl(4bcd-p(4abd-pe)\bigr)=d(4bcd-p)=dg$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the wedge; \status{proved}, {\cite[Prop.~3.1]{O123}}]\label{prop:wedge}
Let $\Lambda_d=\{z=(2ad+i\sqrt d)/f:\ a,f\ge1,\ f\mid4a^2d+1\}$, the roots
of the forms $[f,-4ad,de]$. For $z=x+iy\in\Lambda_d$ and $c\ge1$,
\[\frac{2c\sqrt d\,x-\sqrt d-p\,y}{y}=4acd-f-p\in\Z.\]
Hence $(a,c,d,f)$ is a Type~I tuple of $p$ if and only if $z$ lies in the
wedge $R_c=\{x+iy:\ |2c\sqrt d\,x-\sqrt d-py|<y\}$ at the cusp $1/(2c)$.
The edges of $R_c$ are hypercycles of the geodesic $\{\operatorname{Re}z=1/(2c)\}$,
and $R_c$ is a tube of hyperbolic width
$\operatorname{arsinh}\frac{p+1}{2c\sqrt d}-\operatorname{arsinh}\frac{p-1}{2c\sqrt d}
\approx 2/\sqrt{p^2+4c^2d}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $z\in\Lambda_d$ we have $x/y=2a\sqrt d$ and $\sqrt d/y=f$; substitute.
An integer of absolute value $<1$ is $0$. The width is the difference of the
distances of the two edges from the geodesic.
\end{proof}

The target is therefore at the \emph{Planck scale}: its width is
$\le2/p$, and $\asymp1/p$ when $c\sqrt d\ll p$. The same holds in the $T_p$-translated picture,
where the wedge sits at $-1/(2a)$ \cite[Prop.~3.2]{O123}.

\begin{lemma}[genus condition; \status{proved}; classical]\label{lem:genusI}
If $(a,c,d,f)$ is a Type~I tuple of an odd $n$ with $\gcd(n,2cd)=1$, then
$\chi_{-4d}(-n)=1$. For $n=p$ prime this says $\leg{-d}{p}=-1$: $p$ is
inert in $\Q(\sqrt{-d})$. For an odd square $n=m^2$ the condition fails for
every $d$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every prime $q\mid f$ divides $n^2+4c^2d$ and is prime to $2cd$, so
$-4c^2d\equiv n^2$ is a non-zero square mod $q$, and $\chi_{-4d}(f)=1$. As
$f\equiv-n\pmod{4cd}$ and $\chi_{-4d}$ has period dividing $4d$,
$\chi_{-4d}(-n)=\chi_{-4d}(f)=1$. For $n=m^2$,
$\chi_{-4d}(-m^2)=\chi_{-4d}(-1)=-1$. For $n=p$,
$\chi_{-4d}(-p)=-\chi_{-4d}(p)=-\leg{-d}{p}$.
\end{proof}
This is the Type~I half of \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}, read at a fixed level.
Any fixed-$p$ main term at level $d$ must therefore carry the factor
$(1+\chi_{-4d}(-p))/2$.

\subsection{Type~II: determinant one at level $dp$, and shells}
The following is \cite[Lemma~1.1, Props.~2.1, 2.5, 7.1, Lemma~6.1]{O128}.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}]\label{prop:typeII}
Let $c,d\ge1$, $M=4cd$ and $N_{c,d}=4c^2dp+1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Type~II tuples of $p$ with these $(c,d)$ correspond to
factorisations $ff'-4c^2(dp)=1$ with $f\equiv f'\equiv-1\pmod{M}$, via
$f=4acd-1$, $f'=4bcd-1$. Equivalently, $f$ runs over the divisors of
$N_{c,d}$ with $f\equiv-1\pmod M$ (the cofactor is then automatically
$\equiv-1$).
\item[(b)] The map $(c,f)\mapsto Q=[f,4cdp,dpf']$ sends these solutions
injectively to Heegner forms of discriminant $-4dp$ at level $dp$. For
fixed $(p,d)$ the corresponding points of $\HH$ are pairwise at hyperbolic
distance with $\cosh\ge3/2$.
\item[(c)] In the determinant-$p$ coordinates $Q=[f,4ad,de]$ (level one,
discriminant $-4dp$), with $S_c=[0,2c,1]$ and the bilinear form
$b(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)$ of $q([A,B,C])=B^2-4AC$, one has
$q(Q)=-4dp$, $q(S_c)=4c^2$ and $b(Q,S_c)=4$. Thus the Type~II solutions
are embeddings of the binary lattice $4\cdot[-dp,1,c^2]$ (discriminant
$4c^2dp+1$ before scaling) into the trace-zero lattice of signature $(2,1)$.
Geometrically, a Type~II point is a Heegner point of discriminant $-4dp$ at
hyperbolic distance exactly $\operatorname{arsinh}\bigl(1/(2c\sqrt{dp})\bigr)$
from the cuspidal geodesic $(\infty,-1/(2c))$: it lies on a \emph{shell}.
\item[(d)] (genus lemma) $(p/f)=(p/f')=-1$ (Jacobi symbols).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof of (a), (c), (d)]
(a) $(4acd-1)(4bcd-1)=4c^2d(4abd-e)+1=4c^2dp+1$ using $a+b=ce$ and
$4abd=p+e$. Conversely, given $f\mid N_{c,d}$ with $f\equiv-1\pmod M$, the
cofactor $f'$ satisfies $ff'\equiv1$, hence $f'\equiv-1\pmod M$; put
$a=(f+1)/M$, $b=(f'+1)/M$; expanding $ff'=N_{c,d}$ gives
$4abcd=a+b+cp$, so $c\mid a+b$ and $e=(a+b)/c=4abd-p$.
(c) With $e=(p+4a^2d)/f$ (an integer, \cite[(2.19)]{ET}),
$q(Q)=16a^2d^2-4def=-4dp$ and
$b(Q,S_c)=2\cdot4ad\cdot2c-4f=4(4acd-f)=4$. The distance formula is
\cite[Prop.~2.5]{O128}.
(d) $ef=p+4a^2d$ gives $(2ad)^2\equiv-dp\pmod f$ with $\gcd(dp,f)=1$
($f=4acd-1$ is prime to $d$, and a prime $\ell\mid(p,f)$ would divide
$2ad$, whence $f\equiv-1\pmod\ell$), so
$(-dp/f)=1$. Since $f\equiv-1\pmod{4d}$ and $\chi_{-4d}(-1)=-1$,
$(-d/f)=\chi_{-4d}(f)=-1$, hence $(p/f)=-1$. The same argument applies to
$f'$.
\end{proof}
Part (b) is \cite[Prop.~2.1, Lemma~2.2]{O128}; the separation is the
lemma $\cosh\operatorname{dist}-1=\operatorname{disc}(Q-Q')/(8dp)$ of
\cite[Lemma~2.2]{TH} applied at level $dp$. Part (d) recovers the Type~II
half of \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}: at an odd square $n=s^2$ one has
$(s^2/f)=1$. As for Type~I, it is a level-wise reading of a classical
fact; see Proposition~\ref{prop:brauer}.

\subsection{The Brauer class is a genus character (folklore)}
Bright and Loughran \cite{BL} study the surface
$U_n:4u_1u_2u_3=n(u_1u_2+u_1u_3+u_2u_3)$ and its integral model. Over $\Q$
the group $\Br\tilde U_n/\Br\Q$ is $\Z/2$, generated by the transcendental
quaternion algebra $\alpha=(-u_1/u_3,-u_2/u_3)$ \cite[Cor.~2.7]{BL}. They
show that there is no Brauer--Manin obstruction to positive integral points
for $n\ge2$ \cite[Thms.~1.1, 1.8]{BL}, that $\alpha$ obstructs strong
approximation \cite[Thm.~1.8 (1.5), Thms.~1.2, 1.5]{BL} (though not every
failure of strong approximation comes from $\alpha$ \cite[Thm.~1.9]{BL}), and that it recovers the
odd-square obstruction \cite[Thm.~1.2, Cor.~1.4]{BL}.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; folklore, Yamamoto \cite{Ya} via {\cite[App.~A]{BL}}]\label{prop:brauer}
\textup{(a)} On the Type~I locus at level $d$, the condition imposed by
$\alpha$ is $\leg{-ab}{p}=\leg{-d}{p}=-1$, i.e. Lemma~\ref{lem:genusI}.
For $\leg{-d}{p}=+1$ the positive adelic Brauer set of the level-$d$
chart is empty.
\textup{(b)} On the Type~II locus, $\alpha$ imposes
$(f/p)=-(-1/p)$, which is equivalent to Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(d)
and holds automatically. So the Brauer--Manin obstruction never removes a
Type~II level.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
(a) $4abd=pe+1$ gives $(abd/p)=1$, so $(-ab/p)=(-d/p)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:genusI}
gives $(-d/p)=-1$. For the local statement, $\operatorname{inv}_p\alpha=(-ab/p)$
on $p$-adic points of the chart, $\operatorname{inv}_\infty\alpha=-1$ on the
positive part, and $\operatorname{inv}_\ell\alpha=+1$ for $\ell\ne p$
\cite[Lemmas~3.1, 3.5, 3.8]{BL}.
(b) $e\equiv4abd\pmod p$ gives $(-ab/p)=(-de/p)$, and $ef\equiv4a^2d\pmod p$
gives $(e/p)=(d/p)(f/p)$. Since $f\equiv3\pmod4$, reciprocity gives
$(f/p)=(p/f)(-1/p)$. Details: \cite[Props.~5.1, 5.2]{O126}.
\end{proof}
Nothing here is new. Part (a) is the classical reason why the
Mordell/Elsholtz--Tao families $n\equiv-f\pmod{4ad}$, $f\mid4a^2d+1$, cover
only non-residue classes. Part (b) is Yamamoto's lemma $(p/q)=-1$ for
$q=4abd-1$ in Mordell's notation, as derived from $\alpha$ in
\cite[App.~A]{BL}. (Yamamoto's paper was not accessed directly.)

\subsection{Quaternion orders}
For $d,m\ge1$ let $\Lambda_{d,m}=\Z\langle u,v\rangle$ with $u^2=-d$,
$v^2=-m$, $uv+vu=1$. It is an order $\Z+\Z u+\Z v+\Z uv$ in the definite
algebra $B_{d,m}\cong(-d,-d(4dm-1))_\Q$ (replace $v$ by $2dv+u$, which
anticommutes with $u$), of reduced discriminant $4dm-1$: the Gram matrix of
the reduced trace on $(1,u,v,uv)$ has determinant $-(4dm-1)^2$
\cite[Lemma~1.2]{O130}.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Prop.~1.3]{O130}}]\label{prop:quat}
Let $(a,b,c,d,e)\in V_I(p)$ and $m=ab$. Then $B_{d,m}\cong B_{p,\infty}$, the
quaternion algebra ramified exactly at $p$ and $\infty$, and $\Lambda_{d,m}$
is an order of reduced discriminant $4abd-1=pe$ in it. Consequently there is
a supersingular elliptic curve over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ with
endomorphisms $u,v$ satisfying $u^2=-d$, $v^2=-ab$, $uv+vu=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Compute the Hilbert symbols of $(-d,-dpe)$. At $\ell\nmid2dpe$ they are
trivial. At $p$: $p\nmid e$ (if $p\mid e$ then $-4a^2d\equiv1\pmod p$,
contradicting $(-d/p)=-1$), so the symbol is $(-d/p)=-1$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:genusI}. At odd $\ell\mid e$: $-d\equiv(2a)^{-2}$ is a square
mod $\ell$ ($e\mid4a^2d+1$), so the symbol is $1$. At odd $\ell\mid d$:
$pe\equiv-1\pmod\ell$ gives $1$. At $\infty$ it is $-1$, and the product
formula gives $2$. The last sentence is Deuring's correspondence.
\end{proof}
This was checked on all Type~I tuples with $p<3000$ (\status{evidence},
\cite{O130}). In the language of Gross--Zagier \cite{GZ}, the identity
$(16abd-4)/4=pe$ has the shape $(D_1D_2-x^2)/4$ with $D_1=-4d$, $D_2=-4ab$,
$x=2$, so the CM points of these discriminants have a common supersingular
reduction. This is a \emph{formal analogy only}: the theorem of \cite{GZ}
assumes coprime fundamental discriminants, and $-4d,-4ab$ are never
coprime. No Gross--Zagier formula is used.

\begin{lemma}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Lemma~3.1]{O130}}]\label{lem:dp}
In $B=(-d,-p)_\Q$ with $i^2=-d$, $j^2=-p$, $k=ij=-ji$, Type~II at level $d$
holds if and only if, for some $a,b,c,e\ge1$,
\[(2a\,i+j)(2b\,i-j)=-e(1+2c\,k).\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The left side is $-4abd-2(a+b)k+p=-(4abd-p)-2(a+b)k$; compare coefficients
with $4abd-p=e$, $a+b=ce$.
\end{proof}
Taking reduced norms gives $\Nm(2ai+j)=4a^2d+p=ef$ and
$\Nm(1+2ck)=1+4c^2dp=ff'$, the integers of the ternary form below and of
the shells of Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}. The novelty of the quaternion
dictionary is \emph{unverified}: the survey \cite{LA} found no prior
occurrence, but we do not claim it is new.

\subsection{The CM torus}
Let $p\nmid2cd$, $K=\Q(\sqrt{-d})$, $\alpha=p+2c\sqrt{-d}$, so
$\Nm\alpha=p^2+4c^2d$.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Lemma~5.1, Thm.~5.2]{O131}}]\label{prop:cm}
\textup{(i)} $\mathfrak a\mapsto\Nm\mathfrak a$ is a bijection from the
ideal divisors of $(\alpha)$ in $\Z[\sqrt{-d}]$ to the positive divisors of
$\Nm\alpha$.
\textup{(ii)} The number $T_{c,d}(p)$ of ordered Type~I tuples with these
$(c,d)$ equals $\#\{\mathfrak a\mid(\alpha):\Nm\mathfrak a\equiv-p\pmod{4cd}\}$, and
\[T_{c,d}(p)=\frac1{\varphi(4cd)}\sum_{\chi\bmod4cd}\bar\chi(-p)\,\sigma_\chi(p^2+4c^2d),
\qquad\sigma_\chi(N)=\sum_{f\mid N}\chi(f).\]
\textup{(iii)} Only Hecke characters of the form $\chi\circ\Nm$ occur. In
particular every class-group character of $K$ that is not a genus
character has coefficient $0$, and pairing $\chi\leftrightarrow\chi\chi_{-4d}$
produces the factor $1+\chi_{-4d}(-p)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:genusI}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
$\alpha$ is primitive since $\gcd(p,2c)=1$. Every prime $\ell\mid\Nm\alpha$
is odd and prime to $d$, so it is unramified and not inert, and it splits.
Exactly one of the two primes above $\ell$ divides $\alpha$, to the full
power. This gives (i). Then (ii) is Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}(iii) plus
orthogonality on $(\Z/4cd)^\times$, and (iii) is genus theory.
\end{proof}
Thus the dihedral (Frobenius) information carried by the class of
$(f,\alpha)$ in the ring class field is invisible in the count.

\subsection{The isotropic ternary form $Q_d$}
Let $Q_d(e,f,a)=ef-4da^2$.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Prop.~6.2]{O124}}, {\cite[\S6]{RT1}}]\label{prop:Qd}
\textup{(i)} Type~II at level $d$ with parameter $a$ holds if and only if
$Q_d(e,f,a)=p$ with $4ad\mid f+1$ and $(f+1)e/(4ad)>a$, i.e.
$p+4a^2d$ has a divisor $f\equiv-1\pmod{4ad}$. (The cofactor condition is
automatic: with $c=(f+1)/4ad$ one has $ce-a=(fp+p+4a^2d)/(4adf)>0$.)
\textup{(ii)} $Q_d$ represents every integer primitively over $\Z$
($(e,f,a)=(n,1,0)$), so local conditions, genus and spinor genus impose
nothing; all the arithmetic sits in the side condition $4ad\mid f+1$.
\textup{(iii)} The side condition is not a finite union of lattice cosets:
its slice at $a=a_0$ has $f$-period $4a_0d$, which is unbounded.
\textup{(iv)} On such points the cofactor $f=4acd-1$ is a Type~II
\emph{multiplier witness modulus}; in particular $f\ge W(p)$, the least
such modulus.
\end{proposition}
Among all the parametrisations of \cite[\S2]{ET}, $Q_d$ is the only
rank-$3$ quadratic piece (\status{certified} by a systematic search,
\cite[Comp.~6.1]{O124}, reproduced independently in its review).

\section{The divisor-class reduction}\label{sec:reduction}
This section is \cite[\S2]{RT2}. A \emph{slice} is a choice
$\theta$ of two of the coordinates $\{a,b,c,d,e\}$ together with prescribed
values for them; $V_\theta(p)$ denotes the elements of $V_I(p)$ (or
$V_{II}(p)$) with these values. By the symmetry $a\leftrightarrow b$ the
coordinate pairs are $\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{a,d\},\{a,e\},\{c,d\},\{c,e\},\{d,e\}$.

\begin{lemma}[slice table; \status{proved}, elementary]\label{lem:slices}
For each type and each slice $\theta$ exactly one of the following holds.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(C)] \emph{Congruence slice:} $V_\theta(p)\neq\emptyset$ if and only if
$p$ lies in an explicit finite union of residue classes modulo a modulus
depending only on $\theta$.
\item[(D)] \emph{Divisor slice:} there are an integer $N_\theta(p)$ (a
polynomial in $p$ of degree $\le2$, possibly divided by a constant depending
on $\theta$), a modulus $q_\theta$ and a residue set $R_\theta(p)\subset\Z/q_\theta$
such that $V_\theta(p)$ is in bijection with
$\{f\ge1:\ f\mid N_\theta(p),\ f\bmod q_\theta\in R_\theta(p)\}$. The set
$R_\theta(p)$ depends only on $\theta$, on $p\bmod q_\theta$ and, in the
$\{d,e\}$ rows, on $N_\theta(p)\bmod q_\theta$.
\end{itemize}
The rows are as follows (Type~I rows containing $c$ or $d$ are for
$p\nmid cd$; otherwise the fibre is empty).
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llllll}
\toprule
type & $\theta$ & kind & $N_\theta(p)$ & divisor $f$ & class condition\\
\midrule
I & $\{c,d\}$ & D & $p^2+4c^2d$ & $4acd-p$ & $f\equiv-p\ (4cd)$\\
I & $\{a,c\}$ & D & $ap+c$ & $4acd-p$ & $f\equiv-p\ (4ac)$\\
I & $\{a,e\}$ & D & $(pe+1)/4a$ & $b$ & $f\equiv-a\ (e)$\\
I & $\{d,e\}$ & D & $m=(pe+1)/4d$ & $a$ & $f^2\equiv-m\ (e)$, $(f,e)=1$\\
I & $\{a,d\}$ & C & $4a^2d+1$ & $4acd-p$ & $p\equiv-f\ (4ad)$\\
I & $\{a,b\},\{c,e\}$ & C & --- & --- & $p$ mod $4ab$, resp.\ $4a(ce-a)$\\
II & $\{c,d\}$ & D & $4c^2dp+1$ & $4acd-1$ & $f\equiv-1\ (4cd)$\\
II & $\{a,c\}$ & D & $cp+a$ & $4acd-1$ & $f\equiv-1\ (4ac)$\\
II & $\{a,d\}$ & D & $p+4a^2d$ & $4acd-1$ & $f\equiv-1\ (4ad)$\\
II & $\{a,e\}$ & D & $(p+e)/4a$ & $b$ & $f\equiv-a\ (e)$\\
II & $\{d,e\}$ & D & $m=(p+e)/4d$ & $a$ & $f^2\equiv-m\ (e)$, $(f,e)=1$\\
II & $\{a,b\},\{c,e\}$ & C & --- & --- & $p$ mod $4ab$, resp.\ $4a(ce-a)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In the $\{a,e\}$ and $\{d,e\}$ rows, $N_\theta(p)$ is required to be an
integer (otherwise the fibre is empty).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each D row is an identity plus a converse.
\emph{I$\{c,d\}$} is Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}(iii). For the converse, the
cofactor $f'=N/f$ satisfies $f'\equiv p^2(-p)^{-1}\equiv-p\pmod{4cd}$; put
$a=(f+p)/4cd$, $b=(f'+p)/4cd$; expanding $ff'=N$ gives
$p(a+b)=c(4abd-1)$, so $c\mid a+b$ and $e=(a+b)/c=(4abd-1)/p$.
\emph{II$\{c,d\}$} is Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(a).
\emph{I$\{a,c\}$} is Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}(ii).
\emph{II$\{a,c\}$, II$\{a,d\}$}: with $f=4acd-1$ one has $ef=4a^2d+p$, and
$c(4a^2d+p)=a(f+1)+cp$, so $f\mid cp+a$ (as $\gcd(f,c)=1$). Conversely put
$d=(f+1)/4ac$, resp.\ $c=(f+1)/4ad$, then $e=(p+4a^2d)/f$ and $b=ce-a$.
\emph{$\{a,e\}$, $\{d,e\}$}: with $M=pe+1$ (Type~I) or $M=p+e$ (Type~II)
the defining equations read $bd=M/4a$, resp.\ $ab=M/4d$, together with
$e\mid a+b$. For $\gcd(a,e)=1$, $e\mid a+m/a$ is equivalent to
$e\mid a^2+m$, and every element has $\gcd(a,e)=1$: for Type~I since
$a\mid pe+1$; for Type~II since $\gcd(a,e)\mid p$ and $p\nmid e$ (if
$p\mid e$ then $4abd=p+e\le2e\le2(a+b)$ forces $a=b=d=1$ and then
$4=p+e\ge2p$, impossible).
\emph{C rows}: for I$\{a,d\}$ the fibre is $\{f\mid4a^2d+1: f\equiv-p\ (4ad)\}$,
a condition on $p\bmod 4ad$; for $\{a,b\}$, $e$ runs over the divisors of
$a+b$ and the condition is $4ab\mid pe+1$ (resp.\ $p+e$); for $\{c,e\}$,
$a$ runs over $[1,ce-1]$ with condition $4a(ce-a)\mid pe+1$ (resp.\ $p+e$).
\end{proof}
All bijections were verified mechanically against a brute-force
enumeration of $V_I(p)\cup V_{II}(p)$ for every prime $5\le p<200$
(\status{certified}, \cite[Lemma~2.1]{RT2}; script
\texttt{scripts/rt2\_slices.py}).

\begin{theorem}[divisor-class reduction]\label{thm:reduction}
Consider the encodings of \S\ref{sec:dict}:
E1 the Type~I $\SL_2$/Heegner wedge (Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge},
$\theta=\{c,d\}$; its $T_p$-translated form, per tube, $\theta=\{a,d\}$);
E2 the Type~II shells (Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}, $\theta=\{c,d\}$);
E3 the quaternion order of Proposition~\ref{prop:quat} ($\theta=\{d,e\}$);
E4 the $(-d,-p)$ factorisation of Lemma~\ref{lem:dp} ($\theta=\{d,e\}$;
the norms of $2ai+j$ give $\theta=\{a,d\}$);
E5 the CM torus (Proposition~\ref{prop:cm}, $\theta=\{c,d\}$);
E6 the ternary form $Q_d$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:Qd}, $\theta=\{a,d\}$).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \status{(proved)} The E-solutions with parameter $\theta$ are in
bijection with $V_\theta(p)$. Hence they correspond to the divisors of the
single integer $N_\theta(p)$ lying in $R_\theta(p)$, or (C rows) to a
congruence condition on $p$.
\item[(ii)] \status{(proved, relative to the cited internal statements)}
The only representative-independent invariants of these objects that
enter the count of ES points are congruence or genus data, in the
following precise senses: for E5 only genus characters have non-zero
coefficient (Proposition~\ref{prop:cm}). For E3 the collision datum is a
function of $\theta$, of $p\bmod4d$, of $(-d/p)$ and of $(-d/\ell)$ for
$\ell\mid e$, and it holds automatically on every admissible $\theta$
\cite[Prop.~2.2]{O130}. For E1, E2 and E6 the property of being an ES point
belongs to the representative, not to the CM point or $\Gamma$-orbit: each
class has infinitely many representatives of the required shape and only
finitely many good ones (\cite[Prop.~4.1]{O126}; \cite[Prop.~8.2]{O128}; the
stabiliser of $S_c$ in $\SL_2(\Z)$ is $\pm I$). For E4 the ES elements lie
on the line $\{2xi\pm j\}$, on which the reduced norm is the one-variable
polynomial $4dx^2+p$ \cite[Prop.~3.2]{O130}.
\item[(iii)] \status{(conditional on Hypothesis~H)} Let $\Theta_0$ be a finite
set of slice parameters, of any types and rows, fixed independently of
$p$. Any argument that certifies $\mathrm{ES}(p)$ by exhibiting an
E-solution with parameter in $\Theta_0$ fails for infinitely many primes
$p\equiv1\pmod{24}$. This holds whatever auxiliary automorphic, spectral
or geometric information the argument uses.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) Each source file proves the exact lift from its object to $V_\theta(p)$
(Propositions~\ref{prop:wedge}, \ref{prop:typeII}, \ref{prop:quat},
\ref{prop:cm}, \ref{prop:Qd} and Lemma~\ref{lem:dp}); then apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:slices}. (ii) is a collection of the cited statements.
(iii) The argument can only succeed at primes with
$\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta_0}V_\theta(p)\ne\emptyset$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:slices}
this predicate is decided, together with a witness, by the bounded and
correct procedure ``for $\theta\in\Theta_0$: test $p\bmod q_\theta$; factor
$N_\theta(p)$ (with floor division for the $\{\cdot,e\}$ rows); test each
divisor against $R_\theta(p)$''. Listing all divisors of an integer and
looping over a list are primitive instructions of a bounded program in the
sense of \cite[\S1.2]{PS}, so this procedure is bounded although the number
of divisors grows with $p$. By \cite[Thm.~C]{PS}, under Hypothesis~H
every such procedure fails for infinitely many $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[scope]\label{rem:scope}
Part (iii) says that whatever an automorphic input ``knows'', if it
certifies ES through a fixed finite set of fibres it is no stronger than
factoring finitely many fixed polynomials in $p$, and under H that fails.
It does \emph{not} say that automorphic methods cannot aggregate over a
parameter set $\Theta(p)$ that grows with $p$; Kuznetsov's formula and
Heegner equidistribution aggregate over levels and discriminants in
exactly this way. Aggregation over $\theta$ is legitimate, since $\theta$
is a coordinate of the solution and not fixed by the problem. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:slices} it amounts to controlling, at one prime $p$, the
divisor events of a growing family $\{N_\theta(p)\}_{\theta\in\Theta(p)}$
jointly. The quantitative obstacles to doing so are in
\S\S\ref{sec:negative}--\ref{sec:gaps}, and the open routes in
\S\ref{sec:open}. Unconditional quantitative versions of (iii) exist for
specific growing boxes. For the Type~II box $\{acd\le T/4\}$ one uses
$f=4acd-1\ge W(p)$ together with the $\Omega$-result
$W(p)>\exp\bigl(c(\log p)^{1/4}(\log\log p)^{-1/4}\bigr)$ for infinitely
many $p$ \cite{SE}. For Type~I
heights there is $ck_{\min}(p)\gg\log p\cdot\log\log\log p$ infinitely
often (internal file \texttt{POINTWISE\_OMEGA.md}, Thm.~8.5, modulo
Lau--Wu). Both are cited as summarised in \cite[\S2.2]{RT2}.
\end{remark}

\section{Negative results with exact scope}\label{sec:negative}
Each result in this section concerns a precisely defined mechanism. None of
them says that ES is false or unprovable.

\subsection{Ternary and higher-rank embeddings}
Fix $k\ge3$, a non-degenerate integral quadratic form $Q$ in $k$ variables,
and integers $\kappa\ge1$, $\eta$, all independent of $p$.

\begin{definition}[{\cite[Def.~1.1]{O124}}]
An \emph{embedding of complexity $(J,D)$} is a family of pieces
$(S_j,\Phi_j)_{j\le J}$, where $S_j\subseteq\Z^k$ is an \emph{arbitrary}
subset and $\Phi_j=(X_j,Y_j,Z_j)$ is a triple of rational functions of
degree $\le D$. It is \emph{valid} if for every prime $p$, every $j$ and
every $v\in S_j$ with $Q(v)=\kappa p+\eta$, $\Phi_j(v)\in\N^3$ solves
\eqref{eq:es} for $n=p$. Put $R(p)=\bigcup_j\{v\in S_j: Q(v)=\kappa p+\eta\}$.
A piece is \emph{degenerate} if $X_j,Y_j,Z_j\in\Q(Q)$.
\end{definition}
This covers every statement of the form ``$\kappa p+\eta$ is represented by
$Q$ with side conditions, hence ES$(p)$ by an explicit piecewise rational
recipe'' of fixed complexity. It does not cover recipes of unbounded
algebraic complexity, or families with a parameter that grows with $p$.

\begin{theorem}[\status{proved}, relative to Pila \cite{Pi}, Mordell--Schinzel \cite{Mo,Sch} and {\cite[Thm.~1.1]{ET}}; {\cite[Thm.~4.1, Cor.~4.2]{O124}}]\label{thm:ternary}
Let $Q$ be positive definite. There is a finite union $U$ of primitive
classes modulo some $q^*$, containing no square class, such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] for every large prime $p\notin U$,
$\#R(p)\ll f(p)\,p^{(k-2)/2-1/4+\varepsilon}$, where $f(p)$ is the total number
of solutions;
\item[(b)] for $0<\varepsilon_0<1/4$ the primes $p\le N$, $p\notin U$, with
$\#R(p)\ge c\,p^{(k-2)/2-\varepsilon_0}$ number $\ll N^{3/4+\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon}$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, suppose a representation theorem (circle method for $k\ge5$,
Kloosterman/$\delta$-method for $k=4$, Duke \cite{Du}, Duke--Schulze-Pillot
\cite{DSP} and Blomer-type results for $k=3$) gives
$\#R(p)\ge c\,p^{(k-2)/2-\varepsilon_0}$ on a union $\mathcal P_R$ of primitive
classes, outside $o(\pi(N))$ exceptions. Then $\mathcal P_R\subseteq U$:
the representation input proves ES only for primes that a finite
polynomial (Mordell-type) covering already handles.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
A degenerate piece produces an ES solution depending only on $p$. By a
rationality and integrality argument it is a polynomial identity on
finitely many classes, which by Mordell--Schinzel are not square classes;
this defines $U$. A non-degenerate piece is non-constant on the quadric
$Q=m$, $m=\kappa p+\eta$, outside finitely many $p$. Group the points of
$R_j(p)$ by the value of a non-constant coordinate $X_j$; there are at
most $f(p)$ values. Each group lies on a hypersurface section of the
bounded quadric $Q=m$. For $k=3$ such a section is a curve whose real
components have degree $\ge2$ (no real line lies on a bounded quadric). By
Pila's bound it has $\ll m^{1/4+\varepsilon}$ integer points in a box of side
$\asymp m^{1/2}$. For $k\ge4$ one slices by the last $k-3$ coordinates. This
gives (a). Then (b) follows from (a) and the first-moment
bound $\sum_{p\le N}f(p)\ll N\log^2N\log\log N$ \cite[Thm.~1.1]{ET}, and the
corollary from Dirichlet's theorem.
\end{proof}
The same holds for line-free indefinite ternary forms \cite[\S5]{O124}. The
theorem does \emph{not} say that an embedding proves ES only on $U$. A
non-degenerate piece supported on a \emph{thin} set can cover a positive
proportion of primes. An example from \cite[\S4]{O124}: $Q=\sum_{i\le5}v_i^2$,
with $Q(v)=p+1$, the piece $v_1=1$, $4\mid Q(v)$, and the output
$((p+1)/4+v_1-1,\ p(p+1)/2,\ p(p+1)/2)$. What it excludes is that a
representation \emph{theorem}, which produces only sets of the generic
size, does any work outside $U$. The only rank-$3$ quadratic piece
of ES is $Q_d$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:Qd}). It represents everything and is
isotropic, and its side condition is not mild. \status{evidence}: every
Mordell-hard prime $\le10^8$ is solved through $Q_d$ with $d\le8$,
$a\le22$ \cite[\S8]{O124}.

\subsection{The scale constraint}
\begin{definition}[{\cite[Def.~2.2]{O127}}]
An \emph{ES-detector} assigns to each large prime $p$ a finite target
$\cT_p$ and a map $\Phi_p:\cT_p\to\mathrm{Sol}(p)$ whose fibres have size
$\le\mu(p)$. A hypothesis $\mathsf H$ \emph{drives} it if $\mathsf H$ implies
$\#\cT_p\ge M(p)-R(p)$ with explicit $M(p)>R(p)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}, relative to {\cite[Thm.~1.1, Prop.~1.7]{ET}}; {\cite[Prop.~2.3]{O127}}, {\cite[Prop.~2.4, \S5.5]{RT1}}]\label{prop:scale}
If $\mathsf H$ drives a detector with $R(p)\le M(p)/2$, then
$M(p)\le2\mu(p)f(p)$. Here
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $f(p)\le p^{3/5+O(1/\log\log p)}$ for every $p$ \cite[Prop.~1.7]{ET};
\item[(b)] for each $\delta>0$, $f(p)\le p^{\delta}$ outside a set of primes
with counting function $O_\delta(N^{1-\delta}\log^3N)$;
\item[(c)] for any $\xi(p)\to\infty$, $f(p)\le\xi(p)\log^3p\log\log p$ outside a
set of primes of relative density $o(1)$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, if $\mu(p)\le p^{o(1)}$, then $M(p)\le p^{o(1)}$ for almost
all $p$, and no detector with $M(p)\ge p^\eta$ for all large $p$ can be
driven.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$M/2\le\#\cT_p\le\mu(p)f(p)$. Part (a) is \cite[Prop.~1.7]{ET}. Parts (b)
and (c) follow from Markov's inequality applied dyadically to
$\sum_{p\le N}f(p)\ll N\log^2N\log\log N$ \cite[Thm.~1.1]{ET}.
\end{proof}
Every natural parametrisation of solutions by lattice points, Heegner
points or matrices is $O(1)$-to-one, or $p^{o(1)}$-to-one by the divisor
bound. So any equidistribution statement whose main term for the target is
a power of $p$ is excluded as a driver, unless it detects only a coarse
invariant of solutions (fibres $\mu\ge p^{\eta-o(1)}$). In that case the
lifting step from the invariant to an integer solution must be supplied
separately. Fibres growing with an auxiliary height do not help for
algebraic or bounded-weight orbit recipes (\S\ref{ssec:fibred}). This applies in particular to small-scale equidistribution of
Heegner or CM points in families with $|D|\ge p^\kappa$ through a
$p^{o(1)}$-to-one encoding \cite[Cor.~4.1]{O127}. With the Type~I bound of
\cite{TH} in place of \cite[Thm.~1.1]{ET}, Markov's inequality gives
$f_I(p)\le\psi\cL^3$ for all but a proportion $O(1/\psi)$ of primes
\cite[Thm.~5.1]{O123}. A Planck-scale certificate must therefore have
\emph{polylogarithmic} accuracy.

\begin{proposition}[local data of moderate modulus; \status{proved}; {\cite[Prop.~7.3]{O127}}, {\cite[Lemma~5.1]{RT1}}]\label{prop:localsize}
Let $S'(n)$ count the solutions of the $a=1$ Type~I slice
($t\ge1$, $tn+1=mm''$, $m\equiv m''\equiv-1\pmod t$, $4\mid m$; then
$c=(m''+1)/t$, \cite[Lemma~1.1]{O127}) with $\gcd(n,c)=1$.
Let $\mathsf H(n)$ be any predicate depending only on the dyadic range
$n\in(X,2X]$ and on $n\bmod Q(X)$, where $Q(X)\le X^{1/2-\varepsilon}$. If
$\mathsf H$ holds at one large prime $p\in(X,2X]$ that is a square modulo
$Q(X)$, then it holds at an odd square in $(X,2X]$, where $S'=0$. Hence
``$\mathsf H(n)\Rightarrow S'(n)\ge1$'' is false for any such $\mathsf H$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The odd $m\equiv m_0\pmod{2Q}$ with $m_0^2\equiv p\pmod Q$ give $\asymp X^{1/2}/Q$
squares in the class of $p$. For odd $n=m^2$, a slice solution with
$\gcd(n,c)=1$ is a Type~I solution, which cannot exist by
\cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}.
\end{proof}
This does not apply to ``$\mathsf H(n)\Rightarrow\mathrm{ES}(n)$'': odd
squares satisfy ES, through solutions that are of neither type.

\subsection{Thickening a target is averaging over $n$}
\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Prop.~5.4]{O123}}]\label{prop:thick}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge}, for $z\in\Lambda_d$ the
integer $k(z)=(2c\sqrt d\,x-\sqrt d-py)/y=4acd-f-p$ equals $n-p$, where
$n=4acd-f$ is the integer for which $(a,c,d,f)$ satisfies
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}(i) with $p$ replaced by $n$. Hence the thickened target
$R_c^{(K)}=\{|2c\sqrt d\,x-\sqrt d-py|\le Ky,\ y\ge\sqrt d/(2p)\}$
(the size window $f\le2p$ of \cite[Prop.~3.1]{O123}), of hyperbolic width
$\asymp(2K+1)/p$ when $c\sqrt d\ll p$, counts exactly
\[\sum_{|n-p|\le K}\#\{(a,f):\ f\mid4a^2d+1,\ 4acd-f=n,\ f\le2p\}.\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The computation of Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge}, with $k(z)\in\Z$ allowed
to range over $[-K,K]$.
\end{proof}
The counted points are Elsholtz--Tao points of the integers $n$ near $p$.
For composite $n$ the coprimality $\gcd(c,n)=1$ is not imposed, so the
count is at least the Type~I count of $n$ with these $(c,d)$, not equal to
it. \status{Assessment.} A spectral estimate at Sobolev scale $V$ (target
width $\asymp1/V$) therefore controls only averages of Type~I counts over
intervals $|n-p|\lesssim p/V$. With $V$ a small power of the discriminant
(\S\ref{sec:gaps}) these are intervals of length $p^{1-\kappa}$. In that
range unconditional exceptional-set bounds are already available, and
nothing pointwise follows. The pointwise problem is exactly $K=0$.

\subsection{Everything but one sign is soluble at odd squares}
In the lattice language of Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(c), Type~II at
$(c,d)$ for an integer $n$ asks for $Q=[A,B,C]\in L=\Z^3$ with
$q(Q)=-4dn$, $b(Q,S_c)=4$, $B\equiv0\pmod{4d}$, $d\mid C$ and $A>0$.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[Prop.~8.3]{O128}}]\label{prop:locsq}
For every $n\ge1$, including odd squares, and every $(c,d)$, the vector
$Q_0=[-1,0,-dn]$ satisfies every condition above except $A>0$:
$q(Q_0)=-4dn$, $b(Q_0,S_c)=4$, $B\equiv0\pmod{4d}$, $d\mid C$. Hence the
Type~II embedding problem is everywhere locally soluble, and even globally
soluble without the sign condition, for every $n$. For odd squares
$n=s^2$ it has no solution with $A>0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(d)).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$q(Q_0)=0-4(-1)(-dn)=-4dn$ and, since $b(Q,S_c)=4(cB-A)$,
$b(Q_0,S_c)=4$. Solubility at every prime $\ell$ is witnessed by $Q_0$. At
$\infty$ take $B>0$ large, $A=cB-1>0$ and $C=(B^2+4dn)/(4A)>0$. The last
sentence is \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}.
\end{proof}
So no statement ``everywhere locally soluble $\Rightarrow$ soluble'' holds
for this integral-point problem. $Q_0$ is the trivial factorisation
$N=(-1)(-N)$ of $N=4c^2dn+1$, i.e.\ $a=0$. The content of Type~II is that
$N$ has a positive divisor in the class $-1\pmod{4cd}$, while it always has
the positive divisor $1$ in the class $+1$. The two differ only by the sign
at $\infty$. The only restriction forced locally is the genus character
$\chi_{-4d}\cdot(n/\cdot)$, a $\Z/2$ quotient that is non-trivial exactly
at squares \cite[Prop.~9.2]{O128}. There is no natural spinor genus for
this pair problem, and the phrase ``spinor obstruction'' does not apply.
Once $S_c$ is fixed, the stabiliser is a \emph{split} torus, and the
problem is representation by $x^2-y^2$ in a coset, i.e.\ a
divisor-in-class question \cite[\S8]{O128}. This is the reason (an
\status{Assessment}) why the Gauss/three-squares mechanism (class number
one of a ternary genus, with an anisotropic stabiliser) does not transfer.

\subsection{Geometry: fibrations, strong approximation, Brauer--Manin}
Let $U_n$ and $\alpha$ be as in Proposition~\ref{prop:brauer}, and let
$L_{ij}$ be the lines $u_i=u_j=0$ and $M_k$ the lines $u_k=n/4$,
$u_i=-u_j$ (rational only if $4\mid n$).

\begin{theorem}[\status{proved}, relative to {\cite[Lemma~3.10]{BL}}; {\cite[Thm.~3.1]{O126}}]\label{thm:fibres}
Let $C\subset U_n$ be an irreducible curve over $\Q$ containing infinitely
many integral points. Then $C$ is one of the lines $L_{ij}$, or one of the
$M_k$ (only if $4\mid n$; these carry no positive points). In particular,
for every dominant map $U_n\to B$ to a curve, all fibres except finitely
many lines have finitely many integral points, and no fibre has infinitely
many positive integral points.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Points with a zero coordinate lie on the $L_{ij}$. By \cite[Lemma~3.10]{BL},
integral points with $u_1u_2u_3\ne0$ and $u_i\ne-u_j$ are finite in number.
Points with $u_i=-u_j\ne0$ satisfy $u_k=n/4$ and have a negative coordinate.
\end{proof}
The reason is the real place, not the splitness of the fibres: the open
torus $V_n\subset\tilde U_n$ of \cite[Lemma~2.1]{BL} is split over $\Q$
\cite[Prop.~3.2]{O126}.
No fibration argument can produce infinitely many positive points in a
fibre.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[\S1]{O126}}, {\cite{BL}}]\label{prop:BMonly}
For every $n\ge2$, the statement ``the Brauer--Manin obstruction is the
only obstruction to positive integral points on $U_n$'' is equivalent to
$\mathrm{ES}(n)$. For $n=1$ the Brauer--Manin obstruction does obstruct.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
For $n\ge2$ the positive Brauer set is non-empty by \cite[Thm.~1.8]{BL}, so
``BM is the only obstruction'' says exactly that a positive integral point
exists. For $n=1$ there is no positive solution, and every point of the positive
adelic set has $\operatorname{inv}_\infty\alpha=-1$ and $\operatorname{inv}_\ell\alpha=+1$ for all
finite $\ell$ \cite[Lemmas~3.1, 3.5, 3.8]{BL}, so the Brauer set is empty
\cite[\S1]{O126}. (The proof of \cite[Thm.~1.8]{BL} needs a prime divisor
of $n$; their Thm.~1.1 correctly assumes $n\ge2$.)
\end{proof}
So a ``BM is the only obstruction'' conjecture for this family is ES
restated.

On the homogeneous spaces $X_d:\ ef-4da^2=1$ ($\cong\mathrm{Spin}(2,1)/T$,
where strong approximation holds), the Type~I locus is
$\{ef-4da^2=1,\ f+p=4cda,\ c\ne0\}$. The ES condition $4da\mid f+p$ is a
congruence modulo a \emph{coordinate of the point}, not modulo a fixed
modulus. So it is not an adelic open condition away from $\infty$, and
strong approximation does not reach it. For each $(p,d)$ the locus has
finitely many integral points, while $X_d(\Z)$ is infinite
\cite[Prop.~4.1]{O126} (\status{proved}). That descent and strong
approximation are irrelevant beyond this is an \status{Assessment}
\cite[Cor.~4.2]{O126}. The same shape holds for Type~II on $Q_d$ and on the
level-$dp$ shells.

\subsection{Single targets and fixed shells are empty infinitely often}
\begin{proposition}[\status{conditional} on Hypothesis~H; {\cite[Prop.~5.3]{O123}}]\label{prop:emptyI}
There are infinitely many primes $p\equiv5\pmod{12}$ for which the
genus-allowed Type~I target $(c,d)=(1,3)$ is empty, although its expected
size is $\approx0.17\,\cL$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$x(x^2+12)$ on $x\equiv5\pmod{12}$ has no fixed prime divisor, so H gives
infinitely many primes $p\equiv5\pmod{12}$ with $p^2+12$ prime. Here
$\chi_{-12}(-p)=1$. The target counts divisors $f$ of $p^2+12$ with
$f\equiv-p\equiv7\pmod{12}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}(iii)). The only one is
$f=1\not\equiv7$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[\status{conditional} on Dickson's conjecture for two linear forms; {\cite[Lemma~1.2.1]{RT2}}]\label{prop:emptyII}
For every fixed $(c,d)$ there are infinitely many primes $p$ for which the
Type~II shell $(c,d)$ is empty, although its mean is
$\asymp\cL/\varphi(4cd)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The pair $X,\ 4c^2dX+1$ has no fixed prime divisor, so Dickson's
conjecture gives infinitely many primes $p$ with $4c^2dp+1$ prime. Its
divisors $1$ and $4c^2dp+1$ are $\equiv1\not\equiv-1\pmod{4cd}$.
\end{proof}
Under H, applied to finitely many polynomials at once, any fixed finite
union of such targets is empty infinitely often. This is
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}(iii) seen from the fixed-$p$ side.
\status{evidence}: all $3630$ primes $p\equiv5\pmod{12}$ in $[10^6,3\cdot10^6)$
with $p^2+12$ prime have the $(1,3)$ target empty, and among all
genus-allowed primes near $10^6$, $13$--$89\%$ of the individual targets
$(c,d)$ are empty \cite[\S3, \S5]{O123}.

\section{Quantitative gaps to known equidistribution}\label{sec:gaps}
Everything in this section is an \status{Assessment}: a comparison of
exponents, not a theorem about what any method can do.

\subsection{What is known}
Young \cite[Thm.~2.1]{Yo} proves a hybrid subconvexity bound which gives,
for Heegner points of discriminant $-D$, an error of the shape
$\|\phi\|_2D^{5/12+\varepsilon}V^{1+\varepsilon}$ for a test function at Sobolev
scale $V$. Consequently every fixed ball of radius $D^{-\delta}$ with
$\delta<1/24$ receives its expected share of points, and $\delta<1/8$ under
the Lindel\"of hypothesis; see also Humphries \cite{Hu}, who also gives the
genus and closed-geodesic versions. Young's Prop.~1.2 is a
\emph{single-discriminant existence theorem} proved spectrally:
$b^2\equiv-D\pmod{4a}$ has solutions with $a\asymp\sqrt D$,
$|b|\asymp\sqrt D/V$ for every large odd fundamental $D$, in the range
$V\ll D^{1/18-\varepsilon}$. It is the model of what a spectral pointwise ES
statement would have to look like \cite{LA}. Humphries and Radziwi\l\l
\cite{HR} reach much smaller scales, but only for almost all centres. The
ES targets are specific regions attached to cusps, not random ones.

\subsection{What ES needs}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Type~I.} In the $T_p$-translated picture the discriminant is
$D'=4dp^2$ and the target tubes have width $\asymp1/p\asymp|D'|^{-1/2}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge}; \cite[Prop.~3.2]{O123}): exactly the Planck
scale. In the level-$d$ picture $D=4d$ is tiny and the large parameter
enters only through the shape of the target. Proposition~\ref{prop:thick}
shows that thickening to a reachable width $|D'|^{-\delta}$ produces
averages over $n$ in intervals of length $p^{1-2\delta}$.
\item \emph{Type~II.} The shells of Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(c) have
width $\asymp D^{-1/2}/c$ with $D=4dp$. Young's method reaches shells of
width $D^{-1/18}$ unconditionally and $D^{-1/6}$ under the Lindel\"of
hypothesis \cite[\S4]{O128}. The gap is a factor $D^{4/9}$, resp.\
$D^{1/3}$, so what is needed goes beyond GLH.
\item \emph{Accuracy.} By Proposition~\ref{prop:scale} and
\cite[Thm.~5.1]{O123}, the count to be certified is $\le\psi\cL^3$ for all but
a proportion $O(1/\psi)$ of primes. Termwise use of GLH or of the
Linnik--Selberg conjecture loses $p^\varepsilon$, which exceeds every power of
$\cL$; individual central values are not polylogarithmically bounded.
GRH can enter with only polylogarithmic losses in averaged form (moments,
effective Siegel). But a Cauchy--Schwarz estimate over the spectrum at
Planck scale still loses a factor $\approx p$ against the main term
$\approx\cL$ \cite[\S5]{O123}.
\item \emph{Termwise GRH on a character expansion.} Expanding the $a=1$
slice count into Dirichlet characters and bounding each twisted divisor
sum by a GRH-type estimate gives error $\ge p^{1-o(1)}$ against a main term
$\asymp\log^2p$ \cite[Prop.~9.1]{O127} (\status{proved} bookkeeping, mechanism
scope).
\end{itemize}

\begin{assessment}\label{ass:verdict}
No standard hypothesis (GRH, GLH/Lindel\"of in any aspect,
Ramanujan--Petersson, Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture, twisted
Linnik--Selberg) is known to suffice, by the routes of this note, to prove
ES, or the Type~I or Type~II part of ES, for all large primes. A sufficient
statement would have to be at the Planck scale, of polylogarithmic accuracy,
genus-aware (carrying $1+\chi_{-4d}(-p)$, without which it is false at
$n=p^2$) and aggregated over a growing set of levels and cusps. Such a
statement is ES restated, not a standard conjecture. Its level window must
also be anchored at the least quadratic non-residue $n_p$. For
$p\equiv1\pmod4$, Type~I at level $d$ needs $(-d/p)=-1$, so a window
$d\le D(p)$ forces $n_p\le D(p)$; GRH gives $n_p\ll\cL^2$ (Ankeny, Bach) and
Burgess gives $n_p\ll p^{1/(4\sqrt e)+\varepsilon}$ unconditionally.
\end{assessment}

\section{Two non-counting escapes that fail}\label{sec:longshots}
Two kinds of argument seem to escape Proposition~\ref{prop:scale}. A
\emph{covering} (pigeonhole) argument would show that a whole coset of
residues is hit, hence the target too, without counting solutions. A
\emph{fibred} encoding attaches to each solution a fibre growing like a
power of an auxiliary height $H$; this is the loophole ``fibres
$\mu\ge p^{\eta-o(1)}$'' left open after Proposition~\ref{prop:scale} (the
blind spot of the red team's test~T8, \cite[\S5.5]{RT1}). Both fail, with
the exact scopes stated below. Sources: \cite{LP} and \cite{LF}, with
their internal reviews.

\subsection{Covering in the $e$-slices}\label{ssec:cover}
Let $p$ be an odd prime and $e\ge1$ with $e\equiv-p\pmod4$, $p\nmid e$. Put
\[N_e^{II}(p)=\frac{p+e}4,\qquad N_e^{I}(p)=\frac{pe+1}4,\]
and for $\gcd(N,e)=1$ define the \emph{signed divisor-ratio set}
$\Sigma_e(N)=\{ab^{-1}\bmod e:\ a,b\ge1,\ ab\mid N\}\subset G_e:=(\Z/e)^*$.

\begin{lemma}[$e$-slices; \status{proved}, elementary; {\cite[Lemma~1.1]{LP}}]\label{lem:eslice}
Let $T\in\{I,II\}$ and $N=N_e^T(p)$. \textup{(i)} $\gcd(N,e)=1$.
\textup{(ii)} The elements of $V_T(p)$ with this value of $e$ are in
bijection with the pairs $(a,b)$, $a,b\ge1$, $ab\mid N$, $e\mid a+b$, via
$d=N/(ab)$, $c=(a+b)/e$. \textup{(iii)} So this fibre is non-empty iff
$-1\in\Sigma_e(N)$. \textup{(iv)}
$\Sigma_e(N)=\prod_{\ell^v\parallel N}\{\ell^t:|t|\le v\}$ (a product set in
$G_e$); in particular $|\Sigma_e(N)|\le\tau(N^2)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i) $4N^I\equiv1$, $4N^{II}\equiv p\pmod e$. (ii) is the definition. (iii) $a,b$
are units mod $e$. (iv) $ab\mid N$ iff $\alpha_\ell+\beta_\ell\le v_\ell$, and
$t=\alpha_\ell-\beta_\ell$ takes every value in $[-v_\ell,v_\ell]$.
\end{proof}
Every element of $V_I(p)\cup V_{II}(p)$ has a value of $e$, so by
\eqref{eq:VIVII}, $\mathrm{ES}(p)$ holds iff $-1\in\Sigma_e(N_e^T(p))$ for some
$e$ and $T$: the $e$-slices parametrise all solutions by one coordinate.
(The map from $V_T(p)$ to solutions is onto, and injective once
$\gcd(a,b)=1$ is imposed; existence is unaffected.) For Type~II,
$N=N_e^{II}(p)$ runs over the consecutive integers above $p/4$.

From now on $p\equiv1\pmod4$, so $e\equiv3\pmod4$. Then the Kronecker
symbol $\chi_{-e}$ coincides with the Jacobi symbol $(\cdot/e)$; it is a
character of $G_e$ with $\chi_{-e}(-1)=-1$. Let $K_e=\ker\chi_{-e}$, a
subgroup of index~$2$ not containing $-1$.

\begin{lemma}[genus inside a slice; \status{proved}; {\cite[Lemma~1.2]{LP}}]\label{lem:eslicegenus}
Let $N=N_e^T(p)$. \textup{(i)} Every prime $\ell\mid N$, including
$\ell=2$, has $\chi_{-e}(\ell)=(\ell/p)$. \textup{(ii)} $\chi_{-e}(N^I)=1$
and $\chi_{-e}(N^{II})=(e/p)$. \textup{(iii)} Hence a point of the slice
needs a prime $\ell\mid ab$ with $(\ell/p)=-1$. If $p$ is replaced by an odd
square $m^2$ with $\gcd(m,e)=1$, then $\Sigma_e(N)\subset K_e\not\ni-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i) For odd $\ell$, Type~II gives $-e\equiv p$ and Type~I gives
$-e\equiv p^{-1}\pmod\ell$, so $(-e/\ell)=(p/\ell)=(\ell/p)$. If $2\mid N$
then $8\mid p+e$ (resp.\ $8\mid pe+1$), so $-e\equiv p\pmod8$, and
$(-e/2)=(p/2)=(2/p)$. (ii) Multiplying (i), $\chi_{-e}(N)=(N/p)$, and
$4N^I\equiv1$, $4N^{II}\equiv e\pmod p$. (iii) $\chi_{-e}(ab^{-1})=-1$ and (i);
at $m^2$ all the symbols are $+1$.
\end{proof}
The target $-1$ lies in the non-trivial coset $-K_e$, and only prime
factors with $(\ell/p)=-1$ leave $K_e$. This is the odd-square obstruction
\cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET} inside one slice, and the reason why small levels
fail when $p$ is a residue of all small primes. The naive pigeonhole
aims at the wrong coset:

\begin{lemma}[collisions give signed solutions; \status{proved}; {\cite[Lemma~1.3]{LP}}]\label{lem:collide}
If $2^{\omega(N)}>\varphi(e)$, there are coprime $a>b\ge1$ with $ab\mid N$ and
$a\equiv b\pmod e$. With $c=(a-b)/e$ and $d=N/(ab)$,
\[\tfrac4p=\tfrac1{abd}-\tfrac1{acdp}+\tfrac1{bcdp}\ \ (T=II),\qquad
\tfrac4p=\tfrac1{abdp}-\tfrac1{acd}+\tfrac1{bcd}\ \ (T=I).\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Two of the $2^{\omega(N)}$ unitary divisors of $N$ agree mod $e$; divide
both by their gcd. The identities are $c(p+e)/(abcdp)$ and
$c(1+pe)/(abcdp)$, as for \eqref{eq:VIVII}.
\end{proof}
So collisions and zero-sum theory hit $1\in K_e$ (signed solutions).
Positivity asks for the translate $-K_e$ to be hit: a covering statement.

\begin{proposition}[covering criterion; \status{proved} from Kneser's theorem \cite{Kn}, {\cite[Thm.~5.5]{TV}}; {\cite[Prop.~1.4]{LP}}]\label{prop:kneser}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, $g_1,\dots,g_k\in G$, $v_i\ge1$,
$A_i=\{g_i^t:|t|\le v_i\}$, $\Sigma=A_1\cdots A_k$, $t_0\in G$, and let
$o_H(g)$ be the order of $gH$ in $G/H$. If
\[\textstyle\sum_i\bigl(\min(2v_i+1,o_H(g_i))-1\bigr)\ \ge\ |G:H|-1\]
for every subgroup $H$ with $t_0\notin H$, then $t_0\in\Sigma$. Among
criteria that depend only on the orders $o_H(g_i)$ (for all $H$) and on
the $v_i$, this one is sharp: in $\Z/2m$ with all $g_i=1$, $v_i=1$, $t_0=m$
it reads $k\ge m$, and $\Sigma=[-k,k]\ni m$ iff $k\ge m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $H=\mathrm{Stab}(\Sigma)$. As $1\in\Sigma$, $H\subset\Sigma$; if
$t_0\notin\Sigma$ then $t_0\notin H$ and $|\Sigma/H|\le|G:H|-1$. In $G/H$ every
partial product of the images $X_i$ of the $A_i$ has trivial stabiliser, so
Kneser gives $|\Sigma/H|\ge1+\sum_i(|X_i|-1)$ with
$|X_i|=\min(2v_i+1,o_H(g_i))$.
\end{proof}
In the slice ($G=G_e$, $g_i$ the primes $\ell\mid N$, $v_i=v_\ell(N)$,
$t_0=-1$), $H=K_e$ recovers Lemma~\ref{lem:eslicegenus}, and $H=\{1\}$
requires $\Omega(N)\ge(\varphi(e)-1)/2$. Counting needs only
$\tau(N^2)\ge\varphi(e)/2$ to cover $-K_e$, i.e.\ $\Omega(N)\ge\log_3(\varphi(e)/2)$;
structure-only criteria need $\Omega(N)$ linear in $\varphi(e)$. Closing
this gap requires the actual residues $\ell\bmod e$ of the prime factors.
Forcing $k$ odd primes into $N_e^{II}(p)$ by congruences puts $e$ in one
class mod $4L\ge\exp((1+o(1))k\log k)$, against $3^{k+1}$ residues
\cite[Lemma~2.1]{LP}; and a covered coset of index $\le e^\theta$ ($\theta<1$)
forces $e\le\exp(O_\theta(\log p/\log\log p))$ (Nicolas--Robin \cite{NR};
\cite[Lemma~2.3]{LP}).

The primes of $N$ below the modulus have known residues (themselves). The
next theorem shows that they never suffice on a positive proportion of
primes.

\begin{definition}[{\cite[\S2.2]{LP}}]
Let $N=N_e^T(p)$, $L=\prod_{\ell^v\parallel N,\ \ell<e}\ell^v$ (the
\emph{transparent part}), $M=N/L$, and $R=\Sigma_e(L)\cdot\{M^{-1},1,M\}$.
For $0\le\theta<1$, a \emph{transparent covering certificate of strength
$\theta$} at $(p,e,T)$ is a subgroup $H\le G_e$ with $|G_e:H|\le2e^\theta$ and
$-H\subset R$.
\end{definition}
A certificate yields a solution ($R\subset\Sigma_e(N)$, $-1\in-H$); with $H=K_e$,
$R$ covers the whole target coset.

\begin{theorem}[\status{proved}, relative to PNT in progressions to a fixed modulus, Brun--Titchmarsh \cite{MV}, Shiu {\cite[Thm.~1]{Shiu}} and Nicolas--Robin \cite{NR}; {\cite[Thm.~2.2]{LP}}]\label{thm:transparent}
Fix $0\le\theta<1/2$ and $\varepsilon>0$. There is $C=C(\theta,\varepsilon)$
such that for every $Y\ge3$, with $q_0=8\prod_{3\le\ell\le Y}\ell$,
\begin{multline*}
\#\{p\le x:\ p\equiv1\ (q_0),\ \text{a certificate of strength $\theta$
exists at some }(e,T)\}\\
\le\bigl(CY^{-1+2\theta+\varepsilon}+o_{x\to\infty}(1)\bigr)\,\pi(x;q_0,1),
\end{multline*}
with $e\equiv3\pmod4$ and $T$ arbitrary. Hence, for $Y$ large, a set of primes of positive lower density has no transparent
covering certificate in any $e$-slice of either type.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
A certificate gives $|R|\ge\varphi(e)e^{-\theta}/2$, hence
$\tau(L^2)\ge B_e:=\varphi(e)e^{-\theta}/6$. Fix $p\equiv1\pmod{q_0}$, $p\le x$.

\emph{(a) $e\le Y$.} Every prime $\ell<e$ has $(\ell/p)=1$, so $L\in K_e$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:eslicegenus}(i)); $(e/p)=1$ as all prime factors of $e$ are
$\le Y$, so $\chi_{-e}(M)=1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:eslicegenus}(ii). Thus
$R\subset K_e\not\ni-1$: not even a transparent witness exists.

\emph{(b) $Y<e\le E_1:=\exp((\log x)^{1/3})$.} If $L>x^{1/2}$, multiplying the
prime factors of $L$ (all $<x^{1/4}$) one at a time gives $u\mid N$ with
$x^{1/4}<u\le x^{1/2}$ and $P^+(u)<E_1$; then $p$ lies in one class mod
$4u$, and Brun--Titchmarsh with Rankin's bound for
$\sum_{u>x^{1/4},\,P^+(u)<E_1}1/\varphi(u)$, summed over the $\le2E_1$ pairs
$(e,T)$, gives $o(x/(\varphi(q_0)\log x))$ primes. If $L\le x^{1/2}$, use
Markov's inequality with the second moment:
$1\le B_e^{-2}\tau(L^2)^2=B_e^{-2}\sum_{m\mid L}h(m)$, where
$h=\tau(\cdot^2)^2*\mu\ge0$, $h(\ell^v)=8v$. Each $m\le x^{1/2}$ with $m\mid N$
puts $p$ in one class mod $\mathrm{lcm}(4m,q_0)\le x^{2/3}$ (compatible with
$p\equiv1\ (q_0)$ only if every odd $\ell\le Y$ dividing $m$ divides $e+1$).
Brun--Titchmarsh and an Euler product bound the number of such $p$ by
$\ll\frac{x}{\varphi(q_0)\log x}\,19^{\omega(e+1)}(\log e)^8B_e^{-2}$, and
$\sum_{e>Y}19^{\omega(e+1)}(\log e)^8B_e^{-2}\ll Y^{-1+2\theta+\varepsilon}$ with
a constant independent of $Y$.

\emph{(c) $E_1<e\le x$.} Markov over all $p\le x$ (the map $p\mapsto N$ is
injective): Type~II has $N\le x/2$ and $\sum_{n\le x/2}\tau(n^2)^2\ll x(\log x)^8$;
Type~I has $N\equiv4^{-1}\pmod e$, $N\le X=(xe+1)/4$, and Shiu's theorem
(modulus $e<X^{3/4}$) gives $\ll(xe/\varphi(e))(\log x)^8$. Since
$\sum_{e>E_1}B_e^{-2}e/\varphi(e)\ll E_1^{-1+2\theta+\varepsilon}$, which beats every
power of $\log x$, the count is $o(x/(\varphi(q_0)\log x))$.

\emph{(d) $e>x$.} Then $N\le e^2/4$, and Nicolas--Robin gives
$\tau(N^2)\le\tau(N)^2\le\exp(4.3\log e/\log\log e)<B_e$ for $e\ge e_0(\theta)$.
Finally, $\pi(x;q_0,1)\ge x/(2\varphi(q_0)\log x)$ for $x\ge x_0(Y)$ (PNT in
progressions).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[all $\theta<1$]\label{rem:thetaone}
With the $r$-th moment of $\tau(\cdot^2)$, $r>1/(1-\theta)$ (local factors
$\ll_rv^{r-1}$, Shiu with $(3^r)^v$, Markov with $B_e^{-r}$), the same proof
gives every fixed $\theta<1$: certificates covering a coset of size
$\ge e^\delta$ fail on a positive-density set, for every $\delta>0$ (the
internal reviewer's derivation, checked step by step; \cite[\S2.2]{LP}).
\end{remark}

\emph{Scope.} Theorem~\ref{thm:transparent} concerns certificates that use
only the prime factors of $N_e^T(p)$ below the modulus, plus one opaque
block, and that cover a coset of size $\ge e^\delta$. It says nothing about
arguments that merely hit $-1$, or that use the residues mod $e$ of prime
factors above the modulus: those are the per-slice divisor events of
hatches H-A and H-B. The expected number of transparent certificates over
all $e$ is bounded by $\sum_e(\log e)^8e^{2\theta-2}$, independently of $p$: a
bounded-mass mechanism, not a near miss. The analogous statement for the
two-coordinate rows of Lemma~\ref{lem:slices} should follow by the same
proof (\status{Assessment}; not written out).

\begin{conjecture}[PH, \status{conjecture}; {\cite[Conj.~5.1]{LP}}]\label{conj:PH}
For every sufficiently large prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$ there are
$e\equiv3\pmod4$ and $T$ with $\varphi(e)\ge40$ and
$\Sigma_e(N_e^T(p))\supseteq-K_e$.
\end{conjecture}
PH implies ES$(p)$. \status{evidence} \cite[\S3]{LP} (six primes
re-run independently in the review): every Mordell-hard prime below $10^6$
except $3529$ and $110881$ has such a cover with $e\le2000$; so do $300$
consecutive Mordell-hard primes after each of $10^6,10^7,10^8$ ($e\le5000$)
and $15$ selected hard primes ($e\le20000$). The deep covers are almost
always transparent; for $e\ge500$ covers are only about $5\%$ of the
slices containing a solution at $10^8$. Transparent counts still grow up to
$10^8$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:transparent} is qualitative: its failure set is
not visible numerically. By Theorem~\ref{thm:transparent},
any proof of PH must use prime factors above the modulus on a set of
positive density, so PH is not easier than H-A/H-B
(\status{Assessment}).

\subsection{Fibred encodings}\label{ssec:fibred}
Let $\mathrm{Sol}(n)$ be the (finite) solution set of \eqref{eq:es} and
$f(n)$ the number of Type~I and~II solutions. A \emph{fibred encoding} is
a countable set $E=E(n)$ with a height (finitely many points of height
$\le H$) and a map $\varphi:E\to\mathrm{Sol}(n)$; it is \emph{power-fibred} if
$\#E(\le H)\sim c(n)H^\delta$, $\delta>0$. A counting route evaluates $c(n)$
independently and reads off $c(n)>0\Rightarrow\mathrm{Sol}(n)\ne\emptyset$. But
$c(n)=\sum_{s\in\mathrm{Sol}(n)}c_s$ (fibre densities; \cite[Lemma~1.2]{LF}): the
$H$-asymptotic is free, the content sits in the constant, and whether it
can be evaluated without knowing $\mathrm{Sol}(n)$ depends on how $\varphi$
is computed.

\begin{lemma}[rigidity; \status{proved}, elementary; {\cite[Lemma~2.1]{LF}}]\label{lem:rigid}
Let $V/\Q$ be irreducible, $E\subset V(\Q)$ Zariski-dense, and
$\varphi:V\dashrightarrow\mathbb A^3$ a rational map over $\Q$, regular on $E$,
with $\varphi(E)$ finite. Then $\varphi$ is constant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $U=\operatorname{dom}\varphi$ (irreducible), $E$ is dense and covered by
the finitely many closed sets $\varphi^{-1}(s_i)$; so one of them is $U$.
\end{proof}
By Borel's density theorem, an orbit $\Gamma x$ of an arithmetic subgroup
$\Gamma$ of a connected semisimple $\Q$-group without compact factors is
Zariski-dense in $\overline{Gx}$ \cite[Lemma~2.2]{LF}, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} covers the orbits counted in \cite{DRS,EM}. Consequences
(\status{proved}, \cite[Thm.~2.3]{LF}): if $E$ is a union of pieces $E_i$,
each Zariski-dense in an irreducible $V_i$ with $\varphi|_{E_i}$ rational,
then $\varphi\equiv s_i$ on $E_i$, readable off the equations. If moreover
$\varphi_i$ maps $V_i$ into the ES variety $4xyz=n(xy+yz+zx)$ and has
$\Z_\ell$-coefficients, then one $\Z_\ell$-point for each $\ell$ and one real
point in the counting region (mapped into the positive octant) already
force $s_i\in\mathrm{Sol}(n)$. A
Siegel or Hardy--Littlewood main term carries no information beyond the
explicit point $s_i$: for algebraic recipes counting is idle. (This holds
for pieces as given. A candidate set $V_i(\Z)\cap\Omega$ that is not
Zariski-dense must first be decomposed along the components of its
closure.)

\begin{corollary}[\status{proved}, relative to {\cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}} and Dirichlet; {\cite[Cor.~2.4]{LF}}]\label{cor:uniformalg}
Let $\mathcal V\to\mathbb A^1_n$ be an irreducible $\Q$-family with
geometrically irreducible generic fibre, and $\varphi:\mathcal V\dashrightarrow\mathbb A^3$
rational. Let $P_{\rm cov}$ be the set of primes $p$ for which a
Zariski-dense $E(p)\subset\mathcal V_p(\Z)$ has $\varphi(E(p))\subset\mathrm{Sol}(p)$.
There is $D'\ge1$ such that all but finitely many $p\in P_{\rm cov}$ are
quadratic non-residues mod $D'$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} and generic flatness, $\varphi$ is given on almost
all fibres by one $s_0\in\Q[n]^3$ (polynomial by integrality), e.g.\ of
Type~I shape $x=nx_1$, $y(0)z(0)\ne0$. If $p\equiv m_0^2\pmod{D'}$, a prime
$m\equiv m_0$ (Dirichlet) makes $s_0(m^2)$ a Type~I solution for $m^2$,
contradicting \cite[Prop.~1.6]{ET}.
\end{proof}
This is the classical fact that polynomial identities miss the square
classes (\cite[Props.~1.8--1.9]{ET}; Mordell--Schinzel), for fibred
encodings of bounded complexity, however large the fibres. With a
parameter set growing with $p$, fibring only changes the normalisation
\cite[Cor.~2.5]{LF}.

The remaining power-fibred encodings use \emph{orbit recipes}: $E(n)$ is a
finite union of $\Gamma'$-orbits in $V(\Z)\cap\Omega$, where $V=\overline{Gx_0}$
is a closed orbit of a connected semisimple $\Q$-group $G$ with $G(\R)$
without compact factors, $\Gamma'\subset G(\Z)$ is arithmetic, and $\varphi$
is $\Gamma'$-invariant. Let $\mu(n)$ be the largest number of orbits over one
solution. The \emph{counting input} (C) is the asymptotic
$\#(\Gamma'y\cap B_H)\sim w_O\operatorname{vol}(B_H)$ for well-rounded $B_H$, with
$w_O=\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma'_y\backslash H_y(\R))/\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma'\backslash G(\R))$,
$H_y$ the stabiliser \cite{DRS,EM,EMS}. The statements of these counting
theorems (and of \cite{BR,CTX} below) were checked only second-hand, by
a research subagent of the source; the internal reviewer had no access to
them.

\begin{theorem}[orbit weights; \status{proved} relative to (C); {\cite[Thm.~3.1]{LF}}]\label{thm:orbitweight}
\textup{(a)} $c(n)=\sum_Ow_O=\sum_{s\in\mathrm{Sol}(n)}\sum_{O\mapsto s}w_O\le\mu(n)f(n)\max_Ow_O$.
\textup{(b)} If all stabilisers $H_y(\R)$ are compact, then $w_O=\kappa/|\Gamma'_y|$
with $\kappa$ depending only on the real orbit of $y$, and
$w_{\max}/w_{\min}\le C(\Gamma')$. So the number of orbits is at most
$C(\Gamma')\mu(n)f(n)$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:scale} holds with
multiplicity $C(\Gamma')\mu(n)$.
\textup{(c)} Only non-compact stabilisers (tori or semisimple) can give
weights that vary by a power of $n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) is $c(n)=\sum_sc_s$ with (C) for each orbit. (b) Compact stabilisers in
one real orbit are conjugate, so they have equal volume; $\Gamma'_y$ is finite,
and $G(\Z)$ has finitely many conjugacy classes of finite subgroups.
\end{proof}
Example \cite[\S4]{LF}: the Type~II pairs $(Q,S_c)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(c), moved by $\SL_2(\Z)$ (stabiliser $\pm I$),
give $\asymp H$ points per orbit and
$c(n)=\kappa_{c,d}\#\{f\mid4c^2dn+1:\ f\equiv-1\ (4cd)\}$; all orbit counts agree
to $1.5\%$ at $H=3000$ (\status{evidence}). The Type~II Pell orbits $\{\varepsilon_s^k\}$ grow only
logarithmically, contain no ES point after $k=1$, and have regulators
$O(\log p)$, because the solution is the unit.

\emph{The odd-square test.} Suppose a main term has the Siegel or
Borovoi--Rudnick shape \cite{BR}
$M(n)=m_\infty(n)\prod_\ell m_\ell(n)\cdot|B|^{-1}\sum_{\alpha\in B}\prod_\ell E_\ell(\alpha,n)$
(local densities, and local Brauer averages $E_\ell$ for a finite
$B\subset\Br$), subject to \textup{(U)}: there are a finite set $S_0$ of primes
and $k\ge1$ such that for $\ell\nmid n$, $m_\ell(n)>0$ and $E_\ell=1$ if
$\ell\notin S_0$, while for $\ell\in S_0$ these factors depend only on
$n\bmod\ell^k$; and $m_\infty(n)>0$ for large $n$.

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved} under (U); {\cite[\S5.1]{LF}}]\label{prop:Utest}
Let $Q_1=\prod_{\ell\in S_0}\ell^k$, $p\nmid Q_1$ prime with $M(p)>0$, and $m$ odd,
$\gcd(m,Q_1)=1$, $m\equiv m_0\pmod{Q_1}$ with $m_0^2\equiv p$. If $M(m^2)=0$,
then the two values are separated at a place $\ell\mid m$ (some $m_\ell(m^2)=0$,
or some $E_\ell(\alpha,m^2)\ne1$), or at the place $p$ alone.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By (U), every other factor takes the same value at $p$ and at $m^2$.
\end{proof}
So a main term passes the odd-square test only through the places
dividing $m$, i.e.\ the place $p$ for $n=p$. Such factors occur: the spinor exceptions of $3x^2+4y^2+9z^2$ are exactly the squares
$w^2$ with all prime factors of $w$ $\equiv1\pmod3$ (Earnest \cite[Prop.~5.1]{Ea};
reproduced numerically to $5\cdot10^4$). The ES-natural candidate is the
genus set $\mathfrak G^-_d(n)$ of primitive positive forms of
discriminant $-4dn$ with $\chi_{-4d}(Q)=-1$ (evaluated at any represented
integer prime to $4dn$). The Type~II form $[f,4ad,de]$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(c) lies in it (part (d)).

\begin{proposition}[\status{proved}; {\cite[\S5.3]{LF}}]\label{prop:genusminus}
\textup{(a)} For odd $n=m^2$ with $\gcd(m,2d)=1$, $\mathfrak G^-_d(m^2)=\emptyset$.
\textup{(b)} For a prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$, $p\nmid2d$,
$\#\mathfrak G^-_d(p)=h(-4dp)/2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) A represented $k$ prime to $4dm^2$ has
$1=(-4dm^2/k)=\chi_{-4d}(k)$. (b) On represented values $\chi_{-4d}=(\cdot/p)$,
a genus character which is non-trivial on the class group (there is a
second assigned character, at $2$ or at a prime $q\equiv3\pmod4$ dividing $d$),
so it takes the value $-1$ on half of the classes.
\end{proof}
The mass of $\mathfrak G^-_d$ therefore passes the odd-square test, through
the place $p$, as Proposition~\ref{prop:Utest} requires. But it is not a
valid encoding: it has $h/2\asymp p^{1/2\pm\varepsilon}$ classes against
$F_d(p)=\#\{\text{Type~II points at level }d\}=O(\text{polylog})$ on average.
\status{evidence} (reproduced by the reviewer): for $5$ primes
$p\equiv1\pmod4$ in $[10^3,10^4]$ in the square classes mod $120$ (where
Type~II is sparsest; $19/40$ is not a typical rate) and $d\le8$,
$\#\mathfrak G^-_d(p)\in[10,144]$ while $F_d(p)=0$ in $19$ of the $40$ pairs. So
the per-level criterion ``$\mathfrak G^-_d(p)\ne\emptyset\Rightarrow F_d(p)>0$'' is
false. The aggregate criterion ``$\exists d$: $\mathfrak G^-_d(p)\ne\emptyset$'' is
not false but vacuous: by (b) it holds at every $p\equiv1\pmod4$, so as a
criterion for ES it is ES itself. Likewise the genus-full closure of the
pair embeddings contains $Q_0$ for every $n$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:locsq}), so its mass is positive at odd squares.

\begin{theorem}[fibred encodings, exact scope; {\cite[Thm.~5.4]{LF}}]\label{thm:fibred}
Let $E(n)=\bigsqcup_{\theta\in\Theta(n)}E_\theta$ be power-fibred, with $\Theta(n)$
arbitrary (it may grow with $n$), each piece either \textup{(X1)}
Zariski-dense in an irreducible $V_\theta$ with $\varphi|_{E_\theta}$ rational,
or \textup{(X2)} a finite union of orbits as above, with orbit weights in
$[w_\theta,Rw_\theta]$. Let $\mu(n)$ be the largest number of pieces (X1
pieces, X2 orbits) over one solution.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \status{(proved)} X1 pieces are constant; for X2,
$R=C(\Gamma_\theta)$ with compact stabilisers, $R=O(\log n)$ for Pell orbits.
\item \status{(proved relative to (C) and \cite[Thm.~1.1]{ET})} Proving
$c(n)\ge M(n)/2$ needs $M(n)\le2R\mu(n)f(n)\max_\theta w_\theta$: Proposition~\ref{prop:scale}
with multiplicity $R\mu(n)$. The factor $H^\delta$ cancels.
\item \status{(conditional} on the hypotheses of Colliot-Th\'el\`ene--Xu
\cite[Thm.~3.7]{CTX}: $G$ simply connected, stabiliser connected\status{)} A
main term from a mass formula is the mass of a genus-full set; if that set
is the encoding, its non-emptiness is decided by local and Brauer--Manin
data without counting. These hypotheses fail for the pair space of
Proposition~\ref{prop:typeII}(c) ($G=\SL_2$, stabiliser $\mu_2$); the two
refutations above do not use \cite{CTX}.
\item \status{(proved)} If $\Theta$ is finite and the X1 data are uniform in
$n$ as in Corollary~\ref{cor:uniformalg}, all large $p\equiv1\pmod{D'}$ are
missed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\emph{Exact scope.} Theorem~\ref{thm:fibred} covers algebraic recipes and
orbit recipes with bounded covolume ratio $R$: the \emph{continuous} fibre
factor $H^\delta$ buys nothing. It is not a theorem about all large-fibre
encodings. The T8 blind spot is closed only partially; it moves to
(E-d) the number $\mu(n)\ge n^\eta$ of pieces over one solution (discrete
multiplicity; Theorem~\ref{thm:fibred} is then vacuous) and to
(E-a) weight spread $R\ge n^\eta$ not capped by the solution (none found:
the only split torus met among the ES encodings examined is the Pell unit,
which is the solution itself). Also outside are (E-b) a \emph{valid} genus-full
criterion with a Brauer or spinor factor at $p$, which would be a
structural theorem of the strength of ES, and (E-c) recipes that are
neither algebraic nor lattice-invariant (floors, gcds, factorisations),
which fall under \cite[Thm.~M]{PS} instead.

\section{Open problems: the escape hatches}\label{sec:open}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}(iii), a route that survives must use a
parameter set growing with $p$, integers that are not formal polynomials
in $p$, non-abelian data, or a signed counting certificate. Section~\ref{sec:longshots} removes two
further candidates (Theorems~\ref{thm:transparent}, \ref{thm:fibred}). We list the
concrete forms \cite[\S3]{RT2}. The verdicts are \status{Assessments}. Each
hatch is equally a hatch for \emph{disproof}: a sterile prime, if one
exists, would be a failure not explained by Brauer--Manin
(Proposition~\ref{prop:BMonly}), and none of the encodings above could
detect it either.

\begin{problem}[H-A: joint factorisation of a growing family]
Prove, at a single large prime $p$, a lower bound for
$\sum_{\theta\in\Theta(p)}\#\{f\mid N_\theta(p):\ f\bmod q_\theta\in R_\theta(p)\}$
for a family $\Theta(p)$ growing with $p$; for example
$\sum_{a\le A,\,d\le D}\#\{f\mid p+4a^2d:\ f\equiv-1\pmod{4ad}\}$ (row
II$\{a,d\}$), or the Type~I family $p^2+4c^2d$.
\end{problem}
Divisor sums over the values of a \emph{fixed} quadratic polynomial are
classical \cite{Ho}, and roots of quadratic congruences are equidistributed
on average over the modulus \cite{DFI}. We know no result uniform in the
constant term $p$ in the needed range. More seriously, ES needs a
\emph{moving} modulus $q=4ad$ tied to the variable. Once $q>N^{1/3}$, at
most $11$ divisors of $N$ lie in one class \cite{Le}, so each $\theta$
contributes $O(1)$. The family is a sum of $\asymp\cL^3$ events of size
$O(1)$, with no large sub-family (\S\ref{sec:numerics}). \emph{Verdict:} open;
no known technology. This is the main hatch, and any automorphic route
(Kuznetsov over the level) lives here, with the polylogarithmic accuracy
demanded by Proposition~\ref{prop:scale}.

\begin{problem}[H-B: parity-breaking for a divisor-in-moving-class event]
A slice is empty when $N_\theta(p)$ is prime
(Propositions~\ref{prop:emptyI}, \ref{prop:emptyII}). More generally it is
empty when all prime factors of $N_\theta(p)$ lie in classes generating a
subgroup that misses $R_\theta(p)$. Exclude, at a fixed $p$, the conspiracy
in which every $N_\theta(p)$, $\theta\in\Theta(p)$, is bad in this sense.
\end{problem}
``Bad'' is a multiplicative-class condition of parity type, which
lower-bound sieves cannot detect without bilinear input \cite[ch.~16]{FI}.
Parity-breaking through bilinear sums exists for thin polynomial sequences
averaged over their variables \cite{FI98,HB,Ma}. At fixed $p$ the variables
$\theta$ are free to average over. The differences are the target (a divisor
in a moving class, not primality) and the size of the family ($\asymp\cL^3$
effective members). \emph{Verdict:} open; the closest existing technology in
spirit. The best-defined non-automorphic research target in this list is
a ``Friedlander--Iwaniec theorem for divisors in moving classes over the
Elsholtz--Tao family at fixed $p$''. The $O(1)$-per-block mass makes
bilinear methods, which need long sums, hard to apply.

\begin{problem}[H-D: non-abelian or higher-rank data]
Find an object carrying information not determined by congruences
(Frobenius in non-abelian extensions, $\mathrm{GL}_3$ or higher automorphic
forms) with non-zero coefficient in an ES count.
\end{problem}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:cm}(iii), in the Type~I CM torus the dihedral data
have coefficient $0$, so such data could only enter through the choice of
$\theta$, not by deciding a fibre. The varieties are toric or quadric
(Lemma~\ref{lem:slices}), so no natural higher-rank object is attached.
\emph{Verdict:} open, with no candidate object known.

\begin{problem}[H-E: an exact identity with positive main term]
Find one expansion of an aggregated ES count, with a positive main term
and cancellation between different $\theta$ that is not visible
piecewise.
\end{problem}
Termwise use of all standard hypotheses is excluded at the level of
mechanism (\S\ref{sec:gaps}); even the Linnik--Selberg conjecture for sums of
Kloosterman sums (partial results: \cite{ST}) loses $p^\varepsilon$. The
search of \cite{O131} found no structurally positive identity. Its exact
content: the Type~II count $T_2^+(n)$ of \cite{O131} (positive $a,b$, no
coprimality conditions) vanishes at every square $n=m^2$, odd or even; for $cd\mid6$ the Type~II
$(c,d)$-slice is an exact combination of binary theta coefficients at
$4c^2dn+1$; the Type~II count is ``Eisenstein even/odd parts minus a
boundary $-2$ per level'' \cite[Prop.~4.1]{O131}; and there is no linear
relation between $56$ ES count variants and $54$ classical arithmetic
functions on $n\le2\cdot10^4$ (\status{certified}, exact $\Q$-rank; the
scope is that finite dictionary). In every case positivity is again ``a
specific integer has a divisor in a residue class''. \emph{Verdict:} open.

\begin{remark}[H-F: integral Hasse principles, a cautionary example]
ES$(p)$ asks for an integral point on an affine variety whose expected
number of points is polylogarithmic. In the closest family where the
integral Hasse principle has been settled, the Markoff-type surfaces, it
holds for almost all parameters but fails for infinitely many
\cite[Thm.~1.2]{GS}. In that setting ``almost all'' is what geometry
delivered, not ``all''.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[non-escapes]
Anchoring the levels at the least non-residue $n_p$ (H-C) fixes the genus
condition but leaves H-A at the anchored levels. Taking Type~I and Type~II
together (H-G) has the same $\cL^3$ mass structure, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}(iii) applies to unions of rows of both types
\cite[\S3]{RT2}. Covering reduces to H-A/H-B (Theorem~\ref{thm:transparent});
fibring leaves open (E-a)--(E-d) of \S\ref{ssec:fibred} (T8 blind spot).
\end{remark}

\section{Numerical evidence}\label{sec:numerics}
All statements here are \status{evidence}. The counts are exact
enumerations, validated against brute force for $p\le400$ and reproduced
independently by internal reviewers, as indicated.
\begin{itemize}
\item $f_I(p)\ge1$ and $f_{II}(p)\ge1$ for every prime $5\le p\le10^8$, and the
$a=1$ Type~I slice is non-empty for each of them \cite[\S1]{O123},
\cite[\S10]{O128}. This was reproduced to $10^7$ (Type~I) and to $10^8$
(Type~II) by independent code.
\item Dyadic minima of $f_I$ grow with $p$ and stay at $0.12$--$0.20$ times
the dyadic mean. The mean is $\approx0.1\cL^3$. For example
$\min f_I=105$ on $[2^{25},2^{26})$, at $p=37117609$. All window minimisers
from $2^9$ on are Mordell-hard (in the six square classes modulo $840$).
The $a=1$ slice alone has minimum $1$--$5$ throughout.
\item $\min f_{II}/\log^3p\approx0.012$ over $p\ge10^5$, attained at
$p=2031121$; dyadic minima of $f_{II}$ are $62,74,88,94$ on the blocks
$2^{23},\dots,2^{26}$ (the last one partial). The hardest primes lie in
the square classes modulo $840$; the $20$ smallest normalised values are all
$\equiv1\pmod{24}$ except one.
\item Single fixed targets are often empty: the $d=1$ Type~II family
(the single discriminant $-4p$) is empty for $148$--$198$ primes per block
at $2^{24}$--$2^{26}$. Among primes $p\equiv1\pmod{24}$, $p<10^5$, with
$(-d/p)=-1$, Type~I at the single level $d=7$, $11$, $13$ fails for
$42/612$, $23/611$, $88/608$ primes, while $d=5$ never fails. It is an
``identity level'': a classical polynomial covering \cite[\S5.3]{O126}.
Type~II at level $d=1$ fails for $73$ of the $14183$ primes
$p\equiv1\pmod{24}$ below $1.5\cdot10^6$.
\item The slice of \cite[Lemma~1.1]{O127} solves every Mordell-hard prime
below $10^7$, but its count drops to $1$ at $p=5101441$, where
$f_I=188$ and $f_{II}=78$.
\item Type~II solutions near $10^6$ sit at about one per dyadic $(a,c,d)$
block \cite[\S3.1]{RT2}: the mass is spread over $\asymp\cL^3$ small
events, with no large sub-family.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}
The automorphic and geometric objects naturally attached to
\eqref{eq:es} at a fixed prime ($\SL_2$ matrices and Heegner points, $T_p$
translates, shells, quaternion orders, CM tori, an isotropic ternary
quadric, the Brauer class) are exact reformulations. For fixed $p$ each of
them reduces to genus or congruence data plus divisor events of specific
integers at the Planck scale, with polylogarithmic expected counts
(Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}). \status{Assessment:} by these routes and
with known technology, no standard hypothesis is known to suffice. What
remains open is the joint control of a growing family of divisor events at
one prime (H-A, H-B), or a genuinely new object (H-D, H-E). Deterministic covering
and continuous fibring do not open new routes (\S\ref{sec:longshots}). The
Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture remains open.
