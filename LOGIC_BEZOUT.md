**Claim.** There is a countable discretely ordered Bézout domain whose positive cone satisfies IOpen, with a positive infinite prime p≡1 modulo every standard integer, and no positive x,y,z satisfying `4xyz=p(xy+yz+zx)`. In particular, for every standard N, IOpen + Bézout does not prove `∀p (p prime ∧ p≡1 (mod 840) ∧ p>N → ES(p))`. (z74 Opus xreview fix) This is a weak-theory result, not a standard counterexample or any independence claim about PA or ZFC. The following proof does not use genus theory or a hypothesis about the primes. The non-end stages matter: bounded Bézout witnesses cannot be added by end extension (`LOGIC_COLLECTION.md` §1).

### Stage invariant

A is a smooth geometrically integral affine ℚ-algebra of finite type, a UFD, ordered via its fraction field F. Elements of A of finite absolute value (bounded by a standard integer) are precisely ℚ. Hence its only units are ℚ×. There is a rational point e (a homomorphism A→ℚ). Fix p∈A, positive infinite in the order, prime in A, e(p)=1. Require the following compatibility: in `V=(Spec A)(ℝ)∩{p>0}` the semialgebraic connected component of e contains the tautological F-point when interpreted in RC(F). Components of semialgebraic sets defined over ℚ are definable over the real algebraic numbers and commute with real closed extension (equivalently work over ℝ_alg and transfer). All later orders and rational points extend the earlier ones. Start with A=ℚ[X], X positive infinite, p=X+1 and e(X)=0. Then V is the interval X>−1.

### Two ordering devices

For any countable ordered field F (or its countable real closure), a finite τ may be adjoined whose residue in the natural archimedean residue field is transcendental over the old residue field. Indeed that old field embeds in ℝ and is countable. Choose a real transcendental over it and realize its cut between standard rationals in an ordered extension. For any polynomial in τ with coefficients in F, its absolute value is infinite if some coefficient is infinite: divide by a coefficient of maximal absolute value, and reduce the resulting finite coefficients in the residue field. The reduced polynomial is nonzero (one coefficient is 1); it does not vanish at the residue of τ. We will call this the fresh-residue test.

Alternatively adjoin a new positive infinite D with D^k<c for every k≥1 and every old positive infinite c. The inequalities are finitely satisfiable by standard integers; compactness applied to the ordered field diagram gives an extension. For coefficients in F, distinct nonzero Laurent monomials cⱼD^j at distinct exponents are pairwise of distinct orders of magnitude: an old infinite ratio dominates every standard power of D, an old appreciable ratio is bounded above and below in magnitude by positive rationals, and an infinitesimal ratio is the reciprocal case. Thus the largest monomial dominates a finite sum. In particular D is transcendental. This *new smaller growth scale*, not a fixed tiny positive rational exponent at the old scale, is essential.

### F. A floor stage (no splitting)

Given α∈RC(F), if α is already within finite distance of a∈A, there is nothing to do: a−e(a) has evaluation 0 and is still within finite distance. A finite element of any ordered field has a standard integer floor (possibly one smaller than its integral standard part). Otherwise in an extension of RC(F) take b=α+τ, with τ of fresh residue relative to RC(F), and pass to A[b]. This is a polynomial extension, retaining all old primes. To check the finite-element invariant, suppose a polynomial H(b) of degree k>0 over A were bounded. Factor H over RC(F)[i]. No difference b−θ with a root θ can be infinitesimal in complex absolute value: that would make Re(θ)−α finite with residue equal to τ's residue. Hence every factor has absolute value bounded below by a positive standard rational, as does the nonzero leading coefficient. Bounded product now implies each factor and the leading coefficient are finite. The leading coefficient is rational by the invariant, and the average of the roots belongs to A by Viète (divide by k times that rational). The average is within finite distance of b, hence of α, a contradiction. This also shows transcendence (alternatively τ is transcendental over RC(F)). A shorter independent check: expand H(α+τ) in τ over RC(F). If H(b) is finite then the fresh-residue test bounds every coefficient. Thus aₖ∈ℚ× and `k aₖ α+aₖ₋₁` is bounded, making α finitely close to `−aₖ₋₁/(k aₖ)∈A`. Here aⱼ are the coefficients of H in the original variable, and the degree-one case includes the constant Taylor coefficient. Choose e(b)=0. The real space is the old V times a line, so component compatibility persists. The new b is within finite distance of α. The root-factorisation proof above is Berarducci–Otero, JSL 61 (1996), Lemma 2.6 (attributed there to Wilkie): for a ℤ-ring L and β at infinite distance from L and not infinitely close to AC(L), L[β] is discretely ordered. Here A is a ℚ-algebra, and the conclusion used, “bounded elements are exactly ℚ”, is proved directly above. The fresh transcendental constant mirrors their construction §4, `β_(n+1)=p_(n+1)(x)+π_(n+1)`, with a real-closure element α in place of their Puiseux polynomial `p_(n+1)(x)`. ### B. Forcing a unimodular pair away from the landing point

Let g,h∈A be nonzero and coprime in the UFD, with `(g,h)` a proper ideal, not both zero at e. Pass to

`B=A[U,V]/(gU+hV−1)`, ordered by `U=hτ, V=1/h−gτ` for a fresh-residue τ. The generic-fibre parametrization is injective on B: its kernel over F is the given linear polynomial, primitive over the UFD A, so Gauss's lemma gives exactly the indicated kernel over A.

**No bounded pollution.** Choose a representative H(U,V) of smallest total degree k for an element of B. If k>0 its homogeneous top part Hₖ has `Hₖ(h,−g)≠0`: otherwise over F it is divisible by gU+hV, and so over A by Gauss's lemma, since g,h are coprime. Subtract the appropriate multiple of gU+hV−1 to lower the degree, a contradiction. Thus the coefficient of τ^k is nonzero and lies in `(g,h)^k`, a proper ideal, so it is not a nonzero rational. It is infinite by the A-invariant. The fresh-residue test makes H infinite regardless of the sizes of its other coefficients in F. For k=0 the original invariant applies.

**Independent simpler ordering (audit).** Instead parametrize by `U=S`, `V=(1−gS)/h`, with S greater than every old field element (rational-function order at +∞). The same primitive-relation argument embeds B in F[S]. Every positive-degree S-polynomial over F is infinite: its leading term dominates all lower terms and all old constants. Degree zero is safe because B∩F lies in both `A[1/h][U]∩F=A[1/h]` and `A[1/g][V]∩F=A[1/g]`; U and V are each transcendental over F. The intersection is A by UFD coprimality. Thus this second ordering works independently of the top-homogeneous-form argument, even without requiring the ideal proper. Taking S merely larger than all standard integers, rather than all old field elements, would not justify this proof.

**Geometry and factorization.** The map of spectra is an affine-line bundle over `Spec A` minus `V(g,h)` (trivial on the opens g≠0 and h≠0). Thus the total space is smooth and geometrically integral. For any old prime element q the base change to A/q is again an affine-line bundle over a nonempty open in the integral base: q cannot divide both g,h. Hence B/qB is a nonzero domain, so q stays prime. Localizing at h gives A[1/h][U], a UFD. Each old prime factor of h stays prime, so Nagata's localization criterion for factoriality makes B a UFD (B is Noetherian). Choose a rational pair over e solving e(g)U+e(h)V=1.

**Real component.** Removing the common zeros of g,h from the component of e in V cannot disconnect it: a coprime pair in a UFD has common zero locus of algebraic codimension ≥2, hence its real locus has semialgebraic dimension ≤dim V−2. Here the old component is an open smooth manifold. Algebraic codimension persists on scalar extension to ℝ (or use dimension of fibres under scalar extension). A closed semialgebraic subset of codimension ≥2 does not disconnect a connected smooth semialgebraic manifold: triangulate compatibly and connect points by a piecewise path missing the codimension-two skeleton, equivalently by a transverse path to a finite smooth stratification. In dimensions ≤1 the removed locus is empty. Over this remaining component the real bundle is even trivial, with section `(g,h)/(g²+h²)` and fibre direction `(h,−g)`. So e lifts into the same semialgebraic component as the ordered point. This argument uses **all** real points with p>0 in that component, not a fixed curve which might hit a simultaneous zero.

### D. When g(e)=h(e)=0: a controlled split

B alone would discard the landing point. Instead put 𝔪=ker e. At the new smaller scale D described above form the finite-type domain

`C=A[D,𝔪/D] ⊂ A[D,D⁻¹]`.

Its coefficient at D^(−j), j>0, lies in 𝔪^j, and all nonnegative-degree coefficients lie in A (these conditions characterize the algebra). Thus a nonzero negative-degree coefficient is an infinite old element; its term is still infinite even after division by D^j. Nonzero positive-degree coefficients also give infinite terms, and the constant term is rational or infinite. Dominance of the largest Laurent monomial proves the finite-element invariant.

**Special fibre and transforms.** `C/(D)=gr_𝔪 A`: homogeneous degree j is 𝔪^j/𝔪^(j+1) via division by D^j. This is a polynomial ring over ℚ on the cotangent space: A_𝔪 is regular local at the rational smooth point, and localization does not change these graded pieces. In particular D is prime, and C[1/D]=A[D,D⁻¹] is factorial, hence so is C by Nagata. C is smooth and geometrically integral: flat over ℚ[D] (torsion free over a PID), its nonzero geometric fibres are copies of the old smooth variety and its zero fibre the affine tangent space; use the flat, finitely presented, geometrically smooth fibres criterion. Geometric integrality also follows by embedding after scalar extension in the geometrically integral Laurent ring. For nonzero old q the order s=ord_𝔪(q) is finite by the regular local intersection theorem. If q is old prime, its only new factors are D^s and a single prime q/D^s: after inversion of D it is still prime, and q/D^s is not divisible by D, as its initial form is nonzero. This applies even if q vanishes to high order at e. In particular p stays prime (s=0). Independent quotient check: p−1∈𝔪, so `D·((1−p)/D)=1−p` makes D invertible modulo p. Therefore `C/pC=(A/pA)[D,D⁻¹]` under localization, a nonzero domain.

**Choosing the new rational point.** For coprime g,h both vanishing at e, take k=min(ord_𝔪 g,ord_𝔪 h)>0. Then G=g/D^k and H=h/D^k in C are coprime: no old common factor off D, and at least one is not divisible by D. Choose a rational tangent vector at e where a nonzero degree-k initial form from the pair is nonzero. It defines e′ on the zero fibre, extending e, with G,H not both zero there. Now apply B to G,H if necessary; this principalizes (g,h), generated by D^k after the operation. For arbitrary nonzero pairs in A first divide by their old polynomial/UFD gcd and apply this rule to the cofactors. If their ideal is already the unit ideal, no operation is required.

**Real component through that vector.** Over D>0 the real space over the old component W of e in {p>0} is W×(0,∞), connected. Its closure in {p>0} contains the chosen special-fibre vector v. For detail, represent v by a tangent derivation at e and choose a real smooth curve c(d) through e with that derivative (local coordinates at the smooth point), inside W for small d. For d>0 lift (c(d),D=d). On generators a∈A, D, and y/D for a finite set of generators y of 𝔪, the limits are respectively e(a),0, and dy_e(v); these are precisely the point of gr_𝔪 A specified by v. Hence e′ lies in the same component as the D>0 component. The component assertion transfers to the ordered point since D>0 there and its old point is in W. There is no requirement that a single old analytic path have a prescribed tangent vector.

### The union, its order, and ES

Form a countable increasing chain, dovetailing principalization of every pair of stage elements and floor requests for every element of the ordered real closure of each stage fraction field. Ordered real closures embed compatibly; each is countable. An individual task takes finitely many stages, and old principality of a generated ideal persists. Use e for the compatible rational evaluations on the union A∞ and put **R=e⁻¹(ℤ)** there. Zero inputs are already principal. For nonzero inputs a,b at any stage, the task uses their gcd **at the time of service**, not its factorization at the time of enumeration. Its finite output is an identity exhibiting the generator as a linear combination and explicit divisibility of both inputs; these persist under every later inclusion, even when the generator later splits. The union A∞ is Bézout by the pair tasks, **not asserted to be factorial**: successive controlled splits need not terminate for every old irreducible. This bypasses the finite-splitting requirement of the external-UFD approach. The old p, exceptionally, is provably prime and nonunit at every step and hence in the union. All finite elements of A∞ still lie in ℚ, so R is discrete. Clearing the rational denominator of e(a) shows Frac(R)=Frac(A∞). Each real algebraic element of this field's ordered real closure appears over a finite stage and has an A∞-approximant within finite distance by the F tasks. Subtract its rational evaluation to get an R-approximant, then shift by a standard integer to give a floor. Shepherdson's criterion proves full open induction.

For Bézout in R, given f,g∈R not both zero use h generating (f,g) in A∞ and write f=hf′,g=hg′. Choose U,V∈A∞ with Uf′+Vg′=1. The rational numbers e(f′),e(g′) generate a nonzero cyclic ℤ-module γℤ with γ>0. Write γ=u₀e(f′)+v₀e(g′), u₀,v₀∈ℤ. Then `e(γh)=e(u₀f+v₀g)∈ℤ`, hence γh∈R, and f′/γ,g′/γ∈R. With δ=γ−u₀f′−v₀g′, the identity `(u₀+δU)f+(v₀+δV)g=γh` has coefficients in R since e(δ)=0. This is the desired Bézout gcd. For the semiring (nonnegative-coefficient) form, take `f,g>0` and write `d=±γh=if+jg` with the sign chosen so that `d>0` (negating `u₀+δU,v₀+δV` if needed); with `k=|i|+1`, `d=(i+kg)f−(kf−j)g`, where `i+kg≥1`, so `(i+kg)f≥f≥d` and `kf−j≥0`. (z74 Opus xreview fix) Moreover p is prime in R since division by p in A∞ preserves integral evaluation when e(p)=1. It exceeds all numerals and `(p−1)/m∈R` for each standard m.

Finally if X,Y,Z∈R are a positive ES triple for p, all occur at a common stage. On V of that stage the identity holds. The strictly positive sector of these three functions is both open and closed there: at a limit with p>0 the three positive reciprocals sum to 4/p and remain bounded individually, excluding any zero coordinate. By the component invariant positivity at the ordered point implies positivity at e. Their integer evaluations at e would solve `4=1/e(X)+1/e(Y)+1/e(Z)≤3`. No reduction to a (★) subfamily is used. This proves the claim.

### Relevant comparisons

* This is not an external-UFD integer-landing construction. Every nonzero element of the landing kernel in R is divisible by all standard integers. Inside A∞ the exceptional D is allowed to split old prime elements with positive landing order. Inside R, the final Bézout descent uses pair principalization, not a maximal-ideal argument with finite factorization. The other model in `LOGIC_COLLECTION.md` §2 has finite divisor sets; do not transfer that assertion here.
* Full IOpen and Bézout alone do not yield the bounded primary extraction principle for 2 in this model. For example, assume X=p−1>0 split as X=t z with z odd and t 2-primary (every positive divisor >1 even). Then e(z) is odd, so e(t)=0, implying 3 divides t in R — impossible. No Wilmers theorem or counting principle is used. This locates a concrete additional bounded valuation axiom that defeats this model.
* The proof does not establish IE₁ or IΔ₀, TS, unbounded primes, or ordinary collection for this particular model. `LOGIC_COLLECTION.md` gives a separate model of full collection with IOpen. All results are about nonstandard elements and do not decide ES in ℕ, PA or ZFC.
