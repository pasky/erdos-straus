**Result.** Every positive polynomial ES triple at `P=X²+Y²` in `ℚ[X,Y]` belongs to `ℚ[P]`. The descent persists through the floor and Bézout row stages, and also through the NR stage below. Consequently there exists a nonstandard external PID satisfying IOpen and **universal NR** with an infinite prime `P≡1 mod 840` represented by two squares but failing ES. This is an existence result, not a property of arbitrary IOpen + Bézout models, nor a claim about ordinary primes. Universal TS is not asserted.

## 1. Rigidity along a line

Over a real closed field let three polynomials satisfy the cleared reciprocal identity with constant right side `c>0`, and be simultaneously positive somewhere. Their positive sector is semialgebraically open and closed on the line: on it each coordinate is ≥`1/c`, so the bound persists at every boundary point. Thus they are positive everywhere. At `+∞` every nonconstant coordinate tends to `+∞`. The reciprocals of the constant coordinates alone must therefore sum to `c`; any remaining positive term is impossible. These limits use only polynomial degrees and work over nonarchimedean real closed fields. One can take the real closure of the **larger** ordered field, treating the old coefficients as constants and using the ordered new parameter itself as the initial point.

Hence at an old positive `n`, every positive ES triple descends through a polynomial-variable adjunction. For `E=A[U,V]/(uU+vV−1)` with coprime entries in a UFD, the generic fiber is also a polynomial line over `Frac(A)`. Rigidity places each coordinate in `Frac(A)`; the two charts put it in `A[1/u]∩A[1/v]=A`. Unit and zero entries reduce to a polynomial stage. No choice of section or global smoothness hypothesis is needed.

## 2. The quadratic calculation

Take `P=X²+Y²` (binary), or `P=T²+1` (unary). Positivity at the ordered generic point implies positivity at some ordinary real point by real closed field transfer. The same clopen argument with lower bound `P/4` gives positivity everywhere on `{P>0}`, which is the connected punctured plane or the entire line, respectively. On a generic ray at `+∞` every denominator has its total degree as its growth exponent and positive leading value. The sum of reciprocals has order `−2`, so the minimum degree is 2. Choose such a denominator `x`. In the binary case `x≥P/4` gives a positive definite quadratic leading form, so `x` cannot factor into real affine linear forms. In the unary case `x` has no real root. Thus `x` and `P` are irreducibles over ℚ, possibly associates. The gap `D=4x−P` is strictly positive wherever `P>0`, hence nonnegative everywhere. It is a positive constant or quadratic, never linear; it need not be assumed irreducible or nonvanishing at the origin.

Write `d=Dy−Px`, `d'=Dz−Px`. Then `dd'=(Px)²`, with both factors positive on `{P>0}` (indeed `dz=Pxy`). If `x` is proportional to `P`, both factors are scalar powers of `P`, so all coordinates descend. Otherwise `d=λP^i x^j`, `0≤i,j≤2`, `λ>0` rational. Whenever `x∈ℚ[P]`, this formula and `y=(d+Px)/D` give descent, since `ℚ(P)∩ℚ[X,Y]=ℚ[P]` (likewise in the unary ring): coprime univariate numerator and denominator remain comaximal after substitution, and a nonconstant denominator remains a nonunit. In particular a constant gap gives descent.

For quadratic `D`, `P≡4x mod D` and `gcd(D,x)=1`. Swap `d,d'` so `i+j≤2`. If `i+j=2`, then `D` divides `(λ4^i+4)x²`, impossible. If `i+j=1`, cancellation gives `D | 4x+λ4^i`. Equal degrees make the quotient constant, so `4x+λ4^i=b(4x−P)`, with `b≠1` since `P` is nonconstant. This gives descent. Cancellation uses UFD divisibility, **not** that `x` is invertible modulo a multivariate `D`.

Only `d=λ` remains. Exactly in this case

`D(4y−P)=P²+4λ`,  `x=(P+D)/4`,  `z=Pxy/λ`.

For binary `P`, over ℂ the right side has exactly the two conjugate irreducible factors `X²+Y²±2i√λ`: in coordinates `X±iY` these are `UV±2i√λ`. There is no proper real factor, contradicting quadratic `D`. This proves binary descent even if the original gap vanishes at the origin.

For unary `P`, the complete non-descending list takes `D=h(T²+kT+u)` and `4y−P=h⁻¹(T²−kT+u)`, with `h>0`, `k≠0` rational, `u=1+k²/2`, `4λ=u²−1`. Indeed comparing cubic and linear terms of the monic quartic gives opposite linear coefficients and, unless both are zero, equal constant coefficients. The zero-linear case would factor `(S+1)²+4λ` over ℝ as a product of real linear factors in `S=T²`. Coefficient comparison gives the asserted `u,λ`; conversely the two quadratics are strictly positive, and the displayed formulas give an identity. This recovers menu C of `Z52D_THINFAMILY.md` by a divisor calculation; the sign and degree argument closes the reducible-smallest-coordinate issue here without ET parameter bookkeeping (compare `Z53_THIN15.md`, §3.3, for `15T²+1` and different marks).

## 3. A stage for universal NR

Use the rational floor/row tower and adelic PID descent of `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md`, §§1–2, starting instead with `ℚ[X,Y]` (`X` above ℚ, `Y` above `ℚ(X)` in the order). Dovetail an additional task for **each positive stage element** `s`. Adjoin

`B=A[D,V,H]/(DH−V²−s)`, with `D` positive above the old field and `V` positive above `Frac(A)(D)` (above means larger than every element).

* Algebra: substitute `H=(V²+s)/D`. For every coefficient domain, including `A/(r)` for each old prime `r`, this presents an embedded algebra in the Laurent polynomial chart: reduction modulo `D` shows `(DH−V²−s)` is saturated at `D`, since the monic `V²+s` remains nonzero. Thus old primes stay nonunit prime. Also `B/D=(A[V]/(V²+s))[H]` is a domain since `s>0` in the old ordered field and the quadratic is monic. Inverting the prime `D` gives a factorial chart, hence `B` is factorial by Nagata. In particular `D` is coprime to `s` (`s≠0` stays nonzero modulo `D`).
* Intersection and order: in the Laurent expansion in `D` the coefficient at `D^(−j)` belongs to `(V²+s)^j A[V]` for `j>0`, while all coefficients belong to `A[V]`. Therefore `B∩Frac(A)(D)=A[D]`: if independent of `V`, no negative coefficient can survive. A term of positive `V`-degree makes the whole element infinite at the chosen order, and a nonconstant polynomial in `D` over `A` is infinite as well. The bounded elements remain ℚ; the units remain rational (a unit and its inverse in the Laurent chart are old units times powers of `D`, and `D` is not invertible).
* ES descent: apply §1 over `Frac(A)(D)` on the `V`-line for an old positive `n`. All three coordinates belong to `A[D]` by the intersection just proved. Apply §1 on the **whole** `D`-line to get them in `A`. Using just the Laurent chart for the second step would be invalid. These checks do not require the NR stage to be smooth.

The union `A∞` has finite permanent prime factorizations, serviced Bézout rows and bounded-distance approximations as in Z73; the floor and row proofs require no smoothness for these algebraic and order conclusions. Its fraction field is purely transcendental on the two initial variables and the free parameters (including each new `D,V`).

## 4. Unary one-prime test (independent of the construction)

In fact the non-descending list has **zero** caught mass, not merely small mass, at the proposed test profiles. Let `ℓ≡3 mod 4`, `T_q=0` for `q≠ℓ` and `T_ℓ∈ℤ_ℓ`; integrality at a profile means integrality of all three evaluated denominators. Then `λ=4xy−P(x+y)` is a positive integer (locally integral everywhere), and `v_ℓ(λ)` is even since `λ=(k/4)²(k²+4)` and `−1` is nonsquare mod `ℓ`. Choose an ordinary integer `t` close enough at the finitely many coefficient-denominator primes for integral values, also so `n=t²+1≡1 mod 8` and `n≡1 mod q` for all odd `q|λ`, `q≠ℓ`. These constraints are compatible at the marks; `n` is automatically an `ℓ`-unit. Thus `gcd(n,λ)=1`.

Here is the menu C reciprocity check (cf. Z52D Prop. 3). At these integer values put `g=gcd(x,y)`, `x=ga`, `y=gb`, `w=4gab−n(a+b)`, so `λ=gw` and `z=ngab/w`. Then `gcd(n,xy)=1` since any common prime would divide `λ`. Reduction of `w` modulo `a,b,n` gives `gcd(w,nab)=1` (use `n` odd), whence `w|g`. Write `e=g/w=2^j e₀` with `e₀` odd, so `λ=w²e`. The positive odd `f=4x−n≡3 mod 4` divides `n²+4λ` and is coprime to `nλ`. Thus the Jacobi symbol `(−e/f)=1`. On the other hand reciprocity gives `(−e/f)=−(n/e₀)`: `f≡−n mod e₀` since `e|x`; if `j>0` then `f≡7 mod 8`, so `(2/f)=1`. But `(n/e₀)=1`, since `n≡1` at its odd prime divisors except possibly `ℓ`, whose exponent in `e` is even. Contradiction. The same proof permits square-unit `P` marks off `ℓ` (choose `n` square at these primes); `ℓ` need not have square `P` mark.
