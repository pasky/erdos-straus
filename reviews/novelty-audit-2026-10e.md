## 1. Relaxed third denominator, self-generated mute sets, pointwise caps ((J)64, (J)66)

### 1.1 Findings

* **Dahan, "Sieve dimension and search depth for the Erdős–Straus conjecture,
  n ≡ 1 (mod 24)", arXiv:2608.24035v1 (25 Aug 2026)** [visited; already archived as
  `sources/dahan-2608.24035v1.pdf`, text extracted in this pass].
  * **Prop 4.8 (§4.2.2, p. 22; standing hypothesis n ≡ 1 (24) prime):** for c ≡ 3 (mod 4), K_c = (n + c)/4 and
    H_c(n) = ⟨q mod c : q prime, q | K_c⟩ ≤ (ℤ/cℤ)^×: "If the untwisted branch succeeds at
    shift c, then −1 ∈ H_c(n). If the twisted branch succeeds at shift c, then
    −n ∈ H_c(n)." Thm 4.6 is the special case H_c(n) ⊆ ker (p/·) for a prime p | c,
    p ≡ 3 (4) (Thm 4.6, p. 21): a successful window needs a prime of K_c that is a non-residue mod p.
  * §1.2 (p. 2) and §4.1 (p. 19): Dahan explicitly *avoids* "the subgroup generated by the
    prime factors" because membership of −1 in it does not give a divisor ≡ −1 (bounded
    exponents): "That route is blocked as soon as (ℤ/4m)^× has exponent greater than 2".
  * **Bearing on (J)64 Thm 1.1.** Dahan Prop 4.8 is exactly the *necessary* direction
    (window succeeds ⇒ −1 ∈ H). (J)64 Thm 1.1 is the converse in the relaxed problem:
    −1 ∈ H_q(n) (forced by reciprocity when q is a prime ≡ 3 (4) with (q/n) = −1) yields
    an ES^{(y,e)} solution, the exponent overflow being the cap. The gap Dahan identifies
    (subgroup membership vs an actual divisor with bounded exponents) is precisely what the
    cap e measures (Lemma 3.1 of (J)64). → **PARTIAL**: mechanism (window K = (n + q)/4,
    subgroup of prime residues, quadratic-character obstruction) is in Dahan 2026; the
    converse construction in the capped variant and the effective bounds are not.
