### prob_lll_fixed_p.md

**Verdict-validity: DISAGREE** with the universal collapse, although the Haar/prime-average warnings are sound. Lemma 3.1 correctly limits what a measure bound *alone* says about a designated point; it does not exclude probabilistic existence over solver choices. Proposition 3.2(a) and Remark 3.3 falsely assert that fixed-p choices cannot carry product randomness and that dependency inequalities require monotone events. Take Θ = {0,1}⁶ and bad events Bᵢ = {θ₂ᵢ₋₁ = θ₂ᵢ}: these nonmonotone events are independent, and LLL gives avoidance probability 1/8, confirmed by `scripts/triage_prob.py`. Determinism of each candidate's outcome is no obstruction; arithmetic-specific probability estimates remain missing. Crucially, `redteam-automorphic-2.md` Thm 2.2(iii) excludes only parameter sets fixed independently of p, conditionally on H; §3 H-A explicitly leaves growing families open. **Strongest open sub-question:** can independent solver coordinates at fixed prime p yield bad events whose avoidance implies an ES solution and whose probabilities satisfy P(Bᵢ) ≤ xᵢ∏ⱼ∼ᵢ(1−xⱼ), with 0 < xᵢ < 1, uniformly for large primes? **By-product novelty:** the PROVED atom lemma is known elementary measure theory; Proposition 3.2's finite-Haar counting observation is folklore, but its claimed exhaustive impossibility theorem is false. No ES-specific dependency construction is supplied. **Promise: 2/5.**

### prob_lottery_model.md

**Verdict-validity: PARTIAL.** The simulations do not certify a single prime, but §§3.5–3.6 do not exclude rigidity or joint-type positivity. A count uniformly distributed on {1,11} has variance/mean = 25/6 yet never vanishes; setting both type counts equal also gives perfect positive correlation without excluding a common positive floor. `scripts/triage_prob.py` verifies this counterexample to the inference, not to the reported data. There is also an exact counting misstatement in §3.1: Xₑ must impose the counter's gcd(a,c)=1 normalization. At p=29, e=3, Nₑ=8, raw factorization counting gives six ordered points but only four primitive ones; (a,b,c,d)=(2,4,2,1) duplicates (1,2,1,4), both yielding (8,116,232). The script verifies these identities and the genuine empty slice at p=73, e=3. The bulk log reproduces the quoted κ and tails, but calibration is in-sample, and neither square-class saturation from twelve primes nor the infinite-tail extrapolation is established. **Strongest open sub-question:** prove `Σ_{e>256} X_e^prim(p) ≥ 1` for every sufficiently large prime using growing-family arithmetic, rather than an average across primes; H-A remains open. **By-product novelty:** no PROVED/CERTIFIED outputs; the five empirical findings are plausibly new dataset-specific observations, with items 3–4 remaining heuristic and item 5 not proving absence of complementarity. **Promise: 1/5.**

### rational_to_integral_cubic.md

**Verdict-validity: DISAGREE** with the blanket collapse; several natural instantiations genuinely fail, but M3/M4 overreach. M3(b)'s claim that isotropic forms are never Euclidean under its stated inequality is false: q(x,y)=xy and coordinatewise nearest-integer rounding give |q(x−a,y−b)| ≤ 1/4. `scripts/triage_prob.py` confirms this on rational grids. Anisotropy can still matter because q(error)=0 obstructs the descent formula; the displayed inequality itself is not the obstruction. M4 invokes `LONGSHOT_FIBRED.md` Cor. 2.4 without establishing its geometrically irreducible fibres and Zariski-dense integral solver subsets. Bounded algebraic complexity alone is insufficient. Nor does unbounded composition imply monotone multiplicative height growth: secant composition with a fixed point is generically involutive, so repeating it returns the original point. The asserted polylogarithmic output bound is unsupported. **Strongest open sub-question:** is there a positivity-preserving secant/rounding descent on Uₚ(ℚ) with strictly decreasing common denominator D until D=1, uniformly for primes p? **By-product novelty:** M2's PROVED reduction is probably folklore: for odd p the special fibre is three coordinate planes and type strata have the stated dimensions. But the vertex is not empty as a scheme—it contains (0,0,0); only reductions of positive global ES(p) points avoid it. The square extension needs care: ES(9) has (3,12,36), reducing to that vertex modulo 3. **Promise: 2/5.**
