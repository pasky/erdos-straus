## Remaining defect

### MEDIUM — §77.7 confuses the prime supports of L and L/4

**Quote, `notes.md:31197–31202`:** “For p=4t+1≡1 (mod L) and a modulus q=4s−1 dividing L, both targets are −1 and the prime factors of t+s dividing L/4 are those of s (with the valuation proviso of §77.1), which never produce −1; so at such moduli the hit must come from prime factors of t+s coprime to L …”.

Take

    p=13, L=12, t=3, s=1, q=3, x=t+s=4.

All stated hypotheses hold: p is prime and 1 mod 4, p≡1 mod L, and q|L. Both targets are −1 mod 3. Yet

    Rat_3(4) = {1,2},

and u/v=2/1 hits −1. The only prime factor of x is 2, which divides L. No prime factor coprime to L is present. Formula (77.2) gives the genuine solution

    4/13 = 1/4 + 1/26 + 1/52.

The error is the passage from L/4 to L: when v₂(L)=2 their prime supports differ. The section's stated standing assumption is p≡1 mod 4, not p≡1 mod 24, and no assumption 8|L appears in this paragraph.

**Repair:** either require 8|L explicitly (one may choose L divisible by 24 for the intended hard-class discussion), or replace “coprime to L” by “coprime to L/4” and adjust the following interpretation. The self-prime exclusion itself need not be discarded: for every prime r|s and q=4s−1, Jacobi reciprocity gives (r/q)=1, whereas (−1/q)=−1. Thus even increased exponent budgets supported on those primes cannot produce −1. The counterexample exploits the missing prime 2, not a failure of this character argument.
