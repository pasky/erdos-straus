# Independent adversarial check: the norm stage

Reviewed `LOGIC_NORM_SPLIT.md` at logic-lift commit `88bc21143`. This is a check of **its absolute, countable** construction over ℚ, not an additional assertion of collection for that model. Verdict: no fatal error found. The following are the points where weakening the hypotheses or swapping the order of steps would break the proof.

1. **Gaussian alternative.** For A factorial over both ℚ and ℚ(i) in the sense of the note, and s prime in A, the ring (A/s)[i] injects in its generic quadratic algebra (free rank two). In the split case the two evaluation kernels at the two generic roots of −1 are distinct minimal primes: their intersection is zero and they become the two factors upon localization. Thus A[i]/s is reduced, and unique factorization gives exactly two conjugate irreducible factors with exponent one. Units in A[i] are constant: the norm of a unit is a unit of A, hence constant, and its real and imaginary coordinates must be bounded. For e(s)=1 the scalar ambiguity disappears on dividing one factor by its nonzero Gaussian rational evaluation. This gives coordinates landing at 1 and 0, not just an identity over Frac(A).
2. **Saturation even when the conic splits.** In E[X,Y,W], E a domain, H=X²+Y², q₀ nonzero in E, if Hf=(HW−q₀)g then H divides q₀g. Since E[Y,W][X]/H is free over E[Y,W] by monicity, q₀ acts injectively there. Hence H divides g, and cancellation shows saturation. This proves the localization embedding without treating H as prime over every E. It applies to all scalar extensions of the old geometrically integral algebra and to E=A/s for **untargeted** primes. At the target q the coefficient becomes zero, and that argument would fail: splitting q=HW there is intentional.
3. **UFD and protected divisor.** For E=A/q the hypothesis i∉Frac(E) is essential. It makes X²+Y² irreducible over Frac(E)(Y), since −1 is still nonsquare in a purely transcendental extension. Monicity embeds E[X,Y]/H in its scalar extension to Frac(E). Hence B/H is a domain and H is nonunit prime. Nagata applies in the Noetherian domain B by inverting H. Over A[i], the model UVW=q is integral by the saturation check; U,V are prime with domain quotients because A[i]/q is a domain. Inverting both gives a Laurent polynomial UFD. H is positive, e(H)=1, and it splits nontrivially over the Gaussian ring. It can never become associate to another inert target: neither Gaussian norm factor can become a unit since their product is the same nonconstant real element and all Gaussian units are constants. The initial p=x²+y² has the same protection.
4. **Potential bounded ratios.** The order must choose τ with new nonzero transcendental residue and then T slow relative to *all* positive infinite elements of K(τ). For an element P/H^k the homogeneous block of degree d gives P_d(1,τ)T^(d−2k)/(1+τ²)^k. In a homogeneous block the powers of τ index the distinct monomials, so the fresh-residue test does not discard coefficients. One infinite coefficient from A forces its block to be infinite even after division by T^j; distinct nonzero blocks cannot cancel since ratios of old coefficients are either appreciable or dominate/be dominated by every relevant power of T. Thus boundedness forces P rational. This test alone is not enough: e.g. X²/H has exponent zero and is bounded in the ambient *field*. Membership in the actual algebra gives P ≡ a₀ H^k modulo q, from its original polynomial-in-W expression. Monic division in X over ℚ[Y] commutes with the injection into (A/q)[Y]. Thus H^k divides rational P over ℚ, excluding the field-only example. A remaining nonconstant rational polynomial has a positive-degree highest block with appreciable coefficient, so is infinite. This argument need not assume an optimal presentation in W.
5. **Real component.** With q of either sign the open H≠0 is exactly a product over the old base: W=q/H, with no sign restriction on W. Over the old smooth component of p>0 this is a connected smooth subset (product with the punctured real plane) containing the landing and, after interpretation in a real closed extension, the ordered point. The whole variety need not be smooth or its whole real locus connected. Later codimension-two deletions take place on the smooth component, and later deformations use regularity only at the selected landing.
6. **Limit test.** Schedule by old element, but choose the prime divisor at service time. A nonconstant r of landing ±1 has a nonconstant finite-stage prime factor of nonzero rational evaluation. Scaling this factor to evaluation 1 leaves an integral-landing quotient even if its sign is negative. The recorded positive normalized Gaussian norm divisor of r stays prime in A at later steps and divides r in R. If r is positive irreducible, its quotient by that divisor is a positive unit, hence 1. For nonunit integer landing (including zero) a standard prime divides r, since ℓR is exactly the kernel of reduction of e modulo ℓ. This proves PD and the asserted classification of positive nonstandard primes without invoking factorization of a possibly perpetually splitting cofactor. The cofinally scheduled 1+(S′)² are protected by precisely the same argument. E(−1) uses Bézout in R as well as the norm identity.

The cofinality and TS assertions are not bounded facts about old parameters that automatically give the same **global axioms** after an arbitrary end extension: new elements and new primes require their own witnesses. Neither this audit nor the collection theorem alone justifies merging those axiom lists.
