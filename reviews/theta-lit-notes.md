# Literature notes: proposed universal weighted-level sieve lower bound

**Scope/method.** Search of publisher/book metadata, accessible book previews, arXiv papers and papers directly visited below. I did not have full access to Selberg’s collected-paper volume or all of *Opera de Cribro*. This is a literature assessment, not a proof or a claim of exhaustive priority.

## Verdict

**Apparently new in the stated generality (category (c)).** Classical sieve theory gives optimality statements for particular *arithmetic* sieve optimization problems (notably Selberg’s quadratic-form minimization), and classical large-sieve inequalities have denominator \(L\) of the same Euler-product/squarefree-sum shape. Neither is a theorem about every nonnegative function on the Boolean cube whose Fourier/monomial supports form an arbitrary weighted lower set. High-dimensional beta-sieve bounds are likewise for structured sieve weights and dimension hypotheses. Approximate inclusion–exclusion and Christoffel extremal-mass results are structurally adjacent, but I found no source that supplies the proposed weighted lower-set, arbitrary-probabilities assertion or its stated uniform exponent/constants. The quoted theorem’s formulation should be checked carefully (particularly its dependence on \(p_i,\ell_i,\alpha\), the meaning of constants, and possible degenerate levels); nothing located verifies it.

## 1. Selberg, sieve LP and optimality

- Atle Selberg, “Lectures on sieves,” *Proc. Sympos. Pure Math.* 20 (1971), 1–42, AMS; and Selberg, *Collected Papers*, vol. II (1991), Springer, reprint/details not inspected. These are foundational accounts of Selberg’s sieve, but I could not verify an exact theorem/section in either that says every nonnegative level-\(D\) Boolean majorant has the claimed Rankin-functional lower bound. The optimization defining Selberg’s \(\Lambda^2\)-sieve is a quadratic minimization over divisor-supported coefficients; this is not the claimed unrestricted positivity-constrained LP.
- John B. Friedlander and Henryk Iwaniec, *Opera de Cribro*, AMS Colloquium Publications 57, 2010. [AMS book page](https://bookstore.ams.org/coll-57) (visited): contents labels Chapter 7 “Selberg’s sieve—Kvadrater er positive”; publisher synopsis explicitly says it discusses what sieve methods can and cannot do and parity. Accessible chapter-specific secondary discussion: [Maynard, “Counting primes”](https://ems.press/content/book-chapter-files/33121) (search result; not examined closely). The book is the closest comprehensive source for Selberg machinery and classical limitations, but I found no indication of the universal Boolean-majorant bound. Chapter 7’s standard Selberg setup optimizes a quadratic form; Chapters 11/16 are relevant to beta-sieve/parity limitations, not equivalent to this claim.
- Concrete Selberg main-term formula from Maynard–Pratt? More directly, Friedlander–Iwaniec’s formula is quoted and analyzed in B. B. Conrey et al.? Accessible exact statement in [“Selberg’s sieve of irregular density,” arXiv:2206.03479](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.03479v1) (visited), §1, equations (1.6)–(1.10): \(S(\mathcal A,z)\le XJ^{-1}+R(\Delta^2)\), with \(J=\sum_{d\le\Delta,d\mid P(z)}h(d)\), \(h(p)=g(p)/(1-g(p))\); since \(J\le V(z)^{-1}\), the main term is at least \(XV(z)\). The authors say a useful lower bound for truncated \(J\) is difficult without extra assumptions, and point to *Opera* §7.9 for asymptotics under \(\sum_{p\le x}g(p)\log p=\kappa\log x+O(1)\). This supports the Euler-product baseline, not the stronger arbitrary-majorant lower bound.

## 2. Large sieve / larger sieve

- Montgomery’s large sieve inequality has a denominator \(L=\sum_{q\le Q}\mu^2(q)\prod_{p\mid q}\omega(p)/(p-\omega(p))\). Exact accessible citation and statement: Ernest S. Croot III and Christian Elsholtz, “On variants of the larger sieve,” 2003 manuscript, §1, Theorem 1 (quotes Montgomery): \(|A|\le (N+Q^2-1)/L\). [Visited PDF](https://ecroot.math.gatech.edu/gallagher.pdf). It is an upper bound in a residue-class avoidance problem; it does not assert an extremal lower bound for all Boolean upper-bound sieves, nor optimality in the claimed abstract function class.
- Same paper, Theorem 2 quotes Gallagher’s larger sieve; it concerns sets restricted to at most \(\nu(q)\) classes mod prime powers. This is a distinct sieve inequality, not evidence of the stated universal lower bound.
- I found no basis for the claim that the large sieve is generally “essentially optimal” for arbitrary weights. The familiar resemblance of \(L\) to a truncated Euler product / Selberg denominator is a connection between particular arithmetic inequalities, not an implication of the proposed theorem.
- Related inverse large sieve: Ben Green and Julia Wolf? The located source is Ben Green, “Inverse questions for the large sieve,” *Geom. Funct. Anal.* 24 (2014), 163–176, [Springer DOI page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-014-0288-1) (search-result citation; not visited). Examples/classification issues about sets with small large-sieve constant, not upper-bound-sieve majorants.

## 3. Higher-dimensional / beta sieves

- Harold G. Diamond and H. Halberstam, with William F. Galway, *A Higher-Dimensional Sieve Method: With Procedures for Computing Sieve Functions*, Cambridge Tracts in Mathematics 177, CUP, 2008. [Accessible 29-page CUP preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780511434365_A23678332/preview-9780511434365_A23678332.pdf) (visited; contents extracted). The contents identify Ch. 7 as the linear sieve, Ch. 9 as the method for \(K>1\), Ch. 18 as properties of \(F_K,f_K\), and Appendix 1 for computation. These are asymptotic upper/lower sieve-function results under sieve axioms and a fixed dimension parameter; they do not give the asserted all-majorants theorem or establish its large-\(\kappa\) constants.
- Diamond–Halberstam–Richert, “The combinatorial sieve,” *Proc. Sympos. Pure Math.* 24 (1973), 367–374 (citation found in search; no full text inspected); and Diamond–Halberstam, *A Higher-Dimensional Sieve Method* (above) are the relevant standard references. Ankeny–Onishi / Franze citations were not verified sufficiently to make a theorem-level claim.
- Accessible context: [“Selberg’s sieve of irregular density,” §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.03479v1) quotes *Opera* §7.9 on asymptotics for \(J\) under a dimension condition. It also stresses nonuniform/irregular density can make such estimates difficult. This is related classical density analysis, not a positivity extremal theorem.

## 4. Approximate inclusion–exclusion / positive approximate degree

- Nathan Linial and Nati Nisan, “Approximate inclusion-exclusion,” *Combinatorica* 10 (1990), 349–365 (bibliographic result verified via follow-up; original full text not accessed). Jeff Kahn, Nati Linial, and Alex Samorodnitsky, “Inclusion-exclusion: exact and approximate,” *Combinatorica* 16 (1996), 465–477, DOI 10.1007/BF01271266. [Bibliographic page](https://articles.researchsolutions.com/inclusion-exclusion-exact-and-approximate/doi/10.1007/bf01271266) (search result). These investigate how union probability/size is constrained by low-order intersection data and give approximate inclusion–exclusion phenomena. They are pertinent to uniform-degree symmetric settings, not a general weighted lower-set polynomial with arbitrary Bernoulli biases and pointwise nonnegativity.
- Alexander A. Sherstov, “Approximate Inclusion-Exclusion for Arbitrary Symmetric Functions,” [ECCC report PDF](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2007/116/download/) (search result). Relevant approximation/degree bounds for symmetric Boolean functions, but does not appear to imply this weighted-majorant expectation bound.
- Mark Bun, “A Nearly Optimal Lower Bound on the Approximate Degree of AC⁰,” arXiv:1703.05784 (2017), [arXiv page](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.05784) (visited). This is approximate-degree lower bounds, largely via dual polynomials; it is not the one-sided, arbitrary weighted-level extremal inequality here. Kahn–Linial–Samorodnitsky / Galambos–Simonelli Bonferroni optimality may provide unweighted symmetric special cases, but I found no source establishing weighted lower-set version.
- The one-dimensional exchangeable formulation does fit the general *type* of extremal problem for nonnegative polynomials and a fixed moment functional (Christoffel function / extremal mass). I found no directly matching binomial-measure theorem with this statement. Without a verified citation or derivation, do not label the full result an easy consequence.

## 5. Sparse-grid / combination interpolation

- F. J. Delvos, “d-Variate Boolean interpolation,” *Journal of Approximation Theory* 34(2) (1982), 99–114. [ScienceDirect page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0021904582900855) (visited). This is an appropriate citation for Boolean/sparse-grid interpolation operators and tensor-product inclusion-exclusion. The elementary identity that an interpolation combination reproduces a polynomial space indexed by a lower set is the standard polynomial-exactness mechanism. It does **not** establish the proposed probability bound: one still needs the special nodes, positivity/control and quantitative interpolation constants.
- Griebel–Schneider–Zenger (1992) “A combination technique for the solution of sparse grid problems” is a standard combination-technique reference; exact journal metadata not checked here. Delvos is the verified specific citation. Sparse-grid interpolation is therefore a standard ingredient, not a result about sieves.

## 6. Erdős–Straus exceptional sets

- R. C. Vaughan, “On a problem of Erdős and Straus,” *Mathematika* 17 (1970), 193–198 (standard bibliographic citation; not directly accessed). Elsholtz–Tao (2013) concerns the number of representations / exceptional set and is cited in the following paper. Neither located as a discussion of universal sieve-optimality.
- Carl Pomerance and Andreas Weingartner, “Exceptions to the Erdős–Straus–Schinzel conjecture,” arXiv:2511.16817 (2025 preprint; subsequently journal-published in *The Ramanujan Journal*, 2026 per search result). [Visited arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16817v1). Its abstract and §1 report lower bounds for exceptional instances and an upper bound: Theorem 1.3 gives at most \(N/\exp(C(\log^2 N/\varphi(m))^{1/3})\) exceptions in its stated range. This paper leverages/generalizes Elsholtz–Tao and Vaughan but addresses the Diophantine exceptional set, not an abstract upper-bound-sieve limit theorem.

## What would need proving to claim equivalence

A citation to a classical sieve-function lower bound would not suffice: one must reduce every admissible \(f\) (arbitrary real coefficients, only global nonnegativity, weighted monomial support) to the structured divisor weights treated in sieve theory, or prove a separate extremal-polynomial inequality. Likewise sparse-grid interpolation is only an algebraic reconstruction identity; the nontrivial step is a quantitative bound on evaluation at the all-zero corner versus expectation under independent binomial counts. No located source supplies those steps.
