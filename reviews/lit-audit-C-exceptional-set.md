# Literature audit: exceptional sets and the cubic-class mechanism

**As of 2026-10-04.** Scope: published/preprint work on exceptional sets for representations of `m/n` by three unit fractions (especially `m=4,5`), near-all results, and priority for improvements over Vaughan's exponent. This is an external-literature audit; the campaign's `3/4` and `2/3`-with-`log log` notes are internal, not independent literature. A negative search conclusion is necessarily source-bounded.

## Bottom line

I found **no independent known improvement** of Vaughan's `2/3` exceptional-set exponent for `m=4` (nor a stronger `m=5`/general-`m` exceptional-set theorem specializing to `m=4`). Vaughan's 1970 bound remains the pre-campaign benchmark in the accessible literature. Pomerance–Weingartner (PW), *Exceptions to the Erdős–Straus–Schinzel conjecture*, arXiv:2511.16817v2, explicitly restates Vaughan's `N exp(-c(log N)^(2/3))` estimate and its Theorem 1.3 gives the corresponding uniform-in-`m` bound; at fixed `m=4` or `5` its exponent is still `2/3` (Introduction and Theorem 1.3, p. 2). Its introduction says the exceptional-number bound had been “strongly improved, though not recently” before naming Vaughan's 1970 result (p. 2). This is strong secondary evidence, not a complete priority guarantee.

The listed “almost all” results principally count **many solutions on average**, or say that a density-one set has many solutions; they do not bound the zero-solution exceptional set at a rate beating Vaughan. Elsholtz–Tao's cubic average of solution counts and the `log log` caveat concern a different statistic. Their Remark 1.2 gives a Borel–Cantelli heuristic for finitely many exceptions, explicitly not a proof.

The multiplier identity, forced-class cubic mass, factorial-moment/Bonferroni assembly, and `B/(B+1)` heuristic were **not found stated in prior literature in the note's combined form**. The ingredients have close precedents: Vaughan/PW use one-family (`B=2`) large-sieve class mass and a `2/3` optimization; Elsholtz–Tao (ET) establish cubic-order average solution mass. No source located says “take `B=3`, hence `3/4`,” or explicitly asserts Vaughan's `2/3` can be improved by this route. This is a “not found” assessment, not a novelty certification.

## Source-exact bounds and nearby results

1. **Vaughan (1970).** R. C. Vaughan, “On a problem of Erdős, Straus and Schinzel,” *Mathematika* 17 (1970), 193–198, DOI [10.1112/S0025579300002886](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300002886). Exact primary-source theorem/page was not independently checked: no lawful full text was obtained (see access record). ET, Introduction p. 3 of arXiv:1107.1010v6, attributes to Vaughan: for `f(n)=0`, `#{n<N:f(n)=0} ≤ N exp(-c(log N)^(2/3))`, absolute `c>0`, all sufficiently large `N`. PW, Introduction p. 2, gives the same bound. PW §4 (pp. 8–11) reconstructs the large-sieve route: for each auxiliary prime there are `f(p)` good residue classes; Lemma 4.1 gives aggregate mass of order `(log x)^2/φ(m)`; simultaneous avoidance is bounded by the large sieve.

2. **Pomerance–Weingartner (2025), general ESS.** Theorem 1.3, p. 2: for `4≤m≤(log N)^2`, the number of `n≤N` for which `m/n` is not a sum of three unit fractions is
   `≤ N exp(-C (log N)^(2/3)/φ(m)^(1/3))`, for an absolute positive `C`. Hence for fixed `m=4` or `5` this has `2/3`, not a larger exponent. This is the strongest explicit-in-`m` all-denominator estimate found. It does not improve the fixed-`m=4` exponent. Their Theorem 1.1/3.1 are **lower** results: exceptional `n`/primes exist at large scales for large `m`; they are not upper bounds at `m=4`. Theorem 1.2 is likewise a large-`m` exceptional-prime construction, not an improvement to the `m=4` exceptional count.

3. **Elsholtz–Tao (2013).** C. Elsholtz and T. Tao, “Counting the number of solutions to the Erdős–Straus equation on unit fractions,” *J. Aust. Math. Soc.* 94 (2013), 50–105, arXiv:1107.1010v6. Introduction p. 3 states the same Vaughan exceptional-set estimate and says compare references [48], [84], [39], [89] “for some weaker results.” Theorem 1.1, pp. 3–4, proves `N(log N)^2 ≪ Σ_{p≤N} f(p) ≪ N(log N)^2 log log N` (with component Type-I/II estimates). It yields a density-one upper bound `f(p)=O_ε((log p)^3 log log p)`, not a bound on primes with `f(p)=0`. Remark 1.2, p. 5, models `f(p)` as Poisson with intensity at least `c(log p)^3` and says Borel–Cantelli “suggests” finitely many exceptions, explicitly calling this heuristic and not a rigorous argument; it calls the large-sieve results of Vaughan and ET a rigorous analogue. The introductory discussion says the `log log N` in its average upper bound is a technical Brun–Titchmarsh limitation and conjectures it “should be eliminated” (p. 5)—this is **not** a statement about improving Vaughan's exceptional-set exponent.

4. **Elsholtz–Planitzer (2020; arXiv:1805.02945v1).** “The number of solutions of the Erdős–Straus equation and sums of k unit fractions,” *Proceedings of the Royal Society of Edinburgh: Section A Mathematics* 150 (2020), 1401–1427. Theorem 1, p. 2: for fixed `m`, the number of representations `m/n` by three unit fractions is `O_ε(n^(3/5+ε) m^(2/5))`; Corollary 1 gives `O_ε(n^(3/5+ε))` for `4/n`. The paper concerns representation counts and algorithmic enumeration, not zero-solution exceptional-set savings. The survey-style contextual citation says ET treated `m=4` prime denominators, again not an exceptional-set improvement.

5. **Earlier results named in ET.** ET’s Introduction p. 3 calls the results of Nakayama (1939), Webb (1970), Li (1981), and related sources **weaker** exceptional-set results than Vaughan. This is ET's secondary characterization; I did not access those primary papers to verify their exact constants/rates or theorem pages. ET bibliography [39] identifies Delang Li, “On the Equation `4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z`,” *J. Number Theory* 13 (1981), 485–494; [84] identifies W. Webb, “On `4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z`,” *Proc. Amer. Math. Soc.* 25 (1970), 578–584. Both are explicitly grouped among weaker results in ET's citation sentence.

6. **Other items in the brief / bibliography.** ET §1.4, p. 8, cites earlier work of Viola, Shen Zun, and Elsholtz for suitable subsets of solvable residue classes in related `m/n` problems; these are construction/solvability-class antecedents, not a stronger exceptional-set theorem in the sources accessed. ET bibliography [72] gives Shen Zun, “On the diophantine equation `Σ 1/x_i=a/n`,” *Chinese Ann. Math. Ser. B* 7 (1986), 213–220; [83] gives C. Viola, “On the diophantine equations `∏x_i−Σx_i=n` and `Σ1/x_i=a/n`,” *Acta Arith.* 22 (1973), 339–352 (bibliographic line in the archived PDF). A complete primary-source theorem audit of these, the Chinese-language Yang/Xun Qian papers, Hofmeister–Stoll, and Schinzel's survey was not possible. No sourced indication found that any improves the Vaughan exceptional-set rate. ET bibliography [16] is C. Elsholtz, “Sums of k unit fractions,” *Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* 353 (2001), 3209–3227; it addresses unit-fraction representation counts generally, rather than an identified stronger zero-solution bound for `4/n`.

7. **Huang–Vaughan (2011).** J. Huang and R. C. Vaughan, “Mean value theorems for binary Egyptian fractions,” *J. Number Theory* 131 (2011), 1641–1656 (ET bibliography [31]). Mean-value work for binary Egyptian fractions; no evidence found that it proves an improved three-term `4/n` exceptional-set estimate. It was not downloaded for this audit.

8. **Elsholtz–Planitzer and later recent ES work.** The September 2026 survey `LITERATURE_2026.md` was cross-checked against the citation/search results. The recent related results (Dahan, arXiv:2608.24035; Jiang, arXiv:2609.09204, withdrawn with “some errors”; Pomerance–Weingartner; counting and parametrization papers) address fixed-depth failure subsets, constructions, or solution counts—not an upper bound on the full `m=4` zero-solution set improving `2/3`. Dahan's Theorem 1 (abstract) gives for each fixed depth `J`, `|E_2(N;J)|≪_J N/(log N)^(1+𝔄(J)/2)` in primes `1 mod 24`; this is about failure of a bounded-depth search, not actual non-representability. Its separate fixed-shift result gives `asymp N/(log N)^(3/2)` primes failing both branches at shift `7`, also not actual ES exceptions.

## Structural priority check

The exact multiplier identity in the note (`4/n=1/(suw)+1/(nsvw)+1/(nuvw)`, with `kℓ≡3 mod 4`), its forced-class family, and the named **cubic** mass `Σ|R(M)|/M ≍ (log X)^3` are not stated in these forms in the inspected sources. Closest comparisons:

- PW §4, especially p. 8 and Lemma 4.1 pp. 8–9, reconstructs Vaughan's one-family supply of good classes. Its total mass is quadratic in `log x`, scaled by `1/φ(m)`; the large-sieve optimization across this supply yields Vaughan's `2/3` at fixed `m`. This is the `B=2` instance in the mass/budget heuristic, but PW does not name a general `B/(B+1)` law.
- ET Theorem 1.1 (pp. 3–4) gives a cubic-log **average number of solutions** for integer denominators (and squared-log/loglog-scale sum over primes). Its Remark 1.2 p. 5 connects a Poisson heuristic of intensity `c(log p)^3` with Borel–Cantelli and points to Vaughan/ET large sieve as a rigorous analogue. This is related cubic scale, but it is not the note's harmonic mass of forced residue classes or its factorial-moment/Bonferroni argument.
- I found no counterpart in Vaughan/PW/ET to the particular three-denominator multiplier identity, factorial-moment/Bonferroni assembly over forced classes, or the explicit optimization parameter `θ=B/(B+1)` with `B=3`. Standard sieve optimization is present in PW/Vaughan, but novelty of an abstract optimization principle is not asserted here.
- **Improvability remarks:** PW's “strongly improved, though not recently” is immediately followed by Vaughan's 1970 `2/3` (Introduction p. 2), and PW's Theorem 1.3 retains `2/3` at fixed `m`. ET's heuristic suggests finiteness but explicitly says it is not a proof (Remark 1.2); its technical `log log` comment concerns an average solution-count estimate. Neither source states that the `2/3` exceptional-set exponent is optimal, nor explicitly proposes/implies an improvement. The `3/4` architecture is not in those papers.

## Search limits and confidence

I directly read the archived ET, PW, Dahan, and Elsholtz–Planitzer texts. I did **not** obtain Vaughan's primary PDF, or the primary papers by Webb, Li, Yang/Xun Qian, Shen, Viola, Hofmeister–Stoll, or the relevant older Chinese-language literature. For Vaughan, the repository's detailed access log documents publisher restrictions and failed lawful repository/archive routes. Exact historical theorem/page details for inaccessible sources are therefore metadata/secondary-source reports only. Likewise, arXiv and Semantic Scholar searches can miss unindexed items; this audit does not certify absence from all literature.
