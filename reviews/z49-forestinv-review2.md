41. **PASS — Theorem E′, all branches and the actual A = 2 boundary.** Coprimality follows from A ≡ −1 (v) and A ∣ n+v. For v ≡ 7 (8), reciprocity gives (−v/A′) = (A′/v) = −1 and forces A′ > 1. Thus the **theorem**, unlike its pure-power parenthetical, is sound.

42. **PASS — Theorem 2.6 proof; Proposition 2.8 algebra.** Total degree is legitimate because TUV is nonzero. If both endpoint degrees are ≤ 1, the ab term cannot cancel and the four listed degree triples are exhaustive. For nonzero c, ab+c is primitive linear in a over Q[b], hence irreducible; the same argument applies to c′ab+1 for c′ ≠ 0. These rule out the two linear-factor alternatives. If max(deg U,deg V) ≥ 2, the degree inequality forces the other endpoint and T to be constants, and cc′ = 1 contradicts ab+c ≠ 0. Therefore the theorem classifies exactly its stated shapes. For 2.8, putting k = (b−1)/(a+1) gives v = ak+2 = (n+1)/(a+1)+1, and (a+2)v−(a+2)−v = n. Also n ≡ 5+6k (8), giving the stated k ≡ 2 (4) criterion. At k = 2, gcd(a+2,2a+2) = gcd(a+2,2) = 1. 43. **PASS — Proposition 4.2's truncation and Proposition 4.3's numerical/analytical bound.** For t = 1, n+1 = (u−1)(v−1) and u ≥ 2, so A = v−1 ≤ n+1. 44. **PASS — §§5.1–5.3: interior first digit, boundary dictionary, signed excess, and the short-count bound.** If t,u,v ≥ 2, then 0 < v/(tv−1) < 1, so p/q = u−v/(tv−1) has first digit u, q = tv−1 lies in (0,p), and the continued fraction is exactly [[u,t,v]]. The continuant identity gives the converse and uniqueness. For length two, p/q = [[b₁,b₂]] means q = b₂ and p+1 = b₁b₂. Conversely a divisor q ∈ [2,p−1] of p+1 gives [[(p+1)/q,q]]; q = 1 has length one. Valid boundary-word representatives are (t,u,v) = (b₂+1,b₁,1) and (1,b₁+1,b₂+1). In particular boundary terms have a fixed root 1, irrespective of numerator reduction. Proposition 5.2's mod-8 congruence is exactly DK Cor 4.1, including arbitrary odd n. Prefix cylinders are rational intervals with the appropriate open/closed endpoints; with the missing convention in 35 supplied, their weighted sums are interval character sums. For first digit 3 or 4, u ∈ {3,4}; each interior word injects into a divisor tu−1 of p+u, so their number is at most τ(p+3)+τ(p+4). Boundary terms number at most τ(p+1). Consequently the length ≥ 4 signed contribution in this particular interval is −h(−4p)/2 + O(τ(p+1)+τ(p+3)+τ(p+4)); Siegel's lower bound makes this asymptotic to −h(−4p)/2. This is the rigorous sense in which essentially all this **specific excess** is long, not an arbitrary-weight positivity theorem.
