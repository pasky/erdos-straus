# Hostile review of Z53_GENUSREDEI.md

JRT has a concrete counterexample as written, although its construction appears repairable. The finite-group union bound in Theorem P is correct; its advertised unconditional arithmetic no-go is not.

1. **FATAL — §3 (L1), line 91; JRT Step 5, lines 176–178. Illegal cancellation makes JRT false.** The displayed law is only `s·∏a ≡ s·g (mod T)`, but Step 5 uses `∏a ≡ g (mod T)`. The smooth part need not be invertible modulo T. Counterexample entirely within an ES line: `L(p)=p+4`, `n=1`, `B=5`, `T=120`, `Ω=Q(i)`, `M=18000`, `r=1`, `σ=id`. This datum is admissible, with `s=5`, `g=1`. The one-factor configuration `a=49` satisfies all five displayed laws: `5·49 ≡ 5 (120)` and the others are trivial. Yet every actual cofactor `(p+4)/5` is `1 (120)`, never 49. **Fix:** replace L1 by `∏a=g` in `(Z/T)^×`, derived by first dividing the integer identity by s, not by cancelling a congruence. Recheck all statements referring to the complete list of laws.

2. **FATAL — Corollary P′, lines 289–307; repeated in §§1, 7, 8, 11, 12. Profinite fake residues are not alternative prime factorizations.** The square/odd-order local lifts exist, but the assembled profinite element need not be an integer, let alone a prime factor of the same fixed N. In fact an integer agreeing with ℓ modulo *every* modulus coprime to t must equal ℓ. Thus a nontrivial fake cannot have the claimed arithmetic interpretation. Keeping an old prime as the denominator of a Legendre symbol while changing its residue data does not construct a new mutual-symbol matrix of actual primes. Moreover, quadratic characters of **all** conductors distinguish distinct positive primes: for distinct ℓ, ℓ′, some auxiliary prime q has `(ℓℓ′/q)=-1`. Consequently the asserted all-conductor profile already determines each actual prime. **Fix:** retain P as a theorem about formal finite-group assignments only. Delete the fixed-integer arithmetic non-determination conclusion, or explicitly define a restricted formal information model and make no claim of arithmetic realizability. JRT, after repair, supplies a different, conditional realization statement with p varying.

3. **MAJOR — §1 lines 64–67; §5.5 title and Reading; §7.1; §§8, 11, 12. Squares do not preserve the full 2-power profile.** `G²` kills quadratic characters, not all 2-power-order characters. Concrete ES example: `p=1901`, `C=8`, `t=32`, `N=60833=127·479`. Here `s=1`, `g=1`, `k=2`, `|G²|=4>2`, and both factors are `−1 (32)`. Nevertheless **all characters of G have 2-power order**, so the full 2-power profile determines both classes and the event. There is no odd-order component at this level. The repeated assertion that “only the odd part” is missing is therefore false. **Fix:** distinguish the quadratic quotient `G/G²` from the full 2-primary component. Only the odd-order subgroup supports the latter version of P's formal bound; do not replace its size by `|G²|`.

4. **MAJOR — P′ lines 295–297. Existence of a bad assignment is not non-determination.** P constructs an event-avoiding assignment but need not provide an event-having one. For `p=5`, `L=12p+1=61`, `t=12`, `s=1`, `k=1`, `g=1`, the hypotheses hold (`0<|G²|=1`), yet the sole assignment has no event. The event is completely determined, false, even in the formal model. **Fix:** require that the original assignment has the event when concluding that a profile does not determine its truth value. Else conclude only “this profile does not force the event.” Restore the omitted baseline hypothesis `−1∉Σ(s){1,g}` in all summaries too.

5. **MAJOR — P′ lines 292–296. Additional preservation claims need hypotheses/construction.** Preserving residues away from t preserves symbols at p only when `p∤t`, absent from the setting. Example: `p=5`, `C=25`, `t=100`, `N=501=3·167`; the product-one square twist `(9,89)` sends classes `(3,67)` to `(27,63)`. The quartic symbol of the first factor at 5 changes (3 becomes 2 modulo 5). Also independently chosen square lifts whose product is 1 modulo t need not have product 1 modulo higher powers of its primes. **Fix:** require `gcd(p,t)=1` for that preservation assertion, or impose the appropriate character kernels at p; choose the last local lift as the inverse product of the others to preserve all local product identities. These repairs still do not fix finding 2.

6. **MAJOR — §1 lines 21–36; JRT Step 4(i); Theorem U. Integrality is missing.** Admissibility supplies an upper bound on `v_q(αr+β)`, not the lower bound `v_q(γ)` needed for integral line values. For example `L=(p+1)/3`, `B=3`, `T=24`, `M=6912=2⁸3³`, `r=1`, `n=1`, `Ω=Q(i)`, `σ=id` satisfies the stated conditions, but L(p) is never integral. **Fix:** require `α_j r+β_j ≡0 (mod γ_j)` (with γ_j dividing M), equivalently the missing valuation lower bounds. For ES e-slices explicitly state `e≡3 (4)`. Positivity follows for sufficiently large p; require it separately in the fixed-n section.

7. **MAJOR — §1 D3, Lemma 3.1 L5, Corollary 4.1. Repeated large prime factors are not covered.** Within-line repeated factors give `(ℓ/ℓ)=0`, not an allowed ±1 decoration, and L5 fails. Privacy excludes sharing between different lines, not repetitions within one line. Example: `p=241`, `L=p+4=5·7²`, `B=5`, `T=120`, `M=18000`, `r=241`, `Ω=Q(i)` is admissible. CRT/Dirichlet gives arbitrarily large primes in this datum with `7²∥p+4`. They cannot be discarded by “sufficiently large.” **Fix:** explicitly restrict the decorated JRT/Corollary 4.1 domain to squarefree B-rough parts, including the universal-prime quantifier. Do not claim this exhausts arbitrary factor multisets. Extending to multiplicities needs new definitions and arguments, not merely deletion of “distinct.” Corollary F's one-factor construction can still prove its datum-only criterion.

8. **MAJOR — Corollary F final sentence, lines 236–237; §1 lines 60–63. Fixing factor decorations can change forcing.** The iff concerns conditioning on Π alone, not additionally conditioning on specified D2/D3. Example: the line `p+4`, level 4, with `B=5`, `T=120`, `M=3600`, `r=73`, `n=2`, and σ the Artin element of 73 in `Ω_{5,2}` has `s=1`, `g≡1 (4)`, so B1 fails. But prescribing two large factors with both quadratic symbols at p equal to −1 forces both factor classes to be `3 (4)` by L3, hence forces the event. The actual example is `73+4=7·11`. **Fix:** say only that adding fixed-field Frobenius data of p to the congruence datum does not change the datum-only criterion. Given specified factor decorations, forcing must still be checked over configurations satisfying those decorations and all laws.

10. **MAJOR — Remark 4.2, lines 194–197. The asserted HC⁺ extension omits individual compatibility.** An abelian *product* law cannot make arbitrary prescribed Frobenii of individual factors compatible with their residue classes. For instance, in the S₃ splitting field of `x³−2`, a transposition is nontrivial on `Q(√−3)`; a prime `1 (12)` cannot have that Frobenius. Two prescribed transpositions have trivial abelian product, so a product check would miss the obstruction. **Fix:** impose each factor's compatibility on the intersection with the relevant cyclotomic field and prove the required simultaneous local conditions before asserting an HC⁺ extension.

Do not remove FRONTIER §7.6 on the strength of the present text. “ES remains open” is correctly stated throughout, but does not cure these stronger no-go overclaims.
