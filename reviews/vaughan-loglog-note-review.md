# Hostile referee report: Vaughan loglog note

**Verdict: CORRECT-AFTER-REPAIRS.** The claimed exceptional-set bound survives. I read every line of the original 483-line source and reread the repaired source. I find no fatal collision, distribution, sieve-level, or semigroup error. The original contains false auxiliary statements and overstatements, but none requires changing the theorem or the core construction. The surgical repairs listed below are applied to `paper/vaughan-loglog-note.tex`.

Line numbers and quotations below refer to the **original** source, before repairs (SHA-256 `09578b577617b8bc967f4c1db1a12df237ad3d28de750306a1892ebc7ca5235e`). Severity distinguishes an actual false statement/local gap (**medium**) from missing conventions, citation errors, and explanatory overclaims (**low**); there is no high/fatal defect found.

## Numbered defects and exact repairs

1. **Medium — lines 188–190: false unrestricted two-variable estimate.** Quote: `T(H',z')=\frac{\varphi(k)}{k^2}\Lambda'^2+O\Big(\frac{\Lambda'\tau(k)}{H'}\Big).`, followed by `valid for $H'\ge1$`. Multiplying (5) and (6) also gives an error `O(τ(k)/H'^2)`. For `k=1`, `H'=2−ε`, `z'=2`, the actual sum is `1/4`, whereas both the proposed main term and error tend to zero as `ε→0`. **Applied fix:** restrict this displayed estimate to `H'≥1` **and** `Λ'≥1`, explicitly explaining absorption of the product error. All uses with `d≤H` have `Λ'=Λ≥10 log K`, so this repairs the proof without loss.

2. **Medium — lines 250–251: missing truncation in Lemma 3.2.** Quote: `=\sum_{d\ \mathrm{odd}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^2}\Big(\frac14\log\frac Kd+O(1)\Big)`. For `d>K`, the inner sum over positive `m≤K/d` is empty, while `log(K/d)` is unbounded below; the termwise uniform `O(1)` assertion is false. **Applied fix:** write `d≤K, d odd` on both outer sums. Extending the resulting convergent coefficient sums afterwards costs `O(1)`. The claimed coefficient `2/π²` is correct.

3. **Medium — lines 260–274, 289–290, 299, 340: incomplete block definition and lower-bound bookkeeping.** Quotes: `lying in a dyadic block $(x,2x]$`; `Cover $(X^{1/2},X]$ by blocks $(x,2x]$ with`; `$x=2^jX^{1/2}$`; `Summing over the $\asymp\log X$ blocks gives the lower half of \eqref{mass}.`. An arbitrary choice of a block changes `z` and hence the class set. Except at special endpoints, the last full block extends beyond `X`; its lower bound cannot be counted towards a sum ending at `X`. In addition, `ω_c(ℓ)` was defined only for `ℓ≡3 (4)` but used for all primes in (10) and in the lemma's pointwise assertion. **Applied fix:** fix `x_j=2^j√X`, `0≤j<ceil(log X/(2 log 2))` before defining the classes; define the other prime classes to be empty. Include the terminal block in full only for the upper bound; discard it for the lower bound if it overhangs. There remain `asymp log X` complete blocks. With these conventions, (10) is an exact equality, not an inequality or a multiplicity count in disguise.

4. **Medium — lines 86–91: introductory distinctness and pointwise-count overclaim.** Quotes: `also produces $\asymp(\log \ell)^2$ forced classes`; `the classes obtained from different $k$ and different factorizations are distinct`. The identity alone establishes no such pointwise asymptotic, and unrestricted factorizations need not yield distinct residues. For example, `k=1, ℓ=31, A=8` and `(u,v,w)=(1,1,8),(2,2,2)` both give residue `−1`. **Applied fix:** restrict the statement to compatible classes in a reduced progression and to the small coprime factorizations selected by Lemma 4.1; describe their contribution as averaged. The actual distinctness proof in Lemma 4.1 is sound.

5. **Medium — lines 453–464: unsupported assertions in Remark 7.2.** Quotes: `mass available up to modulus $X$ from $(\log X)^2$ to $(\log X)^3$`; `the total number of solutions of \eqref{ES} for a typical $n$ is` followed by `$\asymp(\log n)^3$, cf.\ \cite{ET}`. The note proves the mass estimate only for `K≤(log X)^4`, not a polynomial range of multipliers that would give a cubic mass. Elsholtz–Tao prove prime-average bounds with a `log log` gap, not the stated typical-order theorem for integers. **Applied fix:** state the proved mass `(log X)²h(K)`, label its cubic extrapolation as formal/unproved, remove the supposed typical-order theorem, and make the `3/4` exponent conditional on comparable sieve and tail control. No such claim is used in the proof.

6. **Low — lines 176–179: progression proof omits an empty interval.** Quote: `the upper bound is $1/u_0+\frac1k\log(z'/u_0)$`. This expression can be negative when no term occurs, e.g. `k=100,a=99,H'=1,z'=2`; it is only an upper bound in the nonempty case. **Applied fix:** say so, and handle the empty case using `z'<u_0≤H'+k`, whence `Λ'/k≤1/H'`. Estimates (5) and (6) themselves are correct.

7. **Low — lines 97–102: sufficient parameter choice presented as necessary, and an excessively broad limitation claim.** Quotes: `which forces $L\le N^{1/2}$`; `no choice of multiplier family compatible with the level constraint does better`. The large sieve does not impose the particular exponent `1/2` on `L`; any fixed positive power of `N` left in `N/L` can work with suitable constants. The Euler-product inequality bounds this particular weighted sum, not every imaginable use of multipliers. **Applied fix:** describe `L≤N^{1/2}` as the chosen sufficient regime and limit the conclusion to `Σ_{k|L}φ(k)/k²`; use `log log(3L)` to avoid small-`L` pathology.

8. **Low — line 349: incorrect theorem locator, now verified rather than guessed.** Quote: `\cite[Thm.~3]{Montgomery}`. In the 1978 BAMS paper, Theorem 3 (p. 559) is Selberg's sharp analytic large-sieve constant `N−1+Δ⁻¹`, not the stated arithmetic sieve denominator. The arithmetic formula needed here appears in **§8, p. 561**, immediately after “Montgomery [58] derived from (20) a sharper bound”. **Applied fix:** cite `\cite[\S8, p.~561]{Montgomery}` and retain the original bibliography entry. I obtained the actual paper through the webpage reader after direct PDF requests were blocked. Thus the numbering is not left uncertain. An alternative is Montgomery, *Topics in Multiplicative Number Theory*, Ch. 3 (“Arithmetic formulations of the large sieve”), or Iwaniec–Kowalski, Thm. 7.14; I did not independently inspect the latter theorem.

9. **Low — lines 350–353, 373, 383–384: implicit sieve conventions.** Quotes: `a set of primes`; `avoid $\omega(\ell)$ residue classes`; `fix a small absolute $\delta>0$`; `Fix a reduced residue $c\pmod M$`; `$\cN_c=\{m\in[0,Y]:\ c+Mm\ \text{is an exceptional prime}\}$`. The products need a finite prime set and `0≤ω(ℓ)<ℓ`; the numerical tail choice should state an adequate bound on `δ`; the interval parametrization needs an integer variable and a chosen representative. **Applied fix:** state these conventions, take `0<δ<1/4`, choose `1≤c≤M`, and define `N_c` as integers `m∈[0,Y]` yielding exceptional primes at most `N`. The original harmless overcount past `N` is also removed. These are not failures in the intended application.

10. **Low — lines 418–436: omitted semigroup and partial-summation details.** Quotes: `generated by the exceptional primes`; `let $c_0$ be the constant of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, so that`; `\ll1+\int_{t_0}^{x}E_{\mathrm{pr}}(t)\,t^{-2+\delta}\,dt`; `Since $g(u)/u$ is decreasing for $u\ge\e$`. The argument must include the empty product `1`, shrink the prime-bound constant to remove its implied prefactor, enlarge `t_0` so `log t_0≥e`, and control the partial-summation endpoint and higher prime powers uniformly. **Applied fix:** state all four points. The endpoint is at most `exp(−(c_0−η)g(L))≤1`; powers of order at least two contribute `O(Σ_p p^{-3/2})` for `δ<1/4`. These details validate, rather than replace, the Rankin argument.

11. **Low — lines 445–449: general-numerator extension too compressed.** Quotes: `$m/n=1/(suw)+1/(nsvw)+1/(nuvw)$`; `The argument above then gives`. The identity is correct, but the locally undefined `s` and the progression choices for the class count should not be left to guesswork. **Applied fix:** define `s=(nv+u)/(kℓ)`; select `k≡1 (m)`, `ℓ≡−1 (m)`; replace modulus `4uv` by `muv`; state `Σ_{k≤K,k≡1(m)}φ(k)/k² asymp_m log K`. This establishes the claimed fixed-`m` extension. It is not an assertion uniform in growing `m`.

12. **Low — lines 38–40 and 62–63: terminology and literature attribution.** Quotes: `an elementary count of the forced classes`; `who describe Vaughan's bound as the current state of the art`. The count uses Bombieri–Vinogradov, although its lattice ingredient is elementary. Pomerance–Weingartner's precise statement is that improvements were “not recently”, followed by Vaughan's bound; their Theorem 1.3 retains its fixed-numerator exponent. **Applied fix:** identify the elementary lattice ingredient and refer directly to their Theorem 1.3 instead of paraphrasing an unrestricted current-priority claim.

Additional non-substantive edits: replace the unnecessary numerical `9Λ²/H` tail bound by `≪Λ²/H` (avoids irrelevant rounding issues when `H` is real); give a short justification of the divisor-moment estimate, the endpoint-max BV formulation, and the `li` part of `E*`; reflow two long formulas. The original numerical 9 is obtainable for sufficiently large `K_0`, so it is **not** a further mathematical defect.

## Independent audit of the potentially fatal steps

### Identity, CRT, and the lattice lemma

- **Identity:** on denominator `nsuvw`, the numerator is `nv+u+s=s(kℓ+1)=4suvw`. Positivity of `s` follows from positivity and divisibility. Any common divisor of `A` and `kℓ` divides `(kℓ+1)−kℓ=1`; therefore `v` is invertible modulo `kℓ`. No tacit primality of `k` is needed.
- **Split (2):** CRT is valid when `(k,ℓ)=1`. In all applications `ℓ>K≥k`. Since every multiplier divides `L_K`, `k|(n−c)` and hence `u+nv≡u+cv (k)` in both directions.
- **Progressions (5), (6):** their respective errors `O(1/H')` and `O(τ(k)/H')` are uniform in the reduced residue. For the Möbius proof of (6), the elementary harmonic estimate remains true for all `0<A<B`, even if `A=H'/e<1`, with the deliberately large error `O(1/A)`. No forbidden application at `H'<1` is needed after splitting the gcd sum.
- **Möbius step:** every common divisor `d` is coprime to `k`, so division by `d` preserves the congruence. Each weight gains exactly `d⁻²`. The ratio `(z/d)/(H/d)=z/H` is exactly unchanged. For `d≤H`, `H/d≥1`; for `d>H`, the unrestricted harmonic bound gives total `O((1+log z)²/H)=O(Λ²/H)`. The main coefficient is `Π_{p∤k}(1−p⁻²)+O(1/H)≥6/π²−O(1/H)`.
- **Lower-bound constant:** `k/φ(k)≤2^{ω(k)}≤τ(k)≤2√k`, so `τ(k)k²/φ(k)≤kτ(k)²≤4K²`. Since `log z≤2Λ`, the entire relative error is `O(K²/H)=O(K⁻⁸)`. Thus `6/π²−O(1/H)−CK⁻⁸≥1/4` is legitimate after one absolute choice of `K_0`.
- **Totient-weight upper bound:** the identity `1/φ(n)=(1/n)Σ_{d|n}μ²(d)/φ(d)` is exact. The convergent coefficient sum has local factors `1+1/(p(p−1))`; convolution with the harmonic series proves `Σ_{d≤H}μ²(d)/φ(d)≪log H`. Also `φ(d)≥√(d/2)` gives the asserted `H⁻¹/²` tail. Relative to `Λ/k`, the two errors are at most `O(K⁻⁹)` and `O(K⁻⁴)`. Relative to `(φ(k)/k)Λ`, they are at most `O(K⁻⁹)` and `O(K⁻⁹/²)`. Here `Λ≥log H=10 log K` is used. Thus both brackets really have relative secondary terms `O(K⁻⁴)`.
- **Lemma 3.2:** the coefficient is `(1/4)Π_{p>2}(1−p⁻²)=(1/4)(8/π²)=2/π²`. There is no lost parity factor.

### Distinct classes and Bombieri–Vinogradov

- Equal residues imply `ℓ|(uv'−u'v)`; absolute value `<z²=x^{1/3}<ℓ` forces equality as integers. Primitive positive pairs representing the same rational number are identical. Since `(ℓ,4uv)=1` follows from `4uv|kℓ+1`, cancelling `ℓ` yields `k≡k' (4uv)`. Finally `4uv>4H²>K` forces `k=k'`. This also proves at most one multiplier per pair and the bound `ω_c(ℓ)≤z²≤ℓ^{1/3}`. It is not merely injectivity of triples into a larger indexing set.
- `k` is invertible modulo `4uv`: it is odd and coprime to both `u,v`. The progression `ℓ≡−k⁻¹ (4uv)` is reduced and already imposes `ℓ≡3 (4)` because `k≡1 (4)`. There is **no additional factor 1/2** to insert. With the repaired zero convention, equality (10) counts distinct forbidden residues exactly.
- The modulus range is `q≤4x^{1/3}≤x^{1/2}` for sufficiently large `x`. Brun–Titchmarsh has `log(2x/q)≫log x`, uniformly over **all** reduced residues. Moreover `φ(4uv)≥2φ(u)φ(v)`. The lower main term is at least `xΛ²h(K)/(16 log(2x))`; using `Λ≥(log x)/12` and `log(2x)≤2 log x`, one can take `c_0=1/4608` before subtracting errors.
- For fixed `q=4uv`, the number of **ordered** positive factorizations is `τ(q/4)≤τ(q)`; multiplying by at most `K` possible integers `k` gives the asserted `Kτ(q)` multiplicity. Coprimality and congruence restrictions only decrease it.
- The moment `Σ_{q≤Q}τ(q)²/φ(q)≪(log Q)^4` is correct for large `Q`: the nonnegative Euler product has local factor `1+4/p+O(p⁻²)`, hence is bounded by a constant times `Π_{p≤Q}(1−1/p)⁻⁴`. There is no hidden fifth logarithm.
- `E*(q)≪x/(φ(q)log x)` follows by bounding the interval prime count using `π(2x;q,a)` and bounding the `li` term separately. This works for the maximum over all reduced `a`, not just `a=−1`.
- The standard BV maximum over `2≤y≤2x`, at level `x^{1/2}(log x)^{-B(R)}`, bounds the interval error with a factor two. The level exceeds `4x^{1/3}` eventually. Cauchy–Schwarz gives `Kx(log x)^{(3−R)/2}`. With `R=19` this is `Kx(log x)⁻⁸≪x(log x)⁻⁴≤x(log x)⁻³`. Since `x≥√X`, the sharper inequality is `K≤16(log x)^4`; the printed 32 is harmless slack.
- Uniform size requirements are consistent: `z≥X^{1/12}` while `H²=K^{20}≤(log X)^{80}`. One absolute `X_0` makes every block admissible. Summing `asymp log X` complete blocks, each with `log x asymp log X`, gives the desired quadratic logarithm. Errors are already negligible block by block.

### Large sieve and the final exponents

- The cited arithmetic sieve is the correct one. Applying its sharp analytic form to an integer interval of diameter at most `Y` gives numerator at most `Y+Q²`; no unmentioned factor depending on `M` occurs.
- Prime factors of `M=L_K` are at most `K`. Thus `p>K` lies in a reduced class, and every sieving prime `ℓ>√X>K` is coprime to `M`. The map `ρ↦(ρ−c)M⁻¹ (ℓ)` is a bijection, preserving the exact forbidden-class count. Nonreduced classes contain no primes greater than `K`; adding at most `K` small primes is sufficient.
- With `Q=√Y`, the bound is `2Y/S(Q)`. Rankin's tail argument is valid: `(1+gℓ^v)/(1+g)≤exp(g(ℓ^v−1))`; for `v=1/log X`, `ℓ^v−1≤e−1`; and `ω≤ℓ/2` gives `g≤2ω/ℓ`. The factor 2 in (12) is present. It could be improved by retaining `g/(1+g)=ω/ℓ`, but no improvement is needed.
- Write `L=log N`, `T=log X`. For `δ<1/4`, the negative tail term is at most `−L/(4T)`. The stated condition `T³ log K≤L/[16(e−1)A']` bounds the positive term by `L/(8T)`. Hence the tail is at most `exp(−L/(8T))<1/2` eventually. Taking `α³≤1/[16(e−1)A']` suffices.
- The mass yields `log S(Q)≫T² log K`, since `0≤g≤1` justifies `log(1+g)≥g/2`. Summing at most `M` progression bounds cancels `Y=N/M`. Finally `T² log K=α²L^{2/3}(log K)/(log L)^{2/3} asymp L^{2/3}(log L)^{1/3}`. The floor in `K=floor(δL)` changes the exponent by `o(1)` in the displayed replacement `log K→log(δL)`. The additive `K` is negligible. Also `(log X)^4/K→∞` and `√X/K→∞`, as required.

### All denominators and general numerators

The semigroup inclusion is one-way, which is all the upper bound requires. Scaling a solution for any prime divisor scales all three denominators by the integer `n/p`. The empty product handles the exceptional integer `1`. For `g(u)=u^{2/3}(log u)^{1/3}`, `g(u)/u=(log u/u)^{1/3}` decreases for `u≥e`; therefore `δu≤ηg(u)` for `e≤u≤L`. After the endpoint and prime-power controls recorded in defect 10, the Euler product is bounded by a fixed constant because `∫_e^∞exp(−(c_0−η)g(u))du<∞`. This proves the stated all-integer exponent, not a weakened exponent from a crude smooth-number split.

For fixed numerator `m`, `kℓ≡−1 (m)` has the **correct minus sign**. The same identity gives numerator `s(kℓ+1)=msuvw`, and `(A,kℓ)=1` still holds. The repaired choices `k≡1 (m)`, `ℓ≡−1 (m)` make the prime progression modulo `muv` reduced. Its size is still below the BV level for fixed `m`; the distinctness and totient inequalities persist. More precisely the multiplier-sum coefficient is `(1/m)Π_{p∤m}(1−p⁻²)>0`. The fixed-`m` conclusion is justified.

## Literature search and priority

I searched variants of “Erdos Straus exceptional set Vaughan bound improvement log log”, searches restricted to 2026, and Sander/Vaughan exceptional-set references. Search snippets alone were not used to certify an improvement; the following relevant sources were visited and inspected.

- **Pomerance–Weingartner, arXiv:2511.16817 (2025), introduction and Theorem 1.3:** <https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16817v1>. Their introduction says the count was strongly improved “though not recently” and cites Vaughan's 1970 `N exp(−c(log N)^{2/3})` bound. Theorem 1.3 proves `N exp(−C(log²N/φ(m))^{1/3})` uniformly for `4≤m≤(log N)²`: an important generalization, but **no improvement of the fixed-4 exponential rate**. Section 6 also confirms the note's `10^{18}` computational-verification citation. Search results identify the subsequent journal publication as *Ramanujan Journal* 69, article 31 (2026); citing the inspected preprint remains valid.
- **“Sieve dimension and search depth for the Erdős–Straus conjecture, n≡1 (24)”, arXiv:2608.24035v1 (25 August 2026):** <https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24035v1>. Its introduction expressly says the fixed-depth density estimate is weaker than Vaughan's. The description of §4.3 reports `O(N exp(−0.065(log N)^{2/3}))` at optimal depth and explicitly states: “This is not an improvement on [1]”. It does **not** supply the claimed loglog gain. I did not referee that separate paper.
- **Elsholtz–Tao, arXiv:1107.1010 / J. Aust. Math. Soc. 94 (2013):** <https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1010>. Its actual prime-average theorem is `N log²N ≪ Σ_{p≤N}f(p) ≪ N log²N log log N`. This does not establish the note's original typical-order assertion, nor is it an exceptional-set improvement.

**Search conclusion:** I found no post-1970 improvement of Vaughan's fixed-numerator exceptional-set rate that pre-empts this loglog factor. The inspected 2025 and August 2026 sources support treating Vaughan's rate as the existing benchmark. This is a quick literature check, not proof of exhaustive priority. Do not advertise the elementary identity itself as new or upgrade the search result to an unqualified “first improvement since 1970” priority certification.
