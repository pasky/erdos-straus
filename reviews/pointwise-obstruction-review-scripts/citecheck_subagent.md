# Citation check: `paper/pointwise-obstruction.tex`

PDF page numbers below mean the archived PDF page (1-based), not journal pagination. Quotes preserve the source wording apart from line-wrap/ligature cleanup.

1. **BL Lemma 3.10 — VERIFIED, with a scope caveat.** BL PDF p. 14, Lemma 3.10: “The set `{u ∈ U_n(Z) : u1u2u3 ≠ 0, ui ≠ −uj for all i,j}` is finite.” It explicitly concludes “In particular, … `U_n(N)` is finite.” This proves finiteness for signed integer triples with nonzero coordinates subject to the excluded cancellation condition. For odd `n` (in particular prime `p`), the excluded `ui=−uj` case would force the remaining coordinate to be `n/4`, impossible integrally; so the paper’s fixed odd-prime claim is valid. The source does not literally state finiteness of every signed solution for every fixed `n` without qualifications.

2. **BL §1 / Jaroma identity — VERIFIED as a claim about existence; identity not printed in BL.** BL PDF p. 1 says “there is always a solution with `u1,u2,u3 ∈ Z`,” citing [14]. The exact Jaroma identity is not displayed in BL; the cited expression is `4/n = 1/((n−1)/2) + 1/((n+1)/2) − 1/(n(n²−1)/4)`, for odd `n≥3`, directly checkable. The introduction’s broad “every odd n” should be understood for admissible `n≥3`, not `n=1` (where the displayed identity is undefined).

3. **BL Theorem 1.2 — VERIFIED but paper’s wording must distinguish product from local symbol.** BL PDF p. 2, Thm. 1.2: for odd `n` and a **positive** solution `u ∈ N³`, “`∏_{p|n} (−u1/u3,−u2/u3)_p = −1`.” For `n=p` prime this says the single `p`-Hilbert symbol is `−1`; it does not require `p≡1 mod 4`. (The paper has `p≡1 mod 4`, a valid specialization.) The theorem does not say `n=p` in general; that is the specialization.

4. **BL Theorem 1.5 — VERIFIED, qualifications noted.** BL PDF p. 2, Thm. 1.5: “Let `n` be an odd integer and `u ∈ Z³` a solution … which is not a natural number solution. Then `∏_{p|n}(...) = 1`.” Thus it applies to all odd `n`, not just prime or `p≡1 mod 4`, and to every integer solution not all-positive. The theorem’s displayed symbol is handled by the paper’s continuity convention even at coordinates zero; for the paper’s nonzero entries its direct use is unproblematic. “Not positive” is exactly “not a natural number solution” in their convention.

5. **BL (3.1), Lemma 3.4 — VERIFIED, with exact exponent condition.** BL PDF p. 10, (3.1) defines `inv_v α(u)=(−u1/u3,−u2/u3)_v`. PDF p. 11, Lemma 3.4: if `p` is odd and `u2/u3 ∈ Z_p^*`, then `inv_p α(u) = ((−u2/u3)/p)^{v_p(u1u3)}`. Therefore when `v_p(u1u3)=1` this is exactly the Legendre symbol `(-u2/u3 | p)`. The manuscript applies this with the unit ratio and valuation one; BL itself does not supply that valuation-one hypothesis there. For `p≡1 mod 4`, `(-u2/u3|p)=(u2/u3|p)`.

6. **BL §1 permutation invariance — VERIFIED, but cite Proposition 2.6.** BL PDF p. 2 says the apparently asymmetric symbols are invariant under `S3` “(see Proposition 2.6).” This applies to their Hilbert-symbol invariant; the paper’s phrase should cite Prop. 2.6 rather than implying an uncited elementary identity.

7. **BL Corollary 1.3 / Yamamoto — VERIFIED with a qualification.** BL PDF p. 2, Cor. 1.3: for odd prime `n=p` and positive solution, some `i≠j` has `ui/uj∈Z_p^*`, and `(-ui/uj|p)=-1`. The same paragraph says it “unifies various quadratic reciprocity conditions found by Yamamoto [24] for `p≡1 mod 4`.” Appendix A (PDF pp. 15–16) explicitly compares Yamamoto’s Type 1/Type 2 conditions and derives them from Cor. 1.3. “Positive ⇒ non-residue” is accurate provided it is the **negative ratio** `−ui/uj` (or use `p≡1 mod4` to drop the minus).

8. **BL Theorem 1.6 / Theorem 1.1 — VERIFIED, with the precise version at Thm. 1.8.** Thm. 1.6 is on BL PDF p. 3: “`Br U_n / Br Q ≅ Z/2Z` generated by the quaternion algebra `(−u1/u3,−u2/u3)`.” Thm. 1.1 is on PDF p. 1 and says exactly: “Let `n≥2`. Then there is no Brauer–Manin obstruction to the existence of natural number solutions” of (1.1). Thm. 1.8 on PDF p. 4 gives the precise adelic formulation: “For all `n ∈ N` we have `(U_n(R)^+ × ∏_p U_n(Z_p))^Br ≠ ∅`.” Thus the paper’s citation and description are right; Thm. 1.8 is useful for its precise form.

9. **BL Corollary 1.4 as polynomial-identity obstruction — WRONG attribution.** BL PDF p. 2, Cor. 1.4 is a local divisibility statement: if `n` is an odd square, there are no natural solutions with either `n|u1, gcd(n,u2u3)=1`, or `gcd(n,u1)=1, n|u2,n|u3`. It is not stated as a general polynomial-identity obstruction or Schinzel’s theorem. The source’s following sentence compares it to ET Prop. 1.6. Polynomial nonexistence is described in ET PDF p. 8 and attributed to Schinzel; see #17. If paper means Cor. 1.4 “polynomial identity obstruction,” replace with a qualified consequence/reference to ET/Schinzel.

10. **ET §1 remark after Prop. 1.6 — VERIFIED in substance; ‘polynomial identities’ needs separate citation.** ET PDF p. 6, Prop. 1.6: “For any odd perfect square `n`, we have `f_I(n)=f_II(n)=0`.” The remark immediately after says this “indicate[s] a key difficulty” and “rules out many strategies (e.g. a finite set of covering congruence strategies, or the circle method).” Thus the paper’s summary that Type I/II vanish at odd squares and covering congruences fail is accurate. Polynomial identities are separately ruled out in ET PDF p. 8 (the intro immediately after): a primitive residue class which is a perfect square “cannot be solved by polynomials,” said to follow from refs [44],[68] or Prop. 1.6. Do not fold that into the quoted post-Prop.-1.6 remark without distinction. ET Prop. 1.6 itself is exactly the vanishing result above.

11. **ET equation coordinates — PARTLY VERIFIED; chart identity needs careful variable map.** ET PDF p. 12 gives Type I (2.1)–(2.9); Proposition 2.2 on the same page maps sextuples to `(x,y,z)=(abdn, acd, bcd)`. Type I criterion in Prop. 2.3, PDF p. 13: `n=4acd−f`, `f | 4a²d+1` (with `c` coprime to `n`). Thus paper’s `4m−1=pe` is a re-expression of (2.1) under its Type I variables, not literally ET’s (2.1) unless it explains the map. ET’s (2.1) is `4abd=n+e`; in the paper chart, its `m` corresponds to ET `abd` and its chart `H=(4m−1)/p` corresponds to ET `e` (with `n=p`).

   ET PDF p. 14 lists Type II (2.13)–(2.21), notably (2.21) `4c²dn+1=f(4bcd−1)`. Paper’s Type II modular identity `(4m−1)(4n−1)≡1 mod p` is indeed the reduction of (2.21) when its two `p`-multiples are matched to ET’s coordinates; the paper’s wording “(2.21) reduced mod p” is accurate with that coordinate identification.

   For the final assertion, ET Type I (2.6) and (2.7) on PDF p. 12 are `f=4acd−n` and `ef=4a²d+1`. Thus ET’s `e` (not chart `e`) times ET’s `f` equals `4a²d+1`; with chart substitutions `A=4x−p=f_ET`, `H=(4m−1)/p=e_ET`, the chart quantity `(AH−1)/4 = a²d`. So “on positive points [chart] `e=a²d` in the coordinates of Prop. 2.2, (2.6)–(2.7)” is valid only if its chart `e` is explicitly distinguished from ET parameter `e`; as printed it is not a literal identity named in ET. ET Prop. 2.2’s Type I map is positive integer coordinates and unique sextuple (up to the stated normalization).

12. **Dahan — MIXED; the references support the sieve and obstruction claims, not the paper’s vague ‘half-dimension’ shorthand without precision.**
   * Lemma 4.2, PDF p. 20 (statement begins PDF p. 19): if `M` has no divisor `≡−1 (mod 4m)`, the set `T` of residues mod `4m` of prime factors of `M` has size at most `φ(4m)/2`; the involution `x↦−x⁻¹` has no fixed point. It also has an attainment/equality clause (used on p. 20). This is the precise half-density/half-dimension input.
   * Theorem 4.3, PDF p. 20: for fixed finite set `P` of coprime pairs, `|E_P(N)| ≪_P N(log N)^{-1-|P|/2}`; the exponent is exactly the dimension of the resulting sieve problem. For one pair (`|P|=1`) this gives exponent `3/2`. Dahan’s PDF p. 2 explains that the `|P|/2` component is exact sieve dimension.
   * Proposition 3.8, PDF p. 12: an **explicit finite computational** statement: `H(5×10^9)` consists of fourteen listed primes for which the `a=1` hyperbolic Type-II family fails for every `u`; it does not assert an infinite unconditional obstruction. All have minimum modulator `a≥2`, and all are resolved by Dahan’s wider search. Phrase it as a branch/family blind spot, not a general obstruction to ES.
   * Theorem 4.14, PDF p. 23: unconditionally, `≍N(log N)^{-3/2}` primes `n≡1 mod24` have `7∤K_7` and every prime factor of `K_7=(n+7)/4` quadratic-residue mod 7; the constructed primes also have `(n/7)=+1`, so “both branches of Corollary 2.7 are obstructed at shift `c=7`.” The paper’s references are real, but Thm. 4.14 is a specific shifted-criterion obstruction, not a signed-graph result. Comparing the `h=1`/one-pair exponent with it is numerically apt, but say explicitly that the mechanisms and sets differ.

13. **Jiang Thm. 3.2 / withdrawal — VERIFIED with crucial scope.** Jiang v1 PDF p. 8, Thm. 3.2 (“Rigidity”): for prime `p≥5` and a denominator `pV` of a **positive** solution, if `p|4V−1`, every solution containing `pV` is Type I; otherwise every such solution is Type II. It further states a Type I and Type II solution share at most one denominator. This is a positive-solution rigidity statement, not established for signed vertices by Jiang. Metadata in `sources/lit2026/README.md` and `LITERATURE_2026.md`: arXiv:2609.09204 v2 was withdrawn 13 Sep 2026 (“some errors”); only v1 is archived. The draft correctly notes withdrawal, but cite v1 and limit attribution to its stated positive case.

16. **Monks–Velingker Thm. 2.1(i) — VERIFIED; PDF is present.** PDF `sources/lit2026/monks-velingker-2008-erdos-straus.pdf`, p. 2, Thm. 2.1(i): `4 ≤ x ≤ ⌊p/2⌋ ≤ y` (for ordered positive solution `x≤y≤z`, prime `p`, numerator 4). Source is an unpublished 2008 manuscript (not a journal publication); metadata README records it present.

17. **Schinzel 2000 and ET’s description — direct source absent; ET statement verified.** No Schinzel 2000 PDF is archived in `sources/` or `sources/lit2026/`, so the original paper itself is **UNVERIFIABLE here**. ET PDF p. 8 states that a primitive residue class `n≡r mod q` “which is a perfect square, cannot be solved by polynomials”; it defines polynomial solvability just above as an identity `4/n=1/P1(n)+1/P2(n)+1/P3(n)` with the `Pi` integer-valued and positive for sufficiently large `n` (in the displayed formulation rational coefficients / integer values). ET references Schinzel [68], *On sums of three unit fractions with polynomial denominators*, *Funct. Approx. Comment. Math.* 28 (2000), 187–194 (ET PDF bibliography p. 56). Separately, Monks–Velingker PDF p. 2 quotes the more general Schinzel condition: if `(m,n)=1` and such an identity for `4/(mt+n)` has integer-polynomial denominators with positive leading coefficients exists, then `n` is not a quadratic residue mod `m`. Thus the user’s tentative “square class / positive leading coefficients” description mixes the ET residue-class formulation with the general affine-parameter form; ET’s stated square-class result is the safe citation. Schinzel bibliographic metadata is confirmed secondhand, not directly from the absent article.

## Main corrections for the draft

* Replace the Cor. 1.4 “polynomial identity obstruction” attribution (#9): Cor. 1.4 is a specific odd-square divisibility obstruction. ET p. 8 / Schinzel is the polynomial statement.
* BL Thm. 1.1 does say there is no Brauer–Manin obstruction to natural-number solutions; Thm. 1.8 supplies the more precise adelic formulation (#8).
* State explicitly the ET-to-chart coordinate map and distinguish the manuscript’s chart `e` from ET’s parameter `e` (#11).
* The Dahan Prop. 3.8 result is a finite list and a restricted `a=1` family failure, while Thm. 4.14 is the separate unconditional positive-density shifted obstruction (#12).
* Drop “windmill partition fragility” as an attribution to Elsholtz 2010 unless the intended exact claim and page are identified (#15).
