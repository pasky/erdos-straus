# Hostile review of FOREST_INVERSE.md

1. **MAJOR — §2, Theorem E′: the stated exclusion of A = 2 is false.** Take n = 5, v = 3, A = 2; more generally this works for every n ≡ 5 (8). The proof explicitly admits this exception while the theorem excludes it. The substantive Jacobi assertion survives. Here is the complete check: gcd(A,v) = 1 and A | n+v imply gcd(A,n) = 1. For v ≡ 7 (8), writing A = 2^a A′ gives (−v/A′) = (A′/v) = (−1/v)(2/v)^a = −1, and A′ = 1 is impossible. Since (2/n) = 1 when n ≡ 1 (8), reciprocity gives (A/n) = −1. For v ≡ 3 (8), the same calculation gives (−v/A′) = −(−1)^a, and (2/n)^a = (−1)^a cancels it, including A′ = 1. For 4 | v, write v = 2^b w, b ≥ 2: A is odd, A ≡ 3 (4), and (−v/A) = −(2/A)^b = −1; this works in both n-classes. Thus using the *calculation* in FDF Theorem E is legitimate. A = 1 is impossible because v | 2; A = 2 occurs exactly at v = 3 in the n ≡ 5 (8) branch. **Repair:** state these boundary cases correctly. Also remove “Theorem E with the coprimality hypothesis removed” in §0: FDF Theorem E already has no such hypothesis and proves the needed coprimality itself.

2. **MAJOR — §2, Theorem 2.6: the polynomial classification is false as quantified.** The theorem allows zero polynomials. For example T = 0, U = a, V = −ab−a satisfies the equation but neither conclusion. Also U = 0, V = −ab permits arbitrary T. Degree comparisons using deg(TUV) do not apply to these cases. **Repair:** require T, U, V to be nonzero, or explicitly classify the zero cases. For the nonzero case the proposed classification is repairable: if max(deg U,deg V) > 2, the degree inequality forces T and the other endpoint to be constants, after which (cT−1)U = ab+c forces degree 2 or a contradiction. For nonzero constant c, ab+c is irreducible in Q[a,b]; similarly c′ab+1 is irreducible for c′ ≠ 0. State those hypotheses, handle cT−1 = 0 explicitly, and give this argument rather than saying degrees ≥ 3 are impossible “by the same degree count.”

3. **MAJOR — §2, Theorem 2.6, integrality conclusion and “Reading”: several conclusions do not follow even after the zero-case repair.** Rational constants need not produce any positive integer words; congruences modulo rational c₁c₂ or cc′−1 are not the displayed integer congruences. The first family has fixed endpoints, but generally no fixed numerator: U = V = 1, T = ab+2 has both numerators ab+1. Furthermore the theorem classifies *words*, not certifying words: this example works at every n, including squares. The claim that these are “the Mordell covering, which misses the square classes mod 840” is false for arbitrary fixed congruence families. The certifying family U = 3, V = 116, T = (n+119)/348 includes n = 3361 ≡ 1 (840). **Repair:** restrict the integrality discussion to positive integral constants and the appropriate nonzero denominators; distinguish fixed-endpoint from fixed-numerator families; impose the certificate condition separately. B1 excludes covering all primes by a fixed finite certificate menu, not covering any prime in a square class modulo 840.

4. **FATAL — §0 items 1 and 6; Lemma 2.5 “These are the only words a factorisation supplies”; Proposition 2.7's title; Theorem 6.1 and its consequences: the advertised factorisation barrier is a non sequitur, with an explicit counterexample to its claimed consequence.** Describing a coprime line using the factorisation of n+v does not prove that information about the factorisation of n cannot force that line. Proposition 2.7's residue-multiset description and the exclusion q ∤ n+v when q | n, q ∤ v are true; no pointwise ordering of prime/composite difficulty follows. For a concrete infinite family, take a ≡ 5 (8), b = 2a+3, n = ab. Then

   n = (a+2)(2a+2) − (a+2) − (2a+2).

   Thus (t,u,v) = (1,a+2,2a+2) is a U-word, with u ≡ 7 (8), 4 | v, and gcd(u,v) = gcd(a+2,2) = 1. These are composites and n ≡ 1 (8), and the proof uses a relation between the factors while giving a **coprime** witness, not a scaled line. Examples are 65, 377, 945, 1769, 2849. This directly contradicts “any mechanism … using their factorisation must do so through the non-coprime (scaled) lines.” Theorem 2.6 concerns two *free* variables, not factors satisfying a relation. Rational functions are also outside its scope: T = 1, U = a, V = a(b+1)/(a−1) satisfies the identity and gives integer families on a divisibility locus. Ordinary integer-valued polynomials are in Q[a,b] and do not escape the corrected polynomial theorem; rational functions, restricted parameter loci, and arithmetic algorithms do. **Repair:** delete the universal barrier/consequences and “composite side closed.” Retain only the explicit divisor dictionaries and a narrowly scoped nonzero-polynomial classification. The brief expressly permits relations between factors; dismissing every resulting construction as “a word family in disguise” is not a valid no-go theorem.

5. **MAJOR — §4, Proposition 4.1(c), its Reading, and §6(v): this is not a mathematical independence theorem.** At a fixed integer n the statements (n/ℓ) = −1 cannot be independently negated while holding the arithmetic of n fixed. If “replace” means syntactically changing unused hypotheses of the definition of U, the assertion is a tautology, not a theorem showing that QNR information cannot help prove U. If it means changing n while preserving selected data, neither such a change nor invariance is established. “The quadratic character of n … is a function of the witness” also overstates what E′ says: one character value at the witness is forced, not the whole character. **Repair:** delete (c), or explicitly label a syntactic observation. Replace “nothing to act on,” “supplies nothing U can consume,” and “why no converse mechanism exists” by “no attachment implication is proved here.” A necessary condition becoming automatic at an existing witness does not make that condition useless for finding a witness.

6. **MAJOR — §4, paragraph after Proposition 4.2: the heuristic summation loses a logarithm.** The text asserts density (log ℓ)/ℓ and then claims summing over QNR *primes* ℓ ≤ X gives (log X)²/16 firings. Even granting that density model, the prime number theorem gives Σ_{ℓ≤X}(log ℓ)/ℓ ≍ log X, not log² X; a half-density QNR restriction does not restore the missing logarithm. FDF §5 M7 already distinguishes the approximately log X prime-numerator mechanism from the log² X all-modulus mechanism. The asserted per-modulus asymptotic is itself only a heuristic, not Proposition 4.2's divisor-count bound. **Repair:** remove the constant and the log² claim, specify the sampling model and overlaps, and label any average estimate HEURISTIC/Assessment.

7. **MINOR — §4, Proposition 4.2(c): the density argument needs hypotheses and a proof.** Specify that A′ is odd and nonsquare; otherwise the conditioning population (n/A′) = −1 is empty. The finite-modulus statements are valid: S_A consists of the eligible divisors of A+1, |S_A| ≤ τ(A+1)−1, and its residues have Jacobi sign −1. The uniqueness parenthesis is also valid for positive v: the only possible collision in [1,A+1] is 1 versus A+1, and 1 is in fact excluded from V. But summing infinitely many progression densities is not automatically an upper bound for the density of their union. **Repair:** supply the truncation argument: a witness at n ≤ X has A ≤ 2X+1, since either t = 1 and A ≤ n+1, or v ≤ (A+1)/2 and A ≤ n+v. The residue-count error Σ_{2^a A′≤2X+1} τ(2^a A′+1) is o(X) using a divisor bound. Then the geometric density sum and φ(A′) = A′^{1−o(1)} give the claimed O_ε(A′^{ε−1}), for a fixed 0 < ε < 1, with harmless extra constants for the condition n ≡ 1 (8). This bounds individual odd-part families, not all possible QNR-to-witness constructions.

8. **MAJOR — §4 final paragraph: the H₄ calibration is misused.** “The surviving cut must sometimes be far from every small vertex” does not follow from a lower bound on the least **larger** endpoint. A certificate can have one tiny endpoint and one enormous endpoint. The z49 context explicitly says its calibration does not constrain the least single endpoint or least López vertex. Nor does a vanishing density for each fixed modulus force a proof to “use unboundedly many moduli at once”: a modulus selected adaptively from n is not excluded. SS8's large-prime gcd/“privacy” lemma is not an impossibility theorem against all relations between moduli. **Repair:** restrict the calibration claim to both endpoints being small, and remove the claimed methodological necessity.

9. **MAJOR — §5, Theorem 5.3(c): the stated size and positivity of W(p) are false.** W is a weighted **count of nonresidues**, not a signed excess. Take j = 1 and w = 1 on the two patterns 3 and 4. The very class-number identity cited in the document gives

   W(p) = #{p/4 < q < p/2}/2 + h(−4p)/4 = p/8 + O(√p log p),

   not p^{1/2+o(1)}. Arbitrary weights can also be negative or cancel; nonvanishing of individual L-values does not make a linear combination positive. B_{1,χ} can vanish by parity, and bounded-conductor twists need not all be real. **Repair:** separate the unweighted interval main term, the signed character excess, and the nonnegative-weight hypothesis. For this particular two-cylinder signed excess, h(−4p) = p^{1/2+o(1)} follows from the class-number formula and Siegel's bound; the general weighted assertion must be deleted. The Dickson conclusion W₃ = 0 does not imply the claimed assertion about W.

10. **MAJOR — §5, Theorem 5.3(c): the cited theorems do not give the stated arbitrary finite-root assertion.** LOWER_TAIL_ASTRA Theorem **3.1** only treats the component of 1. FDA Theorem 6.1 repeats a pattern of an existing seed prime; it does not supply a seed with an arbitrary prescribed finite set of sterile roots. The correct source is LOWER_TAIL_ASTRA Theorem **3.2**, which explicitly constructs primes whose components meeting [1,H] are monochromatic, conditional on Dickson. **Repair:** cite 3.2 directly, keep the Dickson label, and describe the finite-support cylinder obstruction as a corollary of that existing fixed-root theorem, not a new obstruction to unrestricted global identities.

11. **MAJOR — §§5.1–5.3: the literal HJ boundary dictionary and the first-digit assertion are wrong.** For an interior word t,u,v ≥ 2, q = tv−1 satisfies p/q = [[u,t,v]], so b₁ = u is correct. It is not correct for every allowed word. At p = 17, (t,u,v) = (1,4,7) gives q = 6 and 17/6 = [[3,6]], whose first digit is u−1, not u. The word (2,18,1) gives q = 1 and first digit 17, not 18; the displayed inequality 0 < v/(tv−1) ≤ 1 needs a *strict* upper bound to deduce ceil(p/q) = u. Words with u = 1 have q > p: (4,1,6) gives q = 23, not q = 6. The loop (p+2,1,1) has q = p+1, not 1. The verifier silently reduces numerators modulo p, so its successful set comparison does not certify the literal correspondence in the prose. **Repair:** state the interior bijection exactly; for length two [[b₁,b₂]], one valid representative is (t,u,v) = (b₂+1,b₁,1), and another is (1,b₁+1,b₂+1). Handle q = 1 separately. Keep numerator reduction, multiplicities, and ordered versus unordered edges explicit. The finite-root conclusion can survive with these boundary conventions, but the proof currently given does not cover them.

12. **MINOR — §5.2, the paragraph beginning “What this gives”: endpoint ordering and counts are incorrect.** The first digit u is not necessarily the smaller endpoint: p = 17, (t,u,v) = (2,4,3) has first digit 4 > 3. First digits 3 or 4 occupy p/4 < q < p/2, whose size is p/4+O(1), not p/12. The number of short expansions in these lines is **O**(τ(p+3)+τ(p+4)), not ≍ that expression: the short count can be zero under the fixed-root obstruction, while the divisor functions are positive. **Repair:** remove “smaller,” correct the interval length, and use an upper bound. If the claim is about the signed class-number excess, subtracting the O(p^ε) short contribution indeed shows that long expansions carry essentially all of it for sufficiently large p; state that precise result rather than conflating it with W.

13. **MAJOR — §0 item 5 versus Proposition 5.2/Theorem 5.3: the summary removes the essential finite-support hypothesis.** Bounded prefix length does not mean a fixed finite set of prefixes: b₁ ranges up to p. A weight depending on b₁ with unbounded digit support can include every root. Even w ≡ 1 depends on the first digit but covers all q. For each p a decomposition into cylinders is finite, but its size and endpoint denominators can grow with p. **Repair:** everywhere restrict the fixed-root barrier to a fixed finite set of actual digit patterns, independent of p. **Verdict.** The draft is not sound as labelled. Its central “factorisation blindness” conclusion is disproved by a simple coprime factor-relation family, and its general cylinder-count asymptotic is false. Several theorem statements also have boundary/domain defects. The basic word decompositions, corrected reciprocity assertion, fixed-modulus counting obstruction, and finite census withstand the checks described here; much of that content is already in the repository. Retaining those facts requires abandoning the claims that the composite side is closed, that QNR input is mathematically unusable, and that this is a new clean negative for the attempted classes of mechanisms. ES and the stated inverse targets remain open.
