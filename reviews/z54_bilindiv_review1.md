# Z54_BILINDIV hostile review

**Overall verdict: CORRECT WITH FIXES.** The central Fourier-majorant reduction survives. The present package of claimed tool-class closures does not: several statements are false as written, and several purported exclusions must be downgraded to Assessment or EVIDENCE. None of the findings supplies positivity or progress on ES; the memo correctly keeps ES open.

Locations below refer to `Z54_BILINDIV.md` in the reviewed worktree.

1. **MAJOR — §3, “Quadratic-order reading,” lines 86–88: the split case is not a quadratic order in the stated ring.** If D is a square, the displayed α is an integer, so ℤ[α] = ℤ; its algebraic trace and norm are not s and m. D = 0 also requires a nonreduced algebra rather than a quadratic number field. Thus the interpretation fails precisely on the points being counted, although the divisibility calculation is sound. **Fix:** work in the rank-two algebra/order A = ℤ[t]/(t²−st+m), with involution t ↦ s−t and rank-two trace/norm. Then p+ᾱ = α(kᾱ−1) follows from p+s = km. For D ≠ 0 this is an order in a quadratic étale ℚ-algebra, split when D is square; D = 0 is a separate nonreduced case. Do not claim that literal ℤ[(s+√D)/2] gives this split order.

2. **MAJOR — §4.2, “Equivalent reading,” lines 145–148: the equidistribution equivalence is false.** With u* = (s−w)/2 and integer s, e(hw) = e(−2hu*). Equidistribution of w therefore controls only the even Fourier modes of u*, not its odd modes. Equidistribution of u* implies that of w, but the converse requires additional parity information. The following equivalence to fine-scale divisor/grid equidistribution also has neither a defined discrepancy nor a proved quantitative change of variables. **Fix:** replace the equivalences with the exact even-mode identity; formulate any stronger statement with parity classes, measures, ranges and error terms, or mark it Assessment.

3. **MAJOR — §4.2, lines 139–144: the curvature and frequency scales are not uniform on E1⁺.** The correct identity is W″ = −4(kp+1)/W³. It gives |hW″| ≍ hU³ only when W ≍ U. E1⁺ permits W much smaller than U; near the discriminant boundary the curvature is much larger, and W′ = (k²θ−2)/W likewise changes the relevant Fourier scale. **Fix:** restrict this discussion to D ≍ U², as in §4.3, or introduce dyadic W-ranges and account for the boundary. Do not describe ν ≍ hU² and the self-dual regime as a uniform analysis of the entire TD sum.

4. **MAJOR — §4.3 and the closures repeated in §§8–10: the proved obstruction concerns T_φ, not TD as stated.** A positively weighted sub-sum of a complex exponential sum is not bounded by the modulus of the full sum. TD bounds T(h); it need not bound T_φ(h). Failure of a specified implementation for this particular smoothed region therefore does not establish failure for the full T(h). Also, “the other order … returns exactly Σφ” is true only after applying the triangle inequality over k: summing ν first and retaining the k-sum returns T_φ itself. **Fix:** state Proposition 4.3 strictly as a barrier for the specified smoothed sum and implementation, or strengthen TD to a genuinely localized hypothesis and explain the relationship. Explicitly include the triangle inequality in the reversed-order statement. Carry this restriction into the verdict and FRONTIER text.

5. **MAJOR — §4.3, justification of implementation condition (iii), lines 172–175: the blanket coverage of named methods is not established.** The abstract implication from b_ν ≥ c₀‖c_ν‖₂ is correct. Direct exponent-pair bounds with a global Abel-summation amplitude factor can satisfy it: for |ν| ≥ 1 the usual |ν|^κN^λ factor, λ ≥ 1/2, is at least √N. But this does not cover every derivative test, Bombieri–Iwaniec implementation, subdivision, or exploitation of the complex coefficient's phase. The proof also invokes “relevant ν” without restricting the definition, which presently includes ν = 0 and all tails. Derivative magnitude ≥ 1 alone is not a universal square-root lower barrier for available upper-bound expressions. **Fix:** define the exact direct Abel/exponent-pair bound class, handle ν = 0 and discarded tails explicitly, and prove membership for any named additional bound. Otherwise retain (iii) as an assumption and delete the blanket claim. This does not invalidate the Parseval obstruction under the stated assumption.

6. **MAJOR — §5.1(a), lines 203–209: Fouvry–Iwaniec is misquoted.** `LIT_CHECK.md` item 3 gives a mean of |Δ_f(x;rs,a)| over s subject to rs² < x^{1−6ε}; the modulus is q = rs, not rs². The memo confuses the restriction with the modulus. Also, x ≍ p^{3/2} gives q ≍ x^{2/3}, not literal equality for the actual cutoff. **Fix:** quote the displayed averaged statement and its hypotheses exactly, and say “the 2/3 exponent endpoint.” The classical error arithmetic is otherwise correct: both q^{1/2} and x^{1/3} are of order p^{1/2}, comparable in power to the untied main term. The cited mean theorem does not give the needed single-prime-modulus result.

7. **MAJOR — §5.1(b), “Deficit,” and their summaries: the fixed-Q model is not a proved no-go theorem.** No asymptotic or lower bound for N_rel(Q) is established. Uniform averaging of gcd(k,Q) over the finite level interval is not justified, and no allowed dependence of Q on p is specified. In fact, if Q is the lcm of all levels in the interval, gcd(k,Q) = k imposes the entire congruence. Then each counted X gives u = (X+1)/k ∈ (U,2U] and X = ku−1 dividing p+u, so N_rel(Q) ≤ Σ_u τ(p+u) ≪ U^{1+ε}. Meanwhile log log Q = O(log U), contradicting a uniform lower bound ≫ U²/log log Q. **Fix:** restrict any model to specified fixed/small Q, label its main term Assessment, and report the finite counts as EVIDENCE. Replace “a fixed-modulus AP tool can impose it only…” and “the best such a tool could output…” with a statement about this particular relaxation, not all AP tools. The exact AP reparametrization itself is sound.

8. **MAJOR — §5.2, lines 223–230, and §8 short-interval bullet: the asserted necessary Δ-barrier is false.** A short-interval asymptotic can exploit cancellation in Δ(x+y)−Δ(x), without improving the global Δ exponent. Restricted divisors require further analysis not furnished by the unrestricted divisor summatory function. Moreover, Δ(x) = Ω(x^{1/4}) alone does not exclude an asymptotic at y = x^{1/4}: the main term there is x^{1/4}log x, larger by a logarithm. Calling an unverified x^{1/3} threshold “sharper” than 131/416 is also numerically backwards. **Fix:** say only that inserting the cited Huxley endpoint bound directly does not furnish the desired short-interval estimate; delete the necessity and Ω arguments. Keep 131/416−1/4 = 27/416 (correct). Do not claim closure of all short-interval techniques. The Shiu parenthesis in §4.1 should either remain explicitly conditional on a correctly stated theorem or be deleted; its statement was not verified here. The withdrawn Bourgain–Watt result is correctly not used.

## Checks that pass

* **Theorem T:** Lemma A applies under 16U³ ≤ n; each unordered product-divisible pair supplies a distinct square discriminant. The nonnegative periodized majorant is valid, χ̂_δ(h) = δψ̂(δh), the zero and paired nonzero constants 4 and 8 are correct, and the Schwartz tail gives U^{−εA} after increasing the decay order. The injection into (s,k) justifies |E1| ≤ Σ_s τ(p+s). With the small-U convention repaired, δ = U^{−η/2}, H = U^{3η/4} give contributions U^{1−η/2+ε/2} and U^{1−5η/4}; hence the advertised conditional saving follows.
* **Proposition 4.3's mathematical core:** the sign e(−νp/k) is correct. On the prescribed support m ≤ 1.5U²(1+o(1)), m > U², and D ≥ 0.2U² for large U. Differentiation gives W″ = −4(kp+1)/W³. The numerical denominator before rounding is (3/2)(3.5)³/4 = 16.078125, so 16.1 is safe; p² ≥ U⁸/c₂⁸ gives the displayed λ. The oscillatory-integral second-derivative lemma with sup amplitude plus total variation gives |c_ν(k)| ≪ (hU³)^{−1/2}. Parseval gives total squared mass ≫ U, individual ν squared mass ≪ (hU)^{−1}, and therefore Σ_ν‖c_ν‖₂ ≫ h^{1/2}U^{3/2}. The level-range ratio 1.485 is correct. The defects are the implementation/scope claims, not this calculation.
* **Proposition 5.3:** the fourth-derivative ratio on (1.5U,2U] is bounded independently of frequency. In the stated Titchmarsh formula, the derivative-comparability parameter is therefore constant; λ₄ ≍ h/U, K = 8 give h^{1/14}U^{13/14} + h^{−1/14}U^{11/14}. Dropping h^{−1/14} is harmless. Erdős–Turán with H = U^{1/15} gives discrepancy O(U^{−1/15}), hence the U/6 lower bound with O(U^{14/15}) error. P₁ symmetry and the τ² estimate give the claimed U^{1−ε} anatomy lower bound. No Ford short-interval theorem is needed for this proof.
* **Propositions 5.4–5.5:** t^{2/√3} ≥ (t+1)/2 for t ≥ 2 is valid (indeed t^{2/√3} ≥ t suffices). For Pick's argument convexity puts g₁ above its tangent and g₂ below its chord. The quoted width constant 1.4 is safe; substitution gives at most 8c₂⁴L/U + 89.6c₂⁸L³/U², so the displayed 16 and 90 are safe. Clip a window to the possible k-range if its original left endpoint lies below p/(4U²). Choosing small c makes the area < 1/2. The concave-quadratic shell has at most two intervals of total length 2√(2U/(ab)), hence at most that number plus 2 integer points. Horizontal/vertical uniqueness is valid; positive-slope lines also contain at most one strip point. The quartic has geometric genus at most 1, allowing singular/reducible degenerations.
