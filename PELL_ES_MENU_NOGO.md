**PROVED (ordinary integers).** For a finite menu of triples in `ℚ[P,x,y]`, put `M=lcm(840, all coefficient denominators)`. Among primes `p≤B`, `p≡1 mod M`, only `O_menu(√B)` admit **any rational** pair `x²−py²=−1` for which some menu entry is a positive integral ES triple. In particular the relative density of failures on this explicit progression is one. There is no height restriction or uniform selector assumption. More strongly, one can compute a (generally thinner) primitive **unit-square progression**, inside `1 mod M`, on which **every prime** misses the whole menu. Both conclusions still hold for coefficients in `ℚ(P)` rather than `ℚ[P]` with the modulus adjustment below. These are failures of the menu, not of ES; ordinary primes `1 mod 4` have integral negative-Pell witnesses.

### Global identities: no specialization loophole

Let `A=ℚ[P,y,x]/(x²−Py²+1)`, `K=ℚ(P)`. The affine surface is smooth and integral; over K the leading homogeneous form `x²−Py²` is irreducible because P is nonsquare. For a triple `(f,g,h)` form `H=4fgh−P(fg+gh+hf)` in A. A coordinate identically zero disqualifies the triple. If `H=0`, filter the generic conic ring by total `(x,y)` degree. Its associated graded ring is `K[x,y]/(x²−Py²)`, a domain, so minus degree is a valuation on the fraction field. Taking residues of `1/f+1/g+1/h=4/P` retains precisely the degree-zero coordinates; each of those is in K, where reduction is injective. Thus the retained reciprocals **alone** sum to `4/P` in K. Any positive specialization has all the retained denominators nonzero; subtract the specialized identity. No omitted summand can be positive if they are all positive. This excludes **every** positive success when any term is omitted, without bounds on p or the witness. Monic normal form in x and subsequent coefficient comparison in y give `A∩K=ℚ[P]`.

If all coordinates descend, they are polynomials in P. Positivity anywhere on `P>0` implies positivity throughout that ray: the positive locus is open and relatively closed because each coordinate there is at least `P/4`. At `p≡1 mod M`, integrality of each value implies integrality at 1 by clearing coefficients. At 1 three positive integer reciprocals sum to at most 3, not 4. For `ℚ(P)[x,y]` discard the finitely many coefficient poles; a descended rational function integral at unbounded integers must itself be polynomial: divide it into its polynomial part plus a proper fraction, and clear the fixed coefficient denominators of the polynomial part. The scaled proper fraction then has to be an integer tending to zero. Thus either the entry succeeds at only finitely many parameters (effectively bounded by this argument), or the same polynomial case applies. In this version use M formed with the denominators of the descended polynomial entries.

### Nonidentities: one parameter is counted, not the height of a point

If `H≠0`, its vanishing cuts out at most curves and points. Discard finitely many parameters from vertical components and isolated points. For a dominant ℚ-integral curve C write `L=ℚ(C)`; it is finite separable of degree d over K. Degree one would give `x(P),y(P)∈ℚ(P)` satisfying the Pell equation. At any negative rational P off their poles the left side is nonnegative, impossible. Hence `d≥2`. Choose a primitive element `z=x+k y` for the extension, with k an integer avoiding finitely many bad choices (separate the d conjugate pairs). Clear denominators of its minimal polynomial, taking its primitive numerator `G(P,Z)∈ℤ[P,Z]`. This is irreducible over ℚ and vanishes at `(P,z)` on C. A rational point above an integer n gives a **rational** root of `G(n,Z)`; exclude its finitely many degree drops. This argument uses ℚ-irreducible components only, not geometric irreducibility; alternatively a geometrically reducible ℚ-integral curve has rational points only in the finite intersections of conjugate components.

**Citation checked:** M. Paredes and R. Sasyk, [*Effective Hilbert's irreducibility theorem for global fields*, arXiv:2202.10420v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10420), p. 2, Thm 1.1, `K=ℚ`: for fixed irreducible `G(T,Z)` the number of integers `|n|≤B` for which `G(n,Z)` is not irreducible over ℚ is `O_G(√B)`. Here a rational root and preserved degree ≥2 imply reducibility; a parameter-independent irreducible G has no rational root at all. This yields the claimed bound after a finite union; **no log factor** is needed. Alternatively for integral witnesses Thm 1.2 directly gives `O_G(B^(1/d))` for each curve's integral z. The same degree-sensitive bound even works for rational z at integral n: writing `a(T)=lc_Z G`, use the integral root `a(n)z` of the monic polynomial `a(T)^(d−1)G(T,Z/a(T))`; it is still irreducible over `ℚ(T)` and lies in `ℤ[T,Z]`. The constant-polynomial case again contributes nothing. The PNT for the fixed reduced progression gives the relative-density claim.

### An entire unit-square progression of misses (stronger)

A direct appeal to thinness does **not** itself give this square progression. Treat each dominant curve separately, before CRT:

1. If `√P∈L`, then away from the poles of this rational function every rational point gives a rational square P. Rational squares are not primes. The deleted points have only finitely many parameter values.
2. Otherwise `L` and the quadratic `K(√P)` are linearly disjoint over K. Therefore `G(T²,Z)` is still irreducible in `ℚ(T)[Z]` of the same degree d. Hilbert irreducibility supplies an integer `t≠0` with `G(t²,Z)` irreducible of degree d. Finite bad values and any common content in T can be ignored. Its Galois group acts transitively on its d distinct roots and has a derangement (the average number of fixed points is one, while the identity fixes d). Chebotarev supplies a fresh prime `ℓ∤Mt` of good separable reduction and unit leading coefficient where `G(t²,Z)` has **no** root mod ℓ. For every `p≡t² mod ℓ`, the same holds for `G(p,Z)`; a rational root would be ℓ-integral by the unit leading coefficient, hence would reduce to a root. Thus this one *unit-square* residue excludes all rational lifts on C. No assumption that every base change under squaring remains irreducible was made.

Use a different ℓ for each curve in case 2. Combine these conditions with `p≡1 mod M` by CRT. Any remaining finite exceptional primes can be excluded by appending `p≡1` at each such prime not already in the modulus (a prime dividing the modulus cannot equal a member of a unit class). The resulting class is a unit square modulo the full modulus, since each local prescription is. It has infinitely many primes by Dirichlet. This is constructive for a specified menu: factor/eliminate over ℚ, check the quadratic subfield condition in the finite function field (factor `Z²−P` there), and in case 2 simply enumerate fresh ℓ and nonzero square classes until the no-root test passes; no numerical Chebotarev threshold is required. This refinement promises a square class, **not necessarily 1** modulo the additional primes.

**Scope / comparison.** `Z69_NL7.md` conditionally on Schinzel H excludes broader *divisor-selection* rules for a U₇ word along special quadratic prime-value families. The present unconditional exclusion is for **whole triples given by regular polynomials on one Pell fiber** and arbitrary selection among all its points; it neither forces primes of the form `t²+1` nor excludes choosing a divisor, a continued-fraction step, or a fresh ES denominator by unbounded search. Nor does the argument cover arbitrary rational functions of x,y: the valuation would then admit denominators of negative degree or degree-zero nonconstants. No ordinary ES counterexample or restriction on Pell solvability is asserted.
