# Positive Pell, four squares and a prime at which ES fails

**Verdict.** There is a countable discretely ordered, integrally closed domain R satisfying open induction, Lagrange's four-square axiom, and

```
Pell⁺:  ∀s>0 (¬∃r (r²=s) → ∃x>1 ∃y>0 (x²−sy²=1)),
```

with an infinite prime `P≡1 mod 840` and no positive ES triple at P. No PID, Bézout, negative Pell, or bounded induction hypothesis is used. The earliest mistake in a putative *square-landing* objection would be assuming that the inequality `y>0` alone is preserved: the real positive Pell branch can reach `(1,0)`. With Lag there really is an obstruction even to integral square specialization, but none is needed in this construction (§4).

## 1. The Pell stage

Let A be a countable normal finitely generated ℚ-domain in its ordered fraction field F, with only ℚ bounded in A by standard integers. For `s∈A`, `s>0`, `√s∉F`, take

```
B=A[z,v]/(v²−2z−sz²),      x=1+sz,  y=v.
```

Then `x²−sy²=1`. It is `z,v`, not just z, that we will put in every local integer ring. This ensures `x,y∈R` whenever `s∈R`.

**Normality and primes.** The monic normal form is `f(z)+v g(z)` over A. Thus B is free over `A[z]`, in particular flat over A, and after base change to any domain it injects into the generic fiber algebra over the fraction field of that domain. Over every characteristic-zero residue field with `s↦b`, the fiber is geometrically integral: for `b=0` it is the parabola `z=v²/2`; otherwise `z(2+bz)` has distinct simple roots and is not a square even over the geometric rational function field. The relative partials are `2v,−2(1+sz)`; they cannot vanish together by `(1+sz)²−sv²=1`. The morphism is smooth, and consequently B is normal over the normal base. In particular for every old nonunit prime element h the monic form over `A/h` and the integral generic fiber give `B/hB` a nonzero domain. This is permanence of the old prime, not an assertion that every new prime is principal. The monic form also gives `B∩F=A`.

**Order.** In an ordered extension of `F^rc` choose `z>F^rc` (greater than every old element), and take the positive square root `v=z√(s+2/z)`. This embeds the generic field, by the simple-root nonsquare test above, and gives `x>1,y>0`. For a nonconstant `f(z)+vg(z)` let `d=max(deg f,1+deg g)≥1`, with missing coefficients zero. After dividing by `z^d` its leading value is `L=f_d+√s g_{d−1}≠0`, since `√s∉F`. More precisely the error is infinitesimal relative to **every** positive member of `F^rc`: use `v/z−√s=(2/z)/(√(s+2/z)+√s)` and the lower powers of z. In particular the absolute value divided by `z^d` is at least `|L|/2`; even if L is tiny in the old order, the original element exceeds every standard bound in absolute value. Bounded elements of B are therefore exactly ℚ. At a square `s=r²` this argument would be false: on this branch `1+sz−rv` is the reciprocal of the infinite `1+sz+rv`. This is why the test is nonsquareness in **F at service time**.

**ES descent, independent of this order choice.** On `B⊗_A F` filter by total degree. The associated graded ring is `F[z,v]/(v²−sz²)` since the defining ideal is principal and the leading form of a product is the product of leading forms before passage to the quotient. It is a domain precisely here because s is a nonzero nonsquare in F. Thus degrees of products add; the elements of degree zero are the constants in F. For positive `w_i` in B with `Σ1/w_i=c∈F`, set `D=∏ w_i` and compare the top homogeneous parts in `cD=Σ D/w_i` (here `c>0`). Only the constant denominators contribute in degree `deg D`. Cancelling the nonzero leading form of D shows those constants already give reciprocal sum c. Subtraction in the original ordered field excludes any further positive term. Hence all `w_i∈B∩F=A`. Although the generic Pell curve is rational, it is **not** the whole polynomial line; using a rational parametrization alone for descent would be invalid.

**Exact local field embeddings.** Given an embedding `φ:F↪ℚ_l` and `b=φ(s)`, choose t transcendental over `φ(F)` in a sufficiently small `l^kℤ_l` with `k≥0` and `b t²∈lℤ_l`. Set

```
z_l=2t²/(1−b t²),         v_l=2t/(1−b t²).
```

The equation holds identically, both coordinates are integral, and `t=z_l/v_l` recovers the independent parameter. This embeds the function field since the formulas are birational (use `t=z/v` on the generic curve). It works also at 2, when b itself has negative valuation, and when b becomes a square in `ℚ_l`. No joint transcendence of z and v over the old field is possible or needed. If desired k may be enlarged so `1+b z_l` is a unit even for nonintegral b.

## 2. The other stages and simultaneous marks

Start with `A₀=ℚ[P]`, P positive infinite. For each ordinary l choose `π_l∈ℤ_l^×` a square unit transcendental over ℚ and congruent to 1 modulo `l^{v_l(840)}` at divisors of 840. This is possible by taking squares in an appropriate nonempty unit cylinder. Sending `P↦π_l` embeds `ℚ(P)` in each `ℚ_l` individually. Only the stage algebras, not their whole fraction fields, will need to take finite-adele values.

Use the polynomial approximation stages of `Z73_OPEN_BEZOUT.md` to place every finite-stage real-closure target at bounded distance from some stage element. Briefly, for an α not already at bounded distance adjoin `Y=α+t` with t bounded of residue transcendental over the old real closure's natural residue field. A bounded polynomial in Y would have every Taylor coefficient at α bounded by the Gauss residue test; if its degree is d>0 its top coefficient is a bounded old nonzero coefficient, hence rational, and the next coefficient implies α is at bounded distance from A, a contradiction. Locally choose Y integral and transcendental over the old countable image field. Polynomial stages preserve normality and prime elements and have the ES descent by degree of `Z73_ES_ROW_DESCENT.md` §1.

For each positive **infinite** stage element a use the four-squares stage `A[u₁,…,u₄]/(Σu_j²−a)` of `LOGIC_FOUR_SQUARES.md`. The points needed here are as follows.

* Monic normal form in the last coordinate gives flatness over A and intersection with F equal to A. All geometric fibers are integral and normal: at nonzero value the rank-four quadric is smooth, and at zero the integral three-dimensional quadric cone has singular locus only at the vertex, hence is normal by the hypersurface and codimension tests (`S₂` and `R₁`). Thus the flat finite-presentation morphism has geometrically normal fibers and preserves normality over a normal base (ascent of normality for normal morphisms). Monicity and integrality of the generic fiber over `A/h` preserve **every old prime**, even if `h|a`. Do not infer smoothness at the vertex simply from normality.
* Order by taking the first three `u_j/√a` with algebraically independent residues in the real unit ball over the old real closure's countable residue field; take the fourth as the positive root. For a nonzero monic normal form, scale its coefficients including their powers of `√a` by the largest absolute value. The resulting residue `G(t)+√(1−Σ_{1..3}t_i²)H(t)` is nonzero: at least one coefficient survives and the radical is nonsquare in the rational residue function field. Thus a positive-degree monomial with nonzero old coefficient makes the element infinite, since such a coefficient is not infinitesimal in A. Bounded elements stay rational. Filtering the generic algebra by total degree gives the domain `F[u₁,…,u₄]/(Σu_j²)` (rank four). The top-degree argument of §1 descends every positive reciprocal identity with old right hand side.
* At a place where `b=φ_l(a)∈ℤ_l`, four integral squares represent b: their sums form a compact set containing the dense nonnegative ordinary integers. For nonintegral b first scale by an even power of l and then undo the scaling. In either case b is nonzero by injectivity. At any such local point a derivative `2u_j` is **nonzero in ℚ_l**, even at 2; the local implicit function theorem permits varying the other three coordinates in an open ball while solving for this one. For integral b shrink the neighborhood to stay integral. Choose the three free values algebraically independent over the countable old image. This embeds the generic field (the radicand `b−Σ_{1..3}u_i²` is nonsquare there). Thus each coordinate is integral at every prime where a is integral. Requiring this only *almost everywhere* would not suffice for the universal Lag conclusion.

Dovetail the three kinds of tasks over the countable stages, testing the Pell predicate at service time; no effective decision procedure is asserted. A local choice at one stage never changes an old evaluation. Every fixed stage element is integral almost everywhere by induction: polynomial and Pell generators are integral everywhere, four-square generators wherever their input is integral, and a finite expression only adds finitely many scalar denominators. **No finite-prefix avoidance, Gaussian normalization, or sparse-prime argument is needed.** In particular there is no requirement that a nonzero element have valuation zero almost everywhere.

## 3. The pullback and the counterexample

Put `A=⋃A_j`, `F_∞=Frac(A)`, `R={g∈A : φ_l(g)∈ℤ_l for all l}`. Clearing finitely many negative valuations gives `A=R⊗_ℤℚ`, hence `Frac(R)=F_∞`. The directed union A is integrally closed: an element and a monic dependence equation in the union field are already in the field and algebra of one finite normal stage. R is also integrally closed in `F_∞`, being the intersection of A with the inverse images of the local valuation rings under the field embeddings. Its standard-bounded elements are ℤ. Local fractional parts of any fixed A-element can be matched by one rational number, by CRT with denominators. Subtract it from a bounded-distance stage approximant and make a standard integer adjustment: this supplies an R-floor for every member of `F_∞^rc`. The nonnegative cone therefore satisfies IOpen by Shepherdson's criterion.

If `s>0` in R is nonsquare in R then it is nonsquare in `F_∞`: any field root would satisfy `T²−s=0` and hence belong to R by integral closure. Thus at the stage servicing s the Pell task cannot be skipped for being a field square. Its integral z,v provide `x=1+sz>1,y=v>0` in R. This covers **all** nonsquares, not just primes or units, and includes standard nonsquares. For Lag, positive infinite `a∈R` receives witnesses integral at every prime by §2; bounded nonnegative a are the ordinary integers and use ordinary Lagrange, including zero. Coordinates for Lag can be replaced by their absolute values if nonnegative witnesses are wanted.

P remains nonunit prime in A by the stage prime checks; it is prime in R since every local value of P is a unit and an A-quotient by P is therefore integral whenever its numerator in R is. An alleged positive ES triple at P descends by §1 and §2 all the way to `G_i(P)∈ℚ[P]`, without changing the triple or its local values. Let D clear its coefficients. On the primitive class `n≡π mod D` all `G_i(n)` are integers (at l|D use the congruences for `DG_i`) and are positive for large n by positivity at infinite P. They satisfy the polynomial ES identity there. But this class is a square in `(ℤ/D)^×` by CRT on the square-unit marks; such primitive square classes are not polynomially solvable (Mordell–Schinzel; Elsholtz–Tao, discussion preceding Prop. 1.9 in *Counting the number of solutions to the Erdős–Straus equation on unit fractions*). Contradiction. This proves a **nonstandard** countermodel and a nonprovability statement for the displayed theory, not a counterexample in ℕ or independence from PA.

## 4. Arc and exponentiation checks

* If the proposed objection is “a positive Pell solution cannot land at a square, so no such model exists,” the **first false implication** is preservation of nontriviality on an arc: along the real branch `x=√(1+sy²)`, `y>0` can tend to 0 and x to 1. The local Pell chart even contains `(z,v)=(0,0)`. At an integer square `s=m²>0`, the equation forces `(x−my)(x+my)=1`, so `y=0`, indeed. With **Lag** there is a genuine finitely witnessed obstruction: in R, `y≥1` and `y−1=Σ b_i²`. Include those four witnesses; at any integer specialization they force `y≥1`, contradicting the preceding factorization for square s. So at the nonsquare prime P not even an arbitrary integral square specialization of *all those witnesses* exists. This agrees with `Z66_IE1.md` Thm K; the proof here descends ES denominators by degrees and never specializes the Pell or Lag witnesses to ordinary integers.
* P. D’Aquino, [*Pell equations and exponentiation in fragments of arithmetic*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0168-0072(95)00018-6), APAL 77 (1996), 1–34: the published abstract, transcribed in `data/z66/ie1/lit_ie1.md` §5, states equivalence of “every Pell equation has a nontrivial solution” and total exponentiation **over IE₁**. Thus also over stronger base theories; that citation does not establish IE₁ as the *least* sufficient base, or the same implication over IOpen. No IE₁ induction is obtained by adjoining solution variables.
* In fact this particular R has **no positive total unary operation** E on its nonnegative cone obeying `E(n+1)=2E(n)`. The embedding at 2 puts R injectively inside `ℤ₂`; hence `⋂_{k∈ℕ}2^kR=0` (external standard k). For positive infinite n, iterating the successor law an ordinary k times gives `E(n)=2^k E(n−k)`. This would force `E(n)=0`, impossible. So even IOpen + Lag + Pell⁺ here does not force an expansion by a genuine total positive base-2 exponential. This last test uses the recursion law and positivity explicitly; it does not assign a meaning to every proposed weak-base coding of the bare word “exp.”
