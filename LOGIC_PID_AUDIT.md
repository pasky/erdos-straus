# Independent checks on the adelic PID / odd-square construction

**Verdict.** I find no failed implication in the candidate `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` (parent proof, §§1–4), interpreted with finite-place approximation for **marked** regular functions. It gives, for any fixed primitive unit-square residue class, an external PID model of IOpen with an infinite prime P of that class and ¬ES(P). These checks are separate from the integer-landing, two-squares and Bertrand model. In particular, the statement is not that ES fails at an ordinary prime or in a model of PA.

### Ring and adeles

* At a nontrivial row stage take coprime nonzero a,b and `aU+bV=1`, with one free variable dominant over the whole old fraction field K. The two charts give `B∩K⊆A[1/a]∩A[1/b]=A`. A positive-degree element of K[U] is infinite. In reducing modulo an old prime s the residual row may have **one zero entry**; the other then becomes invertible and the ring is a polynomial localization of the remaining base domain. If both entries are nonzero the polynomial charts have a dense common open and glue to an integral scheme. Both cannot be zero. Thus s stays prime and a nonunit; Nagata at the prime factors of a proves the stage factorial. Polynomial floor stages preserve old primes. The union A is factorial (permanence of every factor in a finite stage factorization) and Bézout (dovetailed row tasks). Its fraction field K is purely transcendental with P among the basis coordinates. The standard finite-distance floor construction uses a fresh residue over the **real closure** of the old field, not merely an indeterminate of unspecified order.
* Precise embedding condition: at **each** ℓ the entire countable tuple in ℤ_ℓ must be algebraically independent **over ℚ**, not over ℚ_ℓ, nor merely coordinatewise transcendental. Avoid the reductions of successively longer finite prefixes of the list of nonzero integer polynomials. For each fixed prefix the sum-of-degrees bound leaves available points even with P restricted to the nonzero squares at every sufficiently large ℓ. At the fixed exceptional primes use the required nonempty open of square units (at 2 restrict modulo 8); lift in the residue cylinders by successive avoidance of countable algebraic closures. Every nonzero rational function then has finite valuation everywhere and valuation **zero** almost everywhere. Thus φ is a field embedding into `𝔸_f=ℚ⊗Ẑ` with image a subfield; 𝔸_f itself is not a field. The image is assembled from independent local embeddings, not the usual constant diagonal embedding except on ℚ.
* The positive-integer common denominator trick and `𝔸_f=ℚ+Ẑ` serve different purposes. The former gives Frac(R)=Frac(A): every a∈A has a standard positive multiple in R. For floors, multiplication is not enough: subtract a single rational from a finitely close approximant in A, matching all local fractional parts by CRT. The subtraction is bounded in the order, so standard integer translation gives the actual floor. Finite R-elements are ℤ. P is nonstandard, all local images are units, and `(P−r)/M∈R` follows from the prescribed components at ℓ|M.
* Any irreducible in A can be scaled by a rational to give all valuations zero; it is then prime in R by divisibility descent. Ordinary ℓ is prime by the reduction map onto 𝔽_ℓ with kernel ℓR. The A-factorization of a nonzero R-element into normalized primes leaves a rational scalar, which by its nonnegative valuations is an integer. Thus R is an **external** UFD.
* Divide two nonzero inputs by their R-gcd; the resulting f,g generate (1) in A and have `min(v_ℓ f,v_ℓ g)=0` for each ℓ. Choose uf+vg=1 in A. If f is a local unit, `(1/f,0)` is an integral local solution. The required additive correction to (u,v) equals `(g τ_ℓ,−f τ_ℓ)` with `τ_ℓ=v/f`; if g is a unit take `τ_ℓ=−u/g`. Take τ∈ℚ congruent to τ_ℓ modulo ℤ_ℓ at the finitely many bad primes, integral at all others. Then `u+gτ,v−fτ∈R`. This is a single scalar correction, not two independently rounded coefficients. For any nonzero ideal I of R, take a nonzero element of **minimum external prime-factor length**. Its Bézout gcd with any other member lies in I, so must have that same length and be its associate. This proves I principal, with no internal coding of ideals.
* Consequently each nonzero positive input has an externally finite set of divisors. All divisor-bounded collection/min/max schemes and arbitrary formula-defined partitions of its prime-power factors follow. This does **not** give unrestricted interval schemes: the 2-primary elements here are precisely the standard powers of 2, with no greatest such power below an infinite cutoff. In particular the construction does not imply bounded induction.

### Arithmetic audit: why a local square alone does not suffice, and what gives the integer point

Write `X_j' = X_j ×_{ℚ[P]} ℚ[t]` for the finite tower pulled back by P=t² (notation on spectra understood). Over X_j the map is finite free of rank two, so geometric codimensions of the inverse images of the missing row centers are still ≥2. Inductively X_j′ is smooth and geometrically integral: start from the t-line, take polynomial extensions or line bundles over dense smooth opens. Over t>0 its real locus is connected, by removing real codimension ≥2 in a smooth connected manifold, then taking a real affine line bundle (the explicit orthogonal row section trivializes it). At the ordered point use the **positive** square root of P; over real closures component statements transfer semialgebraically. Real positivity by itself does not force an integer point.

Here are the checks on the arithmetic purity argument. At each stage it needs the following invariant: remove any additional closed codimension-≥2 subset B, impose a nonempty finite-adelic open condition on the remainder, and prescribe t>H at infinity. One can also exclude any proper closed subset **at the rational point**, but not demand integral avoidance of a divisor. At the t-line base this is precisely CRT, allowing finitely many denominators, with arbitrarily large positive representatives. In the induction from base X to total space Y the following order of operations prevents a hidden gcd assumption:

1. On the two row charts (one chart for a polynomial stage) write equations for B as polynomials in the affine fibre coordinate. The entire-fibre-bad locus is given on each chart by vanishing of all their coefficients. It is closed on the row's base open and of codimension ≥2 there: over it the bad locus contains an entire line and `dim B≤dim X−1`. Include its closure and the old row center in the new forbidden base set Z. Separately, `D=closure(π(B))` is proper, but can be a divisor; do **not** include D in the integral codimension-two removal step.
2. These are finite presentations. Spread both charts, coefficient tests and the set-theoretic inclusion of B in the preimage of D after inverting finitely many integers (clearing denominators/powers for radical inclusions). Outside those primes, off Z at least one of the fibre polynomials is nonzero with a uniform finite degree bound d; outside D no fibre points are excluded. Include all ℓ≤d in the exceptional set. This precaution is necessary: a nonzero fibre polynomial can vanish at every rational point of a small residue field. All the induction's finite enlargements can be computed geometrically **before** selecting a rational base point.
3. At any added finite prime retain the original integral or other open constraint as an open condition. Given an adelic point in the required neighborhood, choose a small local chart and perturb within the neighborhood so that the projection is outside D over ℚ_ℓ (smooth chart, nonempty opens are Zariski dense). Projection of the chart neighborhood contains an open neighborhood on the base with the indicated fibre lift. Force `x∉D` rationally via one such local constraint at one place. Induction on X produces an S-integral x with the prescribed neighborhoods, t>H, avoiding Z upon reduction **at all primes not in S**. Forwarding integral constraints at added places is essential: simply declaring a bad prime excused could allow a noninteger ES denominator there.
4. The old row entries at x generate (1) in ℤ[1/S], by integral avoidance of the old center. Choose an ℤ[1/S] section, with the primitive syzygy `(b(x),−a(x))` as parameter direction. At each ℓ∉S where the reduction of x lies in D, choose one allowed fibre residue; these ℓ are finite since x∉D rationally, and an allowed residue exists by ℓ>d and avoidance of Z. At other good primes all integral parameters work. For ℓ∈S choose fibre neighborhoods lifting the prescribed constraints. Ordinary CRT with denominators then gives one rational fibre parameter integral outside S and meeting all required residual and local conditions. It leaves t unchanged. This proves the specialization needed here without an assumption about prime values of polynomials.

In the ES application the local φ_ℓ point extends by a unit square root to the square pullback at every ℓ. At all but finitely many primes **all generators** are integral; at the remaining primes only the marked quotients, Bézout coefficients, ES denominators and t need remain integral. These are open conditions at the given local points; impose oddness of t at 2. This distinction is real: if a chosen stage generator is `P/2`, at t odd it must evaluate to a noninteger. A theorem requiring that every element of the finite **ℚ-algebra** land in ℤ would also fail already for 1/2. Neither assertion is needed. Specialization gives a positive odd integer t>1 and integer values for all marked functions. At any possible boundary of their positive ES sector the inequality `4x,4y,4z≥t²>0` rules out a zero coordinate. The sector is consequently all of the connected positive-t locus when a positive solution exists at the ordered point.

Finally P|4xyz and P∤4, while P cannot divide all three denominators (cancelling yields 4≤3). For every nonmultiple w of P one includes the Bézout coefficients for `(w,P)=1`, not just an assertion of nondivisibility. For every multiple one includes its integral quotient. Thus the specialization is genuinely **strict** Type I or Type II at t²: each denominator is divisible by t² or coprime to t², with both kinds present. Elsholtz–Tao, *Counting the number of solutions to the Erdős–Straus equation on unit fractions*, Prop. 1.6 explicitly excludes both for odd perfect squares (`sources/lit2026/audit-elsholtz-tao-1107.1010.txt`, lines 118–120, 275). No assertion that squares have no arbitrary ES solutions is made.

### Smith comparison and scope

Verified secondary source: [Jeřábek–Kołodziejczyk, *Real closures of models of weak arithmetic*, Theorem 4.1 and its proof, pp. 13–14](https://users.math.cas.cz/~jerabek/papers/realclosures.pdf). They explicitly attribute existence of a nonstandard PID satisfying IOpen to **S. T. Smith, *Building discretely ordered Bezout domains and GCD domains*, J. Algebra 159 (1993), 191–239, Thm 10.7**. They describe his parsimonious remainder maps, Wilkie floor adjunctions, and construction F adjoining `v,(pv−1)/q`; do not claim these mechanisms as new. Their Theorem 4.1 adds a non-recursive-saturation property of the real closure, not the ES sentence. The independently checked extra ingredient in this candidate is the joint square-unit profile and marked odd-square specialization with coprimality. No global integer landing, full collection, simultaneous TS, or unprovability from IE₁, PA or ZFC follows from this audit.
