# Both Pell signs and integral prime two-squares at the ES counterexample

**Verdict (PROVED, relative to the floor and four-square stage lemmas in `LOGIC_POSITIVE_PELL_AUDIT.md`).** There is a countable normal discretely ordered domain `R` with IOpen and Lagrange, positive Pell for **all positive nonsquares**, and both negative Pell and integral two-squares for **every positive irreducible `s≡1 mod 4`**. Nevertheless a positive prime `P≡1 mod 840` has no positive ES triple. Negative Pell here is `x²+1=s y²` with `x,y>0`. This is not a PID or Bézout theorem. In particular extracting a sum of two *integral* squares from the rational norm `(x/y)²+(1/y)²` is not an inference used in the proof.

### 1. The proposed Pell-only base survives; naive independent adjunction does not

In `ℚ[P,x,y]/(x²+1−Py²)` the quotient mod `P` is `ℚ(i)[y]`, a domain. The relative partials cannot both vanish on the fiber, including at zero. Over `ℚ(P)` the leading form `x²−Py²` is irreducible, giving the degree descent of `LOGIC_POSITIVE_PELL_AUDIT.md` §1. The order with `y` above the old field and `x/y` tending to `+√P` preserves the bounded-elements test by that same leading coefficient calculation. Monicity gives intersection with the old field exactly `ℚ[P]`. At the ordinary marks one may start near `P=1,x=0,y=1`, independently at each prime: choose generic `x,y` and put `P=(1+x²)/y²`. Shrink to integral opens with square-unit `P` in the desired congruences. The local point need not lie on the chosen ordered branch. This base suffices for Pell alone. But subsequently adjoining the unrestricted circle `a²+b²=P` **destroys primality**: modulo `P`, `−1` already has a square root and the circle splits. Reversing the order of those two independent equations has the same defect.

### 2. A joint initial base

Start with `A=ℚ[a,b]` ordered at successive positive infinite scales, `P=a²+b²`. Adjoin `U,V` with **one** relation:

`C=A[U,V]/(b(U²−V²)+2aUV−1)`.

The imaginary part of `(a+ib)(U+iV)²` is now 1. Its real part `X=a(U²−V²)−2bUV` and `Y=U²+V²>0` give `X²+1=P Y²`; take `|X|`, which is nonzero in the function field. The *same* `P` is already the integral norm of `(a,b)`.

* **Total space and special fiber.** The projection to `A` is smooth: the Euler identity in `U,V` gives 2 on the hypersurface, so the relative partials generate 1. The generic conic is geometrically integral (its homogenized quadratic has rank three since `P≠0`); flatness over `A` injects `C` into that generic fiber, hence `C` is a normal domain. Modulo `P`, `b` becomes a unit: if also `b=0`, then `a²=0` and `2aUV=1`, an impossibility in a nonzero quotient. Set `i=a/b`. The quotient is *isomorphic* to `ℚ(i)[b,b⁻¹,U,V]/(b(U+iV)²−1) ≅ ℚ(i)[U,V,(U+iV)⁻¹]`, not a product after adjoining `i` independently twice. Thus `P` is a proper prime. The coordinates at the special fiber are tied, which is exactly what the independent adjunction lacked.
* **Descent and order.** Write `F=ℚ(a,b)`. The leading form on the generic conic has discriminant `4P` as a binary quadratic; `P` is nonsquare in `F` since it is irreducible in `A`. Its degree filtration therefore has domain associated graded. On any such filtered generic fiber with degree-zero part `F`, multiply a positive reciprocal identity with old RHS by the product of its denominators and compare top degree: the constant denominators alone give that same identity. Positivity forbids any other terms. Here `C∩F=A`: for each irreducible `r∈A`, `a,b` do not both vanish over `Frac(A/r)`, and the resulting nonzero binary form represents 1 over the algebraic closure (also at rank one). Thus the generic fiber at `r` is nonempty and `rC∩A=(r)`. A reduced fraction with a pole at `r` cannot belong to `C`, by multiplying through its denominator. For the order take `U>F^rc` and choose `V=(aU+√(P U²−b))/b`. In the monic generic normal form `f(U)+Vg(U)`, the leading coefficient at positive degree is `f_d+((a+√P)/b)g_{d−1}≠0`. All lower-order errors after dividing by `U^d` are smaller than every old positive field element. Thus every element outside `F` is infinite in absolute value, and the intersection check leaves exactly `ℚ` bounded in `C`. Descent from `A` to `ℚ[P]` uses the same argument on `a²+b²=P` over `ℚ(P)`: the binary leading form is irreducible over this ordered field and monicity gives the constant intersection.
* **Local starting points.** At *each* ordinary prime choose `a,U,V` algebraically independent in a sufficiently small integral neighborhood of `(0,1,0)`, and set `b=(1−2aUV)/(U²−V²)`. This chart embeds the whole function field in `ℚ_l`. Near the indicated center `b≈1` and `P≈1`, so `a,b,U,V` are integral, `P` is a square unit (at 2 require `1 mod 8`), and `P≡1 mod 840` at its relevant places. No equality `P=1` is imposed. These embeddings may be chosen **before** any later task.

### 3. Later operations; no finite-tree argument is necessary here

Dovetail all finite-stage inputs, extending orders and *all* local embeddings at each service. Keep normality, bounded elements `ℚ`, primality of `P`, and positive reciprocal descent. Polynomial approximation, four squares for positive infinite inputs, and positive Pell for every positive input nonsquare in its current fraction field are exactly `LOGIC_POSITIVE_PELL_AUDIT.md` §§1–2. In particular positive Pell uses the smooth `v²=2z+sz²` stage (parabola if `s` dies mod `P`); four squares need normality, not smoothness of their zero fiber.

Add the following two tasks for each positive infinite `s` with `s∉PA` in its current stage `A`, all `φ_l(s)` units and `φ₂(s)≡1 mod 4` (extend this notation to every current stage). If the test fails skip. It depends only on **already fixed** evaluations.

1. If `s` is nonsquare in the current ordered fraction field `F`, adjoin `x²+1=sy²`. It is monic in `x`, smooth over `A` (the partials `2x,−2sy` cannot vanish simultaneously), with geometrically integral generic fiber over `A` and also over `A/PA` since the respective coefficients `s` are nonzero. Monicity then proves normality via smoothness, domain mod `P` via generic injectivity, and the old-field intersection via the normal form. As in §1 order `y` above the old real closure and choose `x>0`. The slope `√s∉F` guarantees no positive-degree cancellation and gives the irreducible leading form for ES descent. At odd `l`, a unit `s_l` is split or unramified for the quadratic norm: over the residue field the split product or surjectivity of the finite-field quadratic norm gives a point of norm `−1`, and a nonzero partial gives an integral lift. At 2 choose `x=0` for `s_l≡1 mod 8` or `x=2` for `s_l≡5 mod 8`, taking `y²=(1+x²)/s_l` a square unit in either case. At all places take a generic integral point in that neighborhood: a partial is nonzero over `ℚ_l` even at 2, so one parameter varies in an open ball and may be transcendental over the countable old image. Geometric integrality then gives a field embedding (a nonzero kernel on this affine curve would give algebraic coordinate values).
2. Adjoin `u²+v²=s` for the integral two-square witness. This time `s∉PA` is enough; no Gaussian UFD or split/inert test at the target `s` is required, since only `P` must remain prime. Monicity and the nonzero conic over `Frac(A/PA)` prove `P` persists. **Normality caveat:** one may *not* call the circle smooth or geometrically normal over an arbitrary zero of `s`. Here is a direct repair. `D=A[i]` is a normal domain by finite étale base change and the ordering of `F`. The complexified algebra `D[M,N]/(MN−s)` inside `Frac(D)(M)`, `N=s/M`, is exactly `D[M,M⁻¹]∩D[N,N⁻¹]`: for `∑c_j M^j` in the first ring, membership in the second asks `c_j s^j∈D` for every `j`, i.e. divisibility by `s^{−j}` at negative exponents. The intersection is normal; the real ring is normal too as the fixed subring (or intersect upstairs with the real function field). The standard scaled-coordinate order on a circle of positive infinite radius squared maintains bounded elements `ℚ` by normal form and a fresh residue; the degree filtration has irreducible leading form `u²+v²` over ordered `F`, giving ES descent. Local unit targets are sums of two integral squares: at odd places use the finite-field norm/smooth lift, and at 2 take the square root of `s_l` or of `s_l−4` as appropriate. Perturb generically as in the first task.

All newly adjoined coordinates in these two conditional tasks are integral at every mark. Other steps preserve integral-almost-everywhere for each fixed stage element by the old stage lemmas. **No** assertion that every nonzero field element is a unit almost everywhere is required. Hence no prefix avoidance, compactness of the family of embeddings, or guessing the future acceptance path is necessary. If one did use the square pullback for prefix sampling, its generic torus being split would *not* be a valid substitute for the nonsquare leading-form test on the actual ordered stage.

### 4. Final check of the universal predicates

For the path union `A∞`, put `R=A∞∩⋂φ_l⁻¹(ℤ_l)` inside its fraction field. Clearing finite pole support by a standard integer gives `A∞=R⊗ℚ`. CRT translation by a rational clears the fractional parts at the finitely many bad places; hence bounded-distance approximation and an integer adjustment give floors. This proves IOpen. Normality follows from normality of the directed union in its fraction field and intersection with valuation rings. Positive Pell for every nonsquare of `R` follows since a fraction-field square root is integral over `R`, hence in `R`; Lag follows from the all-place witnesses wherever the input is integral. `P` is prime in `R`: dividing by it does not affect local integrality. An ES triple would descend to `ℚ[P]` by these degree tests; the primitive square-class contradiction, including the all-three-divisible case, is in `LOGIC_SPLIT_SQUARE_TEST.md`. No other witnesses are specialized to an ordinary square.

If `s` is an infinite positive irreducible in `R`, every mark is a unit: failure at `l` gives `s/l∈R` and a proper positive factorization. Also `s` is not a square in `Frac(R)` by normality. If `s=Pa` at any rational stage then `a=s/P∈R` by the unit marks of `P`, so irreducibility and discreteness give `a=1`. Thus a final irreducible `s≡1 mod 4` distinct from `P` passes **both** predicates at service; the special base supplies both at `P`. There is no implicit identification of irreducibility and ideal-theoretic primality in a general normal ring. Standard primes use ordinary two squares and negative Pell: for prime `p≡1 mod 4` take the least `A>1` in a positive Pell solution `A²−p B²=1`. Even `A` is impossible since the coprime odd integers `A±1` would be a square and `p` times a square, yet one is `3 mod 4`. For odd `A`, the consecutive coprime integers `(A±1)/2` have product `p(B/2)²`. Assigning the larger one the square gives a smaller positive Pell solution; assigning the smaller one the square gives the required negative solution. This use of ordinary Pell does **not** assert negative Pell for arbitrary composite `1 mod 4`.

**Scope.** The construction intentionally does not protect arbitrary old primes. Their splitting or loss of irreducibility may be essential, in particular on the circle tasks. There is no deduction about ES over the standard integers, no PID strengthening here, and no inference to bounded induction or total exponentiation.
