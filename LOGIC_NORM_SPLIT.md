# Keeping prime divisors and two squares in the Bézout countermodel

**Result (branch proof for audit).** There is one model of IOpen + Bézout + PD + TS + cofinal primes with a positive infinite prime p≡1 modulo every standard integer and ¬ES(p). PD says every a≥2 has a positive prime divisor. TS says that every positive prime ≡1 mod 4 is a sum of two squares. ES is the actual three-positive-denominator equation. The strengthened model also satisfies E(−1): for such a prime, −1 has a square root modulo it. No collection axiom is asserted for this model.

Use the floor, normal-cone, and unimodular-row stages and integer-landing descent of `LOGIC_BEZOUT.md`. This note specifies changes to the induction invariant, verifies the additional stage, and gives the bookkeeping proof. In particular smoothness of the *whole* finite stage is not required and should not be claimed.

## 1. Old stages and initial prime

Maintain a geometrically integral finitely generated ℚ-domain A, both A and A[i] factorial, finite ordered elements of A exactly ℚ, and a **smooth** rational point e. Use the semialgebraic connected component of e in `(Spec A)_sm(ℝ) ∩ {p>0}`; the ordered generic point must lie in that component after real closed extension. The ordered generic point is smooth. Start with A=ℚ[x,y], with x positive infinite and y greater than the whole ordered field ℚ(x), and e(x)=1, e(y)=0. Only constants are bounded (order first by degree in y, then by degree in x). Set **p=x²+y²**. It is prime in the rational polynomial UFD (−1 is nonsquare in ℚ(x)), positive infinite, has e(p)=1 and is already the Gaussian norm of x+iy. The real smooth locus with p>0 is the punctured plane, connected, and contains the landing and ordered points after transfer. Thus p is protected at initialization; it is never an inert target. A linear p=X+1 from the base note would not have this protection. A Gaussian norm prime will never be the prime targeted by the new operation. At all stages A×=ℚ× and A[i]×=ℚ(i)×: for the latter, the norm of a unit is a rational unit, so its two real coordinates are bounded and rational.

* A polynomial floor adjunction preserves both UFDs; the new smooth locus over the old smooth locus is a product. One may also freely add a variable above **all** old field elements with rational landing, since every positive-degree polynomial in it with old coefficients is infinite.
* For a row stage on a coprime pair g,h, the pair remains coprime in A[i]. Indeed a height-one prime upstairs containing both would contract to a height-one prime in A (finite étale constant extension, or going down and incomparability), impossible. Over either field, localizing at h gives a factorial polynomial localization; for each factor l of h the quotient by l is `(A/l)[1/g][V]` over the field in use. It is a nonzero domain. Apply Nagata as in the base note. The real codimension-two removal is applied to the **old smooth component**, not the full singular variety. The resulting real bundle is smooth over the smooth open and connected there. For any old prime s in A, reduction of the row is an affine-line bundle over a nonempty open of Spec(A/s); hence s stays prime.
* At a normal-cone stage `C=A[D,𝔪/D]`, the kernel 𝔪 of e is a smooth rational maximal ideal. Its graded ring is polynomial even if A is singular elsewhere. The same proof and localization at D give factoriality over ℚ(i): formation commutes with scalar extension, and 𝔪A[i] is a smooth ℚ(i)-rational maximal ideal. For D>0 the old smooth open gives a smooth product; the chosen tangent point on D=0 is smooth by flatness over ℚ[D] and geometric regularity of that fibre. The tangent path consists of smooth points, so its limiting tangent point lies in the requisite component of the smooth locus. For any old prime s with e(s)≠0, D is a unit modulo s by `D·((e(s)−s)/D)=e(s)−s`, so `C/s=(A/s)[D,D⁻¹]` is a nonzero domain. No need to preserve all primes through a deformation.

**Baseline without norm splits.** These three operations already prove PD for the unmodified Bézout model. Every nonzero r with nonzero integer landing factors externally finitely in that model: factor at its A-stage and normalize each nonconstant prime factor to evaluation 1, which is possible because none has zero evaluation. Such primes remain prime forever by the preceding checks and descend to the integer-landing ring. The residual integer scalar factors in ℤ. For a nonzero element landing at zero, any standard prime divides it. Standard primes remain prime since reduction of e mod ℓ has kernel ℓR. Additional fresh variables S′ above the old field with e(S′)=0 supply cofinal primes `1+(S′)²` by the same argument. **Do not infer finite factorization at nonzero landing in the norm-split variant below:** it intentionally splits some of these factors.

## 2. New stage for an inert normalized prime

Let q be a nonconstant A-prime with e(q)=1. If q ceases to be prime in A[i], it already is a normalized norm. Indeed `(A/q)[i]` embeds in `Frac(A/q)[i]`, and over the generic point the algebra is either quadratic or a product of two conjugate fields. The fibre ring is reduced (characteristic zero) and its minimal primes lie over the generic point (finite free, here also visible by localization in the indicated embedding). Hence in the nonprime case the UFD factorization consists of two distinct conjugate factors, with multiplicity one. Write `q=c h·conj(h)`; the unit c is rational since it equals its conjugate. Evaluating gives `q=N(h/e(h))`. The two coordinates of h/e(h) have evaluations 1 and 0, hence belong to the final R. In particular these normalized split primes are positive in the order.

In the remaining case A[i]/q is a domain, equivalently i∉Frac(A/q). Put

`B=A[X,Y,W]/(HW−q),  H=X²+Y²`, with `e(X)=1, e(Y)=0, e(W)=1`.

We make H (not q) the protected positive prime divisor of q. Even if q takes negative values on parts of the old real component there is no sign requirement on W. This avoids the obstruction to adjoining a norm representation of q itself.

**Algebra checks.** Over any coefficient domain E with nonzero q₀∈E, `E[X,Y,W]/(HW−q₀)` embeds in `E[X,Y,1/H]` by W=q₀/H. Indeed q₀ is regular mod H: polynomial division by H monic in X makes `E[X,Y]/H` free over E[Y]. If H times a polynomial belongs to (HW−q₀), reduction modulo H shows the multiplier of HW−q₀ is divisible by H; cancel. Thus the ideal is saturated at H, and the localized assertion is immediate. This gives geometric integrality by scalar extension (the old A is geometrically integral), and for every old prime s not associate to q gives **B/s a nonzero domain**, using E=A/s. Hence protected primes other than the target survive, even if H factors modulo them.

`B/H=(A/q)[X,Y,W]/(X²+Y²)` is a domain: pass to Frac(A/q), where −1 is nonsquare, and use monicity to descend injectively. Thus H is prime; B[1/H]=A[X,Y,1/H] is factorial, and Nagata gives B factorial. Over ℚ(i) put U=X+iY, V=X−iY. The relation is UVW=q. Each of U,V is prime: its quotient is a polynomial ring on the other two variables over `A[i]/q`, a nonzero domain. Inverting both gives `A[i][U^±1,V^±1]`, a UFD. Nagata proves B[i] factorial. The open H≠0 is just the old variety times `{H≠0}` in the plane. Its real part over the old smooth component is smooth and connected because ℝ² minus the origin is connected. It contains both the new rational point and (after transfer) the ordered point. This is enough: singularities with H=0 are allowed and will not enter later component arguments.

**Ordering — joint bounded-coefficient test.** Write K=Frac(A). First take a finite τ with residue transcendental over the archimedean residue field of K. Then take a positive infinite T so slow that every positive infinite element of **K(τ)** dominates T^j for every standard j. These choices exist by the cut and compactness constructions in the base note. Use X=T, Y=Tτ, W=q/[T²(1+τ²)]. They are consistent with the indicated embedding of B, since X,Y are algebraically independent over K.

Write an element of B as `P(X,Y)/H^k`, using an expression polynomial in W; if its W-degree is k, this gives

`P=Σ_{j=0}^k a_j(X,Y) q^j H^(k−j)` with `a_j∈A[X,Y]`.

For `P=Σ_d P_d` decomposed by total degree, the ordered element is a Laurent polynomial in T with coefficients `P_d(1,τ)/(1+τ²)^k` and distinct exponents d−2k. If **any** coefficient of P in A is nonrational, it is infinite. In that homogeneous degree the fresh-residue test makes P_d(1,τ) infinite: division by an old coefficient of largest absolute value leaves a nonzero residue polynomial in τ. The denominator (1+τ²)^k is finite and bounded away from zero. At the slow T scale the infinite coefficient still gives an infinite term, even if its exponent is negative. Distinct nonzero terms have distinct magnitudes up to infinitesimal ratio: compare their old coefficients in K(τ) first and then their powers of T. The largest term dominates. Thus a bounded element requires **P∈ℚ[X,Y]**.

Now reduce the displayed expression for P modulo q *in the old polynomial ring*. It yields `P≡a₀ H^k mod q`. The monic division of the rational P by H^k in X over ℚ[Y] has the same remainder after embedding ℚ in A/q. That remainder must vanish. Thus H^k divides P already over ℚ, and our element lies in ℚ[X,Y]. Any nonconstant such polynomial has a positive highest power of T with a finite nonzero residue coefficient (τ has transcendental residue) and is infinite. Hence bounded B=ℚ. This rules out in particular a bounded nonconstant ratio of forms of the same degree; comparison of exponents alone would not rule it out.

## 3. Scheduling and extraction of PD, TS

Besides all floor and Bézout tasks, schedule every nonconstant stage element r with e(r)=±1. When its task is serviced factor it **at the current finite stage**, choose one nonconstant prime divisor q, and scale q to evaluation 1. The quotient r/q still has integral evaluation. If q is Gaussian-split, record q and its normalized norm identity. Otherwise apply the new stage and record H and its norm identity; H divides r with quotient of integral evaluation. In both cases the recorded prime at its creation is positive, has evaluation 1, splits over the Gaussian extension and is irreducible over the rational stage. It remains prime forever: the old stages preserve every prime with nonzero evaluation; at a new norm split it cannot be associate to the target because its two nonunit Gaussian norm factors persist. **Reason they cannot turn into units:** their norm is the same nonconstant element, and all stage units over ℚ(i) are constants. The chosen p is such a protected prime from the start. No induction on potentially unbounded factorization lengths in the union is needed. Even an inert normalized prime negative in the order can undergo this step: W then accounts for the negative sign. The moving cofactor W need never stabilize.

There is an important sign subtlety: scheduling **all** nonconstant r of landing ±1 as just described could deliberately leave no *negative* normalized prime in the final ring; this causes no problem. At any given stage a divisor H of a negative q is positive but its cofactor is negative. All divisibilities here take place in the ring, not its positive monoid, until r itself is positive.

Let R be the preimage of ℤ in the union. The proofs of Bézout, open induction and ¬ES(p) from the base note apply verbatim using the smooth component invariant. A recorded prime s stays prime in R: if s divides an R-element in the union, the quotient has integer evaluation because e(s)=1. Standard primes ℓ remain prime in R and divide precisely those r with e(r)≡0 mod ℓ. Therefore every positive r≥2 has a prime divisor: for |e(r)|≠1 use such an ℓ (also when e(r)=0); for e(r)=±1 and r nonconstant use the task. If a positive nonstandard r is itself irreducible, it must be the recorded positive prime dividing it, as the only units in R are ±1. In particular **all positive nonstandard primes are normalized norms with representations in R**, a little more than TS. For standard positive primes ≡1 (4), use the ordinary integer two-square theorem. To obtain E(−1), for a prime r=a²+b², b is not divisible by r (otherwise r|a, contradicting r²∤r); invert b modulo r by Bézout and square a/b there. This step uses the field quotient, not a representation-only inference in a GCD domain.

For cofinality schedule variables S′ above the entire current ordered field, landing at 0, at cofinally many stages in the countable chain. The primes 1+(S′)² are prime at introduction (−1 nonsquare in the old ordered field, and monicity), split Gaussian norms of evaluation 1, and so survive every operation. They dominate the old stages. This completes the joint construction. No claim that R satisfies collection, general quadratic reciprocity, IE₁, or PA.
