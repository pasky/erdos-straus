# One-prime thickening and adelic Bézout (internal proof audit)

**Outcome.** There is a nonstandard model of IOpen + Bézout + internal Dirichlet, with an infinite prime P≡1 mod 840 at which ES fails and the NR certificate holds with a **standard prime modulus**. This proof does not give an external PID: indeed this model cannot be one. The new ingredient is a multi-prime, variable-depth *summable* bound, needed even to perturb all the exact 1's off their algebraic values. No change of the repository or assertion about standard ES.

## 1. Uniform compressed-modulus bound, including the mixed families

Use the seven families exactly as in ET Prop 1.9. Let m be a displayed family modulus (for the double congruences take their coprime product; count individual unit root classes in I-3). Call a family class r mod m admissible at compression N if N|m, r≡1 mod m/N, and in families with a factor 4 in the displayed principal modulus, the part of N peeled from this modulus comes entirely from the factors after the 4. This last condition holds automatically in the application since at 2 the frozen depth is ≥2. No coprimality of N and m/N is assumed.

**Lemma.** The number of admissible parameterized classes at N≥2, including all possible m, is O_ε(N^(4/5+ε)), and each m≪N^C with C absolute (absolute multiplicative constant permitted). At N=1 no admissible class contains the unit square 1.

Bookkeeping: peel the factors of N from the respective modulus blocks, and, within a product block, allocate them to its factors (e.g. a=Aa', c=Bc'). The number of splits is N^o(1); choose one allocation per event, not necessarily coprime. Every admissible tuple injects, up to these splits, the family label, and the choices of an I-3 root, into positive solutions of

    4xyzd = L x + M y + H z,

where the product of any two distinct coefficient slots is ≤N². Below coefficients may be permuted with their variables. In inverse residue classes r≡−1/e, the compressed condition still implies e≡−1 on the retained block.

* I-4, II-1: a=Aa', b=Bb', N=AB, e=4a'b'D−1, c=(a+b)/e. Then 4a'b'cD=Aa'+Bb'+c.
* II-2: f=F f', F=N, c=(f+1)/(4ad). Since f'|4a²d+1 and c(4a²d+1)−af=a+c, write w=(a+c)/f'. Then 4acdw=F a+F c+w.
* I-2: a=Aa', c=Bc', f=F f', N=ABF. The retained blocks give f=4a'c'D−1 and f'|a+c. With w=(a+c)/f', obtain 4a'c'Dw=FAa'+FBc'+w. Product of the two large slots F²AB≤N².
* I-3, II-3 and I-1: denote the entries of the principal 4cd block by c=Cc', d=Dd' (rename a to c for II-3/I-1). Write f for the auxiliary f or e. In the double-congruence cases f=F f', N=CDF; in I-1 put F=1, f'=f. Then f=4c'd'h−1 and f'|1+4c²d. For I-3 this uses r≡1 on the retained part of the *root* block, not any parity assumption. For II-3 reduce its residue separately on 4cd and f. For I-1 the divisibility is its defining condition. With J=C²D we have h(1+4c²d)−Jc'f=h+Jc', so w=(h+Jc')/f' is integral. Then 4c'd'hw=F h+FJc'+w and F²J≤N². All original parameters are recovered. Each admissible I-3 tuple has at most 2^{ω(f)+1} choices of a unit root by CRT.

Counting the displayed weighted equation is elementary (no uniform-in-S hypothesis on ET needed). Permute the summands so Lx≥My≥Hz. Put e=4yzd−L; then yzd≤3L/4 and 0<ex=My+Hz≤2My. Hence

    e² (zd)(xz)(xd) = (ex)²(zd)² ≪ M²L² ≤ N⁴.

At least one of e,zd,xz,xd is O(N^{4/5}). All variables have polynomial size in N (L,M,H≤N², yzd≤3L/4 and x≤2My). Each fixed small parameter gives N^o(1) solutions: for e factor 4yzd=L+e; for xz split it and factor (4xz d−M)y=Lx+Hz; for xd split it and factor (4xd y−H)(4xd z−M)=MH+4xd Lx; for zd similarly factor (4zd x−M)(4zd y−L)=LM+4zd Hz. All factorization targets are positive, polynomially bounded. This also bounds the recovered moduli polynomially and absorbs the I-3 root multiplicity. This proves the lemma; the exponent is weaker than Z66 for some single-prime events but is strictly below one and handles *all* mixed supports.

## 2. Remove all exact zeros without losing uncovered mass

Fix a sufficiently large ℓ≡3 mod 4, ℓ∤840. Let π_ℓ be Haar on units. For a finite list S of other active primes let π_q be independent uniform on 1+q^{K_q}ℤ_q (K_q≥1; at 2 take ≥3 and at all q|840 respect the exponents). Freeze others exactly at 1. Measure survival inside the ℓ-nonresidues. At S empty, Z66_UCOVER Thm T gives surviving mass δ>0 (also follows by the lemma and summation over ℓ-powers). For an event at m set K_ℓ=0 and N=∏_{q∈S∪{ℓ}}q^{max(v_q(m)−K_q,0)}. A possible hit requires r≡1 mod m/N. Each class has probability at most ℓ/(ℓ−1)/N. The compressed-modulus lemma therefore bounds the sum of hit probabilities for N>J by a constant times

    Σ_{N>J, N supported on S∪{ℓ}} N^{−1/5+ε}.

For fixed S and ε<1/5 this converges (finite geometric Euler product), uniformly in the frozen depths.

To unfreeze one new q at depth K, all classes at v_q(m)≤K have exactly their old membership. Newly possible hits have m≥q^{K+1}. Apply the bound with enlarged, **fixed finite** S∪{q}, independent of K: the polynomial size bound m≪N^C forces N→∞ uniformly among these new hits as K→∞. The loss of survival mass tends to zero. Enumerate all primes except ℓ and choose successive K to have total loss <δ/2. Take the infinite product measure. Its finite-active, otherwise frozen approximants converge weakly (cylinder probabilities eventually agree). Since U intersected with the ℓ-nonresidue condition is closed, its infinite-product probability is at least the limsup, hence ≥δ/2. Equivalently approximate from above by decreasing clopens. Each final local distribution is non-atomic, so remove the countable union of events that some coordinate is algebraic over ℚ. This gives an uncovered, componentwise-transcendental unit profile π, with π_q≡1 mod q for every q≠ℓ. Thus no polynomial has exact local zeros, but T−1 has positive valuation at every q≠ℓ. This deliberately **is not parsimony**.

## 3. Restricted adelic stage evaluations, including Dirichlet

Use the Z73 rational ordered row tower with Dirichlet tasks as in LOGIC_PID_DIRICHLET_AUDIT. Need field embeddings φ_q:F→ℚ_q sending P to π_q and evaluating every fixed g∈A integrally almost everywhere; this is NOT required for inverses in F. In each row step choose as pivot an entry not divisible by P−1 in the stage (coprimality); use the other free row coordinate for a transcendence basis. At each finite stage the fiber P=1 is geometrically integral rational: inductively it is a product-line extension or a line bundle above the dense complement of a proper locus in the preceding rational integral fiber. The locus is proper because P−1 stays prime and cannot divide both coprime row entries. The pivots define a dense rational chart in this fiber. Every finite prefix Dirichlet output Q=a+mY is nonzero in this fiber, initially since a,m are not both divisible by P−1 and Y is free, and hence in subsequent stages by dominance of fiber projections. Choose a rational point of this chart avoiding the finitely many output zeros. Equivalently choose values for the free parameters at P=1 at which all chart denominators and these outputs are nonzero.

Outside a finite set of primes, this prefix point is integral, its chart denominators and outputs are units. For q≠ℓ we have π_q≡1 mod q. Taking the prefix parameters congruent to the chosen point therefore gives all stage generators integral and all prefix Dirichlet outputs units there, **even after fixing P=π_q**. Use successively increasing cutoffs so q handles prefixes of length tending to infinity. Lift each prefix to coordinates algebraically independent over ℚ(π_q) in their prescribed opens. For the tail choose successive algebraically independent integral parameters; for each Dirichlet Y use the conditional rule in LOGIC_PID_DIRICHLET_AUDIT (if inputs integral with one unit, exclude the at most one forbidden residue of Y). At finitely exceptional small q use an empty prefix, same tail rule. Thus φ_q are field embeddings and every fixed rational-stage element of A is integral almost everywhere, though (P−1)^−1∈F emphatically is not. Every Dirichlet Y is integral everywhere. A task with globally Bézout-coprime R-inputs now has Q a unit at every mark and prime in A, hence in R, and is a witness to the internal progression axiom without rescaling.

## 4. Exact content descent

Let R={g∈A:v_q(g)≥0 for all q}. Clearing finitely many negative valuations gives A=R⊗ℚ. CRT translates an A-element by a rational to one in R, preserving bounded-distance approximation as in Z73, hence open induction and discreteness. P is already a prime in R since it is prime in A and v_q(P)=0 everywhere.

For a,b∈R nonzero, choose h generating (a,b)A (in the *serviced union*), u=a/h, v=b/h. Pick U,V∈A with Uu+Vv=1. Since all four elements are integral almost everywhere, m_q=min(v_q(u),v_q(v)) vanishes almost everywhere (and is finite everywhere). Choose γ∈ℚ^× with v_q(γ)=m_q. Then d=γh is in R with v_q(d)=min(v_q(a),v_q(b)), and a/d,b/d are in R, locally primitive. The A-row for a/d,b/d has coefficients integral outside finitely many q. At the remaining primes correct the coefficients by (b/d,−a/d) times a single rational CRT parameter so both are integral everywhere. Thus (a,b)R=dR. Zero-input cases are trivial. **The content matched is that of the residual pair, not of h**: h may have infinitely many positive valuations. If the residual minima have infinite support, rational scalar normalization is impossible; it cannot occur for a serviced unimodular A-row with restricted adelic evaluations. Before service it can occur, e.g. in ℚ[P,Y] with marks P,Y≡1 mod almost every q for (P−1,Y−1), and no integral-almost-everywhere row witnesses could then exist. Since A=R⊗ℚ and A^×=ℚ^×, any R-generator must become a rational associate of h; its valuations must equal the local minima by divisibility and linear combination. By contrast, making h itself a unit at every mark by rescaling requires v_q(h) of finite support, which fails for h=P−1. All q≠ℓ divide P−1 in R; P−1≠0, so external PID is impossible.

Finally the embeddings supply the feasible local points for LOGIC_MARKED_SECTIONS (E); stage generators are integral almost everywhere and R-triple evaluations integral everywhere. Its polynomial section criterion therefore gives ¬ES(P) for this uncovered profile. At ℓ≡3 mod 4 both π_ℓ and −1 are nonsquares modulo ℓ; choose a standard integer x with x²≡−π_ℓ mod ℓ. Then (x²+P)/ℓ∈R by local integrality, and ℓ is Bézout-coprime to P (P mod ℓ≠0). This is genuine NR at P with d=ℓ, not the universal NR axiom.

**PID boundary.** Thinning the original ℓ-fiber cannot even fix exact zeros at the other primes. Nor does preserving the square classes of all components preserve U. For an original surviving g choose d>0 with −4d≡g mod ℓ. Choose an arbitrarily large prime q≡−ℓ^{-1} mod 4d by ordinary Dirichlet. Then f=ℓq satisfies 4d|f+1, and the II-2 class −4d mod f catches the profile obtained by changing just the q-component to −4d. This changed component is a square unit: for the odd quadratic character χ of the negative discriminant associated with −d (conductor dividing 4d), χ(q)=χ(−1)χ(ℓ)=+1, since (−d/ℓ)=−1. Thus even a single square-valued change away from any prescribed finite set can destroy survival. The thickening proved above fixes exact zeros but does not fix finite support for T−1. The finite-S estimate uses convergence only on S-smooth N; summing N^{−1/5+ε} over arbitrary primes does not converge. It supplies no procedure for simultaneously releasing almost every mod-q congruence. I have no proof of an uncovered nonsquare parsimonious profile or an obstruction to every such profile; the external PID + internal Dirichlet + NR-at-P strengthening is not established by this argument.
