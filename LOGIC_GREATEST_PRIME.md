# Audit: a greatest prime divisor in the joint ES countermodel

**Result.** The model construction of `LOGIC_NORM_SPLIT.md` can simultaneously satisfy IOpen, Bézout, TS, Bertrand and

`∀n≥2 ∃p≤n [Prime(p) ∧ p|n ∧ ∀l≤n ((Prime(l) ∧ l|n) → l≤p)]`.

In particular it has PD. The cofinal primes of the form 1+Z² at which **full ES** fails are retained. This is a modification of the construction, not a deduction of greatest prime divisors in the previous model. Two additions are needed: a norm divisor larger than its cofactor, and a way of handling **all** zero-landing prime factors of one input in a single deformation.

## 1. The one-variable split

Use the stage invariant in `LOGIC_NORM_SPLIT.md` §1. Let s be a nonunit prime of A still prime in A[i], of either ordered sign and arbitrary rational evaluation. In particular |s| is infinite. Put

`H=1+Z²,   B=A[Z,W]/(HW−s),   e(Z)=0, e(W)=e(s)`.

**Factorization and geometry.** Over a domain E and a nonzero s₀∈E the analogous ring embeds into `E[Z,1/H]` by W↦s₀/H: s₀ is regular modulo H since E[Z]/H is free of rank two over E. Reducing a putative identity `Hf=(HW−s₀)g` modulo H gives H|g, proving saturation. This also gives geometric integrality after extending the constant field. For any other old prime l, use E=A/l and s₀=s mod l to prove B/l a nonzero domain. Moreover its fraction field is `Frac(A/l)(Z)`. Thus distinct old prime factors remain prime when processing one of them. No distinct nonassociate factors can become associates because stage units remain rational.

`B/H=((A/s)[i])[W]` is a domain by inertness, so H is prime. Inverting H gives a factorial localization of A[Z], hence B is factorial by Nagata. Over ℚ(i), Z+i and Z−i are prime (both quotients are `(A[i]/s)[W]`); inverting them again gives a factorial localization. Hence B[i] is factorial. The prime H has e(H)=1 and splits into nonunits over the Gaussian extension, so later norm stages do not target it. At real points H>0 identically. The whole real hypersurface is the graph of s/H over the old real variety times the Z-line. On the chart H≠0 this is an algebraic product with an open subset of the line, so the old smooth component lifts smoothly and connectedly and contains the rational and ordered points. This is **not** an assertion that the whole complex variety is smooth.

**Order and sizes.** Let K be the ordered real closure of Frac(A); put b=√|s| there. Choose T>0 infinite with every positive infinite element of K dominating every fixed power of T (compactness, as in `LOGIC_BEZOUT.md`). Set `Z=b/T, W=s/(1+Z²)`. T and hence Z are transcendental over K. We have Z infinite, `H/√|s|` infinite, `W/(sign(s) T²)` infinitesimally close to 1, and `H/|W|=H²/|s|` infinite. In particular `1<|cW|<H` for **every fixed nonzero rational** c; no positivity assumption on s is necessary.

To verify the finite-element invariant, reduce using `Z²W=s−W`. Every element has an expression `P(Z)+Σ_{j≥1}(a_j+b_j Z)W^j` with P∈A[Z] and finitely many a_j,b_j∈A. The leading equivalents of its individual nonzero summands are `c Z^d` (d≥0), `a_j s^j Z^(−2j)`, and `b_j s^j Z^(1−2j)`. The exponents are distinct. For d≠d′ and c,c′∈K×, their magnitude ratio is `|c/c′| b^(d−d′) T^(d′−d)`; its old-field factor is infinite, infinitesimal or appreciable. In each case the ratio is infinite or infinitesimal, using the slow-scale condition in the first two cases. Thus the largest term dominates: nonconstant terms do not cancel. Every actual nonzero summand of positive Z-degree or positive W-degree has infinite absolute value, since |Z|, |W| are infinite and nonzero A-coefficients are not infinitesimal. The constant term of P is rational or infinite. This proves `bounded B=ℚ` (and uniqueness of the reduced expression). The claimed distinct exponents are **not** a degree argument in one Archimedean variable; independence from old coefficients is essential.

## 2. A finite certificate for all future prime divisors

Schedule each R-element n≥2, i.e. positive of integer evaluation, at a finite stage. Factor there as c times a product of powers of pairwise nonassociate nonconstant primes in A, c∈ℚ×. A standard integer n needs no stage: its positive divisors remain standard by discreteness. For nonconstant n proceed as follows.

1. If any factors have zero evaluation, do **one** normal-cone operation `C=A[D,𝔪/D]` at 𝔪=ker e, using the slow order of `LOGIC_BEZOUT.md`. For every old prime s in the factorization let k=ord_𝔪(s). Then `s=D^k u` with u prime: u remains prime after inverting D and is not divisible by the prime D, and C is a UFD. Choose a single rational tangent vector avoiding the zeros of the product of the nonzero degree-k initial forms for all the zero-landing factors. This is possible over ℚ since `gr_𝔪 A` is a polynomial ring; the vector is accessible in the old smooth real component by the tangent-path proof. Consequently every u has nonzero evaluation, including k=0 factors. The new prime D, of positive multiplicity, is the **only** zero-landing factor left. It is inert even over ℚ(i), since C[i]/D is the polynomial tangent ring over ℚ(i). If no factors have zero evaluation, omit this operation.
2. Normalize each nonzero-landing factor to evaluation **+1**, absorbing rational units in c. This is an evaluation normalization, not a claim about its ordered sign. For each distinct normalized factor, if it splits over ℚ(i), it already is a positive protected norm prime with coordinates in R by `LOGIC_NORM_SPLIT.md` §2. Otherwise apply §1 to it, recording a positive protected prime H and cofactor W; `e(W)=1` but W may be negative. Apply §1 also to D, if present, obtaining its own H₀ and W₀ with `e(W₀)=0`. Distinct old factors are still prime when serviced; the earlier new H's stay prime since they split in the Gaussian extension. Both real and Gaussian nonunit factors remain nonunits by the bounded-elements and units invariants. Use each replacement with its original multiplicity.
3. Every norm prime and every W now has integer evaluation. If e(n)≠0, there was no D, and after normalization **c=e(n)∈ℤ**. Factor |c| into its standard primes, ignoring its sign and omitting units. If e(n)=0, **do not factor c in R**. Replace just **one occurrence** of W₀ by cW₀ in the finite product, removing the outside scalar. This is in R even if c∉ℤ, and `|cW₀|<H₀` by the infinite ratio in §1. Other copies of W₀ stay unscaled. Take positive absolute values of all factors: their product is n since n>0. All quotients obtained by omitting any displayed factor are therefore in R.
4. Form the finite list of protected prime factors in this product together with any standard scalar prime factors. It is nonempty (the standard n case apart, a nonconstant stage factor is either a protected prime or has been given one). Let P be its largest. It is a prime divisor of n in the final ring. Every remaining factor is either a unit or is positive and smaller than **its own** listed H, hence smaller than or equal to P. The finite product identity, strict order inequalities, and primality of each listed factor persist. In the final Bézout domain an irreducible l is a ring prime, so any positive prime divisor l of n divides one of the factors of this **externally finite** product. If that factor is a listed prime, l equals it; otherwise `l≤factor≤P` by positivity and discreteness. This proves maximality even for primes added long after service. No requirement is imposed on further splitting or prime-factorization length of the residual factors.

**Simultaneity.** Interleave these tasks with the base-model floor and Bézout tasks and the polynomial adjunctions for cofinal/short-interval primes. Each service takes only finitely many steps and outputs an identity permanent under all later inclusions. For nonconstant inputs of evaluation ±1 the displayed certificate supplies a protected positive normalized norm prime (there is no scalar prime). Consequently every positive nonstandard irreducible is such a norm, as before, giving TS. If instead the evaluation is 0 or has absolute value ≥2, a standard prime already divides the element. Standard positive primes ≡1 (4) have their standard representations. The graph over the real line preserves the component argument for full ES, and inert-only splitting never targets the short-interval primes `1+Y²`. This proves the stated joint result. Existing short-interval witnesses still supply Bertrand on infinite inputs and ordinary Bertrand handles the standard inputs. There is no claim of collection, atomicity, or a PA/ZFC result.

**Standalone comparisons.** `LOGIC_COLLECTION.md` §2 obtains greatest prime divisors in a different, externally factorial model; external factoriality there still proves the standalone assertion and is *not* used here. Its counterexample to greatest prime divisors in L65 Thm T is valid: β₃ is almost surely divisible by infinitely many standard primes (independent uniform third coordinate away from finitely many primes; harmonic sum diverges), and not by the sole nonstandard prime β₁²+β₂². In I66 Thm E's simpler ring ℤ+Xℚ[X], X likewise has every standard prime divisor and no nonstandard prime divisor (in ℚ[X] its only possible nonconstant prime factor is associated to X, which has zero landing). Thus earlier PD arguments alone do not justify the new axiom.
