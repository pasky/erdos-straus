Branch note, not a claim about ES in the standard integers. Write `ES(n)` for the existence of three **positive** denominators satisfying `4xyz=n(xy+yz+zx)`. All primality assertions refer to positive irreducibles in the model. Main results here are separate: full collection with open induction (§1); strong bounded divisor principles with normal open induction (§2); Bézout with partial root floors, but **not** full open induction (§3). ## 1. Full collection with arbitrary parameters

**Theorem.** IOpen + Coll does not prove ES at the primes in any prescribed primitive unit-square residue class, even after discarding any standard initial segment. Here Coll is the full bounding scheme, not coded choice. On the nonnegative cone, for **every** first-order formula, with no complexity restriction, it is

`∀a,c̄ [(∀x<a ∃y φ(x,y,c̄)) → ∃b ∀x<a ∃y<b φ(x,y,c̄)]`.

All parameters may come from the final model. By IOpen we mean discretely ordered semirings with open induction; **not** a definition of `BΣ₁` which includes IΔ₀ as base. PA proves each axiom here (induct on the length of the bounded domain). No induction for quantified formulas is added. Nothing here separates PA, IE₁ or IΔ₀ from ES.

**Countable successor.** Given a countable ordered-ring model R of open induction, let K be the ordered real closure of Frac(R). In the ordered Puiseux polynomial ring K[Y^(ℚ≥0)] with Y above K, form

`S = R + {finite sums of positive rational powers of Y with coefficients in K}`.

This is a ring. Every nonconstant has absolute value greater than every element of R. Thus S is discrete and its positive cone end extends that of R. S is countable and Y dominates R. It is an integer part of the ordered real closure of Frac(S): use the algebraic Puiseux expansion at 1/Y over the real closed field K, not over an Archimedean completion. Explicitly, Frac(S) = K(Y^(1/m):m≥1) (k=(kY)/Y). Its ordered real closure embeds in the ordered real Puiseux series field `⋃_m K(((Y^(-1/m))))`; this field is real closed by the characteristic-zero Newton–Puiseux theorem applied over K[i], followed by complex conjugation. Its order is by the leading term and restricts to our order. Each such series has only finitely many positive powers. Subtract those terms, take the R-floor of the constant coefficient in K (Shepherdson's criterion for R), and subtract one more if that constant coefficient was exactly an R-element and the remaining infinitesimal is negative. A nonzero infinitesimal here is smaller in absolute value than every positive constant of K, including a nonstandard constant. The resulting S-element is the required floor. Shepherdson now gives open induction in S.

**Limits and collection.** Start at the countable U64 Thm A model for the chosen class, with fixed infinite prime n and ¬ES(n). Repeat the successor step through all countable ordinals, taking unions at limits. At a limit the union is still a discrete integer part of its real closure: use compatible ordered real closures (unique ordered embeddings over inclusions); their directed union is real closed and algebraic over the union fraction field. Every algebraic element thus gets a floor at one stage. Endness of the nonnegative cones is transitive and survives unions. Stages indexed below ω₁ are countable. In the final union M each initial interval [0,a) already belongs elementwise to a countable stage. If the antecedent of Coll holds in **M**, choose witnesses there for these countably many x, externally. Their stage indices have a countable supremum <ω₁. The Y at a later successor bounds all the witnesses. There is no appeal to elementarity of the stages or absoluteness of φ. The same argument works for a fixed finite witness tuple without coding it.

**Preservation of the actual prime and of full ES.** A positive factor of n is ≤n. For an ES solution sort x≤y≤z; then 4x≤3n. The residual `(4x−n)/(nx)=1/y+1/z` is positive and at most 2/y; discreteness gives 4x−n≥1 and y≤2nx. Finally `z(4xy−nx−ny)=nxy` gives z≤nxy since the parenthesis is a positive ring element. In particular all three are ≤2n⁴. These are ordered-ring arguments, with divisions interpreted in the fraction field. The bounds n and 2n⁴ belong to the initial model; an end extension introduces neither a new factor nor a new solution. Primality, residue class, nonstandardness and ¬ES(n) persist.

**Why this does not upgrade to OI + Bézout.** For positive a,b in a ring with Euclidean division, any identity ua+vb=1 can be normalized to `0≤u<b`, `|v|≤a`: reduce u modulo b and solve for v. The positive common divisors are ≤min(a,b). Thus if an old coprime pair lacks a Bézout identity, no ordered end extension satisfying division can fix it. This is an obstruction to the actual bounded Bézout axiom, not to a proposed algorithm for constructing its witnesses. Independently, in the displayed S, Y and √2 Y have ratio √2. No discretely ordered **normal** overring of S exists: √2 in its fraction field is integral and would have to lie strictly between 1 and 2 in the ring. In fact no GCD overring of this kind exists, since GCD domains are integrally closed. This particular end extension does not preserve the normality/GCD of its starting model. Section 3 takes the other route, maintaining Bézout and testing extra root floors.

## 2. Bounded valuation schemes with GCD retained

**A factorial variation of U64.** Use its BO variables βᵢ, with β₁ positive infinite, and `A=ℚ[β₁,β₂,…]`. Set `R={g∈A : g(a)∈Ẑ}` with the BO order. Choose residues aᵢ as follows. At every prime ℓ, demand coordinatewise algebraic independence over ℚ in ℤ_ℓ and require a₁ to be a unit square, in the prescribed class at the primes of q. In addition require, for each nonzero integer polynomial f in finitely many of the variables, `f(a)≠0 mod ℓ` for all sufficiently large ℓ (threshold depending on f).

Here is simultaneous existence. Enumerate the nonzero polynomials. For any fixed finite prefix and all sufficiently large primes, the reductions are nonzero; their union of zero sets has size at most `(Σ deg f)ℓ^(k−1)` in k coordinates (k≥1), less than `(ℓ−1)ℓ^(k−1)/2`, the number of tuples with first entry a nonzero square. Pick prefix thresholds increasing to infinity and avoid successively longer prefixes, with the empty prefix at the exceptional small primes. Lift each chosen tuple in its residue cylinders, restricting the first to an open set of unit squares meeting the required class (in particular at 2). Algebraic independence can be imposed there by successively avoiding countable algebraic closures. Thus every nonzero rational polynomial has finite valuation at every ℓ and valuation zero at almost every ℓ. U64 §§2.1, 2.7 give normal OI for these residues. Its Thm A specialization proof with individual residues (§2.7) gives ¬ES(β₁) in the prescribed class.

For each nonconstant irreducible f of A, scale by a nonzero rational c so that `v_ℓ(cf)=0` for every ℓ: only finitely many exponents need adjusting. Such a normalized cf is prime in R: divisibility in A lifts to R by its unit evaluations. The standard primes ℓ are prime by the evaluation mod ℓ map with kernel ℓR. Factoring any nonzero g in A and using these normalizations leaves a rational scalar with all valuations ≥0, hence an integer. This is a factorization into finitely many primes in R, whose only units are ±1. Therefore R is an **external UFD**. It has unbounded nonstandard primes, for example the normalized irreducibles β₂ + β₁^j with positive sign, whose degrees in the BO infinite variable tend to infinity (each normalizing factor is a standard rational). No coding of nonstandard-length factorizations is being assumed.

Consequently each positive element has **externally finitely many** positive divisors. The model supports all of the following simultaneously, in addition to normal OI, GCD and PD:

* For each formula φ with parameters, Coll as in §1 with x ranging instead over the positive divisors of any given a>0. Choose the finitely many witnesses in R and take one plus their maximum. Every nonempty definable subset of those divisors has a greatest and least member.
* Each positive a>1 has a greatest prime divisor. This is a genuine extra bounded sentence: in L65 Thm T, |β₃| almost surely has infinitely many standard prime factors (Lemma 3.2's independent-primes argument applied to β₃), but the unique nonstandard prime n does not divide β₃. There is no greatest prime factor. Thus even the L65 theory with TS does not imply this axiom (for the μ-almost-sure residue choice of L65 Lemmas 3.2–3.4, which is the model of L65 Thm T; z74 Opus xreview fix). We do **not** claim the factorial variation satisfies TS.
* For any cutoff b and a>0 there are s,t≤a with st=a, every prime divisor of s at most b and every prime divisor of t greater than b (**Split**). Multiply the two sublists of its finite factorization. More generally one may partition the prime factors by **any** formula with parameters. Singleton support gives primary-part extraction for arbitrary internal primes, not just each standard numeral.

**Alternate retaining TS; failure of bounded Bézout at the split.** L65's TS model itself satisfies Split, despite not generally having finite factorization. For standard b only finitely many primes are ≤b; take out exactly their finite powers by the finite ℓ-adic valuations (L65 Lemma 3.3 (G1)). For nonstandard b all standard primes are ≤b. The only possible prime >b is n; if n>b, remove its exact polynomial multiplicity (finite, since it is prime in A, with unit evaluation), putting that power in t. Otherwise take t=1. This also proves singleton primary-part extraction: the standard primes use their valuations and n uses polynomial multiplicity. The counterexample is L65's nonstandard n=β₁²+β₂², not a square integer.

Now strengthen Split by requiring witnesses to `us+vt=1` with `0≤u<t, |v|≤s` (take u=0, v=1 if t=1). This holds in PA by Euclid. It fails in this same model at `a=n|β₃|`, `b=n−1`. Any possible t has no standard prime factor, hence has unit evaluation. L65 Lemma 3.2's factor normalization then makes it a power of n up to sign; since n occurs exactly once and cannot divide s, positivity forces t=n and s=|β₃|. Yet `(n,β₃)` is proper even in A (evaluate all variables at zero). So not even unbounded coefficients exist. This obstruction applies also to Bézout at that particular prime. No claim that the strengthened theory proves ES.

**Divisor bound versus interval bound.** In the factorial model add the axiom that for every A≥1 there is a greatest 2-primary integer d≤A (primary = every prime divisor equals 2, allowing 1). PA proves it. Every 2-primary there is a *standard* power of 2 by external factorization. For any infinite A, these powers are all below A and have no largest. Thus extraction among divisors is not the existence of a largest power below an arbitrary cutoff. This is also an explicit limit on the factorial variation.

## 3. Keeping Bézout: a conic power tower

Here is a different actual extension of I66 Thm E, in **theory strength** (not an end extension of its displayed ring). It has Bézout, IOpenLin, and the floor scheme for the irrational slope √2, as well as divisible growth exponents at infinity; it still fails full ES. The floor axiom can be stated in the ordered-ring language: for every a≥0 there is b≥0 with `b²≤2a²<(b+1)²`. The old rational-polynomial example fails it already for a=X by the rational leading coefficient. Every axiom in this specified theory is a theorem of PA.

Let K=ℚ(√2) with its positive embedding. For each m≥1 let z_m=z^(1/m) in the group algebra of ℚ over K; order the real functions at z→+∞. Let A_m be the invariants in `K[z_m,z_m⁻¹]` of conjugating the coefficient and inverting z_m simultaneously. For m|m' embed by the power map. Put A=⋃ A_m, e=evaluation at z=1 (values in ℚ), R=e⁻¹(ℤ) inside A. Every invariant Laurent polynomial is a rational constant plus terms `c z_m^j + c̄ z_m^(−j)` (j>0). Hence nonconstants have a strictly positive top exponent and are infinite in absolute value; R is discrete, and the units of R are ±1 by additivity of highest exponent on nonzero invariants. The positive growth exponents include every positive rational (take c=1), so Frac(R) has exponent group ℚ.

**Bézout in A.** Write `s=(z_m+z_m⁻¹)/2`, `t=(z_m−z_m⁻¹)/(2√2)`. Then `A_m=ℚ[s,t]/(s²−2t²−1)`: after scalar extension to K these coordinates recover z_m and z_m⁻¹, and descent takes the invariant coefficients. The smooth projective completion is a rational conic (point (1,0)), hence ℙ¹ over ℚ. Its complement is one closed point of degree 2 (both conjugate infinity points). The affine coordinate ring is Dedekind; its divisor class group is ℤ/2 by degree, since removing that closed point quotients Pic(ℙ¹)=ℤ by its class of degree 2. Equivalently any even-degree divisor on the affine curve becomes a degree-zero projective divisor after adding a multiple of this infinity point, hence principal. Under `A_m→A_(2m)` pullback has degree 2 on the projective curves (over K it is w↦w²); no finite point pulls back to infinity. Thus every affine divisor class pulls back to zero: the extended ideal of any nonzero ideal at stage m is principal at 2m. For any f,g∈A, take a common stage and apply this to (f,g); the principal generator remains a linear combination over A, so A is Bézout.

**Bézout descends to R.** Given f,g∈R not both zero, write f=hf′, g=hg′ with h a generator of their ideal in A and `(f′,g′)=A`. The rationals α=e(f′), β=e(g′) are not both zero. Let γ>0 generate αℤ+βℤ, with γ=u₀α+v₀β for integers u₀,v₀. Then γh∈R, since its evaluation is u₀e(f)+v₀e(g); f/(γh) and g/(γh) belong to R as well. Choose U,V∈A with Uf′+Vg′=1. For δ=γ−u₀f′−v₀g′, e(δ)=0. Thus `(u₀+δU)f+(v₀+δV)g=γh` with both coefficients in R. This proves Bézout.

**Floors and their limit.** A fraction of two R-elements has a Laurent expansion at infinity at one finite stage with coefficients in K, finitely many strictly positive exponents, and a constant in K. So does √2 times such a fraction. For each positive exponent match its coefficient c by the invariant term with coefficient c̄ at the negative exponent. Subtract the rational evaluation at 1 of the resulting polynomial to make it an R-element. The difference has a real algebraic constant and a tail tending to zero. Floor the constant in ℤ, adjusting down one if the tail is negative and the constant integral. This gives integer parts of Frac(R) (hence IOpenLin, which is equivalent to being an integer part of the fraction field: Glivická–Glivický Thm 3, verified in `Z66_IE1.md` §1; z74 Opus xreview fix) and the asserted √2 floors. In fact it floors every element of Frac(R)(√2), contained in ⋃K(z_m). Put `a=z+z⁻¹>0`. No member b∈R is within 1 of √3 a: its largest exponent and coefficient would have to be 1 and √3, but √3∉K. Thus R does **not** have full OI. This is not a missing proof of density in a known dense field; the slope already gives an explicit gap.

**Permanent prime and ES failure.** At stage 1 put `p=(3s+1)/4`, with s=(z+z⁻¹)/2. It has e(p)=1, is positive infinite, and p(z)>0 for real z≥1. In `K[z_m,z_m⁻¹]` it is a Laurent unit times `3z_m^(2m)+2z_m^m+3`. This polynomial is irreducible over K for every m. Indeed, at m=1 its two roots are α,α⁻¹ with α=(−1+2√(−2))/3 and L=K(α)=K(i) quadratic over K. In ℚ(√(−2)), 3 splits into (1+√(−2)) and (1−√(−2)) (norm 3); the numerator of α is `(1+√(−2))²`. At the first prime α has valuation 1. It still has normalized valuation 1 at a prime of L above it: adjoining √2 is unramified at 3 (discriminant of X²−2 is 8). Hence `X^m−α` is Eisenstein there, so a root has degree m over L and degree 2m over K (its m-th power generates L). This proves the asserted irreducibility, including even and composite m. Invariants divisible by p in the Laurent ring have invariant quotient; thus p generates a proper prime ideal in A_m and in their union (properness also follows from the exponent). Since e(p)=1, divisibility by p descends to R. So p is a prime in R and `p≡1 mod k` for every standard k, since `(p−1)/k∈R`.

Suppose a positive ES triple at p existed in R. At a common stage, evaluate the coordinates at z≥1 as real analytic functions (real positive m-th root). All three are eventually positive. They have no zero on [1,∞): at the largest such zero the sum of the positive reciprocals from the right would diverge whereas 4/p stays bounded. Each nonzero Laurent polynomial has only finitely many positive zeros. Thus their evaluations at 1 are positive **integers** giving `4=1/x+1/y+1/z≤3`. Contradiction. No Type I/II restriction was imposed. This settles the indicated partial-root-floor theory at a genuine infinite prime, not IOpen + Bézout.

## 4. Why changing the one-place curve tower cannot finish OI + Bézout

This proves a scoped version of the one-dimensional tower assessment in I66 §6. Let Cᵢ be smooth geometrically integral projective curves over ℚ in a chain of finite nonconstant maps `Cⱼ→Cᵢ` (j≥i), let Pᵢ be a closed point with **unique** point above it, Pⱼ, and `Aᵢ=Γ(Cᵢ−{Pᵢ},O)`. Put F=⋃ℚ(Cᵢ). Take a compatible ordered real branch at these points: a uniformizer is infinitesimal, a nonzero element with a pole has absolute value exceeding every standard integer, and the residue ordering embeds the number field κ(Pᵢ) in ℝ. Thus the place valuation equals the natural valuation on each ordered function field. Assume R⊆⋃Aᵢ is a discrete ordered ring with Frac(R)=F. Then **R cannot be a model of full open induction**, whether or not it is Bézout.

1. **Positive genus obstructs even floors of F.** At a stage of genus g>0 let d=deg Pᵢ. For Nd>2g−2, Riemann–Roch gives `dim L(NPᵢ)=Nd+1−g`. Principal parts at Pᵢ of order at most N have ℚ-dimension Nd (`t^(−N)O_P/O_P`, with t a uniformizer), and the map from L(NPᵢ) has one-dimensional kernel (constants). Hence some principal part is not hit. Represent it by f∈ℚ(Cᵢ), possible by definition of the local ring. No a∈Aᵢ has f−a regular at Pᵢ: any such a would already lie in L(NPᵢ). Nor can a∈Aⱼ repair this later. If f−a were regular at Pⱼ, the normalized field trace of a to ℚ(Cᵢ) would be in Aᵢ and differ from f by an element regular at Pᵢ. Indeed regularity away from P descends by trace of integral elements; at Pᵢ every point above it is Pⱼ, giving the same integrality for f−a. Division by the extension degree causes no pole in characteristic zero. This contradicts the missing principal part. A floor would give |f−a|≤1 and therefore regularity at the chosen place. Thus even IOpenLin is impossible if any stage has positive genus.

2. **Genus zero obstructs full floors.** Assume now all gᵢ=0. If R were an integer part of its real closure, choose any positive infinite a∈R (if there were none, R=ℤ, contrary to Frac(R)=F). For D=2,3 the floor of √D a would give b/a with residue √D, since the error is ≤1/a. More explicitly `(b/a)²−D` is infinitesimal, so its residue vanishes. Taking a common stage shows its residue number field contains ℚ(√2,√3), hence dᵢ≥4 there. For a later stage let N be the degree of its map to Cᵢ and e,f the ramification index and residue degree above Pᵢ. Uniqueness gives N=ef and dⱼ=f dᵢ. In characteristic zero Riemann–Hurwitz gives total ramification degree 2N−2, at least `(e−1)dⱼ = dᵢ N(1−1/e)`. For e≥2 this is ≥2N, impossible. Hence all later maps have e=1 above Pᵢ: the value group of the union, normalized there, remains ℤ. Choose a positive element of F with pole order 1 at Pᵢ (either sign of an inverse uniformizer). Its positive square root has pole order 1/2, and no element of F can be within 1 of it. This contradicts the integer-part criterion for full OI.

The hypotheses matter: this excludes neither higher-dimensional constructions nor towers with extra places at infinity where a floor might have uncontrolled poles. It proves **no** impossibility theorem for OI + Bézout itself. The conic example in §3 attains indefinite ramification at residue degree two; it is not ruled out at its stated partial-floor strength.

**Picard norm check.** In a one-place tower, if an old nonzero ideal I becomes principal in the union, finite generation of I and the two inclusions in equality with a principal ideal descend to some finite stage. For the finite étale-at-the-generic-point map of affine smooth curves there, norm of the extended ideal is I^[L:F] (pushforward after pullback of divisors multiplies by [L:F]), while the norm of a principal ideal is principal. Thus [I] is torsion on the old affine curve. Torsion does not imply capitulation or supply a floor: part 1 applies even to an elliptic curve of rank zero.

**Why extra places do not rescue transcendence degree one in the normal case.** In fact the fraction field of a nonstandard normal model R of OI cannot have transcendence degree one over ℚ. Otherwise write Q₀=ℚR for the localization by nonzero standard integers and F=Frac(R); Q₀ is normal and its finite ordered elements are precisely ℚ (clear a standard denominator and use discreteness). The relative algebraic closure of ℚ in F is ℚ: an algebraic element is integral over ℚ⊆Q₀ and finite in the order. Fix an infinite t∈R. Then F is algebraic over ℚ(t), in fact countable. Exhaust F by finite extensions L of ℚ(t) inside F, giving smooth geometrically integral projective curves. On each, the natural valuation (finite elements of the ordered field, modulo infinitesimals) extends the place at t=∞: on ℚ(t) this follows by degree. A valuation extending a discrete function-field place to a finite extension is again such a place; its residue is an ordered number field, hence Archimedean. Call this place P. The normalization of ℚ[t] in L is contained in Q₀ by normality, and is the ring of functions with poles only over t=∞. If there were a second place Q over ∞, then for large N, Riemann–Roch supplies a nonzero function in L(NQ−P). It lies in Q₀ but is infinitesimal at P, a contradiction. Thus P is unique. Moreover every nonconstant a∈Q₀∩L is infinite in absolute value. Apply the **same** argument with base ℚ(a) in place of ℚ(t) (L is finite over ℚ(a)); its only pole in L is P. Hence Q₀∩L is exactly the affine ring away from P, and R meets all the hypotheses of parts 1–2. Contradiction. This deals also with places introduced by adding an extra affine generator, not just covers totally concentrated over the originally chosen t. The argument requires normality; the simple Puiseux successor in §1 is not normal.

**Scope.** The first construction attains full bounding, the second attains strong bounded valuation assertions, the third retains actual Bézout with new irrational floors. These are different models. In particular the first and third conclusions cannot be combined by taking their union. The full IOpen + Bézout construction in the separate `LOGIC_BEZOUT.md` is transcendental; it does not go through any of these three model closures. All nonprovability here is one-sided and is unrelated to PA or ZFC independence.
