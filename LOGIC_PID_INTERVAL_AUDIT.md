**Conclusion.** Using `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §§1–4, there is a single external PID model R of IOpen such that for **every** a≥1 in R, every externally infinite positive b in R, and each standard primitive unit-square class r mod M, there is a positive infinite prime P of R with `a<P<a+b`, `P≡r mod M`, and `¬ES(P)` for the full three-denominator sentence.

1. **Scheduling before defining R.** Schedule the request for all eligible a,b in the stage ℚ-algebras, not just in the as-yet-undefined R; precisely, every triple `(a,b,(r,M))` with a≥1, b positive infinite and r a primitive unit-square class modulo a standard M (z74 Opus xreview fix). This is a countable external dovetail (no effective test for infinitude required). In the ordered real closure of the old field put L=√b and adjoin `P=a+Lz`, with 1<z<2 of residue transcendental over the old real closure's natural residue field. For a nonconstant old-ring polynomial G of degree d, the coefficient of z^d in G(P) is lc(G)L^d, infinite because nonzero old-ring elements are noninfinitesimal. The Gauss residue test prevents cancellation, so G(P) is infinite. This is a free polynomial stage, with P itself chosen as the new transcendence-basis entry; z need not belong to the ring. Since `L>2`, `0<Lz<b`, and P is infinite. Every old prime survives, as does this coordinate prime in all subsequent stages.
2. **Simultaneous local constraints.** Give every marked coordinate its own finite congruence and impose the square-unit condition on it at every prime. For a fixed finite avoidance prefix involving d basis variables of which k are marked, at odd ℓ away from their finitely many prescribed moduli the number of available tuples is exactly `((ℓ−1)/2)^k ℓ^(d−k)`. The fraction is **not** literally 2^(-k); it is `((1−1/ℓ)/2)^k`, bounded below by 3^(-k) for odd ℓ. The `O(prefix)ℓ^(d−1)` forbidden zero count is eventually smaller. Choose successive thresholds including the relevant modulus primes; constraints on coordinates outside the prefix do not restrict those inside. At each ℓ all coordinate cylinders admit jointly ℚ-algebraically independent lifts, using unit-square cylinders also at 2. This preserves the finite valuation-support condition for every nonzero rational function. All marked P are therefore in R, already valuation-normalized, prime there and in their own prescribed class. PID descent and floors are unchanged. This is not a prescription of exact profinite values (equal values on two coordinates, for example, would destroy injectivity).
3. **One square specialization at a time.** For a marked P added after B, a common stage of a hypothetical ES triple and its type certificates may be presented starting with ℚ[P]. Replay all stages through B with scalar extension by ℚ[P], obtaining exactly B[P]; the original initial coordinate, if present, is now a free polynomial variable above ℚ[P]. Earlier row entries do not involve P. Preserve the later stages unchanged. This reorders the **algebra presentation**, not the order on the field. After `P=t²` the earlier stages are just products with the t-line and the later row centers still pull back with codimension ≥2 by finite flatness. Thus the connectedness over t>0 and finite-adelic approximation proofs of §3 apply to this finite tower. Use the already chosen local embeddings with a square root of this P, keep the marked divisibility and Bézout witnesses integral, and land at an odd integer t>1. Positivity transfers from the original ordered point with t=+√P. A strict Type I/II solution at t² contradicts ET Prop. 1.6. Neither the interval inequalities nor square roots of the **other** marked coordinates need to hold at the ordinary landing point.

**Logical boundary.** For nonstandard a take b=a to obtain Bertrand; standard a≥2 are covered by standard Bertrand, since standard primes stay prime. For the explicit gap predicate `G(b):=∃a≥1 ∀n (a<n<a+b → ¬Prime(n))`, the positive b satisfying G in this model are **exactly the standard ones**. For each standard length use the ordinary factorial gaps inside ℕ (or beyond any internal H by adding a sufficiently large multiple of the same standard factorial). For every infinite length the interval theorem above excludes a gap. Hence the ordinary true first-order sentence `∀b≥1 G(b)` fails here. The external interval theorem is not a standard uniform bound on prime gaps or a claim about standard ES failure. The PID property supplies finite factorizations of individual inputs, not nonstandard factorials: indeed a nonzero multiple of every positive integer up to an infinite b would have infinitely many distinct standard prime factors, impossible in this external UFD. There is no contradiction with an individual finite factorization, open induction, or the existence of arbitrarily long **standard** composite gaps. No claim about PA independence.
