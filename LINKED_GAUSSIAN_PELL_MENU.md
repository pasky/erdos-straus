**PROVED.** Fix finitely many triples of rational-coefficient polynomials in `a,b,U,V`, where `P=a²+b²` and `bU²+2aUV−bV²=1`. Try **all** integral row tuples at every ordinary prime `p=P`; require three positive integral outputs. There is a fixed `L` divisible by 840 such that at most `O_menu(B^(3/4) log B)` primes `p≤B`, `p≡1 mod L`, succeed. This is a vanishing proportion on that progression (PNT in fixed progressions). The bound counts projections, not Pell witnesses. The row supplies `X+i=(a+ib)(U+iV)²`, `Y=U²+V²`; polynomial dependence on `X,Y` costs nothing. The obstruction is not a scarcity assumption about the row data: ordinary negative Pell at primes `1 mod 4` and Gaussian factorization give such tuples (see `LOGIC_NEG_PELL_MARKS.md`, commit `91ff30eaf`).

**Global identities.** Write `C` for the row ring over ℚ, `A=ℚ[a,b]`, `K=Frac A`. Discard identically zero denominator coordinates. For a triple `F`, test the *cleared* difference `G=4F₁F₂F₃−P(F₁F₂+F₁F₃+F₂F₃)` **in C**. This is a three-dimensional domain: the row is irreducible over `K` (smooth rank-three projective conic), primitive over `A` by its constant `−1`; use Gauss. If `G=0`, filter the generic row ring over `K` by total `(U,V)` degree. Its graded ring is the quotient by the leading binary form, irreducible since the discriminant `4P` is nonsquare in `K` (`a²+b²` is prime in `A`). Degrees add. In the cleared identity the top terms imply that the degree-zero denominators alone sum reciprocally to `4/P` in `K`. Subtract and clear the remaining denominators in `C`: if any were omitted their reciprocals would sum to zero at **every** successful tuple, impossible by positivity. One success suffices; no density or global sign assumption on the lifts. The generic constants in `C` are exactly `A`: on inverting `b` the equation is monic up to a unit in `U`; on inverting `a` substitute `V=U+w` to get leading coefficient `2a` in `U`. Normal forms put a generic constant in `A[1/b]∩A[1/a]=A` (cf. `LOGIC_NEGATIVE_PELL_ROOT_AUDIT.md`, `bf5a3f343`). Now apply the same degree argument to the circle over `ℚ(P)`, with irreducible leading form `a²+b²`; monic contraction gives `F_i∈ℚ[P]`.

For each remaining global identity include its descended coefficient denominators in `L`. If an integral positive success occurred at `p≡1 mod L`, all three polynomials would also be integral at 1. They are positive there: the nonempty common positivity locus is open and relatively closed on the positive real half-line since there each `F_i(t)≥t/4` by the identity. At 1 three positive integral reciprocals cannot sum to 4. This elementary choice of progression avoids an overclaim: global identities *can* cover other classes.

**Nonidentities.** If `G≠0`, take the finitely many irreducible components over ℚ of its **reduced** zero locus in the row. Each has dimension at most two. Nondominant components project into proper closed subsets of the `(a,b)` plane, contributing `O(N)` integer pairs in the box of radius `N`. Each dominant component is generically finite and separable over `K`; it is a finite cover after shrinking to a nonempty base open (clear denominators in integral equations for its affine algebra generators). Include the omitted proper closed sets in the preceding bound. No claim of a finite map on exceptional fibers. That every specialized lift off a fixed proper closed set of the `(a,b)` plane, on any component, is a root of exactly one specialized eliminant factor of unchanged degree is proved explicitly by the specialization lemma in `GAUSSIAN_PELL_MENU.md`; it uses the cleared radical memberships of the eliminant and of the interpolation relations (z74 Opus xreview fix).

**Stronger than the origin test: degree one never occurs.** It would give a rational point `(U,V)∈K²` on the generic conic. Put `t=a/b`, use the `b`-adic valuation in `ℚ(t)(b)`, and let `m=min(ord U,ord V)` ignoring a zero coordinate. The residue binary form `u²+2tuv−v²` is anisotropic over `ℚ(t)` by its discriminant `4(t²+1)`. After scaling by `b^(−m)` the two residues are not both zero. Thus `ord[b(U²+2tUV−V²)]=1+2m≠0`, whereas the required value 1 has order zero. Substitution at the plane origin alone excludes only *polynomial* sections, not rational ones. This valuation in fact excludes even a rational point at infinity (use the same anisotropy).

For comparison, the proposed slicing fallback for a nonpolynomial coordinate also works: reduce it to `f/g` with coprime `f,g∈ℤ[a,b]` over ℚ, `g` nonconstant, and swap axes to have `deg_a g>0`. Gauss and Bézout over `ℚ(b)[a]` give a nonzero `D(b)∈(f,g)` after clearing denominators. For fixed `b` off the zeros of `D` and the leading coefficient of `g`, integrality requires `0≠g(a,b)|D(b)`; each signed divisor gives at most `deg_a g` choices of `a`. Since `|D(b)|≪(1+N)^O(1)`, the divisor bound gives `O_ε(N^(1+ε))` pairs; finitely many exceptional lines cost `O(N)`. This fallback is **not needed** on the linked row.

The degree-≥2 dominant components project rational points to a thin subset. Equivalently pass to the smooth locus off lower-dimensional exceptions; a non-geometrically-integral component has rational points only on the intersections of geometric conjugates. Explicitly (z74 Opus xreview fix): off that proper set a rational lift gives a rational root of a separable specialization, of unchanged degree `d≥2`, of a monic irreducible `φ∈K[z]`. Its Galois group then fixes a root, so it is intransitive and differs from the transitive generic group. The base points therefore lie in a thin set. The quantitative integral-box thin-set theorem gives `O(N^(3/2) log N)` pairs: S. D. Cohen, as in J.-P. Serre, *Topics in Galois Theory*, 2nd ed., A K Peters 2008, §3.4, Thm 3.4.4, `Int_A(N)=O(N^(n−1/2) log N)`. Equivalently use the first bound of D. Zywina, arXiv:1011.6465, Thm 1.2, for monic irreducible `f` over `k(T)` and `t∉Ω_f`. Serre allows `(log N)^γ`, `γ<1`, which does not affect the conclusion. Use `N=⌊√B⌋`, not a bound on `U,V`. This bound for nonidentities holds on **all** classes. No uniformity for growing menus or arbitrary rational output coordinates is asserted.

**Stress test / certified success.** Put `J=(P+3)/4`, `K=(P+3)(P+1)/12`, and try `(J,K,PK+[(b−1)(V−2)]²)`. Without the perturbation this is a global identity; on `P>0` the perturbed triple satisfies the equality exactly when `b=1` or `V=2` (the last reciprocal changes strictly otherwise). Thus the cleared locus has multiplicities and a vertical component over `b=1`; there one cannot count Pell lifts by the degree of the cover. On `V=2` the equation is `bU²+4aU−4b−1=0`, of generic degree two (discriminant `16a²+16b²+4b` nonsquare), but **linear** at `b=0, a≠0`. Dropping leading-coefficient exceptions or treating the entire zero locus as one irreducible finite cover would be wrong. At `(a,b,U,V)=(-2,5,3,2)` the row gives `P=29, X=−70, Y=13`; the triple `(8,80,2320)` really sums reciprocally to `4/29`. This is a nonidentity success on the horizontal exceptional set, not a universal construction. The argument uses neither square specialization of the lift nor an ES assertion for composite inputs, numerator 8, or restricted (★)/gstar counts.
