## 1. What to schedule and what the sign count proves

First build the whole rational tower, independently of all local evaluations. Its smooth, geometrically integral finite stages are UFDs; old primes remain nonunit primes. Indeed in a row `uU+vV=1` over the quotient by an old prime, at least one entry is nonzero; the nonempty line charts are domains with dense common open when both are present. The union `A` is Bézout, with units `ℚ*`, and factors nonunits into permanent primes. Use the fresh large-variable Dirichlet task `Q=a+mY` for every coprime stage pair `a,m` with `m>0` and every bound `H`; primitive linearity makes `Q` a permanent prime and the ordering gives `Q>max(H,2)`. These are ordinary product-line stages. Schedule approximation and row tasks fairly as usual. Enumerate the actual stage prime **elements** `s`, not just one per association class. Repetitions are harmless. Prefix `j` for sign task `j` includes a stage containing `s` and at least the first `j` stages; it may be extended to be nested. The choice of prefix costs only a bigger threshold for the next prime.

The function field of this prefix is rational in `t=(P,t₂,…,t_h)`. In each row solve for the coordinate whose coefficient is not divisible by `P−1` (choose either if both qualify); the other is free. The center is removed only at the common zero of both entries. This gives a dense rational chart with an open parameter domain. Restrict that domain further so that `P`, all prefix pivots, chart denominators, `s`, and all prefix Dirichlet outputs are nonzero; clear scalar denominators. It contains a principal open `D(G)` in parameter space over ℚ; keep only this open, choosing `G` to include the numerators/denominators for the listed requirements. These are finitely many nonzero functions. The nonzero output condition includes the case `a=0`, in which `m` is a unit and `Q=mY`.

**Multiplicity check.** Write `C` for the smooth coordinate ring at this prefix. `(s)` is still a height-one prime of `C`. Over `ℚbar`, `C/(s)` base-changes to a *nonzero reduced* ring: a finite type domain in characteristic zero is geometrically reduced. The base-changed ambient ring is smooth, integral, hence normal. At each generic component of the geometric divisor the local ring is a DVR and `(s)` is a nonzero radical ideal there, so its order is **one**, not the number of conjugate components. Equivalently, a square root in the geometric function field would belong to the normal ambient ring by integrality and give a nonzero nilpotent modulo `s`. Thus `s` is not a square in `ℚbar(t)`, even if its divisor splits or has been omitted from `D(G)`. Multiplication by a rational scalar does not affect this test. An argument just from “nonconstant” or from an irreducible polynomial having no zeros on the chosen chart would not work.

**Count in all parameters.** Write `s=g/h₀` on the chart. The squarefree odd-exponent part `K` of `g h₀` over ℚ is nonconstant by the preceding paragraph; absorb a scalar and a square so that `g h₀=c K H₀²`. Exclude zeros of these factors and poles. `K` is geometrically squarefree in characteristic zero (irreducible factors can split, not repeat). For completeness, make a fixed invertible rational linear change of counting variables with the last direction nonzero on the leading form of `K`. In that direction the polynomial has full degree and constant nonzero leading coefficient. It is squarefree over the field of the other parameters: each nonconstant factor has positive degree in the last direction, and in characteristic zero it is separable. Its discriminant in that variable is thus not identically zero. These properties persist modulo all sufficiently large primes, by excluding finitely many scalar or coefficient primes. For `h≥2`, all but `O_prefix(q^{h−2})` slices are good. On each good slice the univariate Weil bound for a quadratic character is `≤(deg K−1)√q` in absolute value; use the trivial `q` on bad slices. For `h=1` there is just the good univariate count for large `q`. Other excluded polynomial zeros cost `O_prefix(q^{h−1})` points. Undo the linear change before projecting onto **the original coordinate** `P`. Thus the number of chart tuples with `χ_q(s)=−1` and all the stated avoidance conditions is

    q^h/2 + O_prefix(q^{h−1/2}).

The scalar `χ_q(c)` can flip the sum but not the estimate. For example `s=P²+1` is real-positive and its divisor splits geometrically; there is not even an `𝔽_q`-point of the divisor when `q≡3 mod 4`. Nevertheless `∑_P χ_q(P²+1)=−1`: counting `(z−P)(z+P)=1` gives `q−1` pairs. It is values *away from* the divisor that matter. There is no Chebotarev or absolute-irreducibility hypothesis on the divisor `s=0`. The same count follows from Lang–Weil on the geometrically integral étale double cover `z²=s` of the open. Do **not** apply a fiberwise character estimate in the other coordinates with `P` fixed: for `s=P` it is false.

Let `B_q⊂𝔽_q^×` be the **projection of the good negative-sign tuples**. Each fiber has at most `q^{h−1}` tuples; hence `|B_q|≥q/3` for every sufficiently large prime, with the threshold depending on the whole prefix and `s`, but `1/3` absolute. This works along arbitrarily large `q≡3 mod 4`. For each `b∈B_q` store any one witnessing tuple. Later we sample `P_q` uniformly on the union of its residue cylinders, **not** by pushing forward an arbitrarily biased choice among witness tuples. Lifting the stored tuple at a fixed `P_q≡b` preserves all these unit and sign tests.

## 2. Check on the loss estimate

Use the seven displayed ET Prop. 1.9 families, splitting unit roots into classes and using the coprime product modulus for the two-block congruences. Denote a class by `r mod m`. Let `U` be the complement in the unit profiles of their union; it is closed. Freeze all components initially at 1, a point of `U` (the unit square). At any finite step only a finite set `S` has been released. A deep component has uniform law on `1+p^K ℤ_p` (`K≥1` and respect `840`, in particular `K₂≥3`). A custom component has depth zero and normalized Haar law on the cylinders `B_p`. Components are independent, the rest fixed at 1. Set

    N = ∏_{p∈S} p^{max(v_p(m)−K_p,0)}.

A possible hit requires `r≡1 mod m/N`, and its probability is at most `C_S/N`. At a depth-zero prime with `v_p(m)=e>0`, the exact probability for an allowed class is `1/(|B_p|p^{e−1})≤3 p^{−e}`. A deep prime contributes at most `p^{−max(e−K_p,0)}`. The factor 4 in a principal ET modulus is retained even when `2|N`: only strip powers of 2 from the factors after the 4. No assumption `gcd(N,m/N)=1` is legitimate.

Here is the check of the input count `O_ε(N^{4/5+ε})` of admissible parameterized classes at fixed `N≥2`, with `m≪N^C`. Allocate the peeled factors to blocks and entries, in `N^{o(1)}` ways. Lowercase primed entries denote retained quotients. The weighted equations for positive integers are as follows:

* I-4 / II-1: `a=A₁a′, b=Bb′, N=A₁B`, `e=4a′b′D−1`, `c=(a+b)/e` give `4a′b′cD=A₁a′+Bb′+c`, for either `−e` or `−1/e` on the principal modulus.
* II-2: `f=Nf′=4acd−1`; the retained congruence gives `f′|1+4a²d`, hence `f′|a+c` by `c(1+4a²d)−af=a+c`. For `w=(a+c)/f′`, `4acdw=Na+Nc+w`.
* I-2: `a=A₁a′, c=Bc′, f=Ff′`, `N=A₁BF`. The two retained congruences give `f=4a′c′D−1`, `f′|a+c`. Then `4a′c′Dw=FA₁a′+FBc′+w` with `w=(a+c)/f′`.
* I-1 / I-3 / II-3: write `c=C₁c′, d=Dd′, f=Ff′, N=C₁DF`, renaming `a,e` as needed. In I-1 take `F=1`. In all three, `f=4c′d′h₁−1`, `f′|1+4c²d`: for I-1 this divisibility is the family condition, in I-3 the retained *root* is 1 mod `f′`, in II-3 reduce `−4c²d−f` separately on the principal and auxiliary moduli. Setting `J=C₁²D`, we get `f′|(h₁+Jc′)` by subtracting `Jc′f` from `h₁(1+4c²d)`. Therefore `4c′d′h₁w=Fh₁+FJc′+w` with `w=(h₁+Jc′)/f′`. I-3 adds at most `2^{ω(f)+1}` choices for a unit root, not a free variable of length `f`.

Each equation is `4xyzd=Lx+My+Hz` with the product of any two distinct coefficient slots `≤N²` (in the last, `F²J=N²/D`). Given the split, the variables recover the parameters. Permute summands to `Lx≥My≥Hz`. Then `yzd≤3L/4`, `e=4yzd−L>0`, `ex≤2My`, so `e²(zd)(xz)(xd)=(ex)²(zd)²≪N⁴`. One of `e,zd,xz,xd` is `≪N^{4/5}`. Fixing `e` fixes the product `yzd` and then `x`; fixing `xz` allows its divisor splits and `(4xz d−M)y=Lx+Hz`. Fixing `xd` or `zd`, split it and use respectively

    (4xd y−H)(4xd z−M)=MH+4xd Lx,
    (4zd x−M)(4zd y−L)=LM+4zd Hz.

All targets are nonzero and polynomially bounded (`x≤2My`; each coefficient `≤N²`). Each fixed small parameter therefore costs `N^{o(1)}` divisor choices, all recovered ET moduli are polynomial in `N`, and the unit root multiplicity is absorbed. At `N=1` there is no admissible class by square avoidance. Fix `α=1/5−ε>0`. Couple the finite laws before and after releasing a prime by keeping every other coordinate. Losing an old survivor on releasing a **new whole** component `q` requires some hit with `q|m`, hence `q|N` because its new depth is 0. Summing the class bounds gives loss at most

    O_ε(3 C_S) · [q^{−α}/(1−q^{−α})] · ∏_{p∈S}(1−p^{−α})^{-1}.

The constants before the `q` factor do not depend on the new `q`; in particular we did not condition a measure on survival and then assume independence. For a **fixed new deep** component `r₀`, losing a survivor requires `v_{r₀}(m)>K`. Thus `m≥r₀^{K+1}` and `m≪N^C` force `N→∞` uniformly. Sum the tail of `N^{−α}` over `S∪{r₀}`-supported integers. Alternate sign tasks (take new `q∤840`, above both the sign and the loss thresholds) and deep releases of the least as yet unassigned prime (take `K` large). ## 3. The local embeddings, including all Dirichlet tasks

At custom place `q_j` use the stored prefix tuple above `π_{q_j} mod q_j`. At a deep place `l` one has `π_l≡1 mod l`. For each fixed prefix the chosen pivots give a dense parameter chart also in the fiber `P=1`: that fiber starts as a point and evolves by line charts over nonempty opens or product lines. At a row step the chosen pivot is nonzero in `C/(P−1)`, and the old fiber is geometrically integral with dense rational chart, so the restriction is still nonzero there. A Dirichlet output does not vanish identically in this fiber either: `a,m` cannot both be divisible by the permanent prime `P−1`, and `Y` is fresh. The fiber projections are dominant onto the preceding fiber, so earlier nonzero restrictions remain nonzero. Thus there is a rational parameter point at `P=1` with all prefix denominators, pivots and **outputs** nonzero (no sign condition here). Outside a finite set of denominator and numerator primes it gives integral residue choices and all the required units. Use increasing cutoffs at deep primes so each handles a finite prefix and these lengths tend to infinity; the finitely many small exceptions can use an empty prefix. At custom primes the prefix lengths tend to infinity by the separate task index. Using only the deep-prime construction at the custom places would not prove integrality almost everywhere.

At each place fix `P=π_l` exactly. Lift the prescribed prefix basis residues to integral elements successively transcendental over the preceding countable field (starting with `ℚ(π_l)`). The pivot units give integral solved row coordinates on the prefix and every prefix output is a unit. For the tail choose integral transcendental basis coordinates in chronological order, solving rows in the **field**. At a Dirichlet variable always choose `Y` integral. If `a,m` evaluate integrally and at least one is a unit, require `a+mY` a unit: at most one residue is excluded if `m` is a unit, none if `m` is not; this works even at 2. Otherwise impose no output restriction. Algebraic independence can be obtained inside any of these opens by countability. This gives injections `φ_l:Frac(A)→ℚ_l`. Each fixed stage generator is integral at almost all places by the two prefix schedules, hence so is every fixed element of `A` (clear its finitely many scalar denominators). This statement emphatically excludes arbitrary inverses in the **field**. All Dirichlet variables themselves are integral everywhere.

## 4. Descent and universality

Set `R={g∈A : v_l(φ_l(g))≥0 for all l}` and abbreviate the pulled-back valuations to `v_l`. Its bounded elements are exactly ℤ. Clearing the finitely many negative valuations gives `A=R⊗ℚ`; canceling the finitely many fractional parts instead gives `g+b∈R` for a rational translate `b` of each `g∈A`. Thus bounded-distance approximation to the real closure passes from `A` to `R` and a standard integer adjustment supplies floors. This gives IOpen by Shepherdson.

For `a,b∈R` nonzero let `(a,b)A=hA`, `u=a/h,v=b/h`; there exist `Uu+Vv=1` in `A`. All four elements `u,v,U,V` are integral almost everywhere, hence `min(v_l(u),v_l(v))=0` almost everywhere. Match these minima at all places by `v_l(γ)` for a single rational scalar `γ`; `d=γh` then has exactly the minima for `a,b`. Thus `a/d,b/d` are integral everywhere and at least one is a unit at each mark. Their `A`-Bézout coefficients are integral except at finitely many places. There correct them by a multiple of the syzygy `(b/d,−a/d)` to an integral local solution, using the local unit. One rational parameter approximates those corrections and is integral elsewhere by CRT with denominators. Hence `(a,b)R=dR`; zero cases are immediate. Do not try to remove all positive valuations of `h`.

For an actual coprime pair `a,m∈R` the Bézout identity implies no common rational-stage prime and a unit input at every place. For every `H∈R` and `m>0` the scheduled `Q=a+mY` is thus prime in `A` and a unit locally everywhere by prefix **or** tail. It descends prime without rescaling: when it divides an `R`-element in `A`, the quotient is integral everywhere. Since `Y∈R`, the progression is respected in `R`. This proves internal Dirichlet, and `P` itself similarly descends prime.

Now let `p` be a positive nonstandard irreducible of `R`. If `v_l(p)>0`, then `p/l∈R`, since `l` is a unit at every other place. This would be a proper factorization (`p/l` is infinite), so **all** `v_l(p)=0`. If `p=fg` with both factors nonunits in `A`, then `v_l(f)+v_l(g)=0` at every place. Both factors are integral almost everywhere, so each has valuation zero almost everywhere. Choose `c∈ℚ*` making `cf` everywhere a local unit; then `g/c` is too. Both are still nonunits in `A`, so this factors `p` into nonunits in `R`, impossible. Thus `p` is an irreducible element of `A` (not an `A`-unit since `A^×=ℚ*`), hence is itself a scheduled element of a finite stage, including its scalar. This argument needs neither atomicity of `R` nor parsimony of the marks for all `A`-elements.

At the assigned `q≡3 mod 4` the value of `p` is a nonsquare unit; therefore `−p` has a root `x mod q`, with `x` a standard integer. Both `y=(x²+p)/q` and `u=(1−vp)/q`, with `v` a standard inverse of the residue of `p`, are in `R`: integral at `q` by their congruences and elsewhere because `q` is a unit. These are precisely `x²+p=qy` and `uq+vp=1`. Take `d=q, k′=(q−3)/4`. Ordinary primes `p≡1 mod 4` have their classical certificates in ℤ by ordinary Dirichlet and reciprocity. This proves the universal sentence, in fact without the `1 mod 4` restriction on nonstandard primes. There is no uniform finite list of moduli asserted.

Finally the embeddings are locally feasible for the marked-section theorem and every `π_l` is transcendental, so (E) holds. At an alleged ES triple choose a common finite stage, spread its regular functions at almost all places, and at the exceptional places prescribe just the open conditions that the three values be integral in the fixed marked fiber. Here the marked-section correction uses only finitely many **exact** zeros of a fixed nonzero polynomial `f` vanishing on the forbidden base values. After a preliminary constant line parameter avoids the geometric bad locus, its section avoids that locus on reduction away from finitely many primes by clearing a polynomial Bézout identity. Correct the parameter by `f(T)c` at the remaining finitely many places: where `f(π_l)≠0` this is a field-affine open constraint on `c`, regardless of its valuation. Elsewhere outside the exceptions, either the bad locus is absent or the correction vanishes on reduction. Thus infinite divisibility of `P−1` does not invalidate the section. For positivity use the *original* tower over `P>0`: deleting a real codimension-at-least-two center from the smooth connected open at a row step preserves semialgebraic connectedness, and the real row bundle is a product there. The positive sector of the cleared triple identity is relatively clopen since each positive denominator is `≥P/4` there. It contains the ordered generic point, hence contains all points over `P>0` (over the real closure, then by real closed field transfer). No sign of an arbitrary function is transported through a finite-place condition. The resulting polynomial section has `P=T`; its triple is positive for `T>0` and integral on the primitive progression `T≡π mod D` for a common coefficient denominator `D`, by local congruence at its divisors. ET Prop. 1.9 and ordinary Dirichlet would then place `π` in the union of the seven families, contrary to `π∈U` (`LOGIC_MARKED_SECTIONS.md`, ES consequences). This proves `¬ES(P)`. *(z74 Opus xreview fix.)* Independently of the section lemma: all stages are line, row or Dirichlet edges, so the descent lemma L2 of `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md` puts any positive triple at `P` in `ℚ[P]`, and the same profile criterion applies.

Infinitely many deep primes divide the nonzero `P−1` in `R`; indeed `R/lR=𝔽_l` by local reduction and division by `l` at the other places. Consequently this is not a PID.
