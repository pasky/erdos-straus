# Simultaneous standard-prime signs at an ES counterexample

**Result (internal proof audit).** There is a countable discretely ordered Bézout domain `R` satisfying open induction and internal Dirichlet, with a positive infinite prime `P ≡ 1 mod 840` failing ES, with the following external property:

> For every finite tuple of pairwise nonassociate positive nonstandard primes `s₁,…,sₖ` of `R`, and every word `ε∈{±1}ᵏ`, infinitely many **ordinary** primes `q ≡ 3 mod 4` have `(sᵢ mod q | q) = εᵢ` simultaneously. The symbol is the ordinary Legendre symbol in `R/qR = 𝔽_q`.

Thus each pair has different sets of standard-prime nonresidues, even with infinitely many discrepancies in each direction. The model satisfies the single internal **NR** sentence of `Z65_LOGIC2.md` §1, always with a standard prime witness. Standardness and the infinite set of tests are external conclusions, not extra predicates. The construction does **not** keep a previously chosen adelic profile fixed. In particular a profile constrained to equal 1 mod every prime but one cannot realize even the one-letter negative word infinitely often at `P`.

The inputs from earlier audits are the rational row tower of `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §1, the tasks in `LOGIC_PID_DIRICHLET_AUDIT.md`, and the exact-zero polynomial section theorem `LOGIC_MARKED_SECTIONS.md`. Instead of square marks, we use a sparse set of fresh test places. Below we include the compressed-family bound from the supplied one-prime audit, since its **uniformity in the fresh prime** matters here. The finite check at the end does not certify an infinite sieve.

## 1. The price of releasing one more place

Write `U` for the closed set of profinite unit profiles in no Elsholtz–Tao Prop. 1.9 class. Begin with the profile identically 1, in `U` by the unit-square obstruction. The active set `S` at each finite step is finite. At an active **background** prime `r` take normalized Haar on `1+r^{K_r}ℤ_r`, `K_r≥1`, at 2 at least 3 and at divisors of 840 at least their exponent in 840. At a **test** prime `r∤840` take normalized Haar on the lifts of `B_r ⊂ 𝔽_r^×`, and put `K_r=0`. All coordinates are independent; the unactivated ones are exactly 1. Put `D_S=∏_{tests r∈S} r/|B_r|`. In particular no uniform bound on `D_S` as levels grow is being assumed.

For a displayed ET class `b mod m`, take the product modulus for the two-block families, and count I-3 unit roots separately. Its hit probability is zero unless `b≡1 mod m/N`, and at most `D_S/N`, where

```
N = ∏_{r∈S} r^{max(v_r(m)−K_r,0)}.
```

Indeed each deep-place congruence either conflicts, is fixed already, or costs a reciprocal power `r^{−(v_r(m)−K_r)}`. At a test with `r^j || m`, `j>0`, one class has probability at most `1/(|B_r| r^{j−1})`. This does not require `(N,m/N)=1`.

**Compressed-family estimate.** For each `N≥2` there are `O_η(N^{4/5+η})` parameterized unit classes with `N|m`, `b≡1 mod m/N` and the fixed factor 4 of a principal block left intact (`N` peels only from the parameters in this block). Every such `m≪N^C` for an absolute `C`. At `N=1` a hit would require the class to contain the unit square 1, impossible. The 2-depth above ensures the fixed-4 restriction in applications.

Here are details of the estimate. Split the removed factor `N` among the modulus factors: e.g. `a=Aa′, c=Bc′`; no coprimality of a removed and a retained factor is needed. The number of ways to split is `N^{o(1)}` (boundedly many divisor choices). The two displayed blocks of a double congruence are coprime. Reduction to the *retained* parts gives the following positive equations, with the names of ET family parameters as in `Z66_UCOVER.md` §2.1. A congruence to the inverse `−1/e` becoming 1 still means `e≡−1` on the retained part.

* I-4, II-1: `a=Aa′, b=Bb′, N=AB`; `e=4a′b′h−1`, `c=(a+b)/e`. Then `4a′b′hc=Aa′+Bb′+c`.
* II-2: `f=Ff′`, `F=N`, `c=(f+1)/(4ad)`. Since `f′|4a²d+1` and `c(4a²d+1)−af=a+c`, take `w=(a+c)/f′`. Then `4acdw=Fa+Fc+w`.
* I-2: `a=Aa′, c=Bc′, f=Ff′, N=ABF`. We have `f=4a′c′h−1` and `f′|a+c`. With `w=(a+c)/f′` obtain `4a′c′hw=FAa′+FBc′+w`.
* I-3, II-3, I-1: write the principal block as `4cd` and the auxiliary parameter as `f` (rename `a` or `e` as needed). Put `c=Cc′, d=Dd′, f=Ff′, N=CDF`, with `F=1` in I-1. Then `f=4c′d′h−1` and `f′|1+4c²d`, in I-1 by definition, in II-3 by the second residue, in I-3 by **squaring the retained root 1**. Put `J=C²D`. As `h(1+4c²d)−Jc′f=h+Jc′`, take `w=(h+Jc′)/f′`. This gives `4c′d′hw=Fh+FJc′+w`.

In each case recover the parameters from the split and the four variables (e.g. in the last line `f=4c′d′h−1`). An I-3 tuple gives at most `2^{ω(f)+1}` unit roots by CRT. In the common form `4xyzd=Lx+My+Hz`, pairwise products of different coefficient slots are ≤`N²` (notably `F²J≤N²`). Permute slots so `Lx≥My≥Hz` and let `e₀=4yzd−L`. Then `yzd≤3L/4`, `0<e₀x=My+Hz≤2My`, and

```
e₀² (zd)(xz)(xd) = (e₀x)² (zd)² ≪ (ML)² ≤ N⁴.
```

Thus some `e₀,zd,xz,xd` is `O(N^{4/5})`. For fixed `e₀` split `4yzd=L+e₀` and determine `x`. For fixed `xz` split it and use `(4xz d−M)y=Lx+Hz`. For fixed `xd` split it and factor `(4xd y−H)(4xd z−M)=MH+4xd Lx`; for fixed `zd` use `(4zd x−M)(4zd y−L)=LM+4zd Hz`. All targets have polynomial size (`L,M,H≤N²`, `x≤2My`), and each divisor choice determines the remaining variables. The recovered moduli have polynomial size by the displayed formulas, also absorbing the I-3 root factor into `N^{o(1)}`. This proves the estimate.

Fix `0<β<1/5` (take `η=1/5−β`). With a new variable attached, couple by keeping the old coordinates unchanged. Two types of releases suffice:

1. **Fresh test at a large `q`.** Suppose `|B_q|≥c q` for a fixed task-dependent `c>0`. A new hit, i.e. membership after but not before release, requires `q|m`. Under the new law `q|N` since the test depth is zero. By the estimate and a union bound the loss of surviving probability is at most

   `O_β(D_S c⁻¹ · [q^{−β}/(1−q^{−β})] · ∏_{r∈S}(1−r^{−β})⁻¹)`.

   This tends to zero for fixed task and old history. There is no sum over candidate test primes: **choose one sufficiently large** after seeing the finite product. Conditioning on a *single prescribed* `P mod q` could cost a factor `q`, invalidating this inference.
2. **Deep release at a fixed new `r`.** Put it at depth `K≥1`. New hits require `v_r(m)>K`. Since `m≪N^C`, the least possible `N` tends to infinity with `K`. Sum `O_β(D_S N^{−β})` over the tail of the `S∪{r}`-smooth integers; for this fixed finite set the series converges by the geometric Euler product. The loss tends to zero with `K`, with no need to condition on survival at earlier steps.

## 2. Dense residue choices for a finite task

Use the countable ordered tower over `ℚ[P]`, before imposing any integral marks. Polynomial approximation steps, coprime row steps `aU+bV=1` and polynomial Dirichlet steps `Y` are as in the cited tower proofs. Denote its union by `A` and field by `F`. Each finite stage `X` is smooth factorial and rational, old primes persist, `A^×=ℚ^×`, and `A` is Bézout. Each stage has a dense chart open in affine `d`-space with coordinates `(P,t₂,…,t_d)`: use a nonzero row entry as pivot and the other free coordinate. **Choose pivots not identically zero on `P=1`**. This works inductively since `P−1` stays prime, the two entries are coprime, and every nonempty chart of the row over the integral fiber dominates its dense base open. Thus the fiber has a dense rational chart too. Each Dirichlet output `a+mY` is not identically zero on that fiber: `a,m` are coprime before the fresh `Y`, and later projections on the fiber are dominant. The finite towers can use consistent charts by continuing to restrict their dense opens.

In a task specify a finite prefix stage, pairwise `ℚ^×`-nonassociate irreducibles `s_i` there, and signs `ε_i`. Shrink its affine coordinate chart to a nonempty affine principal open `V` where `P`, all `s_i`, chart denominators, and every Dirichlet output in the prefix are nonzero and the expressions for stage generators are regular. After clearing finitely many scalar denominators the chart works integrally away from finitely many ordinary primes. No assertion about nonvanishing on `P=1` for the *sign* list is necessary.

**Geometric sign lemma.** On `V` the cover `z_i² = ε_i s_i` is geometrically integral of dimension `d`. For every sufficiently large prime `q≡3 mod 4`, at least `q^d/2^{k+1}` tuples in `V(𝔽_q)` realize all the signs, hence at least `q/2^{k+1}` different nonzero `P` residues can be extended to such a tuple.

To prove this, use valuations on the **original** smooth stage, before deleting any chart denominator. At its height-one prime `(s_i)`, `s_i` has order 1 and all other `s_j` have order 0. After extension to `ℚbar` the divisor is still nonempty and reduced (the quotient in characteristic zero is geometrically reduced), though it may split. Its geometric components lie over its generic point (flat algebraic extension of constants); none is shared with a different divisor. Equivalently, work first over finite number fields, where base change is étale, with ramification index 1 at each prime over `(s_i)`. An odd order at such a component detects each nonempty product of distinct slots even up to constant factors. This is a statement in the function field and survives restriction to `V`. By the elementary multiquadratic extension criterion the generic cover has degree `2^k` over `ℚbar(X)`. The algebra with these monic equations is finite free over the base with the squarefree monomials as basis, hence injects into its generic algebra; it is a domain geometrically.

Spread out this **one fixed cover**; geometric integrality of its generic fiber persists outside finitely many primes (spreading geometric integrality for finite type varieties). Lang–Weil then gives `q^d+O_task(q^{d−1/2})` points on it. The implicit constant need not be uniform in the tuple, chart or prefix. At `q≡3 mod 4` the fixed twist `−1` is a nonsquare: every point below the cover contributes either exactly `2^k` lifts if all signs match or zero otherwise. Since `V` is in affine coordinates with `P` first, a fixed `P mod q` has at most `q^{d−1}` base tuples. Projection gives the claimed set `B_q`. No Chebotarev or uniform-in-fiber bound is required; the lemma does **not** prescribe a word after arbitrarily fixing `P mod q`.

## 3. Countable scheduling and integral descent

Enumerate all finite sign tasks over `A` with infinite repetition, including all rational rescalings that remain pairwise nonassociate. At the `n`th test take a prefix containing its entries and at least the first `n` stages; the entries stay nonassociate irreducibles there by permanent primality and unchanged units. Use §2 to form the candidate projections for this *fixed prefix*, then choose a fresh `q≡3 mod 4`, `q∤840`, arbitrarily large so release 1 costs less than the allotted error. Between successive tests activate the smallest unused prime by release 2, making its error as small as required and respecting all background depths. Use positive error allowances with sum `<1/2`. The schedule, projections and depths are chosen from finite task data and bounds, **not** in response to sampled old residue values. Thus the coordinate laws really do form a product. Every prime is eventually activated. The approximants with frozen tails converge weakly to the product with all these marginals: on each clopen cylinder the probabilities eventually agree. By closedness of `U` (or finite-cylinder approximation from above), the final mass of `U` is at least `1/2`. Every marginal is now non-atomic, so avoid simultaneously the countable union of events that some `π_r` is algebraic over `ℚ`. Fix such a surviving `π` in the product of the allowed cylinders. In particular `π≡1 mod 840`.

Build embeddings `φ_r:F→ℚ_r` with `φ_r(P)=π_r`. At each test pick a realizing prefix tuple above this residue and lift the remaining prefix parameters in their residue disks, successively transcendental over the countable field of earlier lifts including `π_r`. At a background place `π_r≡1 mod r`: for each fixed prefix choose a rational point on the `P=1` chart avoiding zeros of all prefix Dirichlet outputs. Outside finitely many primes per prefix it gives integral chart coordinates, unit denominators and unit outputs there. Use increasing cutoffs so the chosen background prefix length tends to infinity with `r` (empty prefix allowed at small `r`); lift in the same way. At tests the prefix lengths also tend to infinity outside finite exceptions. The lifts give integrality of every fixed stage generator **almost everywhere**. At each place continue the tail chronologically by transcendental integral free parameters. For a Dirichlet variable, if the two inputs are integral and one is a unit, exclude the unique forbidden residue for `Y` when `m` is a unit; otherwise `a` is already a unit when the condition is active. Thus `Y` is everywhere integral, and the output is a unit whenever this condition applies. At prefix variables this follows from the prefix constraints. Tail row denominators may be nonunits at a place; only almost-everywhere integrality of each fixed generator is asserted.

Set `R={g∈A : v_r(φ_r(g))≥0 for every r}`, abbreviating the valuations to `v_r(g)`. For clarity, here are the descent checks, which do **not** use finite support of the positive valuations of a single polynomial.

* Every `g∈A` has finitely many negative valuations. Clearing them gives `A=R⊗ℚ`; rational CRT translation moves `g` to `R` within bounded distance. The bounded elements of the ordered `A` are rational, those of `R` integral. By the approximation stages `R` is an integer part of `F^rc` and satisfies IOpen.
* For `a,b∈R` nonzero write `(a,b)A=hA` and `a=hu,b=hv`. There are `U,V∈A` with `Uu+Vv=1`; at almost every place all four terms `U,V,u,v` are integral, so `min(v_r(u),v_r(v))=0` there. Take `γ∈ℚ^×` with exactly these residual minima as valuations. Then `d=γh` lies in `R`, divides both in `R`, and `a/d,b/d` are locally primitive everywhere. Their `A`-row solving the unit identity is integral almost everywhere. Where it is not, integral solutions exist locally by primitivity; adjust by the direction `(b/d,−a/d)` times a single rational parameter, approximating the finitely many local corrections and integral elsewhere. This gives a unit row over `R`, so `(a,b)R=dR`. A zero input is immediate.
* Each positive nonstandard prime of `R` has valuation **zero everywhere**: `R/rR=𝔽_r` by reduction (division by `r` changes no other integrality condition), and a nonstandard irreducible cannot be a multiple of the nonunit `r`. It is a prime element by Bézout, and divides no nonzero standard integer by discreteness. Hence localization at the standard integers keeps its ideal proper and prime: it is an irreducible of `A`. Conversely an `A`-prime with zero valuations everywhere is prime in `R`, since a quotient by it in `A` preserves integrality. In particular `P` is prime. For two such `R`-primes with `s₂=k s₁`, `k∈ℚ^×`, every `v_r(k)=0`, so `k=±1`, and positivity forces equality. This is why the enumeration over *nonassociate* `A`-primes covers all required tuples of `R`-primes without guessing which elements will be normalized. Signs of arbitrary rational associates are correlated by `(k|q)`; they are not independent slots. Constants of `A` are not irreducibles, and constants in `R` are precisely `ℤ`.
* For internal Dirichlet inputs with `m>0` and `(a,m)R=R`, their common stage has coprime inputs (a common prime factor would persist). At every place one of `a,m` is a unit, by the global identity. Thus the scheduled positive prime `Q=a+mY>H` in `A` has unit valuations everywhere, and is prime in `R` with `Y∈R`. The divisibility in the progression is in `R`.
* For ES, a hypothetical positive triple at `P` occurs on a finite stage with the cleared identity. The embeddings give feasible local points, integral on this stage almost everywhere and with the three values integral everywhere. Each `π_r` is transcendental, so the exact-zero hypothesis (E) of `LOGIC_MARKED_SECTIONS.md` holds. Its section with `P↦T` yields a positive polynomial triple integral on an ordinary progression containing `π` (positivity uses connectedness of the real `P>0` tower, not approximation of arbitrary inequalities). Its class is then polynomially solvable, contrary to `π∈U` by the profile criterion. This proves **nonstandard** `¬ES(P)`.

## 4. NR and the boundaries

For a nonstandard prime `s`, a test with sign `−` has `−s` square modulo the **standard** prime `q≡3 mod 4`. Take an ordinary root `x`; then `y=(x²+s)/q∈R`. As `s mod q` is a unit, choose an ordinary inverse `v` modulo `q` and `u=(1−vs)/q∈R`. This is exactly `x²+s=qy, uq+vs=1`; it works in particular for every `s≡1 mod 4`. For ordinary positive primes `p≡1 mod 4`, the usual integer NR witnesses work unchanged: by ordinary CRT and Dirichlet choose `q≡3 mod 4` whose residue is a nonresidue mod `p`, then ordinary reciprocity gives `(p|q)=−1`. This handles the constants in the universal quantifier. If one also wants finite sign slots at distinct positive **standard** primes alongside the nonstandard slots, choose the candidate `q` in a compatible reduced progression first: at odd `p` prescribe `(q|p)=ε_p(−1|p)` and at 2 use `q≡3` or `7 mod 8` for sign `−` or `+`. Ordinary Dirichlet supplies arbitrarily large candidates; the sign lemma holds at all sufficiently large ones. Adding these tasks to the enumeration costs the same. For other rational constants only signs compatible with their multiplicative relations and `(-1|q)=-1` can be prescribed.

**Reciprocity does not follow.** In fact `QR_std` of `Z65_LOGIC2.md` fails here. Take a `+` test at `P` with `q≡3 mod 4`. Then `P` is a square mod `q`, but `−q` has no square root mod `P`: a root would give one in a finite stage modulo `P`. Starting from `ℚ[P]/(P)=ℚ`, that quotient has purely transcendental fraction field at every stage: over the generic point of `P=0` one row pivot survives by coprimality, so the generic fiber adds a free variable, as does a polynomial step. It cannot contain `√(−q)`. The sign tests read primes *modulo standard primes*; there is no symmetry exchanging numerator and modulus in the model.

**No external PID.** For all background primes, in particular all primes `r≡1 mod 4`, `r | P−1` in `R`. Thus the nonzero element `P−1` has infinitely many standard prime divisors. This rules out parsimony (unit valuation almost everywhere for every nonzero polynomial value) and external factoriality/PID in this construction. The summability argument covers finite active sets at each release, not an unrestricted Euler product. Neither a parsimonious uncovered nonsquare profile nor an obstruction to *all* such profiles follows. Nothing about the truth of ES at an ordinary prime follows either.

**Finite replay.** `uv run --no-sync python scripts/z73_prime_sign_audit.py` enumerates a row chart with five prime slots, counts a fixed signed twist by its roots, checks the size bound for projection, checks the obstruction for a rational associate, and tests mixed-depth class probabilities with an actual sign projection. At `q=43,47,59,71` all 32 words occur; the smallest projection sizes are respectively `10,10,14,16`, including split and nonsplit reductions of `P²−2`. These are small-instance checks, not asymptotics used in the proof.
