# z52b lane `dividing`: where small-count problems split into solved and failed

**ES is OPEN.** Nothing here proves or disproves it. Items are candidate connections, listed breadth-first. The only
filter was against obvious nonsense: one draft item, "4/n with three odd denominators", was rewritten after a parity
check showed it is impossible. Data: `data/z52b/dividing.jsonl` (generated by `scripts/z52b_dividing_build.py` from
`scripts/z52b_dividing_batch*.py`).

- **Tag histogram (≥ 3):** {{HIST}}
- **The dividing line, read off the catalog (Assessment).**
  - Proved pointwise results with O(1)–polylog witnesses come from one of:
    1. rigid algebra: involutions, class number one, Heegner points, cyclotomic or Zsigmondy divisors, descent from
       a forced object;
    2. a power-size reservoir;
    3. GRH-conditional fixed-modulus character events.
  - Known failures come from:
    1. covering systems on periodic witness orbits;
    2. explicit sparse algebraic families (Crocker), reciprocity or spinor obstructions on thin sets (squares), and
       non-BM reduction-theory obstructions for integral points (Markoff);
    3. count deficits, as for m/n with large m (Pomerance–Weingartner).
  - Where ES stands:
    - It has none of the success mechanisms.
    - Of the failure mechanisms, it has only the thin-set reciprocity obstruction (T1 at squares).
    - Its witness count (log² p, or log³ intensity in the residue model below) is one log above the only known
      infinitely failing log-count problem (p + 2^a + 2^b).
    - Its exact count twin, p + 2^a + 2^b + 2^c, is open.
- **New computations in this lane** (EVIDENCE; scripts `scripts/z52b_dividing_{avoid,leastfam,indep,qnr}.py`, output
  in `data/z52b/dividing_*.txt`):
  1. *Coverage decay.* Take the residues in Mordell's hard classes that are not covered by any Type I/II family with
     parameter product ≤ X. Their fraction δ(X) decays like exp(−0.028 (ln X)³) over X = 56..1778.
  2. *Least family size.* The least family size s(p) has a distribution that does not depend on p: the median is 22
     both for p < 10⁷ and for p < 10⁸. Setting (number of primes)·δ(X) = 1 predicts max s ≈ 1210 for p < 10⁷;
     the observed max is 1133. For p < 10⁸ the prediction is ≈ 1990 and the observed max is 2814, at
     p = 53049049.
  3. *Correlation.* Families are strongly positively correlated: the survivor fraction is about 8.5·10⁵ times the
     independent prediction at X = 1000. Even so, δ keeps the log³ shape.
  4. *Least non-residue.* Difficulty tracks the least quadratic non-residue n(p): corr(log s, log n(p)) = 0.42.
     The underlying fact is elementary and probably the campaign's known sign layer: Type I at (a, d) needs
     (d/p) = −1.
  - Together these suggest a conjectural law: the least family size grows like exp(((ln x)/c)^{1/3}). This is
    consistent with H4_NOT_POLYLOG's proved lower bounds exp((log p)^κ), κ < 1/12.
  - They also link ES-hard primes to *pseudosquares*; that word does not appear in the repo's memos.

### Ten items I find most exciting

1. **#0310–0313: the residue (CRT) model of ES coverage.** It decays like exp(−c log³ X) and predicts the observed
   extremes. It also sets up a "random covering intensity" axis on which ES (log³) sits above Crocker's problem
   (log).
2. **#0312: conjectural Linnik law exp((ln x/c)^{1/3}) for the least certificate family.** This gives a target
   exponent of 1/3 for the H4_NOT_POLYLOG lower-bound programme.
3. **#0329: certificate families are strongly correlated, yet the decay keeps its shape.** This measures the one
   unknown dividing-line coordinate (independence), and suggests the correlation is only a rescaling of the
   intensity.
4. **#0330/#0331: ES-hardness tracks pseudosquare-ness (QR patterns).** Pseudosquare tables give a ready stress-test
   family, and T1 is the limiting case.
5. **#0058/#0222/#0326: Schinzel's m/p family as a calibration set.** Pomerance–Weingartner (arXiv:2511.16817) give
   a proved failure regime and a Poisson model. Running the residue model at m = 8..15, where real exceptions
   exist, is the strongest available check of any "ES is true" heuristic.
6. **#0003/#0166: is p + 2^a + 2^b + 2^c ever not representable?** Sun conjectures it always is, and it has the
   same log² count as ES. Whether Crocker's construction extends to it is the single most informative calibration.
7. **#0255 / #0141 ("k = 2 reduction"): Stewart's criterion.** m/n is a sum of two unit fractions iff two coprime
   divisors of n sum to a multiple of m. With it, ES becomes a union of fully classified k = 2 gates, and each
   failure is a subgroup that avoids −1.
8. **#0334: ES as a covering of the quadratic-character cube {±1}^ℓ minus its all-ones corner.** T1 and ES become
   two halves of one combinatorial statement.
9. **#0195 / #0318: ES as "Frobenius always lands in a union of growing-level Chebotarev sets".**
   The required level is only exp((log p)^{1/3}), far below power level, but it is needed pointwise.
10. **#0218 + the two-squares forced-object item: Legendre-style descent from a forced object.** Legendre's descent
    is the only elementary O(1)-count pointwise proof at p ≡ 1 mod 4. The forced objects to start from are the
    fundamental unit of Q(√p) and the decomposition p = a² + b². The cheap first test is whether identities in
    (a, b) evade Mordell–Schinzel, which only rules out identities in p.

## Method

The brief asked for ≥ 100 small-count problems classified by outcome, then for the features that separate them, then
for in-between problems.

- **Catalog.** I wrote the catalog from memory, organised by mechanism:
  - primes plus sparse sequences, and coverings;
  - quadratic forms and class field theory;
  - Egyptian fractions;
  - continued fractions and thin orbits;
  - local–global failures and integral points;
  - dynamics and probabilistic constructions;
  - count extremes;
  - calibration items for "numerically true, then false".
- **Features and pairs.** I mined features by looking at what separates proved from failed entries in each
  mechanism group. Minimal pairs are entries differing in essentially one feature.
- **Computations.** Following the "follow the lens" instruction, I ran four cheap computations. They all use the
  Type I/II parametrisation: f | 4a²d+1 and 4ad | p+f, or e | a+b and 4ab | p+e (Pomerance's 2026 slides).
- **Citations checked this session:**
  - Pomerance–Weingartner arXiv:2511.16817 (abstract) and Pomerance's Alladi-70 slides (math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/esconjfl.pdf);
  - arXiv:2007.11062 (abstract);
  - Machiavelo–Tsopanidis arXiv:2003.02592;
  - Bourgain–Kontorovich's Hensley counterexample {2,4,6,8,10};
  - Rouse's 451 theorem arXiv:1111.0979;
  - Crocker 1971 (via secondary source);
  - Sun arXiv:0901.3075 (conjecture on three powers of 2);
  - Melfi 1996 (via secondary source);
  - Cassels' cubic-reciprocity congruences (via Siksek).
- **Unverified leads** are phrased as "check whether …". Other classical facts are stated from memory at the usual
  textbook level.
- `seen:` tags come from quick `git grep` checks; tagging is not exhaustive.
