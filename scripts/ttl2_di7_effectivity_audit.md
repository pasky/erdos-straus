# DI Theorem 7: audit of effectiveness

## Verdict and a necessary normalization correction
- **The proof supports an effective double-exponential constant for the actual DI theorem**, for 0 < ε ≤ 1: Cε ≤ exp(exp(A/ε)), with an absolute, effectively choosable A. This requires elementary constant bookkeeping and an explicit choice of smooth cutoff; DI does not itself state this quantitative conclusion.
- **The inequality in the question is not literally (1.41).** On journal p. 233, the weight X^{4iκj} accompanies **Q + N + X√N**, not Q + N + √(NX). Equivalently, with weight Y^{2iκj}, the bound is (QN)^ε(Q + N + √(NY))N. This agrees with (8.4), (8.17), and the end of the proof on p. 278. Thus the audit establishes EFF for this correctly normalized statement, not the stronger, mismatched formula in the question.
- No Siegel lower bound, ineffective zero-free region, or ineffective spectral-gap constant occurs in the dependency chain. The induction raises an already double-exponential constant to a polynomial-in-1/ε power; it does not introduce a third exponential.

## Scope and classification
I visually checked the supplied scan at journal pp. 228–236, 242–245, and 253–278. I did not inspect pp. 246–252, which derive identities rather than the ε-dependent inequalities audited here. Page references below are journal pages; relevant scanned PDF page k is journal page 218+k. Theorem 7's proof is actually in §8.3, after Theorem 14; its recurrence is established in §8.2, not §8.1.
Classes: (a) divisor/gcd estimates; (b) logarithms; (c) induction thresholds; (d) transforms, smoothing, integration by parts; (e) an ineffective or unquantified dependency needing attention.
Exact Kuznetsov/Petersson identities and Weil's bound are accepted as established results; their original external proofs were not re-audited. DI explicitly omits some convergence details of the trace-formula derivation on p. 255. These are identities, not hidden ε-dependent numerical bounds.

## 1. Theorem 2: where N^{1+ε} actually comes from
Statement: p. 230, (1.28)–(1.30), with bound (K² + μ(a)N^{1+ε})||a||². Proof: §§5.1–5.3, pp. 255–261. It is not an appeal to ineffective bounds on individual Fourier coefficients or to Ramanujan–Petersson.
- Arithmetic input: Weil (2.17), p. 243, |S(m,n;c)| ≤ (m,n,c)^{1/2}c^{1/2}τ(c), and its cusp analogue Lemma 2.6, (2.18). The quadratic bound (1.25), p. 229, contains τ(c)². These are **(a)**, effective, with absolute constants. The cusp parametrization (2.14)–(2.16), p. 242, is elementary and exact.
- Proposition 3, (1.26): hybrid additive large sieve (5.1), p. 255, and Mellin separation, p. 256. The Mellin integral has stationary-phase decay (1+|t|)^{-1/2} and a t^{-2} tail. This introduces no ε-loss; **(d)** with fixed-order derivatives. DI leaves (5.1) as an exercise; the elementary hybrid large sieve has effective absolute constants.
- Proposition 3, (1.27): split c ≤ N^{1−δ} from N^{1−δ} < c ≤ N, using (1.26) in the latter range (p. 256). In the former, Cauchy–Schwarz and Poisson lead to the integrations by parts on p. 257, with **p = floor(2/δ)**. The nonresonant tail is bounded by N(c/N)^p ≤ c^{-1}; the resonant term is bounded by Ramanujan sums and gcd sums. The final displayed bound there is θ^{-1}cN^{1+δ}||b||⁴ before taking a square root.
- In particular, the resonant gcd sum is controlled by Σ_{h≤N}(h,c)/h ≤ τ(c)(1+log N). Thus its loss is **(a)+(b)**, not solely the original Weil bound.
- **Important smoothing qualification:** the p = O(1/δ) step is **(d)**, not automatically polynomial in 1/δ. DI only says η is C∞, equals 1 on [1,2], and is supported in (1/2,3). An arbitrary unspecified C∞ cutoff has no advertised derivative-growth bound. Choose a standard effective Gevrey-2 cutoff, ||η^{(p)}||∞ ≤ B^{p+1}(p!)². The repeated differentiations then cost at most exp(O(δ^{-1}log(2/δ))), harmless here. This is a necessary quantitative implementation of DI's freely chosen test function, not a new number-theoretic hypothesis.
- Holomorphic part: Proposition 4, (5.2), pp. 258–259, follows from Petersson, the explicit Bessel kernel E_K, and the preceding quadratic estimates. The split at Δ = √(c/N) or π/2 produces (cN)^δ and the bound labelled (5.5), c^{-δ}N^{1+5δ}. Summing c^{-1−δ} costs O(1+1/δ); replace 5δ by the target ε. Sources: **(a)+(b)+(d)**, no induction.
- Maaß/continuous part: pp. 260–261, (5.5)–(5.7) (DI reuses the label (5.5)). Gaussian integration and one integration by parts give explicit kernels. The four c-ranges use (1.25), (1.26), (1.27). Summing them gives μ(a)KN(N^δ + μ(a)NK e^{-K²})||a||². Enlarging K to K + (μ(a)N)^δ absorbs the exponential error. Partial summation yields N^{1+δ}log(2K), then N^{1+2δ} together with K². These losses are **(a)+(b)+(d)**; no ineffective threshold.
Conclusion: Theorem 2 admits C₂(δ) ≤ exp(exp(B/δ)); the potentially worst arithmetic cost is the divisor bound, while the growing derivative order must not be overlooked.

## 2. Theorems 9 and 14: no recursive dependence on Theorems 5 or 6
- Theorem 9: statement (1.50)–(1.52), p. 235. Proof is Theorem 13, (7.6)–(7.8), pp. 269–270, specialized to Γ₀(rs), the cusps ∞ and 1/s, and the explicit Kloosterman identification (1.6). It uses **Theorem 8**, not Theorem 5 or 6.
- Theorem 8: (1.46), p. 234; proof pp. 267–268. Apply the exact trace formula, Cauchy–Schwarz, Theorem 2 and Lemma 7.1. Split the real spectrum at R₁ = X+1 and R₂ = max(X,Y)+1. The N^δ and M^δ come from Theorem 2; log factors come from the transforms. Classes **(a)+(b)+(d)** through the already audited inputs.
- Lemma 7.1, (7.1)–(7.4), pp. 264–267, expressly has absolute constants. Its proof uses integral representations, a Mellin contour at fixed real part −1, Stirling, and at most two integrations by parts. No ε-dependent contour shift or increasing derivative order occurs here: **(d)** with absolute constants.
- Theorem 13 separates m,n by a double Fourier integral, taking derivative orders 0 or 2; its integral is explicitly 16 on p. 270. The (CMN)^δ loss is inherited from Theorem 8 plus absorption of logarithms: **(a)+(b)**; separation itself has fixed effective constants.
- Exceptional eigenvalues in the general Theorem 9 are retained as the explicit factor involving θ_{rs}. In the only case used for Theorem 14, **r=s=1**, Theorem 3 gives θ₁=0 and (1.52) applies. Theorem 3 has an explicit geometric proof, pp. 261–262, actually λ₁ ≥ 3π²/2. No exceptional-spectrum induction is hidden here.
- Thus Theorem 14 does not feed back through Theorem 6 or Theorem 7. The discussion on p. 236 about subsequently using Theorem 6 belongs to other applications, not this dependency.
- Theorem 14: (8.13), p. 275; proof p. 276. Dyadically restrict c to [C,2C] and use Lemma 8.2, (8.14). Its Fourier majorants satisfy (8.15), |f̂_M(m)| ≤ 1+4log M, and (8.16), |f̂_M(m)| ≤ (M/m)² for m≠0: **(b)+(d)**, explicit fixed-order operations.
- Zero frequencies in p. 276 are Ramanujan sums, bounded by gcds. Divisor sums and (8.15)–(8.16) give (CMN)^δ(C²+CM+CN): **(a)+(b)**. For nonzero frequencies use Theorem 9 in the modular-group case (1.52), with Fourier-coefficient tails summed by dyadic decomposition; these give the stated C^{1+δ}MN, allowing ε-reallocation to absorb logarithms. Dyadic summation adds only logarithmic/geometric-series constants. Consequently C₁₄(δ) ≤ exp(exp(B/δ)).

## 3. Test function (8.6), identity (8.7), and Lemma 8.1
- Choose φ(x)=Φ(Yx) with one fixed smooth plateau Φ, supported in [1/2,5/2] and equal to 1 on [1,2], as in (8.6), p. 271. Its fixed-order derivative constants do not depend on Y or ε. Similarly take f(q)=Φ(q/Q), p. 272. These choices are **(d)** and do not require ε-dependent transition widths.
- The small-argument Bessel expansion (8.1), p. 270, gives the real-spectrum bound (8.2) and positive exceptional lower bound (8.3). Formula (8.7), p. 272, is **a fixed-q** Kuznetsov identity with error O((1+q^{-1}N^{1+δ})||a||²); averaging over q is done afterwards. The error comes from Theorem 2 applied to all three nonexceptional spectral pieces. Its δ-dependence is precisely the audited large-sieve dependence.
- Exchange q and c via the second exact Kuznetsov identity (8.8). Mellin separation (8.9) has χ(it) ≪ (1+t⁴)^{-1}; four fixed integrations by parts suffice. The regular spectrum/error (8.10) again uses Theorem 2. Classes **(a)+(d)** through those inputs, not any bound for inverse L-values of Eisenstein series.
- On p. 273 split exceptional parameters at iκ=δ. For 0<iκ≤δ, the transform is ≪Y^{2δ}log Y. Absorb log Y into a further Y^δ, with O(1/δ) cost: **(b)**. For δ<iκ≤1/4, the displayed sine/Gamma denominators have at most O(1/δ) growth: **(d)**. This is the source of the displayed (YN)^{3δ} error; replace 3δ by the desired tolerance.
- Cauchy–Schwarz and four dyadic enlargements (Q,Y)→(2^lQ,2^lY), l=0,1,2,3, finish Lemma 8.1, (8.5), pp. 271–273. No ε-dependent number of these enlargements. Its c(δ) has the same double-exponential envelope as Theorem 2.
- The bound iκ≤1/4 used here and in all comparisons of Y-weights is Selberg's explicit **λ≥3/16**, Theorem 4, pp. 232, 262–263. DI derives it from Weil and Kuznetsov by letting Y→∞. Choosing a nonzero Fourier coefficient establishes a numerical exponent bound; no effective lower bound for that coefficient or rate of convergence is subsequently needed. This is not Siegel-type ineffectivity.

## 4. Theorem 7 itself, pp. 276–278
- In (8.17)–(8.18), remove the smooth weight by partial summation, put k=qc, and bound its multiplicity by τ(k)≪δ k^δ (explicitly mentioned on p. 277). Apply Theorem 14. The losses are **(a)** plus the preceding constants. Comparing weights and taking **Y₁=Q+N** gives (8.18); this uses only iκ≤1/4, with effective absolute constants. **A typographical issue in DI:** the following line actually prints Y₁=√(Q+N), which does not give the displayed estimate from the preceding bound. The choice Q+N does, and is an immediate repair with no effectiveness cost.
- For (8.19), prove S(Q,Y,N,0)≪Q^{1+4δ}N at Y=Q^{2−2δ}/N. The base range Q≤Q₀(δ) uses Theorem 2 and S(Q,Y)≤Y^{1/2}S(Q,1); its constant can be bounded by K(δ)Q₀^{O(1)}. The upper-N range is supplied by (8.18), not Theorem 6.
- In the remaining range, Lemma 8.1 changes the level to Q₁=πNY/Q=πQ^{1−2δ}. Require Q₁≤Q−1 by increasing Q₀ effectively. The changing coefficients n^{it} are removed by the displayed partial summation on p. 277, costing O(1+t²); its integral against (1+t⁴)^{-1} is an absolute finite constant.
- The last estimate on p. 278 saves Q^{8δ²} against Q^{1+4δ}. Choose Q₀ so that Q₀^{cδ²}≥2K(δ), together with the preceding decrease condition. Absorb the Q^{1+3δ}N error. This is **(c)**, and yields an induction constant at most K(δ)^{O(δ^{-2})}, after increasing K to include fixed constants.
- Dyadically passing from intervals (8.17) to n≤N and q≤Q adds at worst fixed logarithmic losses (**b**). All exponent reassignments can use δ=ε/B for an absolute B. **There is no need to invoke a divisor or large-sieve bound with tolerance ε²**; ε² occurs in the induction saving only.

## 5. Quantitative envelope and what is/is not printed
For fixed r, sup_{x≥1}(log x)^r x^{-δ}=(r/(eδ))^r. For fixed B, the elementary prime-factor argument gives sup_n τ(n)^B n^{-δ}≤exp(exp(O_B(1/δ))). Fixed-degree divisor sums have the same envelope. The Gevrey cutoff step costs only exp(O(δ^{-1}log(2/δ))); fixed-order Mellin/Gamma estimates and geometric sums cost polynomial factors in 1/δ. Hence all noninductive inputs can be placed under K(δ)≤exp(exp(B/δ)). Finally,

    Cε ≤ K(ε/B)^{O(ε^{-2})} ≤ exp(exp(A/ε))  (0<ε≤1).

DI supplies neither a numerical A nor bounds for the derivative norms of its unspecified η. Accordingly one should say “the proof can be made effective with this growth by explicit cutoff choice and bookkeeping,” not “DI explicitly proves this constant.” The arbitrary-C∞ issue is initially an unquantified dependency **(e)** if one insists on reading off constants without making that choice; it becomes the effective **(d)** bound above. Together with the evident Y₁ typo noted above, it is readily repairable, not an obstruction to EFF for the correctly normalized theorem. No other ε-dependent ineffectivity was found in the inspected dependency chain.

## 6. Nebentypus: Drappeau, arXiv:1504.05549
Downloaded the PDF and checked its text, especially pp. 10, 13–19 (the PDF's printed “Page … of 44”). The following is a plausibility assessment, not a separate complete quantitative reproof of its cited twisted trace formulas.
- Lemma 4.2 (p. 10) uses an effective twisted Weil bound with τ(c)^{O(1)}(cq₀)^{1/2}; Lemma 4.6 (p. 13) adapts DI Proposition 3, the character factors cancelling in the resonant Poisson term. Proposition 4.7, pp. 13–15, explicitly repeats DI pp. 258–261. These bring the same **(a),(b),(d)** losses, including the increasing-order cutoff qualification.
- Lemmas 4.9 and 4.10 are stated on p. 16. Their recurrence is Lemma 4.11, (4.29), proved on p. 17 using scaled fixed cutoffs, Kuznetsov, Proposition 4.7, and the same small-exceptional-parameter split; the transform estimate there is explicitly ≪εY^{2|t_f|}+Y^ε.
- The strengthened initial estimate (4.30), pp. 17–18, uses k=q₀qc and divisor multiplicities. Taking absolute values removes χ from the incomplete sums; one then extends to all k, applies the Fourier majorants of DI Lemma 8.2 and **untwisted DI Theorem 14**. Thus the crucial modular-group/no-exceptional input remains untwisted even here.
- On p. 19 Drappeau reproduces the two DI inductions using (4.29), (4.30), Proposition 4.7, and E(Y)≤(Y/Z)^{1/2}E(Z). He explicitly notes that the q₀ factors help the induction, and that only Selberg's θ≤1/4 is used. No Siegel estimate appears in these lemmas.
- **Same EFF envelope is therefore very plausible, uniformly in the stated character/level parameters**, after the same explicit smoothing and threshold bookkeeping. Lemma 4.10 uses weight Y^{2|t_f|} and √(NY) on the right, confirming the normalization warning at the start. This inspection does not certify every external nebentypus trace-formula reference, nor does Drappeau print a quantitative Cε bound.
