## 1. The coordinate form of the certificate

### 1.1 Theorem 1 (PROVED)

Notation: for n ≡ 1 (mod 4) put **𝒞_n := {x ∈ ℕ : x ≡ 0 (mod 4) or x ≡ −n (mod 8)}** (so 𝒞_n = 4ℕ ∪ (8ℕ + 7) for n ≡ 1 (8)
and 4ℕ ∪ (8ℕ + 3) for n ≡ 5 (8)). A *word* of n is (t, u, v) ∈ ℕ³ with tuv − u − v = n; A = tu − 1, A* = tv − 1, AA* = tn + 1.
The five-slice certificate count is N₋(n) = #{words : 4 divides one of t, u, v, A, A*} (ordered words), and for gcd(uv, n) = 1
Theorem A (FOREST_DEEP_FABLE) says this is #{words : (uv/n) = −1} (Jacobi symbol).

**Theorem 1.** Let n ≡ 1 (mod 4) and let (t, u, v) be a word of n with gcd(uv, n) = 1. Then
  (uv/n) = −1  ⟺  {t, u, v} ∩ 𝒞_n ≠ ∅.
Equivalently (for any n ≡ 1 (4), without the coprimality): 4 | t or 4 | u or 4 | v or 4 | A or 4 | A*  ⟺  some coordinate
lies in 𝒞_n.

*Proof.* By Theorem A it suffices to prove the second equivalence. (⇐) If 4 divides a coordinate it is a slot. If
u ≡ −n (mod 8): n + u ≡ 0 (mod 8) and vA = n + u, so v₂(v) + v₂(A) ≥ 3 and one of v, A is ≡ 0 (mod 4) — a slot. The same
for v (with uA* = n + v). If t ≡ −n (mod 8) (t odd): AA* = tn + 1 ≡ 1 − n² ≡ 0 (mod 8), so one of A, A* is ≡ 0 (mod 4). (⇒) Suppose 4 | A = tu − 1
and none of t, u, v is ≡ 0 (mod 4); then t, u are odd and n = vA − u. If A ≡ 0 (mod 8) then u ≡ −n (mod 8). If A ≡ 4 (mod 8)
and v is even, again u ≡ −n (mod 8). If A ≡ 4 (mod 8) and v is odd, then n ≡ 4 − u (mod 8) and tu ≡ 5 (mod 8), so
t ≡ 5u (mod 8): for u ≡ 3 (8), t ≡ 7 and n ≡ 1, i.e. t ≡ −n (8); for u ≡ 7 (8), t ≡ 3 and n ≡ 5, again t ≡ −n (8); u ≡ 1, 5 (8)
would give n ≡ 3, 7 (mod 8), excluded. The case 4 | A* is symmetric. ∎

CERTIFIED (`scripts/z49_ltfable2_lemmas.py` L1): on all 160 admissible cells of (ℤ/8)³ (tuv − u − v ≡ 1 mod 4) the five-slot
predicate equals the coordinate predicate (120 certificate cells, 0 mismatches); on all 45 437 coprime words of all
n ≡ 1 (4) ≤ 4000 the Jacobi symbol (uv/n) = −1 agrees with the coordinate predicate (0 mismatches); the C scan
`scripts/z49_ltfable2_scan.c` reports `viol(slot!=coord) = 0` on every word of every prime p ≡ 1 (4) ≤ 10⁸ (2 880 504 primes).

*Remarks.* (i) The statement is an elementary corollary of Theorem A and Lemma P (FOREST_DEEP_FABLE); Lemma P(ii) is the
direction (⇐) for t, u, v, and (⇒) — **the numerator slots are redundant** — is the half not stated before; it also
follows in two lines from y5's Ptolemy law (BRAINSTORM/y5_transport_is_ptolemy Cor 2: if the even edge of the pentagon
contains a coordinate ≡ 0 (4) we are done, otherwise the opposite vertex o is a coordinate and 8 | ee′ puts o ≡ −n (8)).
So the novelty is the statement, not the mechanism. (ii) The numerator classes
{A or A* ≡ 0 (4) or 7 (8)} (Lemma P(iii)) do *not* cover all certificates: 8 of the 120 certificate cells have both
numerators outside {0, 4, 7} mod 8 (e.g. (t, u, v) ≡ (1, 3, 4) mod 8, n ≡ 5: A ≡ 2, A* ≡ 3); so among the three single-quantity
descriptions (endpoints, levels, numerators) only endpoints ∪ levels is complete. (iii) The split by coordinate: 94 of the 120
certificate cells have an endpoint in 𝒞_n, 72 have the level in 𝒞_n, 46 both, 48 endpoints only, 26 level only (`L1`).
(iv) Each pair of coordinates of a word determines the third (**Lemma 1.5**, trivial: v = (n+u)/(tu−1), t = (n+u+v)/(uv),
u = (n+v)/(tv−1)); hence there is no single-coordinate (Vieta-type) mutation at fixed n — the pentagon relations of
FRIEZE_ES are the definitions of A, A*, and the frieze rotations change n (DISCOVERIES (J)9 remark). Multi-coordinate maps
do exist on the free layer: (1, u, v) ↦ (v, 1, u − 1) preserves n (both families are indexed by the divisors of n + 1) and
maps the level-1 line onto the vertex-1 line; no such map between interior words is known, and Theorem 2.3 shows none is
forced on cell counts. Work item 1's "partial moves" give nothing beyond this.

### 1.2 The sign-free covering form (Corollary 1.1, PROVED)

For every n ≡ 1 (mod 4), with ES₅(n) meaning "some word of n has one of the five slots" (each slot is an explicit 4/n
identity valid for every n; no coprimality is needed because both halves of Theorem 1's second equivalence are 2-adic):
  ES₅(n) ⟺ ∃ u ∈ 𝒞_n, ∃ v ≥ 1 with uv | n + u + v,  or  ∃ t ∈ 𝒞_n, ∃ d | tn + 1 with d ≡ −1 (mod t).
In the first case the word is (t, u, v) with t = (n+u+v)/(uv); in the second (t, (d+1)/t, ((tn+1)/d + 1)/t) (for t ≥ 3 the
cofactor is automatically ≡ −1 (mod t), and 𝒞_n ∌ 1, 2). Each case yields an explicit 4/n identity through the slot produced
in the proof of Theorem 1 (the five slot formulas of the PR-B note; e.g. u ≡ −n (8) gives 4 | v or 4 | A*, FOREST_DEEP_FABLE
Cor C). ∎

*Reading.* This is Prop 3.1's covering form (ES₅(p) ⟺ p mod A ∈ G_A = S_A ∩ Q_A, shadow ∩ colour) **with the colour half
removed**: for u ∈ 𝒞_p the classes −u − v (mod uv) are pure shadow classes, and for t ∈ 𝒞_p the classes −t̄ (mod d), d ≡ −1
(mod t), likewise. So for p ≡ 1 (mod 8),
  ES₅(p) ⟺ p ∈ ⋃_{u ∈ 4ℕ ∪ (8ℕ+7), gcd(u,v)=1} (−u−v mod uv) ∪ ⋃_{t ∈ 4ℕ ∪ (8ℕ+7), d ≡ −1 (t)} (−t̄ mod d),
a union of residue classes with **no character condition anywhere** (and the mirror statement with 3 in place of 7 for

### 1.3 T1 as six reciprocity statements (Corollary 1.2, PROVED)

Let m be odd. By Theorem E (FOREST_DEEP_FABLE) no word of m² has an endpoint ≡ 0 (4) or ≡ 7 (8), and by Lemma 4.3
(LOWERTAIL_DEEP_FABLE) no word of m² has level t ≡ 0 (4) or ≡ 7 (8). Since m² ≡ 1 (mod 8), 𝒞_{m²} = 4ℕ ∪ (8ℕ+7), so no word
of m² has a coordinate in 𝒞_{m²}, and Theorem 1 gives N₋(m²) = 0 (the five-slice T1 of DEDEKIND_ES / PARITY_AUDIT Lemma
3.2). Conversely, N₋(m²) = 0 together with Theorem 1 *is* the statement that all six coordinate lines are empty at m². So the
odd-square obstruction for ES₅ is **exactly** the six single-coordinate reciprocity facts, each of the form "an integer of
the shape m² + u (u ∈ 𝒞) or tm² + 1 (t ∈ 𝒞) has no divisor in the class −m² (mod u), resp. −1 (mod t)", proved by
(−u/A) = +1 for A | m² + u versus the forced value (−u/A) = −1 in that class. CERTIFIED: `z49_ltfable2_scan.c sq` on all
odd squares ≤ 10⁷ reports every line empty (N₋ = Nt = Ne = 0 at all 1 580 odd squares, no coprimality assumed).

*Consequence for screening line 1 (Assessment).* A proof of ES₅ via the covering of §1.2 must use that the integers
p + u (u ∈ 𝒞_p) or tp + 1 (t ∈ 𝒞_p) *do* have divisors in the tied classes — at squares they provably never do. The step
that is false at squares is the divisor-in-class existence itself, not any character bookkeeping.

### 2.1 The sign-aware free-word count (Theorem 2.1, PROVED)

### 2.2 No hidden cell identity (Theorem 2.3, PROVED by exact computation)

For a prime p ≡ 1 (4) and a cell c = (t, u, v) mod 8 let W_c(p) be the number of ordered words of p in c. The involution
u ↔ v gives W_{(t,a,b)} = W_{(t,b,a)}.

**Theorem 2.3.** Every linear relation Σ_c λ_c W_c(p) = 0 with constant coefficients λ_c ∈ ℚ, over the 160 admissible
cells, that holds for all primes p ≡ 1 (mod 4) is a linear combination of the involution relations; the same holds over
the 80 cells admissible for p ≡ 1 (8) [resp. p ≡ 5 (8)] for relations valid at all primes in that class.
*Proof.* The relation space is contained in the relation space over any finite set of primes. For the 12 980 primes
p ≡ 1 (4) < 3·10⁵ (`z49_ltfable2_scan.c cells`, `z49_ltfable2_rank.py`): 160 occupied cells, 84 involution orbits; the
exact rank of the 12 980 × 160 count matrix is 84 (computed modulo 2³¹ − 1, which can only decrease the rank, while the
involution bounds it above by 84). Hence the relation space has dimension 160 − 84 = 76, which is the number of

### 3.3 HC is Landau-type, H₄ is congruence-type (Theorem 3.2, PROVED rel. the sieve; Assessment for the contrast)

**Theorem 3.2.** #{p ≤ x prime, p ≡ 1 (mod 8), HC(p) > 4} ≪ x/(log x)².
*Proof.* By §3.1, HC(p) > 4 forces both p + 4 and 4p + 1 to be free of prime factors ≡ 3 (mod 4). Sift the integers
n ≤ x, n ≡ 1 (mod 8), by the set 𝒫 of primes consisting of all primes ℓ ≤ x^{1/3} (to isolate the primes n = p among them,
up to O(x^{1/3}) small n) and of the roots of (n+4)(4n+1) modulo the primes ℓ ≡ 3 (mod 4), ℓ ≠ 3, ℓ ≤ x^{1/3}: for such ℓ
the two roots −4 and −4⁻¹ are distinct (they coincide only for ℓ | 15). The sifting density is 1/ℓ at every prime plus
2/ℓ at the primes ≡ 3 (4) — dimension 1 + 1 = 2 — and the sequence of integers in a progression has level of distribution
x^{1−ε} trivially, so the elementary upper-bound sieve of dimension 2 (Halberstam–Richert, *Sieve Methods*, Thm 2.2, or
the fundamental lemma) gives ≪ x·∏_{ℓ ≤ x^{1/3}}(1 − 1/ℓ)·∏_{ℓ ≡ 3 (4), 3 < ℓ ≤ x^{1/3}}(1 − 2/ℓ) ≍ x/(log x)². ∎
(Alternatively: a dimension-1 sieve on the sequence {(p+4)(4p+1) : p ≤ x} with Bombieri–Vinogradov supplying the level
x^{1/2−ε} — in the divisor-weighted form Σ_{q ≤ x^{1/2−ε}} 3^{ω(q)} max_a |E(x; q, a)| ≪ x/(log x)^A, which follows from BV by
Cauchy–Schwarz — gives the same bound; the elementary route above needs no input on primes in progressions.)

So for every fixed X ≥ 4 the primes with HC(p) > X have relative density 0. The saving should grow with X (each further
line u ∈ 𝒞 or t ∈ 𝒞 below X adds a sifting condition), but only the X = 4 case is proved here; the product heuristic below
is speculative. **Contrast (Assessment, with the PROVED model facts of the z49 calibration):** H₄(p) ≥ X is a pure
congruence event (p avoids finitely many residue classes modulo the lcm of the moduli ≤ X²), so P(H₄ ≥ X) tends to a
positive constant δ(X) as p → ∞, and the prime-Haar CRT model predicts −log δ(X) ≍ (log X)², i.e. H₄ > (log p)^C i.o. for
every C. HC(p) > X is instead the event that finitely many shifted integers p + u, tp + 1 (one coordinate bounded, the
other free) each avoid a positive-density set of primes in their factorisation — a Landau-type event whose probability
tends to 0 as p → ∞ (PROVED for X = 4). This is the structural reason the z49 calibration kills two-endpoint
(polylog-window) targets but says nothing against one-coordinate ones. No extreme-value estimate for HC is proved.

*What Theorem 3.2 does not give (PROVED limits).* Nothing about any single prime: the exceptional set is non-empty at every
scale ≤ 10⁸ (§3.2), and the campaign's exceptional-set bound E(N) ≪ N exp(−c(log N)^{3/4}) (FRONTIER §5.1) is far stronger
on average. The value of Theorem 3.2 is only the classification of HC as Landau-type, which licenses Conjecture 3.4 as a
target compatible with the calibration.

### 3.4 Shadow primes: HC is unbounded (Proposition 3.3, CONDITIONAL on Dickson; CERTIFIED instances)

**Proposition 3.3.** Assume Dickson's conjecture. (a) For every X ≥ 4 there are infinitely many primes p ≡ 1 (mod 24)
with HC(p) > X. (b) For every X ≥ 4 and K ≥ 1 there are infinitely many primes p ≡ 1 (mod 24) such that every word of p
with a coordinate ≤ X is free (t ≤ 2 or min(u, v) ≤ 2) and monochromatic, while p has at least K such words; in
particular HC(p) > X and every finite union of coordinate lines with a bounded coordinate is empty of certificates.

*Proof of (a).* Let 𝒞_X = {x ∈ [4, X] : x ≡ 0 (4) or x ≡ 7 (8)}, Q = lcm(24, x(x+1) : x ∈ 𝒞_X), and consider the linear forms
  p(s) = 1 + Qs,  F_x(s) = 1 + Qs/(x+1) (so p + x = (x+1)F_x),  G_x(s) = 1 + xQs/(x+1) (so xp + 1 = (x+1)G_x),  x ∈ 𝒞_X.
All coefficients are positive integers, all forms equal 1 at s = 0 (so the family is admissible), and the slopes are
pairwise distinct (Q/(x+1) = yQ/(y+1) would need xy = 1). Dickson gives infinitely many s with all forms prime. For such p:
p ≡ 1 (mod 8), so 𝒞_p ∩ [1, X] = 𝒞_X. Endpoints x ∈ 𝒞_X: the divisors of p + x = (x+1)F_x are e and eF_x with e | x + 1
(F_x is a prime > x + 1), and F_x ≡ 1 (mod x) because x(x+1) | Q; a word with endpoint x needs a divisor v of p + x with
v ≡ −p ≡ −1 (mod x); e ≡ −1 (mod x) with e | x + 1 forces e ≥ 2x − 1 > x + 1 or e = x − 1 | x + 1 (x ≤ 3): none for x ≥ 4.
Levels x ∈ 𝒞_X: the divisors of xp + 1 = (x+1)G_x are e, eG_x with G_x ≡ 1 (mod x); a word at level x needs a divisor ≡ −1
(mod x), excluded in the same way. Hence no word has a coordinate in 𝒞_X, i.e. HC(p) > X. ∎

*Proof of (b).* Let ℓ₁ < … < ℓ_k be primes ≡ 1 (mod 4) with ℓ₁ > X + 1 and k = ⌈log₂ K⌉, L = ∏ℓ_i; let Q₀ = lcm(24, u(u+1)
(2 ≤ u ≤ X), all primes ≤ 2X + 2), Q₁ = Q₀·rad(Q₀), M = Q₁L², and let p₀ be the residue mod M with p₀ ≡ 1 (mod Q₁),
p₀ ≡ L − 1 (mod L²). Consider the 2X − 1 linear forms in s (all with integer coefficients, as the stated congruences
ensure):
  p = p₀ + Ms;  F₁ = (p₀+1)/(2L) + (M/2L)s  [p + 1 = 2L·F₁];  F_u = (p₀+u)/(u+1) + (M/(u+1))s, 2 ≤ u ≤ X  [p + u = (u+1)F_u];
  G_t = (tp₀+1)/(t+1) + (tM/(t+1))s, 3 ≤ t ≤ X  [tp + 1 = (t+1)G_t];  G₁ = (2p₀+1)/3 + (2M/3)s  [2p + 1 = 3G₁].
*Admissibility.* For a prime ℓ | Q₀ every form is constant mod ℓ, with constant ≡ 1 (p, F_u, G_t, G₁: e.g. F_u(0) =
1 + (Q₁/(u+1))·m with ℓ | Q₁/(u+1) = (Q₀/(u+1))·rad(Q₀)) or ≡ L⁻¹ (F₁(0) = (1 + (Q₁/2)m)/L), all non-zero. For ℓ = ℓ_i the
constants are p₀ ≡ −1, F_u(0) ≡ (u−1)/(u+1), G_t(0) ≡ (1−t)/(t+1), G₁(0) ≡ −1/3, F₁(0) = ((p₀+1)/L)/2 ≡ 1/2, all non-zero
because ℓ_i > X + 1. For ℓ ∤ Q₀L all slopes are non-zero mod ℓ, each form has one root, and ℓ > 2X + 2 > 2X − 1 = number of
forms, so some s is a non-root of all. *Distinct slopes:* M/(u+1), M/(2L), tM/(t+1), 2M/3, M are pairwise distinct
(u + 1 = 2L is impossible as 2L > X + 1; t + 1 = t(u+1), 3t = 2t + 2, 4L = 3, etc. have no solutions in range).
Dickson gives infinitely many s with all forms prime (and > every fixed bound). For such p, p ≡ 1 (mod 24) and p ≡ 1 (mod 8).
(i) *No word has an endpoint in [4, X].* As in (a) with F_u ≡ 1 (mod u) (p ≡ 1 (mod u(u+1))).
(ii) *The words with endpoint 3 are free.* p + 3 = 4F₃, F₃ ≡ 1 (mod 3); the divisors ≡ −p ≡ 2 (mod 3) are 2 and 2F₃,
giving (t, 3, 2) and (1, 3, (p+3)/2).
(iii) *No non-free word has level t ∈ [3, X].* tp + 1 = (t+1)G_t with G_t ≡ 1 (mod t); the divisors ≡ −1 (mod t) are
e | t + 1 with e ≡ −1 (mod t) — only e = 2 at t = 3 — and eG_t ≡ e; so levels 4..X are empty and level 3 carries only
d = 2 and d = 2G₃, i.e. (3, 1, (p+1)/2) and its mirror.
(iv) *Every free word is monochromatic.* u = 1: (t, 1, v) with v | p + 1 = 2L·F₁. Every odd prime factor of p + 1 is ≡ 1
(mod 4) (ℓ_i ≡ 1 (4); F₁ ≡ 1 (4) because (p+1)/2 = L·F₁ ≡ 1 (mod 4) for p ≡ 1 (8) and L ≡ 1 (4)), so every odd divisor is
≡ 1 or 5 (mod 8) and every even divisor is ≡ 2 (mod 8): v ∉ 𝒞_p; and (p+1)/v is odd ≡ 1 (4) or ≡ 2 (8), so t = (p+1)/v + 1
≡ 2 (4) or ≡ 3 (8): t ∉ 𝒞_p. u = 2: (t, 2, v) with v | p + 2 = 3F₂, F₂ ≡ 1 (mod 8) (p ≡ 1 (24)): v ∈ {1, 3, F₂, 3F₂} is

(2u−1)(2v−1) = 2p + 1 = 3G₁: {u, v} ⊆ {1, 2, F₂, p + 1} with F₂ ≡ 1 (8), p + 1 ≡ 2 (8). Mirrors likewise.
(v) *Count.* p + 1 = 2·ℓ₁⋯ℓ_k·F₁ with F₁ a prime distinct from the ℓ_i (F₁ > L for large s) has 2^{k+2} divisors, giving
2^{k+2} ≥ 4K words (t, 1, v).
Now a word with a coordinate ≤ X is free: if t ≤ 2 or min(u, v) ≤ 2 by definition; if min(u, v) = 3 by (ii); if
min(u, v) ∈ [4, X] it does not exist by (i); if t ∈ [3, X] by (iii). By (iv) it is monochromatic, and by (v) there are ≥ K
such words. In particular no certificate has a coordinate ≤ X, so HC(p) > X. ∎

## 5. Using M_gen (work item 3)

### 5.1 The exact statement that closes (∗) (PROVED bookkeeping)

With q = 4uB, a = −(u+B), G = (ℤ/q)^×, over the ordered pairs 𝒫(p) = {(u, B) : gcd(u, B) = 1, u + B odd, 4uB − u − B ≤ p}:
  R(p) = Σ_𝒫 1[p ≡ a (q)],  M_gen(p) = Σ_𝒫 (s(q)/φ(q))·1[pa⁻¹ ∈ G²],  R = M_gen + X_nr  (LOWERTAIL_DEEP_ASTRA (1)).
Two readings, both exact:
* **Twisted words.** 1[pa⁻¹ ∈ G²]·s(q) = #{x ∈ G : p ≡ −(u+B)x² (mod q)}, so
  M_gen(p) = Σ_𝒫 (1/φ(q))·#{x ∈ (ℤ/4uB)^× : p ≡ −(u+B)x² (mod 4uB)}:
  the φ-normalised number of pairs (u, B) with a *square twist* x for which 4uB | p + (u+B)x². R(p) is the x = 1 slice.
* **Genus-admissible pairs.** 𝒫_gen(p) := {(u, B) ∈ 𝒫 : pa⁻¹ ∈ G²}; then
  X_nr(p) = Σ_{𝒫_gen(p)} (1[p ≡ a (q)] − 1/|G²_q|),
  because for p outside the genus both the full and the real character sums vanish. The genus condition is: p ≡ a (mod 4)
  (uB odd) or (mod 8) (uB even) — a 2-adic shape condition — together with (p/ℓ) = (−B/ℓ) for ℓ | u and (p/ℓ) = (−u/ℓ)
  for ℓ | B, ℓ odd — a quadratic condition at every odd prime of uB.

**(G_ε).** R(p) ≥ ε M_gen(p) ⟺ Σ_{𝒫_gen(p)} 1[p ≡ a (q)] ≥ ε Σ_{𝒫_gen(p)} 1/|G²_q|: *among the pairs whose genus admits p,
the element y(u,B) := −p(u+B)⁻¹ ∈ G²_q equals the identity at ≥ ε times its a-priori rate.* This is B2 with the whole
real-character layer (colour = BM character, plus the other genus characters) removed: the remaining condition is the
class of p inside the coset −(u+B)G² of size φ(q)/s(q), exactly one class of which is the word. Under H_θ (Astra Thm 5.4)
the right side is ≥ ε(2/π⁴ − o(1)) log²p, so (G_ε) ⟹ R(p) → ∞ ⟹ ES for large p.

### 5.2 Shape equidistribution versus (G_ε) (PROVED implications, Assessment for the rest)

Let (SE_κ) be sign-free shape equidistribution at p: every admissible cell c of (t, u, v) mod 8 satisfies W_c(p) ≥ κ W(p)
(κ > 0 fixed; the cells have positive local densities, so this is the weakest form of "no cell is starved").
* (SE_κ) ⟹ (★)-eventual and ES₅: every admissible cell with t ≡ 0 (mod 4) is a certificate cell, so R(p) ≥ κ W(p) ≥ 15κ > 0.
  PROVED; no sign-free tail is used.
* (SE_κ) ∧ (W_log: W(p) ≥ c log²p) ⟹ (∗)_BM(ε) (PROVED, §4) and ⟹ (G_ε) provided M_gen(p) ≪ log²p (an upper bound not
  proved unconditionally; M_gen ≤ Σ_𝒫 s(q)/φ(q) ≪ log⁴p trivially; EVIDENCE M_gen ≈ M_BM, §5.3).
* (SE_κ) alone is not shown to imply (G_ε) (or (∗)_BM): (G_ε) requires R(p) ≫ log²p while (SE_κ) bounds R only by κW(p),

  I3_log: any admissible cell whose three residues avoid 1 and 2 mod 8 contains only interior words, so I3 ≥ κW. This is

* *In divisor language,* the statement "the remainder is not too negative" is: the genus-admissible pairs (u, B), each
  carrying its square twist x(u,B) with 4uB | p + (u+B)x², have x ≡ ±1-type (x² ≡ 1 mod 4uB) at a positive proportion of the
  weighted count. Shape equidistribution speaks about actual words only (the x² = 1 pairs) and about their 2-adic residues;
  the twists x live at the odd primes of uB. The two statements are about disjoint data; the only bridge is the size of

* *Calibration (heuristic, parent's z49 data).* Restricting (G_ε) to the pairs with modulus 4uB ≤ Y = (log p)^C gives a
