# Audit comment: prescribe the field at the distinguished prime

**Accept, as a replacement for the flag, not in addition to it.** Relative to the joint PID tree and its stage lemmas, I find no countability, Gaussian, order, or local-lifting obstruction to specifying `R/PR ≅ k` for any countable field `k` of characteristic zero and infinite transcendence degree containing `i`. Not every such `k` has a quadratic nonresidue (take `k` algebraically closed). The pseudofinite choice below does. Here are the checks on the new step, rather than a second construction of the full model.

* **Induction data.** At a node retain a field embedding `κ=Frac(A/PA)→k` extending the parent's; at the root `κ=ℚ(i)(X)` with `Y/X` a square root of `−1`. Schedule a fixed enumeration of the whole of `k` independently on every path; admission is by the **current** image. Ordinary edges mod `P` induce rational extensions of `κ`: a row has a surviving pivot (need not be the ordinary-prime chart pivot); a progression/floor adds a free coordinate; NR has the chart `D≠0` even if its target vanishes. For TS use `UV=s̄` over `κ`: `s̄≠0` since a target associated to `P` is already Gaussian-split and cannot be an inert-circle task. Four squares give `UV+WT=s̄`, rational on `U≠0` even when `s̄=0`. These are **function-field** assertions, not claims about all affine points. Each finite node has finite transcendence degree, so any required finite number of independent new parameters can be chosen in `k`; incompatible siblings need not share their new choices. In the transcendental prescription step choose the residue of the new free variable to map to the requested element itself.
* **Integral polynomial, not just cleared coefficients.** For `α` algebraic over the current image but not in it, let `g` be its monic minimal polynomial of degree `d`. Take a common nonzero denominator `b∈A/PA` of the coefficients. Then `b^d g(T/b)` is monic over `A/PA`, irreducible over `κ`, and has root `bα` (identifying elements with their images). Lift coefficients to a **monic** `f∈A[T]` and form `B=A[V,H]/(PH−f(V))`. The fraction field over `P` becomes `κ(bα)(H)`, not just `κ(α)`; send `H` to a new transcendental over `κ(α)` in `k`. Recovery of `α` in the field suffices. Merely multiplying `g` by a denominator without scaling the variable loses the monic argument.
* **Old primes and factoriality.** Over `A[H]`, the relation is monic in `V`, giving a free module with basis `1,…,V^(d−1)`. After reducing mod `P` it gives a domain by monicity and irreducibility over `κ`. Write `C=A[i]` and `P=λ λ̄` up to scalar (`λ=X+iY` initially). In the two retained prime quotients of `C` over `P`, the fraction fields are **both `κ`**: localize `(A/P)[i]` to `κ[i]=κ×κ` here meaning `κ[T]/(T²+1)`. The maps on `κ` are the identity; they differ on the adjoined `i`. Thus the same monic residue polynomial is irreducible on **both** components. For any other old prime on either side, the image of `P` is nonzero, and monicity makes localization at `P` injective on the quotient; the localized quotient is a polynomial chart, hence the original quotient a nonzero domain. Inverting `P` gives `A[1/P][V]` or `C[1/P][V]`; Nagata at the retained primes over `P` gives both UFDs. Units in the chart are old localized units; valuations at the retained primes exclude new units in the unlocalized algebra. Rational primes persist, with the usual exception **only upstairs at a different, targeted TS edge**.
* **Order, ES, and ordinary places.** Write an element uniquely as `Σ_{j<d} V^j g_j(H)`. With `H=f(V)/P`, the degrees of its nonzero blocks in `F[V]` are `j+d deg(g_j)`, distinct mod `d`; hence `B∩F=A`. Taking `V` above the old ordered field keeps bounded elements constant. Whole-line ES rigidity applies on this polynomial generic fiber, then intersection puts the triple back in `A`. At a finite good prefix use the `P≠0` chart (already a root condition at ordinary primes), with one uniform free coordinate and atom bound `1/q`. At an exceptional tail prime any transcendental integral `V` works for a **field** embedding; `H` is integral if the coefficients evaluate integrally, but need not be everywhere integral for an arbitrary lifted `f`. No such condition is required. At a parity-only step `f(V)=V²−a` with `a∈R`, `H` is indeed integral everywhere. The new edges are deterministic choices at each finite history of the countable tree and preserve the earlier proof of parsimony, Dirichlet, TS, NR and Lag.
* **No root-lifting gap at the end.** For the union, `A∞=S⁻¹R`, `S=ℤ\{0}`. `R/PR` is a field (`R` PID, `P` prime); every `s∈S` maps to a unit since `P` is nonstandard (also `P` cannot divide any nonzero rational constant in `A∞`). Therefore `R/PR → A∞/PA∞ = S⁻¹(R/PR)` is an isomorphism. This does **not** assert that the finite-stage residue rings are fields. The final field is the union of the compatible finite-stage residue **fraction fields**, since a fraction uses finitely many coefficients. The fair target list makes its image all of `k`. A residue-field root or nonresidue has a representative in `R`, even a least nonnegative representative `<P` by the integer-part division property. For the original parity variant, extend the `(X−1)`-adic valuation on `ℚ(i)(X)` as a **field valuation**: Gauss on rational parameters and an unramified extension on adjoining a root of even value (divide by an even power of the uniformizer before extracting the root; residue characteristic zero). The value group stays `ℤ`; the uniformizer remains a nonsquare while servicing every even class gives index two. **Do not require a divisor centered on every stage:** the Bézout row on `P,X−1` makes `X−1` invertible mod `P`.

**Pseudofinite instance.** Take an increasing tower of finite Galois number fields exhausting `ℚbar`. For each level `j` choose a distinct increasing prime `p_j` splitting completely in its field **and admitting ES**. The first condition has positive prime density for each fixed level by Chebotarev; for the second use Vaughan's all-integer exceptional bound `O(x exp(−c(log x)^(2/3)))=o(π(x))` (ET introduction, `sources/lit2026/audit-elsholtz-tao-1107.1010.txt`, lines 98–105). Merely `o(x)` all-integer exceptions would **not** suffice. No need for uniform Chebotarev in `j`. In `L=∏𝔽_{p_j}/𝒰` for a nonprincipal ultrafilter, every irreducible polynomial over `ℚ` splits completely, by Łoś and eventual complete splitting. Thus the subfield of elements algebraic over `ℚ` is a copy of `ℚbar`. `L` is uncountable (encode longer and longer binary prefixes since `p_j→∞`), hence has a countably infinite algebraically independent set. Downward Löwenheim–Skolem in the countable pure ring language gives a countable `k≺L` containing that set and the algebraic part. It has `i`, two nonzero square classes, and **every standard nonzero rational is a square**. This is a residue-field QNR at `P`, with a nonstandard representative, not a standard nonresidue. It does not establish reciprocity for pairs of arbitrary primes of `R`.

**Separation.** Do not simultaneously demand the earlier `ℤ`-valued parity flag in this pseudofinite `k`. The pure-field sentence `∀t≠0 ∃(x,y,z)≠0: x³+t y³+t²z³=0` holds in `L` and `k`: away from characteristic 3 this is a smooth projective plane cubic and Hasse's bound gives a point. A discrete valuation with uniformizer `t` would make the nonzero term valuations distinct mod 3, impossible. More generally pure-field elementary equivalence transfers fixed-degree ring formulas, not a distinguished order/height on representatives, an internal exponent `(P−1)/2`, coded point counts up to `P`, or positive integer ES triples at the ambient modulus. The fact that each `p_j` admits ES does not contradict `¬ES(P)` in `R`.

**Literature scope.** Macintyre–Marker (APAL 43 (1989), 57–77; statement (D) as sourced in `data/z66/ie1/lit_ie1.md` §4) already allow fields of characteristic zero and infinite transcendence degree at an infinite prime of open induction. Jeřábek–Kołodziejczyk, *Real closures of models of weak arithmetic*, Thm 4.1 (building on Smith, J. Algebra 159 (1993), Thm 10.7), construct an unbounded countable PID model of IOpen, not a requirement that its prime residue fields be finite fields or carry an internal cardinality theory. Smith's distinct NDJFL 1993 Thm 3.11 is a **one-direction** reciprocity implication in `IE₁` with one standard prime; a square profile at standard places and every rational constant square in this residue field are compatible with the signed implication at `P≡1 mod 4`. This construction does not give `IE₁`. The joint axioms and the ES failure, not general residue-field flexibility in bare open induction, are the added constraints.
