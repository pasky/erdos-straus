**Verdict.** The ordinary-place induction works, with no rationality assertion about the iterated norm fibers. It works for *each* later eligible circle, not just for the norm plane at initialization. There are two qualifications: at 2 a unit norm target must be 1 mod 4, not merely odd; and field embeddings use **one** transcendental circle parameter, not two independent coordinates. The split TS case also works, but needs all-place unit values and restricted integrality including the sparse places. This note does not establish the separate sign/survival law on the sparse places.

Write `H=P−1`, let O be the infinite set of ordinary primes, and keep the old maps fixed when extending a stage. Assume `π_q≡1 mod q` for q in O. Use local embeddings of the old fraction field or, where only ring maps are needed, embeddings of the old image field. For the requested field embeddings at initialization, the fixed `π_q` must be transcendental over ℚ. The induction invariant on a stage A is:

1. `C=A/HA` is geometrically integral;
2. each fixed element of A is integral at almost every ordinary place;
3. for each `a∈A` with nonzero image in C, a has unit value at almost every ordinary place.

These are elementwise assertions. For a finite list of coefficients there is a common finite exceptional set. The additional all-place restricted integrality needed for the global assertions below is an input from combining the ordinary and sparse constructions; a finite exception in O alone says nothing about infinitely many sparse places.

## The norm step

Suppose `s∈A` has all-place unit values and `s≡1 mod 4` in the pullback, and put `B=A[x,y]/(x²+y²−s)`. Then `s̄≠0` in C: if `s=Ht` with `t∈A`, then at almost every ordinary place H reduces to zero and t is integral. This proof needs O infinite and does not rely on `s` having a nonconstant image in C. Nor need `s̄` be everywhere invertible on Spec C.

`B/HB=C[y]⊕x C[y]` as a module (monic division). After any algebraically closed scalar extension of ℚ the old base remains a domain; the quotient injects into its corresponding generic conic by the same free normal form. Over the fraction field that conic is geometrically integral since the scalar `s̄` is nonzero: over its algebraic closure use `(x+iy)(x−iy)=s̄`. This proves geometric integrality of the **whole** new H-fiber even if special subfibers at zeros of `s̄` degenerate. It does not prove smoothness everywhere or existence of a rational section. Flatness over the old ring follows from the displayed monic basis, also before reduction by H.

For residue tests enumerate all `g∈B` nonzero modulo H, each represented uniquely by `g=G(y)+xJ(y)` with old coefficients. At least one coefficient is nonzero in C. For a finite initial list, all coefficients are integral, and for each g at least one coefficient is a unit, away from a finite subset of O by the old invariant. Thus on `x²+y²=s_q mod q` every test has a nonzero normal form as a regular function; no choice of rational conic chart is needed. For odd q and `b=s_q mod q≠0` this affine conic has `q−χ_q(−1)` rational points, by the split factorization or the finite-field norm map. A test of total degree d cuts out at most `2d` geometric points: apply plane Bézout to the geometrically irreducible conic, which is not a component of the test's zero set. A nonzero constant excludes none. Choose increasing thresholds for the initial lists so that `q−1>2Σ d` and the coefficient tests hold. At each q choose a residue point avoiding the longest list its threshold permits. Then *every fixed* g nonzero modulo H has unit value almost everywhere in O. All new elements are integral almost everywhere because the coordinates are integral and the old coefficients are integral almost everywhere. The old choices did not anticipate the identities of these tests: their only job was to preserve all nonzero old coefficient tests eventually. This is why the construction iterates through adaptive stages.

**Exact integral lifts.** At an odd q, including one too small for any avoidance list, take an arbitrary allowed residue point (any point if the list is empty). One of `2x,2y` is a unit. If it is `2x`, choose `y` in its cylinder transcendental over the countable old local image field; Hensel's lemma gives `x` in its cylinder with `x²=s_q−y²`. Swap roles if needed. This embeds the generic circle field over the old field: `s−Y²` has two distinct simple roots geometrically, so is not a square in the rational function field. These coordinates have transcendence degree **one over the old field**. No arbitrary pair of independent lifts, and no preexisting rational point over the old field, is claimed.

At 2 use an empty avoidance list. From `s≡1 mod 4` in the pullback one has `s_2∈1+4ℤ₂`. If `s_2≡1 mod 8`, choose transcendental `y∈4ℤ₂`; if `s_2≡5 mod 8`, choose it in `2+4ℤ₂`. In both cases `s_2−y²∈1+8ℤ₂` has an odd square root x. Conversely an odd 2-adic sum of two integral squares must be 1 mod 4. Thus excluding just the nonunits would not suffice; and using only the first cylinder fails already at `s_2=5`. Any further compatible square condition on the *old* mark is unchanged since its exact value is fixed; imposing an independent square condition on these new coordinates is not part of this proof.

Initialization at `A=ℚ[X,Y]`, `P=X²+Y²` is this same argument over `ℚ[P]` with target s=P and `P_q=π_q` fixed (take `π₂≡1 mod 4`). It gives integral X,Y at every prime and the generic reduction invariant on `X²+Y²=1` at ordinary primes. For a concrete danger, assigning `(X,Y)≡(1,0)` almost everywhere satisfies the norm condition but fails the nonzero test Y on that very fiber; the later row `(H,Y)` could then have no integral coefficients almost everywhere.

## Interleaving the other steps

At a free polynomial step use the coefficient tests for each polynomial nonzero modulo H. A finite list excludes at most the sum of its degrees in residues at sufficiently large ordinary primes. For a Dirichlet output `a+mY`, choose Y integral everywhere, and whenever the two old inputs are integral with a unit entry require the output to be a unit. This excludes at most one further residue, and is mandatory even at small primes with an empty list. For a row `aU+bV=1` use a pivot outside H (coprimality in the old UFD); it is a unit almost everywhere in O. The other entry is integral almost everywhere. On this chart coefficients for a finite list of tests are old elements divided by powers of the pivot, and a nonzero test in the new H-fiber has nonzero numerator polynomial there. Indeed the reduced row is covered by its nonempty polynomial charts over the old geometric domain; they have dense common open if both survive. Polynomial avoidance in the free variable preserves the invariant on the row stage. Where the pivot fails, solve over the local field with any nonzero pivot; one can take an integral row solution when the smaller input valuation is ≤0, but not when both are positive. These are finitely many exceptions **per row in O**, not a license for infinitely many negative valuations over the sparse set. A circle task, unlike a general row task, really does provide its two integral coordinates at *every* prime whenever the old hypotheses hold.

## Which primes must be serviced?

For this paragraph assume the compatible global evaluations are integral almost everywhere on each fixed element at **all** primes, and write `R_j={x∈A_j : x_q∈ℤ_q ∀q}`. Assume the usual factorial stage invariants and units `A_j^×=ℚ^×`, with bounded ring elements rational. Positive finite primes of `R_j` are the usual standard primes; each q is prime by `R_j/qR_j=𝔽_q`. For a positive infinite `r∈R_j` one has the finite-stage equivalences, *without* assuming Bézout for `R_j`:

```
r irreducible in R_j  ⇔  r prime in R_j
                     ⇔  r prime in A_j and v_q(r)=0 at every mark.
```

If a mark is not a unit, `r=q(r/q)` in `R_j` and both factors are nonunits. If r has all unit values but factors `r=ab` with nonunits in `A_j`, then each of a,b has valuation zero almost everywhere: their product is a unit and both are integral almost everywhere. They are nonzero locally at every q. A rational `c` can thus normalize `ca,b/c` simultaneously to local units everywhere. Both are still nonunits even in `A_j`, contradicting irreducibility. Conversely, if r is prime in `A_j` with all values units, division by it in `A_j` preserves local integrality; it descends as a prime element. This criterion uses the all-place hypotheses even if the proposed prime is split over `A_j[i]`. There is no assumption that every `A_j`-prime admits normalization; a prime divisible at infinitely many marks need not do so.

Rational old prime elements remain prime under the circle steps: for an old prime t not associate to s, the quotient is the conic of a nonzero scalar over `A_j/t`, a domain by monicity and generic integrality. For the target itself, `B/(s)` has equation `x²+y²=0` over `A_j/s`; it is a domain precisely here because the target is inert, i.e. `−1` has no root in `Frac(A_j/s)`. Over the Gaussian ring the target does split; this is intentional. Other Gaussian primes away from s survive by the `uv=s̄≠0` argument. In particular no rational prime is lost even at a later different inert target. To see the relevant UFD hypotheses at a circle step, over `A_j[i]` one has `uv=s`; u is prime (inert target) and inverting u gives a factorial Laurent ring. Its units before inverting u are just the old Gaussian units: any such unit becomes `c u^n` after localization with c an old unit, and its divisor at u forces n=0. Rational factoriality descends: a height-one prime divisor of the real ring pulls back under the finite étale Gaussian extension to an invariant Weil divisor, principal there; the generator and its conjugate differ by a Gaussian constant unit. Hilbert 90 over `ℚ(i)/ℚ` makes a constant multiple invariant. Pullback of Weil divisors is injective; the real ring is normal by descent (or by intersection of the Gaussian normal ring and the real fraction field). Thus it is principal downstairs. Rational units remain rational constants. These arguments do not require the affine circle stage to be globally smooth.

A final positive infinite irreducible in R is already irreducible at any stage containing it: an earlier factorization by nonunits persists (the only pullback units are ±1). At such a stage its local values and its residue modulo 4 are already fixed; since `(r−1)/4∈A_j`, `r≡1 mod 4` in the final ring is also true in `R_j`. One may therefore dovetail all **actual elements**, rechecking at service time for positivity, local units at every mark, congruence, and rational/Gaussian primality. It is not enough to use one arbitrarily chosen scalar associate. This is an external construction with all old local maps fixed, not a computable primality test or a prediction about future evaluations. If some sparse local maps have *not* been fixed on the old ring, an independent argument is needed to justify such a test; declaring a future place ordinary and then changing its old values need not preserve eligibility.

## A split prime and its integral norm coordinates

Let a positive all-place-unit rational-stage prime r split in the Gaussian UFD. Then `r=c w w̄`, `c∈ℚ_{>0}`, `w∈A_j[i]`. Indeed `(A_j/r)[i]` is finite étale of rank two and injects into the generic quadratic algebra. In the split case it is reduced with two conjugate generic components; factoriality and reduction give multiplicity one on each. The scalar is real since it is fixed by conjugation, positive since r and `w w̄` are positive. The norm is not assumed already normalized.

For every standard prime `q≡3 mod 4`, `w_q∈ℚ_q(i)^×` has norm of even q-valuation (unramified inert quadratic extension). Since `r_q` is a unit, `v_q(c)` is even. Hence c is a **rational Gaussian norm**: factor the positive rational c, using the Gaussian factor at 2, one factor at each split prime, and rational powers of inert primes for their even exponents (negative exponents allowed). Replace w by a Gaussian rational multiple to get `N(w)=r` exactly. At inert q and at 2, unit norm makes w a unit in the local integer ring `ℤ_q[i]` (also at the ramified prime 2), so its two coordinates are integral. At a split q choose an ordering of the two Gaussian places; if their valuations on w are n and −n, choose a Gaussian prime `λ_q` above q with valuations `(1,0)` in that order. Multiplication by `(λ_q/λ̄_q)^(−n)` cancels the two valuations and has norm one. Only finitely many n are nonzero: the real and imaginary parts of w are integral almost everywhere globally, while the norm is a unit. Each correction is a Gaussian unit at all other rational primes. Their finite product produces `w'∈A_j[i]` with `N(w')=r` and both coordinates integral everywhere (at odd split q, `ℤ_q[i]=ℤ_q×ℤ_q`). Thus they belong to `R_j`, no rescaling of **r** is necessary, and the representation is permanent. This is why a split TS target needs no new circle.

For example the norm-one factor `(2+i)/(2−i)=(3+4i)/5` has nonintegral coordinates at 5 despite its unit norm. And `c=3` is positive but not a rational Gaussian norm: all-place unit hypotheses at inert primes, not positivity alone, rule that scalar out. These two tests detect different errors. For standard positive primes `≡1 mod 4` the ordinary integer sum-of-two-squares theorem suffices.

**Boundary.** This gives the ordinary-place and TS bookkeeping under the stated hypotheses. For a joint universal NR conclusion the sparse schedule must separately prove that its old restrictions, all-place unit tests, and near-everywhere integrality are simultaneously available while delivering the required signs and the uncovered P-profile. Nothing in ordinary-place genericity forces a sparse nonresidue sign.
