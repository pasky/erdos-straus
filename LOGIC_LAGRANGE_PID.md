# Four-square adjunction and the square-marked PID countermodel

**Verdict.** There is a countable nonstandard discretely ordered **external PID** satisfying IOpen, Lagrange's four-square axiom, universal TS, universal NR and internal Dirichlet, with an infinite prime `P≡1 mod 840` failing ES. Here TS and NR are the sentences for primes `≡1 mod 4` in `Z65_LOGIC2.md` §1. The NR moduli need not be standard. Use the square-marked PID construction, **not** the sparse-prime non-PID construction. The new step neither specializes the tower at an integer nor makes the order bounded by an Archimedean landing.

One correction to “every old positive `s`”: adjoin a generic four-square witness for every **positive infinite** `s` of the rational stage, with no test for membership in `R`. For bounded `s` do not adjoin the sphere. Already `Σx_j²=1` forces four bounded coordinates in every ordering, destroying the bounded-elements invariant for a generic adjunction. Bounded final `R`-elements are ordinary integers and have their classical representations. Even in the rational algebra a positive rational `a/b` already has a rational representation, by dividing a four-square representation of `ab` by `b²` in the sum-of-squares identity.

## Four-square stage lemma

Let `A` be a geometrically integral finite type ℚ-algebra with ordered fraction field `F`, with `A, A[i]` UFDs having only scalar units, and with the standard-bounded elements of `A` exactly ℚ. Fix `s∈A` positive infinite. Put

    B=A[x₁,x₂,x₃,x₄]/(x₁²+x₂²+x₃²+x₄²−s).

There is no assumption that `s` is squarefree or even irreducible.

**Algebra.** The normal form `a(x₁,x₂,x₃)+x₄b(x₁,x₂,x₃)` is unique by monicity. In particular `B∩F=A` after passage to the generic fiber. Over any characteristic-zero coefficient field, the quadratic of rank four in the leading variables cannot factor into linears even geometrically: the homogeneous quadratic part of a product of affine linear polynomials has rank at most two. This applies with **any** right-hand side, including zero. By the normal form the quotient embeds in its generic-field version over any coefficient domain. Consequently `B` is geometrically integral, and for every old prime `r`, `B/rB` is a nonzero domain, even if `r|s`. This argument also works for old Gaussian primes. Global smoothness is neither needed nor in general true (take `A=ℚ[t]`, `s=t²`, at the origin).

Let `C=A[i]`, `U=x₁+ix₂`, `V=x₁−ix₂`, `h=s−x₃²−x₄²`. Then `B[i]=C[x₃,x₄,U,V]/(UV−h)` embeds in `C[x₃,x₄,U,U⁻¹]` by `V=h/U` (reduce mixed monomials first). `h` is prime in `C[x₃,x₄]`: over the algebraic closure of `Frac(C)` a linear change makes its zero set `ab=s≠0`, integral; monicity up to sign in `x₄` gives the injection from the unlocalized quotient. Thus `U` is nonunit prime in `B[i]`, and inverting it gives a factorial ring. Nagata proves factoriality upstairs. A unit there viewed in the Laurent chart is `c U^j` with `c∈ℚ(i)*`; its `U`-valuation is zero, hence `j=0`. For descent, `B=B[i]^conj` is normal. The pullback of a height-one prime divisor to the finite étale Gaussian extension is principal; a defining function is carried under conjugation to a scalar multiple of itself since the units are constants. Hilbert 90 on `ℚ(i)*` rescales it to a fixed function. Divisor pullback is injective by lying over and the valuation formula, so the original divisor is principal. Thus `B` too is factorial and has scalar units. No bogus descent of arbitrary UFDs under field restriction is being used.

**The order.** In `F^rc` put `r=√s`. Choose three bounded ordered parameters `t₁,t₂,t₃` with residues algebraically independent over the natural residue field `k` of `F^rc`, each between `1/8` and `1/4`. At a countable stage one can choose three successive real transcendental cuts in these intervals and extend the natural valuation by the residue-indeterminate construction. Set `x_j=rt_j` for `j≤3` and `x₄=r√(1−Σt_j²)` in the real closure. To check injection and boundedness, write a nonzero normal form as `Σa_e x^e` with `e₄≤1`. Divide its evaluated expression by `M=max_e |a_e r^{|e|}|` over the nonzero coefficients. The residues of the normalized coefficients lie in **the old** `k`, and at least one survives. The resulting residue is

    f(ζ₁,ζ₂,ζ₃) + √(1−Σζ_j²) g(ζ₁,ζ₂,ζ₃),    ζ_j=res(t_j),

where `f,g∈k[Z₁,Z₂,Z₃]` are not both zero. This cannot vanish: `1−ΣZ_j²` is nonsquare in `k(Z₁,Z₂,Z₃)` (an irreducible quadratic by rank three of its leading form). So the value is nonzero and has the same natural valuation as `M`. If any positive-degree coefficient occurs, that coefficient is noninfinitesimal since it is nonzero in `A`; as `r` is infinite, `M` is infinite. This proves both the ordered embedding and `bounded(B)=ℚ`, including cancellation between several terms of equal total degree. It is not a claim that arbitrary rational functions of the coordinates are infinite.

**Positive reciprocal identities descend.** This is a *different valuation* from the one used for the order. In `B_F=B⊗_A F` use the total-degree filtration. Its associated graded is `F[x₁,…,x₄]/(Σx_j²)`: the initial ideal of the principal relation is generated by its degree-two initial form, since leading forms multiply in the polynomial ring. This graded ring is a domain by rank four. Hence degree is additive on nonzero products and its negative defines a valuation on `Frac(B_F)`, trivial on `F`; the elements of `B_F` of degree zero are exactly `F`. If `Σ1/w_j=c∈F` with `0<w_j∈B`, a reciprocal of a nonconstant fiber coordinate function has strictly positive valuation, and for a constant denominator the reciprocal is in `F`. Take the residue of the identity: since `F` injects into the residue field,

    c = Σ_{w_j∈F} 1/w_j      in F.

Subtract in the **original ordered field**. A sum of strictly positive omitted terms cannot be zero. Thus each `w_j∈F∩B=A`. This does not evaluate at a real point at infinity; the real sphere over an ordered real closure is bounded relative to `√s`. The identity need not have positive denominators at every real point of that sphere.

This proof also applies without change to the rational two-square circle over an **ordered** `F`: `x²+y²` is irreducible over `F` since `−1` is nonsquare, although not over `F(i)`. This gives an especially short check on the binary **base**: realize `ℚ[X,Y]` as the first circle over `ℚ[P]` with `X²+Y²=P`. The same filtered argument with `F=ℚ(P)` sends any positive ES denominators straight back to `ℚ[P]`. The monic basis establishes the intersection there as well. Alternatively start on `ℚ[P]` and leave TS at `P` to the fair circle schedule. Neither variant needs a polynomial section of the norm map or an assertion about positivity of arbitrary functions upon specialization.

**Local lifting, including 2.** Suppose the old field has been embedded in `ℚ_l`, and let `b` be the image of `s` (nonzero). The image of `ℤ_l⁴` under sum of squares is compact and contains the nonnegative ordinary integers by classical Lagrange, hence contains their closure `ℤ_l`. If `b` is nonintegral, make `l^{2m} b` integral, represent it integrally, and divide the four coordinates by `l^m`. In the first case require all four new coordinates integral; in the second only require a field lift. Every point just obtained has some coordinate nonzero and is smooth **over `ℚ_l`**. The implicit function theorem gives an open ball of choices of three coordinates, with the fourth solved locally, small enough to retain original integrality when required. Choose the free triple algebraically independent over the countable old embedded field. The lift is generic: by monicity in the solved variable, the generic quadric algebra is integral and finite over the other three polynomial variables, and after localization to their fraction field it is a field. The injective map on the polynomial subring therefore gives injection of the whole domain. This needs no residue-field unit derivative at 2 or when `l²|b`.

## Assembly with the PID, TS, NR and Dirichlet tasks

Use the rational tower of `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §1 with `LOGIC_PID_DIRICHLET_AUDIT.md`, the **prime-target** NR stage in `LOGIC_ES_RIGIDITY_NR.md` §3 as restricted in `LOGIC_CIRCLE_AUDIT.md`, and the inert-prime circle and split-case normalization there. Start at `ℚ[P]` with `P>0` infinite, optionally forcing its TS circle as the first step. Service all new four-square labels as polynomial/row tasks are serviced: dovetail pairs (birth stage, enumeration index), checking the order on the actual node. No future mark needs to be known for a four-square task. Because order is fixed under extension, an old positive infinite element stays so. For clarity about the other invariants and quantifiers:

* Floor tasks give bounded-distance approximation to all finite-stage real closures; a genuinely new approximant is a polynomial variable in its prescribed order. Primitive coprime rows give Bézout certificates; they preserve old primes, the intersection with the old field, and factoriality also over Gaussian constants. A Dirichlet task for coprime `a,m` and bound `H`, `m>0`, makes `Q=a+mY_new>max(H,2)` a permanent rational prime. All embeddings make `Y_new` integral, and `Q` a unit whenever its inputs are locally integral with one unit.
* A TS task for each **actual element**, including scalar multiples, tests positive stage primality, everywhere-unit marks, and `1 mod 4` at 2. If inert take `x²+y²=s`; the odd-unit and 2-adic `1 mod 4` conditions give integral generic local lifts. Rational primality persists even for the target by inertness; conjugate factors appear upstairs. If already split, `s=c h h̄`; the positive scalar has even inert-prime valuations, hence is a rational Gaussian norm. Absorb it; at finitely many split places use norm-one Gaussian scalars to cancel opposite local valuations of the factors, as in `LOGIC_CIRCLE_AUDIT.md` §3. This produces coordinates integral **everywhere**, not just a field norm identity. The base `P` is inert before its first circle.
* For NR use `DH=V²+s` with `D` then `V` successively above their old fields, scheduled for positive rational-stage **primes**. Here `V²+s` is irreducible over `F(i)`: a square root of `s>0` in `F(i)` would belong to `F`, impossible by its prime valuation. Thus `D` is prime upstairs too; localization at `D` gives factoriality on both sides. Monicity yields prime persistence by embedding each old-prime quotient in the Laurent chart (also when `s` becomes zero). Give `D` unit values everywhere, `D≡3 mod 4` at 2, `V` integral. At an actual prime of `R`, `H` is integral by the identity. Modulo `D` the old `s` is nonzero, so `D,s` are coprime in the rational stage. There is no common ordinary prime by the evaluations of `D`. These nonstandard `D` are **not** the local testing primes of the sparse construction. Do not allow all positive `s` at this NR stage: e.g. `V²+1` splits over Gaussian constants. This restriction does *not* apply to the four-square stage.

**Parsimony, not just separate local solubility.** Build the countable finite-branching tree of possible histories before choosing which TS branches are accepted. At a finite level there are finitely many nodes: branch only take/skip at each potentially inert TS task; the enumerations, order extensions and other tasks, including four-square tasks, are fixed per node. At each prime prepare compatible evaluations on the tree, with the following growing-prefix safeguard.

For any fixed finite subtree and finite lists of nonzero functions, form a common variety of tuples over its root, starting with `P=T²≠0` (equivalently start with the open `T≠0` of the line). On each edge shrink to dense opens: polynomial and row fibers are lines; on `D≠0` the NR fiber is `𝔾_m×𝔸¹`; for the two- and four-square steps restrict to `s≠0` and use the smooth geometrically integral quadric fibers. These are assertions about characteristic zero, stable on spreading at large primes. **Geometric** generic integrality ensures after any dominant base change that there is a unique dominating integral component and its projection is dominant; concretely, work on smooth opens with nonempty geometrically integral fibers and multiply the sibling fibers over their common parent. Starting from the geometrically integral root yields a smooth geometrically integral open of the joint variety dominating every requested node. In particular adjoining the formal root `T` does not invalidate the geometry of a subsequent sphere. Remove all zeros and poles of the finitely listed functions pulled back from the nodes, and enforce the prefix unit conditions on the nonzero `D`, pivots, and Dirichlet outputs. Work in integral models away from finitely many primes. Lang–Weil on this fixed nonempty open gives good residue tuples at **every sufficiently large prime**, not merely a positive density of primes. Hensel at a smooth point lifts the joint tuple, and avoiding countably many proper algebraic sets over ℚ in its analytic neighbourhood makes each projected node evaluation injective. Equivalently use regular parameters and the fact that a nonzero regular function on a smooth integral variety has zero set with empty analytic interior. A finite initial segment/cutoff per prime, tending to infinity, suffices for valuations to be zero almost everywhere for **every fixed nonzero function** of each visited node. No smoothness is required at the singular points of an entire stage model.

At primes outside their assigned prefix (including all small primes if desired) extend down the tail over the already evaluated parent. At an empty root choose an integral transcendental `T` in a small unit open (near 1 at primes of 840), `P=T²`; `T` only specifies marks, **not** a square root adjoined to the ordered stage. The possibly forced base circle has integral lifts there. Use the conditional Dirichlet and `D,V` constraints just given, and choose integral TS coordinates for locally eligible circle targets. A hypothetical infeasible TS edge can be marked unavailable; along the actual path take that edge only for the everywhere-unit target with the required 2-adic class, so it always exists. On a four-square edge **whenever its evaluated `s` is integral**, use the integral generic lift proved above, whether or not integrality holds at other places; when nonintegral use the field lift. At prefix primes the stage generators already have integral values. Thus a four-square task for `s∈R` has its four witnesses in `R` at *all* places. Mere almost-everywhere integrality of those witnesses would be insufficient. The square marks occur at every place and may satisfy `P≡1 mod 840`; do not fix `P≡1 mod l` for almost all primes, which would defeat parsimony.

**Limit and ES.** Every old rational prime persists through all later stages. Units are scalar, so finite rational-stage factorizations persist in the union `A∞`. For its adelically integral subring `R`, parsimony allows each rational-stage prime to be scaled by a rational to valuation zero everywhere; so scaled it is prime in `R`. Factoring an `R`-element leaves an ordinary integral scalar, and ordinary primes are prime by local reduction. Thus `R` is atomic. For a primitive pair after these factors are removed, a serviced rational-stage Bézout row is integral away from finitely many places. At those places the pair has a unit entry, so the row can be made integral by a multiple of the one syzygy, approximating the local multiples by one rational number integral elsewhere. Thus `R` is Bézout, hence an external PID (gcd with a new ideal member decreases finite factor length until it generates the ideal). A rational translate clears the finitely many local fractional parts of any `A∞`-element; real-closure approximation therefore gives bounded-distance approximants in `R`, then actual floors by an ordinary integer adjustment. Its bounded elements are ℤ; Shepherdson gives IOpen. All nonstandard `R`-irreducibles are actual rational-stage prime elements, with zero valuation everywhere, by the same factor normalization and permanence. Thus all necessary prime tasks, including the correct scalar associates, are covered. Standard primes use ordinary TS/NR witnesses. For internal Dirichlet the final coprime inputs were coprime in every common rational stage and locally have a unit entry everywhere. Hence the scheduled output is a unit at every mark, descends prime without rescaling and is in the actual progression. Universal Lag holds by its scheduled infinite targets and ordinary Lagrange on finite nonnegative elements.

For a positive ES triple at `P` the finite-stage descent is **algebraic**. Four-square and circle stages descend by the valuation above; a polynomial or row descends by degree on its line and intersection with the old ring. At an NR stage first use polynomial degree in `V` on the `D≠0` Laurent chart, then note that a coefficient at `D^(−j)`, `j>0`, is divisible in `A[V]` by `(V²+s)^j`. Thus the intersection with `F(D)` is `A[D]`, and ordinary polynomial degree in `D` completes descent (`LOGIC_ES_RIGIDITY_NR.md` §3). We are left with three `g_i(P)∈ℚ[P]`. Their leading coefficients are positive since `P` is positive infinite. Let `M` clear their coefficients. For every ordinary `n≡π mod M` (the congruence read from the `P` marks), `g_i(n)` is integral: at primes dividing `M` compare with the integral `g_i(π_l)` and at all other primes use integral coefficients. For all large such `n` they are positive and give an identity in `n`. The progression is a **primitive square class** by CRT, as `π_l` is a square unit at every place. Mordell–Schinzel's polynomial obstruction (Elsholtz–Tao, discussion before Prop. 1.9) forbids a polynomially solvable primitive square class. It is *not* the claim that a single square cannot have any positive ES solution. This proves `¬ES(P)`.

## The floor obstruction is present, not disproved

Let `z=⌊√P⌋` in `R`, obtained by IOpen. `P` is a nonstandard prime, not a square. Therefore `w₁=P−z²` and `w₂=(z+1)²−P` are positive integers of the model, each at least 1. Lag gives `w_j−1=Σh_{jk}²`, so `w_j·1=1+Σh_{jk}²`: the exact two gap certificates in `Z66_IE1.md` Theorem K. At any ordered integer specialization of the certificates with both gaps positive, the purported square lies strictly between consecutive squares, impossible. Hence an odd-square landing of those data **cannot** exist. Nor does the construction give a homomorphism `R→ℝ`: Lag would make it monotone, incompatible with an element above all standard integers. The adeles here are nonordered local fields; their square roots of `P` need not be compatible with either the real floor or any common integer specialization. Only the three ES polynomials were transferred to a progression, not the floor, gap certificates, or arbitrary witnesses.

At this square-unit `P` no **standard** positive modulus `d≡3 mod 4` coprime to `P` can witness NR: an ordinary prime `l≡3 mod 4` dividing `d` would make `−π_l` a square mod `l`, whereas `π_l` is a square unit there. This limitation is compatible with the nonstandard `D` tasks. No inference to IE₁, IΔ₀, PA, standard ES, or any form of uniform short witnesses follows.
