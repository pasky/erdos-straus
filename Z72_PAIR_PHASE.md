## From a pair of factors to three denominators

Let `n ≡ 1 (4)`, let `c ≡ 3 (4)` be a positive prime not dividing `n`, and set `K=(n+c)/4`. Choose distinct primes `q,r` with `q^v r^w | K`, `v,w≥1`. Write `a,b` for their discrete logs modulo `c` in any primitive root, and `m=c−1`. With `||x||` denoting distance to the nearest integer, put

```
T = min over odd j mod m of (v+1)||ja/m|| + (w+1)||jb/m||.
```

**Proposition (PROVED).** If `T>1`, or `T=1` and either `v` or `w` is odd, then `4/n` is a sum of three positive unit fractions. In fact there are `|i|≤v`, `|k|≤w` with `ia+kb=m/2 mod m`. More precisely, conditional on `T=1`, this congruence fails in the box **if and only if** the additive orders of `a,b mod m` are respectively `2(v+1),2(w+1)` and `gcd(v+1,w+1)=1`.

To recover denominators, put `A/B=q^i r^k` in lowest terms. Then `AB | K` and `c | A+B`. Write `D=K/(AB)`, `E=(A+B)/c`. The triple is

```
(x,y,z) = (K, nADE, nBDE).
```
Indeed `1/y+1/z=(A+B)/(nABDE)=c/(nK)=4/n−1/K`. This last step works for **composite** shifts too; the character test as stated uses a prime shift. At the Mordell-class prime `1009`, use `c=11`, `K=255`, `q=5`, `r=17`, `v=w=1`. Relative to primitive root `2`, the logs are `4,9`; the minimum is `T=1`. Neither factor works alone, but `A=17,B=5` gives `(255,102918,30270)`.

### Proof of the congruence criterion

Write `a⃗=(a,b)`, `V=v+1`, `W=w+1`, `D₀=diag(V,W)`. If `m/2` is outside the group generated by `a,b`, this group has odd order; taking that order for `j` shows `T=0`. Otherwise let

```
Λ={z∈ℤ² : a⃗·z=0 mod (m/2)},    L=D₀⁻¹Λ.
```
Call a vector of `L` odd if its preimage in `Λ` hits `m/2 mod m`. The odd/even label is a nontrivial homomorphism to `ℤ/2`. Write `λ` for the shortest odd length in the infinity norm. A hit in the integer box means precisely `λ<1`, not `λ≤1`.

The dual lattice is `L* = D₀(ℤ² + ℤ·2a⃗/m)` (the quotient by the kernel is cyclic; equivalently compare the index with the order of `2a⃗/m mod ℤ²`). The linear functionals taking integral values on `L` and inducing its odd/even label modulo 2 form `D₀(2a⃗/m)+2L*`. Their least ℓ¹ length is `2T`: after dividing by two this is exactly the minimum over odd `j` and independent integer shifts in the two coordinates. Call this dual minimum `ρ`.

**Bound:** `λρ≤2`. Take a Euclidean Gauss-reduced basis `b₁,b₂` of `L`; let `s=|b₁|/|b₂|≤1`, `t=|cos θ|≤s/2` where `θ` is the angle between them. If just `bᵢ` is odd, use it and its dual basis vector `bᵢ*`. Their Euclidean length product is `1/sin θ`; passing to infinity and ℓ¹ norms gives `λρ≤√2/sin θ≤√(8/3)<2`. If both are odd, use `b₁` and the shorter of `b₁*±b₂*`. The square of the Euclidean length product is

```
(1+s²−2st)/(1−t²) ≤ 2,
```

since `1−s²+2t(s−t)≥0`. Passing again to infinity and ℓ¹ yields `λρ≤2`. Thus `T>1` forces `λ<1`.

For a miss at `T=1` all those inequalities are equalities: `λ=1`, both basis vectors are odd, `s=1,t=0`, and `||b₁||∞=|b₁|=1`. So the two basis vectors are signed coordinate unit vectors, and `Λ=Vℤ×Wℤ`. Its index `VW` divides `m/2` (odd), and its quotient is cyclic, so `V,W` are odd and coprime. The two axis generators are odd, hence the respective orders of `a,b` modulo `m` are `2V,2W`. Conversely, those orders and coprimality force this rectangular kernel: modulo `m/2` the two subgroups have orders `V,W` and meet trivially. Both axis generators are odd and the open box misses the odd coset. The scaled primal lattice is `ℤ²` with parity the sum of the coordinates, giving `ρ=2` as well. For sharpness take `c=31,(a,b)=(5,3),(v,w)=(2,4)` (a group-level example of the box criterion, not a specific `n`; z74 Opus xreview fix). ∎

The threshold is local. There is no argument here that makes either the phases large or the exponents adequate at a prime chosen in advance. Even the weaker demand of *some* solution using only two factors in *some prime shift* has a counterexample.

## Why the search must include composite shifts

**Exact finite counterexample.** At `p=1201`, for any shift set `c=4K−p>0` and look for coprime `A,B` with `AB|K` and `c|A+B`. Necessarily `c≤A+B≤AB+1≤K+1`, hence `c≤(p+4)/3`. Checking every `c≡3 mod 4` in that range gives the following complete list of shifts that admit a Type II certificate of this shape (other triple shapes, e.g. Type I, are not considered; z74 Opus xreview fix); displayed are the minimum number of distinct prime factors of `AB` and one minimizing pair.

| c | min ω(AB) | A,B |
|---:|---:|---|
|23|3|6,17|
|31|3|1,154|
|39|2|1,155|
|47|3|8,39|

Thus no prime shift works at support ≤2, even without the phase test. At `c=39`, `K=310`, `A=1,B=155`, `D=2,E=4`, giving `4/1201=1/310+1/9608+1/1489240`. This is not a counterexample to ES, nor does it exclude an eventual theorem for prime shifts.

**Finite computation (not the conjecture).** Allowing *all* shifts, all **13,176,808** primes `p≡1 mod 4` up to **500,000,000** admit a Type II certificate with `ω(AB)≤2`. This is a restriction on the shape of the witnesses; the range is not an extension of the known ES verification record. To reproduce:


Completeness of the sieve: for `k=AB` with `ω(k)≤2`, the unordered coprime splittings are just `(1,k)` and, when two distinct primes occur, the two prime powers on opposite sides. Mark `n=4kD−c` for divisors `c≡3 (4)` of `A+B`, and `D≥1`. Since `c≤k+1`, `k≤(N+1)/3` suffices. Every mark is a constructive certificate, and these loops include every certificate of the specified shape. This is a test of an all-shift strengthening, **not** a consequence of the prime-shift lemma. **Unconditional partial forcing (PROVED).** *(z74 Opus xreview fix: "arbitrary shifts" above and in the master files means shifts not restricted to primes. This statement is elementary: it uses two fixed identities. It is much weaker than the classical fact that almost all integers satisfy ES (Vaughan 1970).)* For `p≡5 mod 8`, take `A=1,B=2,c=3,D=(p+3)/8`. For `p≡1 mod 8`, if `h=(p+1)/2` has a prime divisor `r≡3 mod 4`, take `A=1,B=2r,c=2r+1,D=(h/r+1)/4`. These are integral because `h≡1 mod 4` implies `h/r≡3 mod 4`; in both cases `c=A+B` and `p=4ABD−c`. This proves the small-support property on a relative-density-one subset of the primes `1 mod 4`: among primes `1 mod 8` the relative density avoiding `p≡−1 mod r` for every `r` in any *fixed finite* list is `∏(1−1/(r−1))` by CRT and the prime number theorem in progressions. This product tends to zero as the list exhausts primes `3 mod 4` (their reciprocal sum diverges). No uniformity in the growing modulus is used. This `p+1` construction does not work at `1201` (`h=601` prime), precisely where the independent shift `39` succeeds.

The remaining pointwise implication would have to force at least one such divisor `c | A+B` **and** `4AB | p+c` at an arbitrary prime. Local transference alone cannot do that.
