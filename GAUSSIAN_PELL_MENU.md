**Theorem (unconditional, fixed finite menu).** For ordinary primes `p≤B`, let the selector use any integral `a,b` with `a²+b²=p`, as well as any fixed finite number of integral negative-Pell witnesses at p. For any finite menu of polynomial ES triples in all these coordinates over ℚ there is a computable `M`, `840|M`, such that at most `O_menu(B^(3/4) log(2B))` primes `p≡1 mod M` have any success. Hence the misses have relative prime density one there. The same conclusion holds with the Pell inputs replaced by one pair `U,V` on the Gaussian-coupled row `b(U²−V²)+2aUV=1`; in this row version even **rational** `U,V` are allowed. One may include the induced Pell coordinates `X=a(U²−V²)−2bUV, Y=U²+V²` in that menu, being polynomials on the row. No height cap on either kind of conic input is used.

**Rational-function integrality lemma.** For fixed `g/h∈ℚ(a,b)∖ℚ[a,b]` in lowest terms, there are `O_{g,h,η}(N^(1+η))` integer pairs in `|a|,|b|≤N` where it is defined and integral, for every `η>0`. Clear numerical denominators so g,h are integral polynomials; swap a,b to make `deg_b h>0`. Coprimality over `ℚ(a)[b]` gives an identity `Ag+Ch=J(a)≠0` with `A,C∈ℤ[a,b]`, `J∈ℤ[a]`, by Bézout and clearing denominators. Exclude the finitely many a where J or the leading coefficient of h vanishes (cost `O(N)`). Now `h(a,b)|J(a)` and `|J(a)|≪(1+N)^O(1)`. There are `O_η(N^η)` signed divisors for each a by the elementary divisor bound; each value for h gives at most `deg_b h` choices of b. This proves the lemma, including denominators depending on just one variable. We will also discard fixed proper algebraic loci in the parameter plane at cost `O(N)` by the same slicing root bound, without divisibility.

**Compression.** Set `P=a²+b²`, `K=ℚ(a,b)`. For multiple integral Pell witnesses apply the signed-power gap lemma of `PELL_ES_MULTI_MENU.md` to each output at the given `(a,b)`. After Laurent expansion the coefficients belong formally to `K(s)`, `s²=P`, and the conjugation fixes a,b. They specialize with both embedding magnitudes and common integer denominator bounded by fixed powers of p: in addition to the old bounds one has `|a|,|b|≤√p`. Vanishing coefficients/tied sums need not be removed individually. Thus the gap cutoff, list of bounded exponents and symmetric surviving subsets are fixed by the menu, not by the chosen pair. Conjugate averaging gives a finite menu in one integral Pell pair over K, in fact over `ℚ[a,b,1/P]` (powers of s in denominators suffice, besides numeric constants). Every original successful output is captured, since every positive ES denominator is `≤2p⁴`. The following single-conic argument allows *arbitrary fixed rational coefficients in a,b* on their domains, so P denominators are harmless. Do not instead compress independent rational Pell points: they need not belong to the cyclic integral unit group.

**Global identities.** Consider the generic conic ring over K for either `x²−Py²=−1` or the stated row. The homogeneous leading binary quadratic is irreducible over K: P is nonsquare (valuation 1 at the irreducible divisor `a²+b²`), and for the row the discriminant is `4P`. The degree filtration has graded ring obtained by quotienting by that leading form, a domain. Thus minus degree is a valuation on the function field, zero-degree elements of the conic ring being precisely K. For a triple with identically vanishing ES defect, discard any identically zero coordinate as a nonsolution. In `Σ1/f_j=4/P` take residues: the coordinates in K **alone** already sum reciprocally to `4/P` in K. Outside a proper parameter locus this identity specializes wherever all three values are positive. No omitted positive summand is possible. Polynomial identities in the generic conic ring specialize after clearing a fixed nonzero parameter denominator, so there is no hidden infinite family of exceptional fibers off these loci.

If all three coordinates are in K and any is not polynomial in a,b, the integrality lemma bounds the successes by `O_η(N^(1+η))`. Otherwise apply the degree argument **again**, now writing the norm plane over `ℚ(P)` as `a²+b²=P`. The graded leading form `a²+b²` is irreducible there since `−1` is nonsquare in `ℚ(P)`. Consequently either the positivity argument excludes success or all three coordinates belong to `ℚ(P)∩ℚ[a,b]=ℚ[P]`: coprime numerator and denominator in P remain comaximal after `P↦a²+b²`, and a nonconstant denominator remains a nonunit. This proves the needed binary descent without classification of quadratic slots. For the original unlocalized polynomial Pell ring the first intersection with K is already `ℚ[a,b]` by monicity in x; for compressed formulas one must account for the rational coefficients instead, as just done. For each resulting polynomial triple positivity somewhere on `P>0` gives positivity throughout by the clopen argument (each coordinate ≥`P/4` on the positive locus). Take M the lcm of 840 and all their coefficient denominators. At `p≡1 mod M`, integrality of all values transfers to 1, contradicting the upper bound 3 on the sum of three positive integer reciprocals there. All operations selecting these identity slots and M are finite polynomial arithmetic.

**Nonidentities.** For nonzero cleared defect W on the generic conic, the fiber intersection over K is zero-dimensional or empty: the projective conic is smooth and geometrically integral in both cases (the binary leading form has nonzero discriminant and the constant is nonzero). Its reduced geometric points have finitely many Galois orbits. Outside a fixed proper locus in the `(a,b)`-plane every specialized lift is detected by these orbits; this is made explicit in the specialization lemma below (z74 Opus xreview fix). Explicitly separate the finitely many distinct pairs by an integer linear form `z=x+ky` (or `U+kV`); over K the reduced finite algebra is generated by z by separation. Its squarefree eliminant factors as the minimal polynomials for the orbits and both coordinates are interpolating polynomials in z over K. These relations hold up to nilpotents before reduction: some powers are in the conic/defect ideal, and clearing the finitely many parameter denominators in those ideal memberships makes them valid at **all** specialized points off a proper locus. If the generic intersection is empty use the identity 1 in the ideal instead. Remove also poles, degree drops, discriminants and collisions, at cost `O(N)` pairs in total. This includes nongenerically dominant components; no bound on the lifted coordinates is needed.

**Specialization lemma (z74 Opus xreview fix).** Let `I⊂K[x,y]` be generated by the conic equation and `W`, with `V(I)` finite, `z=x+ky` separating its geometric points, `E∈K[z]` the squarefree eliminant and `X,Y∈K[z]` the interpolating polynomials, so `K[x,y]/√I≅K[z]/(E)` and `E(z)`, `x−X(z)`, `y−Y(z)∈√I`. Fix `e≥1` with `E(z)^e, (x−X(z))^e, (y−Y(z))^e∈I` and write each membership with multipliers in `K[x,y]`. Multiplying by a common nonzero `c∈ℚ[a,b]` gives identities `cE(z)^e=Σλᵢgᵢ`, etc., with `λᵢ`, the cleared `cE`, `cX`, `cY` in `ℚ[a,b][x,y]` and `gᵢ` the cleared generators. Let `Z⊂𝔸²` be the proper closed set where `c`, the discriminant of `E`, the leading coefficients of `E` and of each irreducible factor `φ` of `E`, or a coefficient denominator of the menu/compressed formulas vanish. If `(a,b)∉Z` and `(x,y)∈ℚ²` (or `ℚ̄²`) lies on the specialized conic with `W=0`, on **any** component, then `E_{(a,b)}(z)=0`, `x=X_{(a,b)}(z)`, `y=Y_{(a,b)}(z)`, and `z` is a root of exactly one specialized factor `φ_{(a,b)}`, which is separable of unchanged degree. If `V(I)=∅`, then `1∈I` and `c` alone shows there are no lifts off `Z`. The integer points of `Z` in the box number `O(N)`, and `Z` contains the exceptional set `Ω_f` (poles, inseparable specializations) of Zywina's theorem for each `f=φ`.

* For a factor of degree ≥2, a rational lift gives a rational root of a specialization of a fixed monic irreducible polynomial `φ` over `ℚ(a,b)`. Off `Z` that specialization is separable of the same degree `d≥2` (z74 Opus xreview fix): its Galois group `G_t` fixes the rational root, so it is intransitive, whereas the generic group `G` is transitive because `φ` is irreducible; hence `G_t≠G`. Equivalently the base points lie in a thin set. The two-parameter **integral box** bound is `O(N^(3/2) log(2N))`. Verified statement: J.-P. Serre, *Topics in Galois Theory*, 2nd ed., A K Peters 2008, §3.4, Thm 3.4.4 (S. D. Cohen), in the remark after Prop. 3.4.3 (z74 Opus xreview fix: published reference instead of a course-mirror URL). It states `Int_A(N)=O(N^(n−1/2) log N)` for a thin set `A` in affine n-space; Serre notes that `log N` may be replaced by `(log N)^γ`, `γ<1`, which does not affect the conclusion. Equivalently use the **first**, not second, bound of [D. Zywina, *Hilbert's irreducibility theorem and the larger sieve*, Thm 1.2, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.6465), with `k=ℚ,n=2`, which is stated directly for monic irreducible polynomials and needs no geometric-splitting-field assumption. It counts `t∈ℤⁿ∖Ω_f` with `‖t‖≤N` and `G_t≠G`; here `f=φ` is monic as a minimal polynomial over K, and `Ω_f⊂Z` (z74 Opus xreview fix). Serre cites S. D. Cohen, *The distribution of Galois groups and Hilbert's irreducibility theorem*, Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 43 (1981), 227–250.
* For a linear factor the lifted coordinates are the values of the fixed K-rational section, by the interpolation above. On the Pell conic these sections **do exist** (`x=a/b, y=1/b`). At least one coordinate is a nonpolynomial rational function: otherwise evaluating the polynomial identity at `(0,0)` gives a rational square equal to `−1`. Its integral specializations cost `O_η(N^(1+η))` by the lemma. It matters here that the compressed fundamental witness has **integer** coordinates.
* The coupled row in fact has **no K-rational point**. Put `z=bU+aV`. A section would give `z²−PV²=b`. In the DVR at P the two left terms have unequal valuation parity (ignore a zero term), so their minimum must be `v_P(b)=0`. This forces `v_P(z)=0`, `v_P(V)≥0`. Modulo P, b would be a square in `Frac(ℚ[a,b]/(P))=ℚ(i)(b)`, with `i=a/b` in that field. But b is an indeterminate there and has order 1 at zero. Thus the degree-one case is absent for the row; merely evaluating a *polynomial* section at the origin would suffice for the integral-input version.

**Remark R1 (z74 Opus xreview fix; reviewer's derivation, not checked; would extend the single-witness case to rational Pell points).** The integrality of the Pell witness is used above only through the integrality lemma for a section coordinate. Alternatively: on a degree-one orbit with K-section `σ`, the relation `W∘σ=0` makes `F∘σ∈K³` a reciprocal ES identity in K. If some `F_i∘σ` is identically zero, success is impossible. Otherwise the output integrality lemma, or the descent to `ℚ[P]` with `M` also absorbing these denominators, applies. If correct, a single **rational** Pell point would suffice, as in `PELL_ES_MENU_NOGO.md`; integrality would then be needed only to compress several witnesses. The theorem statement above is **unchanged** and does not rely on this remark.

**Count and scope.** Use `N=√B`, e.g. `η=1/4`. A successful prime gives at least one of the counted pairs, and each pair determines just one parameter p. Summing over the fixed menu proves the bound; the PNT in the fixed reduced class gives the density conclusion. No Schinzel H or other prime-values conjecture, no ordinary ES counterexample, and no positive construction for all primes is implied. This proof treats one coupled row in place of the Pell witnesses (including its induced `X,Y`); it does not treat a product of that row with additional *independent* arbitrary conic data by the dimension-one intersection argument.
