**Verdict (proved).** Compactness supplies a multiple of all *named* old elements. It does not supply a multiple of an entire internally bounded interval that lacked one in the base. In fact no initial subring of any elementary extension containing that base can repair the latter failure. Separately, the floor/four-square successor of `LOGIC_LAG_END_MULTIPLE.md` **fails the odd-quotient condition**, at the explicit odd modulus `h+1`. This is not an unavoidable feature of a successor: a new quotient-selection step repairs *every* odd modulus at the first, PID-based successor. Iteration from an arbitrary resulting non-PID is not proved.

### The bounded-formula obstruction

Use the nonnegative cone. Put `C(b,L) := L>0 ∧ ∀t≤b (t>0 → ∃z≤L (tz=L))` and `G(b) := ∃L C(b,L)`. The bound on z is automatic. `C` is bounded and absolute between an initial subring and its ambient discretely ordered ring. If `D≺D*` and `D⊂I⊂D*` with I initial in `D*`, then for **each old b**, `I⊨G(b)` iff `D⊨G(b)`: a witness in I works in `D*` by bounded absoluteness, and existence reflects to D by elementarity. Conversely an old witness works in `D*` and in I. More generally this works for existential bounded-matrix formulas, and the fits-inside-I/bounded absoluteness assumption cannot be silently replaced by an arbitrary subring. The set G is downward closed; if D already satisfies G at a cofinal set of its own cutoffs then it already satisfies global BCM. Merely covering all standard cutoffs is much weaker.

The type `h>D, t|h (0<t∈D)` is finitely satisfiable (take a sufficiently large multiple of a finite product); modest compactness and downward Löwenheim–Skolem suffice. Suppose an old b lacks G. In `D*` the bounded countercondition at the chosen h then gives `0<t≤b` with `t∤h`. This t is necessarily **new**. In particular, this failure of endness does not depend on high saturation or on a badly chosen realization. It occurs for *every* realization of the named type. The convex hull of D in `D*` excludes h and contains t. It does retain old ES failure (the ambient model already has failure), and it has Lag and IOpen: four-square coordinates at a positive s are bounded by `s+1`; each element of `rc(Frac hull)` is bounded by a hull element by the root bound and cofinality of the hull in its fraction field, so its ambient floor is in the hull. Neither fact gives G. More generally even if `D⊨BCM`, arbitrary elementary extensions need not be end extensions: for an old externally infinite b one may simultaneously realize `0<t<b` and `t≠d` for every named d alongside the type for h. Compactness does not enforce omission of this cut type. Unions of elementary chains remain elementary, so going through more elementary stages cannot remove the bounded obstruction. Without **ambient initiality**, selecting a non-elementary subring omitting the bad t would be a substantive new construction, not compactness.

There really are bad cutoffs in the proposed starting PID. If an external PID with IOpen has a nonstandard positive b and a nonzero L divisible by everything up to b, every standard prime integer divides L. Each such prime is prime in the ring, since floor division and the standard finite remainder interval give `D/ℓD=𝔽_ℓ`; they are pairwise nonassociate. This contradicts external finite prime factorization. Thus in this starting ring precisely the standard cutoffs satisfy G. Even starting at the one-step successor does not evade the criterion at b=h: there `h−j` for standard positive j cannot all divide a nonzero finite-stage element (free bases with 1 over `F[h]`, as in `LOGIC_LAGRANGE_END_TEST.md` §4).

### Odd quotient selection: necessary, false in the unmodified successor, repairable at the first step

Let `O(d)` mean `−1` is a sum of two squares in `D/dD`, `d>0` odd. In IOpen this is **bounded at d**: use remainders `0≤x,y<d` and `0≤z≤2d+1` with `x²+y²+1=dz`. Consequently `O(d)` for an old odd d is absolute in genuine end extensions with IOpen. A failure cannot be repaired *one stage later*. Indeed such extensions induce isomorphisms on all old principal quotients: division gives an old remainder `0≤r<d`, and a nonnegative quotient of an old multiple is bounded by its old dividend.

**Countertest at the actual PID successor.** Take the unmodified tower over `F=Frac(D)` of `LOGIC_LAG_END_MULTIPLE.md`. It admits an auxiliary ring map `ψ:A*→ℚ₂` extending the old embedding at 2, now with `ψ(h)=−1`, not the distinguished mark `h↦0`. Indeed at each free floor choose 0 and at each sphere use universal representability over `ℚ₂` (scale to `ℤ₂`, where four squares are surjective). Exact zero is allowed. The restriction `ψ:R₁→ℚ₂` need not be integral, but kills `d=h+1`. This d is positive odd since `h/2∈R₁`. A representation modulo d would give `−1=x²+y²` in `ℚ₂`, impossible: after clearing a minimal power of 2, squares mod 4 show neither an integral representation nor one with a negative valuation can work. Equivalently the homogeneous ternary sum has no primitive zero mod 4. Thus the odd-quotient invariant is actually **false** in this successor, although its prescribed 2-adic mark takes d to a unit. Old Lag alone did not address the new odd quotients.

**Positive modification (one successor, proved).** Start from the countable external PID with Lag as before. Adjoin to the floor and four-square tasks this operation: for each positive stage element d outside the old convex hull whose prescribed marks are integral everywhere and whose 2-mark is odd, adjoin the algebra

```
A[X,Y,Z]/(dZ−1−X²−Y²).
```

These conditions recognize every new positive odd d in the final pullback, since the marks extend and do not change; no definable test inside the ring is required. (z74 Opus xreview fix: unlike `LOGIC_LAG_END_MULTIPLE.md`, where the tower is built before the marks, this task list depends on the marks. That is harmless: marks are fixed on each finite stage before its tasks are scheduled, and later stages never change them. Recall also that `d∉ 2R′` iff `e₂(d)∈ℤ₂^×`, because `R′/2R′=R₀/2R₀=𝔽₂`.) Use `Y` larger than the old real closed field, then `X` larger than its real closure with Y. In `Frac(A)[Y,X]` substitute `Z=(1+X²+Y²)/d`. The monic-in-X normal form is `g(Y,Z)+X h(Y,Z)`; surviving highest X-degrees are respectively even and odd, proving injectivity and showing that the elements independent of X are exactly `A[Y]`. Thus bounded stage elements remain exactly F, and the two largest-scale steps add no ordered residues. These checks do not use d prime or factor d. Polynomial and monic presentations suffice; no finite-stage UFD hypothesis is needed here.

At *each* odd PID prime q choose one integral norm `−1` pair in its completed DVR. To see it exists, use old Lag at q to obtain a nonzero four-square zero mod q. In the residue field view it as `N(z)+N(w)=0` in the quadratic étale Gaussian algebra. If `−1` is already square we are done; otherwise this algebra is a field, both z,w are nonzero, and `N(z/w)=−1`. A unit coordinate gives a Hensel lift since 2 is invertible. Use this pair for X,Y and set `Z=0`, even when `e_q(d)=0`. At the 2-mark take `X=Y=0, Z=1/e₂(d)`, integral because d is a unit there. All coordinates are therefore integral at all marks. This operation cannot have the preceding auxiliary `h=−1` specialization when it services `h+1`; that is exactly the defect it repairs. Old positive odd moduli already satisfy O by the same local argument modulo their finitely many prime powers and CRT. With dovetailing, the original floors, Lag, common h, endness, and old ES failure persist, and the modified successor satisfies O at **all** its odd moduli.

### What still has to iterate

For any countable domain D, the principal completion `S=lim_{t≠0}D/tD` has `S/tS=D/tD` and is torsion-free: coherent division by t reads a component mod `tu` and cancels t to specify the component mod u. Hence in `S_F=(D∖{0})⁻¹S` one has `S∩F=D` and `S_F=S+F`, without PID. An element in an elementary extension gives values in this **old** inverse system only if all its old-modulus residues are old. For a genuine end extension with division this is automatic; arbitrary saturation does not give it. The named h itself has residue zero, but Lag witnesses at its translates must also meet compatible conditions.

One concrete sufficient interface for repeating the modified relative tower is: (i) every element of S is four squares in S, and (ii) for every `e∈S` with `e≡1 mod 2S` there are `x,y,z∈S` with `ez=1+x²+y²`. Use `S⊂S_F` as the single marking target; clear field denominators by squares for Lag and use (ii) for every integral odd d, including nonunits and zero divisors at individual components. The intersection and sum identities supply translations and floors as above. This is a sufficient landing test, **not** a necessary condition for all possible constructions. O at each finite modulus supplies norm choices separately, not coherent choices in this interface. Also old principal quotients need not be finite and extracting a dyadic factor by writing every old integer as `2^j·odd` with *standard* j is impossible for the very h constructed here. Neither Presburger division by standard numerals nor the elementary named type provides (i) or (ii). The proved bounded necessity is the different assertion that O must already hold at each retained odd modulus and that ambient-elementary cuts cannot add G at old bad cutoffs. No iterated Lag + BCM ES countermodel follows from these checks.
