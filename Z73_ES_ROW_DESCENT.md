**Verdict.** The proposed descent is valid for *any* extending order. In fact it needs no real closure, continuity, or change of order. The actual denominators descend, not just the existence of a solution. Consequently the ES profile criterion needs neither marked polynomial sections nor the exact-zero condition (E). There is also a separate realization argument below: if ring evaluations rather than field embeddings are used, **every prescribed unit profile** is realizable in the Bézout + internal Dirichlet tower. This last assertion does **not** extend to external PIDs.

## 1. The degree argument

Let F be an ordered field, give `F(T)` any extending order, and suppose `x₁,…,x_k ∈ F[T]` are positive in this order and `Σ 1/x_i = c ∈ F`. Then **all `x_i ∈ F`**. Indeed, put `D=∏x_i`. Compare coefficients of `T^(deg D)` in

```
cD = Σ D/x_i.
```

For `deg x_i>0` this coefficient on the right is zero; for constant `x_i` it is `lc(D)/x_i`. Since `lc(D)≠0`, the constant denominators already contribute exactly c. Subtract their terms in the original ordered field: a nonempty sum of the remaining positive reciprocals cannot be zero. This comparison is equivalently evaluation of the reciprocals at polynomial infinity; that place need not respect the given order. It works even when T is infinitesimal, negative, or ordered at a finite cut.

In the proposed geometric proof one should use **semialgebraic** connectedness, not topological connectedness of a non-Archimedean real closure. Alternatively take its real closed ordered extension containing the actual T, so that nonemptiness on the full line there needs no model-completeness step. Neither issue affects the coefficient proof.

Let A be a UFD, `F=Frac(A)`, and let `p>0` belong to A. A free polynomial extension has `A[T]∩F=A`. For a row take

```
E = A[U,V]/(aU+bV−1),   gcd_A(a,b)=1.
```

For nonzero entries the generic fiber is **exactly** `F[V]`, not a localization in the variable V: `U=(1−bV)/a`. The map of E to that chart is injective (the primitive linear relation is prime by Gauss). If `x∈E∩F`, the a-chart puts x in `A[1/a][V]∩F=A[1/a]`, and the b-chart similarly puts it in `A[1/b]`. Their intersection is A by the coprimality in the UFD. If one entry is zero, the other is a unit and this is just a polynomial extension. Thus for a positive ES triple in E apply the lemma at `c=4/p`; each denominator belongs to **A**, positive in its original order. This does not require p to be prime. Iterating down a finite prefix proves the same assertion for the countable union; a triple uses only finitely many stages. For an old p in a pullback R defined by fixed compatible evaluations, descended denominators retain those very evaluations, hence their integrality.

Failure tests delimit the argument:

* Without positivity, `1+1/T+1/(−T)=1`.
* Mere regularity on the **real** line is not polynomiality: `1/(T²+2) + 1/((T²+2)/(T²+1)) = 1` gives positive nonconstant denominators in `F[T,1/(T²+1)]`. Splitting the first term into two equal reciprocal terms gives a triple. On a Laurent fiber `1/(1+T)+1/(1+1/T)=1` for `T>0`. Thus this proof does not justify descent for general norm, normal-cone, rational-function, or open-fiber stages.
* Without the intersection hypothesis one gets only membership in F. For `A=ℚ[d]`, `d>0`, the noncoprime row `dU+dV=1` introduces `1/d`; `1/(d+1)+1/((d+1)/d)=1`, with the second denominator new. The tested hypothesis is coprimality *after division by the old gcd*.

## 2. The exact ES criterion, independent of realization

Start the polynomial/row tower at `ℚ[P]` with P positive infinite. Let π be any unit profile. All that is needed here of an ordered subring `R⊆A` containing P is

```
R ∩ ℚ[P] = {G(P) : G∈ℚ[T], G(π_q)∈ℤ_q for every q}.    (*)
```

If there is an ES triple in R, §1 shows it is already `G₁(P),G₂(P),G₃(P)` with `G_i∈ℚ[T]`. Positivity at positive infinite P means positive leading coefficients. The identity at P is a polynomial identity, since P is transcendental. For any common denominator D of their coefficients, at all integers `n≡π mod D` the values `G_i(n)` are integers: at q|D use `DG_i(n)≡DG_i(π_q) mod q^{v_q(D)}` and (*); elsewhere the coefficients are integral. The values are positive for all sufficiently large n. Conversely, polynomials solving a class containing π give integral values at every π_q: sufficiently large integers in that class approximate π_q to arbitrary q-adic precision, by CRT. Their eventual positivity gives positivity at P. Thus the actual ES truth value is exactly polynomial solvability on some primitive class containing π. By ET Prop. 1.9 this is equivalent to π lying in a family class: for the nontrivial direction the finitely many family classes covering the large primes of a solvable class must cover its unit profiles by ordinary Dirichlet on the common modulus.

This replaces the ES-specialization steps of `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` and `LOGIC_MARKED_SECTIONS.md`, not their general specialization results, pure-ring universal transfer, or the proof of failure of NR in the square-profile PID. For a later *free coordinate* mark the earlier finite stages can be replayed over `ℚ[P]` by polynomial base change as in `LOGIC_PID_INTERVAL_AUDIT.md` §3: an old coprime pair stays coprime after adjoining an indeterminate. The given order restricts to each ring in this rearranged presentation; it need not be rebuilt. This observation does not reclassify every primitive linear Dirichlet output with nonunit slope as a free coordinate. For square profiles one still uses the arithmetic fact that unit squares are uncovered; the descent is not by itself that arithmetic fact.

## 3. Realization of arbitrary unit marks (a separate argument)

For completeness, here is how to meet (*) **without (E)** while keeping open induction, Bézout, and the internal unbounded Dirichlet axiom. Use the same ordered tower as in `Z73_ADELIC_ROW_AUDIT.md`: polynomial floor stages, coprime rows, and free variables Y for primitive Dirichlet tasks `Q=a+mY`, `m>0`. Old primes remain nonunit prime. Fix any `π∈∏ ℤ_q^×`. At some primes π_q might even equal the same ordinary integer exactly. Seek unital **ring** maps `φ_q:A→ℚ_q` with `φ_q(P)=π_q`; at algebraic marks they cannot extend to the entire fraction field. The following construction never needs such an extension.

**Finite-prefix chart cover of the base line.** For a fixed finite prefix there exist finitely many recipes and nonzero `d_j∈ℤ[P]` with no common geometric zero. Recipe j specifies which slot to invert at each row and an integer value for each free coordinate (one per step); starting with P as variable, the resulting prefix point is defined over `ℚ[P,1/d_j]`. Every chosen pivot and every Dirichlet output is a unit there. This is a finite open cover, **not** one global section avoiding those divisors.

To prove it, work first over `k=ℚ(P)` at the generic point, or over `k=ℚ[P]/s` at any closed point, s irreducible. In the latter case the stage fiber is the domain `B/sB` since the old prime s stays prime. The map on consecutive fibers is injective: at a row at least one entry survives by coprimality, and on that chart one has a polynomial ring over a localization of the preceding domain. In particular the row does not delete the whole fiber. The same holds on the generic fiber. In each case, inductively the fiber is rational over k, with a dense chart obtained by choosing at each row a surviving pivot. A Dirichlet output on the fiber is a nonzero polynomial in its fresh variable since at least one input survives; it remains nonzero later. In the resulting rational parametrization every pivot and every such output is therefore a nonzero rational function. Choose the free coordinates to be ordinary integers avoiding the finitely many numerator and denominator zeros over k: `ℤ^n` is Zariski dense over any characteristic-zero k. Replay these fixed integer choices with P variable; all resulting rational functions required to be inverted are defined and nonzero at that base point. Clearing their numerators and denominators gives one principal open `D(d_j)` containing it and one recipe there. Take a recipe at the generic point, then a recipe for each irreducible factor of its `d_j`; these opens cover the line. This proof allows *different pivots* at different base points. One chart chosen solely on the generic fiber need not work at a fixed bad base value.

After excluding finitely many rational primes, the recipes and their inverses spread over `ℤ_q[P,1/d_j]`. The `D(d_j)` cover every residue line there: clear scalar denominators in an identity `Σ e_j(P)d_j(P)=1` over `ℚ[P]`. Therefore at **every** residue of P over those primes there is a recipe with unit pivots and unit prefix outputs. Taking actual free coordinates in its residue cylinders (not exactly the listed integers) preserves these units and integrality of the stage generators. This argument is uniform in the prescribed profile; no bound on how many primes divide `f(π_q)` and no condition on its exact zeros enter.

**Prefix lifts and infinite tails.** For each prefix length n take a threshold above its finitely many excluded primes; arrange strictly increasing thresholds tending to infinity. Each q handles one prefix, of length tending to infinity with q, possibly empty at small q. Start from the actual `P=π_q` and use a covering recipe at its residue for this prefix. At every free step take a lift transcendental over the countable field generated by the preceding local images; the allowed cylinder is uncountable. At a row solve for the pivot's coordinate in `ℚ_q`. Consequently for each stage B the local map has kernel exactly `sB`, with `s=0` for transcendental π_q and otherwise s its minimal polynomial over ℚ: induction embeds `Frac(B/sB)` and extends that embedding across a free variable or the surviving row chart. In particular exact vanishing of an old nonzero polynomial in P causes **no further accidental kernels** on that fiber.

In the tail, for each row choose a pivot nonzero in this exact fiber (at least one exists by coprimality); solve over `ℚ_q`, allowing poles in the sense of *negative valuation*, never division by zero. Choose the free coordinate transcendental over the old image field. For every tail Dirichlet variable require `Y∈ℤ_q`; when both inputs are integral and one a unit, avoid `a+mY≡0 mod q`. This rule forbids at most one residue and applies even at 2. Prefix outputs are already units. This produces the claimed compatible ring maps. Every fixed element of A is integral almost everywhere because its stage is eventually included in the handled prefix and only finitely many scalar denominators are involved. Set `R={g∈A : ∀q φ_q(g)∈ℤ_q}`. It satisfies (*) even if the product map itself has nontrivial kernel: R is a **subring of A**, not the image of A in the adeles.

**Descent check at an exact zero.** CRT still shifts any A-element into R by a rational, and a standard integer clears its finite negative support. The same bounded-elements and approximation proof gives an integer part and IOpen. For a nonzero pair write `a=hu, b=hv` in A with `Su+Tv=1`, using the serviced union. Define `v_q(x)=ord_q φ_q(x)` with `ord_q(0)=+∞`. Although `v_q(h)` can be infinite, the identity forces `e_q=min(v_q(u),v_q(v))` **finite at every prime** and zero almost everywhere. With `γ=∏ q^{e_q}`, `d=γh, f=u/γ, g=v/γ`, we have `f,g∈R` and at least one local unit everywhere. If `φ_q(h)=0` then `φ_q(d)=0` is integral automatically; otherwise `v_q(d)=min(v_q(a),v_q(b))≥0`. Use the single rational syzygy correction of `Z73_ADELIC_ROW_AUDIT.md` on the row for f,g; it only ever divides by a **unit** local entry. This yields `(a,b)R=dR` even when both original inputs evaluate to zero. The residual pair is defined algebraically in A, **not** by dividing local images by zero. Zero-input cases are trivial.

P stays prime in R because it is prime in A and has unit image at every q. For Dirichlet inputs Bézout-coprime in R, there is no common stage prime divisor, so their scheduled primitive task gives `Q=a+mY` prime in A above the requested order bound. Their R-identity forces a local unit input at each q, so Q has unit value everywhere, whence it stays prime in R. The witness Y is integral at every prime, so `m | Q−a` in R. These arguments require ring maps, not valuations on F. P has exactly the prescribed standard residues since `(P−r)/M∈R` iff `π≡r mod M`.

**Consequence for the nonsquare model.** Use `Z66_UCOVER.md` Thm T to choose sufficiently large `l≡3 mod 4`, `l∤840`, and an uncovered profile equal to 1 **exactly** off l with nonsquare l-component. For this profile the chart argument simplifies further: off l use only the recipe whose open contains `P=1`, reducing it outside the finite bad set for each prefix, and embed the exact fiber fields `Frac(B/(P−1))` into the local fields. No cover at the other base values is needed away from l. The realization just proved and §2 give an infinite prime `P≡1 mod 840` with `¬ES(P)` in a model of IOpen + Bézout + internal Dirichlet. The standard-modulus NR witnesses from `Z73_ADELIC_ROW_AUDIT.md` apply unchanged. The new multi-prime thickening is not needed for **this model theorem**; it has the separate benefit of making the marks componentwise transcendental. More generally, substituting the arbitrary-profile realization in the countermodel direction of `Z65_LOGIC2.md` Thm D gives the same primitive-class prime-tail dichotomy after adding Bézout and internal Dirichlet to IOpen (the provable direction already holds in IOpen).

**Theorem (dichotomy; z74 Opus xreview fix, stating the preceding sentence).** For a primitive class r mod M, with K_{r,M}=(r+MẐ)∩Ẑ^×, the following are equivalent: (i) K_{r,M}∩U=∅; (ii) IOpen ⊢ ∀n≥N₀ (Prime(n) ∧ n≡r (M) → ES(n)) for some numeral N₀; (iii) the same with IOpen + Bézout + internal Dirichlet. (i)⇒(ii) is Z65 Thm D (U64 Thm B, ET Prop 1.9); (ii)⇒(iii) is trivial; ¬(i)⇒¬(iii): realize any π∈K_{r,M}∩U by the construction above (Dirichlet tasks are serviced for every bound B₀, so Q>B₀), then §2 gives ¬ES(P) at the prime P≡r (M), which exceeds every numeral.

None of this realizes arbitrary profiles in an external PID. In the one-prime example the nonzero `P−1` is divisible in R by *all powers* of every standard q≠l, which even by itself rules out an external PID. Nor does this give the universal NR axiom, or a counterexample in ordinary integers.
