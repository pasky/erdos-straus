## 1. Equivalent forms (PROVED; elementary)

**Lemma 1 (symmetric form).** Let n be odd.
(a) U₇(n) ⟺ ∃ x, z ≥ 1 with lcm(8, xz) | n + x + z.
(b) If n ≡ 1 (mod 8): U₇(n) ⟺ ∃ x, z ≥ 1 with xz | n + x + z and x + z ≡ 7 (mod 8).
(c) In the setting of (b), any such pair has gcd(x, z) | n, exactly one of x, z even, and
(x mod 8, z mod 8) ∈ {(0,7), (4,3), (2,5), (6,1)} up to swapping; call the *type* the residue 0, 4, 2, 6 of the even
letter. Type 0 pairs are exactly the U⁽⁸⁾ "8 | x" words; a pair of type 4, 2, 6 is a U⁽⁸⁾ word iff 8 | y. Since U⁽⁸⁾
fails at 45 primes ≤ 10⁸ (Z60), a proof valid for all primes must use pairs of type 4, 2 or 6 with 8 ∤ y.
(d) ("sum–product" form) If n ≡ 1 (8): U₇(n) ⟺ ∃ k ≡ 7 (mod 8), k > 0, ∃ 1 ≤ x < k with x(k − x) | n + k ⟺ ∃ k ≡ 7 (8),
k > 0, and a positive divisor m of n + k with k² − 4m a non-negative perfect square.

*Proof.* (a) If (x, y, z) is a word with 8 | xy then xz | n + x + z = xyz and 8 | xyz. Conversely, if lcm(8, xz) | n + x + z
put y = (n + x + z)/(xz); then xyz = n + x + z and 8 | xyz. If z is odd, 8 | xy; if x is odd, 8 | yz and (z, y, x) is a
word with 8 | (first)(second); if both were even then n + x + z would be odd, contradicting 8 | n + x + z. (b) n ≡ 1 (8)
and 8 | n + x + z ⟺ x + z ≡ 7 (8). (c) g = gcd(x,z): g² | n + x + z and g | x + z give g | n. x + z odd forces one even
letter; the list is x + z ≡ 7 (8). A type 0 pair (8a, z) is the word (8a, y, z), the "8 | x" half of U⁽⁸⁾ (Z60 Lemma
7(iv): targets r = 8a | f+1); for the other types the even letter e has v₂(e) ∈ {1, 2}, the word is (e, y, odd) with
v₂(y) ≥ 3 − v₂(e), and it is in U⁽⁸⁾ iff 8 | y. (d) Put k = x + z; x(k − x) = xz; the divisor m = xz of n + k has
k² − 4m = (x − z)² ≥ 0. Conversely if m | n + k, m > 0, k² − 4m = j² with j ≥ 0 then j is odd (k odd), j < k, and
x = (k − j)/2, z = (k + j)/2 are positive integers with xz = m | n + k and x + z = k ≡ 7 (8); apply (b). ∎

**Lemma 1′ (modular hyperbola / continued-fraction form).** For f ≡ 7 (8) coprime to n write a = (−n mod f),
b = (−n⁻¹ mod f) ∈ [1, f−1]. Then ab ≡ 1 (mod f), so ab ∈ {1, f+1, 2f+1, …}, and
  f is a U₇ witness of n ⟺ ab = 1 (i.e. a = b = 1, f | n + 1) or ab = f + 1.
For a ≥ 2: a | f + 1 ⟺ b = (f + 1)/a. In continued-fraction terms, with t = ⌈n/f⌉ and s = (f+1)/a:
  n/f = [t − 1; 1, s − 2, 1, a − 1]  for a ≥ 2, s ≥ 3;  n/f = [t − 1; 2, a − 1] for s = 2;  n/f = [t − 1; 1, f − 1] for a = 1.
So for a ≥ 2, s ≥ 3 the witnesses are the denominators f ≡ 7 (8) for which n/f has a simple continued fraction of
the shape [·; 1, ·, 1, ·]. Reversing the CF (a ↔ t, i.e. x ↔ z) gives the fraction n/f′ with f′ = ts − 1, the other
factor of sn + 1; f′ is itself a U₇ witness iff f′ ≡ 7 (8), i.e. iff 8 | ts.

*Proof.* ab ≡ 1 and 1 ≤ ab ≤ (f−1)² give the first line; a | f + 1 with a ≥ 2 forces b ≡ a⁻¹ ≡ (f+1)/a and
1 ≤ (f+1)/a ≤ f − 1, so b = (f+1)/a and ab = f + 1; conversely ab = f + 1 gives a | f + 1. For the CF: with
f = as − 1, (f − a)/f = [0; 1, s − 2, 1, a − 1] by direct expansion (f/(f − a) = 1 + a/(f − a); (f − a)/a = s − 2 +
(a − 1)/a; a/(a − 1) = 1 + 1/(a − 1)), valid when s − 2 ≥ 1 and a − 1 ≥ 1; the two degenerate shapes are checked the
same way; and n/f = (t − 1) + (f − a)/f since n = ft − a. ∎

Remarks. (i) The branch XY = f + 1 of the modular hyperbola XY ≡ 1 (mod f) carries τ(f+1) − 2 points in
[1, f−1]², plus the point (1, 1) on the branch XY = 1: total τ(f+1) − 1 target residues, as in Z60. A hit is the
event that (−n, −n⁻¹) mod f lies on the lowest branch. Kloosterman-type equidistribution of (a/f, b/f) predicts hit
probability ≈ (1 + log f)/f for generic f, matching (τ(f+1) − 1)/f on average — and shows why equidistribution
results (error ≫ f^{1/2}) cannot detect a single hit (§3.3). (ii) None of this is deep; the (x, z)-form without the
8-condition is (F1) of LORENZINI_W.md. The use here is to make the x ↔ z symmetry and the 2-adic bookkeeping explicit.

**Lemma 2 (small outer letters: exact failure conditions).** Let p ≡ 1 (mod 8) be prime. For a fixed value of an
*outer* letter (a pair {x, z} of Lemma 1(b) with one letter prescribed) or of the middle letter y, the U₇ condition
is a divisor condition D on one shifted integer, equivalent to a subgroup condition F on its prime factors. Each
line: [family] — [U₇ holds via this family iff D] — [fails iff F]. "Divisor" means positive divisor.
* outer letter 1: (p+1)/2 has a divisor ≡ 3 (mod 4) — fails iff (p+1)/2 ∈ S_{{1} mod 4}.
* outer letter 2: p + 2 has a divisor ≡ 5 (mod 8) [equivalently one ≡ 7 (8)] — fails iff p + 2 ∈ S_{{1,3} mod 8}.
* outer letter 4: p + 4 has a divisor ≡ 3 (mod 8) [equivalently one ≡ 3 (4)] — fails iff p + 4 ∈ S_{{1} mod 4}.
* outer letter 3, for p ≡ 1 (mod 3): (p+3)/4 has a divisor ≡ 2 (mod 3) — fails iff (p+3)/4 ∈ S_{{1} mod 3}.
* y = 2: 2p + 1 has a divisor ≡ 7 (8) — fails iff 2p + 1 ∈ S_{{1,3} mod 8}.
* y = 4: 4p + 1 has a divisor ≡ 7 (8) — fails iff 4p + 1 ∈ S_{{1} mod 4}.
* y = 8: 8p + 1 has a divisor ≡ 7 (8) — fails iff 8p + 1 ∈ S_{{1,3}} ∪ S_{{1,5}} (mod 8).
* outer letter 8 (oriented word (8, y, z)): p + 8 has a divisor ≡ 7 (8) — fails iff p + 8 ∈ S_{{1,3}} ∪ S_{{1,5}}.
* y = 1 and the oriented word (1, y, z) are implied by outer letter 1 (both need a divisor ≡ 7 (8) of (p+1)/2).
Consequence (necessary conditions, **not** equivalences): a U₇-zero prime p ≡ 1 (8) would have (p+1)/2, p + 4, 4p + 1
composed of primes ≡ 1 (4) — in particular sums of two squares — and p + 2, 2p + 1 composed of primes ≡ 1, 3 (8) — in
particular of the form u² + 2v²; and (p+3)/4 composed of primes ≡ 1 (3). The converse fails: 9 = (17+1)/2 is a
sum of two squares but has the divisor 3 ≡ 3 (4), and 17 has the word (6, 4, 1).
Check (`scripts/z68_u7f_smallshift.py`): for all 19 552 primes p ≡ 1 (8) ≤ 10⁶ and for the eight Z₇ composites,
the three predicates [pair/word exists, computed from Lemma 1(b)/(the y-form) by divisor enumeration] ⟺ D ⟺ ¬F
agree in every line (0 mismatches); 43 primes ≤ 10⁶ fail all eight lines (12721, 42169, 53089, …). This is a check of
the divisor/subgroup equivalences, not an independent enumeration of integer triples.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1(b) a pair {x, z} with z prescribed is a divisor x of p + z with x ≡ −p (mod z) and x + z ≡ 7 (8);
with c := (p + z)/x this reads c ≡ −1 (mod z) and v₂(x) + v₂(c + 1) ≥ 3 (as 8 | p + x + z = x(c + 1)).
Outer letter 1: p + 1 = 2m, m odd, m ≡ 1 (4); x + 1 ≡ 7 (8) forces x = 2x′ with x′ | m, c = m/x′ odd, and 8 | 2x′(c + 1)
⟺ c ≡ 3 (4); m has a divisor ≡ 3 (4) iff it has a prime factor ≡ 3 (4) (a product of primes ≡ 1 (4) is ≡ 1 (4)).
Outer letter 2: x odd, x | p + 2 ≡ 3 (8), x ≡ 5 (8); the cofactor of a divisor ≡ 5 is ≡ 7 and conversely; the
subgroups of (ℤ/8)^× not containing 5 (equivalently not 7, given the product ≡ 3) whose elements can multiply to 3
are only {1, 3}: hence no such divisor ⟺ all prime factors ≡ 1, 3 (8). Outer letter 4: x ≡ 3 (8), x | p + 4 ≡ 5 (8);
cofactor of a 7 is a 3; subgroups avoiding 3 and 7 that produce 5: only {1, 5}. Outer letter 3 with p ≡ 1 (3): p + 3 =
4m, m ≡ 1 (3) odd; x ≡ −p ≡ 2 (3) and x + 3 ≡ 7 (8) give x = 4x′, x′ | m, x′ ≡ 2 (3) (4 ≡ 1 mod 3); m has a divisor
≡ 2 (3) iff a prime factor ≡ 2 (3). y = 2, 4, 8: (xy − 1)(yz − 1) = py + 1 with 8 | xy; for y = 2 the factor 2x − 1 ≡ 7
(8) ⟺ 4 | x; for y = 4, 4x − 1 ≡ 7 ⟺ 2 | x; for y = 8 always; conversely a divisor d ≡ 7 (8) of py + 1 with d < py + 1
gives x = (d + 1)/y, z = ((py+1)/d + 1)/y integers (both factors ≡ −1 mod y) with 8 | xy = d + 1. The subgroup analysis
is as before (2p + 1 ≡ 3, 4p + 1 ≡ 5, 8p + 1 ≡ 1 (mod 8); for residue 1 both {1,3} and {1,5} occur). Outer letter 8,
oriented word (8, y, z): f = 8y − 1 ≡ 7 (8), f | p + 8; conversely any divisor f ≡ 7 (8) of p + 8 (f < p + 8 as
p + 8 ≡ 1) gives y = (f+1)/8, z = (p+8)/f. The norm-form consequences are classical (an integer all of whose prime
factors are ≡ 1 (4) is a sum of two squares; ≡ 1, 3 (8) ⟹ u² + 2v²). ∎

Reading (Assessment + heuristic). The expected number of *prime* witnesses q ≤ Q is Σ_{q ≡ 7 (8)} (τ(q+1) − 1)/q

cannot exclude an eventual prime-witness theorem with the 231 as exceptions. The N^{0.27} growth is the evidence that
A is the right expectation.

## 3. Attack log — what was tried, and the exact point where each line breaks

Labels: PROVED for the negative statements as stated; Assessment where marked.

### 3.1 One-shift mechanisms are sieve statements; bounded outer letters form a finite covering
For a fixed outer letter z the condition is "p + z has a divisor c ≡ −1 (mod z) with v₂((p+z)/c) + v₂(c + 1) ≥ 3"
(proof of Lemma 2). The union over all pairs with x, z ≤ Z is a finite union of congruence classes
p ≡ −x − z (mod lcm(8, xz)); p ≡ 1 (mod 8·lcm{xz : x, z ≤ Z}) avoids all of them (the only coprime pairs with
xz | x + z + 1 are (1,1), (1,2), (2,3), none with x + z ≡ 7 (8)). Hence **(PROVED)** no proof can use only pairs with
both outer letters bounded: such a proof fails on a positive-density set of primes (Dirichlet). With one bounded
letter the condition is a genuine divisor condition (Lemma 2); heuristically (Assessment, not proved) each such line
fails for a proportion ≍ (log p)^{−1/2} of primes, and the lines' failures are positively correlated at hard primes
(Z60 §3 data). The 43 primes ≤ 10⁶ failing all eight Lemma 2 lines are the start of that tail.

### 3.2 Rules defined on a whole class of representation data are impossible — PROVED (narrow statement)
Lemma 2 says a hard prime carries representations p + 1 = u² + v², p + 2 = a² + 2b², …. A natural hope is to build the
word from such data. The following excludes the simplest version.

**Proposition 3.2.** Let N(u, v) = u² + v² − 1. There is no rule (polynomial or otherwise) that assigns to *every*
integer pair (u, v) in some class 𝒞 mod 8 with u, v odd a triple of positive integers (x, y, z) with xyz − x − z = N(u, v)
and 8 | xy.
*Proof.* The family (u, v, m) = (A² + A − 1, 2A + 1, A² + A + 1), A ∈ ℤ, satisfies u² + v² − 1 = m² with m odd; A = 0,…,7
give (u, v) ≡ (7,1), (1,3), (5,5), (3,7), (3,1), (5,3), (1,5), (7,7) mod 8, and the sign changes u ↦ −u, v ↦ −v and the
swap u ↔ v (all preserving the quadric) fill the remaining odd classes (16/16, `z68_u7f_prop36check.py`). At such a
point the rule would give a U₇ word of the odd square m², contradicting U₇(m²) = 0 (Z60 Lemma 7(ii)). ∎

What this does and does not exclude. It excludes rules that must work on all of 𝒞 — e.g. polynomial identities in
(u, v) — because the class contains squares; polynomiality is not used. It does *not* exclude rules required to
succeed only when N(u, v) is prime, or rules with further admissibility conditions on (u, v); a polynomial rule of
that kind is *not* excluded by a Zariski-density argument either, since integrality/positivity of the rule at the
square points would also be needed (e.g. (4, (u² + v² + 6)/12, 3) is a valid rule on u ≡ v ≡ 3 (mod 6), u, v odd, and
fails integrality at (1, 1)). For the other norm forms of Lemma 2 and for finer moduli the analogous statement would need integer
points of the corresponding ternary quadric in prescribed classes; we did not prove that and do not use it.
(Schinzel's theorem that no polynomial identity in p covers a class containing squares is the one-variable analogue.)

### 3.3 Counting / equidistribution cannot see one hit
By Lemma 1′ the hit at f is the event that (−p mod f, −p⁻¹ mod f) lies on the branch XY = f + 1 of the modular
hyperbola (or is the point (1, 1)). The pairs (a/f, ā/f) equidistribute in [0,1]² with error O(f^{1/2+ε}) (Weil–Kloosterman), while the
branch carries τ(f+1) − 2 points among φ(f) (≈ log f on average): an event of probability ≈ (log f)/f. Averaged over p the main term
Σ_f τ(f+1)/f ≍ log² F is fine; pointwise in p the error per modulus exceeds the target by f^{1/2}. The square
obstruction shows this is not an artefact: a main-term-plus-error count whose main term does not vanish at n = m²
is wrong by its whole main term there. Writing the count as Σ_f ½(1 − (p/f))·1[(−p mod f) | f+1] is a tautology (the
indicator already forces (p/f) = −1), not a reduction to a character sum with a p-independent weight. (Assessment;
this is the usual B2 wall, seen from the symmetric form.)

### 3.4 Descent / transfer: none found in the natural coordinates
The hit set T_f = {−r mod f : r | f+1} is invariant under n ↦ n⁻¹ (mod f) (Z60 Prop E). It is not invariant under
n ↦ n + c or n ↦ (n − a)/b in general; multiplicative invariances exist for particular f (T₇ = {3, 5, 6} is
invariant under n ↦ 2n mod 7). A necessary condition for n ↦ cn to preserve T_f is (c/f) = 1 (T_f ⊂ nonresidues);
this is not sufficient (c = 4: 19 ∈ T₂₃ but 4·19 ≡ 7 ∉ T₂₃). No nontrivial uniform-in-f symmetry beyond inversion
was found; we did not prove there is none. Words of n
and n ± f correspond (z ↦ z ± 1): periodicity, not descent. The divisor-complement move (x, z) ↦ (x, (p + x)/z) on
pairs with xz | p + x + z stays in the solution set iff x | p − 1; it can leave U₇ entirely (p = 17: {4, 3} ↦ {4, 7},
sum 11 ≢ 7 (8)), and on the families of Lemma 2 it is just
the complementary-divisor equivalence ("divisor ≡ 3 (8) ⟺ divisor ≡ 7 (8)" at outer letter 4), so it produces nothing
beyond Lemma 2. No descent to a smaller n was found, and the natural reformulation suggests none: an integer n is a
U₇-zero iff n lies in the compact null set 𝒰 = ∩_f (ℤ/f ∖ T_f) of the profinite completion, which contains the
profinite squares and much more; "p ∉ 𝒰" is a statement about one integer avoiding a null set. (PROVED where stated;
Assessment for the last two sentences.)

### 3.5 Controlled factorisations give only the trivial word — PROVED, routine
The shifted values of p with factorisations controlled by primality are multiples of p and cyclotomic values
Φ_n(p) (whose prime factors are ≡ 1 (mod n) or divide n). We checked the y-form candidates yp + 1 ∈ {(p−1)² (y = p−2),
(p+1)² (y = p+2), (ap ± 1)² (y = a²p ± 2a), Φ₃(p) = p² + p + 1 (y = p+1)}: the divisors ≡ −1 (mod lcm(8, y)) are forced
to be ≈ p and the only solution is the trivial word (1, p + 2, 1), which has 8 | xy iff p ≡ 6 (8). Shifts r > p + 1 are
useless: c ≡ −1 (mod r), c | p + r < 2r − 1 forces c = r − 1, so r − 1 | p + 1 with r − 1 ≥ p + 1, i.e. r = p + 2 and
the word is (p + 2, 1, 2), which has 8 ∤ xy in either orientation — no U₇ word at all.

### 3.6 The smooth-part reduction: where the non-congruence input is located — PROVED
Fix R ≥ 1 and y ≥ R + 1, and let M = M_{R,y} := ∏_{q ≤ y} q^{e_q} with e_q := ⌊log_q(8R(R+1))⌋ + 1. For r ≤ R write
L_r := lcm(8, r), and for odd n write n + r = s_r(n)·Q_r(n) with s_r(n) the y-smooth part.

**Proposition 3.6.** Let p ≡ 1 (mod M) be prime, p > M. Then for every r ≤ R: s_r(p) = 1 + r, Q_r(p) ≡ 1 (mod L_r),
and a U₇ pair {x, z} of p with an **outer** letter equal to r exists only if Q_r(p) is composite; in that case the
other outer letter (a divisor of p + r) is e·g with e | 1 + r and g a proper divisor of Q_r(p), 1 < g < Q_r(p) — so
both g and Q_r(p)/g have all prime factors > y. (The middle letter y = r is *not* covered: p = 10369 ≡ 1 (mod 5184),
R = 2, y = 3, has Q₂(p) = 3457 prime and the word (16, 2, 335).)
*Proof.* For q ≤ y, v_q(p + r) = v_q(1 + r) since p + r ≡ 1 + r (mod q^{e_q}) and q^{e_q} > R + 1 ≥ 1 + r; hence
s_r(p) = 1 + r (y-smooth as y ≥ R + 1), and Q_r(p) = 1 + kM/(1 + r) with L_r | M/(1 + r) (e_q ≥ v_q(L_r) + v_q(1 + r)
since q^{e_q} > 8R(R+1) ≥ L_r(1 + r)), so Q_r(p) ≡ 1 (mod L_r). If Q_r(p) is prime, the divisors of p + r are e and
eQ_r(p), e | 1 + r, with eQ_r(p) ≡ e (mod L_r), so every divisor of p + r is congruent mod L_r to a divisor of 1 + r.
A pair {x, r} is a divisor x of p + r with x ≡ −p ≡ −1 (mod r) and x + r ≡ 7 (8), i.e. x in a prescribed class mod
L_r; the same class condition on a divisor of 1 + r would be a U₇ pair of n = 1, impossible (1 is an odd square). If
Q_r(p) is composite, the divisors not of the form e, eQ_r(p) are exactly e·g with g a proper divisor. ∎
Checked (`z68_u7f_prop36check.py`, R = 4, y = 5, M = 38 880 000, the 7 primes p ≡ 1 (M) below 2·10⁹): all pairs with
an outer letter ≤ 4 split the rough part as asserted; 0 violations.

Reading. Z60 Prop D: a positive-density set of primes has no witness f ≤ X. Prop 3.6 says what a witness *with a
small outer letter* must look like at such primes: it splits the y-rough part of p + r into two parts whose residues
mod L_r are right — it uses the exact class mod lcm(8, r) of large prime factors of p + r, not merely their Jacobi
symbol (which is forced anyway: (−r/q) = (p/q) for q | p + r). No known tool controls the class mod 8r of a specific
large prime factor of a specific integer; this is where every attempt here stopped. §2 adds that at the hard primes
the rescue is typically *not* a single large prime factor in the class −1 (none exists at 231 primes ≤ 10⁹) but a
divisor with two distinct prime factors.

## 5. Growth of the least witness (EVIDENCE; extrapolation conditional on a Poisson model)
max_{p ≤ N} f_min(p): 21383 at N = 10⁷ (p = 2 031 121); in [2¹⁶, 2¹⁷) at N = 10⁹ (two primes; histograms in

### 9.3 Growing k in Lemma 1(d); cancellation across f with square-sensitive weights — negative
Lemma 1(d) organises words by k = x + z: p + k must have a divisor of the form x(k − x). For a fixed quotient
y = (p + k)/(x(k − x)) this is the y-form (y = 1: k² − 4(p + k) = □, i.e. factorisations of 4(p+1); y = 2:
factorisations of 2p + 1; …), so "growing k" is the union over y of the y-form divisor conditions — a re-indexing,
not a new mechanism. For cancellation: the natural square-sensitive weight is ½(1 − (n/f)) restricted to
gcd(n, f) = 1, which is 1 on every hit and 0 at n = m² (coprime f); the weighted expectation
Σ_{f, (f,p)=1} ½(1 − (p/f))(τ(f+1) − 1)/f = ½Σ (τ(f+1) − 1)/f − ½Σ (p/f)(τ(f+1) − 1)/f has a p-independent weight
in its character sum, and one *expects* (heuristically; we quote no theorem for character sums with the shifted
divisor weight τ(f+1)) cancellation there for F large enough, leaving the main term ≍ log² F.
This displayed sum is a heuristic proxy, not a conditional expectation (the conditional hit probability given
(p/f) = −1 on units is 2(τ(f+1) − 1)/φ(f), i.e. w_f · 2(τ(f+1) − 1)/φ(f) with w_f = (1 − (p/f))/2, not
w_f (τ(f+1) − 1)/f). The finding is simply negative: we obtained no pointwise lower bound for N(p; F) from any weight
we tried, and the per-modulus indicator is not a function of the single symbol (p/f). This does not exclude methods
exploiting correlations of the character vector ((p/f))_f across moduli, nor is it a theorem that variance methods
give only almost-all results; the direction remains open.
