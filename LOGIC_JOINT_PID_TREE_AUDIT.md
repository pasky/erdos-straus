# Joint PID / Dirichlet / two-squares / NR / Lagrange countermodel: audit of the branching step

**Verdict: accept with three qualifications.** The finite-tree conditioning works. It needs no uniform Lang–Weil constant over stages, no measure on infinite paths and no rational parametrization of an inert circle. (1) Enumerate prime **elements**, not ideals modulo rational associates. (2) Only rational-stage old primes must stay prime: a serviced inert prime deliberately splits upstairs. (3) Force Dirichlet output units unconditionally only on the good finite prefixes; impose the conditional rule in the tail. The witness modulus for NR at the distinguished prime cannot be standard.

**PROVED relative to the stage lemmas** `LOGIC_ES_RIGIDITY_NR.md` and `LOGIC_CIRCLE_AUDIT.md` (versions at `496bca130`), the Z73 floor/row and PID descent, and the classical polynomial square-class obstruction: there is **one countable external PID** satisfying IOpen, the internal unbounded Dirichlet sentence, universal TS, universal NR and universal Lagrange (four squares), with a positive infinite prime `P=X²+Y²`, square unit at every standard place, `P≡1 mod 840`, and `¬ES(P)`. One may instead specify any **one** primitive unit-square class modulo a standard integer. The model has PD by external factorization, Bézout, and `E(−1)` by TS and Bézout. No inference to standard ES, collection, bounded induction, or simultaneous primes in tuples.

## 1. Fix the countable tree **before** specifying any marks

The root is the ordered norm plane `ℚ[X,Y]`, with successively dominant positive variables. Each vertex carries a finite type algebra `A`, its ordered field `F`, a compatible countable ordered real closure, and all ancestor inclusions. Nodes are histories, not isomorphism classes; no requirement to glue marks when distinct descendants happen to be isomorphic. Every node has one child except a genuine inert-prime TS task, when it has two (identity/accept). Trivial operations are identity edges, not infinite searches. Depth is the number of clock ticks, including skipped tasks. Thus depth ≤n is **finite** even though the list of potential inputs at a node is infinite.

One precise fair clock enumerates pairs `(j,k)` by increasing `j+k`, one per tick, beginning at `(0,0)`; the current depth is ≥`j`. On each path consult task `k` in an enumeration fixed at its depth-`j` ancestor. Include: real algebraic targets of the ancestor field; binary ideal inputs; progression triples `(a,m,H)`; four-square requests for **every positive infinite element**, not just primes; and separate NR and TS requests for every positive rational-stage prime **as an element of the stage**, including all positive rational associates. Use type tags and no-ops for inapplicable requests. Repetitions can be included. Compute the relevant stage gcd, primality, Gaussian splitting and order bounds at **service time**. Real closures embed along each edge by the ordered inclusions. Floor steps that are already approximated do nothing; decide using the current ordered algebra, not integer-part predicates of the future PID. On a nontrivial floor step choose once an allowed fresh residue cut; similarly fix the fresh circle angular order, row chart/orientation and any other choices separately at each node. This is an external existence construction, not a computable tree or a first-order definition of the branch.

Rows use primitive entries (neither zero, or bypass trivial cases); for prefix sampling select one nonzero pivot. Progressions with `m>0` and coprime `(a,m)` in the stage use a fresh `Z` above the old field, `Q=a+mZ` (including `a=0` when `m` is a unit). A positive rational-stage prime `s` gets the NR extension `DH=V²+s`; do **not** do this for all positives including squares: at `s=1` upstairs the quotient by `D` is reducible, invalidating the prime-`D` proof of Gaussian factoriality. For TS, if `s` is still inert in `A[i]`, predeclare both the identity and `X'²+Y'²=s` children. If already split there is no circle edge; normalization is done at the end. Nothing here tests `s∈R` or a local norm at tree-definition time.

**Stage interface.** All old prime elements on the rational (`A`, not `A[i]`) side persist and units on the two sides remain `ℚ*`, `ℚ(i)*`. Both sides are factorial. For a rational row, a common Gaussian prime divisor of the two entries would lie over a rational height-one divisor (finite étale constant extension), contradicting primitivity. For NR, `V²+s` is irreducible even over `F(i)`: otherwise `s` would be a square in `F(i)` and, since `s>0`, in `F`, impossible at the prime divisor `s`. Localizing at `D` and reducing old primes by the `D`-saturated Laurent presentation gives the claimed NR invariants. For the inert circle, upstairs `UV=s` in the new variables makes `s` a product, **not** a persistent Gaussian prime. The rational special quotient at `s` is nevertheless a domain by inertness. Future rational primes remain prime regardless of this split. Absolute geometric integrality of the stage varieties used below follows also without factoriality: induct up a dense chart over the old geometric function field (line, row line, NR chart `D≠0`, or the geometrically integral conic with nonzero right side). The chart is dense after scalar extension: the row charts cover; the NR Laurent embedding survives field extension; for the conic use its monic normal form. No assumption of everywhere smooth stages. The extra unconditional four-square edge, with these same invariants, is proved in §5.

## 2. Finite-tree lemma: compatible joint residues, with a uniform bound **only for fixed depth**

Fix a finite rooted prefix and a finite list of nonzero elements at each node. Work away from finitely many rational primes depending on this *entire finite datum*. Spread generators, equations, chosen chart pivots and these functions over a localization of `ℤ`. For a fraction specify a nonzero numerator and denominator. Spread the chosen opens and impose their denominators as additional unit tests. At a row also require the parent's chosen pivot nonzero, at an accepted conic its target `s` nonzero, at each NR edge `D≠0`, and at each progression child `a+mZ≠0`. These are proper bad loci: `s` is a nonzero function, `m≠0`, `Z` free. An element that vanishes identically on a **special** parent fiber need not have any good child there. We count this as a bad outcome; we never assume a bound uniform over every parent.

Here is an explicit joint law for odd `q` outside the exceptional set. Sample only **once** at a common ancestor. With its residue fixed, make independent choices for distinct nontrivial children. A failed chart transition goes to a cemetery state inherited by that child's descendants; it never calls for a new draw at a shared ancestor. Identity children copy the parent. Rules before conditioning:

* At the root draw a uniform pair whose norm is a nonzero square in `𝔽_q`. Each nonzero norm has `q−χ_q(−1)` preimages, by the split product or finite-field norm, so each allowed root atom has mass `2/((q−1)(q−χ_q(−1)))`. Without this initial conditioning the square event tends to **1/2**, not to one.
* At a line (floor or progression) draw uniformly on `𝔽_q`; at a row use a uniform free coordinate if the designated pivot is nonzero, otherwise cemetery. On the NR chart draw `(D,V)` uniformly on `𝔽_q^× × 𝔽_q`, then set `H=(V²+s)/D`.
* At a circle with `s≠0` draw uniformly among its `q−χ_q(−1)` solutions for **this** parent, otherwise cemetery. Such a conic always has a smooth residual point at odd `q`. No `F`-point on the generic conic is implied. At a four-square edge sample the smooth residual fiber, even at `s=0` (§5).

If a stage has dimension `d`, the unconditional mass of any one live residue tuple there is `≤C_node q^(−d)`. Induct on depth: a tuple determines its parent, and edge atom bounds are `1`, `1/q`, `1/(q(q−1))`, `1/(q−χ_q(−1))`, or `O(q⁻³)` on a four-square fiber (§5), matching dimension increments `0,1,2,1,3` as appropriate. Siblings need not be marginally independent. For each proper bad locus on this `d`-dimensional chart the number of residual points is `O_prefix(q^(d−1))`: spread the characteristic-zero dimension bound off finitely many primes and apply the bounded-degree point **upper** bound (e.g. finite affine cover and Noether normalization on components after clearing denominators). A first cemetery transition is charged at its **live parent** to one of the pivot/target loci. The union bound on live bad loci and first failures now gives success simultaneously on **all nodes** with probability `1−O_prefix(1/q)`. No appeal to lower bounds on the number of points of arbitrary or singular fibers. In particular this argument does not multiply conditional success estimates for different paths or require that two siblings choose independent values for their common parameters.

For each depth `n` list all nodes to that depth and the first `n` nonzero elements from fixed enumerations at **each** such node, including numerator/denominator tests as necessary. Use nested test lists. Choose increasing thresholds `b_n→∞` so all primes `q>b_n` are good for that finite prefix and give positive joint success probability; put the finitely prescribed primes (including 2 and those of 840 or the prescribed modulus) outside the prefix regime. For each remaining `q` set `n(q)=max{n:q>b_n}` if nonempty; otherwise use no prefix. Sample the **finite joint law at this place** conditioned on simultaneous success, or just fix a single successful outcome. The conditioning can bias a shared parent. It need not agree with a law conditioned at a different depth: only **one depth is used per prime**. Across primes no probability argument is needed. For a fixed node and fixed nonzero function, its test is passed at all sufficiently large primes, before anyone selects a branch. This is the required universal quantifier over potential paths; a separately constructed almost-sure pathwise event on an uncountable set of paths would not suffice.

## 3. Lift once, then choose the actual branch

Success residues on each finite tree lift to compatible field embeddings into `ℚ_q`, not merely algebra homomorphisms with possible kernel. Lift the root pair successively transcendently in its prescribed cylinders. At an empty-prefix place take integral opens near `(1,0)` so the norm is a unit square and has the required congruence; for a different square class use a unit root of that class in the first coordinate instead. At 2 use congruences sufficiently deep for squarehood (`1 mod 8`). No *exact* value of the norm has been fixed. At a line/row/NR extension choose the free variable(s) successively transcendental over the **already embedded countable field**, retaining any prescribed residues. Solve for the constrained coordinates on their charts. On a circle use a smooth residual point: e.g. if the second coordinate is a unit at odd `q`, choose the **first** coordinate transcendental in its cylinder and Hensel lift the second for the **actual lifted parent value of `s`**. Over the old field in the first variable `T`, `s−T²` is nonsquare even geometrically as a rational function (two simple roots), so this defines an embedding of the conic function field. Swap coordinates if needed. Sibling fields need not be linearly disjoint inside `ℚ_q`; they need only extend exactly the same parent embedding. Thus there is no demand that a countable nonrational field admit arbitrary residue assignments.

One can dispense entirely with an **infinite** cemetery law. Store the above lifted finite tree at each `q`. Starting at the real root, traverse **one common path**. At a node reached, the old field already has a marking at every place. Use the stored extension where the chosen edge belongs to the stored prefix; at all other places extend the actual current embedding as follows. From the root on, field embeddings never change on old elements.

* Lines/rows need only a field embedding in this tail. The pivot need not be a local unit but is nonzero in the field. At NR choose `D` in a unit open (`3 mod 4` at 2), and `V` integral, with field independence. If `s` is not integral at that place it is harmless for `H` not to be integral. At a progression insist `Z∈ℤ_q`. For integral `a,m` with at least one unit insist `a+mZ` be a unit: at most one residue is forbidden if `m` is a unit, and none if `m` is a nonunit. Otherwise impose no output unit condition (if both are nonunits, such a condition is impossible). For a stored prefix the unconditional unit test already implies the rule.
* At an inert TS fork **accept globally iff** `s` is a unit at every mark and `φ₂(s)≡1 mod 4`; otherwise skip at **all** places. These predicates on old elements have definite values now; there is no test on a field not yet constructed. If accepted, integral norm lifts exist at every place. At odd tail places use the same smooth residual norm argument. At 2 the derivative need **not** be a unit: choose the free even coordinate transcendental in `0 mod 4` if `s≡1 mod 8`, in `2 mod 4` if `s≡5 mod 8`. Subtract its square and take a square root of the remaining `1 mod 8` unit. Both coordinates are integral, and the transcendental coordinate again guarantees injectivity. A false predicate requires no local point on the unused accept edge at exceptional places.

For any `g≠0` in the actual union, take a fixed finite stage containing it. Its node/test is eventually in *every* stored prefix, including the prefix's root-to-that-node history. So `v_q(g)=0` for all sufficiently large `q`, **even though the branch depended on the marks**. Fractions have the same property by subtraction of valuations. Merely almost-everywhere integrality would not suffice here. Prescribed everywhere-integral coordinates include the initial `X,Y`, each Dirichlet `Z`, each NR `D,V`, and both accepted circle coordinates. For a Lagrange edge all four coordinates are integral at every place where its input is integral, not necessarily at other places. This is independent of order-theoretic relations among parameters: the field embeddings are not required to preserve the real order.

## 4. Check the joint conclusion element by element

Write `A∞` for the path union and `R={a∈A∞:φ_q(a)∈ℤ_q for all q}`. Bounded elements of `A∞` are exactly `ℚ` by the stage order lemmas. Every member of the real closure of `Frac(A∞)` is already in a finite ancestor's real closure (algebraicity involves finitely many coefficients); fairness supplies a bounded-distance approximation. A rational translation clears finitely many local fractional parts and integer adjustment gives floors, hence IOpen. Rational stages have finite permanent prime factorizations and serviced rows. By parsimony, multiplying each rational-stage prime by a rational scalar normalizes **all** its valuations to zero. Such a normalized prime descends prime; the remaining scalar of an integral factorization belongs to `ℤ`. Standard primes have residue ring `𝔽_q` since the kernel of evaluation mod `q` is `qR`. An `A∞` Bézout row for a residual coprime pair, primitive at all marks, corrects to an `R` row by one rational syzygy parameter at finitely many bad primes. Thus `R` is an atomic Bézout domain and an external PID. These arguments require no factoriality of an infinite Gaussian union whose inert primes might subsequently split.

* **Classification/scheduling trap.** A positive nonstandard `R` prime `p` is itself a positive rational-stage prime in some finite stage, with valuations **zero at all marks**. Indeed the preceding integral factorization leaves just one nonconstant normalized rational-stage prime up to sign, and all rational scalars already occur in each stage. Thus the exact element `p`, not just a rational associate, gets both tasks along its path. A witness `V²+s≡0 mod D` does not formally give a root for `−cs` when `c` is a nonsquare. Likewise normalizing `c s` can change its norm status. Enumerating all actual elements avoids both gaps. Standard positive primes ≡1 mod 4 have ordinary integer TS and NR witnesses, independent of service.
* **NR.** For exact `s=p`, the NR stage gives `D>0`, `D≡3 mod 4` in `R` (divide `D−3` by 4), and `H=(V²+p)/D∈R` by unit marks. `D` is a new rational-stage prime not associated to `p` (reduce `p` modulo `D`), hence a permanent rational prime and already normalized. It is Bézout-coprime to `p` in the PID. This even gives a positive **prime** witness modulus. Repeating tasks gives such witnesses arbitrarily high for each nonstandard prime. At the distinguished `P` a *standard* positive `d≡3 mod 4` cannot work: choose a standard prime `q≡3 mod 4` dividing `d`; `−P` is then a nonsquare mod `q`.
* **TS.** For exact `s=p≡1 mod 4` the fork predicate necessarily passes if inert (`p−1∈4R`). The accepted coordinates are in `R` and realize TS. If split at service, `LOGIC_CIRCLE_AUDIT.md` §3 factors in that **finite Gaussian UFD**, corrects the positive rational scalar by a Gaussian norm (inert valuations even) and then corrects split-place valuation imbalances by a norm-one rational Gaussian scalar. The corrections have finite support by parsimony of the two real coordinates. Unit norm gives integral coordinates at inert places and at 2. Thus there is a representation in `R` in this case as well. `E(−1)` follows: not both coordinates are multiples of `p`, and a nonmultiple is invertible mod `p` by Bézout.
* **Dirichlet.** For actual `R`-Bézout-coprime `a,m`, no stage prime divides both (persistence would contradict their final identity), and locally at least one is a unit. The task yields `Q=a+mZ>max(H,2)` primitive linear and rational-stage prime, of valuation zero at every mark. Thus `Q` is prime **as written**, `Q−a=mZ` in `R`. No rescaling of a progression witness.
* **ES.** `P` is an initial rational prime and already normalized. At each last step positive ES triples at an old target descend: line rigidity for floor/progression/row; two applications for NR using the Laurent-intersection lemma between them; at a circle the nonconstant fixed Laurent polynomials on the Gaussian generic fiber have a pole at infinity, hence their reciprocal terms disappear there. Positivity rules out any such omitted term. At the four-square edge use degree valuation as in §5. Induct to the plane, then binary rigidity gives a triple in `ℚ[P]`. If its coefficients have common denominator `M`, its integral values at the marks imply integral values on the entire primitive progression `n≡P mod M`. Its coordinates are positive for large positive `n`, contradicting the polynomial square-class obstruction, since `P` is a square unit at every place. NR witnesses are **not** specialized in this argument. Requiring that square specialization preserve arbitrary witness tuples would in fact contradict the NR conclusion.

## 5. PROVED: unconditional Lagrange edge, including nonunit and nonintegral inputs

For any positive infinite old `s` put `B=A[x₁,…,x₄]/(Σxᵢ²−s)`; no primality or adelic hypothesis on `s`. This is one deterministic edge, not another fork. The exclusion of bounded positive inputs from the **stage** matters: if `s=1`, the new real coordinates are bounded nonconstants. Finite elements of the final `R` are integers and have ordinary Lagrange representations (zero included).

**Algebra.** Over `C=A[i]` a linear change gives `E=C[U,V,W,T]/(UV+WT−s)`. This sits in `C[U,U⁻¹,W,T]` by substitution for `V` (primitive linear relation over the UFD, or monomial reduction). The quotient by `U` is `(C[W,T]/(WT−s))[V]`, a domain for **every nonzero** `s`, not only a prime: embed by `T=s/W`. Hence `U` is a nonunit prime, `E[1/U]` is factorial, and Nagata gives factoriality of `E`. Any unit of `E` is `aU^j` on the Laurent chart, `a∈ℚ(i)*`, and both it and its inverse have zero valuation at `U`, forcing `j=0`. Constant units and finite étale Gaussian divisor descent as in `LOGIC_CIRCLE_AUDIT.md` §1 give factoriality of `B` and units `ℚ*`. For each old rational (also Gaussian) prime `r`, after reduction mod `r` the monic normal form injects into the quadric over `Frac(A/r)` (respectively `Frac(C/r)`). Over any algebraic closure in characteristic zero its degree-two leading form has rank 4, whereas a product of two affine linear forms has leading rank ≤2. Thus the quotient is an integral nonzero algebra **also when `r|s`**: old primes persist. The same observation gives geometrically integral generic fiber; the extension has relative dimension three.

**Order.** Write `h=√s` in the old ordered real closure. In a fresh ordered extension choose three bounded `z_j` in `(1/8,1/4)` whose simultaneous residues are algebraically independent over the old real-closed residue field (countable; take independent real cuts and the Gauss extensions). Set `x_j=h z_j` for `j≤3`, `x₄=h√(1−Σz_j²)`. Write a nonzero element in the unique monic normal form `f(x₁,x₂,x₃)+x₄g(x₁,x₂,x₃)`. Divide its evaluated expression by the largest absolute coefficient magnitude **after weighting each monomial by `h` to its total degree**. Its residue is `f₀(ζ)+√(1−Σζ_j²)g₀(ζ)` with at least one nonzero polynomial by the normalization. This cannot vanish: `1−ΣZ_j²` is nonsquare in the residue rational function field (irreducible, rank-three leading form). The map is therefore injective and the magnitude has that of the maximum up to a finite noninfinitesimal factor. Any positive-degree term makes the maximum infinite since `h` is infinite and a nonzero old coefficient in `A` is noninfinitesimal. Bounded elements remain exactly `ℚ`.

**ES descent.** Filter `B` by degree in the four new variables, giving old coefficients degree zero. The associated graded ring is `A[x₁,…,x₄]/(Σxᵢ²)` (the leading ideal of this single relation is generated by its leading form). It is a domain by the same rank test and monicity. Hence minus the degree defines a valuation on the fraction field, trivial on `F=Frac(A)`; degree zero in `B` means membership in `A`. In `Σ1/w_j=4/n` at an old positive target, terms for positive-degree denominators vanish in the valuation residue field. The remaining terms and `4/n` are old constants, on which reduction is injective. Their sum is therefore already the identity in `F`; at the ordered point any omitted positive reciprocal would make the original sum strictly larger. Thus none was omitted. This is a valuation argument, not a real point at infinity on a positive definite sphere.

**Uniform prefix transitions.** For odd `q`, sample uniformly from `Σxᵢ²=t` in `𝔽_q⁴`, excluding only the origin when `t=0`. Whatever the parent `t`, there are at least `(q−2)(q−1)²` smooth choices: split `t=b+(t−b)` with both summands nonzero and represent each by two squares in `q−χ_q(−1)` ways. This proves the `O(q⁻³)` atom bound even at a parent with a nonunit input. The exact counts are `q³−q` for `t≠0`, `q³+q²−q−1` for the smooth part at zero (convolve the two-square fiber sizes). A nonzero coordinate supplies a unit derivative; lift the other three in their prescribed cylinders successively algebraically independently, then solve for this coordinate by Hensel at the actual value of `s`. The monic quadratic defines a field extension of the rational function field in the three free coordinates: `s−Σ_{j≤3}T_j²` is nonsquare by the rank-three test. Thus this is a field embedding. No large-prime condition on `v_q(s)` was needed; one could also omit the proper locus `s=0` in a fixed prefix, but that shortcut does not justify tail lifts.

**All tail places, especially 2.** Every `s_q∈ℤ_q` is a sum of four integral squares: the image of compact `ℤ_q⁴` is closed and contains the dense nonnegative ordinary integers by the classical Lagrange theorem. If `s_q` is not integral, first represent `q^(2k)s_q` integrally and rescale by `q^(−k)`. A nonzero stage `s` never maps to exact zero by field injectivity. Hence at any chosen representation a partial derivative `2x_j` is **nonzero in the field**. The local implicit-function theorem (equivalently, Hensel with sufficiently deep perturbations compared with `v_q(2x_j)`) gives an open neighborhood in the three free variables and an analytic lift. Shrink it inside integral coordinate balls whenever the original point was integral. Choose those three variables successively transcendental over the old embedded field and previous choices: the preceding irreducibility test again gives a field embedding. At `q=2`, even `s_q∈8ℤ₂` is legitimate: all integral representations then have all coordinates even, so a *primitive residual* lifting condition would be false. The neighborhood radius need not be uniform across places. These rules require no adjustment of any old mark. Thus an actual positive infinite `s∈R` gets one representation with all four coordinates in `R`; at nonintegral places of other scheduled inputs only field-valued coordinates are required. All new elements still obey parsimony by the prefix proof. This proves Lag without altering TS selection, NR, progression units, or the ES rigidity conclusion.
