**Target and scope.** NR means: for every positive prime `s≡1 (4)` there exist `d>0`, `d≡3 (4)`, with `(d,s)=1` and `d | v²+s` for some `v`. TS means every such `s` is a sum of two squares of ring elements. The point is to arrange **both universal axioms**, not merely their instances at a single distinguished prime. This is one-sided nonprovability in weak arithmetic; neither a counterexample in ℕ nor independence from PA follows. The theorem is the PID construction below; the non-PID "Variant" at the end is only a CONDITIONAL sketch, not a second proved construction (z74 Opus xreview fix). In the PID construction the NR moduli can be nonstandard; do not attribute standard test moduli at its square-marked prime to that construction.

## Algebra and the obstruction (used by the PID construction; the variant sketch would reuse it)

Start with `A₀=ℚ[X,Y]`, `P=X²+Y²` positive infinite. Order the variables at successive infinite scales and maintain a countable finite-type tower with `A,A[i]` factorial, units ℚ* and ℚ(i)*, respectively, and no bounded nonconstant elements of `A`. Service polynomial integer-part tasks, coprime rows, and internal Dirichlet tasks as in `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` and `LOGIC_PID_DIRICHLET_AUDIT.md`. A row remains factorial over the Gaussian constants: a coprime rational row has no common height-one zero after finite étale constant extension and the same Nagata proof applies there. The quotient by each old rational prime remains a nonzero domain. Dirichlet uses a primitive linear polynomial and does not change the geometry of a polynomial stage.

**ES rigidity.** On a polynomial fibre over a field, evaluate the reciprocal identity at infinity. Reciprocals of nonconstant polynomial coordinates vanish there. The constant coordinates would already account for the entire sum; positivity in the given order forbids any omitted terms. Thus a positive triple at an old target in any polynomial or row stage belongs to the old algebra (for a row use the intersection of the two charts). In the norm plane base every positive triple at `P` has polynomial coordinates in `ℚ[P]`, not just `ℚ[X,Y]`: the positive region propagates over the punctured real plane; a least-degree denominator `x` has degree two, is positive definite in its leading form and is irreducible over ℚ (a real linear factor would vanish off the origin). Put `L=4x−P`; from `(Ly−Px)(Lz−Px)=P²x²` and positivity, `Ly−Px=kP^i x^j`, `k>0`. Modulo `L`, `x=P/4`, so `L` divides the **nonzero** polynomial `k4^(−j)P^(i+j)+P²/4`. Divisors over ℚ of polynomials in `P` lie in `ℚ[P]`: over ℚbar write `P=uv`; `uv−c` is irreducible for `c≠0`, and at zero the two factors are interchanged by conjugation. This gives `x∈ℚ[P]` and then `y,z∈ℚ[P]`. Details: `LOGIC_NORM_BASE_NR_AUDIT.md`. There is also a **shorter proof**, valid for any finite number of positive summands: regard the base itself as `ℚ[S][X,Y]/(X²+Y²−S)` with `S=P`. On its generic fibre over `ℚ(S)` the degree filtration has domain `ℚ(S)[X,Y]/(X²+Y²)` as associated graded ring, since −1 is not a square in this ordered field. At the degree valuation all reciprocals of nonconstant coordinates vanish, so the constant coordinates already account for the entire old target. Positivity forbids any others. Monic normal form implies `ℚ[X,Y]∩ℚ(S)=ℚ[S]`. Thus the norm-plane descent is an instance of exactly the same rigidity as the later circle stages; it does not require degree classification or a connectedness argument. Consequently it is enough to prevent all polynomial classes at the profile of `P`; all local generator values need not specialize to integers at a single ordinary square.

**TS task.** At service time consider a positive `A`-prime `s` of integral unit value at *every* mark and with `s≡1 mod 4` at 2. If it splits over `A[i]`, use Gaussian scalar correction to normalize the factors of `s` to exact norm `s` with integral real coordinates. Writing `s=c(a²+b²)` from the conjugate factorization, `c>0` by order. At each `l≡3 mod 4`, **unit evaluation of `s`** gives `v_l(c)=−2 min(v_l(a),v_l(b))`; positivity alone would not imply this parity. Thus `c` is a rational Gaussian norm; finitely many imbalanced split valuations are corrected by norm-one scalars. Almost-everywhere integrality of the Gaussian coordinates is used here. Otherwise adjoin `B=A[x,y]/(x²+y²−s)`. In Gaussian coordinates `UV=s`, with `U` prime; inverting `U` gives a factorial Laurent chart. Its only global units are Gaussian rational constants. Divisor descent, using Hilbert 90 on these constants, gives factoriality of `B`. Modulo the targeted prime the rational fibre remains integral precisely because it was Gaussian-inert; all other old rational primes have a nonzero, geometrically integral conic fibre. The ring has the normal form `a(x)+yb(x)` and intersects the old fraction field only in `A`. Choosing `x=√s·t`, `y=√s·√(1−t²)`, with `t` of fresh residue between 1/3 and 1/2, preserves the bounded-elements invariant. Indeed `z^j` and `z^k√(1−z²)` are independent in the two polynomial blocks at the generic residue `z`. On the generic Gaussian circle the ES coordinates are Laurent polynomials fixed by `U↔s/U` with conjugation. Every nonconstant fixed Laurent polynomial has a pole at infinity, so the same constant-coordinate argument descends the triple. These are the hypotheses and arguments of `LOGIC_CIRCLE_AUDIT.md`; in particular, the generic conic need not be rational over the old field.

A local integral unit is representable by the circle at every odd place (finite-field norm or split product and smooth lifting); at 2 a unit `1 mod 4` is representable in `ℤ₂`: if it is `1 mod 8` take an odd square root, if `5 mod 8` subtract 4 first. One can perturb a solution in its integral open neighbourhood on the smooth ℚ₂-conic. Choose a point generic over the countable old evaluation field in the specified local open (avoid countably many proper algebraic zero sets). This provides **one** transcendental parameter, not two independent coordinates with a relation.

## PID version: build the NR moduli rather than require them to be standard

In addition service positive rational-stage **prime elements**, including rational associates, by

```
A[D,V,H]/(DH−V²−s),     D>0 above the old field, V above the field with D.
```

The open `D≠0` is a rational polynomial/Laurent chart. `D` is prime: `V²+s` is irreducible in the old ordered field and also over its Gaussian extension, since `s` is not a square in the ordered field by its prime valuation. A square root of positive `s` in `F(i)` would lie in `F`. Nagata gives factoriality over both constant fields. Modulo any old rational prime, saturation at `D` follows from monicity of `V²+s`; the quotient still embeds in the Laurent chart. Units stay constant. In a Laurent expansion the coefficient of `D^(−j)` is divisible by `(V²+s)^j` for `j>0`, so the intersection with `F(D)` is `A[D]`. Apply reciprocal rigidity first on the `V`-line and then on the polynomial `D`-line. Do not apply that argument to a general Laurent polynomial without this intersection check. The same description and the successive infinite orders show that bounded elements stay constant. Demand locally that `D` be a unit everywhere and `3 mod 4` at 2, and `V` integral. At the service of an actual integral prime `s`, `H` will then be integral as well. Modulo `D` the old `s` is nonzero; there is no shared stage prime and no shared ordinary prime because `D` is a unit at every mark. Service Bézout rows to obtain the required coprimality in the final ring.

**No circular choice of local evaluations.** The algebraic tower is allowed to branch when a circle task comes up. Prepare the countable tree of possible finite histories *first*: at a task for an inert positive infinite prime the two children are the circle step and a skip, and at other steps there is only one child. All fields and their order extensions, the stage-dependent lists of inputs, and the dovetailing are fixed per node, without p-adic information. Include all prime elements, not a single representative up to rational association. Process finitely many levels at a time. For any fixed finite initial subtree there exist compatible generic chart residues on the whole subtree at every sufficiently large ordinary prime, simultaneously satisfying all of the following: the initial norm is a nonzero square; every finite listed nonzero rational function at a node has unit value (including numerators and denominators); all stage generators are integral, listed norm targets are nonzero, prefix Dirichlet outputs unit, and `D` unit. This assertion is only for a *fixed finite list* and sufficiently large places. **(z74 Opus xreview fix.)** The edge conditions are imposed at **every edge of the finite subtree in a prime's prefix**, not only for functions that happen to be among the first `n(q)` test functions at a node. They are: the chosen row pivot nonzero; the target `s` nonzero at every circle edge; `D` nonzero at every NR edge; the output `a+mZ` nonzero at every Dirichlet edge; and all chart denominators nonzero. They are part of the success event in `LOGIC_JOINT_PID_TREE_AUDIT.md` §2. Consequently every prefix Dirichlet output and every prefix `D` is a unit at that prime, and every prefix circle target is a unit there. To see it, add `t²=X²+Y²` with `t≠0` at the base. This is a geometrically integral quadric. At each node on a suitable dense open the projection is smooth dominant with geometrically integral generic fibre: polynomial line, row chart, `D≠0` chart, or a nonzero conic. For a finite tree, take the fibre product of siblings over their common parent, successively restricting to dense opens. The smooth horizontal component is geometrically integral and dominates each node: the product of geometrically integral generic fibres is geometrically integral. Pullbacks of finitely many nonzero functions are still nonzero, so they can be required nonzero simultaneously. Spread the resulting nonempty smooth geometrically integral open over the integers away from finitely many places. Lang–Weil gives points over every sufficiently large finite prime field there. Smooth lifting then gives prefix-compatible integral local lifts generic on each node in its specified open; choose a generic point in the local analytic neighbourhood of the joint smooth variety. This argument imposes a square on the initial norm, **not** two separate square constraints on `X,Y`.

Choose cutoffs successively so each prime handles a finite initial subtree/list with the levels tending to infinity. Once its prefix is lifted, extend down the tail locally, retaining a cemetery value on impossible hypothetical branches. Ordinary rows can always be lifted generically over the local **field**; denominators are allowed at the finitely many small places for old auxiliary generators. Dirichlet and NR variables in the tail obey their unit/integrality constraints: for `Q=a+mY` with locally integral unimodular inputs forbid at most one residue of integral `Y`. At 2 give every NR `D` its prescribed class; choose the base norm a square unit `1 mod 8`, e.g. in a sufficiently small open about `(1,0)`. At all finite exceptions choose a small integral open on the base with the required norm and prescribed unit-square class, e.g. a square unit `1 mod 840` for the distinguished counterexample. Along the actual history **take** a circle step only for the all-place unit target with the required 2-adic value. Consequently it has locally feasible children at every place; a cemetery point is never used there. Fixed square conditions on the base and all simultaneous unit conditions in a prefix are open. The growing cutoffs and avoidance of every nonzero function on its node ensure that on the actual history **every fixed nonzero element of the fraction field has valuation zero almost everywhere**. No arbitrary countable collection of local conditions is imposed at a prime field of fixed size.

The adelically integral subring `R` is therefore an external PID and satisfies open induction by `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §2: scale each permanent rational-stage prime by a rational to zero valuations everywhere, use factorization, correct a Bézout row by one CRT scalar, and use rational translation for floors. Every final nonstandard prime is an all-place unit prime of the rational union (including the correct scalar), and is serviced; a nonirreducible could not become irreducible after pullback because its factors admit that same normalization. It thus acquires genuine NR witnesses, and, when positive and `1 mod 4`, Gaussian coordinates satisfying TS. Standard primes use their ordinary integer witnesses. Internal Dirichlet follows from the scheduled congruence tasks, with unit output and integral multiplier. Yet ES at the initial prime would descend to three polynomials in `P`. Their positive leading behaviour and marked integrality give a polynomial identity integral on the progression of `P` modulo a common standard coefficient denominator. It is a primitive unit-square class, and such an identity is impossible by a **self-contained** argument (z74 Opus xreview fix). Specialize at `n=t²` with `t` odd, coprime to the coefficient denominator and to the nonzero constant terms, and `t²≡P mod M`. This yields a strict Type I or Type II solution, or the impossible all-divisible case `4≤3` (`LOGIC_SPLIT_SQUARE_TEST.md`, “From a polynomial identity to a strict square specialization”). A strict solution at an odd square is excluded by the Jacobi-symbol proof in `Z73_PID_SQUARE.md` §4 (equivalently `LOGIC_SPLIT_SQUARE_TEST.md`, “Sharper sign for odd strict solutions”). The same file explains why the rational identity with three denominators `3P/4` is not a counterexample. For context only: Elsholtz–Tao (arXiv 1107.1010, p. 8, archived in `sources/`) state that “a primitive congruence class n = r mod q which is a perfect square, cannot be solved by polynomials (this also follows from Proposition 1.6)”, citing Mordell's *Diophantine Equations* [44] and Schinzel, Funct. Approx. 28 (2000) [68]. Their Prop. 1.6 reads “For any odd perfect square n, we have f_I(n) = f_II(n) = 0.” The proof here does not depend on these citations. This conclusion uses no claim that arbitrary witness tuples still specialize at odd squares after the NR stage.

## Variant: ordinary prime moduli for NR — CONDITIONAL sketch, not part of the headline (z74 Opus xreview fix)

**Label (z74 Opus xreview fix).** CONDITIONAL / sketch. Nothing in this section is proved in this note, and it is not part of the §0 result. The intended source, `LOGIC_ADAPTIVE_SPARSE_NR.md`, says of itself that it “works for universal NR, conditional on constructing the other local evaluations compatibly, preserving the rational-stage invariants and ensuring restricted adelic integrality”, and that “this is not an unconditional joint-model theorem about the ordinary-prime anchor construction”. Two simpler versions are known to fail:
* The plain one-prime non-square-profile model (`LOGIC_NONSQUARE_PROFILE.md`) **fails** universal NR, even with nonstandard moduli, at `Q=1+8ℓ(P−1)` (`Z73_NONSQUARE_ADELIC_REVIEW.md` §4).
* The norm-base model **fails** universal TS (`LOGIC_NORM_BASE_NR_AUDIT.md`, “Actual limit on TS”).

A joint non-PID model with both universal axioms would therefore need the sparse adaptive profile *and* an adaptive TS tower proved compatible with each other. No such proof exists in the repository. *(z74 Opus xreview fix: the ordinary-place half of that tower is `LOGIC_CIRCLE_ORDINARY_AUDIT.md`. The NR-models review, `data/z74/xreview/REVIEW_NRMODELS_OPUS.md` row AS6, found no obstruction in the interfaces between the two halves. The label stays CONDITIONAL until an assembly note is written and reviewed.)* The original sketch follows.

A separate version omits the `DH` stages. Use the uncovered non-square profile and the compressed-modulus bound of `LOGIC_NONSQUARE_PROFILE.md` and `Z73_NONSQUARE_ADELIC_REVIEW.md`, starting with the same norm plane. For sparse-prime sampling and ordinary charts in the adaptive TS tower, see the separate stage audits. The relevant distinction: away from the sparse primes one freezes `P≡1 mod ℓ` and then lifts in a small open, not to the exact scalar 1. Thus `P−1≠0` has infinitely many ordinary prime divisors; **this version is not an external PID**. Integral-almost-everywhere is required of stage elements, not their field inverses. These are distinct constructions and their advantages cannot simply be combined.

**Boundary.** NR is a particular existential square-root/divisibility scheme obtained classically using quadratic reciprocity; it is not the law of quadratic reciprocity for all internal moduli. TS is Fermat's two-square theorem for primes `1 mod 4`, not Lagrange's four-square theorem for all positive integers. Lagrange is added to the same PID model by the four-square edge (`LOGIC_FOUR_SQUARES.md`; Proof map A5) (z74 Opus xreview fix). No conclusion about IE₁, IΔ₀, PA, ZFC, or the truth of ES in the standard integers follows.
