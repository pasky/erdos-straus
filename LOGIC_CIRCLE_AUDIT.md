# Direct circle adjunction: algebraic audit

**Verdict.** The inert-prime circle stage preserves rational factoriality, all old rational primes, bounded elements, and positive ES triples at old targets. The split-prime normalization works with restricted adelic evaluations of the old coordinates; a unit norm at every place, by itself, does not imply that restriction. These are stage lemmas, not a construction of a new model.

### 1. Factoriality and prime persistence

Let `A` be a finitely generated ℚ-domain with ordered fraction field `F`, with `A` and `C=A[i]` factorial and their units respectively `ℚ*` and `ℚ(i)*`. Let `s>0` be an `A`-prime still prime in `C`. Set `B=A[X,Y]/(X²+Y²−s)`. After adjoining `i`, take `U=X+iY`, `V=X−iY`; then `E=B[i]=C[U,V]/(UV−s)`. It embeds in `C[U,U⁻¹]` by `V=s/U` (reduce monomials to powers of only one variable). Here `U` is a nonunit prime since `E/U=(C/s)[V]`; `E[1/U]` is factorial, hence so is `E` by Nagata. Every unit of `E` becomes `aU^j`, `a∈ℚ(i)*`, in this chart; the valuation at `U` forces `j=0`.

Write `σ` for conjugation. `B=E^σ` is normal: an element of `Frac(B)` integral over `B` is integral over `E`, hence in `E`, and is fixed. For each height-one prime divisor `p` of `B`, pull it back along the finite étale extension `B⊂E`. Its pullback is the sum of the primes above it with multiplicity one, and is principal, say `div(f)`. Thus `σ(f)/f=a∈ℚ(i)*` with `a ā=1`. Hilbert 90 gives `b∈ℚ(i)*` with `b/b̄=a`; now `bf` is fixed and belongs to `Frac(B)` (the invariant subfield of `Frac(B)(i)`). Pullback of its divisor is the given sum. Pullback on divisors is injective by lying over and the valuation formula, so `div_B(bf)=p`. Hence `B` is factorial; its units are `ℚ*`. This descent uses the **constant** units upstairs, not a general descent of factoriality.

For an old rational-stage prime `r`, monicity in `Y` embeds `B/r` into the conic algebra over `K=Frac(A/r)`. If `r` is not associate to `s`, the conic has nonzero right side and is geometrically integral (over an algebraic closure it is `UV=const≠0`). For `r=s`, `−1` is nonsquare in `K` since `(A/s)[i]` is a domain and stays so after localization. Thus `X²+Y²` is irreducible over `K`. Both quotients are nonzero domains. In particular the targeted `s` stays prime **in `B`**, even though it now splits in `E`. Finally the monic normal form `P(X)+YQ(X)` already shows `B∩F=A`, by comparing over `F`.

### 2. ES rigidity and the order

For an old `n>0`, suppose the ES coordinates `w_j∈B` are positive in an ordered extension and satisfy `Σ1/w_j=4/n`. On the Gaussian generic fiber they are Laurent polynomials in `U` over `F(i)`, fixed by the involution sending `U` to `s/U` and conjugating coefficients. Every nonconstant fixed Laurent polynomial has symmetric exponent support, hence has a **pole** at `U=∞`; its reciprocal has value zero there. Taking the value at infinity of the identity gives `Σ_{constant w_j}1/w_j=4/n`. Back in the ordered field every omitted term is strictly positive, so none was omitted. All coordinates lie in `F`, hence in `A`. No real point at infinity, sign of a complex leading coefficient, or rational parametrization of the circle is required.

Assume the elements of `A` bounded by a standard integer are exactly ℚ; in particular the positive nonunit `s` is infinite. Take `r=√s` in `F^rc`, and an ordered extension with `1/3<z<1/2` whose natural residue `ζ` is transcendental over the residue field `k` of `F^rc` (use the fresh-residue extension for countable stages). Take `X=rz`, `Y=r√(1−z²)`, positive branch. For a nonzero normal form `Σa_jX^j+YΣb_jX^j`, divide the substituted expression by the maximum `M` of the absolute values of the nonzero `a_j r^j, b_j r^(j+1)`. The residue is `p(ζ)+√(1−ζ²) q(ζ)`, with `p,q∈k[Z]` not both zero: a coefficient of modulus one survives. This is nonzero, since `1−Z²` has a simple zero at `1` and is nonsquare in `k(Z)`. Thus the substitution is injective and the magnitude has the same natural valuation as `M`. As every nonzero coefficient in `A` is noninfinitesimal, any positive-degree monomial makes `M` infinite. The bounded elements remain exactly ℚ. Equal *total* degrees in the two blocks need not be distinct; it is this residue independence that handles their potential cancellation.

### 3. Normalizing the already split case

Instead let `s` be a positive `A`-prime which splits in `C`. In the finite étale Gaussian extension the generic fiber over `(s)` is then a pair of conjugate fields: `(A/s)[i]` embeds into that fiber, so a field fiber would imply `s` was still prime. Factoriality and the unramified valuation formula give `s=c h h̄` with `h∈C` prime and `c∈ℚ*`; positivity forces `c>0`. Suppose given embeddings `F→ℚ_l` with `s` a unit at every prime, and with the two old coordinates of `h` integral almost everywhere. The latter follows, for example, from `A=R⊗ℚ` for the integral-evaluation ring `R`.

* For `l≡3 mod 4`, `v_l(N(h))` is even: it is twice the minimum valuation of its coordinates, by nonsquareness of `−1` on reduction. Thus `v_l(c)` is even at every inert prime. Together with `c>0` this is exactly the rational Gaussian norm condition. Explicitly factor `c` in ℚ: use rational `l` for each pair of inert exponents, one Gaussian prime of norm `l` at split primes, and `1+i` at 2, allowing negative powers. Obtain `γ∈ℚ(i)*` of norm `c` and put `h₀=γh`. Now `N(h₀)=s` exactly.
* Its two coordinates are integral at each inert place by the same minimum formula. At 2, writing their common minimum as `m`, the norm has valuation `2m` if just one rescaled coordinate is odd and `2m+1` if both are odd. Unit norm therefore forces `m=0` and exactly one odd. In particular `s≡1 mod 4` here follows already; it is not an extra hypothesis of the split lemma.
* At `l≡1 mod 4`, fix `i_l²=−1` and write `h₀^±` for the two evaluations using `±i_l`. Their valuations are `(t_l,−t_l)`. Only finitely many `t_l` are nonzero, by almost-everywhere coordinate integrality and unit norm. Choose a Gaussian prime `π_l` of norm `l` oriented to have valuation one at the `+` evaluation. Multiply `h₀` by `∏(π_l/π̄_l)^(−t_l)` over these places. This scalar has norm one, corrects the two valuations simultaneously to zero, and is a local Gaussian unit elsewhere. Recover the coordinates as the half-sum and the difference divided by `2i_l`; both are integral at split places since `l` is odd. At inert places and 2 the unit-norm arguments still apply. The resulting coordinates belong to `A` and are integral everywhere, as required.

**Two scope checks.** (i) Without the almost-everywhere hypothesis the split valuations can imbalance at infinitely many places: for `s=X²+Y²`, `h=X+iY` in `ℚ[X,Y,i]`, at each split `l` choose `X±i_lY` of valuations `(1,−1)` in open cylinders, choosing the two parameters algebraically independent. This specifies field embeddings with unit `s` there; at other places take integral embeddings near `(1,0)`. No rational Gaussian scalar repairs infinitely many imbalances. Likewise the *inert* adjunction does not justify assuming its fraction field is a pure transcendental extension of `F`: `s` has odd valuation at its fixed Gaussian prime, whereas every norm from `F(i)` has even valuation there, so the generic conic has no `F`-point.

(ii) Combining with `LOGIC_ES_RIGIDITY_NR.md` needs the Gaussian invariant also at **NR** stages. Do not reuse its schedule for *all* positive `s` unchanged: already for `s=1`, in `ℚ(i)[D,V,H]/(DH−V²−1)` the element `D` is irreducible but nonprime. Indeed after inverting `D` its factors must be scalar powers of `D`, negative powers being impossible in the original ring, while the quotient by `D` is reducible. For a positive rational-stage **prime** `s`, `V²+s` is irreducible over `F(i)`: a root would give a square root of `s` in `F(i)`, necessarily real and hence in `F`, contrary to its odd `s`-valuation. The same localization-at-`D` proof then gives Gaussian factoriality for that NR stage. These checks leave scheduling and simultaneous local evaluations to the global construction.
