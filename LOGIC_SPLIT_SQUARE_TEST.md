# Red-team test: split norms and the primitive square-class obstruction

**Verdict at `ea9ad3e95`.** Neither proposed counterexample breaks the combined external PID argument in `LOGIC_JOINT_NR_TS.md` + `LOGIC_FOUR_SQUARES.md`. Both break statements obtained by dropping a hypothesis. This is an audit of the two inferences, not an independent construction of the entire evaluation tree. The local sign below is a necessary condition on an **ordinary solution**, not a method for finding one.

## Split normalization: what is the scalar?

Let `A` and `A[i]` be the factorial stages with the specified constant units. For a positive `A`-prime `s` that splits, the finite étale constant extension gives two conjugate primes over `(s)`, each of multiplicity one (factor `T²+1` over `Frac(A/s)`). Thus `s=c h h̄`, with `h=a+ib` in `A[i]` and `c` a conjugation-fixed scalar in `ℚ(i)*`, hence rational. Since `h h̄=a²+b²>0`, `c>0`. Positivity alone **does not** make `c` a Gaussian norm (try `c=3`). Rather, for *this* scalar, at each `l≡3 mod 4` the nonsquareness of `−1` gives

`0=v_l(s)=v_l(c)+2 min(v_l(a),v_l(b))`.

So every inert exponent is even. Factoring `c` in `ℚ*` gives a rational Gaussian scalar of norm `c`: use powers of `l` at inert primes, `1+i` at 2, and a Gaussian prime above each split prime, allowing negative powers. Absorb it into `h`. Now `N(h)=s` exactly. For inert `l` the same minimum is zero. At 2 a rescaled pair of minimum valuation zero has norm valuation 0 or 1 according as one or both entries are odd; hence unit norm before rescaling forces the original minimum to be zero.

At a split `l`, choose `i_l`, and let `k_l=v_l(a+i_l b)` for the adjusted `h`. Unit norm gives valuation `−k_l` at the other embedding. Both entries are integral almost everywhere by the tower hypothesis, so only finitely many `k_l` are nonzero. Choose `π_l` of norm `l` oriented to have valuation one at the first embedding. Multiplying by the finite product `∏(π̄_l/π_l)^{k_l}` corrects both valuations and has norm one. At split places recover integral coordinates by the half-sum and the difference divided by `2i_l`; at 2 and the inert places reapply the unit-norm test. All scalars used lie in `ℚ(i)*`, so the final coordinates still belong to `A`. Four-square stages do not alter the argument; it needs the stated factoriality, units and restricted evaluations at the stage where `s` is processed.

**Falsifier without restriction.** For `P=X²+Y²` in `ℚ[X,Y]` choose `X±i_lY` of valuations `±1` at infinitely many split primes and choose the other evaluations near `(1,0)`. These are open conditions, so `X,Y` can be algebraically independent at each place. All marks give unit `P`, but no Gaussian scalar can correct infinitely many imbalances, even if one swaps the two global factors. This is a counterexample to the unit-only version, **not** to the PID version.

## What ET actually excludes

Elsholtz–Tao ([archived paper](sources/elsholtz-tao-1107.1010.pdf), Prop. 1.6, pp. 6, 19) assert `f_I(n)=f_II(n)=0` for odd squares, **not** `f(n)=0`. At a composite target, “not divisible by `n`” does not mean “coprime to `n`”. On p. 8, before Prop. 1.9, they expressly require polynomial denominators taking *positive integer values for all sufficiently large integers in a primitive residue class*. They exclude three zero constant terms using arbitrarily large primes in the class. Section 4 uses reciprocity for Type I and points to the corresponding two identities for Type II. The archived p. 19 prints `(−1)^((q−1)/4)` for `(-1/q)`; the correct exponent is `(q−1)/2`. The following gives both types without that typo or the torsor parametrizations.

**Ordinary two-term descent.** If `1/X+1/Y=d/w` in positive reduced terms, put `U=dX−w`, `V=dY−w`. Then `UV=w²`, `U=wX/Y>0`, and `Uw≡−w² mod d`. Thus `g=sf(XY)`, the unique positive squarefree integer with `XY/g` square, is also `sf(Uw)` (their ratio is `(Y/w)²`). It divides `w`. Writing `Uw=g h²` shows that `−g` is a unit square modulo `d` if `d>1`: `g h²≡−w² mod d` with `g,h,w` invertible. This is a local check for **any** ordinary ES solution: remove any one denominator `x` and reduce `(4x−n)/(nx)=d/w` for the other two. No primality or parity of `n` is needed for this check.

**Sharper sign for odd strict solutions.** For `n>1` odd, in strict Type I write the denominators as `(nw,X,Y)` with `gcd(n,XY)=1`. The cleared equation gives `n | 4w−1`, so set `d=(4w−1)/n`. In strict Type II write `(w,nX,nY)` with `gcd(n,w)=1` and set `d=4w−n`. In both types `1/X+1/Y=d/w` in reduced positive terms, `gcd(n,w)=1`, and `d≡−n mod 4`. For each odd prime `l|w`, `d≡−n⁻¹` or `−n mod l`, respectively. Quadratic reciprocity therefore gives `(l/d)=(l/n)`: on flipping the two symbols, the sign difference from `d≡−n mod 4` cancels the factor `(-1/l)`. Here lower-case fraction symbols in parentheses denote Jacobi symbols, with value 1 at denominator 1. If `2|w`, also `d≡−n mod 8` (odd units are self-inverse mod 8), so `(2/d)=(2/n)`. Since `g|w`, the two-term descent gives

`(g/n)=(g/d)=(-1/d)`, hence **`(-g/n) = −1`**.

In particular this covers *every* ordinary odd prime solution: clearing the identity forces a multiple of `p` among the three entries; all three cannot be multiples, since after cancellation `4≤3`. When `p≡1 mod 8`, some **odd** prime `l` dividing `g` must have `(p/l)=−1`: its multiplicity in `XY` is odd and it divides `w`. Thus it is not just an unrelated NR witness at `p`. In fact `X/Y=U/w` is a `p`-unit and `X/Y` differs from `g` by a rational square. The prime sign is therefore the local condition of [Bright–Loughran](sources/bright-loughran-1908.02526.pdf), Cor. 1.3, rather than a new obstruction to ES. At an odd square, the same Jacobi sign is impossible: `g` is coprime to `n` and every unit has symbol +1. No such conclusion holds for *nonstrict* triples, e.g. `4/9=1/3+1/10+1/90`.

## From a polynomial identity to a strict square specialization

Suppose positive stage denominators at the marked infinite `P` have descended to `G_i(P)∈ℚ[P]`. In the degree-at-infinity descent, only constant denominators survive. Their reciprocals already give the entire sum, so omitted **positive** reciprocals cannot sum to zero. In particular on the four-square fibre its degree filtration has domain associated graded (the leading rank-four form cannot factor into linear forms); on the binary norm base the corresponding binary form is irreducible over the ordered field `ℚ(P)`. Thus positivity is not a claim about arbitrary signed identities or specialization of the new generators.

Take a common coefficient denominator `M>0`, `F_i=M G_i∈ℤ[T]`. Since `G_i(P)` is integral at every mark, `F_i(r)≡0 mod M` for the CRT residue `r` of `P` modulo `M`. All `G_i(n)` are therefore integral on that progression, and positive for large `n` by their leading signs. If `r` is a unit square mod `M`, choose arbitrarily large odd `t` coprime to `M` and to each **nonzero** `F_i(0)` with `t²≡r mod M`. This is just CRT: prescribe a unit square root at `M`, oddness, and avoid zero at the remaining finitely many prime divisors. At `n=t²`:

* `F_i(0)=0` implies `n | F_i(n)` and, since `gcd(n,M)=1`, `n | G_i(n)`.
* `F_i(0)≠0` gives `gcd(n,G_i(n))=1` by reduction modulo primes dividing `t`.
* There is at least one zero constant term by the cleared polynomial identity at `T=0`. If all three vanish, cancelling `n` from the three integer denominators gives `4≤3`. Otherwise this is a strict Type I or II solution, contradicting `(-g/n)=−1` at the odd square.

This deals with the all-divisible case **before** invoking Prop. 1.6 and needs no prime-value theorem. Consequently any polynomial identity giving an ordinary positive integer solution at a prime `p` must have either `p|M` or `p` nonsquare modulo some prime power exactly dividing `M`. Indeed integral values propagate along the progression through `p`. Positivity at `p` also suffices: on the common positive real locus of the three polynomials one has `G_i(T)≥T/4` for `T>0`, so this locus is both open and closed in the connected positive half-line. This is a restriction on a *polynomial explanation* of a solution, not on the existence of an isolated integer triple. The proposed `G_i=3T/4` indeed satisfies the rational identity and works on the nonprimitive class `0 mod 4`, but at the marked unit `v₂(G_i(P))=−2`. This is why the same triple cannot slip past descent.

## An extra axiom that really excludes these models

**Bounded common multiples:** `∀B≥1 ∃L>0 ∀j (1≤j≤B ⇒ j|L)`. In ℕ take `L=B!`. At any infinite cutoff in *any* of these rings, `L` would have to be divisible by `2^k` for every standard `k`. A faithful integral 2-adic mark gives finite `v₂(L)` for every nonzero `L`. This contradiction does not use external factoriality, the square-class profile, or a particular stage list. Even the weaker demand for common multiples of the 2-powers below each cutoff fails. This excludes the models; it **does not** show that adjoining common multiples proves ES, or that these are the only possible countermodels.

**Finite cross-check (not used as proof).** Exact sorted enumeration at odd `3≤n<160` gave 442 Type I and 193 Type II prime triples and 426 Type I and 167 Type II composite triples, all with `g|w` and `(-g/n)=−1`. There were 3,632 nonstrict composite triples, excluded from the sign test. Bounds were `n/4<x≤3n/4`, `max(x,⌊nx/(4x−n)⌋+1)≤y≤2nx/(4x−n)`, with `z=nxy/((4x−n)y−nx)` tested for integrality. As a larger point check, `(636,69748,131876031)` at `p=2521` gives `w=52311`, `g=987`, and `(−987)^1260≡−1 mod 2521`. These checks are for numerator 4 only.
