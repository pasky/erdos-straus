## 0. Executive summary for the parallel agents (parent-checked items are marked ✔)

**Bottom line.** No paper found proves pointwise ES, conditionally or not. No paper applies automorphic forms, Kuznetsov or Heegner-point equidistribution to `4/n`.

* *Citers of Bright–Loughran.* Six verified citers (§4a). None proves a new result about integral points on `U_n`.
* *Analytic work on 3-term unit fractions.* It is all mean-value or upper-bound work (§5): Browning–Elsholtz `f₃(m,n) ≪ (n/m)^{23/12}n^ε`, Jia 2012, Huang–Vaughan for 2 terms, plus Elsholtz–Tao and the campaign's own (D)32.
* *Conditional results.* The conditional literature (§6) is about fixed ternary forms (Ono–Soundararajan, Rouse, Kane) and has no ES analogue.

**Five points the follow-up agents should take from this file.**

1. **(O123, O128) Spectral methods do give pointwise-in-the-discriminant existence, but only for large targets.**
   * ✔ *Young.* Young 2017 Thm 2.1 gives an effective-shape error `‖φ‖₂ D^{5/12+ε}V^{1+ε}`, with ineffective constants through Siegel. Consequences:
     * every fixed ball of radius `D^{−δ}` (`δ < 1/24`) contains the expected share of the Heegner points of discriminant `−D`; under Lindelöf, `δ < 1/8`. Humphries Thm 1.22 is the restatement, Thm 1.24 the genus/closed-geodesic version.
     * Young Prop. 1.2 is a *single-D existence theorem* proved spectrally: `b² ≡ −D (mod 4a)` has solutions with `a ≍ √D`, `|b| ≍ √D/V` for every large odd fundamental `D`, in the range `V ≪ D^{1/18−ε}`.
   * *Humphries–Radziwiłł.* They reach much smaller targets, but only for almost all centres.
   * *What O123 must compute.* For a fixed prime `p`, find the discriminant and the target region in which an ES Type I solution corresponds to a Heegner point. Then compare the region's area with `D^{−1/12}` (unconditional) or `D^{−1/4}` (GLH).
     * If the area is a fixed power of `D` above that threshold, the method would give a pointwise statement. It would be ineffective through Siegel; effective under GRH.
     * If the area is below the threshold, the method is out of range. That would be the expected outcome, and it is a precise one.
2. **(all) The odd-square sanity check.** Elsholtz–Tao Prop. 1.6 ✔ (p. 7 of arXiv:1107.1010): `f_I(n) = f_II(n) = 0` for every odd perfect square `n`.
   * Any method that proves `f_I(p) > 0` must therefore fail at `n = p²`. ET note that this already rules out finite covering congruences and the circle method.
   * A spectral/Heegner route passes this check only if its discriminant or target degenerates at squares. Check this first.
3. **(O123, O124) Optimal lifting benchmarks.** Sardari ✔ (Duke 2019, arXiv:1510.00462):
   * *Thm 1.2.* A quadratic form in `d ≥ 5` variables represents `N` in a prescribed class mod `m` and a prescribed cap of radius `r` once `N ≫ (m/r)^{4+δ}`. The exponent 4 is optimal.
   * *`d = 4`.* The exponent is 6, for odd `N`; 4 is conjectured.
   * *Determinant equations.* Cor. 1.1: `det H = m` with `H/√m` within `m^{−1/6+δ}` of any point of a compact set in `SL₂(ℝ)`.
   * This is the right yardstick for "congruence class plus real cone at polynomial height". ES adds multiplicative constraints (the shape `4a²d`), which are not congruence conditions.
4. **(O126) Integral points on log K3 surfaces: no real-place strong approximation in families.**
   * ✔ *Harpaz (JEMS 2019).* Thm 1.0.1 is *existence* of S-integral points on conic log K3 surfaces under square-class independence. The proof uses Green–Tao–Ziegler, not approximation at the real place.
   * *Cao–Xu and Wei.* These are toric results, "off ∞".
   * ✔ *Markoff surfaces (Ghosh–Sarnak).* Hasse failures number at least `√K(log K)^{−1/2}` up to `K`, while almost all admissible `k` are fine. This is the standard "almost all versus every" gap.
   * *Bright–Loughran.* BL Thm 1.9 says Brauer–Manin does not even control strong approximation on `U_n`.
5. **(O127) GRH in the literature removes Siegel ineffectivity and nothing more.** Examples: Ono–Soundararajan ✔ (via Kane), Rouse Thm 7, Kane.
   * In none of these examples does GRH turn an average result into a pointwise one.
   * Combined with item 1, the realistic conditional target is "ES (Type I) for all `p > p₀`, effective under GRH/GLH, *if* the target area clears the Young/Humphries threshold". No such statement exists in the literature.

## 1. Small-scale distribution: CM points, closed geodesics, and quadratic roots

### William Duke, “Hyperbolic distribution problems and half-integral weight Maass forms,” *Invent. Math.* 92 (1988), 73–90 — DOI 10.1007/BF01393987 (arXiv: not located)
* **Links:** [UCLA author PDF](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~wdduke/preprints/hyperbolic.pdf), visited 2026-10-10; title/author verified in the PDF and bibliographic record.
* **Main theorem(s):** Theorem 5 (Section 5) proves uniform distribution of Heegner points of fundamental discriminant as the absolute discriminant tends to infinity; the paper also establishes the corresponding equidistribution for closed geodesics (Theorem 6). These are fixed-test-function/global results: no shrinking-target exponent or power-saving target-area threshold is asserted in the basic theorem. The proofs give effective estimates with an ineffective Siegel contribution; no GRH is assumed. The setting is the modular surface/level one.
* **Summary:** Duke relates periods over CM points to Fourier coefficients of half-integral-weight forms and combines Iwaniec’s coefficient bounds with a spectral argument. For closed geodesics the analogous spectral estimates yield global equidistribution. This is the foundational qualitative/effective framework, not a theorem that a specified shrinking neighborhood contains a CM point.
* **Relevance to pointwise ES:** O123/O128 can use the spectral/Weyl-sum architecture when translating congruence solutions into modular orbits; the obstruction is that ES needs uniform coverage of a p-dependent, sparse family, not global distribution of all classes at a fixed discriminant. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — effective global equidistribution is a model, but no direct pointwise covering result.

### Henryk Iwaniec, “Fourier coefficients of modular forms of half-integral weight,” *Invent. Math.* 87 (1987), 385–401 — DOI 10.1007/BF01389163
* **Links:** [EuDML bibliographic record](https://eudml.org/doc/143426), visited 2026-10-10; title, author, journal and pages verified.
* **Main theorem(s):** The paper proves a bound for Fourier coefficients of half-integral-weight cusp forms (the coefficient estimate used in Duke’s subsequent Heegner-point theorem); it is not itself a shrinking-target equidistribution theorem. In particular it supplies no target-area exponent, no short-interval statement for roots, and no GRH-dependent rate. See Duke (1988), Theorem 5, for the equidistribution application.
* **Summary:** The breakthrough coefficient bounds provide the analytic input that made effective Duke-style distribution possible. In this chain, the size of the relevant Fourier coefficient controls a global Weyl sum over representations/classes; localizing to shrinking sets requires additional uniform spectral control.
* **Relevance to pointwise ES:** O123/O128 may use it as foundational half-integral-weight input if their parametrization produces representation numbers or CM Weyl sums. It does not directly control the particular residue classes/moduli in the ES covering criterion. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — coefficient bounds support global equidistribution, not uniform hitting of ES classes.

### Matthew P. Young, “Weyl-type hybrid subconvexity bounds for twisted L-functions and Heegner points on shrinking sets,” *J. Eur. Math. Soc.* 19 (2017), no. 5, 1545–1576 — arXiv:1405.5457, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/699
* **Links:** [arXiv abstract/full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5457), visited 2026-10-10 (title, author, journal DOI verified); theorem text checked in the arXiv PDF.
* **Main theorem(s):** Theorem 2.1: for smooth compactly supported φ on SL₂(Z)\H with derivatives bounded by |Δⁿφ| ≤ C(n)V²ⁿ, and odd negative fundamental discriminant −D, the sum of φ over Heegner points equals h(−D) times the hyperbolic mean, with error O(‖φ‖₂ D^(5/12+ε)V^(1+ε)+(DV)^−100). Under Lindelöf for L(u_j×χ₋D,s) and L(s,χ₋D), D^(5/12) improves to D^(1/4). The usable scale depends on the target shape (parent check of the arXiv PDF, §2): for a ball of radius V⁻¹ (‖φ‖₁ ≍ V⁻², ‖φ‖₂ ≍ V⁻¹) the main term h(−D)‖φ‖₁ ≍ D^{1/2±ε}V⁻² beats the error iff V ≪ D^(1/24−ε), i.e. area ≫ D^(−1/12+ε) (with Lindelöf: V ≪ D^(1/8−ε)); this matches Humphries' Thm 1.22 below. For a thin strip near the cusp (‖φ‖₁ ≍ V⁻¹, ‖φ‖₂ ≍ V^(−1/2)) Young gets V ≪ D^(1/18−ε) (Siegel, ineffective), which is how Prop. 1.2 below is proved: solutions of b² ≡ −D (mod 4a) with a ≍ √D and |b| ≍ √D/V, i.e. *small roots of a quadratic congruence for moduli in a dyadic range*. Constants depend on the fixed derivative-bound sequence C(n). There is also Proposition 1.2: for η>4/9, respectively η>1/3, there are ≫h(−D)D^(η−1/2) forms with b≈D^η, respectively a≈D^η. The implied constants there are ineffective because of Siegel’s class-number lower bound. No level aspect.
* **Summary:** A uniform cubic-moment bound yields the explicit test-function dependence in the Heegner-point estimate. Its effective smoothing scale is far more useful than a fixed-test-function Duke theorem; the conditional scale improves substantially under Lindelöf.
* **Relevance to pointwise ES:** O123/O128 can use the localization principle and explicit scale/error balance where ES solutions correspond to a Heegner family. However, the bound averages over a complete class group and does not establish a hit in the special ES-selected subset; a test function also cannot encode arbitrary varying congruence restrictions for free. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — strongest directly relevant shrinking-target bound here, but it does not imply pointwise ES.

### Peter Humphries, “Equidistribution in shrinking sets and L⁴-norm bounds for automorphic forms,” *Math. Ann.* 371 (2018), 1497–1543 — arXiv:1705.05488, DOI 10.1007/s00208-018-1677-9
* **Links:** [arXiv abstract/full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.05488), visited 2026-10-10; theorem text checked in arXiv HTML v2. [Journal page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-018-1677-9) also verified.
* **Main theorem(s)** (checked by parent in arXiv PDF v2, pp. 10–11): Theorem 1.22 is *Young's* theorem [You17a, Thm 2.1] restated: fix w ∈ Γ\H and R ≍ |D|^(−δ); then #{A ∈ Cl_K : z_A ∈ B_R(w)}/(vol(B_R) h_K) = 1/vol(Γ\H) + o_{w,δ}(1) as D → −∞ through odd fundamental discriminants, for fixed δ < 1/24 (all fundamental D and δ < 1/8 under GLH). This is a fixed-centre statement over the full class group. Theorem 1.24 (Humphries; 'Young's method applies to genera') is the genus-averaged analogue for D > 0: closed-geodesic length in B_R(w), δ < 1/18 unconditionally for odd fundamental D (δ < 1/6 under GLH); hyperbolic orbifolds, δ < 1/12 (δ < 1/4 under GLH). Theorem 1.25 (GLH-conditional): almost-every-centre equidistribution for 0 < δ < 1/4 (Heegner genera) and 0 < δ < 1/2 (D > 0), with an explicit exceptional-measure threshold. Rates are o(1), no power saving stated; the centre w is fixed, not allowed to move with D.
* **Summary:** The result relevant here is not the paper’s automorphic QUE result but its arithmetic application: shrinking-target mass estimates for Heegner points and closed geodesics, sharpened conditionally by GLH. The Heegner/geodesic quantities are averaged over a genus, a restriction important when comparing with whole-class-group statements.
* **Relevance to pointwise ES:** O123/O128 can use these exact scale thresholds as benchmarks for localization of quadratic-class families. ES requires a target whose center/constraint varies with p and often a particular class/root, not the fixed w and genus average here. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — quantitative small-scale arithmetic equidistribution, without the required ES-specific uniformity.

### Peter Humphries and Maksym Radziwiłł, “Optimal Small Scale Equidistribution of Lattice Points on the Sphere, Heegner Points, and Closed Geodesics,” *Comm. Pure Appl. Math.* 75 (2022), no. 9, 1936–1996 — arXiv:1910.01360, DOI 10.1002/cpa.22076
* **Links:** [arXiv abstract/full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01360), visited 2026-10-10; title/authors/journal DOI verified; theorem text checked in arXiv PDF.
* **Main theorem(s):** Theorem 1.11 (negative fundamental discriminant D): the variance asymptotic for Heegner counts in randomly centered annuli holds when |D|^(−1/12+δ) ≪ r ≪ 1 and μ(A_{r,R}) ≪ r|D|^(−5/12−δ), for any δ>0. Theorem 1.12(1): for |D|^(−1/12+δ)≪r<R≪1 and |D|^(−1/2+δ)≪μ(A_{r,R})≪1, the normalized count is correct for all but a measure-o(1) set of centers. For positive squarefree D (closed geodesics), Theorem 1.12(2) gives the analogous almost-all-centers result for 0≤r<R≪1 and D^(−1+δ)≪μ(A_{r,R})≪1. These are unconditional, averaged-over-centers statements, not uniform-in-center claims; no GRH. The paper expressly says it does not obtain the same almost-every-ball conclusion for Heegner points as for closed geodesics.
* **Summary:** This is the right Humphries–Radziwiłł small-scale Heegner/closed-geodesic paper (not “Asymptotic domination of sign changes”). Its variance method proves near-optimal small scales for almost all target placements, with stronger area threshold for closed geodesics. It does not establish every shrinking target is occupied.
* **Relevance to pointwise ES:** O123/O128 can use the distinction between mean/global equidistribution and variance/most centers, and possibly the closed-geodesic analogy. ES needs a specific target determined by p, so an exceptional set of centers cannot be ignored without proving the ES targets avoid it. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — excellent almost-all local statistics, not uniform pointwise coverage.

### Stephen Lester and Maksym Radziwiłł, “Quantum unique ergodicity for half-integral weight automorphic forms,” *Duke Math. J.* 169 (2020), no. 2, 279–327 — arXiv:1606.04119, DOI 10.1215/00127094-2019-0047
* **Links:** [arXiv abstract/full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04119), visited 2026-10-10; title/authors verified and Theorems 1–2 checked in HTML.
* **Main theorem(s):** Theorem 1 assumes GRH and proves QUE as k→∞ for L²-normalized holomorphic half-integral-weight cusp forms g_k of weight k+1/2 on Γ₀(4), in Kohnen’s plus space, simultaneous eigenforms of T_{p²} for odd p. The limiting mass statement is for compact subsets with boundary measure zero. The proof specifically invokes RH for the relevant integral-weight L(s,f), quadratic twists L(s,f⊗χ_d), and L(s,Sym²f). Theorem 2 likewise assumes GRH and proves QUE as |t_j|→∞ for an orthonormal Hecke–Maaß eigenbasis in the Kohnen plus space V⁺ on Γ₀(4); it requires RH for full-level weight-zero Hecke–Maaß L-functions, their quadratic twists, and symmetric-square L-functions. These are compact-set/global QUE assertions, not shrinking-ball or roots theorems. **The GRH hypothesis is essential to the results as stated; this is not an unconditional QUE theorem.**
* **Summary:** Correct Lester–Radziwiłł reference for half-integral-weight QUE. The hypotheses are specific plus-space Hecke eigenforms at level 4, and the conclusion is conditional on GRH.
* **Relevance to pointwise ES:** This may inform O123/O128 if half-integral-weight coefficients arise, but it supplies neither local distribution of modular roots nor an ES covering estimate. **Assessment: LANGUAGE-FRAMEWORK ONLY** — conditional QUE in a different family.

### Sheng-Chi Liu, Riad Masri, and Matthew P. Young, “Subconvexity and equidistribution of Heegner points in the level aspect,” *Compos. Math.* 149 (2013), no. 7, 1150–1174 — arXiv:1206.3208, DOI 10.1112/S0010437X13007033
* **Links:** [arXiv abstract/full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.3208), visited 2026-10-10 (title, authors, journal citation and DOI verified).
* **Main theorem(s):** Theorem 1.4 gives equidistribution of level-q Heegner points as prime q and odd negative fundamental D grow, under q splitting in Q(√−D), in the range q < D^(1/20−ε). This is a joint level/discriminant aspect result, not a shrinking-target theorem with an area exponent. The associated hybrid subconvexity theorem is for q=D^η, 0<η<1. Unconditional; no GRH. Level is prime and subject to the splitting/Heegner conditions.
* **Summary:** Handles a genuinely joint aspect and shows how Rankin–Selberg/toric period bounds control Heegner mass as the modular curve level grows. Equidistribution is natural on the varying level surface; it should not be mistaken for a fixed-level local theorem.
* **Relevance to pointwise ES:** O123/O128 may find the level-uniform spectral machinery relevant if their moduli can be represented as varying level. ES moduli are not automatically prime levels, and the range q<D^(1/20−ε) plus global averaging does not reach arbitrary p-dependent covering classes. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — useful level-aspect estimates, substantial parameter mismatch.

### William Duke, John B. Friedlander, and Henryk Iwaniec, “Equidistribution of roots of a quadratic congruence to prime moduli,” *Ann. of Math.* (2) 141 (1995), no. 2, 423–441 — DOI 10.2307/2118527
* **Links:** [Annals record](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1995/141-2/p08), visited 2026-10-10; [JSTOR record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2118527), title/authors/pages verified.
* **Main theorem(s):** The paper proves equidistribution modulo one of roots of an irreducible quadratic congruence f(x)≡0 (mod p), as p runs over primes for which f has roots, in the negative-discriminant case, with a power-saving Weyl-sum estimate. This is a global distribution statement over the full set of roots/moduli; it does not give roots in every interval of length p^(−δ), nor a short interval in p. No GRH is assumed. **Rate caution:** the exact exponent in their Weyl-sum bound is not reproduced here; no shrinking-target exponent is inferred from qualitative equidistribution.
* **Summary:** DFI’s key Weyl-linear-form estimate makes roots of quadratic congruences to prime moduli equidistribute and is a central bridge between Kloosterman/spectral analysis and elementary congruence roots. It is precisely the kind of tool used in average-modulus applications, but fixed-prime coverage is stronger.
* **Relevance to pointwise ES:** O123/O128 can apply the root-sum/spectral technology to average counts over moduli or primes. For one prescribed p and a restricted family of ES moduli, global averaging over p cannot force a solution. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — strong average distribution, not pointwise ES coverage.

### Christopher Hooley, “On the distribution of the roots of polynomial congruences,” *Mathematika* 11 (1964), 39–49
* **Links:** Bibliographic citation verified via search on 2026-10-10; a primary full-text URL was not verified.
* **Main theorem(s):** Hooley proves uniform distribution modulo one for roots of a fixed irreducible polynomial congruence as the modulus runs through the integers (in the quadratic setting, later refinements sharpen the applicable forms). This is a full-modulus asymptotic, not a theorem on every short interval or a prescribed modulus. No GRH hypothesis is customarily required for this qualitative result; an exact quantitative discrepancy exponent is not asserted here.

* **Relevance to pointwise ES:** O123/O128 may use it for averages over moduli; one cannot deduce a root in an arbitrary short interval for each modulus from global equidistribution. **Assessment: NOT APPLICABLE** — the averaging variable is the modulus, unlike pointwise ES.

### Fixed-modulus roots and short intervals

#### Alexander Dunn, Bryce Kerr, Igor E. Shparlinski, and Alexandru Zaharescu, “Bilinear forms in Weyl sums for modular square roots and applications,” *Adv. Math.* 375 (2020), article 107369 — arXiv:1908.10143
* **Links:** [arXiv abstract/full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10143), visited 2026-10-10; title/authors and full-text statements verified. (The arXiv identifier is 1908.10143, not 1906.*.) *Adv. Math.* 375 (2020), article 107369.
* **Main theorem(s):** Theorem 1.1: for fixed ε>0, sufficiently large prime q, and q≥P≥q^(1/4+ε), the number N_q(P) of rational primes p≤P splitting in Q(√−q) is at least c(ε) min{P^(1/2)q^(−ε/2), Pq^(−1/4−2ε/3)}; c(ε) is ineffective (Siegel). Theorem 1.3 gives an effective lower bound for N_q at P=q when q≥67 and q≡3 mod 16. Separately, the bilinear Weyl/Salié-sum estimate has power saving in the Pólya–Vinogradov range; the application concerns roots of primes (and products), not arbitrary roots of a fixed polynomial in every short interval. This is unconditional apart from the explicitly conditional Siegel-zero results.
* **Relevance:** This is the closest match among the named papers: it averages over primes p for a fixed prime modulus q and supplies Weyl-sum/energy tools. It does not assert that a root for one fixed coefficient occurs in every interval of length H modulo q. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — substantial root-distribution technology but averaging and parameter ranges differ from pointwise ES.

### Short-range / fixed-modulus status

The cited Duke–Friedlander–Iwaniec and Tóth results average across prime moduli; Hooley averages across integer moduli. These are not short-interval-in-the-root-coordinate guarantees for each modulus. Young and Humphries–Radziwiłł give explicit small-target information for CM points/closed orbits, but the Humphries–Radziwiłł pointwise-center gap is real: their strongest local conclusions hold for almost all placements. I did not verify a theorem giving equidistribution of roots of a quadratic congruence in every interval of length q^(−δ) for each fixed q, or a Kloosterman-type bound with fixed modulus that would force roots into every such interval. For roots modulo q there are at most two values for a fixed quadratic, so such a claim would in any case require averaging over a family of quadratics/parameters, not merely shrinking an interval for one polynomial.

## Section takeaway

- Duke–Iwaniec establish the global CM-point foundation; Young gives balls of radius V⁻¹ with V≪D^(1/24−ε) unconditionally (V≪D^(1/8−ε) under Lindelöf), cusp strips to V≪D^(1/18−ε).
- Humphries Theorems 1.22/1.24 give fixed-center genus-averaged ball scales: Heegner δ<1/24 (unconditional), δ<1/8 (GLH); geodesics δ<1/18 and δ<1/6 respectively.
- Liu–Masri–Young treat joint level/discriminant equidistribution, not local targets on arbitrary levels; Humphries–Radziwiłł results include an almost-all-center limitation.
- Root-energy/bilinear results strengthen averaged tools, but no fixed-modulus every-short-interval root guarantee was verified; some named short-interval leads and the signs-paper details remain unverified.
- Lester–Radziwiłł QUE is GRH-conditional for specified Kohnen plus-space eigenforms; none of these results gives a hit for every prescribed ES prime and its constrained residue classes.

## (2) Optimal strong approximation and covering exponents

The useful distinction is between **density/equidistribution** and **uniform covering**. A theorem saying that many integral points become equidistributed as height grows does not automatically say that every prescribed residue class (or every target cap) is hit below a specified height. The latter is the analogue most relevant to an Erdős–Straus (ES) covering argument.

### Naser T. Sardari, “Optimal strong approximation for quadratic forms,” Duke Math. J. 168 (2019), no. 10, 1887–1927 — arXiv:1510.00462
* Link(s): [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.00462) and [full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1510.00462v4), visited and title/author confirmed 2026-10-10 via arXiv pages; theorem checked in full HTML.
* Main theorem (Theorem 1.2): Let F be a non-degenerate integral quadratic form in d variables and Ω its relevant real domain; let B⊂Ω be a ball of radius 0<r<1, m an integer, λ mod m with F(λ)≡N (mod m), and suppose F(X)=N has local integral solutions in every Z_p in the prescribed residue class λ mod p^{ord_p(m)}. For d≥5, if N≫(r⁻¹m)^{4+δ} for every δ>0, with constants depending on Ω,δ, then there is an integral x with F(x)=N, x≡λ (mod m), and x/√N∈B. The exponent 4 is optimal. For d=4 the stated theorem requires N odd and N≫(r⁻¹m)^{6+δ}; the authors conjecture the optimal exponent 4+δ (and say 6 cannot be lowered below 4).
* Summary: This is an effective, height-controlled local-to-global theorem that simultaneously imposes a congruence condition and a shrinking real target. It uses a circle-method/Kloosterman-sum treatment; it is substantially stronger than qualitative strong approximation. In dimension ≥5, the height scale is essentially (m/r)^4, not merely polynomial in the modulus with an unspecified exponent.
* Relevance to pointwise ES: This gives O124 a precise model for a quadratic-form route: if ES data could be encoded as a locally soluble quadratic equation in ≥5 variables with a target region capturing positivity and the desired residue condition, it would deliver an explicit polynomial covering scale. The actual ES surface is not such a fixed quadratic form, and the sharp d=4 case is conjectural; it does not hit the ES shifted-divisor residue classes. O123/O128 may use the height-versus-modulus framework when comparing their congruence counts. Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE — quantitative congruence-plus-cap representation, but no direct ES parametrization.

* **Parent note: the SL₂/determinant case is the one closest to ES (checked in the local PDF, pp. 2–3).** Sardari's Corollary 1.1 reads: "Fix Ω a compact subset of SL(2,R) and any δ>0. Then for every matrix A∈Ω and m∈Z there exists an integral H with det H = m and ‖A − H/√m‖ ≪ m^(−1/6+δ); −1/6 cannot be replaced by anything smaller than −1/4." Remark 1.4 derives it from Thm 1.2 with F = x₁x₄ − x₂x₃, i.e. d = 4 with exponent 6. Sardari also credits Sarnak [Sar15a,b] with an *elementary* proof that SL₂(Z) → SL₂(Z/qZ) has optimal lifts. For O123/O128 this is the benchmark "congruence class plus real cone, at height polynomial in the modulus" for determinant equations. The campaign writes Type I in the form ef − 4a²d = 1. ES needs more than such a lift: the off-diagonal product must have the shape 4a²d, and the congruence data come from p, which is a multiplicative constraint and not a residue class. So it is a benchmark only. *Assessment: LANGUAGE-FRAMEWORK ONLY for ES*, while it remains a TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE for pure congruence-lifting subproblems.

### Context: affine quadrics, strong approximation, and the missing “level-to-height” step

Kneser’s classical strong-approximation results for spin groups/quadratic forms and the later homogeneous-space theory of Eskin–McMullen provide qualitative density or counting in suitable local-open sets. For a citable exposition of the algebraic-group criterion (including spinor groups of isotropic quadratic forms), see A. S. Rapinchuk, “Strong approximation for algebraic groups,” [survey PDF](https://library.slmath.org/books/Book61/files/70rapi.pdf) (visited 2026-10-10; title/author confirmed). For the counting input, see Alex Eskin and Curt McMullen, “Mixing, counting, and equidistribution in Lie groups,” Duke Math. J. 71 (1993), 181–209, [Project Euclid page](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/duke-mathematical-journal/volume-71/issue-1/Mixing-counting-and-equidistribution-in-Lie-groups/10.1215/S0012-7094-93-07108-6.short) (visited 2026-10-10; metadata confirmed). Their mixing/counting framework is not itself a uniform every-residue-class bound as the modulus grows. For affine quadrics, strong approximation off a place is often available when the relevant spin group is isotropic/noncompact and local obstructions are absent. These results say that integral points are dense in the adelic/local topology; by themselves they do **not** give an explicit height H(q) such that every locally soluble residue class modulo q has a representative of height ≤H(q). Duke–Rudnick–Sarnak-type asymptotics can become such a bound only with errors uniform as q grows, and with the main term larger than the error for each target class. Sardari’s Theorem 1.2 is a concrete example where this difficult step is completed, with H essentially (q/r)⁴ in ≥5 variables.

Cao, Huang, and Zhang, “Effective equidistribution, arithmetic purity of strong approximation, and geometric sieve for affine quadrics,” arXiv:2509.23320, is a verified existing preprint: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23320), visited 2026-10-10; title/authors confirmed on the record. It reports effective S-integral equidistribution, arithmetic purity off one place, and a geometric sieve for p₀-integral points. **Unverified in this draft:** its exact theorem statements/ranges were not checked in the PDF; it does not follow from the abstract that there is a universal optimal polynomial covering exponent for every mod-q class. Strong approximation/purity is not a synonym for a best possible least-height bound.

### Section takeaway

- The closest analogue of ES “x≲p” is a uniform least-height bound for every locally admissible residue class, not qualitative density or average equidistribution.
- Sardari gives an essentially optimal H≈(m/r)⁴ bound for quadratic forms in at least five variables; the four-variable exponent 4 remains conjectural in that theorem.
- S³ has optimal cap-covering exponent 4/3 only conditionally on twisted Linnik; unconditional bounds and exceptional holes matter.
- Spectral gap, Ramanujan expansion, and homogeneous counting do not alone imply worst-case covering at polynomially optimal height.
- None of these results directly supplies positive-denominator ES solutions for every prime; they offer quantitative templates and cautions, not a proof of ES.

## (3) Pointwise representation of integers by ternary quadratic forms

A positive-definite integral ternary form is a fixed form \(Q(x,y,z)\); the question is whether a given \(n\) lies in its value set. This is not a generic “three variables can absorb anything” principle: local obstructions, genus-versus-class discrepancies, and spinor-exceptional square classes matter. The principal analytic mechanism is the theta series decomposition into Eisenstein and cusp parts. The Eisenstein coefficient measures local representability; a sufficiently strong cusp-coefficient bound makes the former dominate for large \(n\). In rank three, the main term is governed by imaginary quadratic class numbers, hence Siegel’s ineffective lower bound (and possible Siegel zeros) is central.

### Duke–Schulze-Pillot, “Representation of integers by positive ternary quadratic forms and equidistribution of lattice points on ellipsoids,” *Invent. Math.* 99 (1990), 49–58 — DOI: 10.1007/BF01234411
* Link(s): [EuDML record/full-text link](https://eudml.org/doc/143750), visited 2026-10-10 (title, authors, journal, volume and pages confirmed); [Duke’s 1997 Notices survey PDF](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~wdduke/preprints/old.pdf), visited 2026-10-10 (title/author confirmed; theorem discussion checked); [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01234411), title and bibliographic match confirmed.
* Main theorem(s): Kane’s verbatim quotation of Duke–Schulze-Pillot [9, Theorem] is: “If Q is a positive definite quadratic form in 3 variables, then every sufficiently large eligible integer represented primitively by the Spinor Genus with bounded divisibility at the anisotropic primes is represented by Q.” “Eligible” means locally represented everywhere; retain the primitive-representation and bounded-anisotropic-divisibility hypotheses. The theorem is ineffective because it relies on class-number lower bounds. The theta-series comparison is between the weighted spinor-genus average (main term) and cusp part. The rank-three Eisenstein coefficient is a quadratic class-number/local-density term; Siegel gives a lower bound of size n^(1/2−ε), ineffectively. Duke’s 1997 Notices survey, “Some old problems and new results about quadratic forms,” states the relevant Iwaniec/Duke cusp-coefficient bound O(n^(1/2−1/28+ε)); the saving 1/28 is not an effective numerical cutoff.
* Summary: This is the foundational ternary-form result connecting pointwise representation to equidistribution and half-integral-weight modular forms. “Large enough” depends on the form and the exceptional square classes are not a license to ignore local/genus conditions. The classical representation cutoff is generally ineffective when it uses Siegel’s lower bound for quadratic class numbers; the equidistribution statement concerns directions of represented vectors, not arbitrary coordinate boxes.
* Relevance to pointwise ES: O124 can use the genus/class/spinor framework if an ES condition can be reduced to values of one fixed ternary form with the prime as target. It cannot be applied merely because an ES identity has three denominator variables: the resulting equation is not generally a fixed quadratic polynomial. No uniform result here ranges over forms and targets in the coupled way ES requires.
* Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE — strong pointwise representation theory, but no present reduction from every prime to a fixed form.

### Duke, “On ternary quadratic forms,” *J. Number Theory* 110 (2005), 37–43 — DOI: 10.1016/j.jnt.2004.06.013
* Link(s): [ScienceDirect article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X04001404), visited 2026-10-10 (title, author, journal and pages confirmed); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2004.06.013).
* Main theorem(s): The short paper revisits Duke’s 1988 square-free-representation result. ### ES relevance check: is this a ternary quadratic-form problem?
No direct ternary quadratic-form formulation follows from the campaign identities stated so far. The campaign relation \(ef-4a^{2}d=1\) is bilinear in \(e,f\) and cubic in \(a,d\), not a homogeneous/inhomogeneous quadratic polynomial in three integral variables. Type II, \(p=qr-4s_1s_2\), is bilinear in four variables; fixing two variables does not turn the remaining relation into the required fixed ternary quadratic form in any evident way. Thus the ternary-form literature is not directly applicable without a new, explicit three-line identity and proof of the needed positivity/congruence/size conditions. The classical ES equation itself, after clearing denominators, is degree three: \(4xyz=n(xy+xz+yz)\), not ternary quadratic.

### Effectivity, exceptional sets, and variable restrictions
* **Ineffective versus effective.** Siegel’s class-number lower bound is ineffective because a possible exceptional real zero cannot be quantitatively excluded. It often proves “all sufficiently large” with a form-dependent but uncomputed threshold. Effective bounds require stronger inputs (often GRH), explicit finite computations, or restricted hypotheses. Rouse’s GRH conclusion for the remaining candidate ternaries and Ono–Soundararajan’s GRH result are conditional; unconditionally, no blanket GRH-style cutoff is claimed here.
* **Finite exceptional sets.** For genus/class questions the natural exceptions are spinor-exceptional square classes, potentially infinite sets of integers, not merely a finite list of small exceptions. Outside those classes and with local solubility, sufficiently large targets are represented in the cited framework. Rouse’s *46 critical integers* are a different finite test set for odd-universality, not a theorem that each individual ternary form has only 46 exceptions.
* **Size constraints.** Equidistribution on ellipsoids gives angular distribution at macroscopic scales; it is not by itself a representation in prescribed short intervals/boxes. Blomer–Brüdern do obtain arithmetic restrictions (almost-prime coordinates) for the special sum-of-three-squares problem. This is not a general short-box theorem. Congruence-restricted or fixed-variable conclusions require their own hypotheses and estimates; no cited result here supplies ES’s required positive denominators in the right ranges.

### Section takeaway
* Ternary-form pointwise representation is governed by local densities, genus/class distinctions, spinor exceptions, and half-integral-weight cusp bounds.
* The strongest general “all sufficiently large” conclusions can be ineffective through Siegel zeros; effective thresholds need extra hypotheses or explicit special-family work.
* GRH gives powerful results for selected forms (notably Ono–Soundararajan); Rouse’s 451 criterion is conditional on the remaining ternary candidates and concerns universality.
* Restricted-variable results exist in special settings, but equidistribution is not a short-box guarantee and almost-prime coordinates are not ES denominators.
* The campaign’s \(ef-4a^2d=1\), Type II \(p=qr-4s_1s_2\), and cleared ES equation are not ternary quadratic forms as written; no direct transfer is justified.

### Yonatan Harpaz, “Integral points on conic log K3 surfaces,” *JEMS* 21 (2019), 627–664 — arXiv:1511.04876
* Links: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04876), [full text PDF](https://www.math.univ-paris13.fr/~harpaz/integral_points.pdf). Verified 2026-10-10 via arXiv abs page and author PDF; theorem and definitions checked in PDF.
* Definition 3.0.1 (paper’s deliberately restrictive convention): a log K3 surface is a smooth, geometrically integral, simply connected surface \(Z\) equipped with a compactification \(Z\subset\bar Z\) such that \(D=\bar Z\setminus Z\) is a simple-normal-crossings divisor and \([D]=-K_{\bar Z}\) in \(\operatorname{Pic}(\bar Z)\). Other conventions weaken simple connectedness or the anticanonical condition. A “conic log K3” here means the case \(n=m=1\) of the pencil (1.0.1): the smooth affine surface in the bundle \(\mathcal O(-1)\oplus\mathcal O(-1)\to\mathbf P^1\) with equation \(f(t,s)x^2+g(t,s)y^2=1\), where \(f,g\) are products of two distinct linear forms; its compactification is a degree-4 del Pezzo conic bundle with four singular fibers and anticanonical elliptic boundary (Proposition 3.0.3).
* Theorem 1.0.1 (exact sample statement). Let \(S\) be a finite set of places of \(\mathbf Q\) containing \(2,\infty\). For \(i=1,\ldots,4\), take \(c_i,d_i\in\mathbf Z_S\), each pair S-coprime, with \(\Delta_{ij}=c_jd_i-c_id_j\ne0\) for \(i\ne j\). The pencil
  \[
  (c_1t+d_1s)(c_2t+d_2s)x^2+(c_3t+d_3s)(c_4t+d_4s)y^2=1
  \]
  has an S-integral point if the seven square classes \([-1],[\Delta_{12}],[\Delta_{13}],[\Delta_{14}],[\Delta_{23}],[\Delta_{24}],[\Delta_{34}]\) are linearly independent in \(\mathbf Q^*/(\mathbf Q^*)^2\). (The printed theorem has an evident typographical bracket in the \(\Delta_{23}\) term.) This is a specialization of Theorem 3.1.16; the latter has a local-fiber condition at primes dividing its square-free parameter \(d\), as well as the stated restrictions on \(S_0,I,c_i,d_i,a,b\).
* This is **existence**, not strong approximation. Remark 1.0.2 proves real and every p-adic points, and integral p-adic points for odd p, under the theorem’s hypotheses; it does not assert an S-integral point close to an arbitrarily prescribed real point, nor density of integral points in the real locus. The proof’s geometric fibration method adjusts the parameter to obtain an integral fiber point. Its input theorem 3.3.1 is Green–Tao–Ziegler: for pairwise non-proportional linear forms, if for each prime outside S there is a local pair at which their product is a unit, then one can choose infinitely many S-integral parameter pairs close to prescribed local data, including the real projective parameter, with each linear form equal to an S-unit times a distinct prime. This is the finite-complexity prime-tuples theorem, replacing Schinzel’s hypothesis; it supplies parameter choices, not strong approximation on the total surface. GTZ unconditionality is essential to the advertised unconditional existence result. The general fibration strategy explains the possible role of Schinzel: it predicts simultaneous prime values in parameter polynomials, but this theorem avoids that conjecture in its linear-form setting.
* Relevance: direct model for a conic fibration where the coefficient and its scale vary together, and for separating “a real/local point” from approximation to a target real component. It does not give the positive-coordinate point required by ES, and its square-class hypotheses do not hold uniformly for the ES family. Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE — fibration plus prime-values technology, but no pointwise ES implication.

### What transfers to ES, and what does not

The decisive mismatch is quantifier and topology. Harpaz proves an unconditional *existence* theorem for a special conic-log-K3 family using GTZ prime values; Green–Tao–Ziegler’s own parameter selection can approximate prescribed local data including a real projective parameter, but Harpaz’s conclusion is not strong approximation on the surface and does not guarantee the ES-positive real component. Schinzel is a standard conditional substitute in broader fibration arguments, not a hidden assumption in Harpaz’s Theorem 1.0.1. CT–Xu provides a genuine bridge from integral strong approximation/Brauer–Manin to quadratic-form representations, but only under homogeneous-space hypotheses and not automatically at the real place. Markoff results demonstrate that strong finite-level approximation and almost-all integral solubility can coexist with infinitely many Hasse failures; neither yields every-coefficient solubility. For ES, one needs an integral point on each coefficient fiber with all three denominators positive—strictly stronger than signed integral solubility, local solubility, BM non-obstruction, or approximation off infinity.

## Section takeaway

Harpaz’s conic-log-K3 theorem is unconditional existence, not strong approximation on the surface.
GTZ replaces Schinzel for its linear prime-value parameter choice; it does not solve the real sign/component problem.
CT–Xu links BM-controlled strong approximation to selected integral quadratic representations, subject to group and model hypotheses.
Markoff theory gives strong congruence dynamics and almost-all results, yet still has infinitely many Hasse failures.
None of these results gives a positive integral point on every varying ES fiber, including its prescribed real chamber.

### Cayley cubic, torsors, and what Elsholtz–Tao actually use

#### D. R. Heath-Brown, “The density of rational points on Cayley’s cubic surface,” (preprint 2002; published in *Proceedings of the Session in Analytic Number Theory and Diophantine Equations*, Bonner Math. Schriften 360 (2003), 1–30)
* Link(s): [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0210333) and [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0210333), verified 2026-10-10 (title/author and theorem checked in full text).
* Main theorem: The paper’s unnumbered main theorem states `B(log B)^6 ≪ N(B) ≪ B(log B)^6`, where `N(B)` counts primitive integer vectors on the open Cayley cubic `Σ 1/X_i=0`, of height `max |X_i|≤B` (excluding its nine lines). This is the Manin-predicted order, not a pointwise ES theorem. Lemma 1 gives the universal-torsor parametrisation: `X_i=ε y_j y_k y_l z_ij z_ik z_il` with the stated pairwise coprimality conditions and torsor equation `Σ y_i z_jk z_kl z_lj=0`.
* Summary / relevance: Elsholtz–Tao Remark 2.10 explicitly maps a solution to `(-n,4x,4y,4z)` on this surface and cites Heath-Brown’s Lemma 1; when `n` is prime this point is primitive. They say Type I/II correspond roughly to one `z_1i`/one `y_i` factor being `±n`. Bright–Loughran identify the projective closure of the ES surface with the Cayley cubic (their geometric formulation). Subsequent work by de la Bretèche counts rational points on the Cayley ruled cubic, but neither height-counting theorem ensures a positive solution in each prime fibre. O124/O126 can use this torsor/surface identification; O123/O128 may use it to understand the parametrisation, not as a pointwise estimate. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — precise geometric parametrisation and global count, with wrong uniformity/sign/height for pointwise positive ES.

#### Elsholtz–Tao, “Counting the number of solutions to the Erdős–Straus equation on unit fractions,” *J. Number Theory* 133 (2013), 算 (arXiv:1107.1010)
* Link(s): [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1010) and [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.1010), checked against the campaign’s local PDF on 2026-10-10.
* Exact results/inputs: Theorem 1.1 gives `Σ_{p≤N}f_I(p) ≍ N(log N)^2` up to a `log log N` loss on the upper bound, and `Σ_{p≤N}f_II(p) ≍ N(log N)^2`; hence `Σ_{p≤N}f(p) ≍ N(log N)^2` with the same upper `log log N` loss. Remark 1.3 states higher moments are out of reach because the relevant divisor-sum level is too high; it records Heath-Brown’s private-communication lower bound `Σ_{n≤N}f(n) ≫ N(log N)^6` and asks for a matching upper bound.

## 6. Conditional results

### Direct conditional Erdős–Straus results

No verified source located establishes ES for every sufficiently large prime, an `O(1)` exceptional set, or any other pointwise ES-like conclusion under GRH, Elliott–Halberstam, Hardy–Littlewood/Schinzel, Bateman–Horn, `abc`, or a comparable standard conjecture. In particular, neither GRH nor Elliott–Halberstam is known to turn the classical mean-value/counting results for `4/p` into `f(p)>0` for every sufficiently large prime. No conditional ES theorem should be cited without an exact statement; the conjecture remains open.

The obstacle is structural: the standard covering criterion expresses ES as a prime lying in one of a family of residue classes whose moduli can be comparable to the prime. GRH for Dirichlet L-functions supplies prime-distribution estimates for fixed/moderate moduli, while EH is still an averaged statement over moduli. Neither by itself guarantees that each individual prime is hit by the highly structured ES family. This is a diagnosis of the gap, not a theorem that stronger hypotheses could never suffice.

### Polynomial identities, Schinzel, and the quadratic-residue obstruction

#### A. Schinzel, “Sur quelques propriétés des nombres `3/N` et `4/N`, où `N` est un nombre impair,” *Mathesis* 65 (1956), 219–222; L. J. Mordell, *Diophantine Equations* (1969)
* Link(s): Schinzel’s bibliography confirms title/journal/pages at [IM PAN](https://www.impan.pl/~schinzel/), visited 2026-10-10; Elsholtz–Tao arXiv full text (above, §1 and Proposition 1.9) verified the polynomial-solvability discussion. Mordell’s book bibliographic details are independently confirmed by ET’s bibliography/discussion.
* Exact content: Elsholtz–Tao Proposition 1.6 (attributing the observation essentially to Schinzel and Yamamoto) proves that for every odd perfect square `n`, `f_I(n)=f_II(n)=0`, by quadratic reciprocity. Their text stresses that this does not contradict ES because (1.2) is not equality for composite denominators; it illustrates why a strategy relying on Type I/II or a fixed finite set of covering congruences cannot simply be extended uniformly to squares. The precise Mordell obstruction cited in discussions of polynomial identities is that such fixed polynomial parametrisations leave quadratic-residue classes uncovered; it is not a claim that arbitrary, varying-modulus constructions cannot cover them. ET Proposition 1.9 lists three families of polynomially solvable residue classes and says conversely every class on that list is solvable by polynomials. Consequently a conditional argument based only on distribution among a fixed finite set of classes has a structural gap; the pointwise covering criterion allows moduli growing with `p`.
* Relevance: O127 can use this to rule out a simplistic “Hypothesis H makes a fixed polynomial cover universal” argument. The phrase “cannot cover quadratic-residue classes” should not be strengthened to all arbitrary polynomial identities without checking the precise identity class; the safely verified statement is the obstruction for the standard Schinzel/Mordell polynomial-identity framework. **Assessment: TRANSFERABLE TECHNIQUE** — explains why fixed-modulus polynomial covering is inadequate, not a conditional ES theorem.

### GRH least nonresidue and absence of an ES application

Under GRH, Ankeny’s least-quadratic-nonresidue bound is `≪ (log q)^2`; Bach gives an explicit version (commonly quoted as a least nonresidue `< 2(log q)^2` for sufficiently large modulus, with small-modulus qualifications depending on the formulation). This concerns finding a small prime nonresidue modulo a given prime `q`, not producing an Egyptian-fraction decomposition. Searches of Elsholtz–Tao and the cited Yamamoto parametric/congruence literature found no theorem of the form “if `p` is a quadratic nonresidue modulo some small `q`, then ES holds.” A nonresidue may help choose a congruence class in a parameter family, but no verified implication to the full covering criterion was found. Therefore the GRH bound is not an ES conditional result and cannot be imported without a new explicit construction.

No verified Hypothesis H/Bateman–Horn implication to pointwise ES was located. Schinzel’s conjectural statement for fixed `m>k≥3` that `m/n` is representable for all sufficiently large `n` is itself the open Egyptian-fraction problem recorded by Elsholtz–Tao §11, not a consequence of prime-pattern conjectures. Hypothesis H controls simultaneous prime values of fixed polynomials; it does not make the family of ES covering moduli grow with the target prime.

### Negative search / scope note

Targeted web and bibliographic searches (arXiv API, OpenAlex and web search, 2026-10-10) for “Erdős–Straus” with GRH, Elliott–Halberstam, Hardy–Littlewood, Schinzel, Bateman–Horn and `abc` found no verified conditional pointwise theorem. ### Section takeaway

- No verified conditional proof of pointwise ES (even for all sufficiently large primes) was found.
- GRH/EH-style distribution theorems do not presently overcome the individual-prime, large-modulus covering problem.
- Ono–Soundararajan provide a genuine GRH finite-exception theorem for a particular ternary quadratic form, not for ES.
- Rouse’s finite-test universality work is an analogy, not a conditional ES result.
- Do not promote average results, analogues, or the Schinzel generalization to conditional progress on ES.
